## Popularity and the Route to Candidacy

Jakarta's 2024 election illustrates the difference between **polling support and eligibility to run**. A poll measures respondents' preferences at a particular time. To become an election contestant, a prospective pair must still pass through nomination, verification, and formal designation by the KPU.

That distinction matters when examining KIM Plus support for Ridwan Kamil-Suswono, debate over Anies Baswedan's prospects, and the independent candidacy of Dharma Pongrekun-Kun Wardana. Scenarios discussed in August 2024 were not the same as the eventual candidate list.

In September 2024, the KPU designated three candidate pairs. Jakarta's election therefore involved multiple pairs, rather than one pair competing against an empty ballot box option.

| --- | --- |
| 1 | Ridwan Kamil-Suswono |
| 2 | Dharma Pongrekun-Kun Wardana |
| 3 | Pramono Anung-Rano Karno |

## Coalitions and Party Choices

KIM Plus brought several parties behind Ridwan Kamil-Suswono. A **coalition agreement** can combine campaign resources and change a party's nomination choices. Joining a coalition does not, by itself, establish coercion or a hidden agreement.

PKS and PKB had previously featured in discussions about possible support for Anies. Their shift toward another pair shows that early recommendations do not necessarily become final nominations. Suswono is a PKS figure, and his involvement also concerns the party's position within the coalition.

The legislative seats relevant to Jakarta's nomination debate belonged to the provincial DPRD, not the national DPR. Nomination rules also changed in August 2024. A statement that a party could not nominate a pair must therefore identify the date and applicable rule.

An assessment of coalition motives should distinguish parties' official explanations, observers' interpretations, and evidence of agreements. Disputes between organizations or elites do not establish why a party acted without a clear evidentiary connection.

## Changes to Nomination Rules

Constitutional Court Decision 60/PUU-XXII/2024 changed **the threshold for party nominations**. The earlier rule used 20% of DPRD seats or 25% of valid votes. Following the decision, valid-vote thresholds ranged from 6.5% to 10%, with categories determined by the relevant area's registered electorate.

| Stage | Status of the decision |
| --- | --- |
| Party endorsement or declaration | A political choice, not formal KPU designation |
| Independent support qualification | Eligibility to proceed with the nomination process |
| Registration and verification | Examination of documents and requirements by the KPU |
| Formal candidate designation | Establishment of the official election contestants |

PKPU No. 10 of 2024 implemented the change on 25 August 2024. An analysis ending with the coalition map on 19 August therefore misses an important development. [The 2024 nomination threshold change](/en/articles/politics/regional-elections-turmoil) altered the available routes, while a party's decision to use them remained political.

## The Empty Box and Allegations of Controlled Candidates

The **empty box option** applies to elections with only one candidate pair under the applicable rules. It is not another name for invalid votes, abstention, or every form of opposition to a candidate. Jakarta's designation of three pairs meant that the single-pair scenario did not occur.

Calling someone a “puppet candidate” alleges that another actor controls the candidacy. Establishing that claim requires evidence of coordination, financing, or control. Low popularity, absence from a polling list, or a strategic benefit to an opponent does not establish that relationship.

For example, a candidate with limited support may still pursue independent political aims. The fact that entering the race changes the ballot does not explain who directed the decision. Analysis can test strategic possibilities, but it needs to identify them as hypotheses.

## Polls Are Not Election Results

**Polling figures depend on questions and timing**. Before registration, a survey may include names that never become candidates. Open-ended questions, lists of individuals, and simulations of official pairs answer different questions. Results cannot simply be transferred between formats.

| Information to examine | Effect on interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Fieldwork dates | Support can change after coalition or candidate changes |
| Population and sampling method | Defines whose preferences the results represent |
| Names and order of options | Determines the choices offered to respondents |
| Undecided respondents | Limits claims that voters have settled their decisions |
| Measurement uncertainty | Prevents small differences from being treated as certainty |

A candidate not included in a simulation should not be assigned zero support. An absent measurement is different from a measurement of zero. A percentage of respondents also differs from the percentage of valid votes later counted by the KPU.

## Complaints About Identity Data and Evidence

On 19 August 2024, Bawaslu South Jakarta announced a **complaint service for alleged misuse of identity numbers** in support for the independent Dharma-Kun pair. The announcement establishes that complaints and a reporting channel existed at that time. It is not, by itself, a criminal finding against the candidates.

An investigation needs to distinguish valid support, the data owner's consent, the conduct of people submitting information, and the candidates' responsibility. Each question requires evidence. Designation as a contestant does not automatically resolve every data concern. A complaint does not automatically invalidate a candidacy either.

Jakarta's election shows why chronology matters. Popularity, party endorsement, support verification, and formal designation are different stages. Criticism that identifies the stage, date, and evidence can scrutinize the process without turning speculation into fact.

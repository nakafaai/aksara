## Assessing the Legal Basis and Risks of Nusantara

Moving Indonesia's governmental center to Nusantara brings together **legal certainty, residents' rights, and security planning**. Criticism needs to distinguish policy objections, petitioners' allegations, and judicial findings. They do not have the same status.

This article examines the formation of the 2022 State Capital Law, its 2023 amendment, and geostrategic arguments discussed in research from that period. It does not claim that the entire relocation has been completed or that anticipated security threats will necessarily materialize.

## The Sequence of Legal Decisions

**Parliamentary approval and promulgation** are different stages. Approval of the IKN bill on 18 January 2022 should not be described as the date the president promulgated Law No. 3 of 2022. The law was enacted and promulgated on 15 February 2022.

| Date | Event | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2019 | The government announced the proposed location in East Kalimantan | The policy decision preceded the IKN Law |
| 18 January 2022 | The DPR approved the IKN bill | Approval of the draft legislation |
| 15 February 2022 | Law No. 3 of 2022 was enacted and promulgated | The statutory basis for establishing IKN took effect |
| 31 October 2023 | Law No. 21 of 2023 was promulgated | Several provisions of the IKN Law changed |

The 2023 amendment addressed matters including the authority of the IKN administration, spatial planning, financing, and budget management. Criticism of the 2021 draft or original 2022 provisions is therefore not a complete account of the amended framework.

Whether the legislative process was too rushed remains a legitimate policy question. The number of days alone, however, does not establish whether mandatory stages occurred, public submissions were considered, or reasons for rejecting suggestions were explained.

## Petitions and Court Findings

In case 25/PUU-XX/2022, petitioners challenged the IKN Law's formation, including the deliberation period and public participation. On 20 July 2022, the Constitutional Court **rejected the petition**. It found the arguments about speed and inadequate openness legally unsubstantiated. Speed alone did not determine constitutionality.

Case 40/PUU-XX/2022 had a different outcome: the petition was declared inadmissible because it was unclear. Standing and the merits were not considered further. That outcome is not equivalent to rejecting every substantive objection after examining it.

| Term | What it establishes | Conclusion to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Petitioner's allegation | An argument made by the party requesting review | Treating an allegation as a judicial finding |
| Rejected petition | The requested relief was not granted for the reasons in the judgment | Treating all policy criticism as irrelevant |
| Inadmissible petition | A barrier prevented further consideration | Assuming every challenged provision was substantively vindicated |

The public can continue to assess benefits, expenditure, and consequences. A claim that a court found the law “defective,” however, must match the holding and reasoning of a specific case.

## Public Participation That Can Be Examined

Questions about **meaningful participation** become more precise when connected to actual decisions. Residents affected by land acquisition, for example, need information about boundaries, valuation, settlement options, and objections. An invitation to a meeting does not by itself show whether their input influenced a decision.

The same applies to customary communities and environmental protection. Scrutiny should trace recognition of rights, dispute resolution, environmental documents, and implementation of restoration obligations. A risk of dispossession or environmental damage needs a specific location and evidence. It should not be presented as an inevitable outcome for every resident.

Budget scrutiny asks what work is funded, who is responsible, and how results are measured. Large expenditure does not automatically prove waste. Equally, a valid legal basis does not guarantee efficient implementation.

## Geography and Security Risks

**Defense geography** examines how terrain, distance, transport networks, and facilities affect protection. Research by Wirawan (2022) and Habibie (2022) raised these questions for IKN. Analysis from that period should be treated as risk assessment, not as a current intelligence report.

The area of the whole island of Borneo should also not be presented as the area of Indonesian Kalimantan. The island includes Indonesian, Malaysian, and Bruneian territory. Island size alone does not establish the security readiness of a governmental district.

| Assessment element | Illustrative question | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Threat | Is an actor both willing and able to attack? | A dated threat assessment with a defined scope |
| Vulnerability | Are facilities or routes inadequately protected? | Security audits and facility conditions |
| Impact | Which services would an incident disrupt? | Continuity and emergency plans |
| Mitigation | Do protective measures reduce the identified risk? | Implementation, exercises, and evaluation |

The past presence of a terrorist network on an island does not establish that capital relocation increases the probability of attack. Proximity to shipping routes or borders also needs analysis alongside protective capacity. Moving directly from geography to a certain threat skips the evidentiary step.

## Geopolitics and Continuing Oversight

Relocation can change **infrastructure and cooperation needs**, but a city's coordinates do not determine foreign policy. Economic interests, diplomatic choices, and security relationships still require examination through actual policies. A need for investment does not prove that another country automatically controls Indonesia's political direction.

Oversight should connect four things: the rules applicable at the time, implementation decisions, effects on residents, and promised outcomes. A revised construction plan, for example, should explain costs and consequences for services, rather than merely announce a new target.

These standards allow both support for and opposition to IKN to be tested. The question extends beyond whether a law exists: are powers exercised openly, are residents' rights protected, and are risks addressed using evidence?

## Candidacy Rules Amid Political Turmoil

The debate before Indonesia's 2024 regional elections showed that **candidacy rules shape access to competition**. A lower threshold can allow parties to nominate candidates. Legal eligibility, however, does not guarantee that a party will choose a particular person.

Understanding the events of August 2024 requires distinguishing court rulings, proposed legislation, and the election commission's implementing regulations. This article examines that episode as part of the history of the 2024 elections, rather than predicting an election that has yet to take place.

## The Sequence of Rule Changes

Approval by the Legislative Body does not turn **a bill into law**. This procedural distinction matters when reading reports about changes to regional election rules.

| Date | Event | Significance for candidacy rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 August 2024 | The Constitutional Court issued Decisions No. 60 and No. 70/PUU-XXII/2024 | Nomination thresholds changed. Reasoning clarified when age requirements must be met |
| 21 August 2024 | The Legislative Body and government approved sending the regional election bill to a plenary session | It remained proposed legislation |
| 22 August 2024 | The plenary session lacked a quorum | The planned passage of the amendment did not take place |
| 25 August 2024 | The election commission issued Regulation No. 10 of 2024 | Regulation No. 8 of 2024 was amended for the nomination process |

Public opposition and demonstrations formed part of the debate. To establish which rules were ultimately applied, however, we need to read the operative decisions and regulations rather than treating every political proposal as law.

## Registered Voters and Valid Votes

Constitutional Court Decision No. 60/PUU-XXII/2024 changed the requirements for nominations by parties or coalitions. Under Election Commission Regulation No. 10 of 2024, **the number of registered voters in the final voter list (DPT)** determines the percentage category. That percentage is then applied to valid votes in the relevant regional legislative election, not to the population or the DPT count.

### Governor and Deputy Governor Candidates

At provincial level, **the previous election's DPT** determines the applicable row. Having identified that row, use the total valid votes in the provincial legislative election as the calculation base.

| Registered voters in the province | Minimum share of valid votes |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 2 million | 10% |
| More than 2 million, up to 6 million | 8.5% |
| More than 6 million, up to 12 million | 7.5% |
| More than 12 million | 6.5% |

### Regent or Mayor and Their Deputy Candidates

For regencies and cities, **the DPT category boundaries differ** from provincial boundaries. The vote calculation also uses the relevant regency or city legislative election.

| Registered voters in the regency or city | Minimum share of valid votes |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 250,000 | 10% |
| More than 250,000, up to 500,000 | 8.5% |
| More than 500,000, up to 1 million | 7.5% |
| More than 1 million | 6.5% |

### A Worked Threshold Calculation

Suppose a province has **8 million registered voters** and 5 million valid votes in its provincial legislative election. These are hypothetical figures, not data from a particular province. Eight million falls in the category above 6 million and up to 12 million, so the applicable percentage is 7.5%.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Threshold} &= 7.5\% \times 5{,}000{,}000 \\ &= 0.075 \times 5{,}000{,}000 \\ &= 375{,}000\ \text{valid votes} \end{aligned}" />

A party or coalition with 400,000 valid votes exceeds this threshold by 25,000 votes. It therefore meets the vote-count requirement in this example. Candidate qualifications and nomination documents still require examination. Multiplying 7.5% by the 8 million registered voters would use the wrong base.

## Age Is Assessed at Formal Candidate Designation

Constitutional Court Decision No. 70/PUU-XXII/2024 **rejected the petition**, while its reasoning affirmed that requirements must be satisfied before candidates are formally designated. Election Commission Regulation No. 10 of 2024 sets candidate designation as the reference point for age. This differs from both registration and the inauguration of elected officials.

| Office sought | Minimum age | Reference point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governor and deputy governor | 30 years | Formal designation of candidate pairs |
| Regent and deputy regent | 25 years | Formal designation of candidate pairs |
| Mayor and deputy mayor | 25 years | Formal designation of candidate pairs |

For example, someone who turns 30 only after candidate designation does not meet the gubernatorial age requirement on that date, even if they would be 30 at inauguration. The issue is when the requirement must be met, not a reduction of the minimum age for mayors from 30 to 25.

## Nomination Eligibility and Party Decisions

A lower threshold changes **the choices available to parties**, but does not decide whom they nominate. Coalition negotiations, internal decisions, and individual qualifications still affect the final list.

In Jakarta, the election commission ultimately designated three pairs: Ridwan Kamil-Suswono, Dharma Pongrekun-Kun Wardana, and Pramono Anung-Rano Karno. A person discussed publicly but never designated as an official candidate should not be described as withdrawing from the candidate list. The distinction between popularity, party support, and official candidacy also explains why [the KIM Plus and empty-box debate](/en/articles/politics/kim-plus-empty-box) must be read in its chronological context.

Scrutinizing nominations means checking documents and decisions at each stage. This allows criticism of elite political strategies without conflating suspected motives, proposed rules, and the law actually applied.

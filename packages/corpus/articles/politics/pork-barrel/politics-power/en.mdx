## Public Assistance and Electoral Interests

Social assistance helps people meet basic needs. A political problem arises when officials use **control over assistance** to benefit a particular candidate. This article examines that risk in the debate surrounding Indonesia's 2024 election. Budgets, disbursement, and election results need separate scrutiny so that criticism rests on the right evidence.

Imagine two families with the same level of need. The same program criteria should apply to both. If a candidate's supporters receive priority, or recipients are threatened with losing assistance because of their vote, a public service becomes a means of pressure. This hypothetical example describes a mechanism to investigate. It does not establish that every distribution of assistance follows that mechanism.

## Targeted Spending and Political Advantage

**Pork-barrel politics** generally refers to directing public benefits toward particular areas or groups for political advantage. John A. Ferejohn's 1974 book examined the politics of river and harbor projects in the United States. The study connects local projects with national budget decisions.

Popularity alone does not make a policy abusive. Governments can earn support by delivering effective programs. The relevant questions concern eligibility, the use of public office, and whether benefits are exchanged for political support. These distinctions help keep the analysis precise.

| Concept | Mechanism to examine | Hypothetical example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Programmatic policy | Need-based criteria apply consistently | Every household meeting the eligibility rules receives assistance |
| Pork-barrel politics | Targeted benefits serve electoral advantage | Projects receive priority in areas considered decisive for victory |
| Clientelism | Benefits are linked to political support | An intermediary offers assistance on condition that a recipient supports a candidate |

These mechanisms may overlap, but evidence for one does not automatically prove another. Drazen and Eslava’s 2006 model explains how targeted spending can influence voter support. It helps formulate hypotheses, but is not direct evidence about Indonesia’s 2024 election.

## Reading the Social Protection Budget

The Ministry of Finance's *Informasi APBN Tahun Anggaran 2024* lists **Rp496.8 trillion for social protection**. This category is broader than social assistance spending: it also includes subsidies and other support. The entire amount therefore cannot be described as assistance distributed immediately before the election.

## Moving from Suspicion to Evidence

Distribution close to polling day can be **a reason to investigate**, but timing alone does not prove abuse. Disbursement schedules may also reflect program design, data updates, or economic needs. An investigation should compare official explanations with budget documents and actual implementation.

Three kinds of evidence complement each other. Eligibility rules reveal whether need or political affiliation determines access. Records and distribution activities identify who controls the program. Testimony and campaign materials can indicate whether assistance is presented as a candidate's personal gift or accompanied by pressure to vote.

Official visits likewise require examination of their purpose, financing, and messages. A visit count without a clear unit of expenditure or supporting documents cannot establish budget misuse. Criticism is stronger when it identifies a specific action, evidence, date, and responsible actor.

## Why Correlation Does Not Establish an Effect

Suppose assistance spending and support for a candidate both rise. **Changes occurring together** do not establish that assistance caused the increase in support. Campaigning, economic conditions, views of the government, debates, and changes in survey composition may also matter.

An assessment of the effect needs sufficiently comparable groups, information about when assistance was received, and an explanation of other factors controlled for. Surveys with different questions, samples, and dates should not be joined as if they formed one consistent measurement. Research published before 2024 cannot, by itself, establish what happened in the 2024 election.

In case 1/PHPU.PRES-XXII/2024 on 22 April 2024, the Constitutional Court majority rejected the Anies-Muhaimin petition and did not find convincing evidence for the alleged link between assistance and electoral advantage. Three justices dissented. The majority's reasoning and those objections matter. The legal outcome is not a statistical estimate proving either an effect or its absence.

## Oversight That Protects Recipients

The purpose of oversight is **fair access to assistance**. Residents should not lose social protection simply because a program becomes the subject of a political dispute. Scrutiny can focus on transparent eligibility rules, distribution records, neutral administration, and complaint channels that do not intimidate recipients.

This is where [nepotism in public decisions](/en/articles/politics/nepotism-in-political-governance) intersects with budgeting: personal or family connections can undermine decisions that should serve the public. Those connections still need to be linked to conduct that can be examined.

For voters, a useful question is whether assistance is delivered as a rule-based entitlement or treated as a political debt. For oversight bodies, the answer requires evidence. Criticism of politicized assistance can then support, rather than undermine, protection for people who need it.

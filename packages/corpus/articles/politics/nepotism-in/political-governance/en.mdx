## Family Relationships and Public Decisions

Nepotism must be assessed through **how power is used**, rather than officials' family names alone. Two relatives can work in the same institution without that fact proving misconduct. The problem arises when kinship overrides rules, competence, or the public interest in official decisions.

This distinction matters when discussing political dynasties in Indonesia. Citizens may scrutinize recruitment and conflicts of interest. Allegations of wrongdoing, however, need evidence about conduct, authority, and the rules breached. This article examines those boundaries through antinepotism law, governance data, and the controversy over candidacy requirements in 2023.

## Distinguishing Relationships from Misconduct

Article 1(5) of Law No. 28 of 1999 connects nepotism to **unlawful conduct by state officials** that favors family members or cronies over the interests of society, the nation, and the state. This legal definition is narrower than everyday uses of the word.

| Concept | Hypothetical example | What needs examination? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family relationship | Two siblings enter an open recruitment process | Do both meet the requirements and face the same assessment standards? |
| Conflict of interest | An official evaluates a relative's company | Is the relationship disclosed, and does the official withdraw from the decision? |
| Suspected nepotism | An official breaks rules to benefit a relative | What conduct, benefit, and breach can be established? |
| Political dynasty | Several family members hold political office | How do nomination, competition, and oversight work? |

A conflict of interest is a situation requiring management. Misconduct requires examination of conduct. Political dynasties can increase the risk of favoritism, but kinship alone does not establish a criminal offense. Both [relatives' political rights and fair competition](/en/articles/politics/dynastic-politics-asian-values) need protection.

## The Age Ruling and Ethics Proceedings

The debate over Gibran Rakabuming Raka's candidacy in the 2024 presidential election involved **two proceedings with different subjects**: eligibility and judicial conduct. Constitutional Court Decision No. 90/PUU-XXI/2023 created an exception to the minimum age of 40 for people who had held or were holding office obtained through elections, including regional elections. Gibran's experience as mayor of Surakarta made that exception relevant to his candidacy.

This was a ruling by a judicial institution, not a rule changed by the chief justice acting alone. Arguments about legal reasoning and the decision-making process must be distinguished from the findings of the subsequent ethics proceedings.

On 7 November 2023, the Constitutional Court's Honorary Council, in Decision No. 2/MKMK/L/11/2023, found that Anwar Usman had committed serious ethical violations and removed him as chief justice. **Removal as chief justice** differs from removal as a constitutional judge. That ethical sanction did not itself annul Decision No. 90.

| Question | Relevant document | Scope of the conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who meets the age requirement? | Constitutional Court Decision No. 90/PUU-XXI/2023 | Interprets the candidacy requirement |
| Did a judge violate ethical rules? | Honorary Council Decision No. 2/MKMK/L/11/2023 | Assesses judicial conduct and imposes ethical sanctions |
| Did unlawful nepotism occur? | Evidence of conduct and proceedings under applicable law | Kinship or election results alone do not answer this question |

At selection, parties should explain eligibility rules, competence assessments, and their reasons for choosing candidates. During elections, scrutiny turns to voter freedom, competition, and the use of state resources. Once officials take office, assessment concerns budgets, procurement, appointments, and service outcomes.

Suppose an official's relative wins a procurement contract. The relationship raises a conflict-of-interest question, but investigation must continue: who set the criteria, who assessed the bids, and did the official influence the decision? Process records provide a firmer basis for criticism than assumptions drawn from a surname.

## Protecting Political Rights and the Public Interest

Preventing nepotism requires **rules that apply equally to everyone**. Transparent recruitment, conflict disclosures, recusal from decisions involving relatives, and scrutiny of service outcomes can reduce opportunities for favoritism.

Family ties are sometimes said to support coordination or policy continuity. Those benefits still need evidence from performance and cannot justify exceptions to the rules. Criticism of political dynasties is stronger when it identifies unfair processes or abuses of authority while respecting every citizen's right to political participation.

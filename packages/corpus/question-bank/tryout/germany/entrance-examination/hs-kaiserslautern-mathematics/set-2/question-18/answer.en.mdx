# Given

**Two perpendicular roads** cross at village <InlineMath math="D" />. A hiker stands at a hut <InlineMath math="H" /> on the meadow <InlineMath math="3\text{ km}" /> from the first road and <InlineMath math="4\text{ km}" /> from the second road. She walks at <InlineMath math="3\text{ km/h}" /> on the meadow and <InlineMath math="5\text{ km/h}" /> on either road.

# Asked

How many minutes does the fastest route from the hut to the village take?

# Solution

> Just like light bending into a faster medium, **the fastest angle onto any road has <InlineMath math="\sin\alpha = 0.6" />**, meaning a clean <InlineMath math="3" />-<InlineMath math="4" />-<InlineMath math="5" /> right triangle tells you immediately where to hit the nearer road.

## One rule for both roads

On a beach you would rather walk a stretch on the firm wet sand than cut straight through the soft sand, even if the path gets a little longer. **The trick is to leave the meadow at the right point**. Take a road at distance <InlineMath math="d" /> from the hut. Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the distance along the road from the hut's nearest point to where the hiker joins it, and let the village lie <InlineMath math="a" /> from that nearest point. The time in hours is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{d^2+x^2}}{3}+\frac{a-x}{5}\\
T'(x)&=\frac{x}{3\sqrt{d^2+x^2}}-\frac15=0\\
5x&=3\sqrt{d^2+x^2}\\
16x^2&=9d^2,\qquad x=0.75\,d
\end{aligned}" />

Both roads obey this rule, each with its own <InlineMath math="d" /> and <InlineMath math="a" />.

## The far road leads straight to the village

For the road <InlineMath math="4\text{ km}" /> away, <InlineMath math="d=4" /> and the village lies <InlineMath math="a=3\text{ km}" /> along it. **The best joining point is <InlineMath math="x=3" />, the village itself**. Up to there <InlineMath math="T'<0" />, so every step towards the village still pays off, and this road does not help at all. What remains is the straight walk across the meadow:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{3^2+4^2}&=5\text{ km}\\
T&=\frac53\text{ h}=100\text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

## The near road saves time

For the road <InlineMath math="3\text{ km}" /> away, <InlineMath math="d=3" /> and <InlineMath math="a=4" />. **Now the joining point <InlineMath math="x=2.25" /> lies before the village, so the road really helps**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{3^2+2.25^2}&=3.75\text{ km}\\
T&=\frac{3.75}{3}+\frac{4-2.25}{5}\\
&=1.25+0.35=1.6\text{ h}=96\text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

That beats the straight walk by <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes, so the fastest way takes <InlineMath math="96" /> minutes. Walking straight to the near road and then along it would take <InlineMath math="1+0.8=1.8\text{ h}" />, which is <InlineMath math="108" /> minutes.

# Measure the queue in turnstile minutes

Imagine a bathtub with the tap still running while a few plugholes are open. The water already in the tub and the water still flowing in all have to leave through the holes. **Count everything in turnstile minutes, the number of fans one turnstile lets through in one minute**. Call the starting queue <InlineMath math="S" /> and the arrivals per minute <InlineMath math="z" />. On both known days the turnstiles let through exactly the starting queue plus everyone who came meanwhile:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
4\cdot30&=S+30z\\
6\cdot15&=S+15z
\end{aligned}" />

Subtracting gives <InlineMath math="30=15z" />, so <InlineMath math="z=2" /> and <InlineMath math="S=60" />. You never learn how many fans one turnstile handles, and you never need to.

# Three turnstiles barely beat the stream

With <InlineMath math="3" /> turnstiles the queue shrinks by only <InlineMath math="3-2=1" /> turnstile minute per minute. At that pace the <InlineMath math="60" /> would take <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes. **But kick-off comes first, after <InlineMath math="45" /> minutes, so the pace changes before the queue is gone**. At kick-off this much is left:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S_{45}&=60-45\cdot1=15
\end{aligned}" />

Answering <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes overlooks that the stream stops.

# After kick-off the stream dries up

Now nobody joins any more, and all <InlineMath math="3" /> turnstiles work through the rest. **The last <InlineMath math="15" /> turnstile minutes take**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{15}{3}=5\\
T&=45+5=50\text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

The queue is gone <InlineMath math="50" /> minutes after the gates open.

# Where the cloud starts

Think of two hikers walking towards each other: the temperature drops fast as the air rises, the dew point drops slowly, so the gap between them shrinks. **Clouds form where the two lines meet**. Both are linear in the height <InlineMath math="h" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T(h)&=15-0.01\,(h-500)\\
\tau(h)&=7-0.002\,(h-500)\\
T-\tau&=8-0.008\,(h-500)=0\\
h&=500+1000=1500\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

The gap of <InlineMath math="8\,^\circ\text{C}" /> closes by <InlineMath math="0.8\,^\circ\text{C}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" />, so it is gone after <InlineMath math="1000\text{ m}" /> of climbing.

# Inside the cloud the air cools more slowly

When water vapour turns into droplets it releases heat, like a radiator switched on inside the rising air. **From <InlineMath math="1500\text{ m}" /> up to the ridge the air loses only <InlineMath math="0.5\,^\circ\text{C}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T(1500)&=15-10=5\,^\circ\text{C}\\
T_{\text{K}}&=5-0.005\cdot1500=-2.5\,^\circ\text{C}
\end{aligned}" />

Using <InlineMath math="1\,^\circ\text{C}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> all the way up would put the ridge at <InlineMath math="-10\,^\circ\text{C}" /> and bring the air back down at exactly <InlineMath math="15\,^\circ\text{C}" />, with no warm wind at all.

# Down the lee side

The clouds stay on the windward side, so the sinking air is dry again. **It warms by <InlineMath math="1\,^\circ\text{C}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> over the full drop of <InlineMath math="2500\text{ m}" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T_{\text{L}}&=-2.5+0.01\cdot2500=22.5\,^\circ\text{C}
\end{aligned}" />

The air reaches the lee town <InlineMath math="7.5\,^\circ\text{C}" /> warmer than it left the windward town, which is exactly the warm fall wind called foehn.

# How much earth the old dyke holds

Slice a loaf of bread and every slice has the same face. A dyke is the same: **its volume is the cross-section area times the length, and the area under the parabola is an integral**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{0}&=\int_{-3}^{3}\left(3-\frac{x^2}{3}\right)dx\\
&=\left[3x-\frac{x^3}{9}\right]_{-3}^{3}=2\,(9-3)=12\text{ m}^2
\end{aligned}" />

# The slope limit decides the shape

The new parabola meets the ground where <InlineMath math="6-ax^2=0" />. The bigger <InlineMath math="a" />, the narrower and steeper the dyke, and the slope <InlineMath math="\lvert g'(x)\rvert=2a\lvert x\rvert" /> is largest right at the foot. **So the rule only has to hold at the foot <InlineMath math="x=r" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
r&=\sqrt{\frac{6}{a}},\qquad \lvert g'(r)\rvert=2ar=2\sqrt{6a}\\
2\sqrt{6a}&\le2\ \Rightarrow\ a\le\frac16
\end{aligned}" />

Stretching the old shape to <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" />, with <InlineMath math="a=\frac13" />, gives a foot slope of <InlineMath math="2\sqrt2>2" /> and is not allowed.

# As little earth as the rule allows

A wider foot means more earth, because with <InlineMath math="r^2=\frac6a" /> the area simplifies to <InlineMath math="8r" />, which grows with the half-width <InlineMath math="r" />. **Least earth means the largest allowed <InlineMath math="a" />, which is <InlineMath math="\frac16" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A(a)&=\int_{-r}^{r}\left(6-ax^2\right)dx=12r-\frac{2a}{3}r^3=8r\\
a=\tfrac16&:\ r=6,\quad A=48\text{ m}^2\\
V&=(48-12)\cdot50=1800\text{ m}^3
\end{aligned}" />

This dyke still covers the old one, since <InlineMath math="g(x)-f(x)=3+\frac{x^2}{6}>0" />. The whole new dyke would be <InlineMath math="2400\text{ m}^3" />, but <InlineMath math="600\text{ m}^3" /> of it are already there.

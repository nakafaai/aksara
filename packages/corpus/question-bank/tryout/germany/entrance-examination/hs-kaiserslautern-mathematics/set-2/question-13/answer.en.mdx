# How high the ceiling is at each spot

Walk from an outer wall towards the middle and the sloping ceiling rises above you like a ramp. Let <InlineMath math="u" /> be your distance from the wall. **Every metre you walk, the roof climbs by the same amount, so the heights grow in the ratio of the intercept theorem**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{h-0.5}{u}&=\frac{4.5-0.5}{5}=0.8\\
h(u)&=0.5+0.8u
\end{aligned}" />

The roof starts at <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}" /> on top of the knee wall, not at the floor. Drawing it from the floor corner gives a flatter roof and the wrong zone limits.

# Where the counting rules switch

The rule draws two invisible lines at heights <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" />. **Find where the ceiling crosses each line**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.5+0.8u_1&=1\ \Rightarrow\ u_1=0.625\text{ m}\\
0.5+0.8u_2&=2\ \Rightarrow\ u_2=1.875\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

# Weighing the strips

On each side the first <InlineMath math="0.625\text{ m}" /> count nothing and the next <InlineMath math="1.25\text{ m}" /> count half. The middle counts fully. **Add the widths with their weights and multiply by the length**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
b_{\text{full}}&=10-2\cdot1.875=6.25\text{ m}\\
b_{\text{half}}&=2\cdot(1.875-0.625)=2.5\text{ m}\\
A&=\left(6.25+\tfrac12\cdot2.5\right)\cdot8=60\text{ m}^2
\end{aligned}" />

Of the <InlineMath math="80\text{ m}^2" /> of floor, <InlineMath math="60\text{ m}^2" /> count as living space.

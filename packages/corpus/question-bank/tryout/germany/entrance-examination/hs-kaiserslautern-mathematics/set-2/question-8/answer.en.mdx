# What one metre of cable costs in space

Picture a roll of sticky tape: the outer turns are much longer than the inner ones, so a millimetre of thickness on the outside holds far more tape. **That is why the length follows the area of the ring, <InlineMath math="\pi\left(R^2-r^2\right)" />, and not the radius**. Put <InlineMath math="\pi" /> and everything about the thin cable into one constant <InlineMath math="k" /> and measure the radii in cm. Full, <InlineMath math="R=30" /> and <InlineMath math="L=40" />. After unwinding, <InlineMath math="R=20" /> and <InlineMath math="L=15" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L&=k\left(R^2-r^2\right)\\
40&=k\left(900-r^2\right)\\
15&=k\left(400-r^2\right)
\end{aligned}" />

Subtract the two lines and the unknown core drops out. The <InlineMath math="25\text{ m}" /> you unwound filled exactly the ring between <InlineMath math="20" /> and <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
40-15&=k\left(900-400\right)\\
25&=500k,\qquad k=0.05
\end{aligned}" />

# The core the text never names

To fill an empty drum you have to know where the winding starts. **Put <InlineMath math="k=0.05" /> back into the full state and the core radius appears**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
40&=0.05\left(900-r^2\right)\\
900-r^2&=800\\
r^2&=100,\qquad r=10
\end{aligned}" />

So the core is <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> across. Letting the cable start at the axis would give <InlineMath math="R^2-r^2=1225" /> instead of <InlineMath math="1125" />, more room than the drum has.

# A fatter cable fills the ring sooner

Think of pasta in a jar: thick spaghetti fills it with fewer strands. **A diameter <InlineMath math="1.25" /> times as big makes every metre take <InlineMath math="1.25^2=1.5625" /> times the space, so the constant shrinks by that factor**. From the core at <InlineMath math="r=10" /> to the rim at <InlineMath math="R=35" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
k_2&=\frac{0.05}{1.25^2}=\frac{0.05}{1.5625}=0.032\\
L_2&=0.032\cdot\left(35^2-10^2\right)\\
&=0.032\cdot1125=36\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

So the drum takes at most <InlineMath math="36\text{ m}" /> of the thicker cable. Dividing by <InlineMath math="1.25" /> alone, as if the space grew with the diameter itself, gives <InlineMath math="45\text{ m}" />, and the thin cable would have reached <InlineMath math="56.25\text{ m}" />.

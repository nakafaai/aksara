# Tilting the walls

Open a book standing on a table: the covers can stand upright or lean outwards. Leaning makes the opening wider but the walls lower. **Let <InlineMath math="\theta" /> be the angle between a side wall and the horizontal outside the gutter**. Each wall then rises <InlineMath math="10\sin\theta" /> and reaches <InlineMath math="10\cos\theta" /> outwards, so the top opening is <InlineMath math="10+20\cos\theta" /> wide:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
h&=10\sin\theta,\qquad o=10+20\cos\theta\\
A(\theta)&=\frac{10+o}{2}\cdot h=100\sin\theta\,(1+\cos\theta)
\end{aligned}" />

# Where the area stops growing

At the largest area the derivative is zero. **Replace <InlineMath math="\sin^2\theta" /> by <InlineMath math="1-\cos^2\theta" /> and the derivative becomes a quadratic in <InlineMath math="\cos\theta" /> that factors**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A'(\theta)&=100\left(\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta\right)\\
&=100\left(2\cos^2\theta+\cos\theta-1\right)\\
&=100\,(2\cos\theta-1)(\cos\theta+1)
\end{aligned}" />

The factor <InlineMath math="\cos\theta+1" /> vanishes only at <InlineMath math="\theta=180^\circ" />, where the strip lies flat and holds nothing. So <InlineMath math="\cos\theta=\frac12" /> remains.

# Which angle the question wants

The tilt <InlineMath math="\theta" /> is measured outside the gutter, against the horizontal continuing the bottom. **Inside, the bottom and the wall enclose the angle that completes <InlineMath math="\theta" /> to a straight line**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\cos\theta&=\tfrac12\ \Rightarrow\ \theta=60^\circ\\
A(60^\circ)&=100\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\tfrac32=75\sqrt3\approx129.9\\
180^\circ-60^\circ&=120^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

Upright walls give only <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^2" />, so <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> is a real maximum. Typing that tilt instead of the inside angle is the trap.

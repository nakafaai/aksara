# Given

**A cup of** <InlineMath math="300\text{ ml}" /> tea starts at <InlineMath math="100\,^\circ\text{C}" /> in a <InlineMath math="20\,^\circ\text{C}" /> room, and its temperature excess above room temperature decays exponentially with constant half-life. After <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes the tea is <InlineMath math="60\,^\circ\text{C}" />, and <InlineMath math="50\text{ ml}" /> of <InlineMath math="4\,^\circ\text{C}" /> milk is poured in (volume-weighted average).

# Asked

How many minutes after pouring the tea does the drink first reach <InlineMath math="36\,^\circ\text{C}" />?

# Solution

> Track **how far the drink sits above the <InlineMath math="20\,^\circ\text{C}" /> room**, not the raw thermometer reading: the first <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes halve that gap from <InlineMath math="80" /> to <InlineMath math="40" />, the milk knocks it down to <InlineMath math="32" />, and one more <InlineMath math="10" />-minute half-life halves it to <InlineMath math="16" />.

## It is the gap that halves

Hot tea does not cool towards zero, it cools towards the room. **So track the gap <InlineMath math="\Delta" /> between drink and room, not the temperature itself**. The gap starts at <InlineMath math="80" /> and is <InlineMath math="40" /> after ten minutes:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta(0)&=100-20=80\\
\Delta(10)&=60-20=40=\tfrac12\cdot80
\end{aligned}" />

The gap halves every <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes. The temperature does not halve: <InlineMath math="100" /> became <InlineMath math="60" />, not <InlineMath math="50" />.

## The milk starts a new curve

Pouring cold milk into the cup is like mixing two bowls of water of different warmth: the bigger one counts more. **Weight each temperature by its volume**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T_{\text{M}}&=\frac{300\cdot60+50\cdot4}{350}=\frac{18200}{350}=52\,^\circ\text{C}\\
\Delta&=52-20=32
\end{aligned}" />

A plain average of <InlineMath math="60" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> would give <InlineMath math="32\,^\circ\text{C}" /> and ignore that there is six times as much tea.

## One more half life

From the new gap of <InlineMath math="32" /> the same halving continues. **The target <InlineMath math="36\,^\circ\text{C}" /> is a gap of <InlineMath math="16" />, exactly one half-life later**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
32\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{s/10}&=36-20=16\\
s&=10\text{ min}\\
10+10&=20\text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

Without milk the drink would need about <InlineMath math="13.2" /> more minutes from <InlineMath math="60\,^\circ\text{C}" />, so the milk saves time.

# Answer

The drink first reaches **<InlineMath math="36\,^\circ\text{C}" />** after **<InlineMath math="20" />** minutes.

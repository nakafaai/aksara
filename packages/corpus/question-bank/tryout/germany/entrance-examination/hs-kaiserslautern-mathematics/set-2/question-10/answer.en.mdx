# Given

**A grain silo** consists of a cylinder of radius <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> and height <InlineMath math="8\text{ m}" /> on top of a conical outlet funnel of radius <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> and depth <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" />. Filled grain forms a top cone whose flank rises <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> over a <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> horizontal run, and the silo is filled to <InlineMath math="75\,\%" /> of total capacity.

# Asked

How high above the bottom outlet is the tip of the grain heap in metres?

# Solution

> Every cone inside the silo shares the exact same circle base <InlineMath math="\pi r^2" />, so **comparing volumes turns into comparing plain heights** (with every cone counting as one third of its height).

## What the silo holds

Picture an ice cream cone with a tin can standing on it: the cone is the funnel, the can is the cylinder. **The capacity is both volumes together, and three quarters of it is in the silo**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_1&=\tfrac13\pi\cdot3^2\cdot4=12\pi\\
V_2&=\pi\cdot3^2\cdot8=72\pi\\
0.75\cdot\left(12\pi+72\pi\right)&=63\pi
\end{aligned}" />

## The heap is a cone of its own

Pour sugar into a glass from the middle and it does not lie flat, it piles up into a little mountain. **Here the flank climbs <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> over the <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> from the axis to the wall, so above the level where it meets the wall sits a cone with radius <InlineMath math="3" /> and height <InlineMath math="2" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_3&=\tfrac13\pi\cdot3^2\cdot2=6\pi
\end{aligned}" />

Below that level the grain is a flat layer of unknown height <InlineMath math="h" />, and below the layer the full funnel.

## The flat layer in between

**After the funnel and the heap, the layer gets what is left, <InlineMath math="45\pi" />, spread over the circle <InlineMath math="9\pi" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
12\pi+9\pi h+6\pi&=63\pi\\
9\pi h&=45\pi\\
h&=5
\end{aligned}" />

Treating the surface as flat would put <InlineMath math="51\pi" /> into the cylinder, a layer of <InlineMath math="\approx5.67\text{ m}" /> and a top at <InlineMath math="\approx9.67\text{ m}" />. Leaving the funnel out of the capacity gives <InlineMath math="0.75\cdot72\pi=54\pi" />, a layer of only <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" /> and a tip at <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" />.

Stacking the three shaded parts in the cross-section from the outlet up gives <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" /> for the funnel, <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> for the flat cylinder layer, and <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> for the top heap, reaching <InlineMath math="11\text{ m}" />.
## Counting up from the outlet

The funnel is <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" /> deep, the layer reaches <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> higher, and the heap adds <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" />. **The tip is <InlineMath math="11\text{ m}" /> above the outlet**. The roof is at <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}" />, so the heap fits under it. Measuring from the funnel's rim instead of the outlet would give <InlineMath math="7\text{ m}" />.

# Answer

The tip of the grain heap is <InlineMath math="11\text{ m}" /> above the outlet.

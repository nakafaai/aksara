# Watch from the bottle

On an airport walkway you walk away from your suitcase for half a minute, turn and walk back at the same pace. The belt carries you both, and you are back after another half minute. **The river does the same: seen from the water, the bottle stands still, so paddling back to it takes as long as paddling away, <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t_{\text{away}}&=30\text{ min}\\
t_{\text{back}}&=30\text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

She reaches the bottle at 11:00, one hour after it fell in.

# The bottle measures the current

In that hour the bottle drifted <InlineMath math="3\text{ km}" /> from the bridge, carried only by the water. **So the current flows at**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v_{\text{S}}&=\frac{3\text{ km}}{1\text{ h}}=3\text{ km/h}
\end{aligned}" />

# Against the current to the bridge

Upstream the current eats into her <InlineMath math="9\text{ km/h}" />. **On the bank she moves at only <InlineMath math="6\text{ km/h}" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
9-3&=6\text{ km/h}\\
t&=\frac{3\text{ km}}{6\text{ km/h}}=0.5\text{ h}=30\text{ min}\\
60+30&=90\text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

Using the full <InlineMath math="9\text{ km/h}" /> for the way back would give <InlineMath math="80" /> minutes.

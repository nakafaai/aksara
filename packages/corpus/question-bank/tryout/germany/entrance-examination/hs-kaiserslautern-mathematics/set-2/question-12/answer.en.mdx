# Given

**A clothing shop** offers two non-combinable promotions: Offer A makes the cheapest of three items free, while Offer B gives <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> off the total. Mr Brandt picks two shirts costing <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" /> and <InlineMath math="50\text{ €}" />, plus a third shirt costing <InlineMath math="x" /> euros.

# Asked

What is the width in euros of the price interval for the third shirt where Offer A is at least as good as Offer B?

# Solution

> Offer A's saving bends sharply at <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" /> because **below <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" /> the third shirt is the free one, while above <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" /> the free saving freezes at <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" />**.

## Which shirt is free depends on the third price

Offer A is like a friend who pays for the cheapest item in your bag. Which item that is, nobody knows until you choose the third shirt. **Compare the savings, not the bills**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
E_{\text{A}}&=\text{price of the cheapest shirt}\\
E_{\text{B}}&=0.2\,(95+x)=19+0.2x
\end{aligned}" />

## A cheap third shirt

If <InlineMath math="x\le45" />, the third shirt itself is the free one. **Then A saves only <InlineMath math="x" />, and a very cheap shirt makes A worse than <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> off everything**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x&\ge19+0.2x\\
0.8x&\ge19\\
x&\ge23.75
\end{aligned}" />

So in this case A keeps up from <InlineMath math="23.75\text{ €}" /> to <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" />. Assuming the <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" /> shirt is always the free one hides this lower limit completely.

## An expensive third shirt

If <InlineMath math="x\ge45" />, the <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" /> shirt is free and A saves a fixed <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" />, while B keeps growing with the price. **A stays at least as good only up to**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
45&\ge19+0.2x\\
0.2x&\le26\\
x&\le130
\end{aligned}" />

Both cases join at <InlineMath math="45\text{ €}" /> into one range from <InlineMath math="23.75\text{ €}" /> to <InlineMath math="130\text{ €}" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
130-23.75&=106.25
\end{aligned}" />

The chart highlights the wedge between <InlineMath math="23.75\text{ €}" /> and <InlineMath math="130\text{ €}" /> where the bent **Offer A** line sits above the steady **Offer B** line, giving a width of <InlineMath math="106.25\text{ €}" />.

# Answer

The price range spans <InlineMath math="106.25\text{ €}" />.

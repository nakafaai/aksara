# Every route is a word of letters

Write down the route as a string: E for a step east, N for a step north. From <InlineMath math="S" /> to <InlineMath math="Z" /> that is always six E and four N, only in a different order. **Counting routes between two crossings means choosing where the N steps go, a binomial coefficient**. For the routes through pharmacy <InlineMath math="A" /> you multiply the two halves:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S\to A&:\ \tbinom{4}{2}=6,\qquad A\to Z:\ \tbinom{6}{2}=15\\
6\cdot15&=90
\end{aligned}" />

# The closed street only hurts the routes through A

A route through <InlineMath math="A" /> uses the closed section if it goes from <InlineMath math="A" /> straight east to <InlineMath math="(3\mid2)" />, which is one way only, then along the section, then from <InlineMath math="(4\mid2)" /> to <InlineMath math="Z" />. **Those routes must come off**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
6\cdot1\cdot\tbinom{4}{2}&=6\cdot1\cdot6=36\\
90-36&=54
\end{aligned}" />

# Pharmacy B and the overlap

For <InlineMath math="B(4\mid1)" /> the same counting works. **No route passes both pharmacies, because after <InlineMath math="A" /> at height <InlineMath math="2" /> the courier can never come down to height <InlineMath math="1" /> at <InlineMath math="B" />, and before <InlineMath math="A" /> he would need <InlineMath math="x=4" /> and then <InlineMath math="x=2" />**. So nothing is counted twice. A route through <InlineMath math="B" /> reaches height <InlineMath math="2" /> only at <InlineMath math="x\ge4" />, so it never uses the closed section either:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S\to B&:\ \tbinom{5}{1}=5,\qquad B\to Z:\ \tbinom{5}{2}=10\\
54+5\cdot10&=104
\end{aligned}" />

The courier has <InlineMath math="104" /> different routes. Forgetting the roadworks gives <InlineMath math="140" />.

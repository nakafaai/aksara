# Given

**A runner sprints** steadily at <InlineMath math="5\text{ m/s}" /> behind a bus whose rear door is <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> ahead. At that instant the bus is moving at <InlineMath math="15\text{ m/s}" /> and brakes uniformly at <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m/s}^2" /> to a stop, waits <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" />, and departs.

# Asked

How many seconds before the bus departs does the runner reach the rear door?

# Solution

> Find **exactly where and when the bus comes to rest** first, because once stopped the door stays put while the runner closes the remaining distance.

## When the bus stands still

A rolling ball that slows by the same amount every second stops after a fixed time and then stays put. **The bus loses <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m/s}" /> every second, so it stops after <InlineMath math="10" /> seconds, covering the average speed times the time**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t_{\text{s}}&=\frac{15}{1.5}=10\text{ s}\\
d_{\text{s}}&=\frac{15\cdot10}{2}=75\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

## The equation that rolls backwards

Setting the two positions equal seems natural:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
5t&=100+15t-0.75t^2\\
0&=3t^2-40t-400\\
t&=20\text{ s}
\end{aligned}" />

**But the braking formula holds only until <InlineMath math="t=10\text{ s}" />**. After that it would make the bus drive backwards towards the runner, and that is exactly what <InlineMath math="t=20" /> describes. At <InlineMath math="t=10\text{ s}" /> the runner has covered only <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" /> while the door already waits at <InlineMath math="175\text{ m}" />.

The runner line meets the flat parked-bus line **exactly <InlineMath math="5\text{ s}" /> before the departure mark at <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />**.

## Catching the standing bus

From <InlineMath math="10\text{ s}" /> on the door does not move, so the runner just has to cover the whole <InlineMath math="175\text{ m}" />. **The bus leaves at <InlineMath math="10+30=40\text{ s}" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
5t&=100+75=175\\
t&=35\text{ s}\\
10+30-35&=5\text{ s}
\end{aligned}" />

She reaches the door <InlineMath math="5" /> seconds before the bus leaves. The false <InlineMath math="20\text{ s}" /> would promise her a comfortable <InlineMath math="20" /> seconds.

# Answer

The runner reaches the rear door <InlineMath math="5\text{ s}" /> before the bus departs.

# Given

**A parabolic bridge** arch spans <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> between its feet and rises <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" /> above them. The flat road sits <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> above the feet. A <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" /> tall rectangular truck keeps at least <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> right of the centre line with at least <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}" /> vertical clearance above its roof.

# Asked

What is the maximum possible width of the truck in metres?

# Solution

> Because the arch drops as you move outward, **the outer top corner of the truck is always the tightest point**, so checking that single corner decides the maximum width.

## The arch as a parabola

Lay the picture on graph paper: feet on the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at <InlineMath math="x=\pm10" />, top on the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis at height <InlineMath math="10" />. **The parabola that is <InlineMath math="0" /> at both feet and <InlineMath math="10" /> in the middle** is <InlineMath math="y(x)" />, because <InlineMath math="y(\pm 10) = 0" />.

## How high the arch must be

Measure everything from the feet, like stacking boxes on a shelf. The road adds <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" />, the truck <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" />, the gap <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}" />. **Wherever the roof is, the arch must be at least <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ m}" /> high**. Measuring from the road and forgetting its <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> makes the truck look far too small.

The arch is lowest above the roof edge furthest from the middle, so that edge decides:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
10-\frac{x^2}{10}&\ge7.5\\
x^2&\le25\\
x&\le5
\end{aligned}" />

The diagram shows **the outer top corner of the truck touching the clearance limit** at <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> from the centre line, locking the safe width at <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" />.

## The gap at the centre line

The truck may not hug the centre line. Its inner side stands at <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and its outer side at most at <InlineMath math="x=5" />. **So it can be at most <InlineMath math="5-1=4\text{ m}" /> wide**. Starting the truck at <InlineMath math="x=0" /> would give <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> and break the rule about the gap.

# Answer

The truck can be at most **<InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" />** wide.

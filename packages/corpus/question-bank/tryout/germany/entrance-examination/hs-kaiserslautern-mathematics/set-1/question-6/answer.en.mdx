# Given

**A boat sails** straight from harbour <InlineMath math="A" /> toward buoy <InlineMath math="D" />, where <InlineMath math="AD = 5\text{ km}" />, <InlineMath math="BD = 7\text{ km}" />, and harbour to island <InlineMath math="B" /> is <InlineMath math="8\text{ km}" />. The first time the boat is <InlineMath math="7\text{ km}" /> from <InlineMath math="B" />, it turns straight to <InlineMath math="B" /> at a constant <InlineMath math="15\text{ km/h}" />.

# Asked

How many minutes does the entire trip from the harbour to the island take?

# Solution

> A circle of radius <InlineMath math="7\text{ km}" /> around the island crosses the straight course twice, and **the crossing closer to the harbour is reached first**.

## The angle hidden in the buoy

The text never states the course angle, but the buoy pins it down the way a peg pins a tent. **Harbour, island and buoy form a triangle with sides <InlineMath math="5" />, <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="8\text{ km}" />, so the law of cosines gives the angle at <InlineMath math="A" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\cos\angle DAB&=\frac{5^2+8^2-7^2}{2\cdot5\cdot8}=\frac{40}{80}=\frac12\\
\angle DAB&=60^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

## Two points on the course

Picture a round fence of radius <InlineMath math="7\text{ km}" /> around the island. A straight course crosses such a fence twice. **Let <InlineMath math="x=\overline{AC}" /> and use the law of cosines in triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> with the <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> angle**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
7^2&=8^2+x^2-2\cdot8\cdot x\cdot\cos60^\circ\\
0&=x^2-8x+15=(x-3)(x-5)
\end{aligned}" />

The second point, <InlineMath math="x=5" />, is the buoy itself. The first one, <InlineMath math="x=3" />, comes earlier.

## The first time counts

Walk towards a lamp post and past it: first you get closer, then farther away. **On its way to the buoy the boat is first <InlineMath math="7\text{ km}" /> from the island after <InlineMath math="3\text{ km}" />, then comes closer still, and at the buoy it is <InlineMath math="7\text{ km}" /> away again**. So it turns at <InlineMath math="C" /> with <InlineMath math="\overline{AC}=3\text{ km}" />. Sailing on to the buoy would mean <InlineMath math="5+7=12\text{ km}" /> and <InlineMath math="48" /> minutes, and that is the trap.

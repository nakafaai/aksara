# Given

**A rectangular tank** has inner dimensions <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}" /> long, <InlineMath math="25\text{ cm}" /> wide, and <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}" /> high, filled with water to <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" />. It is tilted about a <InlineMath math="25\text{ cm}" /> bottom edge until the base makes a <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> angle with the horizontal.

# Asked

How many litres of water spill out?

# Solution

> Because gravity keeps the water surface flat, **a <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> tilt traps an exact right-angled isosceles triangle of water** inside the lower corner.

## How much water there is

Before tilting, the water is a box of its own inside the tank. **Its volume is length times width times depth**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{0}&=60\cdot25\cdot30=45\,000\text{ cm}^3=45\text{ l}
\end{aligned}" />

## Where the surface ends at forty five degrees

Tip a full mug and the tea stays level while it pours over the low rim. The same happens here: the surface stays horizontal and passes through the rim of the lowered wall. **At <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> that surface drops <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> for every centimetre along the floor, so it meets the floor <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}" /> from the corner, before the far wall at <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}" />**. The water is therefore a triangle, not a trapezoid. Treating it as a trapezoid would need a water height of <InlineMath math="40-60=-20\text{ cm}" /> at the far wall, which cannot be.

The shaded right triangle in the lower corner shows **the exact water remaining**, so subtracting it from the starting volume immediately yields the <InlineMath math="25\text{ l}" /> spilled.

## What stays and what spills

The triangle has two legs of <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}" />, and the tank is <InlineMath math="25\text{ cm}" /> deep behind it. **The rest of the water has run out**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A&=\tfrac12\cdot40\cdot40=800\text{ cm}^2\\
V&=800\cdot25=20\,000\text{ cm}^3=20\text{ l}\\
45-20&=25\text{ l}
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="25" /> litres have spilled.

# Answer

A total of <InlineMath math="25\text{ l}" /> of water spills out.

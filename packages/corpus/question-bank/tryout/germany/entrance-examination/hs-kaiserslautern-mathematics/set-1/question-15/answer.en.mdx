# Given

**A shed with** a <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> by <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> square cross-section stands against a vertical wall. An <InlineMath math="8.75\text{ m}" /> ladder rests on the ground, touches the shed top outer edge, and leans on the wall, with its foot at most <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> in front of the shed.

# Asked

At what height in metres does the top of the ladder meet the wall?

# Solution

> Because the shed cross-section is square, **similar triangles link the floor overrun and the wall height symmetrically**, leaving two mirror positions where only one fits the foot constraint.

## Two triangles with the same slope

The ladder cuts off two small triangles, one above the shed and one in front of it. Like two slides with the same steepness, they have the same shape. **Call <InlineMath math="x" /> the height of the ladder's top above the shed and <InlineMath math="y" /> the distance from its foot to the shed**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{3}&=\frac{3}{y}\\
xy&=9
\end{aligned}" />

## Pythagoras for the whole ladder

The whole ladder is the long side of a right triangle with legs <InlineMath math="x+3" /> and <InlineMath math="y+3" />. **The trick is to use the sum <InlineMath math="s=x+y" />, because <InlineMath math="s^2 - 18" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x+3)^2+(y+3)^2&=8.75^2\\
x^2+y^2+6\,(x+y)+18&=76.5625\\
s^2-18+6s+18&=76.5625\\
s^2+6s-76.5625&=0\\
s&=\frac{-6+18.5}{2}=6.25
\end{aligned}" />

The other root, <InlineMath math="-12.25" />, is negative and no length.

## Sum and product give the two lengths

Two numbers with sum <InlineMath math="6.25" /> and product <InlineMath math="9" /> are the roots of one quadratic. **That makes <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> the numbers <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="2.25" />, in some order**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
z^2-6.25z+9&=0\\
z&=\frac{6.25\pm1.75}{2}\\
z&=4\ \text{or}\ z=2.25
\end{aligned}" />

## Which way round

The foot may stand at most <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> in front of the shed. **So <InlineMath math="y=2.25" /> and <InlineMath math="x=4" />, and the top leans at <InlineMath math="4+3=7\text{ m}" />**. The swapped choice puts the foot <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" /> out and the top at only <InlineMath math="5.25\text{ m}" />.

# Answer

The ladder touches the wall at a height of **<InlineMath math="7\text{ m}" />**.

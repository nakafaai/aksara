# Given

Over <InlineMath math="0\le t\le6" /> hours, **rainwater enters** a basin at rate <InlineMath math="z(t)=18t-3t^2" /> cubic metres per hour while a pump drains a steady <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}^3" /> per hour. The basin must never hold less than <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}^3" />, and starts with the smallest volume that satisfies this.

# Asked

What is the maximum amount of water in the basin during the first six hours in cubic metres?

# Solution

> Water drops whenever pumping beats inflow and rises whenever inflow beats pumping, so **the lowest trough pins the starting volume and the rise to the peak gives the maximum**.

## In minus out

Think of a bathtub with the tap on and the plug pulled. The water rises only while more flows in than drains out. **The net rate is <InlineMath math="-3(t-1)(t-5)" />**: negative before <InlineMath math="t=1" />, positive between <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />, negative again after <InlineMath math="5" />.

## Adding up the change

The integral collects the net flow hour by hour, like counting coins dropped into and taken from a jar:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V(t)-V(0)&=\int_0^t\left(18s-3s^2-15\right)ds\\
&=9t^2-t^3-15t
\end{aligned}" />

At <InlineMath math="t=1" /> this is <InlineMath math="-7" />, at <InlineMath math="t=5" /> it is <InlineMath math="+25" />, at <InlineMath math="t=6" /> it is <InlineMath math="+18" />. **The lowest point is at <InlineMath math="t=1" />, the highest at <InlineMath math="t=5" />**. Stopping at <InlineMath math="t=3" />, where the inflow itself peaks, misses that water keeps gathering as long as inflow beats the pump.

The volume curve touches **the <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}^3" /> safety floor at hour one and climbs to its <InlineMath math="36\text{ m}^3" /> peak at hour five**.

## The smallest safe start

The basin must never drop below <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}^3" />, and it drops by <InlineMath math="7\text{ m}^3" /> in the first hour, so it starts with <InlineMath math="11\text{ m}^3" />. **At <InlineMath math="t=5" /> it holds <InlineMath math="11+25=36\text{ m}^3" />**. Starting with only <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}^3" /> would let the pump suck air in the first hour.

# Answer

The basin holds at most **<InlineMath math="36\text{ m}^3" />** of water during the six hours.

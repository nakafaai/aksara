# Given

**Tariff A charges** a <InlineMath math="15\text{ €}" /> base fee plus <InlineMath math="0.25\text{ €}" /> per kWh. Tariff B has no base fee, charges <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ €}" /> per kWh for the first <InlineMath math="100\text{ kWh}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.35\text{ €}" /> per kWh beyond that, with a <InlineMath math="18\text{ €}" /> monthly minimum.

# Asked

How many kWh wide is the monthly usage range where Tariff B is cheaper than Tariff A?

# Solution

> A piecewise tariff bends at each threshold, so **Tariff A crosses Tariff B once on the flat minimum floor and once on the steeper upper slope**.

## Tariff B has three pieces

A phone plan with a minimum fee works like this: use a little and you still pay the floor. Tariff B adds a second rule on top, the expensive kilowatt hours after the first <InlineMath math="100" />. **So B is <InlineMath math="18\text{ €}" /> flat up to <InlineMath math="90\text{ kWh}" />, then <InlineMath math="0.20x" /> up to <InlineMath math="100\text{ kWh}" />, and after that <InlineMath math="20+0.35\,(x-100)" />**.

## The lower limit

At very small use B still charges <InlineMath math="18\text{ €}" /> while A charges only a little more than <InlineMath math="15\text{ €}" />. **B wins once A climbs past the minimum**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
18&<15+0.25x\\
x&>12
\end{aligned}" />

Between <InlineMath math="90" /> and <InlineMath math="100\text{ kWh}" /> B costs at most <InlineMath math="20\text{ €}" /> while A costs at least <InlineMath math="37.5\text{ €}" />, so B stays cheaper there.

## The upper limit

Above <InlineMath math="100\text{ kWh}" /> each extra unit costs more with B than with A, so A catches up. **Only the units after the first <InlineMath math="100" /> cost <InlineMath math="0.35\text{ €}" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
20+0.35\,(x-100)&<15+0.25x\\
0.10x&<30\\
x&<300
\end{aligned}" />

The value <InlineMath math="300" /> lies in this third piece, so it counts. Charging <InlineMath math="0.35\text{ €}" /> for every unit would wrongly give <InlineMath math="150\text{ kWh}" />.

Tariff B stays **below Tariff A strictly between the two crossover points**, spanning a width of exactly <InlineMath math="288\text{ kWh}" />.

## The width of the range

B is cheaper from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="300\text{ kWh}" />. **The limits lie <InlineMath math="300-12=288\text{ kWh}" /> apart**.

# Answer

The two crossover boundaries are <InlineMath math="288\text{ kWh}" /> apart.

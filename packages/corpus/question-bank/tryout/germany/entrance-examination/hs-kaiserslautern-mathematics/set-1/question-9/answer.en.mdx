# Given

**Mia shoots independent** free throws with hit probability <InlineMath math="0.8" /> each. She gets at most four shots and wins immediately, stopping play, as soon as she makes two shots in a row.

# Asked

What is the probability that Mia wins, expressed as a decimal?

# Solution

> Stopping the instant two consecutive shots go in makes **every winning sequence completely disjoint**, so we simply multiply along each path and add them up.

## Listing the ways to win

Think of a path of stepping stones: you stop the moment you land on two hits in a row. Write <InlineMath math="H" /> for a hit and <InlineMath math="M" /> for a miss. **The game ends at the first <InlineMath math="HH" />, so each win is a sequence that has no <InlineMath math="HH" /> before its last two throws**. With at most four throws there are exactly four such sequences: <InlineMath math="HH" />, <InlineMath math="MHH" />, <InlineMath math="HMHH" /> and <InlineMath math="MMHH" />.

A sequence like <InlineMath math="HHMH" /> is not a fifth case. Mia already stopped after <InlineMath math="HH" />, so it is the same win as the first one. Counting it again would add probability twice.

## Multiplying along each path

Independent throws multiply, like choosing from separate menus. **A miss has probability <InlineMath math="0.2" />, a hit <InlineMath math="0.8" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P&=0.8^2+0.2\cdot0.8^2+0.8\cdot0.2\cdot0.8^2+0.2^2\cdot0.8^2\\
&=0.64+0.128+0.1024+0.0256\\
&=0.896
\end{aligned}" />

Stopping after three throws would give only <InlineMath math="0.64+0.128=0.768" />. **Mia wins with probability <InlineMath math="0.896" />**.

# Answer

Mia wins with probability <InlineMath math="0.896" />.

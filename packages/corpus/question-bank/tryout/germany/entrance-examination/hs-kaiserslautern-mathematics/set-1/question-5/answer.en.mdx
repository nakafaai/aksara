# Given

**Two candles are** lit together and burn steadily: the first burns out in <InlineMath math="8" /> hours and the second in <InlineMath math="6" /> hours. Their combined starting length is <InlineMath math="45\text{ cm}" />, after <InlineMath math="3" /> hours their lengths differ by <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" />, and they reach the same length once while both are burning.

# Asked

How many minutes after being lit are both candles the same length?

# Solution

> Two burning candles can only cross in height if **the faster-burning candle starts taller**, otherwise the gap only widens until it burns out.

## Each candle shrinks at its own pace

Think of a candle as a pencil that a sharpener eats from the top at a steady speed. If the whole candle lasts <InlineMath math="8" /> hours, each hour takes away <InlineMath math="\frac18" /> of its length. **After <InlineMath math="3" /> hours the first candle keeps <InlineMath math="\frac58" /> of its length <InlineMath math="L_1" /> and the second keeps <InlineMath math="\frac36=\frac12" /> of <InlineMath math="L_2" />**. With the total length that gives two equations:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_1+L_2&=45\\
\left|\tfrac58L_1-\tfrac12L_2\right|&=9
\end{aligned}" />

## A gap without a direction

If someone says two friends' heights differ by <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" />, you still do not know who is taller. **The absolute value hides the same choice, so try both signs**. Put <InlineMath math="L_2=45-L_1" /> into each case:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac58L_1-\tfrac12\left(45-L_1\right)&=\pm9\\
\tfrac98L_1&=22.5\pm9\\
L_1&=28\ \text{or}\ L_1=12
\end{aligned}" />

So the pair is <InlineMath math="L_1=28\text{ cm}" /> / <InlineMath math="L_2=17\text{ cm}" /> or <InlineMath math="L_1=12\text{ cm}" /> / <InlineMath math="L_2=33\text{ cm}" />. Both pairs pass every equation so far.

## Only one pair ever meets

A car only catches up with another one if it is faster. **The longer candle must burn down faster than the shorter one, and the meeting must come before the second candle is gone after <InlineMath math="6" /> hours**.

In the first pair the first candle is longer by <InlineMath math="11\text{ cm}" /> and loses <InlineMath math="3.5\text{ cm}" /> per hour against <InlineMath math="2.83\text{ cm}" />. It closes the gap by only <InlineMath math="\frac23\text{ cm}" /> per hour and would need <InlineMath math="16.5" /> hours, long after both candles have burned out. Reading <InlineMath math="990" /> minutes off this case is the trap.

In the second pair the second candle is longer by <InlineMath math="21\text{ cm}" /> and loses <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ cm}" /> per hour, the first only <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ cm}" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
12-1.5t&=33-5.5t\\
4t&=21\\
t&=5.25\text{ h}=315\text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

That is before the second candle goes out after <InlineMath math="6" /> hours. **The two candles are equally long <InlineMath math="315" /> minutes after lighting**.

The two straight candle-height lines **intersect once at <InlineMath math="315" /> minutes**, where the taller faster-burning candle is finally caught by the slower one.

# Answer

The two candles reach equal length after <InlineMath math="315\text{ min}" />.

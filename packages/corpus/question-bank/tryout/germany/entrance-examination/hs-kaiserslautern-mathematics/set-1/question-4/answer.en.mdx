# Given

**Stacked cubes have** edge lengths in geometric progression from bottom to top, and every cube must rest completely on the cube below it. The second cube has edge <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ m}" />, and the first and third cubes together are <InlineMath math="2.6\text{ m}" /> tall.

# Asked

What is the fewest number of cubes needed for the tower to exceed <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" />?

# Solution

> The no-overhang rule means **each upper cube must shrink rather than grow**, which immediately eliminates the ratio greater than one.

## Two clues one ratio

Think of a set of nesting boxes where each box is the same fraction of the one below. That fixed fraction is the ratio <InlineMath math="q" /> of a geometric sequence. **The second cube gives <InlineMath math="a_1" />, and the first plus the third cube give the equation for <InlineMath math="q" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a_1q&=1.2,\qquad a_1+a_1q^2=2.6\\
\frac{1.2}{q}\left(1+q^2\right)&=2.6\\
6q^2-13q+6&=0\\
q&=\tfrac{3}{2}\ \text{or}\ q=\tfrac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}" />

## The overhang rule picks the ratio

Put a big cube on a small one and it hangs over the edge like a hat that is too wide. The text forbids that, because every cube must rest on the one below with its whole base. **So each cube is smaller than the one under it, and <InlineMath math="q = 2/3" />**. Then <InlineMath math="a_1=1.8\text{ m}" />, and the cubes are <InlineMath math="1.8" />, <InlineMath math="1.2" />, <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ m}" /> and so on. Keeping <InlineMath math="q = 3/2" /> builds a tower that widens upwards and passes <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> after only <InlineMath math="4" /> cubes.

## How tall the tower can get

Adding smaller and smaller cubes is like walking half of the rest of a path again and again: you get closer to an end you never pass. **Even endless cubes stay below <InlineMath math="5.4\text{ m}" />, so <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> is reached only late**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S_n&=\frac{1.8\left(1-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n\right)}{1-\frac{2}{3}}=5.4\left(1-\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^n\right)\\
5.4\left(1-\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^n\right)&>5\\
\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^n&<\tfrac{2}{27}
\end{aligned}" />

Compare the fractions over the same denominator <InlineMath math="3^n" />. For <InlineMath math="n=6" /> you get <InlineMath math="64" /> against <InlineMath math="2\cdot27=54" />, still too big. For <InlineMath math="n=7" /> you get <InlineMath math="128" /> against <InlineMath math="2\cdot81=162" />, small enough. **The tower needs at least <InlineMath math="7" /> cubes**.

# Answer

The tower needs at least <InlineMath math="7" /> cubes.

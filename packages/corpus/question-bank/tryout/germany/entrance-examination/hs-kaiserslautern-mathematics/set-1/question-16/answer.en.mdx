# When one gondola is high enough

Imagine a clock hand of length <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> whose centre sits <InlineMath math="22\text{ m}" /> up. Turned by <InlineMath math="\theta" /> from the bottom, its tip is at <InlineMath math="h=22-20\cos\theta" />. **Above <InlineMath math="32\text{ m}" /> means <InlineMath math="\cos\theta<-\tfrac12" />, so <InlineMath math="120^\circ<\theta<240^\circ" />**. That is a third of the circle, <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes for one gondola. Answering <InlineMath math="4" /> ignores the second gondola.

# Two windows a quarter turn apart

The second gondola is <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> ahead, so it is high when <InlineMath math="\theta+90^\circ" /> lies between <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="240^\circ" />, that is for <InlineMath math="30^\circ<\theta<150^\circ" />. **Both are high only while <InlineMath math="120^\circ<\theta<150^\circ" />**, a slice of <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />. If the second gondola trails instead, the slice is <InlineMath math="210^\circ<\theta<240^\circ" />, again <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />.

# From degrees to minutes

A full turn of <InlineMath math="360^\circ" /> takes <InlineMath math="12" /> minutes, so <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> takes <InlineMath math="\frac{30}{360}\cdot12" /> minutes. **Both gondolas are above <InlineMath math="32\text{ m}" /> for <InlineMath math="1" /> minute per turn**.

# Four minutes are two kilometres

In a race where one runner starts a little later, you could just as well push that runner's start line back. **At <InlineMath math="30\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" /> an engine covers <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ km}" /> a minute, so B's <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes of delay act like <InlineMath math="2\text{ km}" /> of extra distance**. Inside the city <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> are never negative, so A arrives first whenever this inequality holds.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
|x|+|y|&<|x-6|+|y-4|+2\\
x+y&<|x-6|+|y-4|+2
\end{aligned}" />

# Four cases around B

The absolute-value bars only switch signs on the lines <InlineMath math="x=6" /> and <InlineMath math="y=4" /> through B. **Cut the city into the four rectangles around B and drop the bars in each one**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x\le6,\ y\le4&:\quad x+y<12-x-y\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x+y<6\\
x\le6,\ y\ge4&:\quad x+y<4-x+y\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x<2\\
x\ge6,\ y\le4&:\quad x+y<x-y\ \Longleftrightarrow\ y<0\\
x\ge6,\ y\ge4&:\quad x+y<x+y-8
\end{aligned}" />

The last two conditions never hold inside the city, so B wins everywhere east of <InlineMath math="x=6" />. North of <InlineMath math="y=4" /> every extra step north costs both engines the same, so only the east-west offset decides and the border turns straight up at <InlineMath math="x=2" />.

# The size of A's district

**Add the trapezoid with parallel sides <InlineMath math="6" /> and <InlineMath math="2" /> and height <InlineMath math="4" /> to the <InlineMath math="2" /> by <InlineMath math="4" /> northern strip**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac{6+2}{2}\cdot4+2\cdot4&=16+8\\
&=24
\end{aligned}" />

The professional crew arrives first on **<InlineMath math="24\text{ km}^2" />** of the <InlineMath math="64\text{ km}^2" />. Stopping at <InlineMath math="y=4" /> and missing the northern strip leaves only <InlineMath math="16\text{ km}^2" />.

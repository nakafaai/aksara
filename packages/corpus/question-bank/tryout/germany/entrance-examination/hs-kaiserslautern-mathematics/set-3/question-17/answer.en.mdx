# Given

**A radioactive mixture** contains substance A (half-life <InlineMath math="3\text{ h}" />) and substance B (half-life <InlineMath math="6\text{ h}" />). Total mass is <InlineMath math="176\text{ mg}" /> at <InlineMath math="t=0" /> and <InlineMath math="56\text{ mg}" /> at <InlineMath math="t=6\text{ h}" />. The sample is usable only once B makes up at least <InlineMath math="70\,\%" /> of the remaining mass.

# Asked

After how many hours is the sample usable while substance B exceeds substance A by exactly <InlineMath math="4\text{ mg}" />?

# Solution

> Because substance A halves **twice as fast as substance B**, writing <InlineMath math="q = 2^{-t/6}" /> makes substance A decay like <InlineMath math="q^2" /> while B decays like <InlineMath math="q" />, turning the <InlineMath math="4\text{ mg}" /> lead into one clean quadratic.

## Separating the two initial masses

After <InlineMath math="6" /> hours, substance A has halved twice (factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" />) while substance B has halved only once (factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />). **That contrast between the two decay speeds lets you solve for both initial masses <InlineMath math="a_0" /> and <InlineMath math="b_0" /> directly from the two total-mass readings**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a_0+b_0=176,\qquad \tfrac14 a_0+\tfrac12 b_0=56\ \Longrightarrow\ a_0+2b_0=224\\
b_0=48\text{ mg},\qquad a_0=128\text{ mg}
\end{aligned}" />

## Turning the decay difference into a quadratic

Set <InlineMath math="u=2^{-t/6}" />. Because A decays twice as fast as B, its factor is simply <InlineMath math="u^2" />. **Requiring <InlineMath math="B(t)-A(t)=4\text{ mg}" /> produces a quadratic with two clean power-of-two roots**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
u=2^{-t/6}\ \Longrightarrow\ A(t)=128u^2,\quad B(t)=48u\\
48u-128u^2=4\ \Longleftrightarrow\ 32u^2-12u+1=(4u-1)(8u-1)=0\\
u_1=\tfrac14\ (t_1=12\text{ h}),\qquad u_2=\tfrac18\ (t_2=18\text{ h})
\end{aligned}" />

## Filtering with the purity requirement

**Both times give a <InlineMath math="4\text{ mg}" /> lead, so picking the first hit <InlineMath math="12\text{ h}" /> is the hidden trap**. Check the share of B in the remaining total mass at each root:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t_1=12\text{ h}:\quad A=8,\ B=12\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{B}{A+B}=\frac{12}{20}=60\,\%<70\,\%\\
t_2=18\text{ h}:\quad A=2,\ B=6\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{B}{A+B}=\frac{6}{8}=75\,\%\ge70\,\%
\end{aligned}" />

This occurs after <InlineMath math="18\text{ h}" />.

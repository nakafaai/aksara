# Given

**A balanced baby mobile** weighs <InlineMath math="200\text{ g}" /> in total. The uniform top rod (<InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="10\text{ g}" />) hangs <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" /> from its left end, with moon <InlineMath math="C" /> on the right and the lower rod on the left. The uniform lower rod (<InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="12\text{ g}" />) hangs <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> from its left end, with star <InlineMath math="A" /> on the left and cloud <InlineMath math="B" /> on the right.

# Asked

What is the mass of star A in grams?

# Solution

> Each wooden rod pulls down **right from its own midpoint**, so balancing the top seesaw first gives the exact weight of the lower assembly, and then balancing the bottom seesaw finds star <InlineMath math="A" /> immediately.

## A seesaw with a heavy plank

On a seesaw a light child sitting far out can balance a heavy child sitting close in. **Here the planks weigh something too, and a uniform rod acts as if all its weight sat at its middle**. The lower rod's middle is <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" /> from its left end, so <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" /> right of its thread. The upper rod's middle is <InlineMath math="25\text{ cm}" /> from its left end, <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> right of its thread.

## Three balances and three equations

**Each rod gives one equation, turning effect on the left equal to turning effect on the right, and the total mass gives the third**. You may write masses in grams instead of weights, because the factor <InlineMath math="g" /> cancels on both sides.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
10\cdot A&=20\cdot B+5\cdot12\\
15\cdot(A+B+12)&=35\cdot C+10\cdot10\\
A+B+C+12+10&=200
\end{aligned}" />

## Solving from the top down

Write <InlineMath math="s=A+B" />. **The upper rod and the total mass only contain <InlineMath math="s" /> and <InlineMath math="C" />, so solve those two first**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
15s+180&=35C+100\\
s+C&=178\\
15s+80&=35\,(178-s)\\
50s&=6150,\qquad s=123,\qquad C=55
\end{aligned}" />

The lower rod then splits <InlineMath math="s=123" />. Its equation says <InlineMath math="A=2B+6" />, so <InlineMath math="3B+6=123" />, <InlineMath math="B=39" /> and <InlineMath math="A=84" />.

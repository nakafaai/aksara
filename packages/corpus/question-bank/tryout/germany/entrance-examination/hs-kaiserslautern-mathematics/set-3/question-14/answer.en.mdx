# Given

**Two wind turbines** follow <InlineMath math="P \propto d^2 v^3" /> below their rated cap <InlineMath math="P_{\max}" />. Turbine A has diameter <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}" />, cap <InlineMath math="1152\text{ kW}" />, and outputs <InlineMath math="144\text{ kW}" /> at <InlineMath math="4\text{ m/s}" />. Turbine B has diameter <InlineMath math="40\text{ m}" /> and cap <InlineMath math="500\text{ kW}" />. At exactly two wind speeds <InlineMath math="v_1 < v_2" />, B beats A by <InlineMath math="14\text{ kW}" />, and wind blows <InlineMath math="3\text{ h}" /> at <InlineMath math="v_1" /> then <InlineMath math="5\text{ h}" /> at <InlineMath math="v_2" />.

# Asked

How much electrical energy do both turbines deliver together over the <InlineMath math="8" /> hours, in kWh?

# Solution

> Turbine B has wider blades so it pulls ahead in light wind, **then hits its <InlineMath math="500\text{ kW}" /> ceiling early so turbine A catches up from below**, creating one <InlineMath math="14\text{ kW}" /> gap on the climb and a second one on the flat cap.

## Calibrating both curves and their capping speeds

Below its ceiling, a wider rotor catches more wind proportional to <InlineMath math="d^2" />, so turbine B climbs faster at first until its smaller generator flattens out. **Find <InlineMath math="k_A=c d_A^2" /> from the reference point, scale by <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac{40}{30}\right)^2=\tfrac{16}{9}" /> to get <InlineMath math="k_B" />, and solve for both rated wind speeds**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
k_A=\frac{144}{4^3}=\frac94,\qquad \frac94 v_A^3=1152\ \Longrightarrow\ v_A^3=512\ \Longrightarrow\ v_A=8\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\\
k_B=\frac94\cdot\left(\frac{40}{30}\right)^2=4,\qquad 4v_B^3=500\ \Longrightarrow\ v_B^3=125\ \Longrightarrow\ v_B=5\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}
\end{aligned}" />

## Finding both speeds with the same power lead

**Without noticing the cap at <InlineMath math="5\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}" />, the uncapped difference <InlineMath math="\tfrac74 v^3" /> is strictly rising and could only hit <InlineMath math="14\text{ kW}" /> once**. Once B levels off at <InlineMath math="500\text{ kW}" /> while A keeps climbing, the gap shrinks back down through <InlineMath math="14\text{ kW}" /> a second time before A overtakes B.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v\le5:\quad \left(4-\tfrac94\right)v_1^3=\tfrac74 v_1^3=14\ \Longrightarrow\ v_1^3=8\ \Longrightarrow\ v_1=2\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\\
5\le v\le8:\quad 500-\tfrac94 v_2^3=14\ \Longrightarrow\ v_2^3=\frac{486\cdot4}{9}=216\ \Longrightarrow\ v_2=6\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="v_2 = 6" />, turbine A is still below its own <InlineMath math="8\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}" /> cap, and for <InlineMath math="v\ge8" /> turbine A sits at <InlineMath math="1152\text{ kW}>500\text{ kW}" />, confirming these are the only two solutions.

## Summing both turbines over the eight hours

**Add both turbines' outputs at <InlineMath math="v_1=2" /> and <InlineMath math="v_2=6" /> and weight by <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> hours**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v_1=2:\quad P_A=18\text{ kW},\ P_B=32\text{ kW}\ \Longrightarrow\ P_A+P_B=50\text{ kW}\\
v_2=6:\quad P_A=486\text{ kW},\ P_B=500\text{ kW}\ \Longrightarrow\ P_A+P_B=986\text{ kW}\\
E=3\cdot50+5\cdot986=150+4930=5080\text{ kWh}
\end{aligned}" />

**Together both turbines deliver <InlineMath math="5080\text{ kWh}" />**.

# Answer

Both turbines together deliver **<InlineMath math="5080\text{ kWh}" />** of electrical energy.

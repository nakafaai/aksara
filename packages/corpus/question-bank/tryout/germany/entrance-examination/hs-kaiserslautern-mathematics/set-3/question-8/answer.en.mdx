# Given

**A convex quadrilateral** <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> has <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}=30\text{ m}" />, <InlineMath math="\overline{AD}=14\text{ m}" />, <InlineMath math="\overline{BC}=30\text{ m}" />, <InlineMath math="\cos(\angle BAD)=-0.6" />, <InlineMath math="\cos(\angle BDC)=0.8" />, and an acute interior angle <InlineMath math="\angle BCD < 90^\circ" />.

# Asked

What is the length of diagonal AC in metres?

# Solution

> Find the shared bridge <InlineMath math="BD = 40\text{ m}" /> first, because **the acute-angle condition at <InlineMath math="C" /> picks the right triangle corner uniquely** and opens straight into <InlineMath math="AC" /> via the law of cosines at <InlineMath math="D" />.

## Start with the shared diagonal BD

Split the quadrilateral along <InlineMath math="BD" /> into two triangles that share that bridge. **In <InlineMath math="\triangle ABD" /> you know two sides and the cosine of the angle between them, so the law of cosines gives <InlineMath math="BD" /> at once**. Notice the negative cosine turns the minus sign into a plus.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
BD^2=30^2+14^2-2\cdot30\cdot14\cdot\left(-\tfrac35\right)=900+196+504=1600\ \Longrightarrow\ BD=40\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

## Two candidate places for C

Now move to <InlineMath math="\triangle BCD" /> and write <InlineMath math="x=CD" />. **Because <InlineMath math="BC=30\text{ m}" /> is shorter than <InlineMath math="BD=40\text{ m}" />, a circle of radius <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}" /> around <InlineMath math="B" /> cuts the ray from <InlineMath math="D" /> twice, giving two candidates for <InlineMath math="CD" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
30^2=40^2+x^2-2\cdot40\cdot x\cdot\tfrac45\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x^2-64x+700=0\\
(x-14)(x-50)=0\ \Longrightarrow\ CD=14\text{ m}\quad\text{or}\quad CD=50\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Now the condition <InlineMath math="\angle BCD<90^\circ" /> decides. At <InlineMath math="CD=14\text{ m}" /> we have <InlineMath math="1096 < 1600" />, so the angle at <InlineMath math="C'" /> is obtuse and ruled out. At <InlineMath math="CD=50\text{ m}" /> we have <InlineMath math="2500 = 50^2" /> (a right angle at <InlineMath math="B" />), so <InlineMath math="\angle BCD" /> is acute and **<InlineMath math="CD=50\text{ m}" /> survives**.

In the drawing you can see both intersections on the ray from <InlineMath math="D" /> and why only the farther one keeps <InlineMath math="\angle BCD" /> sharp.

## Combining the two angles at D

Since <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="C" /> lie on opposite sides of <InlineMath math="BD" />, the corner angle is <InlineMath math="\angle ADC" />. **Find <InlineMath math="\cos(\angle ADB)" /> from <InlineMath math="\triangle ABD" />, apply the cosine addition formula, and finish with the law of cosines in <InlineMath math="\triangle ADC" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\cos(\angle ADB)=\frac{14^2+40^2-30^2}{2\cdot14\cdot40}=\frac{896}{1120}=\tfrac45,\qquad \sin(\angle ADB)=\tfrac35\\
\cos(\angle ADC)=\cos(\angle ADB+\angle BDC)=\tfrac45\cdot\tfrac45-\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac35=\tfrac{7}{25}\\
AC^2=14^2+50^2-2\cdot14\cdot50\cdot\tfrac{7}{25}=196+2500-392=2304\ \Longrightarrow\ AC=48\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

The diagonal <InlineMath math="AC" /> is **<InlineMath math="48\text{ m}" />** long. Picking the rejected corner <InlineMath math="C'" /> with <InlineMath math="CD=14\text{ m}" /> would give the trap diagonal <InlineMath math="16.8\text{ m}" />.

# Answer

The diagonal <InlineMath math="AC" /> has a length of <InlineMath math="48\text{ m}" />.

# Every overhaul costs a fee plus one month of revenue

Waiting until maintenance eats up the monthly revenue sounds sensible, yet resetting early saves quadratically growing costs. **First sum the arithmetic maintenance series for a run of <InlineMath math="n" /> months, and remember that <InlineMath math="k" /> overhauls leave only <InlineMath math="17-k" /> operating months split across <InlineMath math="k+1" /> runs**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
W(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(200+100j)=\frac{n}{2}\bigl(400+100\,(n-1)\bigr)=150n+50n^2\\
G=1500\,(17-k)-400k-\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\bigl(150n_i+50n_i^2\bigr)=22\,950-1750k-50\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}n_i^2
\end{aligned}" />

Because <InlineMath math="\sum n_i=17-k" />, each overhaul removes <InlineMath math="1500-150=1350\text{ €}" /> of linear margin plus the <InlineMath math="400\text{ €}" /> fee, or <InlineMath math="1750\text{ €}" /> in total, while shrinking the quadratic term <InlineMath math="50\sum n_i^2" />.

# Balancing the runs minimizes the squares

**For any fixed <InlineMath math="k" />, the sum of squares <InlineMath math="\sum n_i^2" /> is smallest when the run lengths differ by at most <InlineMath math="1" />**. Whenever two runs satisfy <InlineMath math="a-b\ge2" />, moving one month from the longer run to the shorter lowers the sum because <InlineMath math="(a-1)^2+(b+1)^2=a^2+b^2-2(a-b-1)<a^2+b^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
k=0\ (17)&:\quad 22\,950-50\cdot289=8500\\
k=1\ (8,8)&:\quad 22\,950-1750-50\cdot128=14\,800\\
k=2\ (5,5,5)&:\quad 22\,950-3500-50\cdot75=15\,700\\
k=3\ (4,4,3,3)&:\quad 22\,950-5250-50\cdot50=15\,200
\end{aligned}" />

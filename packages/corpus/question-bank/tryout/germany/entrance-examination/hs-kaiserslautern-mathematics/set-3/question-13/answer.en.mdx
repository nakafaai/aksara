# Given

**A garden path** is <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> wide and <InlineMath math="7\text{ m}" /> long. It is paved seamlessly using unlimited <InlineMath math="1\text{ m} \times 2\text{ m}" /> slabs (laid either way) plus at most one <InlineMath math="2\text{ m} \times 2\text{ m}" /> square slab.

# Asked

How many different paving patterns are possible?

# Solution

> Using only <InlineMath math="1 \times 2" /> slabs gives the classic Fibonacci counts, and **placing the one square slab splits the path cleanly into independent left and right strips** whose Fibonacci counts simply multiply.

## Paving from the door outwards

Lay the path slab by slab, starting at the door. **Look at the first metre: either one slab lies across it, or two slabs lie lengthwise side by side and fill the first two metres, so the count <InlineMath math="a_n" /> of patterns without the square follows the Fibonacci recurrence**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a_n&=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2},\qquad a_0=a_1=1\\
a_0,\dots,a_7&:\quad 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 8,\ 13,\ 21
\end{aligned}" />

Without the square slab there are <InlineMath math="a_7=21" /> patterns.

## Where the square can go

**The square fills the full width over two metres, so it cuts the path into a left part and a right part that are paved independently**. Covering metres <InlineMath math="k" /> and <InlineMath math="k+1" /> leaves lengths <InlineMath math="k-1" /> and <InlineMath math="6-k" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=1}^{6}a_{k-1}\,a_{6-k}&=1\cdot8+1\cdot5+2\cdot3+3\cdot2+5\cdot1+8\cdot1\\
&=38
\end{aligned}" />

Summing those six products gives <InlineMath math="38" /> patterns that contain the square slab.

## All patterns together

A pattern either uses no square or exactly one, never both, **so the two counts add to <InlineMath math="21+38=59" /> patterns**. Two lengthwise slabs side by side also cover a <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> block, yet they form a different pattern from the single square slab. Adding only the <InlineMath math="6" /> positions of the square to <InlineMath math="21" /> gives the trap <InlineMath math="27" />.

# Answer

There are **<InlineMath math="59" />** different paving patterns.

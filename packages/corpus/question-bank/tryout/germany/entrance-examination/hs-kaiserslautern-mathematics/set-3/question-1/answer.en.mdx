# Given

**At a university**, subject A has <InlineMath math="400" /> male applicants (<InlineMath math="60\,\%" /> admitted) and <InlineMath math="100" /> female applicants (<InlineMath math="70\,\%" /> admitted). Subject B has <InlineMath math="100" /> male applicants (<InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> admitted) and <InlineMath math="x" /> female applicants (<InlineMath math="25\,\%" /> admitted). Overall, women have a lower admission rate than men.

# Asked

What is the minimum number of women who applied to subject B?

# Solution

> Mixing two rates works like pouring strong and weak juice into one bowl: **even when women win in every single subject, applying heavily to the harder subject B drags their overall average below the men's <InlineMath math="52\,\%" />**.

## Two jugs in one mixture

Pour a jug of strong juice and a jug of weak juice into one bowl. How strong the bowl tastes depends on how much came from each jug. **An overall admission rate works the same way: it mixes the two subject rates, weighted by the number of applicants**. For the men there are <InlineMath math="240" /> admissions in A and <InlineMath math="20" /> in B.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.60\cdot400+0.20\cdot100&=240+20=260\\
\frac{260}{500}&=0.52
\end{aligned}" />

Overall, <InlineMath math="52\,\%" /> of the men get a place.

## How the rate for women depends on x

Call the number of women applying for B <InlineMath math="x" />. Then <InlineMath math="70" /> women are admitted in A and <InlineMath math="0.25x" /> in B, out of <InlineMath math="100+x" /> women. **Their overall rate drops below the men's <InlineMath math="52\,\%" /> once the harder subject B carries enough weight**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{70+0.25x}{100+x}&<0.52\\
70+0.25x&<52+0.52x\\
18&<0.27x\\
x&>\frac{200}{3}\approx66.67
\end{aligned}" />

Inside each subject nothing changes. Only the weight of B grows, pulling the mixed curve below the men's horizontal line.

## Admissions come in whole people

Now the word exactly in the text decides the integer value. **Exactly <InlineMath math="25\,\%" /> of the women in B are admitted, and nobody is admitted in part, so <InlineMath math="x" /> must be a multiple of <InlineMath math="4" />**. That rules out <InlineMath math="67" />, and the next multiple of <InlineMath math="4" /> above <InlineMath math="66.67" /> is <InlineMath math="68" />.

A quick check confirms it. With <InlineMath math="68" /> women in B, <InlineMath math="70+17=87" /> of <InlineMath math="168" /> women are admitted, while <InlineMath math="0.52\cdot168=87.36" />, so the women stay just below the men. With <InlineMath math="64" /> they would have <InlineMath math="86" /> of <InlineMath math="164" />, about <InlineMath math="52.4\,\%" />. **At least <InlineMath math="68" /> women applied for subject B**.

# Answer

At least <InlineMath math="68" /> women applied to subject B.

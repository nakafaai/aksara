# The absolute value splits into two cases

The expression <InlineMath math="2x-3" /> changes sign at <InlineMath math="x=\frac32" />. So each region has **a different inequality without the absolute value**.

# The two cases

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x\ge\tfrac32:&\quad 2x-3\le x+1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\le4,\quad\text{so}\ \tfrac32\le x\le4 \\
x<\tfrac32:&\quad 3-2x\le x+1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\ge\tfrac23,\quad\text{so}\ \tfrac23\le x<\tfrac32
\end{aligned}" />

In the second case you divide by <InlineMath math="-3" />, and **the inequality sign flips**: <InlineMath math="-3x\le-2" /> becomes <InlineMath math="x\ge\frac23" />.

Check <InlineMath math="x=0" />: <InlineMath math="|{-3}|=3>1" />, so the inequality fails. With <InlineMath math="x=2" />: <InlineMath math="1\le3" />, it holds. The solution is <InlineMath math="\frac23\le x\le4" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac32\le x\le4" /> works out **only the first case**.
- <InlineMath math="x\le4" /> keeps the upper bound and forgets the lower one from the second case.
- <InlineMath math="\frac23\le x<\frac32" /> works out only the second case.
- <InlineMath math="-4\le x\le-\frac23" /> flips every sign.
- <InlineMath math="\frac23<x<4" /> excludes the endpoints. There both sides are equal, and <InlineMath math="\le" /> allows that.

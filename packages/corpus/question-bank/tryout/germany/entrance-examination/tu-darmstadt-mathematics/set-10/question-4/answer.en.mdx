# Recognise the shape of the quadrilateral

A radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent. So <InlineMath math="\overline{M_1A}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{M_2B}" /> are both perpendicular to <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" /> and parallel to each other. The quadrilateral <InlineMath math="M_1ABM_2" /> is **a right trapezium** with parallel sides <InlineMath math="9" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> and height <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />.

# The length <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />

Because the circles touch from outside, <InlineMath math="\overline{M_1M_2}=9+4=13" />. Draw a line from <InlineMath math="M_2" /> parallel to <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />. With <InlineMath math="\overline{M_1M_2}" /> it forms **a right triangle with legs <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" /> and <InlineMath math="9-4=5" />**:

<BlockMath math="\overline{AB}=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{144}=12" />

# The area of the trapezium

<BlockMath math="A=\frac{9+4}{2}\cdot12=78\text{ cm}^2" />

The quadrilateral has **an area of <InlineMath math="78\text{ cm}^2" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="156\text{ cm}^2" /> **forgets to halve** in the trapezium formula.
- <InlineMath math="84.5\text{ cm}^2" /> takes the distance between the centres, <InlineMath math="13" />, as the height: <InlineMath math="\frac{13}{2}\cdot13" />.
- <InlineMath math="39\text{ cm}^2" /> halves twice: <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot\frac{13\cdot12}{2}" />.
- <InlineMath math="32.5\text{ cm}^2" /> takes the difference of the radii, <InlineMath math="5" />, as the height: <InlineMath math="\frac{13}{2}\cdot5" />.
- <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^2" /> uses <InlineMath math="5\cdot12" />, only the difference of the radii instead of their sum.

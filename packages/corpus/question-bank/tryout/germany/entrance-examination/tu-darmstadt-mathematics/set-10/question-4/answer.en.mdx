# Given

Two circles with centers <InlineMath math="M_1" /> and <InlineMath math="M_2" /> and radii <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> **touch externally**.

A common external tangent touches the large circle at <InlineMath math="A" /> and the small circle at <InlineMath math="B" />, with both circles on the same side of the line.

# Asked

What is the area (*Flächeninhalt*) of quadrilateral <InlineMath math="M_1 A B M_2" />?

# Solution

Picture two wheels of different sizes resting side by side in contact on a flat floor. The vertical spoke from the floor to the first axle has length <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" />, and the spoke to the second axle has length <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" />. Because both spokes meet the floor at right angles, **quadrilateral <InlineMath math="M_1 A B M_2" /> is a right trapezoid** whose height is the floor segment <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />.

> Radii to tangent points are always perpendicular to the tangent line, so two circles on a common external tangent form a right trapezoid whose height comes from the Pythagorean theorem.

## Computing the tangent length by Pythagoras

Because the two circles touch externally, their center distance <InlineMath math="\overline{M_1 M_2}" /> equals the sum of both radii, <InlineMath math="13\text{ cm}" />.

Slide segment <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" /> straight up until it meets <InlineMath math="M_2" />. That creates a **right triangle** with hypotenuse <InlineMath math="13" /> and vertical leg <InlineMath math="9 - 4 = 5" />:

<BlockMath math="\overline{AB} = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{169 - 25} = \sqrt{144} = 12\text{ cm}" />

You can also view the trapezoid as a <InlineMath math="12 \times 4 = 48" /> bottom rectangle topped by a right triangle of area <InlineMath math="30" />.

## Computing the trapezoid area

Plug the two parallel sides and height into **the trapezoid area formula**:

<BlockMath math="A = \frac{9 + 4}{2} \cdot 12 = 13 \cdot 6 = 78\text{ cm}^2" />

Adding the split pieces gives the same <InlineMath math="48 + 30 = 78\text{ cm}^2" />.

# Answer

The area of quadrilateral <InlineMath math="M_1 A B M_2" /> is **<InlineMath math="78\text{ cm}^2" />**.

The task asks you to write the product as one power of <InlineMath math="x" />. Roots can be written as **powers with fractional exponents**: <InlineMath math="\sqrt[n]{x^m}=x^{\frac mn}" /> for <InlineMath math="x>0" />. Then the usual power rules apply.

<BlockMath math="\sqrt{x^3}=x^{\frac32}\qquad\sqrt[4]{x^2}=x^{\frac24}=x^{\frac12}" />

With equal bases, the exponents are added.

<BlockMath math="x^{\frac32}\cdot x^{\frac12}=x^{\frac32+\frac12}=x^{\frac42}=x^2" />

As a check, substitute <InlineMath math="x=4" />. You get <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4^3}=\sqrt{64}=8" /> and <InlineMath math="\sqrt[4]{4^2}=\sqrt[4]{16}=2" />, together <InlineMath math="8\cdot2=16=4^2" />.

So the expression equals <InlineMath math="x^2" />.

- <InlineMath math="x^6" /> only multiplies the exponents under the roots, <InlineMath math="3\cdot2" />, and drops the roots.
- <InlineMath math="x" /> subtracts the exponents, <InlineMath math="\frac32-\frac12" />. In a product they are added.

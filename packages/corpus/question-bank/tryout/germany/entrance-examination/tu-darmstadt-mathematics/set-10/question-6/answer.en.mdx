# Given

An exponential function (*Exponentialfunktion*) <InlineMath math="f(x) = a \cdot b^x" /> with <InlineMath math="b > 0" /> passes through **two known values**:

<BlockMath math="f(1) = 6 \qquad \text{and} \qquad f(3) = 54" />

# Asked

What is the smallest integer <InlineMath math="x" /> for which <InlineMath math="f(x) > 1000" />?

# Solution

In exponential growth, every single step forward **multiplies the output by a fixed growth factor <InlineMath math="b" />**. Moving from <InlineMath math="x = 1" /> to <InlineMath math="x = 3" /> takes two steps, and the value jumps ninefold from <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="54" />. Multiplying by <InlineMath math="b" /> twice gives <InlineMath math="9" />, so each step **triples the value**.

> Dividing two values of an exponential function cancels the starting factor immediately and leaves the growth factor raised to the step gap.

## Finding the growth factor and starting value

Divide <InlineMath math="f(3)" /> by <InlineMath math="f(1)" /> so **the starting factor <InlineMath math="a" /> cancels cleanly**:

<BlockMath math="\frac{f(3)}{f(1)} = \frac{a \cdot b^3}{a \cdot b} = b^2 = \frac{54}{6} = 9" />

Since <InlineMath math="b > 0" />, we get <InlineMath math="b = 3" />. Plug back into <InlineMath math="f(1) = 6" /> for the starting value:

<BlockMath math="a \cdot 3 = 6 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad a = 2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad f(x) = 2 \cdot 3^x" />

## Finding the first integer above one thousand

The inequality <InlineMath math="2 \cdot 3^x > 1000" /> simplifies to <InlineMath math="3^x > 500" />. Because powers of three climb quickly, **test the two neighboring integers directly**:

<BlockMath math="3^5 = 243 < 500, \qquad 3^6 = 729 > 500" />

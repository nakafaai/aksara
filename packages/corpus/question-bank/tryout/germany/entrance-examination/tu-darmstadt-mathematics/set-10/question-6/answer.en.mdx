# Find the base and the starting value

Divide the two function values. The factor <InlineMath math="a" /> cancels and **<InlineMath math="b^2" /> is left**:

<BlockMath math="\frac{f(3)}{f(1)}=\frac{a\,b^3}{a\,b}=b^2=\frac{54}{6}=9" />

Since <InlineMath math="b>0" />, <InlineMath math="b=3" />, and <InlineMath math="f(1)=3a=6" /> gives <InlineMath math="a=2" />. So <InlineMath math="f(x)=2\cdot3^x" />.

# Check the inequality with whole numbers

<BlockMath math="3^5=243<500,\qquad 3^6=729>500" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="x=5" /> takes **<InlineMath math="a=6" /> instead of <InlineMath math="a=2" />**: <InlineMath math="6\cdot3^5=1458>1000" />. But the starting value is <InlineMath math="f(0)=a" />, not <InlineMath math="f(1)" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=7" /> shifts the exponent by one, for example with <InlineMath math="f(x)=2\cdot3^{x-1}" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=4" /> takes <InlineMath math="\frac{f(3)}{f(1)}=9" /> as the base: with <InlineMath math="b=9" /> and <InlineMath math="a=\frac23" />, <InlineMath math="f(4)=\frac23\cdot6561=4374" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=43" /> works linearly: from <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="54" /> in two steps is <InlineMath math="24" /> per step, and <InlineMath math="6+24(x-1)>1000" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=43" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=3" /> is where the second given value is, <InlineMath math="f(3)=54" />.

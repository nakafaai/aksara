# The height in right triangle <InlineMath math="QFT" />

In triangle <InlineMath math="QFT" /> the angle at <InlineMath math="F" /> is a right angle and the angle at <InlineMath math="Q" /> is <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />. The height is **the side opposite the <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> angle**:

<BlockMath math="h=\overline{QT}\cdot\sin60^\circ=40\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=20\sqrt3" />

# Check with both angles

Then <InlineMath math="\overline{QF}=40\cdot\cos60^\circ=20" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{PF}=60" />. Indeed <InlineMath math="\tan30^\circ=\frac{20\sqrt3}{60}=\frac{\sqrt3}{3}" />. The tower is **<InlineMath math="20\sqrt3\text{ m}" /> tall**, about <InlineMath math="34.6\text{ m}" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40\sqrt3\text{ m}" /> uses <InlineMath math="\tan60^\circ" /> with the whole <InlineMath math="40" />: <InlineMath math="40\cdot\sqrt3" />. But the <InlineMath math="40" /> **isn't the distance from <InlineMath math="Q" /> to the tower**.
- <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> is the distance <InlineMath math="\overline{QF}" />, not the height.
- <InlineMath math="40\text{ m}" /> is the slanted distance <InlineMath math="\overline{QT}" />.
- <InlineMath math="10\sqrt3\text{ m}" /> halves the height a second time.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{40\sqrt3}{3}\text{ m}" /> works out <InlineMath math="40\cdot\tan30^\circ" />, as if <InlineMath math="P" /> were <InlineMath math="40" /> from the tower.

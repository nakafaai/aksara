# Given

From point <InlineMath math="P" /> on flat ground, the top of a vertical tower (*senkrechter Turm*) appears at an **angle of elevation (*Höhenwinkel*) of <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />**.

After walking <InlineMath math="40\text{ m}" /> straight toward the base of the tower to point <InlineMath math="Q" />, the top appears at an **angle of elevation of <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />**.

# Asked

How tall is the tower?

# Solution

You do not need a messy system of two tangent equations. Look at the slanted triangle <InlineMath math="PQT" /> formed by the two sightlines to the top <InlineMath math="T" />: the exterior angle <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> at <InlineMath math="Q" /> equals the sum of <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> at <InlineMath math="P" /> plus the top angle at <InlineMath math="T" />. So the angle at <InlineMath math="T" /> is also <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />, making **triangle <InlineMath math="PQT" /> isosceles** with second sightline equal to <InlineMath math="40\text{ m}" />.

> When the angle of elevation doubles from <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> to <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, the slanted triangle between the two vantage points is isosceles, so the second sightline equals the distance walked.

## Spotting the isosceles triangle between the vantage points

Label the tower base <InlineMath math="F" /> and its top <InlineMath math="T" />. In triangle <InlineMath math="PQT" />:

- The angle at <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />.
- The interior angle at <InlineMath math="Q" /> is the supplement of <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, namely <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.
- The remaining angle at the top <InlineMath math="T" /> is <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />.

Because the angles at <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="T" /> both equal <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />, **their opposite sides have the exact same length**:

<BlockMath math="\overline{QT} = \overline{PQ} = 40\text{ m}" />

Once hypotenuse <InlineMath math="\overline{QT} = 40\text{ m}" /> is locked in, one sine calculation yields the height.

## Computing the height and checking both angles

In right triangle <InlineMath math="QFT" />, the tower height is **the leg opposite <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />**:

<BlockMath math="h = \overline{QT} \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 40 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 20\sqrt{3}\text{ m}" />

Full check: <InlineMath math="\overline{QF} = 20\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{PF} = 60\text{ m}" />, so the ratio of height to <InlineMath math="60" /> matches <InlineMath math="\tan 30^\circ" /> exactly. Numerically the tower stands about <InlineMath math="34.6\text{ m}" /> tall.

# Answer

The height of the tower is **<InlineMath math="20\sqrt{3}\text{ m}" />**.

# Given

A **regular tetrahedron** (*regelmäßiges Tetraeder*) is made of four congruent equilateral triangles with **all edges of length <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />**.

# Asked

What is the distance from one vertex to the opposite triangular face (the height of the tetrahedron)?

# Solution

Set the tetrahedron flat on one face and drop a plumb line straight from apex <InlineMath math="S" /> to the table. Because all three slanted edges have equal length, the line hits **the base centroid <InlineMath math="O" />**, not an edge midpoint. Slicing vertically through right triangle <InlineMath math="OAS" /> yields the height in one Pythagorean step.

> The altitude of a regular tetrahedron lands directly on the base centroid, which divides each base median in a <InlineMath math="2 : 1" /> ratio from the vertex.

## Finding the distance from a base corner to the centroid

In an equilateral base triangle of side <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />, the altitude (which is also the median) measures <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}" />. Since centroid <InlineMath math="O" /> divides each median <InlineMath math="2 : 1" />, **the distance from corner <InlineMath math="A" /> to center <InlineMath math="O" />** is two thirds of that median:

<BlockMath math="\overline{OA} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 3\sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}" />

## Computing the height in the vertical cross section

The vertical slice through apex <InlineMath math="S" />, base corner <InlineMath math="A" />, and centroid <InlineMath math="O" /> forms a **right triangle at <InlineMath math="O" />** whose hypotenuse is edge <InlineMath math="\overline{AS} = 6\text{ cm}" />.

Apply the Pythagorean theorem to right triangle <InlineMath math="OAS" />:

<BlockMath math="h = \sqrt{6^2 - \left(2\sqrt{3}\right)^2} = \sqrt{36 - 12} = \sqrt{24} = 2\sqrt{6}\text{ cm}" />

Sanity check: the height is about <InlineMath math="4.9\text{ cm}" />, slightly shorter than a slanted face altitude of about <InlineMath math="5.2\text{ cm}" />, just as geometry requires.

# Answer

The distance from a vertex to the opposite face is **<InlineMath math="2\sqrt{6}\text{ cm}" />**.

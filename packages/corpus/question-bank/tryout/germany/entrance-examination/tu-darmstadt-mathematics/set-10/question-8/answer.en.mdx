# Where the foot of the height lies

The apex <InlineMath math="S" /> is equally far from all three corners of the base. So the height meets the base at its **centroid <InlineMath math="O" />**, which is also its circumcentre. In the equilateral triangle with side <InlineMath math="6" /> the median is <InlineMath math="3\sqrt3" /> long, and <InlineMath math="O" /> splits it in the ratio <InlineMath math="2:1" />:

<BlockMath math="\overline{OA}=\frac23\cdot3\sqrt3=2\sqrt3" />

The distance from a corner to the opposite face is **<InlineMath math="2\sqrt6\text{ cm}" />**, about <InlineMath math="4.9\text{ cm}" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3\sqrt3\text{ cm}" /> is **the height of one face**, not of the tetrahedron.
- <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}" /> uses the whole median <InlineMath math="3\sqrt3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="2\sqrt3" />: <InlineMath math="\sqrt{36-27}=3" />.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt3\text{ cm}" /> is the distance <InlineMath math="\overline{OA}" /> in the base.
- <InlineMath math="3\sqrt2\text{ cm}" /> uses <InlineMath math="3\sqrt2" /> as the distance <InlineMath math="\overline{OA}" />, like half the diagonal of a square: <InlineMath math="\sqrt{36-18}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{30}\text{ cm}" /> subtracts only <InlineMath math="6" /> instead of <InlineMath math="12" />: <InlineMath math="\sqrt{36-6}" />.

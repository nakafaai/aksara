# What the fold means

Call the rectangle <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> with <InlineMath math="AB=8" /> and <InlineMath math="BC=6" />, and fold it so that <InlineMath math="A" /> lands exactly on <InlineMath math="C" />. Every point of the crease is equally far from <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="C" />, because those two points meet when you fold. So **the crease is the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AC" />**: the line perpendicular to <InlineMath math="AC" /> through its midpoint <InlineMath math="M" />.

# Where the crease meets the sides

Say the crease meets <InlineMath math="AB" /> at <InlineMath math="E" /> with <InlineMath math="AE=x" />. Since <InlineMath math="E" /> is on the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AC" />, **<InlineMath math="E" /> is equally far from <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="C" />**. You get <InlineMath math="EC" /> from the right triangle <InlineMath math="EBC" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AE^2&=EB^2+BC^2 \\
x^2&=(8-x)^2+6^2 \\
0&=64-16x+36 \\
x&=6.25
\end{aligned}" />

# The length of the crease

The diagonal is <InlineMath math="AC=\sqrt{8^2+6^2}=10" />, so <InlineMath math="AM=5" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="AME" /> has its right angle at <InlineMath math="M" />, because **the crease is perpendicular to the diagonal**:

<BlockMath math="ME=\sqrt{AE^2-AM^2}=\sqrt{39.0625-25}=\sqrt{14.0625}=3.75" />

The picture is symmetric about <InlineMath math="M" />, so <InlineMath math="MF=ME" /> and <InlineMath math="EF=2\cdot3.75=7.5\text{ cm}" />.

So the crease is <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ cm}" /> long.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> is the diagonal <InlineMath math="AC" />. **The crease crosses the diagonal at right angles**, it doesn't lie along it.
- <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" /> is half the diagonal, <InlineMath math="AM" />.
- <InlineMath math="6.25\text{ cm}" /> is <InlineMath math="AE" />, the distance from the corner to the end of the crease.
- <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="8\text{ cm}" /> are the width and length of the sheet, the crease you'd get folding parallel to a side. Such a fold doesn't bring opposite corners together.

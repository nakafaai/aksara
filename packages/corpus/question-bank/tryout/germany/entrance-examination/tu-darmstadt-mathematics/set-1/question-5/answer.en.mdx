# Given

A **rectangular sheet of paper** <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> measures <InlineMath math="8\text{ cm}" /> in length and <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> in width. It is folded once so that two opposite corners (<InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="C" />) meet exactly.

# Asked

Find the length of the resulting **crease** (*Faltkante*) in centimeters.

# Solution

Think of the crease as a **flat mirror** reflecting corner <InlineMath math="A" /> straight onto corner <InlineMath math="C" />. Because every point on the crease stays fixed during the fold, its distance to <InlineMath math="A" /> always equals its distance to <InlineMath math="C" />.

> A crease that brings two opposite corners together is always the perpendicular bisector of their diagonal, crossing it at right angles through its midpoint.

Watch how the crease <InlineMath math="EF" /> cuts diagonal <InlineMath math="AC" /> perpendicularly through the center <InlineMath math="M" />.

Triangles <InlineMath math="AME" /> and <InlineMath math="ABC" /> are similar because both have a right angle and share corner <InlineMath math="A" />. We can also locate <InlineMath math="E" /> directly with Pythagoras.

## Locating endpoint E

Let <InlineMath math="AE = x" />. Since <InlineMath math="E" /> sits on the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AC" />, **distance <InlineMath math="EC" /> equals <InlineMath math="AE" />**. Apply Pythagoras to right triangle <InlineMath math="EBC" /> with <InlineMath math="EB = 8 - x" /> and <InlineMath math="BC = 6" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2 &= (8 - x)^2 + 6^2 \\
x^2 &= 64 - 16x + x^2 + 36 \\
16x &= 100 \implies x = 6.25\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

## Computing the crease length

The diagonal measures <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> since <InlineMath math="8^2 + 6^2 = 100" />, so **half the diagonal is <InlineMath math="AM = 5\text{ cm}" />**. Find half the crease <InlineMath math="ME" /> from right triangle <InlineMath math="AME" />, then double it by symmetry across <InlineMath math="M" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
ME &= \sqrt{6.25^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{39.0625 - 25} = \sqrt{14.0625} = 3.75\text{ cm} \\
EF &= 2 \cdot ME = 7.5\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Check with similar triangles <InlineMath math="AME" /> and <InlineMath math="ABC" /> to verify the exact match.

<BlockMath math="\frac{ME}{AM} = \frac{BC}{AB} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4} \implies ME = \frac{3}{4} \cdot 5 = 3.75\text{ cm}, \quad EF = 7.5\text{ cm}" />

## Why other choices fail

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> is the diagonal <InlineMath math="AC" />, whereas **the crease crosses the diagonal perpendicularly**.
- <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" /> is only half the diagonal, <InlineMath math="AM" />.
- <InlineMath math="6.25\text{ cm}" /> is the side segment <InlineMath math="AE" /> from corner to crease, not the crease itself.
- <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="8\text{ cm}" /> are the sheet edges from parallel folds, which never match opposite corners.

# Answer

The length of the crease is <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ cm}" />.

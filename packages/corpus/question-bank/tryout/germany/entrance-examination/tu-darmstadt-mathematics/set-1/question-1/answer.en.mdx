The task says *Vereinfachen Sie den Term* (simplify the expression). A product of fractions is simplified by **factoring every numerator and denominator** and then cancelling equal factors. Two binomial identities do the work here.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2-9&=(a-3)(a+3) \\
a^2+6a+9&=(a+3)^2
\end{aligned}" />

Substitute the factors. Because <InlineMath math="a\ne3" /> and <InlineMath math="a\ne-3" />, the factors <InlineMath math="a-3" /> and <InlineMath math="a+3" /> are not zero and may be cancelled.

<BlockMath math="\frac{(a-3)(a+3)}{(a+3)^2}\cdot\frac{a+3}{a-3}=\frac{(a-3)(a+3)(a+3)}{(a+3)^2(a-3)}=1" />

As a check, substitute an allowed value such as <InlineMath math="a=1" />: <InlineMath math="\frac{1-9}{1+6+9}\cdot\frac{4}{-2}=\frac{-8}{16}\cdot(-2)=1" />.

So the expression equals <InlineMath math="1" /> for every allowed value of <InlineMath math="a" />.

- <InlineMath math="-1" /> comes from confusing <InlineMath math="a-3" /> with <InlineMath math="3-a" />. The expression contains the same factor <InlineMath math="a-3" /> twice.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> cannot be right, because the expression would be zero only if a factor of its numerator were zero. That happens exactly at <InlineMath math="a=3" /> or <InlineMath math="a=-3" />, and both values are excluded.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{a-3}{a+3}" /> is only the simplified first fraction. The second factor <InlineMath math="\frac{a+3}{a-3}" /> is missing.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{a+3}{a-3}" /> is only the second fraction. The first fraction is not <InlineMath math="1" /> but <InlineMath math="\frac{a-3}{a+3}" />.
- <InlineMath math="a+3" /> comes from factoring the denominator <InlineMath math="a^2+6a+9" /> as <InlineMath math="a+3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="(a+3)^2" />.

# What's left after each drain

Say the first drain removes <InlineMath math="x" /> litres. Water carries no antifreeze, so **each drain removes the same share of the antifreeze as of the liquid**, and then the volume is back to <InlineMath math="40" /> litres.

- The first drain removes a share of <InlineMath math="\frac{x}{40}" />, so <InlineMath math="1-\frac{x}{40}" /> of the antifreeze is left.
- The second drain removes <InlineMath math="2x" /> litres, a share of <InlineMath math="\frac{2x}{40}" />, so <InlineMath math="1-\frac{2x}{40}" /> of what was there is left.

The content drops from <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> to <InlineMath math="5\,\%" />, to <InlineMath math="\frac14" /> of what it was. **The two remaining shares multiply, they don't add**:

<BlockMath math="\left(1-\frac{x}{40}\right)\left(1-\frac{2x}{40}\right)=\frac14" />

# Solving the quadratic

**Multiply both sides by <InlineMath math="1600" />** to clear the fractions:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(40-x)(40-2x)&=400 \\
1600-120x+2x^2&=400 \\
x^2-60x+600&=0 \\
x&=30\pm\sqrt{900-600}=30\pm10\sqrt3
\end{aligned}" />

# Picking the possible root

The second drain removes <InlineMath math="2x" /> litres from a <InlineMath math="40" />-litre system, so **<InlineMath math="2x\le40" /> must hold, that is <InlineMath math="x\le20" />**.

- <InlineMath math="30+10\sqrt3\approx47.3" /> is more than the whole system holds, so it drops out.
- <InlineMath math="30-10\sqrt3\approx12.7" /> meets <InlineMath math="x\le20" />.

Check: <InlineMath math="1-\frac{12.7}{40}\approx0.683" /> and <InlineMath math="1-\frac{25.4}{40}\approx0.366" />, and their product is about <InlineMath math="0.25" />.

So the first drain removed <InlineMath math="30-10\sqrt3" /> litres, about <InlineMath math="12.7" /> litres.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="30+10\sqrt3" /> litres **forgets to check the root against the system's volume**. You can't drain that much from <InlineMath math="40" /> litres.
- The option with both roots forgets the same check.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> litres treats both drains as the same size, so <InlineMath math="\left(1-\frac{x}{40}\right)^2=\frac14" />.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> litres adds the drained shares, <InlineMath math="\frac{x}{40}+\frac{2x}{40}=\frac34" />, but the second drain only hits the antifreeze that's still there.
- <InlineMath math="60-20\sqrt3" /> litres **is the second drain**, <InlineMath math="2x" />, but the question asks for the first one.

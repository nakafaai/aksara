# Given

An engine cooling system holds <InlineMath math="40" /> liters of coolant with <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> **antifreeze** (*Frostschutzanteil*). First <InlineMath math="x" /> liters are drained and replaced with pure water, then after mixing <InlineMath math="2x" /> liters are drained and replaced with water, leaving a final concentration of <InlineMath math="5\,\%" />.

# Asked

Find the volume <InlineMath math="x" /> (in liters) drained on the first pass.

# Solution

Picture a glass of sweet syrup where you pour some out and top it back up with plain water. Water adds zero syrup, so each drain simply **multiplies the concentration by the fraction of liquid kept in the tank**.

> Repeated dilution multiplies remaining fractions instead of adding removed fractions, because the second drain acts on liquid that is already diluted.

Look at the curve of final antifreeze percentage against <InlineMath math="x" />. The shaded zone shows the physical tank limit <InlineMath math="0 \le x \le 20" />, since the second drain removes <InlineMath math="2x" /> liters from a <InlineMath math="40" />-liter tank.

The <InlineMath math="5\,\%" /> target line hits the curve twice, yet the red intersection sits well past the <InlineMath math="40" />-liter tank capacity and must be rejected.

## Setting up and solving the equation

The first drain leaves the factor <InlineMath math="1 - \frac{x}{40}" /> and the second drain leaves <InlineMath math="1 - \frac{2x}{40}" />. Concentration drops from <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> to <InlineMath math="5\,\%" />, which is **exactly one quarter of the starting level**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\left(1 - \frac{x}{40}\right)\left(1 - \frac{2x}{40}\right) &= \frac{1}{4} \\
(40 - x)(40 - 2x) &= 400 \\
2x^2 - 120x + 1200 &= 0 \\
x^2 - 60x + 600 &= 0
\end{aligned}" />

Solve this quadratic with the quadratic formula.

<BlockMath math="x = 30 \pm \sqrt{900 - 600} = 30 \pm 10\sqrt{3}" />

## Picking the valid root and checking

Since <InlineMath math="2x \le 40" />, the valid answer must obey **the physical bound <InlineMath math="x \le 20" />**. Compare both numerical estimates below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
30 + 10\sqrt{3} &\approx 47.3 > 20 \quad \text{(impossible)} \\
30 - 10\sqrt{3} &\approx 12.7 \le 20 \quad \text{(valid)}
\end{aligned}" />

Substitute the valid root back into the product of remaining fractions.

<BlockMath math="\left(1 - \frac{12.68}{40}\right)\left(1 - \frac{25.36}{40}\right) \approx 0.683 \times 0.366 \approx 0.25" />

The product lands right on one quarter of the initial concentration.

## Why other choices fail

- <InlineMath math="30 + 10\sqrt{3}" /> liters **exceeds the tank capacity** of <InlineMath math="40" /> liters because the physical bound was not checked.
- Listing both roots together also forgets to reject the impossible root.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> liters assumes both drains have the same volume instead of doubling the second drain.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> liters adds the drained fractions instead of multiplying the remaining fractions.
- <InlineMath math="60 - 20\sqrt{3}" /> liters equals the second drain (<InlineMath math="2x" />) rather than the first drain.

# Answer

The volume drained on the first pass is <InlineMath math="30 - 10\sqrt{3}" /> liters.

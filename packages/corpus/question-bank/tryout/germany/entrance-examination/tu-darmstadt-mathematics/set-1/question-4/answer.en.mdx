# How many corners the base has

A prism with an <InlineMath math="n" />-gon base has <InlineMath math="n" /> edges on the bottom, <InlineMath math="n" /> on the top and <InlineMath math="n" /> upright edges. **So it has <InlineMath math="3n" /> edges**:

<BlockMath math="3n=30\quad\Rightarrow\quad n=10" />

This prism has <InlineMath math="20" /> corners, <InlineMath math="10" /> on top and <InlineMath math="10" /> at the bottom.

# From one corner on top

Take one corner on the top face. Every other top corner lies in the same top face, so none of them gives a space diagonal. At the bottom, **the corner right below it and that corner's two neighbours share an upright face with it**. That leaves <InlineMath math="10-3=7" /> corners.

Each space diagonal joins one top corner to one bottom corner, so none is counted twice:

<BlockMath math="10\cdot7=70" />

# Checking another way

There are <InlineMath math="\binom{20}{2}=190" /> segments between two corners. **Remove every segment that lies in a face**:

- the <InlineMath math="30" /> edges,
- the diagonals of the two bases, <InlineMath math="2\cdot\frac{10\cdot7}{2}=70" />,
- the diagonals of the <InlineMath math="10" /> rectangular upright faces, <InlineMath math="10\cdot2=20" />.

<BlockMath math="190-30-70-20=70" />

So the prism has <InlineMath math="70" /> space diagonals.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="90" /> **removes only the corner right below**, but its two neighbours share an upright face too. That's the same as forgetting the diagonals of the upright faces.
- <InlineMath math="35" /> halves again, as if every diagonal were counted twice. Going from top to bottom counts each one once.
- <InlineMath math="80" /> removes only the corner below and one neighbour.
- <InlineMath math="140" /> forgets to remove the diagonals of the two bases.
- <InlineMath math="160" /> removes only the edges and counts every other segment as a space diagonal.

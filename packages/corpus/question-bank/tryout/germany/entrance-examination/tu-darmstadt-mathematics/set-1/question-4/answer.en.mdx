# A root inside a root

<InlineMath math="\sqrt{11-6\sqrt2}" /> looks hard, but often the number under the root is **the square of something like <InlineMath math="a-b\sqrt2" />**. Look for whole numbers <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b" />:

<BlockMath math="(a-b\sqrt2)^2=a^2+2b^2-2ab\sqrt2" />

To get <InlineMath math="11-6\sqrt2" />, you need <InlineMath math="a^2+2b^2=11" /> and <InlineMath math="2ab=6" />. The pair <InlineMath math="a=3" />, <InlineMath math="b=1" /> works, because <InlineMath math="9+2=11" /> and <InlineMath math="2\cdot3\cdot1=6" />.

# Simplifying both roots

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(3-\sqrt2)^2&=9-6\sqrt2+2=11-6\sqrt2 \\
(3+\sqrt2)^2&=9+6\sqrt2+2=11+6\sqrt2
\end{aligned}" />

A square root is never negative. **Since <InlineMath math="3-\sqrt2\approx1.59" /> is positive**, you get <InlineMath math="\sqrt{11-6\sqrt2}=3-\sqrt2" />. In the same way, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{11+6\sqrt2}=3+\sqrt2" />.

<BlockMath math="(3-\sqrt2)+(3+\sqrt2)=6" />

# A check another way

Call the sum <InlineMath math="S" />. Square it and use <InlineMath math="\sqrt{p}\sqrt{q}=\sqrt{pq}" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S^2&=(11-6\sqrt2)+(11+6\sqrt2)+2\sqrt{(11-6\sqrt2)(11+6\sqrt2)} \\
&=22+2\sqrt{121-72}=22+2\cdot7=36
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="S" /> is positive, <InlineMath math="S=6" />. **Same result**.

So the value is <InlineMath math="6" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{22}" /> adds the numbers under the roots. **A sum of two roots isn't the root of the sum**.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{11}" /> deletes the <InlineMath math="\pm6\sqrt2" /> under the roots, as if they cancelled inside each root.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt2" /> is <InlineMath math="(3+\sqrt2)-(3-\sqrt2)" />, the difference instead of the sum.
- <InlineMath math="3+\sqrt2" /> simplifies only one root and forgets the other.
- <InlineMath math="36" /> is <InlineMath math="S^2" />. The square root hasn't been taken yet.

Two percentage changes in a row cannot simply be added, because the second change applies to the **price that has already changed**. Work with factors instead: an increase of <InlineMath math="25\,\%" /> means the factor <InlineMath math="1.25" />, and a decrease of <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> means the factor <InlineMath math="0.8" />.

Let <InlineMath math="p" /> be the original price. The final price is then:

<BlockMath math="p\cdot1.25\cdot0.8=p\cdot1=p" />

A numerical example confirms this. If the backpack first costs <InlineMath math="80\text{ €}" />, it costs <InlineMath math="100\text{ €}" /> after the increase. Twenty percent of that is exactly <InlineMath math="20\text{ €}" />, so it ends at <InlineMath math="80\text{ €}" /> again.

So the price has stayed the same overall.

- An increase of <InlineMath math="5\,\%" /> comes from combining the percentages directly, <InlineMath math="25\,\%-20\,\%" />. The <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> applies to the larger price, though.
- A decrease of <InlineMath math="5\,\%" /> adds a sign mix-up to the same mistake.
- An increase of <InlineMath math="45\,\%" /> counts both changes as increases.
- A decrease of <InlineMath math="10\,\%" /> comes from swapping the directions: <InlineMath math="0.75\cdot1.2=0.9" />.
- A decrease of <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> considers only the second change.

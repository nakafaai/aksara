# When the roots make sense

You can only take the square root of a number that isn't negative. So <InlineMath math="3x+1\ge0" /> and <InlineMath math="x-1\ge0" />. The second condition is stricter, so **you only look for <InlineMath math="x\ge1" />**.

# Squaring safely

First move <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x-1}" /> to the right, so that only one root is left on the left:

<BlockMath math="\sqrt{3x+1}=2+\sqrt{x-1}" />

**Both sides are not negative**, so you may square them. On the right, use <InlineMath math="(p+q)^2=p^2+2pq+q^2" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3x+1&=4+4\sqrt{x-1}+(x-1) \\
2x-2&=4\sqrt{x-1} \\
x-1&=2\sqrt{x-1}
\end{aligned}" />

# The trap when dividing

Now it's tempting to divide both sides by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x-1}" />. Don't! **<InlineMath math="\sqrt{x-1}" /> can be zero**, and dividing by zero throws away an answer.

It's safer to call <InlineMath math="t=\sqrt{x-1}" />. Then <InlineMath math="x-1=t^2" />, and the equation becomes:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t^2&=2t \\
t^2-2t&=0 \\
t(t-2)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="t=0" /> or <InlineMath math="t=2" />. Go back to <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="x=t^2+1" />:

- <InlineMath math="t=0" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=1" />.
- <InlineMath math="t=2" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=5" />.

# Check in the original equation

**Squaring can create fake answers**, so check both:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x=1:&\quad \sqrt{4}-\sqrt{0}=2-0=2 \\
x=5:&\quad \sqrt{16}-\sqrt{4}=4-2=2
\end{aligned}" />

Both are correct. So the equation has exactly two solutions, <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=5" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Only <InlineMath math="x=5" /> comes from dividing by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x-1}" />. That division throws away <InlineMath math="x=1" />, even though <InlineMath math="x=1" /> passes the check.
- Only <InlineMath math="x=1" /> comes from **squaring term by term**, as if <InlineMath math="\left(\sqrt{3x+1}-\sqrt{x-1}\right)^2=(3x+1)-(x-1)" />. The middle term <InlineMath math="-2\sqrt{3x+1}\sqrt{x-1}" /> goes missing, and so does <InlineMath math="x=5" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=0" /> and <InlineMath math="x=5" /> mixes up <InlineMath math="t=0" /> with <InlineMath math="x=0" />. But <InlineMath math="x=0" /> isn't even allowed, because <InlineMath math="\sqrt{0-1}" /> isn't defined.
- Only <InlineMath math="x=2" /> writes down the value of <InlineMath math="t" />, not of <InlineMath math="x" />. For <InlineMath math="x=2" /> the left side is <InlineMath math="\sqrt7-1\approx1.65" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=2" /> turns <InlineMath math="t=0" /> back into <InlineMath math="x" /> correctly but forgets to turn <InlineMath math="t=2" /> into <InlineMath math="x=5" />.

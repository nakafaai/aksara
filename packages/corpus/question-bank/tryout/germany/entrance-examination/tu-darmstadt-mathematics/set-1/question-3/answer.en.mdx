# Three places for the right angle

The task doesn't say at which vertex the right angle is. **So there are three cases: the right angle at <InlineMath math="A" />, at <InlineMath math="B" /> or at <InlineMath math="P" />**. Two segments are perpendicular exactly when the dot product of their direction vectors is zero.

For <InlineMath math="P(x\mid0)" /> you have:

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}6\\-2\end{pmatrix},\quad\overrightarrow{AP}=\begin{pmatrix}x-1\\-3\end{pmatrix},\quad\overrightarrow{BP}=\begin{pmatrix}x-7\\-1\end{pmatrix}" />

# Right angle at A or at B

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{at }A:&\quad\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AP}=6(x-1)+(-2)(-3)=6x=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=0 \\
\text{at }B:&\quad\overrightarrow{BA}\cdot\overrightarrow{BP}=-6(x-7)+2\cdot(-1)=40-6x=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=\frac{20}{3}
\end{aligned}" />

**Each of these cases gives exactly one point**, because the equation is linear.

# Right angle at P

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{PA}\cdot\overrightarrow{PB}=(1-x)(7-x)+3\cdot1&=0 \\
x^2-8x+10&=0 \\
x&=4\pm\sqrt6
\end{aligned}" />

That's the same as cutting the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis with the Thales circle on <InlineMath math="AB" />, centred at <InlineMath math="(4\mid2)" /> with radius <InlineMath math="\sqrt{10}" />. **The circle crosses the axis at two points**.

None of the four points lies on a line with <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />, since line <InlineMath math="AB" /> only meets the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at <InlineMath math="x=10" />. So each one gives a real triangle.

So <InlineMath math="x=0" />, <InlineMath math="x=4-\sqrt6" />, <InlineMath math="x=4+\sqrt6" /> or <InlineMath math="x=\frac{20}{3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Only <InlineMath math="4\pm\sqrt6" /> **only thinks of the right angle at <InlineMath math="P" />**. The task doesn't name the vertex.
- Only <InlineMath math="x=0" /> or <InlineMath math="x=\frac{20}{3}" /> forgets the case with the right angle at <InlineMath math="P" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{22}{3}" /> gets a sign wrong in case <InlineMath math="B" />, so <InlineMath math="-6(x-7)+2=0" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=10" /> is the point on line <InlineMath math="AB" />. There <InlineMath math="A" />, <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="P" /> are in a line, so there's no triangle.
- <InlineMath math="x=2" /> gets a sign wrong in case <InlineMath math="A" />, so <InlineMath math="6(x-1)-6=0" />.

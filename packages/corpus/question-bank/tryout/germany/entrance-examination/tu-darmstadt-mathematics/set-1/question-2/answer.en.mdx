# Three factors that behave differently

The fraction has three parts, and **each part behaves differently with respect to sign**:

- <InlineMath math="x" /> changes sign at <InlineMath math="x=0" />.
- <InlineMath math="(x-3)^2" /> is a square, so it's never negative. It's zero only at <InlineMath math="x=3" />.
- <InlineMath math="x+2" /> changes sign at <InlineMath math="x=-2" />, and there the fraction isn't defined.

# Where it equals zero

The fraction is zero exactly when the numerator is zero and the denominator isn't: at <InlineMath math="x=0" /> and at <InlineMath math="x=3" />. Since the sign is <InlineMath math="\le" />, **both points are solutions**.

# Where it's negative

For <InlineMath math="x\ne3" />, the factor <InlineMath math="(x-3)^2" /> is positive, so the fraction has the same sign as <InlineMath math="\frac{x}{x+2}" />:

| Part | <InlineMath math="x" /> | <InlineMath math="x+2" /> | Fraction |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> | negative | negative | positive |
| <InlineMath math="-2<x<0" /> | negative | positive | negative |
| <InlineMath math="x>0" /> | positive | positive | positive |

The fraction is **negative only on <InlineMath math="-2<x<0" />**. The point <InlineMath math="x=3" /> doesn't change the sign, so around <InlineMath math="3" /> the fraction stays positive, except exactly at <InlineMath math="3" />, where it's zero.

Check: <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> gives <InlineMath math="\frac{-1\cdot16}{1}=-16\le0" />, <InlineMath math="x=2" /> gives <InlineMath math="\frac{2\cdot1}{4}>0" />, and <InlineMath math="x=-3" /> gives <InlineMath math="\frac{-3\cdot36}{-1}=108>0" />.

So the solutions are <InlineMath math="-2<x\le0" /> or <InlineMath math="x=3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Only <InlineMath math="-2<x\le0" /> **forgets the point <InlineMath math="x=3" />**, where the numerator is zero.
- <InlineMath math="-2\le x\le0" /> or <InlineMath math="x=3" /> includes <InlineMath math="x=-2" />, where the denominator is zero.
- <InlineMath math="x\le0" /> or <InlineMath math="x=3" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="x+2" /> as if it were always positive. For <InlineMath math="x=-3" /> the fraction is actually positive.
- <InlineMath math="-2<x\le0" /> or <InlineMath math="x\ge3" /> switches sign at <InlineMath math="x=3" />, but a square factor doesn't switch sign. For <InlineMath math="x=4" /> the fraction is <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{6}>0" />.
- <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> or <InlineMath math="x\ge0" /> is where the fraction is not negative, so the signs were read the wrong way round.

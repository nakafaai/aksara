The task asks for the *Lösungsmenge* (solution set) of a linear inequality. Solve it like an equation, with one exception: multiplying or dividing by a negative number **reverses the inequality sign**. First bring every term with <InlineMath math="x" /> to the left and every number to the right.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3-2x&>7+3x \\
-2x-3x&>7-3 \\
-5x&>4
\end{aligned}" />

Now divide by <InlineMath math="-5" />. Since <InlineMath math="-5" /> is negative, the sign <InlineMath math=">" /> becomes <InlineMath math="<" />.

<BlockMath math="x<-\frac45" />

As a check, test one value on each side of the boundary. For <InlineMath math="x=-1" />, <InlineMath math="3+2=5>7-3=4" />, so the inequality holds. For <InlineMath math="x=0" />, <InlineMath math="3>7" /> is false.

So the solution set consists of all <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="x<-\frac45" />.

- <InlineMath math="x>-\frac45" /> comes from not reversing the sign when dividing by <InlineMath math="-5" />. The test with <InlineMath math="x=0" /> shows that this set contains wrong values.
- <InlineMath math="x<\frac45" /> comes from losing the sign of the numbers while rearranging, that is, computing <InlineMath math="-5x>-4" />.
- <InlineMath math="x>\frac45" /> combines that sign error with the missing reversal of the inequality sign.
- <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> comes from adding the <InlineMath math="3" /> instead of subtracting it while rearranging, which gives <InlineMath math="-5x>10" />.
- <InlineMath math="x>2" /> combines that error with the missing reversal of the inequality sign.

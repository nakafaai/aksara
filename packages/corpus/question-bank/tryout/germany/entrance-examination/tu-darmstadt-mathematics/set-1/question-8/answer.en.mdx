Neither the largest base nor the largest exponent alone decides how large a power is. Without a calculator, the safest way is to **write every power as a whole number** and then compare.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2^{10}&=1{,}024 \\
3^6&=729 \\
5^4&=625 \\
10^3&=1{,}000 \\
6^4&=36\cdot36=1{,}296 \\
7^3&=343
\end{aligned}" />

The order is <InlineMath math="7^3<5^4<3^6<10^3<2^{10}<6^4" />. To check <InlineMath math="6^4" />: <InlineMath math="6^2=36" /> and <InlineMath math="36^2=1{,}296" />. In fact <InlineMath math="6^4>2^{10}" />, because <InlineMath math="6^4=2^4\cdot3^4=16\cdot81" /> and <InlineMath math="2^{10}=16\cdot64" />.

So the largest number is <InlineMath math="6^4" />.

- <InlineMath math="2^{10}" /> has the largest exponent, but at <InlineMath math="1{,}024" /> it is smaller than <InlineMath math="6^4=1{,}296" />.
- <InlineMath math="3^6=729" /> and <InlineMath math="5^4=625" /> are less than <InlineMath math="1{,}000" />.
- <InlineMath math="10^3=1{,}000" /> looks large because of the base <InlineMath math="10" />, but it is below <InlineMath math="1{,}024" /> and <InlineMath math="1{,}296" />.
- <InlineMath math="7^3" /> has a large base but only the exponent <InlineMath math="3" />, and at <InlineMath math="343" /> it is the smallest.

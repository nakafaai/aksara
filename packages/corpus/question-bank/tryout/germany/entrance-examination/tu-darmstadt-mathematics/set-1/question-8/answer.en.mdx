# Given

We are given an absolute-value equation with a real parameter <InlineMath math="a" />.

<BlockMath math="\left|x^2 - 4x\right| = a" />

The problem asks for **exactly three distinct real solutions** (*genau drei verschiedene reelle Lösungen*).

# Asked

Find all real values of <InlineMath math="a" /> for which the equation has exactly three distinct real solutions.

# Solution

The number of solutions to <InlineMath math="|x^2 - 4x| = a" /> equals **how many times the horizontal line <InlineMath math="y = a" /> crosses the curve <InlineMath math="y = |x^2 - 4x|" />**. Without the absolute value, <InlineMath math="x^2 - 4x" /> dips down to a valley at <InlineMath math="(2 \mid -4)" />.

Taking the absolute value folds that valley upward above the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, creating a **hill peaking right at <InlineMath math="(2 \mid 4)" />**.

> A horizontal line crosses a folded parabola exactly three times only when it grazes the very top of the reflected hill.

As the line rises from below, the crossing count jumps from <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="2" />, then <InlineMath math="4" />, then exactly **<InlineMath math="3" /> at the peak <InlineMath math="a = 4" />**, before dropping back to <InlineMath math="2" />.

Summarize every height level of <InlineMath math="y = a" /> to confirm that **only the hilltop level yields three crossings**.

## Algebraic check and wrong choices

At <InlineMath math="a = 4" />, split the equation into both sign branches to **verify all three distinct solutions explicitly**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
-(x^2 - 4x) = 4 &\implies (x - 2)^2 = 0 \implies x = 2 \\
x^2 - 4x = 4 &\implies (x - 2)^2 = 8 \implies x = 2 \pm 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}" />

All three roots are real and distinct.

- <InlineMath math="a = 0" /> yields only two solutions, <InlineMath math="x = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 4" />.
- <InlineMath math="0 < a < 4" /> (including <InlineMath math="a = 2" />) hits the hill twice and both outer arms twice, giving four solutions.
- <InlineMath math="a > 4" /> clears the hill completely and meets only the two outer arms.
- <InlineMath math="a = -4" /> has no solutions because an absolute value is never negative.

# Answer

The equation has exactly three distinct real solutions only when **<InlineMath math="a = 4" />**.

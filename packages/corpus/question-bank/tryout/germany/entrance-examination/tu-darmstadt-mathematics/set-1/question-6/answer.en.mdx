# Given

An acute angle (*Winkel*) <InlineMath math="x" /> lies **between <InlineMath math="0^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />** and satisfies the trigonometric equation below.

<BlockMath math="\sin x + \cos x = \frac{17}{13}" />

# Asked

Find the exact value of <InlineMath math="\tan x" />.

# Solution

On the unit circle, the point <InlineMath math="(\cos x \mid \sin x)" /> traces the first-quadrant quarter-circle of radius <InlineMath math="1" />. The line whose coordinates sum to <InlineMath math="\frac{17}{13}" /> meets that arc at **two points symmetric across the <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> line**.

> For any acute angle below forty-five degrees, the vertical coordinate on the unit circle is always smaller than the horizontal coordinate, so sine is smaller than cosine.

Look at how the bound below <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> picks out the green point below the diagonal ray.

The green point below the <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> ray has cosine <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{13}" /> and sine <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{13}" />. Now derive these values algebraically.

## Squaring both sides

We do not know sine and cosine separately yet. **Square both sides** so the Pythagorean identity <InlineMath math="\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1" /> appears and isolates their product.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(\sin x + \cos x)^2 &= \frac{289}{169} \\
1 + 2\sin x \cos x &= \frac{289}{169} \\
\sin x \cos x &= \frac{60}{169}
\end{aligned}" />

## Finding the tangent value

With sum <InlineMath math="\frac{17}{13}" /> and product <InlineMath math="\frac{60}{169}" />, both values are the roots of this quadratic equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t^2 - \frac{17}{13}t + \frac{60}{169} &= 0 \\
169t^2 - 221t + 60 &= 0 \\
(13t - 5)(13t - 12) &= 0
\end{aligned}" />

The two numbers are <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{13}" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{13}" />. Because the angle is below <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />, **sine is smaller than cosine**, which fixes the tangent immediately.

<BlockMath math="\sin x = \frac{5}{13}, \quad \cos x = \frac{12}{13} \implies \tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \frac{5}{12}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{13}" /> plus <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{13}" /> equals <InlineMath math="\frac{17}{13}" />, and <InlineMath math="\tan x < 1" /> matches an angle below <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />.

## Why other choices fail

- <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{5}" /> **swaps sine and cosine**, matching an angle above <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{60}{169}" /> is the intermediate product <InlineMath math="\sin x \cos x" />, not the tangent.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{12}" /> or <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{5}" /> ignores the angle bound below <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{120}{169}" /> equals <InlineMath math="\sin 2x" />, not <InlineMath math="\tan x" />.
- Claiming no such angle exists happens when squaring drops the middle term <InlineMath math="2\sin x \cos x" />.

# Answer

The exact value of <InlineMath math="\tan x" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{12}" />.

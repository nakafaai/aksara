# The equation for the meeting points

A meeting point has the same <InlineMath math="y" /> on the line and on the parabola. **Set the two formulas for <InlineMath math="y" /> equal**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-2x+4&=mx \\
x^2-(2+m)x+4&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The two solutions <InlineMath math="x_1" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2" /> are the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinates of the two meeting points.

# The gap between two roots

The quadratic formula gives <InlineMath math="x_{1,2}=\frac{(2+m)\pm\sqrt D}{2}" />. So **the difference of the roots equals <InlineMath math="\sqrt D" />**, because the front parts are equal and cancel:

<BlockMath math="x_1-x_2=\frac{2\sqrt D}{2}=\sqrt D,\qquad D=(2+m)^2-16" />

The difference has to be <InlineMath math="3" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(2+m)^2-16}&=3 \\
(2+m)^2&=25 \\
2+m=5\quad&\text{or}\quad 2+m=-5 \\
m=3\quad&\text{or}\quad m=-7
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking both values

- <InlineMath math="m=3" />: <InlineMath math="x^2-5x+4=0" /> has roots <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />. The difference is <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="m=-7" />: <InlineMath math="x^2+5x+4=0" /> has roots <InlineMath math="-1" /> and <InlineMath math="-4" />. The difference is <InlineMath math="3" /> too.

**Both values meet the condition**, because the problem doesn't restrict where the meeting points are.

So the values are <InlineMath math="m=3" /> or <InlineMath math="m=-7" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Only <InlineMath math="m=3" /> or only <InlineMath math="m=-7" /> **drops one of the two square roots** of <InlineMath math="2+m=\pm5" />.
- <InlineMath math="m=5" /> or <InlineMath math="m=-5" /> forgets the term <InlineMath math="-2x" />, so the equation becomes <InlineMath math="m^2-16=9" />.
- <InlineMath math="m=2" /> or <InlineMath math="m=-6" /> uses <InlineMath math="D=0" />. That's the condition for the line to touch the parabola, where the roots are zero apart.
- <InlineMath math="m=7" /> or <InlineMath math="m=-3" /> has a sign slip, <InlineMath math="m-2=\pm5" />. For <InlineMath math="m=7" />, the equation is <InlineMath math="x^2-9x+4=0" />, whose roots are <InlineMath math="\sqrt{65}" /> apart.

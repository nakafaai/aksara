# Squaring both sides

You don't know <InlineMath math="\sin x" /> and <InlineMath math="\cos x" /> one by one yet. **Square both sides**, because the result contains <InlineMath math="\sin^2x+\cos^2x" />, which is always <InlineMath math="1" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(\sin x+\cos x)^2&=\frac{289}{169} \\
\sin^2x+2\sin x\cos x+\cos^2x&=\frac{289}{169} \\
1+2\sin x\cos x&=\frac{289}{169} \\
\sin x\cos x&=\frac{60}{169}
\end{aligned}" />

# Two numbers from their sum and product

Now you know the sum <InlineMath math="\sin x+\cos x=\frac{17}{13}" /> and the product <InlineMath math="\sin x\cos x=\frac{60}{169}" />. **Two numbers with that sum and product are the roots of this quadratic**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t^2-\frac{17}{13}t+\frac{60}{169}&=0 \\
169t^2-221t+60&=0 \\
(13t-5)(13t-12)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

So the two numbers are <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{13}" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{13}" />. All that's left is to decide which one is the sine and which is the cosine.

# Using the angle range

For <InlineMath math="0^\circ<x<45^\circ" />, the angle is closer to <InlineMath math="0^\circ" />, so **its sine is smaller than its cosine**. That means:

<BlockMath math="\sin x=\frac{5}{13},\qquad\cos x=\frac{12}{13},\qquad\tan x=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}=\frac{5}{12}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{13}+\frac{12}{13}=\frac{17}{13}" />, and <InlineMath math="\tan x=\frac{5}{12}<1" /> fits an angle below <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />.

So <InlineMath math="\tan x=\frac{5}{12}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{5}" /> **swaps the sine and the cosine**. A tangent above <InlineMath math="1" /> means an angle above <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{60}{169}" /> is <InlineMath math="\sin x\cos x" />, the middle step, not the tangent.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{12}" /> or <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{5}" /> ignores the angle range. Without it both values are possible, but here only one holds.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{120}{169}" /> is <InlineMath math="2\sin x\cos x=\sin2x" />.
- "No such angle" comes from squaring without the middle term. That gives <InlineMath math="1=\frac{289}{169}" />, which looks impossible, but only because <InlineMath math="2\sin x\cos x" /> went missing.

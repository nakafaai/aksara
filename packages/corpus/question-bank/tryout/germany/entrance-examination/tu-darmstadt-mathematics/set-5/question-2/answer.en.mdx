# Given

A **rectangular flowerbed** (*rechteckiges Beet*) has perimeter (*Umfang*) exactly <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> with one side of length <InlineMath math="x\text{ m}" />. Its area (*Flächeninhalt*) must be at least (*mindestens*) <InlineMath math="21\text{ m}^2" />.

# Asked

Find all real values of <InlineMath math="x" /> for which the area is at least <InlineMath math="21\text{ m}^2" />.

# Solution

With <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> of edging, any two neighboring sides share half the perimeter, or <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" />. Stretch the bed long and skinny (<InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> by <InlineMath math="9\text{ m}" />) and the area shrinks to <InlineMath math="9\text{ m}^2" />, whereas **shapes closer to a square pack more area inside the same fence**.

> For a fixed perimeter, rectangular area traces a downward-opening parabola peaking at a square, so a minimum-area requirement always produces a closed interval between two symmetric cutoffs.

Write the adjacent side as <InlineMath math="10 - x" /> and solve the quadratic area inequality.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x(10 - x) &\ge 21 \\
10x - x^2 - 21 &\ge 0 \\
x^2 - 10x + 21 &\le 0 \\
(x - 3)(x - 7) &\le 0
\end{aligned}" />

## Reading the area interval on the parabola

Multiplying through by <InlineMath math="-1" /> flips the inequality sign. The product <InlineMath math="(x - 3)(x - 7)" /> is zero or negative **precisely between <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="7" />, inclusive**.

## Testing endpoints and wrong choices

Test three values: at <InlineMath math="x = 3" /> the area is <InlineMath math="3 \cdot 7 = 21\text{ m}^2" />, at <InlineMath math="x = 5" /> it hits **the maximum area <InlineMath math="25\text{ m}^2" />**, and at <InlineMath math="x = 2" /> it falls to <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}^2" />.

| Option | Why it is wrong |
| :--- | :--- |
| <InlineMath math="3 < x < 7" /> | Excludes both endpoints, even though "at least" allows exactly <InlineMath math="21\text{ m}^2" />. |
| <InlineMath math="0 < x \le 3" /> or <InlineMath math="7 \le x < 10" /> | Picks the outer wings where area is at most <InlineMath math="21\text{ m}^2" />, flipping the inequality. |
| <InlineMath math="3 \le x < 10" /> | Drops the upper cutoff <InlineMath math="7" />, yet <InlineMath math="x = 9" /> gives only <InlineMath math="9\text{ m}^2" />. |
| <InlineMath math="-7 \le x \le -3" /> | Comes from wrong signs <InlineMath math="(x + 3)(x + 7)" /> and side lengths cannot be negative. |
| <InlineMath math="0 < x \le 7" /> | Drops the lower cutoff <InlineMath math="3" />, yet <InlineMath math="x = 1" /> gives only <InlineMath math="9\text{ m}^2" />. |

# Answer

The area is at least <InlineMath math="21\text{ m}^2" /> for <InlineMath math="3 \le x \le 7" />.

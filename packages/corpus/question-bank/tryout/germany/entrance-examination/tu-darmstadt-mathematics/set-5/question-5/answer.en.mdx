The task asks *um wie viel* (by how much) the square's area is larger. Equal perimeter does **not** mean equal area. So first find the side of the square from the perimeter, then compare the two areas.

The perimeter of the rectangle is <InlineMath math="P=2\cdot(12+4)=32\text{ cm}" />. The square has four equal sides, so each side is <InlineMath math="32:4=8\text{ cm}" /> long.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{\text{square}}&=8\cdot8=64\text{ cm}^2 \\
A_{\text{rectangle}}&=12\cdot4=48\text{ cm}^2 \\
64-48&=16
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, test the perimeter of the square: <InlineMath math="4\cdot8=32\text{ cm}" />, exactly as for the rectangle. For a fixed perimeter the square has the largest area of all rectangles, so the difference must be positive.

So the square's area is larger by <InlineMath math="16\text{ cm}^2" />.

- <InlineMath math="0\text{ cm}^2" /> assumes that equal perimeter means equal area. That is false, as <InlineMath math="64\ne48" /> shows.
- <InlineMath math="8\text{ cm}^2" /> is the difference of the side lengths, <InlineMath math="12-4" />, or the side of the square, but not a difference of areas.
- <InlineMath math="32\text{ cm}^2" /> is the number of the common perimeter, which is a length in centimetres.
- <InlineMath math="48\text{ cm}^2" /> is the area of the rectangle itself.
- <InlineMath math="64\text{ cm}^2" /> is the area of the square itself. The task asks for the difference from the rectangle.

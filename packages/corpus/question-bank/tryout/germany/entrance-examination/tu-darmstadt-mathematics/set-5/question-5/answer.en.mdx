# Where the centre must be

A circle that touches both axes is at distance <InlineMath math="r" /> from both axes. In the first quadrant **its centre is therefore at <InlineMath math="M(r\mid r)" />**. So the whole circle depends only on <InlineMath math="r" />.

# The condition from the point <InlineMath math="P" />

The point <InlineMath math="P(1\mid8)" /> lies on the circle when **its distance to <InlineMath math="M" /> is exactly <InlineMath math="r" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(1-r)^2+(8-r)^2&=r^2 \\
1-2r+r^2+64-16r+r^2&=r^2 \\
r^2-18r+65&=0 \\
(r-5)(r-13)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- "only <InlineMath math="r=5" />" and "only <InlineMath math="r=13" />" **throw away a valid solution** of the quadratic equation.
- "only <InlineMath math="r=\sqrt{65}" />" is the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to the origin, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{1+64}" />. But the origin isn't the centre.
- "only <InlineMath math="r=4.5" />" halves the sum of the coordinates of <InlineMath math="P" />, as if the centre were halfway between the intercepts.
- "only <InlineMath math="r=9" />" is the average of the two correct radii, the <InlineMath math="r" />-value of the vertex of <InlineMath math="r^2-18r+65" />.

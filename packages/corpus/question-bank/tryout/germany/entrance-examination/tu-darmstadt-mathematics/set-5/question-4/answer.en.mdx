# Write the number of shirts as a fraction

If a shirt costs <InlineMath math="p" /> euros, <InlineMath math="600" /> euros buy exactly <InlineMath math="\frac{600}{p}" /> shirts. At the price <InlineMath math="p-5" /> it would be <InlineMath math="\frac{600}{p-5}" /> shirts, and that's **exactly <InlineMath math="4" /> shirts more**:

<BlockMath math="\frac{600}{p-5}-\frac{600}{p}=4" />

# Solve the equation

Multiply by <InlineMath math="p(p-5)" /> and **then divide by <InlineMath math="4" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
600p-600(p-5)&=4p(p-5) \\
3000&=4p^2-20p \\
p^2-5p-750&=0 \\
(p-30)(p+25)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

# Keep the price that makes sense

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="25" /> euros is **the imagined cheaper price**, not the real one.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> euros is really the number of shirts, <InlineMath math="\frac{600}{30}=20" />.
- "No price fits" comes from setting up the equation the wrong way round: <InlineMath math="\frac{600}{p}-\frac{600}{p-5}=4" />. The left side is negative for every price above <InlineMath math="5" /> euros.
- <InlineMath math="24" /> euros is the number of shirts at the cheaper price.
- <InlineMath math="150" /> euros divides <InlineMath math="600" /> by the <InlineMath math="4" /> extra shirts.

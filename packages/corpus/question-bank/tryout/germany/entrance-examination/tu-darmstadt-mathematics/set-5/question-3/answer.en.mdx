# Given

A **stepladder** (*Stehleiter*) consists of two straight rails (*Holme*) each <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ m}" /> long hinged at the top, with their feet <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> apart on level ground. A taut horizontal chain (*Kette*) attaches to both rails exactly <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> down from the top hinge measured along each rail.

# Asked

Find the length <InlineMath math="k" /> of the chain.

# Solution

Picture the stepladder from the side as a giant letter A meeting at the top hinge! Because the horizontal chain runs parallel to the floor, **the upper part forms an exact miniature copy of the full ladder**. Since the chain sits <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> down out of <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ m}" />, it is two fifths of the way down from the apex!

> When two parallel segments cross two rays meeting at a vertex, the ratio of the parallel segments equals the ratio of their distances measured from that vertex.

Look at the side view below to see the **intercept theorem** (*Strahlensatz*) in action.

## Computing the chain length and checking wrong choices

Set up **the intercept theorem proportion from the top hinge** to solve for <InlineMath math="k" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{k}{1.5} = \frac{1}{2.5} = 0.4 \implies k = 1.5 \cdot 0.4 = 0.6\text{ m}" />

Intuition check: over <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ m}" /> of rail the spread grows to <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" />, widening by <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ m}" /> for every <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> down from the hinge!

- <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ m}" /> is just the scale factor before multiplying by the <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> floor span.
- <InlineMath math="0.9\text{ m}" /> wrongly measures <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> up from the floor instead of <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> down from the hinge.
- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> assumes parallel segments have equal length, ignoring that the rails flare apart downward.
- <InlineMath math="3.75\text{ m}" /> inverts the ratio, producing a chain wider than the ladder's base.
- <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> is the distance from the hinge along the rail, not the horizontal chain length.

# Answer

The chain is <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ m}" /> long.

# Given

A **right circular cone** (*gerader Kreiskegel*) has base radius <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> and height <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" />. A coaxial **right circular cylinder** (*gerader Kreiszylinder*) sits inside it on the same base with its upper rim touching the cone's lateral surface (*Mantelfläche*). The cylinder's height equals its diameter (<InlineMath math="h = 2r" />).

# Asked

Find the volume of the inscribed cylinder.

# Solution

Slice the cone and cylinder straight down their shared central axis so the 3D solid collapses into **a rectangle sitting snugly inside an isosceles triangle**! Because the top corners of the rectangle touch the slanted edges, the little cone sitting above the cylinder is a scaled copy of the entire cone.

> Slicing coaxial solids of revolution through their axis turns a 3D problem into flat similar triangles, letting the intercept theorem link the cylinder's radius and height immediately.

The small cone above the cylinder has height <InlineMath math="12 - h" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r" />, directly similar to the full cone.

## Solving for the cylinder dimensions and volume

Apply **the intercept theorem** (*Strahlensatz*) together with <InlineMath math="h = 2r" />, then compute the cylinder volume.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{r}{6} &= \frac{12 - h}{12} = \frac{12 - 2r}{12} \\
2r &= 12 - 2r \implies 4r = 12 \implies r = 3\text{ cm}, \quad h = 6\text{ cm} \\
V &= \pi r^2 h = \pi \cdot 3^2 \cdot 6 = 54\pi\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

## Sanity check and wrong choices

Quick volume check: the entire cone holds <InlineMath math="144\pi\text{ cm}^3" />, so **a cylinder of <InlineMath math="54\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> fills three eighths of the cone**, well within its bounds.

- <InlineMath math="64\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> sets height equal to radius (<InlineMath math="h = r" />) instead of diameter, giving <InlineMath math="r = 4" />.
- <InlineMath math="216\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> plugs the diameter <InlineMath math="6" /> in place of the radius in the volume formula.
- <InlineMath math="18\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> mistakenly applies the cone's <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}" /> factor to a cylinder.
- <InlineMath math="27\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> uses height <InlineMath math="3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="6" />.
- <InlineMath math="48\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> mixes radius <InlineMath math="4" /> with height <InlineMath math="3" /> from two inconsistent setups.

# Answer

The volume of the cylinder is <InlineMath math="54\pi\text{ cm}^3" />.

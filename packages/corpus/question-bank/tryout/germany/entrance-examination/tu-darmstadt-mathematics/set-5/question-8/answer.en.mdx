# Intercept theorem at the apex

Above the cylinder a small cone remains, with height <InlineMath math="12-h" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r" />. It is **similar to the big cone**:

<BlockMath math="\frac{r}{6}=\frac{12-h}{12}" />

With the condition <InlineMath math="h=2r" /> this becomes:

<BlockMath math="\frac{r}{6}=\frac{12-2r}{12}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2r=12-2r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=3,\ h=6" />

# The volume

<BlockMath math="V=\pi r^2h=\pi\cdot3^2\cdot6=54\pi\text{ cm}^3" />

The cylinder has **a volume of <InlineMath math="54\pi\text{ cm}^3" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="64\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> makes the height equal to the **radius** instead of the diameter: <InlineMath math="\frac r6=\frac{12-r}{12}" /> gives <InlineMath math="r=4" />, and <InlineMath math="\pi\cdot16\cdot4=64\pi" />.
- <InlineMath math="216\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> uses the diameter <InlineMath math="6" /> as the radius: <InlineMath math="\pi\cdot36\cdot6" />.
- <InlineMath math="18\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> applies the cone formula with <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> to the cylinder.
- <InlineMath math="27\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> uses height <InlineMath math="3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="6" />.
- <InlineMath math="48\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> is the volume of a cylinder with radius <InlineMath math="4" /> and height <InlineMath math="3" />, mixing radius and height from two different set-ups.

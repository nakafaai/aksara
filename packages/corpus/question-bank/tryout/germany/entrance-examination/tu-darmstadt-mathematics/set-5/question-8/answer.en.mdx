The task asks for the shape of the cut surface (*Schnittfläche*). It is a triangle, because the cut passes through exactly three corners of the cube and meets only the three faces at <InlineMath math="E" />. What decides its type is **the lengths of its three sides**.

Any two of the three end points lie on a common face of the cube, one of the three faces at the corner <InlineMath math="E" />. So the segment joining them is a face diagonal. With edge length <InlineMath math="a" />, Pythagoras' theorem gives the same length for each side:

<BlockMath math="d=\sqrt{a^2+a^2}=a\sqrt2" />

So all three sides of the triangle are equal. As a check, take the cube with <InlineMath math="a=1" />, the corner <InlineMath math="E" /> at the origin, and the end points <InlineMath math="(1\mid0\mid0)" />, <InlineMath math="(0\mid1\mid0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0\mid0\mid1)" />. Every distance between two of these points is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{1+1}=\sqrt2" />.

So the cut surface is an equilateral triangle.

- A right isosceles triangle comes from carrying the right angles at the corner <InlineMath math="E" /> over to the cut surface. The angles of the cut triangle are all <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />.
- A square would need four corners. The cut passes through only three corners of the cube and meets only three faces.
- A rectangle that is not a square comes from a cut through two opposite edges, not here.
- A regular hexagon comes from a cut through the midpoints of six edges, perpendicular to a space diagonal, not through three corners.
- An isosceles triangle that is not equilateral would have one side of a different length. All three sides are face diagonals of equal length.

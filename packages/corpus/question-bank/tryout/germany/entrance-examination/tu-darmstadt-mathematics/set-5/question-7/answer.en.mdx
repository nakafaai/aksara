# Only the last digits matter

The last digit of a product depends only on the last digits of the factors. So you don't compute the huge numbers, you only follow **how the last digit repeats as you multiply**:

| <InlineMath math="n" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| last digit of <InlineMath math="7^n" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="9" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="9" /> |
| last digit of <InlineMath math="3^n" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="9" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="9" /> |

Both last digits repeat with period <InlineMath math="4" />.

# Find the place in the cycle

What matters is the remainder of the exponent on division by <InlineMath math="4" />:

<BlockMath math="2026=4\cdot506+2" />

The exponent leaves remainder <InlineMath math="2" />, so both powers sit **at the second place of the cycle**: <InlineMath math="7^{2026}" /> ends in <InlineMath math="9" />, and <InlineMath math="3^{2026}" /> also ends in <InlineMath math="9" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0" /> generalises <InlineMath math="7+3=10" />. That only works for exponents with remainder <InlineMath math="1" /> or <InlineMath math="3" />, where one last digit is <InlineMath math="7" /> and the other is <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> belongs to an exponent with remainder <InlineMath math="0" />, where both powers end in <InlineMath math="1" />.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> is the last digit of **one** term, not of the sum.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> comes from miscounting the period and taking last digit <InlineMath math="7" /> for <InlineMath math="3^{2026}" />: <InlineMath math="9+7=16" />.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> mixes up the place in the cycle with the last digit and adds the remainder <InlineMath math="2" /> twice: <InlineMath math="2+2=4" />.

# Given

The **sum of two large powers** (*Potenzsumme*) below is written out in the decimal system (*Dezimalsystem*).

<BlockMath math="7^{2026} + 3^{2026}" />

# Asked

Which last digit (*Ziffer*) does this number end with?

# Solution

The units digit of a power acts like a clock hand cycling through just four stops! You never need to expand <InlineMath math="7^{2026}" /> because **the final digit of every power repeats a short cycle forever**.

> The last digit of a product depends only on the last digits of its factors, so find the cycle period and use the remainder of the exponent divided by that period.

## Finding the exponent position and checking wrong choices

Divide the exponent <InlineMath math="2026" /> by the cycle length <InlineMath math="4" /> to pinpoint **its exact stop in the cycle**.

<BlockMath math="2026 = 4 \cdot 506 + 2" />

With remainder <InlineMath math="2" />, both <InlineMath math="7^{2026}" /> and <InlineMath math="3^{2026}" /> end in <InlineMath math="9" />, so their sum ends with the last digit of <InlineMath math="9 + 9 = 18" />, which is <InlineMath math="8" />.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> holds only for odd exponents (remainder <InlineMath math="1" /> or <InlineMath math="3" />), where <InlineMath math="7 + 3 = 10" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> occurs for multiples of <InlineMath math="4" /> (remainder <InlineMath math="0" />), where both terms end in <InlineMath math="1" />.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> is the last digit of a single term before adding the two terms together.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> comes from misreading the <InlineMath math="3^n" /> cycle as <InlineMath math="7" /> and adding <InlineMath math="9 + 7 = 16" />.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> confuses the remainder <InlineMath math="2" /> with the last digit and adds <InlineMath math="2 + 2 = 4" />.

# Answer

The number **<InlineMath math="7^{2026} + 3^{2026}" />** ends with the digit **<InlineMath math="8" />**.

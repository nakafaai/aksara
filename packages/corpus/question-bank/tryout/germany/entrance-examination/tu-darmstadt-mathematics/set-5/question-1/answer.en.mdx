The task asks you to write the difference *als einen einzigen Bruch* (as a single fraction). You can subtract fractions only once they have **the same denominator**. The lowest common denominator of <InlineMath math="x-1" /> and <InlineMath math="x+1" /> is their product, and by the difference of squares <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x+1)=x^2-1" />.

Expand the first fraction by <InlineMath math="x+1" /> and the second by <InlineMath math="x-1" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{2}{x-1}-\frac{1}{x+1}=\frac{2(x+1)}{(x-1)(x+1)}-\frac{x-1}{(x-1)(x+1)}" />

Now subtract the numerators. The minus applies to the whole second numerator, so put it in brackets.

<BlockMath math="\frac{2(x+1)-(x-1)}{x^2-1}=\frac{2x+2-x+1}{x^2-1}=\frac{x+3}{x^2-1}" />

As a check, substitute <InlineMath math="x=2" />. The difference gives <InlineMath math="\frac21-\frac13=\frac53" />, and the fraction gives <InlineMath math="\frac{2+3}{4-1}=\frac53" />.

So the difference equals <InlineMath math="\frac{x+3}{x^2-1}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{x+1}{x^2-1}" /> comes from applying the minus to <InlineMath math="x" /> only. The numerator then reads <InlineMath math="2x+2-x-1" /> instead of <InlineMath math="2x+2-x+1" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{x^2-1}" /> subtracts only the old numerators, <InlineMath math="2-1" />. When you expand a fraction, its numerator has to be multiplied too.
- <InlineMath math="-\frac12" /> comes from subtracting numerators and denominators separately, <InlineMath math="\frac{2-1}{(x-1)-(x+1)}" />. Fractions are not subtracted that way.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{3x+1}{x^2-1}" /> adds the numerators instead of subtracting them, <InlineMath math="2x+2+x-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{x+3}{x^2+1}" /> has the right numerator but the wrong denominator. The product <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x+1)" /> is <InlineMath math="x^2-1" />, not <InlineMath math="x^2+1" />.

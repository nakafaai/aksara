# Divisibility means a zero

A polynomial is divisible by <InlineMath math="(x-1)" /> with no remainder exactly when **<InlineMath math="x=1" /> is a zero**. That gives an equation for <InlineMath math="a" />:

<BlockMath math="p(1)=1+a-5+6=a+2=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=-2" />

# Divide by the known linear factor

With <InlineMath math="a=-2" />, <InlineMath math="p(x)=x^3-2x^2-5x+6" />. Polynomial division by <InlineMath math="(x-1)" /> leaves **a quadratic factor**, which you then factor:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^3-2x^2-5x+6&=(x-1)\left(x^2-x-6\right) \\
x^2-x-6&=(x-3)(x+2)
\end{aligned}" />

Check: multiplying out <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x-3)(x+2)" /> gives <InlineMath math="x^3-2x^2-5x+6" /> again.

Besides <InlineMath math="x=1" />, <InlineMath math="p" /> has **the zeros <InlineMath math="x=-2" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="x=2" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-3" /> are the zeros of <InlineMath math="x^2+x-6" />. That comes from **factoring <InlineMath math="x^2-x-6" /> with both signs swapped**, as <InlineMath math="(x+3)(x-2)" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=\frac{11\pm\sqrt{97}}{2}" /> comes from using <InlineMath math="p(-1)=0" /> instead of <InlineMath math="p(1)=0" />. Then <InlineMath math="a=-10" /> and the remaining factor is <InlineMath math="x^2-11x+6" />.
- "No further zeros" comes from getting <InlineMath math="x^2-x+6" /> in the division. That factor really has no zero, but it's calculated wrongly.
- <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=6" /> are the zeros of <InlineMath math="x^2-5x-6" />. That factor comes from not dividing at all and just copying the coefficients <InlineMath math="-5" /> and <InlineMath math="6" /> of <InlineMath math="p" /> into a quadratic.
- <InlineMath math="x=2" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" /> are the zeros of <InlineMath math="x^2-5x+6" />, a wrong remaining factor with swapped signs.

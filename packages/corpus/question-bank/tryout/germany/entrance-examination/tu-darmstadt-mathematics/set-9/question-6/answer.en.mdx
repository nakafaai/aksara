# Given

An ant sits on one corner of a cube (*Würfel*) with **edge length <InlineMath math="2\text{ cm}" />**.

The ant may crawl only along the **surface of the cube** to the farthest opposite corner.

# Asked

What is the length of the shortest surface path (*kürzester Weg*) the ant can take?

# Solution

On a box, it is tempting to trace edges or walk a face diagonal plus an edge. The trick is to **unfold the cardboard cube flat onto a table**. Once both crossed faces lie side by side, the shortest path is simply **one straight line** across the middle fold.

> The shortest path across the surface of a solid always becomes a straight line when the crossed faces are unfolded flat into a net.

## Unfolding two adjacent faces into a rectangle

To reach the opposite corner, the ant crosses **two adjacent square faces**. Laid flat, those two <InlineMath math="2\text{ cm}" /> squares merge into a single rectangle with:

- Width <InlineMath math="2\text{ cm}" />.
- Combined length <InlineMath math="2 + 2 = 4\text{ cm}" />.

The start corner <InlineMath math="A" /> and target corner <InlineMath math="B" /> sit at opposite corners of this rectangle.

Notice that the straight line crosses the shared edge right at its midpoint, not at a corner.

## Computing the diagonal and comparing routes

Apply the **Pythagorean theorem** to the <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> by <InlineMath math="2\text{ cm}" /> rectangle:

<BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{4^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{16 + 4} = \sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}\text{ cm}" />

Route comparison: walking three edges takes <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />, and walking one edge plus one face diagonal takes about <InlineMath math="4.83\text{ cm}" />. Our straight net diagonal is only about <InlineMath math="4.47\text{ cm}" />, beating both.

## Checking the distractors

See which path **each wrong choice measures**:

| Option | Why it is wrong |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}" /> | The space diagonal tunneling straight through the interior, forbidden for a surface-crawling ant. |
| <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> | Follows three full edges, much longer than cutting straight across the net. |
| <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> | Only two edges, reaching the opposite corner of one face rather than the whole cube. |
| <InlineMath math="(2 + 2\sqrt{2})\text{ cm}" /> | Follows one edge then one face diagonal (about <InlineMath math="4.83\text{ cm}" />), longer than <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{5}\text{ cm}" />. |
| <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}" /> | Just one face diagonal, ending on the same face instead of the opposite cube corner. |

# Answer

The shortest path along the cube's surface has length **<InlineMath math="2\sqrt{5}\text{ cm}" />**.

# The perpendicular from <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="g" />

In a reflection in a line, a point and its image lie **on a perpendicular to the mirror line**, equally far from it. The line <InlineMath math="g" /> has slope <InlineMath math="2" />, so the perpendicular has slope <InlineMath math="-\frac12" />. Through <InlineMath math="P(5\mid0)" />:

<BlockMath math="y=-\frac12(x-5)" />

# The foot of the perpendicular

The intersection with <InlineMath math="g" /> is **the midpoint between <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="P'" />**:

<BlockMath math="2x=-\frac12(x-5)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4x=-x+5\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=1,\ y=2" />

So <InlineMath math="F(1\mid2)" />.

# The image through the midpoint

<InlineMath math="F" /> is the midpoint of <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="P'" />, so <InlineMath math="P'=2F-P" />:

<BlockMath math="P'=\left(2\cdot1-5\ \middle|\ 2\cdot2-0\right)=(-3\mid4)" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="P'(1\mid2)" /> is only **the foot of the perpendicular**, the midpoint, not the image.
- <InlineMath math="P'(0\mid5)" /> reflects in the line <InlineMath math="y=x" /> instead of <InlineMath math="y=2x" />.
- <InlineMath math="P'(-5\mid0)" /> reflects in the origin, or in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis.
- <InlineMath math="P'(3\mid-4)" /> has both signs flipped: <InlineMath math="P-2F=(3\mid-4)" /> instead of <InlineMath math="2F-P" />.
- <InlineMath math="P'(-4\mid3)" /> swaps the coordinates of the correct image.

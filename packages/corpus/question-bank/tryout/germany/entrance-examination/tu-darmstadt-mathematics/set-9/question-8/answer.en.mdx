# Get the unknown out of the exponent

The bases <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> can't be made equal. So separate the powers so that **all powers with <InlineMath math="x" /> are on one side**:

<BlockMath math="2^{x+1}=3^{x-1}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2\cdot2^x=\frac{3^x}{3}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 6=\frac{3^x}{2^x}=\left(\frac32\right)^x" />

# Take logarithms

The equation <InlineMath math="\left(\frac32\right)^x=6" /> has **exactly one solution**, the logarithm of <InlineMath math="6" /> to base <InlineMath math="\frac32" />:

<BlockMath math="x=\log_{\frac32}6=\frac{\lg6}{\lg1.5}\approx4.42" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\log_{\frac23}6" /> comes from swapping the exponents, solving <InlineMath math="2^{x-1}=3^{x+1}" />. That's **a negative number**.
- <InlineMath math="\log_6\frac32" /> swaps the base and the argument of the logarithm.
- <InlineMath math="\log_{\frac32}3" /> forgets the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> from <InlineMath math="2^{x+1}" /> when rearranging: <InlineMath math="3^{x}=3\cdot2^x" />.
- <InlineMath math="-1" /> makes the left side <InlineMath math="2^0=1" />, but the right side <InlineMath math="3^{-2}=\frac19" />. Checking only one side misses that.
- "No real number" overlooks that <InlineMath math="3^{x-1}" /> grows faster and overtakes <InlineMath math="2^{x+1}" /> exactly once.

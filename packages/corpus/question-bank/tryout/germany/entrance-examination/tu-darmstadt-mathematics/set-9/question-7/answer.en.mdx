# The changes as factors

The <InlineMath math="25\,\%" /> increase multiplies the price by <InlineMath math="1.25" />. In the end it should be <InlineMath math="10\,\%" /> below the old price, so <InlineMath math="0.9" /> times it. You want the cut by the share <InlineMath math="s" />, **measured against the raised price**:

<BlockMath math="1.25\,(1-s)=0.9" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="35\,\%" /> adds the percentage points <InlineMath math="25+10" />. But the cut is measured **against the larger base <InlineMath math="125" />**.
- <InlineMath math="30\,\%" /> takes <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> to undo the increase plus another <InlineMath math="10\,\%" />.
- <InlineMath math="72\,\%" /> is the remaining share <InlineMath math="\frac{0.9}{1.25}" />, not the cut.
- <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> only brings the price back to the start: <InlineMath math="1.25\cdot0.8=1" />.
- <InlineMath math="25\,\%" /> just reverses the increase: <InlineMath math="1.25\cdot0.75=0.9375" />.

# Given

Square <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> has **side length <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" />**.

- Point <InlineMath math="M" /> is the midpoint of top edge <InlineMath math="\overline{CD}" /> (so <InlineMath math="\overline{DM} = 2\text{ cm}" />).
- Segments <InlineMath math="\overline{AM}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{BD}" /> intersect at point <InlineMath math="S" />.

# Asked

What is the area (*Flächeninhalt*) of triangle <InlineMath math="ABS" />?

# Solution

Look at the two triangles meeting tip-to-tip at <InlineMath math="S" /> like an hourglass: the larger triangle <InlineMath math="ABS" /> at the bottom and the smaller triangle <InlineMath math="MDS" /> at the top. Because the bottom edge <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" /> is parallel to the top segment <InlineMath math="\overline{DM}" />, **the two triangles are similar in a two-to-one scale**. That splits the <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> height of the square in the exact same <InlineMath math="2 : 1" /> ratio.

> Two hourglass triangles between parallel sides are always similar, so their altitudes split the distance between those sides in the ratio of their bases.

## Using similarity to find the triangle height

Since <InlineMath math="\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{DM}" />, triangle <InlineMath math="ABS" /> is similar to triangle <InlineMath math="MDS" />. **The ratio of their parallel bases is exactly two**:

<BlockMath math="\frac{\overline{AB}}{\overline{DM}} = \frac{4}{2} = 2" />

Their two vertical altitudes add up to the full square side of <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" />. Splitting <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> in the ratio <InlineMath math="2 : 1" /> gives the larger triangle **two thirds of the total height**:

<BlockMath math="h_{ABS} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 4 = \frac{8}{3}\text{ cm}" />

The diagram confirms that <InlineMath math="S" /> sits above the horizontal midline, two thirds of the way up the square.

## Computing the area and checking with coordinates

Plug base <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> and height <InlineMath math="\frac{8}{3}\text{ cm}" /> into **the triangle area formula**:

<BlockMath math="A = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot \frac{8}{3} = \frac{16}{3}\text{ cm}^2" />

As a coordinate check, intersecting both segments gives:

<BlockMath math="2x = 4 - x \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x = \frac{4}{3}, \quad y = \frac{8}{3}" />

This matches the altitude and triangle area exactly.

# Answer

The area of triangle <InlineMath math="ABS" /> is **<InlineMath math="\frac{16}{3}\text{ cm}^2" />**.

# Two similar triangles at the intersection

The sides <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{DM}" /> are parallel. So triangles <InlineMath math="ABS" /> and <InlineMath math="MDS" /> are **similar**, with <InlineMath math="S" /> as their common tip. The ratio of the parallel sides is

<BlockMath math="\frac{\overline{AB}}{\overline{DM}}=\frac{4}{2}=2" />

# The height of triangle <InlineMath math="ABS" />

The two triangles' heights lie on one vertical line and add up to the side length <InlineMath math="4" />. They are **in the same ratio <InlineMath math="2:1" />**:

<BlockMath math="h_{ABS}=\frac23\cdot4=\frac83" />

# The area

<BlockMath math="A=\frac12\cdot4\cdot\frac83=\frac{16}{3}\text{ cm}^2" />

Check with coordinates: <InlineMath math="\overline{AM}" /> lies on <InlineMath math="y=2x" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{BD}" /> on <InlineMath math="y=4-x" />. That gives <InlineMath math="x=\frac43" />, <InlineMath math="y=\frac83" />. The triangle has **an area of <InlineMath math="\frac{16}{3}\text{ cm}^2" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}^2" /> puts <InlineMath math="S" /> at **the centre of the square**, with height <InlineMath math="2" />. That would be where the diagonals meet.
- <InlineMath math="8\text{ cm}^2" /> uses the whole side <InlineMath math="4" /> as the height.
- <InlineMath math="\frac83\text{ cm}^2" /> is the triangle with height <InlineMath math="\frac43" />, that is with the ratio reversed to <InlineMath math="1:2" />.
- <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}^2" /> uses height <InlineMath math="3" />, as if <InlineMath math="S" /> split the segment in the ratio <InlineMath math="3:1" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{32}{3}\text{ cm}^2" /> forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> in the triangle area.

# Given

For every real number <InlineMath math="x" />, we define a **sum of three absolute values** (*Term*):

<BlockMath math="T(x) = \lvert x - 1 \rvert + \lvert x - 4 \rvert + \lvert x - 6 \rvert" />

# Asked

What is the **smallest** value that <InlineMath math="T(x)" /> can take?

# Solution

Imagine three houses on a straight road at <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="4" />, and <InlineMath math="6" />, and you place one mailbox at <InlineMath math="x" /> to **minimize total walking distance**. Anywhere between <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="6" />, the combined distance to both outer houses stays constant at <InlineMath math="5" />. To zero out the middle distance too, **stand at <InlineMath math="x = 4" />**.

> The sum of distances from a point to three points on a number line always achieves its minimum at the median, the middle point.

## Pairing the outer points and the middle point

On the number line, <InlineMath math="\lvert x - a \rvert" /> is the distance between <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="a" />. Group <InlineMath math="T(x)" /> into **the outer pair and the middle term**:

<BlockMath math="T(x) = \bigl(\lvert x - 1 \rvert + \lvert x - 6 \rvert\bigr) + \lvert x - 4 \rvert" />

- For any <InlineMath math="x" /> between <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="6" />, the distances to both endpoints add up to exactly <InlineMath math="6 - 1 = 5" />. Outside that segment, their sum is strictly larger than <InlineMath math="5" />.
- Meanwhile, <InlineMath math="\lvert x - 4 \rvert" /> is never negative and **reaches zero right at <InlineMath math="x = 4" />**.

## Proving the lower bound on the graph

Since the outer pair is always at least <InlineMath math="5" /> and the middle term is never negative, **we have the strict lower bound <InlineMath math="T(x) \ge 5" />**, attained at <InlineMath math="x = 4" />:

<BlockMath math="T(4) = \lvert 4 - 1 \rvert + \lvert 4 - 4 \rvert + \lvert 4 - 6 \rvert = 3 + 0 + 2 = 5" />

# Answer

The smallest value that <InlineMath math="T(x)" /> can take is **<InlineMath math="5" />**.

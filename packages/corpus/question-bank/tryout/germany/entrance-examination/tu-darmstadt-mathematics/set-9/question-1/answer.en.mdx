# Read absolute values as distances

<InlineMath math="|x-a|" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="x" /> to the number <InlineMath math="a" /> on the number line. So <InlineMath math="T(x)" /> is **the sum of the distances from <InlineMath math="x" /> to <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="6" />**. You want the place where this sum is smallest.

# The distances to the outer points

For every <InlineMath math="x" /> between <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="6" />, <InlineMath math="|x-1|+|x-6|=6-1=5" />, because the two distances add up to the length of the segment. Outside that segment the sum is larger. On top of that comes <InlineMath math="|x-4|" />, and **that part is zero at <InlineMath math="x=4" />**:

<BlockMath math="T(4)=3+0+2=5" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{16}{3}" /> is the value at the **mean** <InlineMath math="x=\frac{11}{3}" /> of the three numbers. But the sum of distances is smallest at the middle value, the median.
- <InlineMath math="7" /> is the value at <InlineMath math="x=6" />.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> is the value at <InlineMath math="x=1" />.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> would require <InlineMath math="x" /> to be <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="6" /> at once.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> is only the distance from <InlineMath math="4" /> to <InlineMath math="1" />, a single term.

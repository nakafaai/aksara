# Write the area in terms of one length

Call the two sides perpendicular to the wall <InlineMath math="x" /> and the side parallel to the wall <InlineMath math="y" />. The fence gives <InlineMath math="2x+y=60" />, so <InlineMath math="y=60-2x" />. The area is then **a quadratic function of <InlineMath math="x" />**:

<BlockMath math="A(x)=x\,(60-2x)=-2x^2+60x=-2(x-15)^2+450" />

# The condition on the wall side

Without a condition the vertex would be at <InlineMath math="x=15" /> with <InlineMath math="A=450" />. But then <InlineMath math="y=60-30=30" />, which is **longer than the allowed <InlineMath math="24\text{ m}" />**. The condition <InlineMath math="y\le24" /> means:

<BlockMath math="60-2x\le24\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\ge18" />

# The maximum at the boundary

For <InlineMath math="x\ge18" /> the vertex is off to the left, and the parabola falls. The largest allowed value is **at the boundary <InlineMath math="x=18" />**:

The enclosure can be at most <InlineMath math="432\text{ m}^2" />, with <InlineMath math="18\text{ m}" /> perpendicular and <InlineMath math="24\text{ m}" /> parallel to the wall.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="450\text{ m}^2" /> is the vertex value, where the wall side would be <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}" />. **It breaks the condition**.
- <InlineMath math="576\text{ m}^2" /> is a square with side <InlineMath math="24" />. That would need <InlineMath math="72\text{ m}" /> of fence.
- <InlineMath math="360\text{ m}^2" /> combines <InlineMath math="x=15" /> from the vertex with <InlineMath math="y=24" />. That uses only <InlineMath math="54\text{ m}" /> of fence.
- <InlineMath math="288\text{ m}^2" /> takes <InlineMath math="24\cdot12" />, a wrong second side.
- <InlineMath math="225\text{ m}^2" /> is a square with side <InlineMath math="15" />, with no link to the fence.

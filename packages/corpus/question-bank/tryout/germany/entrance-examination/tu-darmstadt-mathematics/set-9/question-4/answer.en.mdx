# Given

A rectangular enclosure (*rechteckiges Gehege*) is built along a long straight wall:

- The wall forms one side, while the other three sides use **<InlineMath math="60\text{ m}" /> of fencing in total**.
- The side parallel to the wall (<InlineMath math="y" />) may be **at most <InlineMath math="24\text{ m}" /> long** (<InlineMath math="y \le 24" />).

# Asked

What is the largest possible area (*Flächeninhalt*) of the enclosure?

# Solution

Without any cap on the wall side, the largest three-sided pen always makes the wall side twice as long as each side wing: <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}" /> by <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" />. The catch is that the rule **forbids the wall side from exceeding <InlineMath math="24\text{ m}" />**. Because the unconstrained peak lands in the forbidden zone, the best legal pen must sit **right against the boundary <InlineMath math="y = 24\text{ m}" />**.

> When the vertex of a downward parabola falls outside the allowed domain, the constrained maximum always occurs at the closest legal boundary point.

## Writing the area function and fence constraint

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be each side perpendicular to the wall and <InlineMath math="y" /> the side parallel to it. The fencing gives <InlineMath math="2x + y = 60" />, so <InlineMath math="y = 60 - 2x" />. **The area forms a downward-opening parabola**:

<BlockMath math="A(x) = x(60 - 2x) = -2x^2 + 60x = -2(x - 15)^2 + 450" />

Its unconstrained vertex at <InlineMath math="x = 15\text{ m}" /> needs <InlineMath math="y = 30\text{ m}" />, breaking <InlineMath math="y \le 24\text{ m}" />. Convert the wall limit into a condition on <InlineMath math="x" />:

<BlockMath math="60 - 2x \le 24 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad 2x \ge 36 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad x \ge 18" />

## Finding the maximum at the boundary

On the legal region <InlineMath math="x \ge 18" />, the entire curve lies to the right of the vertex <InlineMath math="x = 15" />, so **area strictly decreases as <InlineMath math="x" /> grows**. The largest allowed area sits right at <InlineMath math="x = 18" />.

Evaluate the area at <InlineMath math="x = 18\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="y = 24\text{ m}" />:

<BlockMath math="A(18) = 18 \cdot (60 - 36) = 18 \cdot 24 = 432\text{ m}^2" />

Two <InlineMath math="18\text{ m}" /> sides plus the <InlineMath math="24\text{ m}" /> side use all <InlineMath math="60\text{ m}" /> of fencing while respecting the wall cap.

# Answer

The largest possible area of the enclosure is **<InlineMath math="432\text{ m}^2" />**.

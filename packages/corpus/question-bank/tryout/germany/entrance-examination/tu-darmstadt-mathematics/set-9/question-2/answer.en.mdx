# Domain and squaring

The root needs <InlineMath math="x\ge-3" />. Both sides are never negative, so you may **square without splitting cases**, and <InlineMath math="|x-3|^2=(x-3)^2" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x-3)^2&=x+3 \\
x^2-7x+6&=0 \\
(x-1)(x-6)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

# Check both candidates

Because both sides were non-negative, squaring is reversible here. Still, **checking in the original equation** is worth it:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x=1:&\quad |1-3|=2,\quad \sqrt{4}=2 \\
x=6:&\quad |6-3|=3,\quad \sqrt{9}=3
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the absolute value matters

Without the absolute value the equation would be <InlineMath math="x-3=\sqrt{x+3}" />. For <InlineMath math="x=1" /> the left side would be <InlineMath math="-2" />, and **that solution would drop out**. With the absolute value both solutions stay: <InlineMath math="\mathbb{L}=\{1;\,6\}" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\{6\}" /> **treats <InlineMath math="|x-3|" /> like <InlineMath math="x-3" />** and so throws away <InlineMath math="x=1" />.
- <InlineMath math="\{1\}" /> throws away the larger solution for no reason.
- <InlineMath math="\{-1;\,6\}" /> comes from a sign slip when factoring: <InlineMath math="(x+1)(x-6)=x^2-5x-6" />.
- <InlineMath math="\{-6;\,-1\}" /> factors <InlineMath math="x^2+7x+6" /> instead of <InlineMath math="x^2-7x+6" />. Besides, <InlineMath math="-6<-3" /> isn't in the domain.
- <InlineMath math="\{\}" /> overlooks that both checks work out.

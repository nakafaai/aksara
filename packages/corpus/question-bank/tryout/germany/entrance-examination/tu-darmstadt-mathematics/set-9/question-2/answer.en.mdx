The task says the sum of a number and its reciprocal (*Kehrwert*) is <InlineMath math="\frac{10}{3}" />. The fraction <InlineMath math="\frac1x" /> disappears when you **multiply the equation by the common denominator <InlineMath math="3x" />**. That is allowed because <InlineMath math="x\ne0" />. A quadratic equation results.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3x^2+3&=10x \\
3x^2-10x+3&=0 \\
(3x-1)(x-3)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

A product is zero when a factor is zero. From <InlineMath math="3x-1=0" /> you get <InlineMath math="x=\frac13" />, and from <InlineMath math="x-3=0" /> you get <InlineMath math="x=3" />.

As a check, substitute both values: <InlineMath math="3+\frac13=\frac{10}{3}" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac13+3=\frac{10}{3}" />. That also makes sense, because a number and its reciprocal just swap roles in the sum.

So the numbers that satisfy the condition are <InlineMath math="x=3" /> and <InlineMath math="x=\frac13" />.

- Only <InlineMath math="x=3" /> misses the second solution. If <InlineMath math="3" /> works, so does its reciprocal <InlineMath math="\frac13" />.
- Only <InlineMath math="x=\frac13" /> also misses the second solution, <InlineMath math="x=3" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=-3" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-\frac13" /> have the wrong sign. Their sum with the reciprocal is <InlineMath math="-\frac{10}{3}" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=\frac{10}{3}" /> takes the sum itself as the number. Then <InlineMath math="x+\frac1x=\frac{10}{3}+\frac{3}{10}\ne\frac{10}{3}" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=3" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-\frac13" /> has a sign error in the second solution. For <InlineMath math="-\frac13" /> you get <InlineMath math="-\frac13-3=-\frac{10}{3}" />.

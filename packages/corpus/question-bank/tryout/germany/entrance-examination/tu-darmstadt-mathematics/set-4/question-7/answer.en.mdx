# Percentage changes as factors

Write the rate you want as a decimal <InlineMath math="p" />. The increase multiplies the price by <InlineMath math="1+p" />. The cut by twice the rate refers to **the already increased price** and multiplies by <InlineMath math="1-2p" />. In the end the price is <InlineMath math="12\,\%" /> lower, so it is <InlineMath math="0.88" /> times the original:

<BlockMath math="(1+p)(1-2p)=0.88" />

# Solve the quadratic equation

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1-p-2p^2&=0.88 \\
2p^2+p-0.12&=0 \\
p&=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1+0.96}}{4}=\frac{-1\pm1.4}{4}
\end{aligned}" />

That gives **<InlineMath math="p=0.1" /> or <InlineMath math="p=-0.6" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12\,\%" /> works linearly: <InlineMath math="p-2p=-12\,\%" />. That measures the cut against the old price instead of the raised one.
- <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> is the rate of the **cut**, not of the increase.
- <InlineMath math="60\,\%" /> is the size of the negative solution <InlineMath math="p=-0.6" />, which describes no increase.
- <InlineMath math="6\,\%" /> halves the <InlineMath math="12\,\%" />.
- <InlineMath math="4\,\%" /> divides the <InlineMath math="12\,\%" /> by three, as if the increase and the cut together were a change of <InlineMath math="3p=12\,\%" />.

# Given

In a **right triangle** (*rechtwinkliges Dreieck*), the hypotenuse (*Hypotenuse*) measures <InlineMath math="13\text{ cm}" /> and the altitude to the hypotenuse (*Höhe auf die Hypotenuse*) measures <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />.

# Asked

Find the perimeter (*Umfang*) of this right triangle.

# Solution

Dropping the altitude from the right angle splits the triangle into two smaller mini-copies of itself! That built-in similarity gives two classic right-triangle laws, the **altitude theorem** (*Höhensatz*) and the **leg theorem** (*Kathetensatz*), letting us break the <InlineMath math="13\text{ cm}" /> hypotenuse into two pieces <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" />.

> In a right triangle, the altitude squared equals the product of the two hypotenuse segments, and each leg squared equals the whole hypotenuse times its adjacent segment.

The two segments add up to <InlineMath math="13" /> and multiply to the altitude squared, <InlineMath math="36" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
p + q &= 13, \qquad p \cdot q = h^2 = 36 \\
p(13 - p) &= 36 \iff p^2 - 13p + 36 = 0 \iff (p - 4)(p - 9) = 0
\end{aligned}" />

So the two hypotenuse segments are <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" />.

With segments <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="9" /> laid out visually, both legs follow in a single step.

## Finding both legs and the perimeter

Apply **the leg theorem** to each segment, then add all three sides to find the perimeter.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2 &= 13 \cdot 4 = 52 \implies a = 2\sqrt{13} \\
b^2 &= 13 \cdot 9 = 117 \implies b = 3\sqrt{13} \\
u &= 13 + 2\sqrt{13} + 3\sqrt{13} = (13 + 5\sqrt{13})\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Pythagorean check: the leg squares sum to <InlineMath math="52 + 117 = 169" />, matching <InlineMath math="13^2" /> exactly.

## Checking wrong choices

Notice how each distractor breaks **the area or segment relationship of the right triangle**.

- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> guesses the familiar triple <InlineMath math="5" />, <InlineMath math="12" />, <InlineMath math="13" />, yet its altitude is <InlineMath math="\frac{60}{13}" /> rather than <InlineMath math="6" />.
- <InlineMath math="26\text{ cm}" /> adds the base segments <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="9" /> to the hypotenuse as if they were the legs.
- <InlineMath math="(13 + \sqrt{481})\text{ cm}" /> mistakenly doubles <InlineMath math="c \cdot h" /> for the leg product instead of using <InlineMath math="78" />.
- <InlineMath math="5\sqrt{13}\text{ cm}" /> sums only the two legs and leaves out the <InlineMath math="13\text{ cm}" /> hypotenuse.
- <InlineMath math="(13 + 2\sqrt{13})\text{ cm}" /> includes only one leg alongside the hypotenuse.

# Answer

The perimeter of the right triangle is **<InlineMath math="(13 + 5\sqrt{13})\text{ cm}" />**.

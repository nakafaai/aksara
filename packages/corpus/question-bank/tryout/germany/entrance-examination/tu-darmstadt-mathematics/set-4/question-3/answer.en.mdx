# Domain and case split

The root is only defined for <InlineMath math="x+5\ge0" />, that is <InlineMath math="x\ge-5" />. Squaring is only a safe step when both sides are non-negative. So split into two cases by **the sign of the right side** <InlineMath math="x-1" />.

# Case 1: the right side is negative

For <InlineMath math="-5\le x<1" /> we have <InlineMath math="x-1<0" />. A root is never negative, so the inequality holds here **without any calculation**. Every <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="-5\le x<1" /> is a solution.

# Case 2: the right side is non-negative

For <InlineMath math="x\ge1" /> both sides are non-negative, and **you may square**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x+5&>(x-1)^2=x^2-2x+1 \\
0&>x^2-3x-4=(x-4)(x+1) \\
-1&<x<4
\end{aligned}" />

Together with <InlineMath math="x\ge1" /> this leaves **<InlineMath math="1\le x<4" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-1<x<4" /> squares without splitting cases and **loses the numbers from <InlineMath math="-5" /> to <InlineMath math="-1" />**, where the right side is negative.
- <InlineMath math="1\le x<4" /> works out case 2 only.
- <InlineMath math="x<4" /> forgets the domain <InlineMath math="x\ge-5" />.
- <InlineMath math="-5\le x\le4" /> includes <InlineMath math="x=4" />. There both sides equal <InlineMath math="3" />, and the inequality is strict.
- <InlineMath math="x>4" /> turns the result around. To the right of <InlineMath math="4" /> the line lies above the root.

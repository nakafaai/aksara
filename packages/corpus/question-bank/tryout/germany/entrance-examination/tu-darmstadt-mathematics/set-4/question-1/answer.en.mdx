# Rationalise each fraction

Each term has the form <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{\sqrt k+\sqrt{k+1}}" /> with <InlineMath math="k=1,2,\ldots,15" />. Multiply top and bottom by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{k+1}-\sqrt k" />. The difference of squares turns the denominator into <InlineMath math="1" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{\sqrt k+\sqrt{k+1}}=\frac{\sqrt{k+1}-\sqrt k}{(k+1)-k}=\sqrt{k+1}-\sqrt k" />

So each fraction is **a difference of two neighbouring roots**.

# The sum collapses

Write the sum with these differences. Every root from <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" /> to <InlineMath math="\sqrt{15}" /> appears once with a plus and once with a minus:

<BlockMath math="\left(\sqrt2-\sqrt1\right)+\left(\sqrt3-\sqrt2\right)+\ldots+\left(\sqrt{16}-\sqrt{15}\right)=\sqrt{16}-\sqrt1=4-1=3" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-3" /> comes from multiplying by <InlineMath math="\sqrt k-\sqrt{k+1}" /> and still reading the denominator as <InlineMath math="+1" />. But every term is **positive**, so the sum is too.
- <InlineMath math="4-\sqrt2" /> drops the first fraction and starts the chain at <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" />.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{17}-1" /> counts one fraction too many, up to <InlineMath math="\sqrt{17}" />.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> forgets that <InlineMath math="\sqrt1=1" /> is subtracted at the start.
- <InlineMath math="15" /> is the number of fractions, not their sum.

# Work with rates per hour

You can't simply add times, but you can add rates. If the weaker pump alone needs <InlineMath math="x" /> hours, it fills <InlineMath math="\frac1x" /> of the tank per hour. The stronger one needs <InlineMath math="x-5" /> hours and manages <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{x-5}" /> per hour. Together they fill **<InlineMath math="\frac16" /> of the tank per hour**:

<BlockMath math="\frac1x+\frac{1}{x-5}=\frac16" />

# Solve the equation

Multiply by **the common denominator <InlineMath math="6x(x-5)" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
6(x-5)+6x&=x(x-5) \\
12x-30&=x^2-5x \\
x^2-17x+30&=0 \\
(x-15)(x-2)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

# Pick the solution that fits

The stronger pump needs <InlineMath math="x-5" /> hours, and **that time must be positive**. For <InlineMath math="x=2" /> it would be <InlineMath math="-3" /> hours, so that solution drops out:

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10" /> hours is the time of the **stronger** pump.
- <InlineMath math="11" /> hours adds the <InlineMath math="5" /> hours to the joint time.
- <InlineMath math="8.5" /> hours treats <InlineMath math="6" /> hours as the average of the two times: <InlineMath math="\frac{x+(x-5)}{2}=6" />.
- "<InlineMath math="2" /> hours or <InlineMath math="15" /> hours" overlooks that for <InlineMath math="x=2" /> the stronger pump would need a negative time.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> hours assumes each pump alone takes twice as long as both together. That only holds for two equally strong pumps.

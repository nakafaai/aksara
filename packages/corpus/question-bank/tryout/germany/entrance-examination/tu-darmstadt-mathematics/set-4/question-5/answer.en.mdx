# Given

Two **water pumps** (*Pumpen*) running at constant rates fill an empty tank together in <InlineMath math="6" /> hours. Working alone, the weaker pump needs <InlineMath math="5" /> hours longer than the stronger pump.

# Asked

How long would the weaker pump take to fill the empty tank on its own?

# Solution

Two painters working on the same wall finish faster than either painter alone, so you never add their total times. What adds up cleanly is **the hourly fill rate**, meaning the fraction of the tank filled each hour!

> In combined-work problems, convert each time into a rate per hour first, add the rates together, and discard any algebraic root that gives a negative time.

Let the weaker pump take <InlineMath math="x" /> hours alone, so the stronger pump takes <InlineMath math="x - 5" /> hours. Together they fill <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{6}" /> of the tank every hour, yielding the rate equation below.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{x - 5} = \frac{1}{6}" />

## Solving the rate equation and filtering physical roots

Multiply both sides by **the common denominator <InlineMath math="6x(x - 5)" />** to turn the equation into a quadratic.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
6(x - 5) + 6x &= x(x - 5) \\
12x - 30 &= x^2 - 5x \\
x^2 - 17x + 30 &= 0 \\
(x - 15)(x - 2) &= 0
\end{aligned}" />

This produces two algebraic roots, <InlineMath math="x = 15" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 2" />. Because the stronger pump's time <InlineMath math="x - 5" /> must be positive (<InlineMath math="x > 5" />), we reject <InlineMath math="x = 2" /> since it would require <InlineMath math="-3" /> hours.

## Verifying the result and checking wrong choices

Check <InlineMath math="15" /> hours and <InlineMath math="10" /> hours directly: **their hourly rates add up to exactly one sixth of the tank**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{2 + 3}{30} = \frac{5}{30} = \frac{1}{6}" />

- <InlineMath math="10" /> hours is the time for the stronger pump rather than the weaker one.
- <InlineMath math="11" /> hours simply adds the <InlineMath math="5" />-hour difference onto the combined <InlineMath math="6" /> hours.
- <InlineMath math="8.5" /> hours mistakenly treats <InlineMath math="6" /> hours as the arithmetic average of the two individual times.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> hours or <InlineMath math="15" /> hours forgets to eliminate <InlineMath math="x = 2" />, which implies a negative time for the stronger pump.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> hours assumes both pumps are equally strong, doubling the combined time.

# Answer

The weaker pump alone needs <InlineMath math="15" /> hours to fill the tank.

The task asks for the length of a *Raumdiagonale* (space diagonal) of the cuboid. You find it with **Pythagoras' theorem twice**. First go diagonally across the base, then straight up from there to the opposite corner.

Take the base with the edges <InlineMath math="2\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}" />. Its diagonal <InlineMath math="d" /> is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with these two edges.

<BlockMath math="d^2=2^2+3^2=4+9=13" />

The space diagonal <InlineMath math="e" /> is the hypotenuse of a second right triangle. Its legs are the diagonal <InlineMath math="d" /> and the vertical edge <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />, which is perpendicular to the base.

<BlockMath math="e^2=d^2+6^2=13+36=49\quad\Rightarrow\quad e=7" />

Together this is <InlineMath math="e=\sqrt{2^2+3^2+6^2}" />. As a check, <InlineMath math="e" /> must be longer than every edge and every face diagonal. The longest face diagonal is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{3^2+6^2}=\sqrt{45}\approx6.7" />, and <InlineMath math="7" /> is larger.

So the space diagonal is <InlineMath math="7\text{ cm}" /> long.

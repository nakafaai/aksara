# First the case without the squared term

For <InlineMath math="a=1" /> the <InlineMath math="x^2" /> disappears and the line <InlineMath math="f(x)=2x+3" /> is left. **A line that isn't horizontal becomes negative somewhere**, for example <InlineMath math="f(-2)=-1" />. So <InlineMath math="a=1" /> is out.

# Conditions for a parabola

For <InlineMath math="a\ne1" /> the graph is a parabola. It lies entirely above the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis exactly when it **opens upwards and has no zeros**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a-1&>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a>1 \\
D=(2a)^2-4(a-1)(a+2)&=4a^2-4\left(a^2+a-2\right)=-4a+8<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a>2
\end{aligned}" />

# Both conditions together

Both hold exactly for <InlineMath math="a>2" />. For <InlineMath math="a=2" />, <InlineMath math="D=0" />: the graph **touches the axis**, and <InlineMath math="f(-2)=0" /> isn't positive.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="a>1" /> checks only which way the parabola opens. For <InlineMath math="a=1.5" />, <InlineMath math="D=2>0" />, and the parabola **has zeros**.
- <InlineMath math="a\ge2" /> includes <InlineMath math="a=2" />, where the graph touches the axis.
- <InlineMath math="1<a<2" /> reverses the discriminant condition.
- <InlineMath math="a=1" /> or <InlineMath math="a>2" /> overlooks that the line for <InlineMath math="a=1" /> takes negative values.
- <InlineMath math="a<2" /> uses only <InlineMath math="D<0" /> with the inequality the wrong way round and ignores the opening.

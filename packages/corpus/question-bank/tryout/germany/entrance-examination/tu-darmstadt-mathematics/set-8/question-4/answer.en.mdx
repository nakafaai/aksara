# The area from the coordinates

If one corner is at the origin, the triangle with the other corners <InlineMath math="(x_1\mid y_1)" /> and <InlineMath math="(x_2\mid y_2)" /> has area <InlineMath math="\frac12\left|x_1y_2-x_2y_1\right|" />. **The absolute value matters**, because without it the expression can be negative, depending on the orientation:

<BlockMath math="A=\frac12\left|6\cdot6-k\cdot2\right|=\frac12\left|36-2k\right|" />

# Solve the absolute value equation

<BlockMath math="\frac12\left|36-2k\right|=15\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left|36-2k\right|=30" />

An absolute value is <InlineMath math="30" /> when **what's inside is <InlineMath math="30" /> or <InlineMath math="-30" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
36-2k=30&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=3 \\
36-2k=-30&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=33
\end{aligned}" />

# Check

For <InlineMath math="k=3" />, <InlineMath math="\frac12|36-6|=15" />, and for <InlineMath math="k=33" />, <InlineMath math="\frac12|36-66|=15" />. Both values give a real triangle, so **<InlineMath math="k=3" /> or <InlineMath math="k=33" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- "only <InlineMath math="k=3" />" works without the absolute value and **loses the second position**.
- "only <InlineMath math="k=33" />" loses the first position.
- <InlineMath math="k=10.5" /> or <InlineMath math="k=25.5" /> forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\frac12" />: these values come from <InlineMath math="|36-2k|=15" />, and the triangle would have area <InlineMath math="7.5" />.
- <InlineMath math="k=-3" /> or <InlineMath math="k=3" /> puts the absolute value around <InlineMath math="k" /> instead of around the whole expression.
- "only <InlineMath math="k=18" />" is the value where <InlineMath math="36-2k=0" />. Then the three points are on one line and there's no triangle.

# Make the bases equal

Write both sides as powers of <InlineMath math="\frac13" />. Since <InlineMath math="\frac19=\left(\frac13\right)^2" />, <InlineMath math="\left(\frac19\right)^x=\left(\frac13\right)^{2x}" />. Now **both sides are powers of the same base**:

<BlockMath math="\left(\frac13\right)^{x^2-8}\le\left(\frac13\right)^{2x}" />

# A base below <InlineMath math="1" /> flips the sign

The function <InlineMath math="t\mapsto\left(\frac13\right)^t" /> is strictly decreasing: a larger exponent gives a smaller value. When you pass to the exponents, **the inequality sign flips**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-8&\ge2x \\
x^2-2x-8&\ge0 \\
(x-4)(x+2)&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The product isn't negative when **both factors have the same sign** or one is zero: for <InlineMath math="x\le-2" /> or <InlineMath math="x\ge4" />.

Check <InlineMath math="x=0" />: the left side is <InlineMath math="\left(\frac13\right)^{-8}=3^8" />, the right side <InlineMath math="1" />, and <InlineMath math="3^8\le1" /> is false. The inequality holds for <InlineMath math="x\le-2" /> or <InlineMath math="x\ge4" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-2\le x\le4" /> **doesn't flip the sign**, as if the base were greater than <InlineMath math="1" />.
- <InlineMath math="x\le-4" /> or <InlineMath math="x\ge2" /> comes from <InlineMath math="\left(\frac19\right)^x=\left(\frac13\right)^{-2x}" />, a sign slip in the exponent.
- <InlineMath math="-4\le x\le2" /> makes both mistakes: wrong exponent and no flip.
- <InlineMath math="x\ge4" /> loses the range to the left of <InlineMath math="-2" />.
- <InlineMath math="x\le-2\sqrt2" /> or <InlineMath math="x\ge2\sqrt2" /> only looks at <InlineMath math="x^2-8\ge0" /> and forgets the right side.

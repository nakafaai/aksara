# What an incircle means for the sides

From each corner the two tangent lengths to the incircle are equal. So in every quadrilateral with an incircle **the sums of opposite sides are equal**. For the trapezium with legs <InlineMath math="s" />:

<BlockMath math="8+2=s+s\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s=5" />

# The height from a supporting triangle

Drop a perpendicular from a top corner to the base. By symmetry it is <InlineMath math="\frac{8-2}{2}=3" /> from the end of the base. This forms **a right triangle with hypotenuse <InlineMath math="5" />**:

<BlockMath math="h=\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=4" />

# The area

<BlockMath math="A=\frac{8+2}{2}\cdot4=20\text{ cm}^2" />

Check: the incircle has radius <InlineMath math="\frac h2=2" />, and <InlineMath math="A=r\cdot\frac{u}{2}=2\cdot\frac{8+2+5+5}{2}=20" />. The trapezium has **an area of <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^2" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="25\text{ cm}^2" /> takes **the leg <InlineMath math="5" /> as the height**: <InlineMath math="\frac{8+2}{2}\cdot5" />.
- <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^2" /> forgets to halve in the trapezium formula: <InlineMath math="(8+2)\cdot4" />.
- <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}^2" /> takes the overhang <InlineMath math="3" /> as the height.
- <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}^2" /> takes the radius <InlineMath math="2" /> instead of the diameter <InlineMath math="4" /> as the height.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}^2" /> uses height <InlineMath math="3" /> and doesn't halve: <InlineMath math="(8+2)\cdot3" />.

# Split the expression

The difference of squares splits the expression into **one known and one unknown bracket**:

<BlockMath math="x^2-\frac1{x^2}=\left(x+\frac1x\right)\left(x-\frac1x\right)=3\left(x-\frac1x\right)" />

# Find the difference through its square

Square the difference and use the known sum. The two squares **differ only in the mixed term** <InlineMath math="\pm2\cdot x\cdot\frac1x=\pm2" />:

<BlockMath math="\left(x-\frac1x\right)^2=\left(x+\frac1x\right)^2-4=9-4=5" />

So <InlineMath math="x-\frac1x=\sqrt5" /> or <InlineMath math="x-\frac1x=-\sqrt5" />.

# The condition <InlineMath math="x>1" /> decides the sign

For <InlineMath math="x>1" />, <InlineMath math="\frac1x<1<x" />, so **<InlineMath math="x-\frac1x" /> is positive**. That rules out <InlineMath math="-\sqrt5" />:

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-3\sqrt5" /> belongs to the other solution, <InlineMath math="x\approx0.38" />. It **doesn't satisfy <InlineMath math="x>1" />**.
- <InlineMath math="3\sqrt5" /> or <InlineMath math="-3\sqrt5" /> overlooks that the condition <InlineMath math="x>1" /> fixes the sign.
- <InlineMath math="7" /> is <InlineMath math="x^2+\frac1{x^2}=9-2" />, the sum of the squares instead of their difference.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt5" /> is only the difference <InlineMath math="x-\frac1x" />. The factor <InlineMath math="x+\frac1x=3" /> is missing.
- <InlineMath math="3\sqrt7" /> squares wrongly: <InlineMath math="\left(x-\frac1x\right)^2=9-2=7" /> instead of <InlineMath math="9-4=5" />.

# Given

For a real number <InlineMath math="x > 1" />, we know **the sum with its reciprocal**:

<BlockMath math="x + \frac{1}{x} = 3" />

# Asked

What is the exact value of this difference of squares?

<BlockMath math="x^2 - \frac{1}{x^2}" />

# Solution

You do not need to solve a quadratic for <InlineMath math="x" /> and square ugly roots. Look at the target: <InlineMath math="x^2 - x^{-2}" /> is a **difference of two squares** that splits neatly into a sum times a difference. You already hold the sum, so you only need the difference via its square.

> Difference of squares splits the target into sum times difference, and the size condition locks in the unique sign of the square root.

## Factoring the target into sum times difference

Expand the expression using **the difference of squares pattern**:

<BlockMath math="x^2 - \frac{1}{x^2} = \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)\left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right) = 3\left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right)" />

The only missing piece is <InlineMath math="x - \frac{1}{x}" />.

## Finding the difference through its square

Compare the square of the sum and the square of the difference: they **differ by exactly four** because the cross term cancels <InlineMath math="x" /> completely:

<BlockMath math="\left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 = \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 - 4 = 3^2 - 4 = 5" />

This leaves two candidates: <InlineMath math="\sqrt{5}" /> and <InlineMath math="-\sqrt{5}" />.

## Using the size condition to fix the sign

Because we know **<InlineMath math="x" /> is strictly larger than one**, its reciprocal <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{x}" /> is strictly smaller than one. Therefore <InlineMath math="x" /> exceeds <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{x}" />, forcing <InlineMath math="x - \frac{1}{x}" /> to be positive and equal to <InlineMath math="\sqrt{5}" />.

Multiply back to get the final answer:

<BlockMath math="x^2 - \frac{1}{x^2} = 3 \cdot \sqrt{5} = 3\sqrt{5}" />

Quick decimal check: for <InlineMath math="x \approx 2.618" />, we have <InlineMath math="x^2 \approx 6.854" /> and <InlineMath math="x^{-2} \approx 0.146" />, whose difference <InlineMath math="6.708" /> matches <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{5}" /> closely.

## Checking the distractors

See how each wrong answer **ignores the sign condition or subtracts the wrong middle term**:

| Option | Why it is wrong |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="-3\sqrt{5}" /> | Belongs to the left root <InlineMath math="x \approx 0.38" />, which violates <InlineMath math="x > 1" />. |
| <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{5}" /> or <InlineMath math="-3\sqrt{5}" /> | Misses that <InlineMath math="x > 1" /> uniquely determines the positive sign. |
| <InlineMath math="7" /> | Gives the sum of squares <InlineMath math="x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} = 9 - 2" />, not the difference. |
| <InlineMath math="\sqrt{5}" /> | Computes only <InlineMath math="x - \frac{1}{x}" /> and forgets the factor <InlineMath math="3" />. |
| <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{7}" /> | Subtracts <InlineMath math="2" /> instead of <InlineMath math="4" /> when converting the squared sum into the squared difference. |

# Answer

The exact value of the expression is **<InlineMath math="3\sqrt{5}" />**.

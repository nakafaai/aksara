# An equation in whole numbers

Call the number of tickets at <InlineMath math="7" /> euros <InlineMath math="a" /> and at <InlineMath math="11" /> euros <InlineMath math="b" />. Both are **whole numbers from <InlineMath math="0" /> up**, and

<BlockMath math="7a+11b=200" />

You want the largest value of <InlineMath math="a+b" />.

# Which values of <InlineMath math="b" /> work

<InlineMath math="200-11b" /> must be divisible by <InlineMath math="7" />. Test <InlineMath math="b=0,1,2,\ldots" /> until <InlineMath math="11b>200" />. The remainders repeat with period <InlineMath math="7" />, and **only <InlineMath math="b=1" />, <InlineMath math="b=8" /> and <InlineMath math="b=15" /> work**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
b=1:&\quad 7a=189,\quad a=27,\quad a+b=28 \\
b=8:&\quad 7a=112,\quad a=16,\quad a+b=24 \\
b=15:&\quad 7a=35,\quad a=5,\quad a+b=20
\end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="b=22" />, <InlineMath math="11b=242>200" />.

# The largest number

More cheap tickets mean more tickets in total. The most tickets are <InlineMath math="27" /> at <InlineMath math="7" /> euros and one at <InlineMath math="11" /> euros: **at most <InlineMath math="28" /> tickets**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20" /> is the **smallest** possible number, <InlineMath math="5+15" />.
- <InlineMath math="24" /> is the middle solution, <InlineMath math="16+8" />.
- <InlineMath math="29" /> rounds <InlineMath math="\frac{200}{7}\approx28.6" /> up, as if there were only <InlineMath math="7" />-euro tickets. But <InlineMath math="200" /> isn't divisible by <InlineMath math="7" />.
- <InlineMath math="18" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{200}{11}" /> rounded down, only <InlineMath math="11" />-euro tickets. That doesn't come out exactly either.
- <InlineMath math="27" /> counts only the <InlineMath math="7" />-euro tickets of the best solution and forgets the one <InlineMath math="11" />-euro ticket.

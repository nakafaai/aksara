# Given

A cinema sells two kinds of admission tickets (*Eintrittskarten*):

- Cheaper tickets at <InlineMath math="7\text{ €}" /> (quantity <InlineMath math="a" />).
- Pricier tickets at <InlineMath math="11\text{ €}" /> (quantity <InlineMath math="b" />).

In one evening, **total revenue is exactly <InlineMath math="200\text{ €}" />**, where <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b" /> are nonnegative integers.

# Asked

What is the **maximum** (*höchstens*) number of tickets the cinema could have sold that evening?

# Solution

If you have a fixed budget of <InlineMath math="200\text{ €}" /> and want as many tickets as possible, the strategy is obvious: **pack in as many cheap <InlineMath math="7\text{ €}" /> tickets as possible and use as few <InlineMath math="11\text{ €}" /> tickets as needed**. Since <InlineMath math="200" /> is not divisible by <InlineMath math="7" />, you trade in the smallest possible number of <InlineMath math="11\text{ €}" /> tickets until the remaining euros divide cleanly by <InlineMath math="7" />.

> With fixed total revenue, ticket count is maximized by taking the smallest number of expensive tickets that leaves a remainder divisible by the cheap price.

## Finding the valid counts of expensive tickets

The revenue equation is:

<BlockMath math="7a + 11b = 200 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad 7a = 200 - 11b" />

So <InlineMath math="200 - 11b" /> **must be divisible by <InlineMath math="7" />**. Test from the smallest <InlineMath math="b" /> upward:

- At <InlineMath math="b = 0" />, <InlineMath math="200" /> leaves remainder <InlineMath math="4" /> mod <InlineMath math="7" />.
- At <InlineMath math="b = 1" />, the remainder is <InlineMath math="189" />, which equals <InlineMath math="7 \cdot 27" /> cleanly right away.

Every time <InlineMath math="b" /> increases by <InlineMath math="7" />, <InlineMath math="a" /> drops by <InlineMath math="11" /> while keeping revenue at <InlineMath math="200" />. All valid integer pairs are:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
b = 1: &\quad a = 27, \quad a + b = 28 \\
b = 8: &\quad a = 16, \quad a + b = 24 \\
b = 15: &\quad a = 5, \quad a + b = 20
\end{aligned}" />

At <InlineMath math="b = 22" />, <InlineMath math="11b" /> already hits <InlineMath math="242" /> (above <InlineMath math="200" />), so these three pairs are the entire list.

Revenue check for the maximum point:

<BlockMath math="7 \cdot 27 + 11 \cdot 1 = 189 + 11 = 200\text{ €}" />

It matches exact to the euro.

# Answer

The cinema sold at most **<InlineMath math="28" />** tickets.

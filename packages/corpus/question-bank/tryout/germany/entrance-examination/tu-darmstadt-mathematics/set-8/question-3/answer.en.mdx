# Given

Square <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> has **side length <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />**.

Two circular arcs (*Kreisbögen*) of radius <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> centered at <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> intersect inside the square at point <InlineMath math="S" />.

# Asked

How far is point <InlineMath math="S" /> from the top side <InlineMath math="\overline{CD}" />?

# Solution

Picture placing a compass spike at corner <InlineMath math="A" /> opened to the full side of the square (<InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />), then doing the exact same from corner <InlineMath math="B" />. Where the two arcs meet at <InlineMath math="S" />, that point is automatically **<InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> from both <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />**, locking in an equilateral triangle sitting on the bottom edge.

> Two arcs with radius equal to the square's side form an equilateral triangle on the base, so the distance to the opposite edge is the side minus that triangle's height.

## Recognizing the equilateral triangle and its height

Because <InlineMath math="S" /> lies on both radius-<InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> arcs, <InlineMath math="\overline{AS} = 6" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{BS} = 6" />. Together with <InlineMath math="\overline{AB} = 6" />, **triangle <InlineMath math="ABS" /> is equilateral**.

The altitude from <InlineMath math="S" /> drops straight down to the midpoint of <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />, splitting the base into two <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}" /> halves. By the Pythagorean theorem:

<BlockMath math="h = \sqrt{6^2 - 3^2} = \sqrt{36 - 9} = \sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt{3}" />

So <InlineMath math="S" /> sits <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}" /> above the bottom side <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />.

## Subtracting the height from the full side

Since <InlineMath math="\overline{CD}" /> runs parallel to <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" /> at height <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />, **the remaining distance to the top side** is:

<BlockMath math="d = 6 - 3\sqrt{3} \approx 6 - 5.196 = 0.804\text{ cm}" />

A narrow gap just under <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> matches the drawing.

# Answer

Point <InlineMath math="S" /> is **<InlineMath math="(6 - 3\sqrt{3})\text{ cm}" />** away from side <InlineMath math="\overline{CD}" />.

# Triangle <InlineMath math="ABS" /> is equilateral

<InlineMath math="S" /> lies on the arc about <InlineMath math="A" />, so <InlineMath math="\overline{AS}=6" />. It also lies on the arc about <InlineMath math="B" />, so <InlineMath math="\overline{BS}=6" />. Together with <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}=6" />, triangle <InlineMath math="ABS" /> is **equilateral**.

# The height above <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />

The height of an equilateral triangle with side <InlineMath math="6" /> halves the base. By Pythagoras:

<BlockMath math="h=\sqrt{6^2-3^2}=\sqrt{27}=3\sqrt3" />

So <InlineMath math="S" /> lies **<InlineMath math="3\sqrt3" /> above the side <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />**.

# The distance to the opposite side

The side <InlineMath math="\overline{CD}" /> is <InlineMath math="6" /> above <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />. You want **what's left up to it**:

<BlockMath math="6-3\sqrt3\approx6-5.196=0.804" />

<InlineMath math="S" /> is <InlineMath math="(6-3\sqrt3)\text{ cm}" /> from <InlineMath math="\overline{CD}" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3\sqrt3\text{ cm}" /> is **the distance to the wrong side**, to <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" /> instead of <InlineMath math="\overline{CD}" />.
- <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="S" /> to the sides <InlineMath math="\overline{AD}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{BC}" />.
- <InlineMath math="(6-3\sqrt2)\text{ cm}" /> uses half the square's diagonal, <InlineMath math="3\sqrt2" />, as the height.
- <InlineMath math="(3\sqrt3-3)\text{ cm}" /> subtracts half the side instead of subtracting the height from the whole side.
- <InlineMath math="(6-2\sqrt3)\text{ cm}" /> confuses the height <InlineMath math="3\sqrt3" /> with the distance <InlineMath math="2\sqrt3" /> from the centroid of triangle <InlineMath math="ABS" /> to its corner <InlineMath math="S" />.

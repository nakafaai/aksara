# Fix the domain first

A logarithm is only defined for positive numbers. The equation contains <InlineMath math="\log_2 x" /> and <InlineMath math="\log_2(x+3)" />, so you need:

<BlockMath math="x>0\quad\text{and}\quad x+3>0\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad x>0" />

This condition belongs to the **original** equation. Later you may keep only numbers with <InlineMath math="x>0" /> as solutions.

# Combine the logarithms

With <InlineMath math="2\log_2 x=\log_2 x^2" /> and <InlineMath math="\log_2 u-\log_2 v=\log_2\frac uv" /> the left side becomes **a single logarithm**. Then use the definition <InlineMath math="\log_2 w=2\iff w=2^2=4" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\log_2\frac{x^2}{x+3}&=2 \\
\frac{x^2}{x+3}&=4 \\
x^2-4x-12&=0 \\
(x-6)(x+2)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The quadratic equation has the solutions <InlineMath math="x=6" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-2" />.

The solution set is <InlineMath math="\mathbb{L}=\{6\}" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\mathbb{L}=\{-2;\,6\}" /> keeps <InlineMath math="x=-2" />. There **<InlineMath math="\log_2 x" /> isn't defined**, even though the rewritten equation holds.
- <InlineMath math="\mathbb{L}=\{-2\}" /> throws away the real solution and keeps the false one.
- <InlineMath math="\mathbb{L}=\{\}" /> comes from reading <InlineMath math="2\log_2 x" /> as <InlineMath math="\log_2(2x)" />. Then <InlineMath math="\frac{2x}{x+3}=4" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=-6" />, which drops out.
- <InlineMath math="\mathbb{L}=\left\{\frac{1+\sqrt{29}}{2}\right\}" /> comes from turning the difference of logarithms into the logarithm of a difference: <InlineMath math="\log_2\left(x^2-(x+3)\right)=2" />.
- <InlineMath math="\mathbb{L}=\{1+\sqrt7\}" /> comes from concluding <InlineMath math="w=2" /> instead of <InlineMath math="w=4" /> from <InlineMath math="\log_2 w=2" />.

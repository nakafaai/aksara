Because the interest is added to the capital each year and then earns interest itself, this is **compound interest** (*Zinseszins*). Every year the capital is multiplied by the factor <InlineMath math="1.03" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{after 1 year}&:\ 2{,}000\cdot1.03=2{,}060\text{ €} \\
\text{after 2 years}&:\ 2{,}060\cdot1.03=2{,}121.80\text{ €}
\end{aligned}" />

In the second year the interest is more than <InlineMath math="60\text{ €}" />: it is <InlineMath math="3\,\%" /> of <InlineMath math="2{,}060\text{ €}" />, which is <InlineMath math="61.80\text{ €}" />. In one line: <InlineMath math="2{,}000\cdot1.03^2=2{,}000\cdot1.0609=2{,}121.80" />.

So after two years the capital has grown to <InlineMath math="2{,}121.80\text{ €}" />.

- <InlineMath math="60.00\text{ €}" /> is only the interest of the first year.
- <InlineMath math="121.80\text{ €}" /> is the total interest after two years, not the balance.
- <InlineMath math="2{,}060.00\text{ €}" /> is the amount after one year.
- <InlineMath math="2{,}120.00\text{ €}" /> uses simple interest, twice <InlineMath math="60\text{ €}" />. The first year's interest earns interest in the second year.
- <InlineMath math="2{,}180.00\text{ €}" /> uses three years of simple interest, <InlineMath math="2{,}000+3\cdot60" />.

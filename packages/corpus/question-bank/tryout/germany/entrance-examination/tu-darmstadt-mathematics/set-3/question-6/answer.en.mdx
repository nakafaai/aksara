# Given

In a **sports club** (*Sportverein*), initially <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> of the members are youth and the remaining <InlineMath math="80\,\%" /> are adults. One year later, total membership has grown by <InlineMath math="20\,\%" />, while the youth share (*Anteil der Jugendlichen*) has risen to <InlineMath math="25\,\%" />.

# Asked

How did the number of adult members change over that year?

# Solution

A slightly smaller slice of a much bigger pizza can still hold more pizza! Even though adults dropped from <InlineMath math="80\,\%" /> to <InlineMath math="75\,\%" /> of the club, **the whole club grew by one fifth**, so you cannot just subtract percentages directly.

> Percentages taken at two different times refer to two different totals, so convert them into concrete counts using a clean base of one hundred before computing the percentage change.

Because every piece of data is a percentage, start with <InlineMath math="100" /> members so every count stays crystal clear.

## Comparing counts before and after

Compute **the adult headcount at the start and end of the year** from a base of <InlineMath math="100" /> members.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Before: } &100 \text{ members} \implies 20 \text{ youth and } 80 \text{ adults} \\
\text{After: } &100 \cdot 1.2 = 120 \text{ members} \implies 0.25 \cdot 120 = 30 \text{ youth and } 90 \text{ adults}
\end{aligned}" />

## Measuring percentage change and checking wrong choices

Always measure percentage change **relative to the initial adult count**, which is <InlineMath math="80" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{90 - 80}{80} = \frac{10}{80} = 0.125 = 12.5\,\%" />

Symbolic check without a sample size: dividing <InlineMath math="0.75 \cdot 1.2" /> by <InlineMath math="0.8" /> gives <InlineMath math="1.125" />, confirming the result for any club size.

- Decreased by <InlineMath math="5\,\%" /> subtracts <InlineMath math="75\,\%" /> from <InlineMath math="80\,\%" /> directly, ignoring that the two percentages come from different totals.
- Decreased by <InlineMath math="6.25\,\%" /> compares the share <InlineMath math="75" /> to <InlineMath math="80" /> rather than comparing actual headcounts.
- Increased by <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> applies the whole club's growth rate blindly to adults.
- Decreased by <InlineMath math="12.5\,\%" /> has the right magnitude but the wrong direction, since <InlineMath math="90" /> exceeds <InlineMath math="80" />.
- Increased by <InlineMath math="50\,\%" /> measures the growth of youth members from <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="30" />.

# Answer

The number of adult members increased by <InlineMath math="12.5\,\%" />.

# Given

A **right triangle** (*rechtwinkliges Dreieck*) has legs (*Katheten*) of lengths <InlineMath math="6" /> and <InlineMath math="8" />. Point <InlineMath math="I" /> is the center of its incircle (*Inkreismittelpunkt*), and point <InlineMath math="U" /> is the center of its circumcircle (*Umkreismittelpunkt*).

# Asked

Find the distance <InlineMath math="\overline{IU}" /> between the incenter and the circumcenter.

# Solution

Push the right angle flush into the corner of a room at the origin <InlineMath math="C(0 \mid 0)" />, placing the two legs along the axes to <InlineMath math="A(8 \mid 0)" /> and <InlineMath math="B(0 \mid 6)" />. With that single move, **both circle centers read straight off as simple coordinates**.

> Placing the right angle at the origin puts the incenter at equal coordinates matching the inradius, while the circumcenter lands directly at the midpoint of the hypotenuse.

By the Pythagorean theorem, the hypotenuse has length <InlineMath math="10" />, since <InlineMath math="6^2 + 8^2 = 100" />.

## Finding both center coordinates

Because the incircle touches both axes, its center is <InlineMath math="I(r \mid r)" />. Connect <InlineMath math="I" /> to all three vertices to split the triangle into three smaller triangles of height <InlineMath math="r" />, showing that **area equals semiperimeter times inradius**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot 6 = \frac{1}{2}\, r\, (8 + 6 + 10) \implies 24 = 12r \implies r = 2" />

Hence <InlineMath math="I(2 \mid 2)" />. By Thales' theorem, the circumcenter of a right triangle is the midpoint of hypotenuse <InlineMath math="AB" />, giving <InlineMath math="U(4 \mid 3)" /> with circumradius <InlineMath math="5" />.

## Computing the distance and checking wrong choices

Compute the distance with the Pythagorean theorem and verify it with **Euler's formula for center distance**.

<BlockMath math="\overline{IU} = \sqrt{(4 - 2)^2 + (3 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 1} = \sqrt{5}" />

Quick check by Euler's theorem: <InlineMath math="R(R - 2r)" /> equals <InlineMath math="5(5 - 4) = 5" />, confirming the distance is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{5}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{13}" /> comes from <InlineMath math="r = 1" /> after dividing area by the full perimeter <InlineMath math="24" /> instead of the semiperimeter <InlineMath math="12" />.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> takes the radius difference <InlineMath math="5 - 2" />, as if the two circles were internally tangent.
- <InlineMath math="7" /> adds the radii <InlineMath math="5 + 2" /> without checking where the centers sit.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{3}" /> confuses the circumcenter <InlineMath math="U" /> with the centroid of the triangle.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{2}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="I" /> to the right-angle vertex <InlineMath math="C" />, not to <InlineMath math="U" />.

# Answer

The distance between **<InlineMath math="I" />** and **<InlineMath math="U" />** is **<InlineMath math="\sqrt{5}" />**.

Both squares are binomial identities. Expand them and keep in mind that the **minus sign in front of the second bracket** applies to every term of that bracket.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x+2)^2&=x^2+4x+4 \\
(x-2)^2&=x^2-4x+4 \\
(x+2)^2-(x-2)^2&=x^2+4x+4-x^2+4x-4=8x
\end{aligned}" />

The difference of squares <InlineMath math="a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)" /> is faster: with <InlineMath math="a=x+2" /> and <InlineMath math="b=x-2" />, <InlineMath math="a+b=2x" /> and <InlineMath math="a-b=4" />, so the result is <InlineMath math="2x\cdot4=8x" />. As a check with <InlineMath math="x=1" />: <InlineMath math="9-1=8=8\cdot1" />.

So the expression simplifies to <InlineMath math="8x" />.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> comes from assuming both squares are equal. They differ in the middle term <InlineMath math="\pm4x" />.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> comes from forgetting the middle terms and applying the minus sign only to <InlineMath math="x^2" />, that is, <InlineMath math="(x^2+4)-(x^2-4)" />.
- <InlineMath math="-8x" /> has the wrong sign. The test with <InlineMath math="x=1" /> gives <InlineMath math="+8" />.
- <InlineMath math="2x^2+8" /> adds the two squares instead of subtracting them.
- <InlineMath math="4x" /> forgets the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> in the middle term, as in <InlineMath math="(x+2)^2=x^2+2x+4" />.

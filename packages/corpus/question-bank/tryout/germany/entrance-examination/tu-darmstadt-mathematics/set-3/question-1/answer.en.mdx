# Write each root as a square

All three radicands have the form <InlineMath math="a\pm2\sqrt b" />. Such an expression is often **a perfect square**, because <InlineMath math="(p\pm q)^2=p^2+q^2\pm2pq" />. So look for two numbers whose squares add up to <InlineMath math="a" /> and whose doubled product is the root part:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
9+4\sqrt5&=5+4+2\cdot2\sqrt5=\left(\sqrt5+2\right)^2 \\
9-4\sqrt5&=5+4-2\cdot2\sqrt5=\left(\sqrt5-2\right)^2 \\
6-2\sqrt5&=5+1-2\sqrt5=\left(\sqrt5-1\right)^2
\end{aligned}" />

# Check the sign of each root

A square root is never negative: <InlineMath math="\sqrt{u^2}=|u|" />. For <InlineMath math="9-4\sqrt5" />, both <InlineMath math="\sqrt5-2" /> and <InlineMath math="2-\sqrt5" /> square to the radicand. The root is **the positive one of the two**. So <InlineMath math="\sqrt5-2>0" /> and <InlineMath math="\sqrt5-1>0" />, but <InlineMath math="2-\sqrt5<0" />. That gives

<BlockMath math="\sqrt{9+4\sqrt5}=\sqrt5+2,\qquad\sqrt{9-4\sqrt5}=\sqrt5-2,\qquad\sqrt{6-2\sqrt5}=\sqrt5-1" />

# Work out the fraction

In the numerator **the two <InlineMath math="\sqrt5" /> cancel**. Rationalise the denominator with the difference of squares:

<BlockMath math="\frac{(\sqrt5+2)-(\sqrt5-2)}{\sqrt5-1}=\frac{4}{\sqrt5-1}=\frac{4\left(\sqrt5+1\right)}{\left(\sqrt5-1\right)\left(\sqrt5+1\right)}=\frac{4\left(\sqrt5+1\right)}{5-1}=\sqrt5+1" />

A check with <InlineMath math="\sqrt5\approx2.236" /> agrees: the numerator is about <InlineMath math="4.236-0.236=4" />, the denominator about <InlineMath math="1.236" />, and <InlineMath math="4\div1.236\approx3.236\approx\sqrt5+1" />.

The expression has the value <InlineMath math="\sqrt5+1" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-\sqrt5-1" /> comes from writing <InlineMath math="\sqrt{6-2\sqrt5}" /> as <InlineMath math="1-\sqrt5" />. That number is **negative, so it can't be a square root**.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{5+\sqrt5}{2}" /> comes from writing <InlineMath math="\sqrt{9-4\sqrt5}" /> as <InlineMath math="2-\sqrt5" />. The numerator then becomes <InlineMath math="2\sqrt5" /> instead of <InlineMath math="4" />.
- <InlineMath math="-\frac{5+\sqrt5}{2}" /> makes both sign mistakes at once.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt5-1" /> comes from taking <InlineMath math="(\sqrt5-1)^2" /> as <InlineMath math="5-1=4" />, like a difference of squares, and then cancelling <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{\sqrt5-1}=\frac{(\sqrt5-1)^2}{\sqrt5-1}" />. In fact <InlineMath math="(\sqrt5-1)^2=6-2\sqrt5" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{2\sqrt5+2}{3}" /> comes from taking <InlineMath math="(\sqrt5-1)(\sqrt5+1)" /> as <InlineMath math="5+1=6" /> instead of <InlineMath math="5-1=4" />.

Both parts of the expression contain <InlineMath math="4x^2" />. If you **multiply out both parts completely**, these squared terms cancel. For the square, use the identity <InlineMath math="(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2" /> with <InlineMath math="a=2x" /> and <InlineMath math="b=1" />. The second product has a minus sign in front of it, so put it in brackets after multiplying out.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(2x-1)^2-4x(x-1)&=(4x^2-4x+1)-(4x^2-4x) \\
&=4x^2-4x+1-4x^2+4x \\
&=1
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, substitute a value such as <InlineMath math="x=3" />: <InlineMath math="5^2-4\cdot3\cdot2=25-24=1" />.

So the expression has the value <InlineMath math="1" /> for every <InlineMath math="x" />.

- <InlineMath math="-1" /> appears if <InlineMath math="(-1)^2" /> is taken as <InlineMath math="-1" /> while squaring.
- <InlineMath math="4x+1" /> appears if the middle term <InlineMath math="-4x" /> is lost while squaring, that is, if <InlineMath math="(2x-1)^2=4x^2+1" /> is used.
- <InlineMath math="4x-1" /> confuses the square with the difference of two squares and uses <InlineMath math="(2x-1)^2=4x^2-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="1-8x" /> changes only the sign of <InlineMath math="4x^2" /> when removing the brackets, not the sign of <InlineMath math="-4x" />.
- <InlineMath math="8x^2-8x+1" /> adds the second product instead of subtracting it.

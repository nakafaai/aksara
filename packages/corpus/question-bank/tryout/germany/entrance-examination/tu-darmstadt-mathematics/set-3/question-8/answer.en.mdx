# Given

An open-top **cuboid tank** (*quaderförmiges Becken*) has an inner base of <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}" /> by <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> and holds water to a depth of <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" />. A solid cube (*massiver Würfel*) of edge <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> is placed on the bottom without any water overflowing.

# Asked

Find the new water height <InlineMath math="h" /> inside the tank.

# Solution

Drop a thick block onto the floor of a shallow tub: as long as the water stays below the top of the block, the block acts like a solid pillar that **steals floor space from the water**. The water volume stays unchanged, so it must crowd into the remaining open floor around the cube and rise higher.

> When a solid rests on the bottom of a tank, first test whether the water stays below the top of the solid, in which case the water simply fills the tank floor minus the solid's footprint.

Compute the fixed water volume and the open floor area left beside the cube.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{water}} &= 40 \cdot 30 \cdot 10 = 12\,000\text{ cm}^3 \\
A_{\text{free}} &= 40 \cdot 30 - 20 \cdot 20 = 1200 - 400 = 800\text{ cm}^2
\end{aligned}" />

## Computing the new height and testing the assumption

Assume the water stays below the top face of the cube (<InlineMath math="h \le 20\text{ cm}" />), then divide the water volume by **the free base area around the cube**.

<BlockMath math="800\, h = 12\,000 \implies h = 15\text{ cm}" />

Because <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm} < 20\text{ cm}" />, our assumption holds and the cube still pokes <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" /> above the surface.

## Checking displaced volume and wrong choices

Double-check via displacement: the submerged <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" /> of the cube displaces <InlineMath math="6\,000\text{ cm}^3" />, which together with the water fills **a combined volume of <InlineMath math="18\,000\text{ cm}^3" />**, matching <InlineMath math="1200 \cdot 15" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{50}{3}\text{ cm}" /> assumes the entire cube (<InlineMath math="8\,000\text{ cm}^3" />) is submerged, which contradicts itself because the resulting height is below <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{40}{3}\text{ cm}" /> counts only the cube volume submerged up to the initial <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" />, forgetting that rising water submerges more of the cube.
- <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> guesses that the water rises flush with the top of the cube.
- <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ cm}" /> adds the cube's base to the tank's base to get <InlineMath math="1600\text{ cm}^2" /> instead of subtracting it.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{31}{3}\text{ cm}" /> uses the base area <InlineMath math="400" /> as the cube's volume instead of cubing the edge.

# Answer

The water stands **<InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" />** high after the cube is placed inside.

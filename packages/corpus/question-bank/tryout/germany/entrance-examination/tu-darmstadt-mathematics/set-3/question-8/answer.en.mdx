Count the edges (*Kanten*) **by their position**. A prism consists of two equal bases joined by lateral edges.

- The bottom hexagon has <InlineMath math="6" /> edges.
- The top hexagon also has <InlineMath math="6" /> edges.
- Each of the <InlineMath math="6" /> vertices of the bottom hexagon is joined by a vertical lateral edge to the matching vertex at the top.

<BlockMath math="6+6+6=18" />

Euler's polyhedron formula <InlineMath math="V-E+F=2" /> gives a check. The prism has <InlineMath math="V=12" /> vertices and <InlineMath math="F=8" /> faces, namely <InlineMath math="2" /> hexagons and <InlineMath math="6" /> rectangles. So <InlineMath math="E=V+F-2=12+8-2=18" />.

So the prism has <InlineMath math="18" /> edges.

- <InlineMath math="6" /> counts only the edges of a single base.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> is the number of faces, not edges.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> is the number of vertices, or the edges of both hexagons without the lateral edges.
- <InlineMath math="14" /> and <InlineMath math="16" /> fit no complete count. With <InlineMath math="12" /> vertices and <InlineMath math="8" /> faces, Euler's formula allows only <InlineMath math="18" /> edges.

# First check whether the cube goes under

The amount of water stays the same. It only spreads out differently. The key question is whether the cube ends up fully under water. Assume first that the water stays **below the top of the cube**, so <InlineMath math="h\le20\text{ cm}" />. Then the water only stands on the free floor area next to the cube:

<BlockMath math="40\cdot30-20\cdot20=1200-400=800\text{ cm}^2" />

# Set the amounts of water equal

The water volume is <InlineMath math="1200\cdot10=12{,}000\text{ cm}^3" />. It now fills the free area up to the height <InlineMath math="h" />:

<BlockMath math="800\,h=12{,}000\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=15\text{ cm}" />

**The assumption holds**, because <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}<20\text{ cm}" />. So the cube still sticks <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" /> out of the water.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{50}{3}\text{ cm}" /> assumes the whole cube, <InlineMath math="8{,}000\text{ cm}^3" />, is under water: <InlineMath math="\frac{12{,}000+8{,}000}{1200}" />. Then the water would have to be at least <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> high, and <InlineMath math="\frac{50}{3}<20" /> contradicts that.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{40}{3}\text{ cm}" /> counts only the part of the cube that was in the water at the start: <InlineMath math="\frac{12{,}000+400\cdot10}{1200}" />. As the water rises, **more of the cube goes under**.
- <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> assumes the water rises exactly to the top of the cube.
- <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ cm}" /> adds the cube's base to the floor area instead of subtracting it: <InlineMath math="\frac{12{,}000}{1600}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{31}{3}\text{ cm}" /> uses <InlineMath math="20^2=400" /> as the cube's volume instead of <InlineMath math="20^3" />.

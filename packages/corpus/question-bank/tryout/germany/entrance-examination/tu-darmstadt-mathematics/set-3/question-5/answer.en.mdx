Two lines that are not parallel to an axis are perpendicular exactly when the **product of their slopes** is <InlineMath math="-1" />. So first find the slope <InlineMath math="k" /> of the line through <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac{5-1}{5-(-1)}=\frac{4}{6}=\frac{2}{3} \\
m\cdot\frac{2}{3}&=-1 \\
m&=-\frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}" />

The intercept <InlineMath math="-4" /> only shifts the line <InlineMath math="g" /> downward and has no effect on its direction.

As a check, <InlineMath math="-\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{3}=-1" />.

So the slope is <InlineMath math="m=-\frac{3}{2}" />.

- <InlineMath math="m=-4" /> is the <InlineMath math="y" />-intercept of <InlineMath math="g" />, not its slope.
- <InlineMath math="m=-\frac{2}{3}" /> changes only the sign of the slope and does not take the reciprocal.
- <InlineMath math="m=\frac{2}{3}" /> is the slope of the line through <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />. With this slope, <InlineMath math="g" /> would be parallel to it.
- <InlineMath math="m=\frac{3}{2}" /> takes the reciprocal but forgets the minus sign. The product of the slopes would then be <InlineMath math="1" />.
- <InlineMath math="m=4" /> takes only the rise <InlineMath math="5-1" /> from <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="Q" /> and does not divide by the horizontal distance.

# Exactly one common point means discriminant zero

Common points of <InlineMath math="g" /> and <InlineMath math="p" /> satisfy both equations. Set the right-hand sides equal and move everything to one side:

<BlockMath math="x^2+bx+7=2x+3\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2+(b-2)\,x+4=0" />

Exactly one common point means this quadratic equation has exactly one solution, so **its discriminant is zero**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(b-2)^2-4\cdot1\cdot4&=0 \\
(b-2)^2&=16 \\
b-2=4\quad&\text{or}\quad b-2=-4 \\
b=6\quad&\text{or}\quad b=-2
\end{aligned}" />

# Find the point of contact for both values

With discriminant zero **the only solution** is <InlineMath math="x=-\frac{b-2}{2}=\frac{2-b}{2}" />. The line gives the <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
b=6:&\quad x=-2,\quad y=2\cdot(-2)+3=-1 \\
b=-2:&\quad x=2,\quad y=2\cdot2+3=7
\end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="b=6" /> the point <InlineMath math="(-2\mid-1)" /> lies in the third quadrant. For <InlineMath math="b=-2" /> the point <InlineMath math="B(2\mid7)" /> lies in the first quadrant.

So <InlineMath math="b=-2" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="b=6" /> belongs to the point of contact <InlineMath math="(-2\mid-1)" />, with two negative coordinates.
- <InlineMath math="b=-2" /> or <InlineMath math="b=6" /> uses only the condition "exactly one point" and **overlooks the quadrant condition**.
- <InlineMath math="b=-6" /> comes from a sign slip when rearranging: <InlineMath math="x^2+(b+2)x+4=0" /> gives <InlineMath math="b=2" /> or <InlineMath math="b=-6" />, and with <InlineMath math="x=-\frac{b+2}{2}" /> the value <InlineMath math="b=-6" /> seems to lead into the first quadrant.
- <InlineMath math="b=0" /> comes from dropping the factor <InlineMath math="4" /> in the discriminant: <InlineMath math="(b-2)^2-4=0" /> gives <InlineMath math="b=0" /> or <InlineMath math="b=4" />. For <InlineMath math="b=0" />, though, <InlineMath math="x^2-2x+4=0" /> has no solution at all.
- <InlineMath math="b=4" /> is the second value from the same wrong discriminant. For <InlineMath math="b=4" />, <InlineMath math="x^2+2x+4=0" /> has no solution either.

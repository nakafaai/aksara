# Given

Line <InlineMath math="g" /> and **parabola** (*Parabel*) <InlineMath math="p" /> share exactly one common point <InlineMath math="B" /> in the first quadrant (*erster Quadrant*), so both coordinates are positive.

<BlockMath math="g: y = 2x + 3, \qquad p: y = x^2 + bx + 7" />

# Asked

Find the real value of parameter <InlineMath math="b" />.

# Solution

Picture sliding a rigid parabola while the straight ramp <InlineMath math="y = 2x + 3" /> stays bolted down. There are two exact positions where the curve just kisses the ramp at one tangent point, one high up and one low down. The first-quadrant rule **picks the single valid tangent parabola out of those two**.

> A line and a parabola share exactly one point when their difference equation has zero discriminant, and the quadrant condition then selects which tangent point belongs to the answer.

Set the two expressions equal to locate common points.

<BlockMath math="x^2 + bx + 7 = 2x + 3 \iff x^2 + (b - 2)x + 4 = 0" />

## Setting the discriminant to zero and locating tangent points

For this quadratic to have exactly one solution, **its discriminant must equal zero**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(b - 2)^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 4 &= 0 \\
(b - 2)^2 &= 16 \\
b - 2 = 4 \quad &\text{or} \quad b - 2 = -4 \\
b = 6 \quad &\text{or} \quad b = -2
\end{aligned}" />

When the discriminant vanishes, the single root equals minus half the linear coefficient, and its <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate comes straight from <InlineMath math="y = 2x + 3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{2 - b}{2} \\
b = 6 &\implies x = -2, \quad y = -1 \\
b = -2 &\implies x = 2, \quad y = 7
\end{aligned}" />

At <InlineMath math="b = 6" />, contact occurs at <InlineMath math="(-2 \mid -1)" /> in the third quadrant, whereas <InlineMath math="b = -2" /> touches at <InlineMath math="B(2 \mid 7)" /> in the first quadrant.

## Verifying tangency and wrong choices

Quick check for <InlineMath math="b = -2" />: the difference simplifies to **the perfect square <InlineMath math="(x - 2)^2 = 0" />**, confirming a single contact at <InlineMath math="x = 2" /> and <InlineMath math="y = 7" />.

- <InlineMath math="b = 6" /> produces the tangent point <InlineMath math="(-2 \mid -1)" /> with two negative coordinates.
- <InlineMath math="b = -2" /> or <InlineMath math="b = 6" /> uses only the zero-discriminant condition and ignores the first-quadrant requirement.
- <InlineMath math="b = -6" /> comes from a sign mistake when moving <InlineMath math="2x" /> across, writing <InlineMath math="b + 2" /> instead of <InlineMath math="b - 2" />.
- <InlineMath math="b = 0" /> drops the factor of <InlineMath math="4" /> in the discriminant, yet the polynomial <InlineMath math="x^2 - 2x + 4" /> has no real roots at all.
- <InlineMath math="b = 4" /> is the second root of that same flawed discriminant, and the polynomial <InlineMath math="x^2 + 2x + 4" /> also has no real roots.

# Answer

The required value is <InlineMath math="b = -2" />.

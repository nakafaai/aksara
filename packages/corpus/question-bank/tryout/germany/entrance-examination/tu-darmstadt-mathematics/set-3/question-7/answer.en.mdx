# The juice forms a similar cone

Because the tip points down, the juice itself forms a small cone with the same tip. It is **similar** to the whole glass: its height is <InlineMath math="\frac34" /> of the glass height, and by the intercept theorem its radius is also <InlineMath math="\frac34" /> of the top radius.

# Volumes grow with the third power

The cone volume <InlineMath math="V=\frac13\pi r^2h" /> contains one length squared and another length once. If every length is multiplied by <InlineMath math="\frac34" />, **the volume is multiplied by <InlineMath math="\left(\frac34\right)^3" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{juice}}&=\left(\tfrac34\right)^3V_{\text{glass}}=\tfrac{27}{64}\,V_{\text{glass}} \\
V_{\text{glass}}&=54\cdot\tfrac{64}{27}=128\text{ ml}
\end{aligned}" />

# What is still missing

The question isn't the whole glass, but **the amount up to the rim**:

<BlockMath math="128\text{ ml}-54\text{ ml}=74\text{ ml}" />

Another <InlineMath math="74\text{ ml}" /> of juice fits into the glass.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="18\text{ ml}" /> works linearly: three quarters hold <InlineMath math="54\text{ ml}" />, so one quarter should hold <InlineMath math="18\text{ ml}" />. But the top quarter of the height is **the widest part of the glass**.
- <InlineMath math="42\text{ ml}" /> uses the factor <InlineMath math="\left(\frac34\right)^2" /> as for an area. Then the glass would hold <InlineMath math="96\text{ ml}" />.
- <InlineMath math="128\text{ ml}" /> is the whole glass, not the missing part.
- <InlineMath math="72\text{ ml}" /> is the whole glass under the linear reasoning, <InlineMath math="54\cdot\frac43" />.
- <InlineMath math="24\text{ ml}" /> multiplies the linear remainder <InlineMath math="18\text{ ml}" /> by <InlineMath math="\frac43" /> once more, mixing up the remainder and the total.

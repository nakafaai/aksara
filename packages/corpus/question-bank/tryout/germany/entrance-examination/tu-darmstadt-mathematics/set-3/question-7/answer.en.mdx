# Given

A glass shaped like a **right circular cone** (*gerader Kreiskegel*) stands with its tip pointing down. Filled to <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{4}" /> of its height, it holds <InlineMath math="54\text{ ml}" /> of juice.

# Asked

How many more milliliters of juice fit into the glass until it is full to the brim (*randvoll*)?

# Solution

Think of a cone-shaped cocktail glass: the pointed bottom holds almost nothing, while the top quarter flares out like a wide bowl! Because all three spatial dimensions (width, depth, and height) shrink together, **volume scales with the cube of height rather than linearly**.

> For similar three-dimensional solids, scaling every linear dimension by a factor k multiplies the volume by k cubed.

The juice forms a smaller cone similar to the whole glass, with height <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{4}h" /> and surface radius <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{4}r" />.

The cross-section makes it immediately obvious why the empty upper band holds more liquid than its quarter-height suggests.

## Finding total capacity and remaining space

In the cone volume formula, radius appears twice and height once, so **the juice occupies only <InlineMath math="\frac{27}{64}" /> of the full glass**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \\
V_{\text{juice}} &= \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^3 V_{\text{glass}} = \frac{27}{64}\, V_{\text{glass}} \\
V_{\text{glass}} &= 54 \cdot \frac{64}{27} = 2 \cdot 64 = 128\text{ ml}
\end{aligned}" />

The question asks for the extra juice needed to reach the brim, so subtract the amount already inside.

<BlockMath math="V_{\text{remaining}} = 128\text{ ml} - 54\text{ ml} = 74\text{ ml}" />

## Checking proportions and wrong choices

Quick check: the empty fraction is <InlineMath math="\frac{37}{64}" />, and <InlineMath math="\frac{37}{64}" /> of <InlineMath math="128\text{ ml}" /> gives **exactly <InlineMath math="74\text{ ml}" />**.

- <InlineMath math="18\text{ ml}" /> assumes volume scales linearly with height, ignoring that the top quarter is the widest part of the cone.
- <InlineMath math="42\text{ ml}" /> uses the square factor for area instead of the cube, giving a total of <InlineMath math="96\text{ ml}" />.
- <InlineMath math="128\text{ ml}" /> is the total capacity of the glass rather than the remaining room.
- <InlineMath math="72\text{ ml}" /> computes the total linearly as four thirds of <InlineMath math="54\text{ ml}" />.
- <InlineMath math="24\text{ ml}" /> multiplies the linear remainder <InlineMath math="18\text{ ml}" /> by <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{3}" /> a second time.

# Answer

Another <InlineMath math="74\text{ ml}" /> of juice still fits into the glass.

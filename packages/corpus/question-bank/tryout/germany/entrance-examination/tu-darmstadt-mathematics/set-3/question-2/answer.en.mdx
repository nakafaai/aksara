A rational equation is defined only where no denominator is zero, here for <InlineMath math="x\ne2" /> and <InlineMath math="x\ne-1" />. Under that condition you may **cross-multiply**: the left numerator times the right denominator equals the right numerator times the left denominator.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3(x+1)&=2(x-2) \\
3x+3&=2x-4 \\
x&=-7
\end{aligned}" />

The value <InlineMath math="x=-7" /> is allowed, because it is neither <InlineMath math="2" /> nor <InlineMath math="-1" />. As a check, substitute it: <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{-9}=-\frac13" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{-6}=-\frac13" />.

So the solution is <InlineMath math="x=-7" />.

- <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> comes from adding the <InlineMath math="3" /> instead of subtracting it when rearranging <InlineMath math="3x+3=2x-4" />. Also, <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> makes the right denominator zero.
- <InlineMath math="x=1" /> comes from a sign error with the <InlineMath math="-4" />, that is, from <InlineMath math="x=4-3" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=7" /> combines both sign errors.
- <InlineMath math="x=8" /> comes from cross-multiplying the wrong way, <InlineMath math="3(x-2)=2(x+1)" />.
- The claim that the equation has no solution is wrong, because <InlineMath math="x=-7" /> satisfies it and is allowed.

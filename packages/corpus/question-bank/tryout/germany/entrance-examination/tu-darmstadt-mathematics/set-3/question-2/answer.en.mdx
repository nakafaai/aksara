For an equation with fractions, **find the domain first**, because a number that makes a denominator zero cannot be a solution. Since <InlineMath math="x^2+4x=x(x+4)" />, the numbers <InlineMath math="x=0" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-4" /> are excluded.

Then multiply both sides by the common denominator <InlineMath math="x(x+4)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x(2x+3)&=20 \\
2x^2+3x-20&=0 \\
(2x-5)(x+4)&=0 \\
x=\tfrac52\ \text{or}\ x&=-4
\end{aligned}" />

The number <InlineMath math="-4" /> is excluded, because it makes both denominators zero. That leaves <InlineMath math="x=\frac52" />.

As a check, the left side is <InlineMath math="\frac{5+3}{\frac52+4}=\frac{8}{\frac{13}{2}}=\frac{16}{13}" /> and the right side is <InlineMath math="\frac{20}{\frac{25}{4}+10}=\frac{20}{\frac{65}{4}}=\frac{16}{13}" />.

So the solution set is <InlineMath math="L=\{\tfrac52\}" />.

- <InlineMath math="L=\{-4\}" /> keeps exactly the excluded number and throws away the valid solution.
- <InlineMath math="L=\{4\}" /> appears if the signs in the factorization are swapped, <InlineMath math="(2x+5)(x-4)" />, and only the positive number is kept.
- <InlineMath math="L=\{-\tfrac52,\,4\}" /> belongs to <InlineMath math="(2x+5)(x-4)=2x^2-3x-20" />, that is, to a swapped sign in the linear term.
- <InlineMath math="L=\{-4,\,\tfrac52\}" /> forgets the domain. At <InlineMath math="x=-4" /> neither side is defined.
- <InlineMath math="L=\emptyset" /> also discards <InlineMath math="\frac52" />, although that number makes no denominator zero.

You solve an equation between two fractions by **multiplying by both denominators**. Afterwards the left side holds the product of <InlineMath math="5" /> and the right denominator, and the right side holds the product of <InlineMath math="2" /> and the left denominator. Because <InlineMath math="x\neq-1" /> and <InlineMath math="x\neq2" />, neither denominator is zero.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
5(x-2)&=2(x+1) \\
5x-10&=2x+2 \\
3x&=12 \\
x&=4
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, for <InlineMath math="x=4" /> the left side is <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{5}=1" /> and the right side is <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{2}=1" />. Also, <InlineMath math="4" /> is not one of the excluded numbers.

So the solution of the equation is <InlineMath math="x=4" />.

- <InlineMath math="x=-3" /> multiplies each numerator by its own denominator and solves <InlineMath math="5(x+1)=2(x-2)" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=-\frac{8}{3}" /> moves <InlineMath math="-10" /> to the right side with the wrong sign and computes <InlineMath math="3x=2-10" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=\frac{4}{3}" /> multiplies the <InlineMath math="5" /> by <InlineMath math="x" /> only and gets <InlineMath math="5x-2=2x+2" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=\frac{11}{3}" /> multiplies the <InlineMath math="2" /> by <InlineMath math="x" /> only and gets <InlineMath math="5x-10=2x+1" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=12" /> stops at <InlineMath math="3x=12" /> and does not divide by <InlineMath math="3" />.

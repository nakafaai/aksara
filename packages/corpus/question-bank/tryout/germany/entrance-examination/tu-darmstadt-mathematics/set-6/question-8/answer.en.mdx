# Given

The six faces of a **cuboid** (*Quader*) have three different areas (*Flächeninhalte*): <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}^2" />, <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}^2" />, and <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^2" />, with opposite faces having equal area.

# Asked

Find the volume of this cuboid.

# Solution

You do not even need to guess the length, width, and height one by one first! Call the three edges <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" />. When you multiply all three distinct face areas together, every edge appears twice, so **the product of the three face areas equals the volume squared**!

> For a cuboid with three distinct face areas, multiplying those three areas together always gives the square of the volume, so one square root finds the volume directly.

Write the three face-area equations and multiply them together.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
ab = 12, \qquad bc &= 15, \qquad ac = 20 \\
(ab)(bc)(ac) = (abc)^2 &= 12 \cdot 15 \cdot 20 = 3600 \\
V = abc &= \sqrt{3600} = 60\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

## Checking wrong choices

Notice how each distractor confuses **length, surface area, and volume**.

- <InlineMath math="47\text{ cm}^3" /> simply adds the three face areas (<InlineMath math="12 + 15 + 20" />) instead of finding volume.
- <InlineMath math="94\text{ cm}^3" /> is actually the total surface area (<InlineMath math="94\text{ cm}^2" />), not the volume.
- <InlineMath math="180\text{ cm}^3" /> multiplies only two face areas (<InlineMath math="12 \cdot 15" />), squaring one edge while omitting proper units.
- <InlineMath math="3\,600\text{ cm}^3" /> is the volume squared before taking the square root.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}^3" /> adds the three edge lengths (<InlineMath math="4 + 3 + 5" />) instead of multiplying them.

# Answer

The volume of the cuboid is <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" />.

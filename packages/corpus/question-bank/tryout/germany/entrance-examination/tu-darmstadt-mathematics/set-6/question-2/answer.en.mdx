The task says *Zerlegen Sie vollständig in Linearfaktoren* (factor completely into linear factors). With four terms, **factoring in two groups** often helps. Group the first two and the last two terms and look for the same bracket in each group.

<BlockMath math="x^3-2x^2-9x+18=x^2(x-2)-9(x-2)" />

Watch the sign in the second group: <InlineMath math="-9x+18=-9\cdot(x-2)" />, because <InlineMath math="-9\cdot(-2)=+18" />. Now both groups contain the factor <InlineMath math="(x-2)" />, so factor it out. The rest, <InlineMath math="x^2-9" />, is a difference of squares.

<BlockMath math="(x-2)(x^2-9)=(x-2)(x-3)(x+3)" />

For <InlineMath math="x=2" /> you get <InlineMath math="8-8-18+18=0" />, for <InlineMath math="x=3" /> you get <InlineMath math="27-18-27+18=0" />, and for <InlineMath math="x=-3" /> you get <InlineMath math="-27-18+27+18=0" />. At <InlineMath math="x=0" /> both forms have the value <InlineMath math="18" />.

So the factorization into linear factors is <InlineMath math="(x-2)(x-3)(x+3)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(x+2)(x-3)(x+3)" /> has no zero at <InlineMath math="x=2" />, while the expression does. The error comes from writing <InlineMath math="-9x+18" /> as <InlineMath math="-9(x+2)" />.

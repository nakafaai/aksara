# Write the roots as absolute values

Under the roots there are squares: <InlineMath math="x^2-6x+9=(x-3)^2" /> and <InlineMath math="x^2+4x+4=(x+2)^2" />. A root is never negative, so **<InlineMath math="\sqrt{u^2}=|u|" />, not <InlineMath math="u" />**:

<BlockMath math="|x-3|+|x+2|=7" />

# Go through the three regions

The absolute values change sign at <InlineMath math="x=-2" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" />. That splits the number line into three regions, and **each region has its own equation**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x<-2:&\quad (3-x)+(-x-2)=1-2x=7\ \Rightarrow\ x=-3 \\
-2\le x\le3:&\quad (3-x)+(x+2)=5\ne7 \\
x>3:&\quad (x-3)+(x+2)=2x-1=7\ \Rightarrow\ x=4
\end{aligned}" />

Both values lie in their region: <InlineMath math="-3<-2" /> and <InlineMath math="4>3" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- "only <InlineMath math="x=4" />" sets <InlineMath math="\sqrt{(x-3)^2}=x-3" /> and <InlineMath math="\sqrt{(x+2)^2}=x+2" /> without absolute values. Then **the solution left of <InlineMath math="-2" /> is missing**.
- "only <InlineMath math="x=-3" />" checks only the left region.
- <InlineMath math="x=-4" /> or <InlineMath math="x=3" /> comes from swapped signs in both absolute values, that is from <InlineMath math="|x+3|+|x-2|=7" />.
- "all <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="-2\le x\le3" />" is the region where the sum is <InlineMath math="5" />, not <InlineMath math="7" />.
- "no such number" overlooks the two outer regions.

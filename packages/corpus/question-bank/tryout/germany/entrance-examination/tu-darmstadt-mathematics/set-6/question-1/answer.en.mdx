# Given

We are given a linear equation with variable <InlineMath math="x" /> and a **real parameter <InlineMath math="a" />**.

<BlockMath math="ax + 3 = 2x - a" />

The problem asks for the condition *keine reelle Lösung*, meaning the equation has **no real solution** for <InlineMath math="x" />.

# Asked

For which values of <InlineMath math="a" /> does the equation have no real solution <InlineMath math="x" />?

# Solution

Picture the equation as a balance scale where <InlineMath math="x" /> is a mystery box. To see how many boxes sit on the scale, **gather every <InlineMath math="x" /> term on the left** and all other terms on the right.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
ax - 2x &= -a - 3 \\
(a - 2)\,x &= -(a + 3)
\end{aligned}" />

Now look at the **coefficient in front of <InlineMath math="x" />**, which is <InlineMath math="a - 2" />. Whenever that number is not zero, you can divide both sides by <InlineMath math="a - 2" /> and find one exact value of <InlineMath math="x" />.

<BlockMath math="x = -\frac{a + 3}{a - 2} \qquad (a \neq 2)" />

> A linear equation has no solution precisely when the **coefficient in front of <InlineMath math="x" /> equals zero**, while the **constant on the other side stays nonzero**, leaving an impossible statement.

Trouble happens only when the **divisor turns to zero**, which occurs at <InlineMath math="a = 2" />. Substitute <InlineMath math="a = 2" /> into the factored form.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(2 - 2)\,x &= -(2 + 3) \\
0 \cdot x &= -5 \\
0 &= -5
\end{aligned}" />

No matter what number you put inside <InlineMath math="x" />, zero times any number stays zero and **can never equal <InlineMath math="-5" />**. As a quick check, substitute <InlineMath math="a = 2" /> directly into the original equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x + 3 &= 2x - 2 \\
3 &= -2
\end{aligned}" />

This contradiction confirms that <InlineMath math="a = 2" /> leaves the equation with no real solution.

## Why the Other Options Fail

- At <InlineMath math="a = -3" />, the right side becomes zero and leaves <InlineMath math="-5x = 0" />. That equation still has the **valid solution <InlineMath math="x = 0" />**.
- The value <InlineMath math="a = -2" /> comes from a **sign mistake** when moving <InlineMath math="2x" /> across the equals sign, wrongly producing <InlineMath math="(a + 2)\,x" />.
- Pairing <InlineMath math="a = 2" /> and <InlineMath math="a = -3" /> merges two different behaviors. Only <InlineMath math="a = 2" /> creates a contradiction, whereas <InlineMath math="a = -3" /> is solved by <InlineMath math="x = 0" />.
- The option *für keinen Wert* (for no value) overlooks that **division by zero is impossible** when <InlineMath math="a - 2" /> vanishes.
- Every <InlineMath math="a \neq 2" /> produces exactly one real solution, which is the opposite of what the question asks.

# Answer

The equation has no real solution precisely for <InlineMath math="a = 2" />.

# Given

In **triangle** (*Dreieck*) <InlineMath math="ABC" />, the side lengths are <InlineMath math="\overline{AB} = 6" />, <InlineMath math="\overline{AC} = 4" />, and <InlineMath math="\overline{BC} = 5" />. The **angle bisector** (*Winkelhalbierende*) at vertex <InlineMath math="A" /> meets side <InlineMath math="\overline{BC}" /> at point <InlineMath math="D" />.

# Asked

Find the length of the angle bisector segment <InlineMath math="\overline{AD}" />.

# Solution

An angle bisector splits the opposite side <InlineMath math="BC" /> fairly in **the exact ratio of the two sides flanking angle <InlineMath math="A" />** (<InlineMath math="6 : 4" />, or <InlineMath math="3 : 2" />)! Once <InlineMath math="D" /> is placed, apply the law of cosines twice to the same corner angle <InlineMath math="B" />: once in the full triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />, and once in the smaller triangle <InlineMath math="ABD" />.

> An angle bisector divides the opposite side in the ratio of the adjacent sides, so pairing the angle bisector theorem with the law of cosines gives the bisector length cleanly.

Split side <InlineMath math="\overline{BC} = 5" /> at point <InlineMath math="D" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{\overline{BD}}{\overline{DC}} = \frac{\overline{AB}}{\overline{AC}} = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}, \qquad \overline{BD} + \overline{DC} = 5 \implies \overline{BD} = 3, \quad \overline{DC} = 2" />

With <InlineMath math="\overline{BD} = 3" /> fixed, triangle <InlineMath math="ABD" /> shares the exact same angle <InlineMath math="\beta" /> as triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />.

## Applying the law of cosines twice

Find **the value of <InlineMath math="\cos\beta" /> in the full triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />**, then plug it into the smaller triangle <InlineMath math="ABD" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\cos\beta &= \frac{6^2 + 5^2 - 4^2}{2 \cdot 6 \cdot 5} = \frac{45}{60} = \frac{3}{4} \\
\overline{AD}^2 &= 6^2 + 3^2 - 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 3 \cdot \frac{3}{4} = 45 - 27 = 18 \implies \overline{AD} = 3\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}" />

Quick check with the angle bisector length identity confirms the squared length:

<BlockMath math="\overline{AD}^2 = \overline{AB} \cdot \overline{AC} - \overline{BD} \cdot \overline{DC} = 6 \cdot 4 - 3 \cdot 2 = 18" />

# Answer

The length of segment <InlineMath math="\overline{AD}" /> is <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{2}" />.

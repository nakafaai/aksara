# Where the bisector meets the side

The angle bisector splits the opposite side **in the ratio of the two adjacent sides**:

<BlockMath math="\frac{\overline{BD}}{\overline{DC}}=\frac{\overline{AB}}{\overline{AC}}=\frac64=\frac32,\qquad \overline{BD}+\overline{DC}=5\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \overline{BD}=3,\ \overline{DC}=2" />

# Find the angle at <InlineMath math="B" />

In the whole triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> you know all three sides. The cosine rule gives **the cosine of the angle at <InlineMath math="B" />**:

<BlockMath math="\cos\beta=\frac{6^2+5^2-4^2}{2\cdot6\cdot5}=\frac{45}{60}=\frac34" />

# The cosine rule in triangle <InlineMath math="ABD" />

In triangle <InlineMath math="ABD" /> you now know <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}=6" />, <InlineMath math="\overline{BD}=3" /> and the angle <InlineMath math="\beta" /> between them. Use the cosine rule again:

<BlockMath math="\overline{AD}^2=6^2+3^2-2\cdot6\cdot3\cdot\frac34=45-27=18\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \overline{AD}=3\sqrt2" />

The segment is **<InlineMath math="3\sqrt2" /> long**, about <InlineMath math="4.24" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{22}" /> splits in **the reversed ratio**, <InlineMath math="\overline{BD}=2" />: <InlineMath math="36+4-2\cdot6\cdot2\cdot\frac34=22" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{\sqrt{79}}{2}" /> is the length of the median from <InlineMath math="A" />, with <InlineMath math="D" /> at the midpoint of <InlineMath math="\overline{BC}" />.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt6" /> is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{\overline{AB}\cdot\overline{AC}}" />. That shortcut leaves out the correction term of the bisector formula.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> is <InlineMath math="\overline{BD}" />, not <InlineMath math="\overline{AD}" />.
- <InlineMath math="3\sqrt3" /> uses <InlineMath math="\cos\beta=\frac12" />, as if the angle at <InlineMath math="B" /> were <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />: <InlineMath math="45-36\cdot\frac12=27" />.

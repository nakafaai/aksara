# Given

A **triangle** (*Dreieck*) has vertices <InlineMath math="A(0 \mid 0)" />, <InlineMath math="B(8 \mid 0)" />, and <InlineMath math="C(2 \mid 6)" />. Its three altitudes (*Höhen*) meet at a single orthocenter (*Höhenschnittpunkt*) <InlineMath math="H" />.

# Asked

Find the coordinates of the orthocenter <InlineMath math="H" />.

# Solution

Because all three altitudes of any triangle concur at one point, you only need **the two easiest altitudes to pin down <InlineMath math="H" />**, then use the third altitude as a free cross-check! Side <InlineMath math="AB" /> lies flat along the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, so the altitude from <InlineMath math="C(2 \mid 6)" /> drops straight down along <InlineMath math="x = 2" />!

> To find an orthocenter in coordinates, intersect the two simplest altitudes using the rule that perpendicular slopes multiply to minus one, then verify with the third altitude.

Next find the altitude from <InlineMath math="A(0 \mid 0)" /> perpendicular to side <InlineMath math="BC" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m_{BC} &= \frac{6 - 0}{2 - 8} = -1 \implies m_{h_A} = 1 \\
h_A &: y = x, \qquad h_C : x = 2 \implies H(2 \mid 2)
\end{aligned}" />

Check using the third altitude from <InlineMath math="B(8 \mid 0)" />: side <InlineMath math="AC" /> has slope <InlineMath math="3" />, so its perpendicular from <InlineMath math="B" /> has slope <InlineMath math="-\frac{1}{3}" /> and passes straight through <InlineMath math="H(2 \mid 2)" />.

<BlockMath math="y = -\frac{1}{3}(2 - 8) = 2" />

## Checking wrong choices

Notice how the top distractors are actually **other famous triangle centers**!

- <InlineMath math="H(4 \mid 2)" /> is the circumcenter (*Umkreismittelpunkt*), where the perpendicular bisectors meet.
- <InlineMath math="H\left(\frac{10}{3} \mid 2\right)" /> is the centroid (*Schwerpunkt*), where the three medians intersect.
- <InlineMath math="H(2 \mid 6)" /> is simply vertex <InlineMath math="C" />, which lies on only one altitude.
- <InlineMath math="H(2 \mid 4)" /> comes from giving the altitude through <InlineMath math="A" /> a wrong slope of <InlineMath math="2" /> instead of <InlineMath math="1" />.
- <InlineMath math="H(6 \mid 2)" /> swaps the coordinates of <InlineMath math="C" /> to <InlineMath math="(6 \mid 2)" /> and uses the vertical line <InlineMath math="x = 6" />.

# Answer

The orthocenter has coordinates **<InlineMath math="H(2 \mid 2)" />**.

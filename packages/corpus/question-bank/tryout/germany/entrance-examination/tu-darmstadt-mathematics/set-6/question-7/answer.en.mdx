The task asks **how many times the small diameter fits into the large one**. That is a quotient: the large diameter divided by the small one. The unit metre cancels.

When you divide powers of ten, subtract the exponents. Watch the two minus signs.

<BlockMath math="\frac{2\cdot10^{-5}}{1\cdot10^{-7}}=2\cdot10^{-5-(-7)}=2\cdot10^{2}=200" />

As a check, work in micrometres. The cell measures <InlineMath math="2\cdot10^{-5}\text{ m}=20\ \mu\text{m}" />, the virus <InlineMath math="1\cdot10^{-7}\text{ m}=0.1\ \mu\text{m}" />. And <InlineMath math="20:0.1=200" />.

So <InlineMath math="200" /> viruses would fit side by side across the diameter of the cell.

- <InlineMath math="50" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot10^2" />. The leading factor is flipped, <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> instead of <InlineMath math="\frac21" />.
- <InlineMath math="2\cdot10^{-2}" /> subtracts the exponents in the wrong order, <InlineMath math="-7-(-5)=-2" />.
- <InlineMath math="2\cdot10^{-12}" /> adds the exponents, as if multiplying instead of dividing.
- <InlineMath math="2\cdot10^{12}" /> uses the sizes of the exponents, <InlineMath math="5+7=12" />, and drops their signs.
- <InlineMath math="5\cdot10^{-3}" /> is the reverse quotient, virus divided by cell. That is the virus's share of the cell's diameter, not a count.

# A new variable gives a quadratic equation

All the powers have base <InlineMath math="2" />. Write <InlineMath math="2^{2x+1}=2\cdot\left(2^x\right)^2" /> and set <InlineMath math="t=2^x" />. **Here <InlineMath math="t>0" />**, because a power of <InlineMath math="2" /> is always positive:

<BlockMath math="2t^2-9t+4=0" />

# Solve the quadratic equation

<BlockMath math="t=\frac{9\pm\sqrt{81-32}}{4}=\frac{9\pm7}{4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=4\ \text{or}\ t=\frac12" />

Both values are positive, so **neither drops out**.

# Back to <InlineMath math="x" />

Each value of <InlineMath math="t" /> gives **exactly one <InlineMath math="x" />**, because <InlineMath math="2^x" /> takes every positive value exactly once:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2^x=4&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=2 \\
2^x=\tfrac12&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=-1
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\left\{\frac12;\,4\right\}" /> are the values of <InlineMath math="t" />. **The way back to <InlineMath math="x" /> is missing**.
- <InlineMath math="\{2\}" /> throws away <InlineMath math="x=-1" />, probably because a negative solution looks odd. Negative exponents are allowed, though.
- <InlineMath math="\{1;\,2\}" /> makes a sign slip on the way back: <InlineMath math="2^x=\frac12" /> holds for <InlineMath math="x=-1" />, not for <InlineMath math="x=1" />.
- <InlineMath math="\{2-\log_2 7\}" /> comes from writing <InlineMath math="2^{2x+1}" /> as <InlineMath math="2t" /> instead of <InlineMath math="2t^2" />: <InlineMath math="2t-9t+4=0" /> gives <InlineMath math="t=\frac47" />.
- The values with <InlineMath math="\frac{9\pm\sqrt{65}}{2}" /> come from dropping the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> in front of <InlineMath math="t^2" />: <InlineMath math="t^2-9t+4=0" />.

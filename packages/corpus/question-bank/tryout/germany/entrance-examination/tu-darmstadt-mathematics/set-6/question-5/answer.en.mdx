# Write the intercepts in terms of the slope

A line through <InlineMath math="P(2\mid3)" /> with slope <InlineMath math="m<0" /> has the equation <InlineMath math="y=m(x-2)+3" />. Its **intercepts with the axes** depend on <InlineMath math="m" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x=0:&\quad y_0=3-2m \\
y=0:&\quad x_0=2-\frac3m
\end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="m<0" /> both are positive, so the triangle lies in the first quadrant.

# Set up the area condition

The triangle is right-angled with legs <InlineMath math="x_0" /> and <InlineMath math="y_0" />. **Don't forget the factor <InlineMath math="\frac12" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac12\left(3-2m\right)\left(2-\tfrac3m\right)&=12 \\
12-4m-\tfrac9m&=24 \\
4m^2+12m+9&=0 \\
(2m+3)^2&=0
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="y=-\frac23x+\frac{13}{3}" /> uses the reciprocal of the slope. Its triangle has legs <InlineMath math="6.5" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac{13}{3}" /> and **not the area <InlineMath math="12" />**.
- <InlineMath math="y=-3x+9" /> or <InlineMath math="y=-0.75x+4.5" /> expects two lines. Both pass through <InlineMath math="P" />, but each bounds a triangle of area <InlineMath math="13.5" />.
- <InlineMath math="y=-\frac23x+4" /> swaps the intercepts <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="6" />. This line doesn't pass through <InlineMath math="P" />, because <InlineMath math="-\frac23\cdot2+4=\frac83\ne3" />.
- <InlineMath math="y=1.5x" /> has a positive slope and passes through the origin.
- "There is no such line" comes from leaving out the factor <InlineMath math="\frac12" />: then the condition leads to <InlineMath math="4m^2=-9" />.

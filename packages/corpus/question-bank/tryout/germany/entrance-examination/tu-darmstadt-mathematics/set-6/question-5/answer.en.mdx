# Given

A **straight line** (*Gerade*) <InlineMath math="g" /> with negative slope (*negative Steigung*) passes through <InlineMath math="P(2 \mid 3)" /> and cuts both the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis and positive <InlineMath math="y" />-axis. Together with the two axes, <InlineMath math="g" /> encloses a right triangle of area <InlineMath math="12" />.

# Asked

Find the equation of line <InlineMath math="g" />.

# Solution

Picture pivoting a straight ruler pinned at <InlineMath math="P(2 \mid 3)" />! Tilt it too flat or too steep and one intercept shoots far out, inflating the triangle's area. The smallest possible triangle occurs right when **<InlineMath math="P(2 \mid 3)" /> sits at the exact midpoint between both intercepts**, <InlineMath math="(4 \mid 0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0 \mid 6)" />, yielding area exactly <InlineMath math="12" />!

> The triangle formed by a negatively sloped line through a first-quadrant point reaches its minimum area precisely when that point bisects the segment between the two intercepts, producing a single unique line.

Write the line of slope <InlineMath math="m < 0" /> through <InlineMath math="P(2 \mid 3)" /> and express both axis intercepts.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y &= m(x - 2) + 3 \\
x = 0 &\implies y_0 = 3 - 2m, \qquad y = 0 \implies x_0 = 2 - \frac{3}{m}
\end{aligned}" />

## Solving the area equation to find the double root

Substitute both legs <InlineMath math="x_0" /> and <InlineMath math="y_0" /> into the triangle area formula, keeping **the factor of one half**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2}(3 - 2m)\left(2 - \frac{3}{m}\right) &= 12 \\
12 - 4m - \frac{9}{m} &= 24 \\
4m^2 + 12m + 9 &= 0 \\
(2m + 3)^2 &= 0 \implies m = -\frac{3}{2} = -1.5
\end{aligned}" />

This double root yields one unique line with intercepts <InlineMath math="(4 \mid 0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0 \mid 6)" />.

## Checking the area and wrong choices

Quick verification: the line passes through <InlineMath math="(2 \mid 3)" /> and forms a triangle with base <InlineMath math="4" /> and height <InlineMath math="6" />, giving **area exactly <InlineMath math="12" />**.

| Option | Why it is wrong |
| :--- | :--- |
| <InlineMath math="y = -\frac{2}{3}x + \frac{13}{3}" /> | Uses the reciprocal slope, producing an area different from <InlineMath math="12" />. |
| <InlineMath math="y = -3x + 9" /> or <InlineMath math="y = -0.75x + 4.5" /> | Both pass through <InlineMath math="P" />, yet each encloses area <InlineMath math="13.5" /> instead of <InlineMath math="12" />. |
| <InlineMath math="y = -\frac{2}{3}x + 4" /> | Swaps the intercepts <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="6" /> and misses <InlineMath math="P(2 \mid 3)" />. |
| <InlineMath math="y = 1.5x" /> | Has positive slope and passes through the origin, enclosing no triangle. |
| No such line exists | Drops the <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}" /> factor from the area formula and hits <InlineMath math="4m^2 = -9" />. |

# Answer

The equation of line **<InlineMath math="g" />** is **<InlineMath math="y = -1.5x + 6" />**.

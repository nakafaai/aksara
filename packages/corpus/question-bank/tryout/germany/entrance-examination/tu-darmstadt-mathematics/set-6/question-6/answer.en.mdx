# Find the rate from two increases

Growth at the rate <InlineMath math="p" /> (as a decimal) multiplies production by <InlineMath math="1+p" />. Two equal increases in a row give **the factor <InlineMath math="(1+p)^2" />**, and that is <InlineMath math="1.44" />:

<BlockMath math="(1+p)^2=1.44\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1+p=1.2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p=0.2" />

The negative root <InlineMath math="1+p=-1.2" /> gives no sensible factor.

# Measure the cut against the new value

In the third year production falls by <InlineMath math="20\,\%" />, measured **from the level it has reached**, that is from <InlineMath math="1.44" /> times the start:

<BlockMath math="1.44\cdot(1-0.2)=1.44\cdot0.8=1.152" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- "by <InlineMath math="24\,\%" />" **subtracts <InlineMath math="20" /> percentage points from <InlineMath math="44\,\%" />** instead of applying the cut to the new value.
- "by <InlineMath math="22\,\%" />" halves <InlineMath math="44\,\%" /> into a rate per year and then works out <InlineMath math="44-22" />.
- "by <InlineMath math="20\,\%" />" assumes one increase and the cut cancel each other.
- "by <InlineMath math="12.32\,\%" />" uses <InlineMath math="22\,\%" /> as the rate: <InlineMath math="1.44\cdot0.78=1.1232" />.
- "by <InlineMath math="0\,\%" />" assumes the cut undoes all the increases.

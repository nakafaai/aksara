# Given

Two congruent circles each have **radius <InlineMath math="r = 6\text{ cm}" />**.

Each circle passes through the center of the other, so **the distance between their centers equals the radius**, <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />. Their overlap forms a symmetric lens.

# Asked

What is the area (*Flächeninhalt*) of this overlapping lens region?

# Solution

Picture two identical coins slid together until each rim touches the center dot of the other coin. Draw a straight line connecting the two points where the rims cross, and the lens splits into **two identical curved cap segments**. Find one cap and double it.

> Because each circle passes through the other center, the triangle formed by the two centers and an intersection point is equilateral with exact <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> angles.

## Finding the central angle from equilateral triangles

Label the centers <InlineMath math="M_1" /> and <InlineMath math="M_2" /> and the intersection points <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />. In triangle <InlineMath math="M_1 M_2 P" />, **all three sides have length <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />**: two radii and the center-to-center segment.

That makes triangle <InlineMath math="M_1 M_2 P" /> equilateral, so <InlineMath math="\angle M_2 M_1 P = 60^\circ" />. By symmetry below, <InlineMath math="\angle M_2 M_1 Q = 60^\circ" /> too, giving a full central angle of <InlineMath math="\angle P M_1 Q = 120^\circ" />.

## Computing one circular segment and doubling

One circular segment equals **the <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> sector minus triangle <InlineMath math="M_1 P Q" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{\text{sector}} &= \frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ} \cdot \pi \cdot 6^2 = 12\pi \\
A_{\text{triangle}} &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6 \cdot 6 \cdot \sin 120^\circ = 18 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 9\sqrt{3} \\
A_{\text{segment}} &= 12\pi - 9\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}" />

Since the full lens is made of two identical segments, double that area:

<BlockMath math="A = 2\left(12\pi - 9\sqrt{3}\right) = \left(24\pi - 18\sqrt{3}\right)\text{ cm}^2" />

Sanity check: this is about <InlineMath math="44.2\text{ cm}^2" />, a little under half of one full circle (<InlineMath math="36\pi \approx 113.1\text{ cm}^2" />), which matches the drawing.

## Checking the distractors

Each wrong option comes from **forgetting to double or misreading the central angle**:

| Option | Why it is wrong |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="(12\pi - 9\sqrt{3})\text{ cm}^2" /> | Measures only one segment, half of the lens. |
| <InlineMath math="(12\pi - 18\sqrt{3})\text{ cm}^2" /> | Uses <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> instead of <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> for each sector angle. |
| <InlineMath math="24\pi\text{ cm}^2" /> | Adds the two sectors without subtracting the two triangles. |
| <InlineMath math="(24\pi - 18)\text{ cm}^2" /> | Substitutes <InlineMath math="\sin 120^\circ = \frac{1}{2}" /> instead of <InlineMath math="\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" />. |
| <InlineMath math="(36\pi - 36\sqrt{3})\text{ cm}^2" /> | Uses <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> sectors and triangles of twice the actual area. |

# Answer

The area of the overlapping lens region is **<InlineMath math="(24\pi - 18\sqrt{3})\text{ cm}^2" />**.

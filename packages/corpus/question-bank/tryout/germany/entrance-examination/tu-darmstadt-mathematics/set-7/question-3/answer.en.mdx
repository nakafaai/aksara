# Find the central angle

Call the centres <InlineMath math="M_1" /> and <InlineMath math="M_2" /> and the intersection points <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />. In triangle <InlineMath math="M_1M_2P" /> every side is <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />: two radii and the distance between the centres. **The triangle is equilateral**, so <InlineMath math="\angle M_2M_1P=60^\circ" />. In the same way <InlineMath math="\angle M_2M_1Q=60^\circ" />, together <InlineMath math="\angle PM_1Q=120^\circ" />.

# One circular segment

The chord <InlineMath math="\overline{PQ}" /> splits the lens into two equal circular segments. One segment is **the <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> sector minus triangle <InlineMath math="M_1PQ" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{\text{sector}}&=\frac{120}{360}\cdot\pi\cdot6^2=12\pi \\
A_{\text{triangle}}&=\frac12\cdot6\cdot6\cdot\sin120^\circ=18\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=9\sqrt3 \\
A_{\text{segment}}&=12\pi-9\sqrt3
\end{aligned}" />

# The whole lens

<BlockMath math="A=2\left(12\pi-9\sqrt3\right)=(24\pi-18\sqrt3)\text{ cm}^2" />

That's about <InlineMath math="44.2\text{ cm}^2" />. The common region has **an area of <InlineMath math="(24\pi-18\sqrt3)\text{ cm}^2" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="(12\pi-9\sqrt3)\text{ cm}^2" /> is **only one circular segment**, half the lens.
- <InlineMath math="(12\pi-18\sqrt3)\text{ cm}^2" /> uses <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> instead of <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> as the central angle: <InlineMath math="2\left(6\pi-9\sqrt3\right)" />.
- <InlineMath math="24\pi\text{ cm}^2" /> counts the two sectors and forgets to subtract the triangles.
- <InlineMath math="(24\pi-18)\text{ cm}^2" /> takes <InlineMath math="\sin120^\circ=\frac12" />.
- <InlineMath math="(36\pi-36\sqrt3)\text{ cm}^2" /> uses <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> sectors and triangles with twice the area.

# The domain first

The logarithm is only defined for positive arguments:

<BlockMath math="x^2-3x=x(x-3)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x<0\ \text{or}\ x>3" />

Only numbers in this range **can be solutions at all**.

# A base below <InlineMath math="1" /> turns the inequality round

Write <InlineMath math="-2=\log_{\frac12}4" />, because <InlineMath math="\left(\frac12\right)^{-2}=4" />. The logarithm to base <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> is **strictly decreasing**: a larger logarithm belongs to a smaller argument. So the sign flips:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\log_{\frac12}\left(x^2-3x\right)&\ge\log_{\frac12}4 \\
x^2-3x&\le4 \\
(x-4)(x+1)&\le0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -1\le x\le4
\end{aligned}" />

# Both conditions together

Intersect <InlineMath math="-1\le x\le4" /> with the domain. The numbers from <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="3" /> **drop out**, and so do the endpoints <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> themselves:

The inequality holds for <InlineMath math="-1\le x<0" /> or <InlineMath math="3<x\le4" />.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-1\le x\le4" /> **forgets the domain**. For <InlineMath math="x=1" /> the argument would be <InlineMath math="-2" />.
- <InlineMath math="x\le-1" /> or <InlineMath math="x\ge4" /> doesn't flip the inequality sign, as if the base were greater than <InlineMath math="1" />.
- <InlineMath math="x<0" /> or <InlineMath math="x>3" /> is only the domain.
- <InlineMath math="-1<x<0" /> or <InlineMath math="3<x<4" /> excludes the endpoints <InlineMath math="-1" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />. There the logarithm is exactly <InlineMath math="-2" />, and <InlineMath math="\ge" /> allows that.
- <InlineMath math="-1\le x\le0" /> or <InlineMath math="3\le x\le4" /> includes <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />. There the argument is <InlineMath math="0" /> and the logarithm isn't defined.

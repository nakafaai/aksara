# Equally far means perpendicular bisector

All points that are equally far from <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> lie **on the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />**. It passes through the midpoint <InlineMath math="M(3\mid3)" /> and is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />. The segment has slope <InlineMath math="\frac{1-5}{5-1}=-1" />, so the bisector has slope <InlineMath math="1" />:

<BlockMath math="y-3=1\cdot(x-3)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=x" />

# Find the intersection with <InlineMath math="g" />

<InlineMath math="P" /> lies on both lines, so **<InlineMath math="2x-1=x" /> holds**:

<BlockMath math="x=1,\quad y=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(1\mid1)" />

# Work out the distance

<BlockMath math="\overline{PA}=\sqrt{(1-1)^2+(5-1)^2}=4,\qquad \overline{PB}=\sqrt{(5-1)^2+(1-1)^2}=4" />

Both distances agree. <InlineMath math="P" /> is **<InlineMath math="4" /> units from <InlineMath math="A" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{4\sqrt2}{3}" /> comes from slope <InlineMath math="-1" /> instead of <InlineMath math="1" />, that is from the line <InlineMath math="AB" /> itself: it meets <InlineMath math="g" /> at <InlineMath math="\left(\frac73\mid\frac{11}{3}\right)" />, **which is much farther from <InlineMath math="B" /> than from <InlineMath math="A" />**.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt2" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="A" /> to the midpoint <InlineMath math="M(3\mid3)" />. That point doesn't lie on <InlineMath math="g" />.
- <InlineMath math="4\sqrt2" /> is the length of the whole segment <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to the origin.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> comes from putting the midpoint's <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate into <InlineMath math="g" />: <InlineMath math="P(3\mid5)" /> is <InlineMath math="2" /> from <InlineMath math="A" /> but <InlineMath math="2\sqrt5" /> from <InlineMath math="B" />.

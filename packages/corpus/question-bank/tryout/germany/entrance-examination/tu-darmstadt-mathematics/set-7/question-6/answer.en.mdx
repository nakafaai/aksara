# Given

A monic quadratic function (leading coefficient <InlineMath math="1" />) has the form:

<BlockMath math="f(x) = x^2 + bx + c" />

- Its graph crosses the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at two points **<InlineMath math="6" /> units apart**.
- Its vertex (*Scheitelpunkt*) lies on the straight line <InlineMath math="y = x - 10" />.

# Asked

What is the function equation (*Funktionsgleichung*) of <InlineMath math="f(x)" />?

# Solution

Picture a standard parabola bowl <InlineMath math="y = x^2" /> with a fixed shape that you can only slide around. The deeper you dip its bottom below the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, the wider the cut becomes. Because that width is fixed at <InlineMath math="6" /> units, **the vertex depth is locked immediately**.

> For a monic parabola, the spacing between its two roots fixes the vertex depth right away, and the given line then locks the horizontal shift.

## Locking the vertex depth from the root spacing

Write the function in vertex form with vertex <InlineMath math="S(h \mid k)" />:

<BlockMath math="f(x) = (x - h)^2 + k" />

By symmetry, the two roots sit <InlineMath math="3" /> units to the left and right of <InlineMath math="h" />. At <InlineMath math="x = h + 3" />, **the output must equal zero**:

<BlockMath math="(h + 3 - h)^2 + k = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad 3^2 + k = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad k = -9" />

No matter what <InlineMath math="h" /> is, the vertex must sit <InlineMath math="9" /> units below the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

## Finding the horizontal shift from the line

Because the vertex <InlineMath math="S(h \mid -9)" /> lies on <InlineMath math="y = x - 10" />, **its coordinates satisfy the line equation**:

<BlockMath math="-9 = h - 10 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad h = 1" />

Expand the vertex form into standard form:

<BlockMath math="f(x) = (x - 1)^2 - 9 = x^2 - 2x + 1 - 9 = x^2 - 2x - 8" />

Verification by factoring:

<BlockMath math="x^2 - 2x - 8 = (x - 4)(x + 2)" />

Its roots are <InlineMath math="-2" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> separated by <InlineMath math="6" />, and vertex <InlineMath math="(1 \mid -9)" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="1 - 10 = -9" />.

## Checking the distractors

Each wrong option breaks **either the vertex depth or the line constraint**:

| Option | Why it is wrong |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2 + 2x - 8" /> | Vertex is <InlineMath math="(-1 \mid -9)" />, not on the line because <InlineMath math="-1 - 10 = -11" />. |
| <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2 - 14x + 46" /> | Uses depth <InlineMath math="k = -3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="-3^2 = -9" />. |
| <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2 - 2x - 9" /> | Copies <InlineMath math="k = -9" /> directly as the constant term without adding <InlineMath math="(-1)^2" />. |
| <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2 - 2x + 8" /> | Flips the constant sign, leaving a parabola with no real roots at all. |
| <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2 - 4x - 5" /> | Roots are <InlineMath math="6" /> apart, but vertex <InlineMath math="(2 \mid -9)" /> misses the line. |

# Answer

The equation of the function is:

<BlockMath math="f(x) = x^2 - 2x - 8" />

# The distance between the zeros fixes the depth

Write <InlineMath math="f" /> in vertex form: <InlineMath math="f(x)=(x-h)^2+k" /> with vertex <InlineMath math="S(h\mid k)" />. The zeros lie symmetric about the vertex, <InlineMath math="3" /> to the left and <InlineMath math="3" /> to the right of <InlineMath math="h" />. At <InlineMath math="x=h+3" />, <InlineMath math="f=0" />:

<BlockMath math="3^2+k=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=-9" />

So the vertex lies **<InlineMath math="9" /> units below the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis**, whatever <InlineMath math="h" /> is.

# The line fixes the position

The vertex lies on <InlineMath math="y=x-10" />, so **it satisfies <InlineMath math="k=h-10" />**:

<BlockMath math="-9=h-10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=1" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="f(x)=x^2+2x-8" /> has vertex <InlineMath math="(-1\mid-9)" />. That point **isn't on the line**, because <InlineMath math="-1-10=-11\ne-9" />.
- <InlineMath math="f(x)=x^2-14x+46" /> takes the depth as <InlineMath math="3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="3^2=9" />: <InlineMath math="k=-3" />, <InlineMath math="h=7" />.
- <InlineMath math="f(x)=x^2-2x-9" /> uses <InlineMath math="k=-9" /> as the constant term without expanding <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2" />.
- <InlineMath math="f(x)=x^2-2x+8" /> has the wrong sign on the constant term. This parabola has no zeros at all.
- <InlineMath math="f(x)=x^2-4x-5" /> does have zeros <InlineMath math="6" /> apart, but its vertex <InlineMath math="(2\mid-9)" /> isn't on the line.

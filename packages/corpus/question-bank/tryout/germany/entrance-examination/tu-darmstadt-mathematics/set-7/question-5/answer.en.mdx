# Two similar triangles at each spot

The lamp, the tip of the shadow and the person's head lie on one light ray. The person and the lamp post both stand upright, so the small triangle at the person and the big triangle at the post are **similar**. If the person stands at distance <InlineMath math="d" /> from the post and the shadow is <InlineMath math="s" /> long:

<BlockMath math="\frac{1.8}{s}=\frac{H}{d+s}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H\,s=1.8\,(d+s)" />

# Write down both spots

You don't know <InlineMath math="d" />, but it grows by <InlineMath math="3" />. That gives **two equations in the unknowns <InlineMath math="d" /> and <InlineMath math="H" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2H&=1.8\,(d+2) \\
3.5H&=1.8\,(d+3+3.5)
\end{aligned}" />

# Get rid of the unknown distance

Subtract the first equation from the second. The <InlineMath math="d" /> drops out:

<BlockMath math="1.5H=1.8\cdot4.5=8.1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=5.4" />

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.6\text{ m}" /> is only <InlineMath math="H-1.8" />, the lamp's height **above the person's head**.
- <InlineMath math="3.15\text{ m}" /> multiplies the height by the ratio of the shadows: <InlineMath math="1.8\cdot\frac{3.5}{2}" />.
- <InlineMath math="6.3\text{ m}" /> is <InlineMath math="1.8\cdot3.5" />, as if the second shadow were the distance to the post.
- <InlineMath math="2.7\text{ m}" /> is <InlineMath math="1.8\cdot1.5" />, the height times the growth of the shadow.
- <InlineMath math="7.2\text{ m}" /> counts the person's height twice: <InlineMath math="5.4+1.8" />.

# Simplify the expression first

Write <InlineMath math="u=a^{-1}=\frac1a" /> and <InlineMath math="v=b^{-1}=\frac1b" />. The denominator <InlineMath math="u^2-v^2" /> is a difference of squares, and **the factor <InlineMath math="u+v" /> cancels**:

<BlockMath math="\frac{u+v}{u^2-v^2}=\frac{u+v}{(u-v)(u+v)}=\frac{1}{u-v}=\frac{1}{\frac1a-\frac1b}=\frac{ab}{b-a}" />

# Product and difference of <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b" />

Now you only need <InlineMath math="ab" /> and <InlineMath math="b-a" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
ab&=(\sqrt3+1)(\sqrt3-1)=3-1=2 \\
b-a&=(\sqrt3-1)-(\sqrt3+1)=-2
\end{aligned}" />

# Work out the value

<BlockMath math="\frac{ab}{b-a}=\frac{2}{-2}=-1" />

Check with approximations: <InlineMath math="\frac1a\approx0.366" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac1b\approx1.366" />. The numerator is about <InlineMath math="1.732" />, the denominator about <InlineMath math="0.134-1.866=-1.732" />. The expression has **the value <InlineMath math="-1" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1" /> flips the difference: <InlineMath math="\frac{ab}{a-b}" /> instead of <InlineMath math="\frac{ab}{b-a}" />. That way **the minus sign gets lost**.
- <InlineMath math="-2" /> takes <InlineMath math="ab=4" />, treating <InlineMath math="(\sqrt3+1)(\sqrt3-1)" /> like <InlineMath math="3+1" />.
- <InlineMath math="-\frac12" /> drops the product <InlineMath math="ab" /> from the numerator: <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{b-a}" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> is just the product <InlineMath math="ab" />, without the denominator.
- <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> is just the reciprocal of <InlineMath math="ab" />, that is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{ab}" />.

The task asks by what percentage the average speed (*Durchschnittsgeschwindigkeit*) has risen. For a fixed distance, <InlineMath math="v=\frac{s}{t}" />. Speed and travel time are therefore **inversely proportional**: if the time falls to a fraction, the speed grows by the reciprocal of that fraction.

The new time is <InlineMath math="\frac23" /> of the old one. So the new speed is <InlineMath math="\frac32" /> times the old one.

<BlockMath math="v_{\text{new}}=\frac{s}{\frac23\,t}=\frac32\cdot\frac{s}{t}=1.5\cdot v_{\text{old}}" />

The factor <InlineMath math="1.5" /> means an increase of <InlineMath math="50\,\%" />. As a check, take a distance of <InlineMath math="300\text{ km}" />. Before, the train averages <InlineMath math="300:3=100\ \tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" />, afterwards <InlineMath math="300:2=150\ \tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" />. That is <InlineMath math="50" /> more, which relative to <InlineMath math="100" /> is <InlineMath math="50\,\%" />.

So the average speed has risen by <InlineMath math="50\,\%" />.

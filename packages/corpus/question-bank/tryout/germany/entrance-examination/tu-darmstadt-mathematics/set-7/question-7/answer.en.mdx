# The average comes from distance and time

The average speed is **the whole distance divided by the whole time**, not the mean of the two speeds. Call the one-way distance <InlineMath math="s" /> and the return speed <InlineMath math="v" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{2s}{\frac{s}{20}+\frac{s}{v}}=24" />

# The distance cancels

Cancel <InlineMath math="s" /> and keep working with reciprocals:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{\frac1{20}+\frac1v}&=24 \\
\frac1{20}+\frac1v&=\frac1{12} \\
\frac1v&=\frac1{12}-\frac1{20}=\frac{5-3}{60}=\frac1{30}
\end{aligned}" />

So **the speed is <InlineMath math="v=30" />**.

# Check with a sample distance

For <InlineMath math="s=60\text{ km}" /> the trip out takes <InlineMath math="3" /> hours and the trip back <InlineMath math="2" /> hours. <InlineMath math="120\text{ km}" /> in <InlineMath math="5" /> hours gives an average of <InlineMath math="24\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" />. She rides back **at <InlineMath math="30\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" />**.

# Why the other answers are wrong

- <InlineMath math="28\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" /> uses **the plain average**: <InlineMath math="\frac{20+v}{2}=24" />. But she spends more time on the slow trip out.
- <InlineMath math="28.8\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{24^2}{20}" />, a geometric mean.
- <InlineMath math="40\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" /> doubles the outward speed.
- <InlineMath math="12\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" /> reads the reciprocal <InlineMath math="\frac1{12}" /> as a speed.
- "Not possible at any speed" comes from leaving out the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> for the doubled distance: <InlineMath math="\frac1{20}+\frac1v=\frac1{24}" /> would only have negative solutions.

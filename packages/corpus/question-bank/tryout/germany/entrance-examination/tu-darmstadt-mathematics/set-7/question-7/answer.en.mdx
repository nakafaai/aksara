The task asks by what percentage the travel time (*Fahrzeit*) gets shorter. For a fixed distance, <InlineMath math="t=\frac{s}{v}" />. Travel time and speed are therefore **inversely proportional**: if <InlineMath math="v" /> is multiplied by a factor, <InlineMath math="t" /> is divided by the same factor.

An increase of <InlineMath math="25\,\%" /> means the factor <InlineMath math="1.25=\frac54" /> for the speed. The new travel time is then:

<BlockMath math="t_{\text{new}}=\frac{s}{\frac54\,v}=\frac45\cdot\frac{s}{v}=0.8\cdot t_{\text{old}}" />

So the travel time falls to <InlineMath math="80\,\%" /> of the old one, which is a reduction of <InlineMath math="100\,\%-80\,\%=20\,\%" />. As a check, take an example: <InlineMath math="100\text{ km}" /> at <InlineMath math="80\ \tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" /> takes <InlineMath math="75" /> minutes, at <InlineMath math="100\ \tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}" /> only <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes. That is <InlineMath math="15" /> of <InlineMath math="75" /> minutes less, so <InlineMath math="20\,\%" />.

So the travel time gets shorter by <InlineMath math="20\,\%" />.

- <InlineMath math="25\,\%" /> assumes that the time changes by the same percentage as the speed. With inverse proportionality that is not true.
- <InlineMath math="75\,\%" /> is <InlineMath math="100\,\%-25\,\%" />. That would be the new time after a <InlineMath math="25\,\%" /> reduction, not the change asked for.
- <InlineMath math="80\,\%" /> is the new time as a share of the old one. The task asks how much shorter it is.
- <InlineMath math="12.5\,\%" /> simply halves the <InlineMath math="25\,\%" />. There is no reason for that.
- <InlineMath math="33\tfrac13\,\%" /> belongs to the reverse question. If the time falls by <InlineMath math="25\,\%" />, the speed has to rise by <InlineMath math="33\tfrac13\,\%" />.

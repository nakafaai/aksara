# Peel from the outside

There are two absolute values, one inside the other. **Peel the outer one first**, treating <InlineMath math="u=\lvert x-2\rvert" /> as a single number:

<BlockMath math="\lvert u-3\rvert<1\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad-1<u-3<1\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad2<u<4" />

So the condition becomes <InlineMath math="2<\lvert x-2\rvert<4" />: the distance from <InlineMath math="x" /> to <InlineMath math="2" /> is more than <InlineMath math="2" /> but less than <InlineMath math="4" />.

# Two distance conditions at once

- <InlineMath math="\lvert x-2\rvert<4" /> means <InlineMath math="-2<x<6" />.
- <InlineMath math="\lvert x-2\rvert>2" /> means <InlineMath math="x<0" /> or <InlineMath math="x>4" />.

**Both have to hold at the same time**, so take the overlap. On the number line, that's two pieces, one on each side of <InlineMath math="2" />:

<BlockMath math="-2<x<0\quad\text{or}\quad4<x<6" />

Check: <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> gives <InlineMath math="\lvert3-3\rvert=0<1" />. <InlineMath math="x=2" /> gives <InlineMath math="\lvert0-3\rvert=3" />, which fails. <InlineMath math="x=0" /> gives exactly <InlineMath math="1" />, so the ends are out.

So the solutions are <InlineMath math="-2<x<0" /> or <InlineMath math="4<x<6" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4<x<6" /> **only looks at the right side** <InlineMath math="x\ge2" /> and forgets that the distance to <InlineMath math="2" /> can also point left.
- <InlineMath math="-2<x<6" /> drops the outer absolute value and only solves <InlineMath math="\lvert x-2\rvert-3<1" />.
- <InlineMath math="0<x<4" /> flips the condition <InlineMath math="\lvert x-2\rvert>2" /> into <InlineMath math="\lvert x-2\rvert<2" />.
- <InlineMath math="x<0" /> or <InlineMath math="x>4" /> forgets the upper bound <InlineMath math="\lvert x-2\rvert<4" />.
- The option with <InlineMath math="\le" /> includes the ends, but there the value is exactly <InlineMath math="1" />, not less than <InlineMath math="1" />.

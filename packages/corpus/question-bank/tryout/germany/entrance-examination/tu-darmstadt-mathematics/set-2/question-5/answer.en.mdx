# Areas of similar triangles

Every line parallel to <InlineMath math="BC" /> cuts off a small triangle at corner <InlineMath math="A" />, and that small triangle is similar to <InlineMath math="ABC" />. **If its sides are <InlineMath math="k" /> times the sides of <InlineMath math="ABC" />, its area is <InlineMath math="k^2" /> times the area of <InlineMath math="ABC" />**.

So you get back from area to length with a square root: <InlineMath math="k=\sqrt{\text{area ratio}}" />.

# Where P and Q lie

The small triangle that ends at the line through <InlineMath math="P" /> holds one of the three equal parts, so its area is <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> of <InlineMath math="ABC" />. The triangle that ends at the line through <InlineMath math="Q" /> holds two parts, so its area is <InlineMath math="\frac23" /> of <InlineMath math="ABC" />. **Take the square root of both area ratios**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AP&=6\sqrt{\frac13}=\frac{6}{\sqrt3}=2\sqrt3 \\
AQ&=6\sqrt{\frac23}=\frac{6\sqrt2}{\sqrt3}=2\sqrt6
\end{aligned}" />

# The length of PQ

<BlockMath math="PQ=AQ-AP=2\sqrt6-2\sqrt3\approx4.899-3.464=1.435" />

The three pieces on <InlineMath math="AB" /> are about <InlineMath math="3.464" />, <InlineMath math="1.435" /> and <InlineMath math="1.101" />. **The closer to <InlineMath math="BC" />, the shorter the piece**, because the triangle gets wider, so a thinner strip already holds the same area.

So <InlineMath math="PQ=2\sqrt6-2\sqrt3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2" /> splits <InlineMath math="AB" /> into three equal pieces, but **area grows with the square of the length**.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt3" /> is <InlineMath math="AP" />, the first piece from <InlineMath math="A" />.
- <InlineMath math="6-2\sqrt6" /> is <InlineMath math="QB" />, the last piece next to <InlineMath math="B" />.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt6" /> is <InlineMath math="AQ" />, the distance from <InlineMath math="A" /> to the second point.
- <InlineMath math="6-2\sqrt3" /> is <InlineMath math="PB" />, the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> all the way to <InlineMath math="B" />.

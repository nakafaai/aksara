How many faces of a small cube are red depends only on its **position in the large cube**. A small cube at a corner of the large cube touches three outer faces, one in the middle of an edge touches two, one in the middle of an outer face touches one, and the cube right in the center touches none.

So the small cubes with exactly two red faces lie on an edge but not at a corner. Each edge of the large cube consists of <InlineMath math="3" /> small cubes, and <InlineMath math="2" /> of them are corner cubes. That leaves <InlineMath math="3-2=1" /> edge cube per edge, and a cube has <InlineMath math="12" /> edges.

So <InlineMath math="12" /> small cubes have exactly two red faces.

- <InlineMath math="8" /> is the number of corner cubes. They have three red faces.
- <InlineMath math="18" /> counts the cubes with one or two red faces together, <InlineMath math="6+12" />. The question asks for exactly two.

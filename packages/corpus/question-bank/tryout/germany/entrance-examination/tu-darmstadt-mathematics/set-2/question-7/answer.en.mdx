A fruit content (*Fruchtgehalt*) is a proportion, that is, **amount of juice divided by total amount**. So you cannot simply average the two percentages, because the mixture contains different amounts of the two drinks. First find the amount of juice in each drink, then divide by the whole volume.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.40\cdot300\text{ ml}&=120\text{ ml} \\
0.15\cdot200\text{ ml}&=30\text{ ml} \\
\frac{120+30}{500}&=\frac{150}{500}=0.30
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, the result must lie between <InlineMath math="15\,\%" /> and <InlineMath math="40\,\%" />, and closer to <InlineMath math="40\,\%" />, because the mixture contains more of the mango nectar. <InlineMath math="30\,\%" /> meets both conditions.

So the mixture has a fruit content of <InlineMath math="30\,\%" />.

- <InlineMath math="27.5\,\%" /> is the plain average of <InlineMath math="40\,\%" /> and <InlineMath math="15\,\%" />. It would only hold for equal amounts.
- <InlineMath math="50\,\%" /> divides the <InlineMath math="150\text{ ml}" /> of juice by the <InlineMath math="300\text{ ml}" /> of mango nectar only.
- <InlineMath math="55\,\%" /> adds the two percentages. A mixture cannot have a higher fruit content than its stronger component.

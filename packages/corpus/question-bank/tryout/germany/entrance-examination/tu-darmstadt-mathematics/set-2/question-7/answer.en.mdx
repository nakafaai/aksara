When water evaporates, the amount of water changes but **the amount of salt stays the same**. So find the salt first, then the total mass in which that salt makes up <InlineMath math="20\,\%" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{salt}&=0.15\cdot200\text{ g}=30\text{ g} \\
0.20\cdot m&=30\text{ g} \\
m&=150\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

The solution must shrink from <InlineMath math="200\text{ g}" /> to <InlineMath math="150\text{ g}" />. So <InlineMath math="200-150=50\text{ g}" /> of water evaporates.

As a check, <InlineMath math="\frac{30}{150}=0.2=20\,\%" />.

So <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" /> of water must evaporate.

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ g}" /> comes from applying the difference of the percentages, <InlineMath math="5\,\%" />, to <InlineMath math="200\text{ g}" />. The salt stays fixed, not the total mass.
- <InlineMath math="25\text{ g}" /> leaves <InlineMath math="\frac{30}{175}\approx17\,\%" /> salt, still too little.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ g}" /> is the amount of salt, not the water that evaporates.
- <InlineMath math="40\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="20\,\%" /> of <InlineMath math="200\text{ g}" />, the salt a solution of unchanged mass would need.
- <InlineMath math="150\text{ g}" /> is the mass of the solution after evaporation, not the mass of the water that left.

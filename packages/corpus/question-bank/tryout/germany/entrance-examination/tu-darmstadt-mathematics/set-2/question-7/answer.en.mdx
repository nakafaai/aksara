# Given

The **standard parabola** (*Normalparabel*) <InlineMath math="y = x^2" /> is shifted without changing its shape so its vertex (*Scheitelpunkt*) <InlineMath math="S" /> lies on line <InlineMath math="g" /> given by <InlineMath math="y = 2x" />. The shifted parabola meets <InlineMath math="g" /> again at a second point <InlineMath math="T" />.

# Asked

Find the length of segment <InlineMath math="\overline{ST}" />.

# Solution

Picture sliding a rigid wire bent into the shape <InlineMath math="y = x^2" /> up and down a straight rail <InlineMath math="y = 2x" />. Because neither the wire's shape nor the rail's slope ever changes, **the horizontal and vertical jump from <InlineMath math="S" /> to <InlineMath math="T" /> stays identical at every position**.

> Translating a curve along a line preserves every relative intersection distance, so the shift parameter cancels out completely.

Slide the vertex coordinate <InlineMath math="h" /> along <InlineMath math="y = 2x" /> below and watch that the horizontal gap from <InlineMath math="S" /> to <InlineMath math="T" /> stays locked at <InlineMath math="2" />.

Regardless of <InlineMath math="h" />, point <InlineMath math="T" /> always sits exactly <InlineMath math="2" /> units to the right of <InlineMath math="S" />.

## Finding the intersections algebraically

With vertex <InlineMath math="S(h \mid 2h)" /> on <InlineMath math="y = 2x" />, write the shifted parabola, **set it equal to <InlineMath math="2x" />** and factor.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x - h)^2 + 2h &= 2x \\
(x - h)^2 &= 2(x - h) \\
(x - h)(x - h - 2) &= 0
\end{aligned}" />

This gives <InlineMath math="x = h" /> (vertex <InlineMath math="S" />) and <InlineMath math="x = h + 2" /> (second point <InlineMath math="T" />).

## Computing length ST and checking wrong choices

From <InlineMath math="S(h \mid 2h)" /> to <InlineMath math="T(h + 2 \mid 2h + 4)" />, the horizontal step is <InlineMath math="2" /> and the vertical step is <InlineMath math="4" />. **The letter <InlineMath math="h" /> disappears completely** under the distance formula.

<BlockMath math="ST = \sqrt{2^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}" />

Quick check at <InlineMath math="h = 0" />: <InlineMath math="y = x^2" /> meets <InlineMath math="y = 2x" /> at <InlineMath math="(0 \mid 0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(2 \mid 4)" />, whose distance is exactly <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{5}" />.

- Claiming the length depends on the shift gives up before writing the algebra in <InlineMath math="h" />.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{5}" /> uses a horizontal difference of <InlineMath math="1" /> instead of <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> measures only the horizontal gap and forgets the vertical rise.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> measures only the vertical rise.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{2}" /> assumes a slope of <InlineMath math="1" /> instead of slope <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Answer

The length of segment <InlineMath math="\overline{ST}" /> is always <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{5}" />.

# Half the width and the height

The two top corners are on the arc at the same height, so **the rectangle is symmetric about the circle's centre**. Call half its width <InlineMath math="u" /> and its height <InlineMath math="h" />. The top right corner <InlineMath math="(u\mid h)" /> is on the arc, and the area is <InlineMath math="2u\cdot h" />:

<BlockMath math="u^2+h^2=25,\qquad 2uh=24" />

# The sum from a square

You don't need the quadratic formula. **The square of the sum holds both equations at once**:

<BlockMath math="(u+h)^2=u^2+h^2+2uh=25+24=49\quad\Rightarrow\quad u+h=7" />

Two positive numbers with sum <InlineMath math="7" /> and product <InlineMath math="12" /> are <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />. But nothing says which one is <InlineMath math="u" />.

# Two rectangles

- <InlineMath math="u=3" />, <InlineMath math="h=4" />: width <InlineMath math="6" />, height <InlineMath math="4" />, perimeter <InlineMath math="2(6+4)=20" />.
- <InlineMath math="u=4" />, <InlineMath math="h=3" />: width <InlineMath math="8" />, height <InlineMath math="3" />, perimeter <InlineMath math="2(8+3)=22" />.

Both fit: <InlineMath math="3^2+4^2=25" /> and the area is <InlineMath math="24" />. **Their perimeters differ**, because the perimeter counts the width as twice the half width.

So the perimeter can be <InlineMath math="20" /> or <InlineMath math="22" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Only <InlineMath math="20" /> or only <InlineMath math="22" /> **uses just one of the two layouts**.
- <InlineMath math="14" /> is <InlineMath math="2(u+h)" />, which uses half the width as the width.
- <InlineMath math="4\sqrt{37}" /> takes the rectangle's diagonal as the diameter, as for a rectangle in a full circle.
- <InlineMath math="24" /> mixes the two layouts, width <InlineMath math="8" /> with height <InlineMath math="4" />. That rectangle has area <InlineMath math="32" />.

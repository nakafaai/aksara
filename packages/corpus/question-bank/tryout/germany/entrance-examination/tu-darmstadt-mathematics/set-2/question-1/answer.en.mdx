# Given

A **rectangle** (*Rechteck*) sits inside a semicircle of radius <InlineMath math="5" />. One side lies along the diameter and the other two corners lie on the arc, with area <InlineMath math="24" />.

# Asked

Find every possible **perimeter** (*Umfang*) of this rectangle.

# Solution

Picture fitting a rectangular card neatly inside a semicircular dome. Because both top corners touch the arc at the same height, **the rectangle must be symmetric about the circle's center**. Let its half-width be <InlineMath math="u" /> and its height be <InlineMath math="h" />.

> The same area inside a semicircle can be reached by both a tall-narrow rectangle and a wide-short one, yet their perimeters differ because the full width doubles the half-width.

Look at how both rectangles (<InlineMath math="6 \times 4" /> and <InlineMath math="8 \times 3" />) fit inside the exact same semicircle and share area <InlineMath math="24" />.

The top-right corner <InlineMath math="(u \mid h)" /> lies at distance <InlineMath math="5" /> from the center, and the total area is <InlineMath math="2uh" />.

## Solving with the square of a sum

Combine the Pythagorean relation and the area condition directly into the **square of the sum** <InlineMath math="(u + h)^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
u^2 + h^2 &= 25, \qquad 2uh = 24 \\
(u + h)^2 &= u^2 + h^2 + 2uh = 49 \implies u + h = 7
\end{aligned}" />

Two positive numbers with sum <InlineMath math="7" /> and product <InlineMath math="12" /> are <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />, and either value can play the role of <InlineMath math="u" />.

## Computing both perimeters and checking wrong choices

Compute the perimeter <InlineMath math="2(2u + h)" /> for **both valid orientations**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
u = 3, \; h = 4 &\implies \text{width } 6, \; \text{height } 4, \; P = 2(6 + 4) = 20 \\
u = 4, \; h = 3 &\implies \text{width } 8, \; \text{height } 3, \; P = 2(8 + 3) = 22
\end{aligned}" />

- <InlineMath math="20" /> alone or <InlineMath math="22" /> alone keeps only one of the two valid rectangles.
- <InlineMath math="14" /> uses the half-width <InlineMath math="u" /> as if it were the full width.
- <InlineMath math="4\sqrt{37}" /> treats the rectangle diagonal as diameter <InlineMath math="10" />, which only holds in a full circle.
- <InlineMath math="24" /> mixes width <InlineMath math="8" /> with height <InlineMath math="4" />, whose area is <InlineMath math="32" />.

# Answer

The perimeter of the rectangle can be **<InlineMath math="20" />** or **<InlineMath math="22" />**.

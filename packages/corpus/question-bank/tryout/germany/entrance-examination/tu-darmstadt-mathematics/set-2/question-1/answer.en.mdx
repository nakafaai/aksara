The bases <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="4" />, and <InlineMath math="8" /> are all powers of <InlineMath math="2" />. So write **every factor as a power of <InlineMath math="2" />**, then use the laws of exponents: a power of a power multiplies the exponents, a product adds them, and a quotient subtracts them.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
4^3&=(2^2)^3=2^6 \\
8^4&=(2^3)^4=2^{12} \\
\frac{2^5\cdot2^6}{2^{12}}&=2^{5+6-12}=2^{-1}=\frac12
\end{aligned}" />

As a check with whole numbers: <InlineMath math="2^5\cdot4^3=32\cdot64=2048" /> and <InlineMath math="8^4=4096" />, so <InlineMath math="\frac{2048}{4096}=\frac12" />.

So the expression has the value <InlineMath math="\frac12" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac14" /> comes from writing <InlineMath math="4^3" /> as <InlineMath math="2^{2+3}=2^5" />. For a power of a power, the exponents are multiplied.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> comes from subtracting the exponents in the wrong order when dividing, <InlineMath math="12-11=1" />.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> would mean that numerator and denominator are equal. The numerator <InlineMath math="2048" /> is half of the denominator <InlineMath math="4096" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac18" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> fit none of the laws of exponents, because the exponent of the result is exactly <InlineMath math="5+6-12=-1" />.

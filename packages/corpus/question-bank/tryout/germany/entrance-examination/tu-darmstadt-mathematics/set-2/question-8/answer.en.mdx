Every point on the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis has the form <InlineMath math="P(0\mid y)" />. Equal distance means **equal squared distance**, and squaring removes the square roots. The German notation <InlineMath math="P(x\mid y)" /> separates the two coordinates with a vertical bar.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overline{PA}^2&=(0+3)^2+(y-4)^2=9+y^2-8y+16 \\
\overline{PB}^2&=(0-5)^2+(y-2)^2=25+y^2-4y+4 \\
25-8y&=29-4y \\
-4y&=4\ \Rightarrow\ y=-1
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, <InlineMath math="\overline{PA}^2=9+25=34" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{PB}^2=25+9=34" />.

The perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />, which holds every point equally far from <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />, shows the same. It passes through the midpoint <InlineMath math="(1\mid3)" /> and has slope <InlineMath math="4" />, because <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" /> has slope <InlineMath math="-\frac14" />. At <InlineMath math="x=0" /> this gives <InlineMath math="y=3+4\cdot(0-1)=-1" />.

So the point is <InlineMath math="P(0\mid-1)" />.

Every point on the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis has the form <InlineMath math="P(0\mid y)" />. Equal distance means **equal squared distance**, and squaring removes the square roots. The German notation <InlineMath math="P(x\mid y)" /> separates the two coordinates with a vertical bar.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overline{PA}^2&=(0+3)^2+(y-4)^2=9+y^2-8y+16 \\
\overline{PB}^2&=(0-5)^2+(y-2)^2=25+y^2-4y+4 \\
25-8y&=29-4y \\
-4y&=4\ \Rightarrow\ y=-1
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, <InlineMath math="\overline{PA}^2=9+25=34" /> and <InlineMath math="\overline{PB}^2=25+9=34" />.

So the point is <InlineMath math="P(0\mid-1)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(0\mid-2)" /> appears if the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> in the middle term is lost while expanding, that is, if <InlineMath math="(y-4)^2=y^2-4y+16" /> and <InlineMath math="(y-2)^2=y^2-2y+4" /> are used.
- <InlineMath math="(-1\mid0)" /> swaps the two coordinates and therefore lies on the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.
- <InlineMath math="(0\mid1)" /> loses the sign in the last step.
- <InlineMath math="(0\mid3)" /> takes only the <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate of the midpoint of <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />.
- <InlineMath math="(1\mid3)" /> is the midpoint of <InlineMath math="\overline{AB}" />. It is equally far from <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />, but it does not lie on the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis.

# A substitution that hides a condition

Write <InlineMath math="t=3^x" />. Then <InlineMath math="9^x=\left(3^x\right)^2=t^2" />, and the equation becomes:

<BlockMath math="t^2-6t+a=0" />

Here's the key: <InlineMath math="3^x" /> is always positive. **Every root <InlineMath math="t>0" /> gives exactly one <InlineMath math="x" />, and a root <InlineMath math="t\le0" /> gives nothing**. So you count the positive roots <InlineMath math="t" />.

# Counting positive roots

The two roots add up to <InlineMath math="6" />, their product is <InlineMath math="a" />, and the discriminant is <InlineMath math="36-4a" />. **Go through every case for <InlineMath math="a" />**:

- <InlineMath math="a>9" />: negative discriminant, no <InlineMath math="t" />, so no <InlineMath math="x" />.
- <InlineMath math="a=9" />: double root <InlineMath math="t=3>0" />, so exactly one <InlineMath math="x" />.
- <InlineMath math="0<a<9" />: two different roots with positive sum and product, so both are positive. That's two values of <InlineMath math="x" />.
- <InlineMath math="a=0" />: <InlineMath math="t(t-6)=0" />. The root <InlineMath math="t=0" /> drops out, <InlineMath math="t=6" /> stays, so exactly one <InlineMath math="x" />.
- <InlineMath math="a<0" />: negative product, so one root is positive and one is negative. Exactly one <InlineMath math="x" />.

Check with <InlineMath math="a=-7" />: <InlineMath math="(t-7)(t+1)=0" />, only <InlineMath math="t=7" /> is positive, so only <InlineMath math="x=\log_37" />.

So there's exactly one solution for <InlineMath math="a\le0" /> or <InlineMath math="a=9" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Only <InlineMath math="a=9" /> **thinks only of the double root** and forgets the case of one positive root next to one that isn't positive.
- <InlineMath math="a<0" /> or <InlineMath math="a=9" /> forgets <InlineMath math="a=0" />. The root <InlineMath math="t=0" /> drops out, but <InlineMath math="t=6" /> still gives one solution.
- <InlineMath math="a\le0" /> forgets the double root at <InlineMath math="a=9" />.
- <InlineMath math="0<a<9" /> actually gives two solutions.
- <InlineMath math="a<0" /> forgets both boundary cases, <InlineMath math="a=0" /> and <InlineMath math="a=9" />.

# Given

A **right circular cone** (*gerader Kreiskegel*) of height <InlineMath math="8\text{ cm}" /> is inscribed in a sphere (*Kugel*) of radius <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" />. Both the apex and the rim of its circular base lie on the sphere's surface.

# Asked

Find the **volume of the cone** in cubic centimeters.

# Solution

Picture slicing the sphere and cone straight down the middle like an apple. The cross-section is an **isosceles triangle inside a circle of radius <InlineMath math="5" />**, with its apex at the top and its flat base below the sphere's center.

> Because the cone's height of eight centimeters exceeds the five-centimeter radius, the base sits three centimeters below the sphere's center, forming a right triangle with the sphere radius as its hypotenuse.

The distance from apex <InlineMath math="S" /> to center <InlineMath math="M" /> is <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" />, so distance <InlineMath math="MO" /> is <InlineMath math="8 - 5 = 3\text{ cm}" />.

## Finding the base radius and cone volume

Because base rim point <InlineMath math="R" /> lies on the sphere, segment <InlineMath math="MR" /> equals the sphere radius <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" />. Apply the **Pythagorean theorem** to triangle <InlineMath math="MOR" />, then compute the volume.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
r^2 + 3^2 &= 5^2 \implies r^2 = 25 - 9 = 16 \implies r = 4\text{ cm} \\
V &= \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi \cdot 16 \cdot 8 = \frac{128\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

Sanity check: the full sphere has volume <InlineMath math="\frac{500\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^3" />, so this cone fills just over a quarter of the sphere.

## Why other choices fail

- <InlineMath math="\frac{200\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^3" /> **uses the sphere radius <InlineMath math="5" /> as the base radius**, as if the base passed through the sphere's center.
- <InlineMath math="24\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> places the sphere center halfway down the cone (<InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" />), giving <InlineMath math="r = 3\text{ cm}" />.
- <InlineMath math="128\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> drops the factor <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}" /> in the cone volume formula.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{272\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^3" /> adds <InlineMath math="25 + 9" /> instead of subtracting, ignoring that <InlineMath math="5" /> is the hypotenuse.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{80\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^3" /> plugs the sphere radius <InlineMath math="5" /> in as the cone's height instead of <InlineMath math="8" />.

# Answer

The volume of the cone is <InlineMath math="\frac{128\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^3" />.

The vertex (*Scheitelpunkt*) is easiest to read from the **vertex form** <InlineMath math="y=(x-d)^2+e" />, which you get by completing the square. Halve the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" />, <InlineMath math="-4" />, to get <InlineMath math="-2" />, and add and subtract its square.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y&=x^2-4x+4-4+3 \\
&=(x-2)^2-1
\end{aligned}" />

The expression <InlineMath math="(x-2)^2" /> is never negative and equals zero exactly at <InlineMath math="x=2" />. So the smallest <InlineMath math="y" />-value is <InlineMath math="-1" />, and the vertex is <InlineMath math="(2\mid-1)" />.

As a check, the zeros <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" /> lie symmetric about <InlineMath math="x=2" />, and <InlineMath math="2^2-4\cdot2+3=-1" />.

So the vertex is <InlineMath math="(2\mid-1)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(2\mid3)" /> has the right <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate but takes the constant term <InlineMath math="3" /> as the <InlineMath math="y" />-value instead of substituting <InlineMath math="x=2" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-2\mid15)" /> comes from the wrong sign of the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate, because the vertex lies at <InlineMath math="x=-\frac{b}{2a}=2" />.
- <InlineMath math="(4\mid3)" /> takes the size of the coefficient, <InlineMath math="4" />, as the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate without halving it.
- <InlineMath math="(1\mid0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(3\mid0)" /> are the zeros of the parabola, not its vertex.

# Looking at a cross-section

Cut the cone and the sphere through the cone's axis. **The cross-section is an isosceles triangle inside a circle of radius <InlineMath math="5" />**. The cone's tip is on the circle, and the sphere's centre <InlineMath math="M" /> is on the cone's axis.

# Where the base sits

From the tip to the centre <InlineMath math="M" /> is <InlineMath math="5" />, the sphere's radius. The base is <InlineMath math="8" /> below the tip, so **the base is <InlineMath math="8-5=3" /> below the sphere's centre**.

The edge of the base is on the sphere too. The sphere's centre, the centre of the base and one point on the edge of the base form a right triangle:

<BlockMath math="r^2+3^2=5^2\quad\Rightarrow\quad r^2=16" />

# The cone's volume

<BlockMath math="V=\frac13\pi r^2h=\frac13\pi\cdot16\cdot8=\frac{128\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^3" />

**The base radius is <InlineMath math="r=4" />**, so the right triangle you used has sides <InlineMath math="3" />, <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />.

So the cone's volume is <InlineMath math="\frac{128\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{200\pi}{3}" /> **uses the sphere's radius as the base radius**, as if the base went through the centre.
- <InlineMath math="24\pi" /> puts the sphere's centre halfway up the cone, so <InlineMath math="r^2=25-16=9" />.
- <InlineMath math="128\pi" /> forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> in the cone's volume.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{272\pi}3" /> adds in Pythagoras, <InlineMath math="r^2=25+9" />, but the hypotenuse is the sphere's radius.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{80\pi}{3}" /> gets <InlineMath math="r^2=16" /> right but takes the sphere's radius <InlineMath math="5" /> as the cone's height.

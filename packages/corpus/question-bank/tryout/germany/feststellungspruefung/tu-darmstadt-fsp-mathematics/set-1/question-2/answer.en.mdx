# Part a: The single solution for <InlineMath math="k=0" />

Before applying any log rule, lock down the domain, just like checking the guest list before opening the door. **For <InlineMath math="\ln(x-1)" /> you need <InlineMath math="x>1" />**, and for <InlineMath math="\ln(4x+k)" /> you need <InlineMath math="4x+k>0" />.

Only on <InlineMath math="x>1" /> can you rewrite <InlineMath math="2\ln(x-1)" /> as <InlineMath math="\ln\!\left((x-1)^2\right)" />, because <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2" /> is also positive for <InlineMath math="x<1" /> and would sneak in false roots. Setting <InlineMath math="k=0" /> gives a quadratic equation:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
4x=(x-1)^2&\iff x^2-6x+1=0\\
x_{1,2}&=3\pm\sqrt{9-1}=3\pm2\sqrt2
\end{aligned}" />

Check both roots against <InlineMath math="x>1" />. Since <InlineMath math="2\sqrt2=\sqrt8>2" />, you get <InlineMath math="x_1=3-2\sqrt2\approx0.17<1" />. Even though <InlineMath math="4x_1>0" /> holds, <InlineMath math="x_1" /> fails <InlineMath math="\ln(x-1)" />. **Only <InlineMath math="x=3+2\sqrt2\approx5.83" /> survives**.

# Part b: Every <InlineMath math="k" /> with exactly one solution

Isolate <InlineMath math="k" /> on one side and, for <InlineMath math="x>1" />, the equation simply intersects the parabola <InlineMath math="y=x^2-6x+1" /> with the horizontal line <InlineMath math="y=k" />. Since <InlineMath math="4x+k=(x-1)^2>0" /> holds automatically for every <InlineMath math="x>1" />, **the single inequality <InlineMath math="x>1" /> decides** which roots survive.

<BlockMath math="x^2-6x+(1-k)=0&\iff x_{1,2}=3\pm\sqrt{8+k}" />

The classic trap is to set only the discriminant to zero. That catches the first case **<InlineMath math="k=-8" />** with the double root <InlineMath math="x=3>1" />, yet misses an entire second regime: once <InlineMath math="k>-8" />, the larger root <InlineMath math="x_2=3+\sqrt{8+k}>3>1" /> always stays valid, while the smaller root <InlineMath math="x_1=3-\sqrt{8+k}" /> can fall behind the wall <InlineMath math="x=1" />:

<BlockMath math="3-\sqrt{8+k}\le1\iff \sqrt{8+k}\ge2\iff 8+k\ge4\iff k\ge-4" />

Watch the boundary <InlineMath math="k=-4" /> in particular: its algebraic roots are <InlineMath math="x_1=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2=5" />, and <InlineMath math="x_1=1" /> is forbidden because <InlineMath math="\ln(0)" /> doesn't exist. The picture shows both regimes at once.

# Part c: Two solutions with <InlineMath math="x_2=x_1^2" />

By Vieta's formula, the sum of the two roots of <InlineMath math="x^2-6x+(1-k)=0" /> is always <InlineMath math="x_1+x_2=6" />, regardless of <InlineMath math="k" />. **Substitute <InlineMath math="x_2=x_1^2" />** to find every candidate pair before even touching <InlineMath math="k" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x_1+x_1^2=6\iff x_1^2+x_1-6=(x_1+3)(x_1-2)=0\\
(x_1,x_2)=(-3,9)\ \text{or}\ (x_1,x_2)=(2,4)
\end{aligned}" />

Now the domain wall <InlineMath math="x>1" /> strikes again. The pair <InlineMath math="(-3,9)" /> gives <InlineMath math="1-k=-27" />, so <InlineMath math="k=28" />, and even satisfies <InlineMath math="4\cdot(-3)+28=16>0" />, yet fails because <InlineMath math="x_1=-3<1" />. Only <InlineMath math="x_1=2" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2=4" /> both sit to the right of <InlineMath math="1" />:

<BlockMath math="1-k=x_1\cdot x_2=2\cdot4=8\implies k=-7" />

#### Identifying Proportions

Given:
- To charge from <InlineMath math="20\%" /> to <InlineMath math="100\%" /> takes <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ hours}" />.
- The percentage charged is <InlineMath math="100\% - 20\% = 80\%" />.

The current charge is <InlineMath math="60\%" />.
- The target charge is <InlineMath math="100\%" />.
- The percentage needed to charge is <InlineMath math="100\% - 60\% = 40\%" />.

#### Direct Proportion

With a constant charging rate, the time required is directly proportional to the fraction of full capacity added. Let <InlineMath math="t_1" /> and <InlineMath math="t_2" /> be the charging times in hours, and <InlineMath math="p_1" /> and <InlineMath math="p_2" /> the corresponding fractions of full capacity added. Then:

<BlockMath math="\frac{t_1}{p_1} = \frac{t_2}{p_2}" />

Substitute the values:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{2.5}{80\%} = \frac{t_2}{40\%}" />
  <BlockMath math="t_2 = \frac{2.5 \times 40\%}{80\%}" />
  <BlockMath math="t_2 = \frac{2.5}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="t_2 = 1.25 \text{ hours}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Time Conversion

Convert <InlineMath math="1.25\text{ hours}" /> into hours and minutes:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="1.25\text{ h}=1\text{ h}+0.25\text{ h}" />
  <BlockMath math="=1\text{ h}+(0.25\times60)\text{ min}" />
  <BlockMath math="=1\text{ h}+15\text{ min}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the time required is <InlineMath math="1 \text{ hour }" /> <InlineMath math="15 \text{ minutes }" />.

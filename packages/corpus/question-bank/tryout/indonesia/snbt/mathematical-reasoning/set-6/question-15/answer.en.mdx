#### Modeling truck counts and loads

Because every truck is **fully loaded**, the total of their capacities equals the cargo weight. The variables count trucks of each type.

| Type | Count | Capacity per truck |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | <InlineMath math="x=4" /> | <InlineMath math="3\text{ t}" /> |
| II | <InlineMath math="y" /> | <InlineMath math="4\text{ t}" /> |
| III | <InlineMath math="z" /> | <InlineMath math="6\text{ t}" /> |

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x+y+z=20\quad(1)" />
  <BlockMath math="3x+4y+6z=96\quad(2)" />
</MathContainer>

#### Solving the System

Substitute <InlineMath math="x = 4" /> into equation (1):

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="4 + y + z = 20" />
  <BlockMath math="y + z = 16 \quad (3)" />
</MathContainer>

Substitute <InlineMath math="x = 4" /> into equation (2):

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3(4) + 4y + 6z = 96" />
  <BlockMath math="12 + 4y + 6z = 96" />
  <BlockMath math="4y + 6z = 84" />
</MathContainer>

Simplify the equation above by dividing both sides by <InlineMath math="2" />:

<BlockMath math="2y + 3z = 42 \quad (4)" />

Match the **coefficients of the variable to eliminate**. To remove <InlineMath math="z" />, multiply equation (3) by <InlineMath math="3" />. Both equations then have the same coefficient of <InlineMath math="z" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}3(y+z)&=3(16) \\ 3y+3z&=48 \\ 2y+3z&=42\end{aligned}" />

Subtract the second equation from the first:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&(3y+3z)-(2y+3z) \\ &\quad=48-42\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="y = 6" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the number of type <InlineMath math="\text{II}" /> trucks rented is <InlineMath math="6" />.

As a check, <InlineMath math="z=16-6=10" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="4+6+10=20" />
  <BlockMath math="3(4)+4(6)+6(10)=12+24+60=96" />
</MathContainer>

First you need the target from Pasar Y, then how many QRIS users have to come from Pasar X's group aged <InlineMath math="30" /> or over. The key is to **work with numbers of people, not straight with percentages**, because Pasar X's two groups aren't the same size.

# The target from Pasar Y

Pasar Y's overall percentage is all its QRIS users divided by all its respondents. **Count the people in each group first.**

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{QRIS users at Y} &= 160\cdot 65\%+40\cdot 20\% \\ &= 104+8=112 \\ \text{Y's percentage} &= \frac{112}{200}\cdot 100\%=56\% \end{aligned}" />

Pasar X also has <InlineMath math="200" /> respondents, so <InlineMath math="56\%" /> means at least <InlineMath math="112" /> QRIS users. The young group still gives <InlineMath math="40\cdot 80\%=32" /> people. **The rest has to come from the group aged <InlineMath math="30" /> or over.**

<BlockMath math="112-32=80" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{80}{160}\cdot 100\%=50\%" />

At <InlineMath math="50\%" />, that group gives <InlineMath math="80" /> people, so Pasar X has <InlineMath math="32+80=112" /> QRIS users, exactly <InlineMath math="56\%" />. # Why the other options fail

Each wrong option **uses the wrong weights or the wrong denominator**.

- <InlineMath math="32\%" /> comes from treating the overall percentage as a plain average of the two groups, <InlineMath math="\frac{80\%+p}{2}=56\%" />.
- <InlineMath math="40\%" /> comes from dividing the <InlineMath math="80" /> people by all <InlineMath math="200" /> respondents instead of the group's <InlineMath math="160" />.

- <InlineMath math="46\%" /> comes from adding the overall gap of <InlineMath math="16" /> percentage points straight onto <InlineMath math="30\%" />.

So the share of QRIS users in Pasar X's group aged <InlineMath math="30" /> or over has to be at least <InlineMath math="50\%" />.

# The statement about the longest wait

The longest wait in the list is <InlineMath math="33\text{ minutes}" />, for riders of the 21:54 bus A. You only get <InlineMath math="27\text{ minutes}" /> if you **ignore the <InlineMath math="4\text{-minute}" /> walk**, because then those riders seem to catch the 22:22 bus. This statement is **false**.

# The statement about ninety minutes apart

Bus A repeats every <InlineMath math="18\text{ minutes}" /> and bus B every <InlineMath math="30\text{ minutes}" />. The least common multiple of <InlineMath math="18" /> and <InlineMath math="30" /> is <InlineMath math="90" />, so the wait pattern does repeat every <InlineMath math="90\text{ minutes}" />, **as long as bus B is still running**. # The statement about arrival order

The 21:54 bus A and the next one, at 22:12, both feed into the 22:52 bus B. Their riders **reach Halte Kampus at the same time**, 23:07. The riders who left earlier don't arrive earlier. This statement is **false**.

# The statement about the changed bus B interval

The first bus A arrives at 21:25 and its riders take the 21:40 bus B, a <InlineMath math="15\text{-minute}" /> wait. Every later bus A arrives <InlineMath math="18\text{ minutes}" /> later, and the bus B it feeds is also <InlineMath math="18\text{ minutes}" /> later. So **the gap between bus A's arrival and the bus B taken is always <InlineMath math="15\text{ minutes}" />**, and every rider who still gets a bus B has a free transfer. The 23:24 and 23:42 buses A get no bus B, but the statement is only about riders who can still board bus B. This statement is **true**.

Tap K can only let out water that's actually there. So **first find when the tank runs dry**, then count how many minutes tap K really delivers <InlineMath math="50\text{ liters}" /> per minute. To know when the tank runs dry, you need the level when the last pump stops, and that level is limited by the capacity of <InlineMath math="6{,}000\text{ liters}" />.

# The level when the last pump stops

The level changes by the inflow minus the outflow, which is called the net flow. Work piece by piece, because **the net flow changes every time a device starts or stops**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{05:00 to 05:20: } 1{,}000+60\cdot 20 &= 2{,}200 \\ \text{05:20 to 05:40: } 2{,}200+(60-50)\cdot 20 &= 2{,}400 \\ \text{05:40 to 06:40: } 2{,}400+(60+80-50)\cdot 60 &= 7{,}800 \end{aligned}" />

The number <InlineMath math="7{,}800" /> is bigger than the capacity, so the tank was already full before 06:40 and the extra went out through the overflow pipe. The real level at 06:40 is <InlineMath math="6{,}000\text{ liters}" />. From 06:40 to 07:00 the net flow is <InlineMath math="60-50=10\text{ liters/min}" />, still positive, so the tank stays full. So at 07:00, when pump P stops, **the tank holds exactly <InlineMath math="6{,}000\text{ liters}" />**.

# When the tank runs dry

After 07:00 no water comes in, and tap K lets out <InlineMath math="50\text{ liters}" /> per minute.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{time until empty} &= \frac{6{,}000}{50} \\ &= 120\text{ minutes} \end{aligned}" />

The tank is empty at 09:00. From 09:00 to 10:00 no pump is running, so **tap K delivers no water at all**, even though it's still open.

# Water through the tap

Tap K **really delivers water from 05:20 to 09:00**, which is <InlineMath math="220\text{ minutes}" />.

<BlockMath math="50\cdot 220=11{,}000" />

# Checking with a water balance

Every liter that was ever in the tank has to end up somewhere: out through tap K, lost through the overflow pipe, or still in the tank. Both sides of this balance **have to match**. The water available is the starting amount plus what both pumps delivered, <InlineMath math="1{,}000+60\cdot 120+80\cdot 60=13{,}000\text{ liters}" />. The lost water is <InlineMath math="7{,}800-6{,}000=1{,}800\text{ liters}" /> up to 06:40, plus <InlineMath math="10\cdot 20=200\text{ liters}" /> up to 07:00, so <InlineMath math="2{,}000\text{ liters}" />. At 10:00 the tank is empty.

<BlockMath math="11{,}000+2{,}000+0=13{,}000" />

The balance matches.

# Traps to avoid

There are **two limits that are easy to forget**: the capacity and the empty tank.

- If you let tap K run for the full <InlineMath math="280\text{ minutes}" /> from 05:20 to 10:00, you get <InlineMath math="14{,}000\text{ liters}" />. That ignores the tank running dry at 09:00.
- If you keep the overflow water in the tank, the level at 07:00 becomes <InlineMath math="8{,}000\text{ liters}" />, the tank only runs dry at 09:40, and you get <InlineMath math="50\cdot 260=13{,}000\text{ liters}" />. That ignores the overflow pipe.

So the water that really flows out through tap K that day is <InlineMath math="11{,}000\text{ liters}" />.

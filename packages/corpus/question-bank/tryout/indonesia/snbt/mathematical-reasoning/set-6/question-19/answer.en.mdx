#### Problem Identification

The camera, marker, and horizontal ground form a right triangle. We need the tangent of its angle of depression <InlineMath math="\tan \alpha" />.

#### Determining Effective Height (Opposite Side)

The camera is <InlineMath math="61.73\text{ meters}" /> above the ground, while the marker is <InlineMath math="173\text{ cm}" /> high. Convert the marker height first.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="173 \text{ cm} = 1.73 \text{ meters}" />
</MathContainer>

The vertical difference between the camera and the top of the marker is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Effective Height} = \text{Building Height} - \text{Target Height}" />
  <BlockMath math="opposite = 61.73 - 1.73 = 60 \text{ meters}" />
</MathContainer>

This is the opposite side <InlineMath math="opposite" /> of the corresponding angle of elevation, which equals the angle of depression.

#### Determining Horizontal Distance (Adjacent Side)

The marker is <InlineMath math="180\text{ meters}" /> horizontally from the building. This is the adjacent side <InlineMath math="adjacent" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="adjacent = 180 \text{ meters}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Calculating Tangent of Angle of Depression

For the angle of depression <InlineMath math="\alpha" />, the tangent is the ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\tan \alpha = \frac{opposite}{adjacent}" />
  <BlockMath math="\tan \alpha = \frac{60}{180}" />
</MathContainer>

Simplify the fraction:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\tan \alpha = \frac{1}{3}" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, the tangent of the angle of depression is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}" />.

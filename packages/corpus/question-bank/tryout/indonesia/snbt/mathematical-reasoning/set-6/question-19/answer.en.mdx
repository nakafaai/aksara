#### Identifying the Right Triangle

Let <InlineMath math="A" /> be the camera position, <InlineMath math="C" /> the top of the marker, and <InlineMath math="B" /> the point on the building wall horizontally level with <InlineMath math="C" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is right-angled at <InlineMath math="B" />.

#### Calculating the Height Difference

The camera is <InlineMath math="61.73\text{ m}" /> above the ground, while the marker is <InlineMath math="173\text{ cm}" /> high. Convert to the same unit:

<BlockMath math="173\text{ cm}=1.73\text{ m}" />

The length <InlineMath math="AB" /> is the height difference between the camera and the marker top. In meters:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="AB=61.73-1.73" />
  <BlockMath math="AB=60" />
</MathContainer>

#### Determining the Horizontal Distance

Since the ground is horizontal, <InlineMath math="BC" /> equals the horizontal distance from the marker to the building:

<BlockMath math="BC=180\text{ m}" />

#### Calculating the Tangent

The angle of depression <InlineMath math="\alpha" /> equals the angle of elevation at <InlineMath math="C" />, because the two horizontal lines are parallel. Relative to the angle at <InlineMath math="C" />, <InlineMath math="AB" /> is the opposite side and <InlineMath math="BC" /> the adjacent side. Therefore:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\tan\alpha=\frac{AB}{BC}" />
  <BlockMath math="\tan\alpha=\frac{60}{180}" />
  <BlockMath math="\tan\alpha=\frac13" />
</MathContainer>

The tangent of the angle of depression is <InlineMath math="\frac13" />.

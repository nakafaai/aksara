The survey only records the choices of shoppers who happened to be met. Shoppers weren't randomly assigned to a market, and the two markets have very different age mixes. **A survey like this can't prove cause and effect.** The data even point to another reason: in every age group, Pasar Y is actually lower. # The statement about cash users

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{cash at X} &= 8+112=120 \\ \text{cash at Y} &= 56+32=88 \\ \text{all cash users} &= 120+88=208 \end{aligned}" />

Half of <InlineMath math="208" /> is <InlineMath math="104" />, and <InlineMath math="120" /> is more than that. So **cash users from Pasar X really are more than half**. This statement is **true**.

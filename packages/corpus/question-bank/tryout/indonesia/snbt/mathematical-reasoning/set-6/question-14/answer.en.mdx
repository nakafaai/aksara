Compare the **monthly salaries** in the three groups. Each variable represents one employee’s salary, not the total payroll of a group.

| Group | Monthly salary |
| --- | --- |
| A | <InlineMath math="x" /> |
| B | <InlineMath math="y" /> |
| C | <InlineMath math="z" /> |

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac13y\quad(1)" />
  <BlockMath math="z=\frac12y\quad(2)" />
</MathContainer>

#### Solving the Problem

Asked: **Income of Group** <InlineMath math="A" /> for <InlineMath math="6\text{ months}" /> is equal to income of Group <InlineMath math="C" /> for <InlineMath math="n\text{ months}" />.

The equation is:

<BlockMath math="6x = n \cdot z" />

Substitute equations (1) and (2) into the equation above (express everything in terms of <InlineMath math="y" />):

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="6\left(\frac{1}{3}y\right) = n\left(\frac{1}{2}y\right)" />
  <BlockMath math="2y = \frac{n}{2}y" />
</MathContainer>

Since <InlineMath math="y \neq 0" />, we can divide both sides by <InlineMath math="y" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2 = \frac{n}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="n = 2 \times 2" />
  <BlockMath math="n = 4" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the income will be equal to the income of Group <InlineMath math="C" /> employees for <InlineMath math="4\text{ months}" />.

A closed can consists of **two circles and one curved side**. Unrolled, the curved side is a rectangle whose length equals the circumference of the base.

# Areas of base, lid and side

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2\cdot\pi r^2&=2\cdot\pi\cdot4^2=32\pi\\
2\pi rh&=2\cdot\pi\cdot4\cdot10=80\pi
\end{aligned}" />

# Material area

<BlockMath math="32\pi+80\pi=112\pi\text{ cm}^2" />

The value <InlineMath math="80\pi" /> is only the curved side, while <InlineMath math="114\pi" /> is the material area of design B. The value <InlineMath math="160\pi" /> is the volume of can A in cubic centimetres, **not an area**.

So the material area of a design A can is **<InlineMath math="112\pi\text{ cm}^2" />**.

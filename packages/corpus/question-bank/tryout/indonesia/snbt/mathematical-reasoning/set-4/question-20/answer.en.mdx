If Rafi holds <InlineMath math="k" /> tickets, a probability of at least <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> for at least one prize means **the probability of no prize is at most <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />**.

# Setting up the condition

No prize means **neither winning ticket is Rafi's**, so both come from the <InlineMath math="20-k" /> other tickets.

<BlockMath math="\frac{\binom{20-k}{2}}{190}\le\frac12\;\Longleftrightarrow\;(20-k)(19-k)\le190" />

# Checking values of k

| <InlineMath math="k" /> | <InlineMath math="(20-k)(19-k)" /> | Works? |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 5 | <InlineMath math="15\cdot14=210" /> | no |
| 6 | <InlineMath math="14\cdot13=182" /> | yes |

With 6 tickets, the probability of at least one prize is <InlineMath math="1-\tfrac{91}{190}=\tfrac{99}{190}" />, **just above one half**. With 5 tickets it is <InlineMath math="1-\tfrac{105}{190}=\tfrac{85}{190}" />, still below one half.

The value 10 assumes Rafi needs half of all tickets. The values 4 and 5 are not enough.

So Rafi needs at least **6 tickets**.

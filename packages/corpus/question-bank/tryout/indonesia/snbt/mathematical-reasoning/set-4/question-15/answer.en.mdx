We start by defining variables for speed and time. Let <InlineMath math="v_1" /> be the speed of the first car, so the speed of the second car is <InlineMath math="v_2 = v_1 + 15" />. We know the difference in their travel times is <InlineMath math="1 \text{ hour }" />.

Since time is distance divided by speed (<InlineMath math="t = \frac{s}{v}" />), we can set up an equation based on this time difference:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="t_1 - t_2 = 1" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac{450}{v_1} - \frac{450}{v_1 + 15} = 1" />
</MathContainer>

Next, we solve this equation to find <InlineMath math="v_1" />. Multiply both sides by <InlineMath math="v_1(v_1 + 15)" /> to eliminate the denominators:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&450(v_1+15)-450v_1 \\ &=v_1(v_1+15)\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&450v_1+6750-450v_1 \\ &=v_1^2+15v_1\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="6750 = v_1^2 + 15v_1" />
  <BlockMath math="v_1^2 + 15v_1 - 6750 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

We factor the quadratic equation:

<BlockMath math="(v_1 + 90)(v_1 - 75) = 0" />

Since speed cannot be negative, we take <InlineMath math="v_1 = 75\text{ km/h}" />. Thus, the speed of the second car is:

<BlockMath math="v_2 = 75 + 15 = 90 \text{ km/h}" />

Finally, we calculate the arithmetic mean of the two speeds:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\bar{v} &=\frac{v_1+v_2}{2} \\ &=\frac{75+90}{2} \\ &=\frac{165}{2} \\ &=82.5\text{ km/h}\end{aligned}" />

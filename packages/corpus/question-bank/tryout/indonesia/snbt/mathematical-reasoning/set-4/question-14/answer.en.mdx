# The formulas

Every <InlineMath math="5\text{ thousand}" /> down adds <InlineMath math="16" /> viewers, so the number of viewers is <InlineMath math="n=280-3.2p" />, at most <InlineMath math="200" />. **The snack gain can be merged with the ticket price**, because both are counted per viewer:

<BlockMath math="\text{profit}=(p+10)\,n-2{,}000\text{ thousand}" />

# The first statement

Compare the ticket revenue at the two prices:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
p=50:\quad 50\times120 &= 6{,}000\text{ thousand}\\
p=45:\quad 45\times136 &= 6{,}120\text{ thousand}
\end{aligned}" />

The price falls, but **the extra viewers more than make up** for the cut. The first statement is **true**.

# The second statement

Compute the profit at both prices:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}" />

The results differ. <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}30{,}000" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}50{,}000" /> are the same distance from <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}40{,}000" />, but **the profit peak isn't exactly at** <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}40{,}000" />. The second statement is **false**.

# The third statement

Every seat is filled when <InlineMath math="280-3.2p\ge200" />, that is, <InlineMath math="p\le25" />. **The highest price that fills the house** is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}25{,}000" />:

A full studio isn't automatically the most profitable. The third statement is **true**.

# The fourth statement

Test the prices around <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}80{,}000" />, since prices must be multiples of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}

At <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}80{,}000" /> the profit is still positive, while at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}85{,}000" /> it's already a loss. Higher prices bring even fewer viewers or none, so **the profit stays negative**. The fourth statement is **true**.

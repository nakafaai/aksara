Let <InlineMath math="A" /> be the hilltop, <InlineMath math="B" /> the foot of the hill, <InlineMath math="C" /> the initial boat position, and <InlineMath math="D" /> the pier. In the right triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />, the hill's height <InlineMath math="AB" /> is opposite the <InlineMath math="37^\circ" /> angle.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\sin 37^\circ = \frac{AB}{AC}" />
  <BlockMath math="0.6 = \frac{AB}{20}" />
  <BlockMath math="AB = 12\text{ km}" />
</MathContainer>

We can verify that the second observation gives the same height. First find the two horizontal distances:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="BC = AC\cos 37^\circ = 20(0.8) = 16\text{ km}" />
  <BlockMath math="BD = BC - CD = 16 - 7 = 9\text{ km}" />
</MathContainer>

At point <InlineMath math="D" />, the line of sight is

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\cos 53^\circ = \frac{BD}{AD}" />
  <BlockMath math="0.6 = \frac{9}{AD} \implies AD = 15\text{ km}" />
  <BlockMath math="\sin 53^\circ = \frac{AB}{AD}" />
  <BlockMath math="AB = 15(0.8) = 12\text{ km}" />
</MathContainer>

Both observations agree, so the hill is <InlineMath math="12\text{ km}" /> high.

At least one prize is the opposite of **getting no prize at all**. That opposite event is easier to count.

# Probability of no prize

Rafi gets no prize if **both winning tickets come from the 16 tickets Rafi does not hold**.

<BlockMath math="P(\text{none})=\frac{\binom{16}{2}}{\binom{20}{2}}=\frac{120}{190}=\frac{12}{19}" />

# Probability of at least one prize

<BlockMath math="1-\frac{12}{19}=\frac{7}{19}" />

Direct check: exactly one prize comes from <InlineMath math="4\cdot16=64" /> pairs, and two prizes from <InlineMath math="\binom42=6" /> pairs. Together that is 70, and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{70}{190}=\tfrac{7}{19}" />.

The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12}{19}" /> is the probability of no prize. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac25" /> adds <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4}{20}" /> twice, although **the two draws are not mutually exclusive**.

So the probability that Rafi gets at least one prize is **<InlineMath math="\tfrac{7}{19}" />**.

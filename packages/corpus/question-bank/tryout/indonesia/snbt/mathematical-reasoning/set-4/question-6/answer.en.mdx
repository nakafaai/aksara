All four statements use the same data, but each one needs its own calculation. The plan: first turn the survival percentages into numbers of fish, then multiply by the mean weight to get the biomass. **Biomass depends on two things at once**, the number of fish and the weight per fish, so don't judge it from the weight per fish alone.

# The first statement

The largest gain per fish happens in the fourth month, <InlineMath math="70\text{ g}" />. The largest biomass increase happens in the second month, <InlineMath math="245\text{ kg}" />, just above the <InlineMath math="236\text{ kg}" /> of the fourth month. The biomass increase also depends on **how many fish are still alive**, not only on the gain per fish, and in the second month there are still the most fish. The two months differ, so the first statement is **false**.

# The second statement

The fish that die during a month are the difference between the fish alive at the start and at the end of that month:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{died in the fifth month} &= 3{,}800-3{,}600=200\text{ fish}\\
\text{died in the sixth month} &= 3{,}600-3{,}500=100\text{ fish}
\end{aligned}" />

The <InlineMath math="100" /> is exactly half of <InlineMath math="200" />, not less than half. Because the statement says **"less than"**, equality makes it **false**.

# The third statement

At the end of the third month the mean weight is <InlineMath math="150\text{ g}" />, so the price is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}20{,}000" />. At the end of the fourth month the weight is <InlineMath math="220\text{ g}" />, so the price rises to <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}24{,}000" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{revenue in the third month} &= 600\times20{,}000=12{,}000{,}000\\
\text{revenue in the fourth month} &= 836\times24{,}000=20{,}064{,}000\\
1.5\times12{,}000{,}000 &= 18{,}000{,}000<20{,}064{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The biomass by itself only grows from <InlineMath math="600" /> to <InlineMath math="836" />, less than <InlineMath math="1.5" /> times. What makes the revenue jump is **the move to a higher price class**. The third statement is **true**.

# The fourth statement

The weight per fish grows from <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> to <InlineMath math="270\text{ g}" />, which is <InlineMath math="2.7" /> times, and that tempts you to say true right away. But **the number of fish falls** from <InlineMath math="4{,}250" /> to <InlineMath math="3{,}500" />:

<BlockMath math="2.5\times425=1{,}062.5\text{ kg}>945\text{ kg}" />

The sixth-month biomass is less than <InlineMath math="2.5" /> times the second-month biomass, so the fourth statement is **false**.

So the first statement is false, the second is false, the third is true, and the fourth is false.

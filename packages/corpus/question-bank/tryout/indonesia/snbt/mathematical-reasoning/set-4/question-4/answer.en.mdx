The cuboid length is **twice its width**, while its height equals its width. Thus <InlineMath math="p=2l" />, <InlineMath math="t=l" />, and <InlineMath math="DF=9\text{ cm}" />.

#### Calculating the width

Apply **the Pythagorean theorem twice**. On the base, <InlineMath math="DB^2=p^2+l^2" />. Right triangle <InlineMath math="DBF" /> then gives:

<BlockMath math="DF^2=DB^2+BF^2=p^2+l^2+t^2" />

Substitute the known values:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="DF^2 = p^2 + l^2 + t^2" />
  <BlockMath math="9^2 = (2l)^2 + l^2 + l^2" />
  <BlockMath math="81 = 4l^2 + l^2 + l^2" />
  <BlockMath math="81 = 6l^2" />
</MathContainer>

Solve for <InlineMath math="l" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="l^2 = \frac{81}{6} = \frac{27}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="l = \sqrt{\frac{27}{2}}" />
  <BlockMath math="l = \frac{\sqrt{27}}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}" />
</MathContainer>

Rationalize the denominator:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="l = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}} \times \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}" />
  <BlockMath math="l = \frac{3\sqrt{6}}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the width of the prism is <InlineMath math="\frac{3\sqrt{6}}{2}\text{ cm}" />.

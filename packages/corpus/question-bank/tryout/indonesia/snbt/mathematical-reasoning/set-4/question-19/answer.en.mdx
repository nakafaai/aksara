The sample space consists of **190 equally likely pairs of tickets**. Count the pairs in each event.

# Two prizes and exactly one prize

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{two prizes})&=\frac{\binom42}{190}=\frac{6}{190}=\frac{3}{95}\\
P(\text{exactly one})&=\frac{4\cdot16}{190}=\frac{64}{190}=\frac{32}{95}
\end{aligned}" />

The first statement is true. Exactly one prize has probability <InlineMath math="\tfrac{32}{95}" />, **not <InlineMath math="\tfrac{16}{95}" />**. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{16}{95}" /> comes from halving the 64 pairs again, although each pair of one of Rafi's tickets and one other ticket is counted only once. The third statement is false.

# Different prizes and at least one prize

If the prizes differ, order matters: there are 20 choices for the first prize and 19 for the second, <InlineMath math="20\cdot19=380" /> outcomes. The second statement is true.

At least one prize has probability <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{95}+\tfrac{32}{95}=\tfrac{35}{95}=\tfrac{7}{19}" />, which is **less than <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />**. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **false**.

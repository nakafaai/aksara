Each piece sold contributes **the selling price minus the ingredient cost** toward the fixed cost. There is a profit once these contributions exceed the fixed cost.

# Condition for a profit

Let <InlineMath math="n" /> pieces be sold. A profit means revenue **greater than all costs**, not merely equal.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1{,}250n&>45{,}000\\
n&>36
\end{aligned}" />

The smallest whole number above 36 is 37. At 36 pieces the stall **exactly breaks even**, with no profit and no loss.

The value 36 is the break-even point. The value 18 divides the fixed cost by the selling price, as if the ingredients cost nothing.

So at least **37 pieces** must be sold.

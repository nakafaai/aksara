You're looking for the smallest number of bags that leaves enough fish at the end of the sixth month. The plan: work backwards. First find how many fish must remain after the sale, then how many must be alive at the end of the fourth month, then how many seeds, and finally **round up to whole bags**.

# The share of fish that survives from the fourth to the sixth month

The table's percentages are taken from the seeds. From the end of the fourth month to the end of the sixth month, the fish alive fall from <InlineMath math="76\%" /> to <InlineMath math="70\%" /> of the seeds. That means out of every <InlineMath math="76" /> fish alive at the end of the fourth month, <InlineMath math="70" /> are still alive at the end of the sixth month:

<BlockMath math="\text{surviving share}=\frac{70}{76}=\frac{35}{38}" />

The sale doesn't change the other fish's chances, so **this share applies to the fish left** after the sale. It isn't <InlineMath math="70\%" /> of the remaining fish, because the <InlineMath math="70\%" /> is measured against the original seeds.

# Fish needed at the end of the fourth month

Let <InlineMath math="S" /> be the number of fish left after the sale. It has to be enough to give <InlineMath math="2{,}800" /> fish:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{35}{38}S &\ge 2{,}800\\
S &\ge 2{,}800\times\frac{38}{35}=3{,}040
\end{aligned}" />

Before the sale, **the fish sold must be added back**, so at least <InlineMath math="3{,}040+1{,}520=4{,}560" /> fish must be alive.

# Seeds and bags

The fish alive at the end of the fourth month are <InlineMath math="76\%" /> of the <InlineMath math="N" /> seeds:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.76N &\ge 4{,}560\\
N &\ge 6{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

One bag holds <InlineMath math="500" /> seeds, so you need at least <InlineMath math="6{,}000\div500=12" /> bags. The division happens to come out whole here, but **bags only come whole**, so a fraction would still be rounded up.

# Checking the two nearest options

Check <InlineMath math="12" /> bags and <InlineMath math="11" /> bags:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
12\text{ bags}:\quad (0.76\times6{,}000-1{,}520)\times\frac{35}{38} &= 3{,}040\times\frac{35}{38}=2{,}800\\
11\text{ bags}:\quad (0.76\times5{,}500-1{,}520)\times\frac{35}{38} &= 2{,}660\times\frac{35}{38}=2{,}450
\end{aligned}" />

With <InlineMath math="12" /> bags exactly <InlineMath math="2{,}800" /> fish remain, so the condition **"at least" is met**. With <InlineMath math="11" /> bags only <InlineMath math="2{,}450" /> remain.

Common slips: using <InlineMath math="70\%" /> of the remaining fish gives <InlineMath math="15" /> bags, treating the deaths as a fixed <InlineMath math="6\%" /> of the seeds gives <InlineMath math="13" /> bags, and forgetting the sale gives <InlineMath math="8" /> bags.

So the farmer has to stock at least <InlineMath math="12" /> bags of seeds.

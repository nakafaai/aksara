This problem can be solved using the concept of a **Linear Function** or an **Arithmetic Sequence**, because the land price increase is constant (fixed) every year.

The initial land price (<InlineMath math="a" />) is given as <InlineMath math="10{,}000{,}000" /> and the annual price increase (<InlineMath math="b" />) is <InlineMath math="1{,}200{,}000" />. We want to find the land price after <InlineMath math="5\text{ years}" /> (<InlineMath math="U_6" />, or <InlineMath math="y" /> when <InlineMath math="x=5" />).

If we model this as a linear function where <InlineMath math="y" /> is the land price and <InlineMath math="x" /> is the number of years, the equation is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="y=a+bx" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}y &= 10{,}000{,}000 \\ &\quad +1{,}200{,}000x\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Substitute <InlineMath math="x = 5" /> to calculate the land price after <InlineMath math="5\text{ years}" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}y &= 10{,}000{,}000 \\ &\quad +1{,}200{,}000\times5\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}y &=10{,}000{,}000 \\ &\quad +6{,}000{,}000\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="y = 16{,}000{,}000" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the land price in <InlineMath math="5\text{ years}" /> will be **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}16{,}000{,}000.00" />** per square meter.

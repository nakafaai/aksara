You're looking for the harvest month with the largest profit. The plan: write the profit as a formula in the biomass and the number of months, compute it for every month in which the fish can already be sold, and compare. **The largest biomass doesn't have to give the largest profit**, because the operating cost keeps growing every month.

# The profit formula

Revenue and feed cost are both charged per kilogram of biomass, so you can merge them into **a net gain per kilogram**: the selling price minus <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}12{,}000" />. For a harvest at the end of month <InlineMath math="n" />:

<BlockMath math="\text{profit}=(\text{price per kg}-12{,}000)\times\text{biomass}-800{,}000n-5{,}000\times200" />

The seed cost <InlineMath math="5{,}000\times200=\text{Rp}1{,}000{,}000" /> is the same for every month. The net gain per kilogram is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}8{,}000" />, <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}12{,}000" />, or <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}16{,}000" /> depending on the price class.

# The profit for each month

At the end of the fifth month the weight is exactly <InlineMath math="250\text{ g}" />, and the top class reads "<InlineMath math="250\text{ g}" /> or more". The words **"or more" include the boundary itself**, so the price is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}28{,}000" />.

Each month's biomass is the number of fish alive times the mean weight, for example <InlineMath math="3{,}600\times0.25=900\text{ kg}" /> in the fifth month. The largest profit comes from **harvesting at the end of the fifth month**.

# Why the sixth month loses

Check it by comparing just the fifth and sixth months. Waiting one more month adds <InlineMath math="945-900=45\text{ kg}" /> of biomass at a gain of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}16{,}000" /> per kilogram:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{extra gain} &= 45\times16{,}000=720{,}000\\
\text{extra operating cost} &= 800{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The extra cost is **bigger than the extra gain**, so the profit drops by <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}80{,}000" />, which matches <InlineMath math="9{,}400{,}000-9{,}320{,}000" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option comes from **one cost left out** or a class boundary misread.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}9{,}320{,}000" /> comes from picking the month with the largest biomass straight away, or from treating <InlineMath math="250\text{ g}" /> as still in the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}24{,}000" /> class, which leaves the fifth month at only <InlineMath math="5{,}800{,}000" /> so the sixth month looks best.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}10{,}400{,}000" /> comes from forgetting to subtract the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}000{,}000" /> seed cost.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}14{,}120{,}000" /> comes from forgetting the operating cost. Without it, the sixth month looks best at <InlineMath math="15{,}120{,}000-1{,}000{,}000" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}20{,}660{,}000" /> comes from forgetting the feed cost: <InlineMath math="26{,}460{,}000-4{,}800{,}000-1{,}000{,}000" /> in the sixth month.

So the largest profit is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}9{,}400{,}000" />, from harvesting at the end of the fifth month.

Each statement has to be tested by working out the calories and the protein of a package. Keep in mind that the rice and the one portion of vegetables that have to be there already hold **<InlineMath math="240\text{ kcal}" /> and <InlineMath math="6\text{ g}" /> of protein**.

# The first statement about grilled chicken

Without chicken, protein can only be added with tempeh and vegetables. Four portions of tempeh bring the calories to <InlineMath math="640\text{ kcal}" />, so there are at most <InlineMath math="3" /> portions of tempeh, which is <InlineMath math="540\text{ kcal}" /> and <InlineMath math="36\text{ g}" /> of protein. The <InlineMath math="60\text{ kcal}" /> left are only enough for one more portion of vegetables, so **the protein is at most <InlineMath math="38\text{ g}" />**. A package without chicken is never a healthy package, so the first statement is true.

# The third statement about a limit of six hundred fifty kilocalories

With a limit of <InlineMath math="650\text{ kcal}" />, four portions of tempeh fit.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}

That package **meets every rule without chicken**, so the third statement is true.

# The fourth statement about extra vegetables

Take the package of rice, <InlineMath math="1" /> chicken, <InlineMath math="2" /> tempeh and <InlineMath math="1" /> vegetables. It holds <InlineMath math="590\text{ kcal}" /> and <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" /> of protein, so it is a healthy package. One more portion of vegetables brings the calories to <InlineMath math="630\text{ kcal}" />, **past the limit of <InlineMath math="600\text{ kcal}" />**. One example is enough, so the fourth statement is true.

So the first statement is true, the second is false, the third is true and the fourth is true.

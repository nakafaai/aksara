To determine the selling price of the mixture, we need to know the total capital expenditure and the total weight of the rice obtained. The cost per kilogram is the weighted average of the two rice prices. Let <InlineMath math="C" /> be the total purchase cost, <InlineMath math="H" /> the cost per kilogram, and <InlineMath math="J" /> the selling price per kilogram. All price values are in rupiah.

The total capital is the sum of the purchase costs for rice type A and type B:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}C&=20\times10{,}000 \\ &\quad+30\times12{,}000\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="C = 200{,}000 + 360{,}000" />
  <BlockMath math="C = 560{,}000" />
</MathContainer>

The total weight of the rice after mixing is <InlineMath math="20 + 30 = 50\text{ kg}" />. From this, we can calculate the cost price per kilogram of the mixed rice:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="H = \frac{C}{50}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}H&=\frac{560{,}000}{50} \\ &=11{,}200\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The merchant wants a profit of <InlineMath math="10\%" />. This means the selling price per kg is the cost price plus the profit, or equivalent to <InlineMath math="110\%" /> of the cost price.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="J = H\times(1+10\%)" />
  <BlockMath math="J = 11{,}200 \times (1 + 0.1)" />
  <BlockMath math="J = 11{,}200 \times 1.1" />
  <BlockMath math="J = 12{,}320" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the selling price of the mixed rice per kg to achieve a <InlineMath math="10\%" /> profit is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}12{,}320.00" />.

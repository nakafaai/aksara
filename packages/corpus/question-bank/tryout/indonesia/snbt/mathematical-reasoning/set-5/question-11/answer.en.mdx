Each statement is tested on its own. A statement with "always" or "never" falls to **one example that breaks it**, and a statement about a specific case is worked out directly. The two rules that decide things most often are rounding down to a multiple of <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ kg}" /> and the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}6{,}000" /> fee cap per deposit.

# Cans deposited separately

Because **rounding happens on every deposit**, small deposits can lose more weight.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{each } 1.4\text{ kg deposit} &: 1.0 \cdot 12{,}000 = 12{,}000, \text{ balance } 12{,}000-1{,}200=10{,}800 \\
\text{two deposits} &: 2 \cdot 10{,}800 = 21{,}600 \\
\text{all } 2.8\text{ kg at once} &: 2.5 \cdot 12{,}000 = 30{,}000, \text{ balance } 30{,}000-3{,}000=27{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The separate deposits add <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}21{,}600" />, less than <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}27{,}000" />. This statement is true.

# The fee for a combined deposit

Take <InlineMath math="4.2\text{ kg}" /> of cans brought in <InlineMath math="2" /> separate deposits. Each one rounds to <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ kg}" /> worth <InlineMath math="48{,}000" />, so each fee is <InlineMath math="4{,}800" />, and together they're <InlineMath math="9{,}600" />. Combined into <InlineMath math="8.4\text{ kg}" />, the weight rounds to <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ kg}" /> worth <InlineMath math="96{,}000" />. A <InlineMath math="10\%" /> fee would be <InlineMath math="9{,}600" />, but **the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}6{,}000" /> cap applies to each deposit**, so the fee is only <InlineMath math="6{,}000" />. The fees differ, so this statement is false.

# Wet and dry cardboard

Take <InlineMath math="6\text{ kg}" /> of cardboard. Because **rounding happens after the water cut**, the result isn't exactly <InlineMath math="80\%" /> anymore:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{wet} &: 0.8 \cdot 6 = 4.8 \to 4.5\text{ kg} \to 4.5 \cdot 2{,}500 = 11{,}250 \\
\text{dry} &: 6\text{ kg} \to 6 \cdot 2{,}500 = 15{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The wet value is only <InlineMath math="\tfrac{11{,}250}{15{,}000}=75\%" /> of the dry value. This statement is false.

# The share of the value that reaches the balance

The <InlineMath math="10\%" /> fee only applies in full up to a deposit value of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}60{,}000" />. Above that **the fee stays at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}6{,}000" />**, so the share that reaches the balance is more than <InlineMath math="90\%" />. For example, <InlineMath math="7\text{ kg}" /> of cans:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{deposit value} &= 7 \cdot 12{,}000 = 84{,}000 \\
\text{added balance} &= 84{,}000 - 6{,}000 = 78{,}000 \\
90\% \text{ of the value} &= 0.9 \cdot 84{,}000 = 75{,}600
\end{aligned}" />

An added balance of <InlineMath math="78{,}000" /> is more than <InlineMath math="75{,}600" />. This statement is false.

So the first statement is true, and the second, third, and fourth statements are false.

We will use the multiplication rule (*filling slots*) to form the two-digit house numbers.

**Identifying Available Digits**

The digits from <InlineMath math="0" /> through <InlineMath math="9" /> are <InlineMath math="\{0,1,\ldots,9\}" />. The condition requires both digits to be odd, so the allowed digits are:

<BlockMath math="\{1, 3, 5, 7, 9\}" />

There are <InlineMath math="5" /> odd digits available. Since the house number consists of two digits, we have two positions to fill:

1.  **First Digit**: Can be chosen from the <InlineMath math="5" /> odd digits.
2.  **Second Digit**: Can also be chosen from the <InlineMath math="5" /> odd digits (repetition is allowed).

**Calculating Total Possibilities**

The total number of possibilities is the product of the choices for each position:

<BlockMath math="\text{Total} = 5 \times 5 = 25" />

So, there are <InlineMath math="25" /> possible house numbers.

You're looking for the smallest weight of wet cardboard whose value still reaches <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}16{,}000" />. The rules work in this order: the wet weight loses <InlineMath math="20\%" />, then it's rounded down, then it's multiplied by the price. So **work backwards**: from the value to the counted weight, from the counted weight to the weight after the cut, then to the wet weight.

# The smallest counted weight

Cardboard pays <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}500" /> per kg, so the counted weight has to satisfy:

<BlockMath math="2{,}500 \cdot b \ge 16{,}000 \iff b \ge 6.4" />

But **the counted weight is always a multiple of <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ kg}" />**. The smallest multiple of <InlineMath math="0.5" /> that's at least <InlineMath math="6.4" /> is <InlineMath math="6.5" />. A counted weight of <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ kg}" /> is only worth <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}15{,}000" />, which isn't enough.

# The weight after the cut

Rounding down gives <InlineMath math="6.5\text{ kg}" /> when the weight after the cut is **at least <InlineMath math="6.5\text{ kg}" />**. If it's <InlineMath math="6.49\text{ kg}" />, for example, it rounds down to <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ kg}" />.

# The wet cardboard weight

A <InlineMath math="20\%" /> cut leaves <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of the wet weight <InlineMath math="w" />. To get back to the wet weight, you **divide by <InlineMath math="0.8" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.8\,w &\ge 6.5 \\
w &\ge \frac{6.5}{0.8} = 8.125
\end{aligned}" />

Check two weights around this answer:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
w = 8.125 &: 0.8 \cdot 8.125 = 6.5 \to 6.5\text{ kg} \to \text{Rp}16{,}250 \\
w = 8 &: 0.8 \cdot 8 = 6.4 \to 6.0\text{ kg} \to \text{Rp}15{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

<InlineMath math="8.125\text{ kg}" /> is enough, and <InlineMath math="8\text{ kg}" /> isn't.

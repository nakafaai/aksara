To determine the number of possible house numbers, we can use the multiplication rule (*filling slots*).

**Step** 1: Identifying Available Digits

The available digits run from <InlineMath math="0" /> through <InlineMath math="9" />: <InlineMath math="\{0,1,\ldots,9\}" />.

From this set, we categorize them into:

*   **Odd Digits**: <InlineMath math="\{1, 3, 5, 7, 9\}" /> (there are <InlineMath math="5" /> digits).
*   **Even Digits**: <InlineMath math="\{0, 2, 4, 6, 8\}" /> (there are <InlineMath math="5" /> digits).

**Step** 2: Filling the Digit Positions

The house number consists of two digits with the following conditions:

1.  **First Digit (Odd)**: We must choose one of the <InlineMath math="5" /> available odd digits.
2.  **Second Digit (Even)**: We must choose one of the <InlineMath math="5" /> available even digits.

**Step** 3: Calculating Total Possibilities

The total number of ways to form the house number is the product of the choices for each position:

<BlockMath math="\text{Total} = 5 \times 5 = 25" />

So, there are <InlineMath math="25" /> possible house numbers that can be formed.

# The counted weight of each kind

Each kind is weighed separately and rounded down to a multiple of <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ kg}" />. Wet cardboard **loses <InlineMath math="20\%" /> first and is rounded after that**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{cans} &: 4.8 \to 4.5\text{ kg} \\
\text{plastic bottles} &: 6.3 \to 6.0\text{ kg} \\
\text{wet cardboard} &: 0.8 \cdot 8.4 = 6.72 \to 6.5\text{ kg}
\end{aligned}" />

# The deposit value and the handling fee

Multiply each weight by its price and add them up:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{deposit value} &= 4.5 \cdot 12{,}000 + 6.0 \cdot 4{,}000 + 6.5 \cdot 2{,}500 \\
&= 54{,}000 + 24{,}000 + 16{,}250 \\

\end{aligned}" />

A <InlineMath math="10\%" /> fee on that value would be <InlineMath math="9{,}425" />, which is over the cap. So **the handling fee taken is only <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}6{,}000" />**:

<BlockMath math="\text{balance} = 1{,}000 + 94{,}250 - 6{,}000 = 89{,}250" />

# The largest withdrawal

A withdrawal has to be a multiple of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}20{,}000" />, and **what's left can't be less than <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}10{,}000" />**. Try from the top:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
89{,}250 - 80{,}000 &= 9{,}250 < 10{,}000 \\
89{,}250 - 60{,}000 &= 29{,}250 \ge 10{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

Taking out <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}80{,}000" /> isn't allowed, so the largest withdrawal is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}60{,}000" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}29{,}250" /> is left.

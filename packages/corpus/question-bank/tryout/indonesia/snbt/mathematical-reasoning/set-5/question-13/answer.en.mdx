You're looking for a download time that passes through two speed changes: the main quota runs out partway through, and then the night hours start at 00.00. The plan: turn each speed into **seconds per GB**, then split the download into three parts by speed.

# Time for one GB

A size of <InlineMath math="1\text{ GB}" /> is <InlineMath math="1{,}000 \cdot 8 = 8{,}000" /> megabits. So downloading <InlineMath math="1\text{ GB}" /> takes:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{at } 40\text{ Mbps} &: \frac{8{,}000}{40} = 200 \text{ s} \\
\text{at } 8\text{ Mbps} &: \frac{8{,}000}{8} = 1{,}000 \text{ s}
\end{aligned}" />

# The fast part and the slow part

The remaining <InlineMath math="6\text{ GB}" /> of quota goes fast, in <InlineMath math="6 \cdot 200 = 1{,}200" /> seconds, which is <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes, until 21.50. After that the main quota is gone and **the speed drops to <InlineMath math="8\text{ Mbps}" />**. From 21.50 to 00.00 there are <InlineMath math="130" /> minutes:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
130 \text{ min} &= 7{,}800 \text{ s} \\
\text{slow download} &= \frac{7{,}800}{1{,}000} = 7.8\text{ GB}
\end{aligned}" />

# The part after midnight

From 00.00, **the night hours bring the speed back to <InlineMath math="40\text{ Mbps}" />** even though the main quota is used up. What's left and how long it takes:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{rest of the file} &= 15 - 6 - 7.8 = 1.2\text{ GB} \\
\text{time} &= 1.2 \cdot 200 = 240 \text{ s} = 4 \text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

So the whole download takes <InlineMath math="20 + 130 + 4 = 154" /> minutes, and the file is done at 00.04. Check the data: <InlineMath math="6 + 7.8 + 1.2 = 15\text{ GB}" />, the size of the file.

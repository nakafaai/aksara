The mean of grouped data uses **the midpoint of each class to stand for every value in that class**, and weights each midpoint by its frequency.

# Midpoints and products

A class midpoint is **the mean of the lower and upper class limits**, for example <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0+9}{2}=4.5" />.

| Class | Midpoint | Frequency | Product |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 0-9 | 4.5 | 4 | 18 |
| 10-19 | 14.5 | 10 | 145 |
| 20-29 | 24.5 | 15 | 367.5 |
| 30-39 | 34.5 | 7 | 241.5 |
| 40-49 | 44.5 | 4 | 178 |

# The mean

The products add up to 950, and **the divisor is the number of students, 40**.

<BlockMath math="\bar x=\frac{950}{40}=23.75" />

The value 23.5 is the median, not the mean. The value 24.5 is the midpoint of the class with the largest frequency.

So the mean travel time is **23.75 minutes**.

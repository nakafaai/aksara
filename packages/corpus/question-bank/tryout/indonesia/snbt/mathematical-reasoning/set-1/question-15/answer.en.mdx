Tickets are sold from the front to the back, so every category A seat sells first, then category B, then category C. The plan: find the revenue of each category once it's sold out, **find the category in which the total passes the target**, then count the tickets inside that category.

# Seats in categories A and B

Row <InlineMath math="n" /> has <InlineMath math="19+2(n-1)=17+2n" /> seats. The seats per row form an arithmetic sequence, so **a category's total is the number of rows times the average of its first and last rows**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{A, rows }1\text{ to }6 &: \tfrac{6(19+29)}{2}=144\text{ seats}\\
\text{B, rows }7\text{ to }16 &: \tfrac{10(31+49)}{2}=400\text{ seats}
\end{aligned}" />

# Revenue once categories A and B are sold out

Multiply the seat count by the category's price, because **the ticket price depends on the row's category**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{revenue from A} &= 144\cdot\text{Rp}250{,}000=\text{Rp}36{,}000{,}000\\
\text{revenue from A and B} &= \text{Rp}36{,}000{,}000+400\cdot\text{Rp}150{,}000=\text{Rp}96{,}000{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

After <InlineMath math="144+400=544" /> tickets, the revenue is only <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}96{,}000{,}000" />. So **category B sells out before the target is reached**, and the rest of the target has to come from category C tickets.

# The category C tickets needed

The rest of the target is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000{,}000-\text{Rp}96{,}000{,}000=\text{Rp}4{,}000{,}000" />. Each category C ticket is worth <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{C tickets} &= \frac{4{,}000{,}000}{100{,}000}=40\\
\text{total tickets} &= 144+400+40=584
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking the answer

After <InlineMath math="583" /> tickets the revenue is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}96{,}000{,}000+39\cdot\text{Rp}100{,}000=\text{Rp}99{,}900{,}000" />, still **short of the target**. Ticket <InlineMath math="584" /> brings it to exactly <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000{,}000" />. Row <InlineMath math="17" /> has <InlineMath math="51" /> seats, so those <InlineMath math="40" /> category C tickets are all in row <InlineMath math="17" />.

# Common wrong answers

- An answer of <InlineMath math="571" /> assumes the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}64{,}000{,}000" /> left after category A comes from category B tickets, which would be <InlineMath math="427" /> tickets. But **category B has only** <InlineMath math="400" /> **seats**.
- An answer of <InlineMath math="400" /> divides <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000{,}000" /> by the category A price, as if every ticket cost <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}250{,}000" />.

So the total revenue first reaches <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000{,}000.00" /> when <InlineMath math="584" /> tickets have been sold.

The question asks for **additional workers**, not the entire workforce. Find the total needed for a forty-day schedule, then subtract the eighty workers already available.

#### Keeping the Workload Equal

One worker-day is the work completed by one person in one working day. With equal, constant daily productivity, **the project workload stays fixed** when the workforce changes.

<BlockMath math="W=80\times50=4{,}000\text{ worker-days}" />

Let <InlineMath math="P" /> be the total workforce from the first day. These workers must complete that workload in forty working days.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}40P&=4{,}000\\P&=\frac{4{,}000}{40}\\&=100\text{ workers}\end{aligned}" />

| Schedule | Workers | Working days | Workload (worker-days) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original | 80 | 50 | 4,000 |
| Shortened | 100 | 40 | 4,000 |

#### Finding the Addition to the Original Workforce

Eighty of the hundred required workers are already available. The **additional number equals the required total minus the original number**.

<BlockMath math="P_{\text{additional}}=100-80=20\text{ workers}" />

Check the schedule: <InlineMath math="(80+20)\times40=4{,}000" /> worker-days, equal to the original workload. Therefore, **20 additional workers** are needed from the first day.

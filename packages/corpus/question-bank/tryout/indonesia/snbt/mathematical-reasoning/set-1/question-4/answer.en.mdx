The tiered bill changes its formula in each block, while the flat plan is always <InlineMath math="1{,}400x" />. So **compare the two bills block by block**.

# Block 1, up to 100 kWh

<BlockMath math="20{,}000+1{,}000x<1{,}400x\;\Longleftrightarrow\;20{,}000<400x\;\Longleftrightarrow\;x>50" />

# Block 2, from 100 to 300 kWh

The tiered bill is <InlineMath math="20{,}000+100{,}000+1{,}500(x-100)=1{,}500x-30{,}000" />.

<BlockMath math="1{,}500x-30{,}000<1{,}400x\;\Longleftrightarrow\;100x<30{,}000\;\Longleftrightarrow\;x<300" />

All of block 2 below 300 kWh qualifies. At <InlineMath math="x=300" />, both bills **are Rp420,000**.

# Block 3, above 300 kWh

The tiered bill is <InlineMath math="420{,}000+2{,}000(x-300)=2{,}000x-180{,}000" />. The condition <InlineMath math="2{,}000x-180{,}000<1{,}400x" /> means <InlineMath math="x<300" />, which contradicts <InlineMath math="x>300" />. In this block **the flat plan is always cheaper**.

Combining the three results gives <InlineMath math="50<x<300" />. The choice <InlineMath math="x>50" /> forgets block 3, <InlineMath math="x<300" /> forgets the fixed charge at small usage, and <InlineMath math="100<x<300" /> and <InlineMath math="50<x<100" /> each check only one block.

So the tiered tariff is cheaper exactly when **<InlineMath math="50<x<300" />**.

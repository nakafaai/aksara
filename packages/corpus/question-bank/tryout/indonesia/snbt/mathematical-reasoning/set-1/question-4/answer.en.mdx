We need to determine the **minimum down payment** amount that allows Jeje to get the lowest interest rate.

#### Interest Rate Analysis

The initial interest rate is <InlineMath math="6\%" /> per year with a minimum down payment of <InlineMath math="10\%" />. Each subsequent multiple of the minimum down payment (i.e., multiples of <InlineMath math="10\%" />) will provide a reduction in the annual rate of one percentage point, up to **two percentage points**.

| Down payment as a share of original price | Rate reduction | Annual rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10% | 0 percentage points | 6% |
| 20% | 1 percentage point | 5% |
| 30% | 2 percentage points | 4% |
| 40% or more | Still capped at 2 percentage points | Still 4% |

Thus, to get the lowest interest rate (<InlineMath math="4\%" />), Jeje must pay a minimum down payment of **<InlineMath math="30\%" />**.

#### Calculating Down Payment Amount

The **original laptop price** is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}12{,}000{,}000.00" />. We calculate <InlineMath math="30\%" /> of that price:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="30\% \times 12{,}000{,}000" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac{30}{100} \times 12{,}000{,}000" />
  <BlockMath math="3{,}600{,}000" />
</MathContainer>

So, the down payment Jeje has to pay is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}3{,}600{,}000.00" />.

The discount changes the purchase price, but the store calculates the down payment from **the original price**, not the discounted price.

We will calculate the normal revenue first, then determine the new price and new sales quantity so that **revenue stays the same** but sales increase.

#### Calculating Normal Revenue

Daily revenue is **price per cup times cups sold**. This is sales revenue before costs are subtracted, rather than profit.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="I_{\text{normal}} = 12{,}000 \times 200" />
  <BlockMath math="I_{\text{normal}} = 2{,}400{,}000" />
</MathContainer>

#### Determining Relationship between Price and New Sales

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the number of price reductions of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}000.00" />.
- New Price: <InlineMath math="H = 12{,}000 - 1{,}000x" />
- New Quantity Sold: <InlineMath math="J = 200 + 20x" />

We want the revenue to remain the same (<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}400{,}000.00" />):

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="H \times J = 2{,}400{,}000" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&(12{,}000-1{,}000x) \\ &\quad\times(200+20x) \\ &=2{,}400{,}000\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Factor <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> out of the price and <InlineMath math="20" /> out of the quantity. Then **divide both sides by <InlineMath math="20{,}000" />** to simplify the equation.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&(12-x)(10+x) \\ &=\frac{2{,}400{,}000}{1{,}000\times20}\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="(12 - x)(10 + x) = 120" />
  <BlockMath math="120 + 12x - 10x - x^2 = 120" />
  <BlockMath math="2x - x^2 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="x(2 - x) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

We obtain two solutions:
1. <InlineMath math="x = 0" /> (Normal condition, no change)
2. <InlineMath math="x = 2" /> (Price reduction occurs)

Since the shop owner wants to **increase** sales, we choose <InlineMath math="x = 2" />.

#### Calculating Number of Iced Coffees Sold

Substitute **two price-reduction steps** (<InlineMath math="x=2" />) into the new quantity formula:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="J = 200 + 20(2)" />
  <BlockMath math="J = 200 + 40" />
  <BlockMath math="J = 240" />
</MathContainer>

So, the number of iced coffees sold is <InlineMath math="240" /> cups.

| Reductions | Price (Rp) | Cups per day | Revenue (Rp) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 12,000 | 200 | 2,400,000 |
| 1 | 11,000 | 220 | 2,420,000 |
| 2 | 10,000 | 240 | 2,400,000 |
| 3 | 9,000 | 260 | 2,340,000 |

The new price of Rp10,000 and sales of 240 cups give <InlineMath math="10{,}000\times240=2{,}400{,}000" /> rupiah, equal to normal revenue. This differs from the Rp2,100,000 cost threshold in the question.

Water entering the tank fills the cylinder from the bottom, so its height is **the volume of water divided by the base area of the tank**.

# Volume of water entering

From the roof area <InlineMath math="77\text{ m}^2" />, the rainfall 0.025 m and **the 80% efficiency** we get

<BlockMath math="V=77\cdot0.025\cdot0.8=1.54\text{ m}^3" />

# Base area and water height

The base radius is half the diameter, 0.7 m.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{base}}&=\tfrac{22}{7}\cdot0.7^2=\tfrac{22}{7}\cdot0.49=1.54\text{ m}^2\\
t&=\frac{1.54}{1.54}=1\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

This height is **less than the tank height of 2 m**, so no water overflows.

The height 1.25 m forgets that only 80% of the water enters. The height 0.8 m multiplies by 80% twice. The value 0.5 is the fraction of the tank that is filled, not the height, and 2 m is the height of the tank.

So the height of the water in the tank is **1 m**.

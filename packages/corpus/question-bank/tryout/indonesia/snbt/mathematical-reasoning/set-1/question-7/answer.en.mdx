We add **the fractions of the field plowed per day**, not the completion times. Treat the entire field as one unit of work.

#### Converting Time to Work Rate

A tractor that plows one field in three days at a constant rate finishes one third each day. In general, its rate is **one field divided by the completion time**.

| Tractor | Time for one field (days) | Rate (fields per day) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 | <InlineMath math="1\div3=1/3" /> |
| 2 | <InlineMath math="4\frac15=21/5" /> | <InlineMath math="1\div(21/5)=5/21" /> |
| 3 | <InlineMath math="3.5=7/2" /> | <InlineMath math="1\div(7/2)=2/7" /> |

#### Combining Two Tractor Rates

The tractors work on **nonoverlapping parts of the field**. In one day, tractor 1 plows <InlineMath math="1/3" /> of the field and tractor 3 plows <InlineMath math="2/7" />. Use a common denominator to add the fractions.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac13+\frac27&=\frac{1\cdot7}{3\cdot7}+\frac{2\cdot3}{7\cdot3} \\
&=\frac7{21}+\frac6{21} \\
&=\frac{13}{21}
\end{aligned}" />

Together they therefore plow **<InlineMath math="13/21" /> of the field in one day**. Rental costs and tractor 2’s rate are not needed for this question.

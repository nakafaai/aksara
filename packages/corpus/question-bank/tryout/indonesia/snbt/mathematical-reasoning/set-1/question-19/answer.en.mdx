Two facts underlie every statement: each team **plays the 5 other teams**, and each win is worth exactly 3 points, so a team's points are always a multiple of 3.

# Number of matches and meaning of points

Each team **meets every other team exactly once**, so each team plays 5 times. The second statement is true.

Twelve points mean <InlineMath math="12:3=4" /> wins, since losses give no points. The fourth statement is true.

# Outcomes that cannot happen

The total of all points is <InlineMath math="15\cdot3=45" />. If all six teams had the same points, each would have <InlineMath math="\tfrac{45}{6}=7.5" />, but **points are always a multiple of 3**. The first statement is false.

Fifteen points mean 5 wins, winning every match. Two teams cannot both win every match, because **they have played each other** and one of them lost. The third statement is false.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **true**.

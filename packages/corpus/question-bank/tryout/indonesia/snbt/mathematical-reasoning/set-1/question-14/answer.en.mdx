The amount of work stays fixed. If every worker has the same constant daily rate, **worker count and completion time are inversely proportional**. Sixty-four workers therefore need longer than eighty workers.

#### Finding the Fixed Workload

One **worker-day** is the work done by one worker in one working day. Eighty workers working for fifty days provide the following workload.

<BlockMath math="W=80\times50=4{,}000\text{ worker-days}" />

| Workers from the start | Time (working days) | Workload (worker-days) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 80 | 50 | 4,000 |
| 64 | <InlineMath math="t" /> | <InlineMath math="64t=4{,}000" /> |

#### Finding the Completion Day

Divide the workload by the worker count to obtain **the project duration**. All workers start at the beginning, so there is no earlier stage of work to subtract.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}64t&=4{,}000\\t&=\frac{4{,}000}{64}\\&=\frac{4{,}000\div32}{64\div32}\\&=\frac{125}{2}=62.5\text{ working days}\end{aligned}" />

After 62 days, they supply <InlineMath math="64\times62=3{,}968" /> worker-days. The remaining <InlineMath math="4{,}000-3{,}968=32" /> worker-days take another half day. The project therefore finishes **on working day 63**, halfway through that day. The number 63 identifies the completion day, while the exact duration remains 62.5 days.

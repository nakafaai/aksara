Each statement has to be tested by counting the card order by order. One thing to hold on to: **the free cup comes with the first order after the card holds <InlineMath math="10" /> stamps**, not with the order that brings the card up to <InlineMath math="10" />.

# The first statement about ten orders in a row

Take a new customer who orders <InlineMath math="10" /> times on Mondays. Each order gives <InlineMath math="1" /> stamp, so the card only holds <InlineMath math="10" /> stamps after the tenth order. **All ten cups are paid**, and the free cup only comes with the eleventh order. One example is enough, so the first statement is false.

# The second statement about Wednesdays and Mondays

Ten Monday orders are all paid, as in the first statement. On Wednesdays, the first five orders give <InlineMath math="5\cdot2=10" /> stamps, so **the sixth order is free** and the next four are paid again.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Mondays}&:10\cdot20{,}000=200{,}000\\
\text{Wednesdays}&:9\cdot15{,}000=135{,}000\\
\text{difference}&:200{,}000-135{,}000=65{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The difference is exactly <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}65{,}000" />, so the second statement is true.

# The third statement about the sixth cup

The Wednesday example already settles it. Five Wednesday orders fill the card with <InlineMath math="10" /> stamps, so **the sixth cup is free**. The third statement is false.

# The fourth statement about orders from Thursday

Count the stamps until the card holds <InlineMath math="10" />. Thursday to Tuesday give <InlineMath math="6" /> stamps, Wednesday gives <InlineMath math="2" /> stamps, then Thursday and Friday give <InlineMath math="2" /> stamps.

<BlockMath math="6+2+2=10" />

The card holds <InlineMath math="10" /> stamps after Friday's order, so **the first free cup falls on a Saturday**, not a Wednesday. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is false, the second is true, the third is false and the fourth is false.

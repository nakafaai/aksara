The new distribution assigns **the six lightest and six heaviest apples** from the combined collection. Exchanging apples does not change their masses.

#### Distributing the Sorted Apples

Using <InlineMath math="x=180" />, the sorted masses give the two groups below. Keep **both 180-gram apples** as separate observations.

| Recipient | Positions after sorting | Apple masses (grams) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Belle | 1 to 6 | 154, 157, 161, 162, 163, 167 |
| Ferdian | 7 to 12 | 170, 171, 175, 180, 180, 182 |

#### Finding the Difference between Total Masses

Let <InlineMath math="S_B" /> and <InlineMath math="S_F" /> be **the total masses carried by Belle and Ferdian** after the exchange. Add the six masses in each row before subtracting the totals.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}S_B&=154+157+161\\&\quad+162+163+167\\&=964\text{ g}\\S_F&=170+171+175\\&\quad+180+180+182\\&=1{,}058\text{ g}\\S_F-S_B&=1{,}058-964\\&=94\text{ g}\end{aligned}" />

The combined mass remains <InlineMath math="964+1{,}058=2{,}022" /> grams. Only its distribution changes. Ferdian carries **94 grams more** than Belle.

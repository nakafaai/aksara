A tiered tariff means **each part of the usage is charged at its own block rate**, not one rate for all of it. A usage of 250 kWh passes through block 1 and stops in block 2.

# Splitting the usage into blocks

The 250 kWh **split into 100 kWh in block 1 and 150 kWh in block 2**.

| Part | kWh | Cost |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Block 1 | 100 | <InlineMath math="100\cdot1{,}000=100{,}000" /> |
| Block 2 | 150 | <InlineMath math="150\cdot1{,}500=225{,}000" /> |

# Adding the fixed charge

<BlockMath math="20{,}000+100{,}000+225{,}000=345{,}000" />

The bill **always includes the fixed charge**, whatever the usage.

A bill of Rp395,000 charges the block 2 rate for all 250 kWh, and Rp520,000 charges the block 3 rate for all of it. A bill of Rp325,000 forgets the fixed charge, and Rp270,000 charges the block 1 rate for all of the usage.

So the electricity bill for that month is **Rp345,000**.

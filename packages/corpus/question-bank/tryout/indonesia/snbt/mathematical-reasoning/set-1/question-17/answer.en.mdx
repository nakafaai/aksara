We evaluate **groups selected by mass order**, not merely their average. The value <InlineMath math="x=180" /> is given, so the two apples weighing 180 grams must still count as two separate apples.

#### Sorting All Apples

Combine both columns and arrange **all twelve masses from lightest to heaviest**. The table uses grams and retains every apple, including repeated masses.

| Position | Masses (grams) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 3 | 154, 157, 161 |
| 4 to 6 | 162, 163, 167 |
| 7 to 9 | 170, 171, 175 |
| 10 to 12 | 180, 180, 182 |

#### Checking Each Group against Its Bound

A quarter of twelve apples contains three apples, while half contains six. The signs **less than and greater than are strict**: a value equal to the bound does not satisfy them.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}25\%\times12&=\frac{25}{100}\times12=3\\50\%\times12&=\frac{50}{100}\times12=6\end{aligned}" />

| Statement | Selected apples (grams) | Check | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First, three lightest | 154, 157, 161 | Largest mass 161 is below 162 | True |
| Second, three heaviest | 180, 180, 182 | Two apples equal 180 rather than exceeding it | False |
| Third, six lightest | 154, 157, 161, 162, 163, 167 | Largest mass 167 is below 170 | True |

The second statement fails even though the 182-gram apple exceeds the bound. The claim concerns every apple in the group, so all three must satisfy it. The sequence is **true, false, true**, or TFT.

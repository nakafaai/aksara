Every statement is tested with the same rule: **the amount halves every** <InlineMath math="6" /> **hours, then goes up by the dose just taken**. Write one dose as <InlineMath math="D" /> so the general pattern shows, then check the specific numbers in each statement.

# The pattern on the first day

With a dose of <InlineMath math="D" />, the amount on the first day changes like this.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{after the morning dose} &= D\\
\text{after the midday dose} &= \tfrac{1}{2}D+D=\tfrac{3}{2}D\\
\text{just before the evening dose} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{3}{2}D=\tfrac{3}{4}D\\
\text{after the evening dose} &= \tfrac{3}{4}D+D=\tfrac{7}{4}D
\end{aligned}" />

# The first statement on an exact amount

For the amount just before the evening dose to be exactly <InlineMath math="375\text{ mg}" />, you'd need <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{4}D=375" />, so <InlineMath math="D=500\text{ mg}" />. But **a single dose is at most** <InlineMath math="400\text{ mg}" />, so that amount is at most <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{4}\cdot400=300\text{ mg}" />. Even a child weighing <InlineMath math="50\text{ kg}" /> gets only <InlineMath math="400\text{ mg}" />. The first statement is false.

# The second statement on twice the dose

Look at the amount just before each dose. Before the first dose it's <InlineMath math="0" />, which is less than <InlineMath math="D" />. If the amount before some dose is less than <InlineMath math="D" />, then after the dose it's less than <InlineMath math="2D" />. By the next dose it has **at least halved**, so it's back below <InlineMath math="D" />.

<BlockMath math="\text{before a dose}<D\ \Rightarrow\ \text{after the dose}<2D\ \Rightarrow\ \text{before the next dose}<\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot2D=D" />

This repeats forever, and between two doses the amount only falls. So the amount never reaches <InlineMath math="2D" />, whatever the child weighs. The second statement is true.

# The third statement on a missed dose

A <InlineMath math="30\text{ kg}" /> child's dose is <InlineMath math="300\text{ mg}" />, exactly <InlineMath math="15\text{ mL}" /> of syrup. Twice the dose is <InlineMath math="600\text{ mg}" />, but **the** <InlineMath math="400\text{ mg}" /> **cap still applies**, so at 18.00 the child takes only <InlineMath math="400\text{ mg}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{on schedule} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot300+300\right)+300=525\text{ mg}\\
\text{midday dose missed} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot300+400=475\text{ mg}
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="475<525" />, the amount is actually smaller. The third statement is false. Without the <InlineMath math="400\text{ mg}" /> cap, the amount would be <InlineMath math="75+600=675\text{ mg}" />, and that's what makes the statement look true.

# The fourth statement on a repeating morning

A <InlineMath math="20\text{ kg}" /> child's dose is <InlineMath math="200\text{ mg}" />. On the first evening the amount is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{7}{4}\cdot200=350\text{ mg}" />, and then **the** <InlineMath math="12" /> **night hours divide it by** <InlineMath math="4" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{before the second morning dose} &= \tfrac{1}{4}\cdot350=87.5\text{ mg}\\
\text{after the second midday dose} &= \tfrac{1}{2}(87.5+200)+200=343.75\text{ mg}\\
\text{after the second evening dose} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot343.75+200=371.875\text{ mg}\\
\text{before the third morning dose} &= \tfrac{1}{4}\cdot371.875\approx92.97\text{ mg}
\end{aligned}" />

The amount rises from <InlineMath math="87.5\text{ mg}" /> to about <InlineMath math="92.97\text{ mg}" />, because what's left from the day before keeps building up. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is false, the second is true, the third is false, and the fourth is false.

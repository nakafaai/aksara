We will find the value of <InlineMath math="x" /> that maximizes the surface area of the box, and then calculate that surface area.

#### Determining Surface Area Formula

The surface area of a cuboid (<InlineMath math="L" />) can be calculated with the formula:

<BlockMath math="L=2pl+Kt" />

From <InlineMath math="2(p+l)=6x" /> and <InlineMath math="l=4x-5" />, the box dimensions in terms of <InlineMath math="x" /> are:
- Length (<InlineMath math="p" />) is <InlineMath math="5 - x" />
- Width (<InlineMath math="l" />) is <InlineMath math="4x - 5" />
- Base Perimeter (<InlineMath math="K" />) is <InlineMath math="6x" />
- Height (<InlineMath math="t" />) is <InlineMath math="5 - \frac{1}{3}x" />

Substitute these dimensions into the surface area formula:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="L(x) = 2(p \times l) + K \times t" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}L(x)&=2(5-x)(4x-5) \\ &\quad+6x\left(5-\frac{x}{3}\right)\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Simplifying the Surface Area Function

We expand the equation above:

**Base Area Part:**

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&2(5-x)(4x-5) \\ &=2(20x-25-4x^2+5x)\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= 2(-4x^2 + 25x - 25)" />
  <BlockMath math="= -8x^2 + 50x - 50" />
</MathContainer>

**Perimeter times Height Part:**

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&6x\left(5-\frac{x}{3}\right) \\ &=30x-2x^2\end{aligned}" />

**Total Surface Area:**

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}L(x)&=(-8x^2+50x-50) \\ &\quad+(30x-2x^2)\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="L(x) = -10x^2 + 80x - 50" />
</MathContainer>

#### Finding the Maximum Value

The function <InlineMath math="L(x)" /> is a quadratic function with a negative <InlineMath math="x^2" /> coefficient, so its parabolic graph opens downwards and has a maximum value. The maximum value is reached when its first derivative equals zero (<InlineMath math="L'(x) = 0" />).

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="L'(x) = -20x + 80" />
  <BlockMath math="-20x + 80 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="20x = 80" />
  <BlockMath math="x = 4" />
</MathContainer>

The length, width, and height must all be positive. The conditions <InlineMath math="5-x>0" />, <InlineMath math="4x-5>0" />, and <InlineMath math="5-\frac{x}{3}>0" /> together give <InlineMath math="\frac54<x<5" />. Since <InlineMath math="1.25 < 4 < 5" />, the value <InlineMath math="x = 4" /> is valid.

#### Calculating Maximum Surface Area

Substitute <InlineMath math="x = 4" /> into the function <InlineMath math="L(x)" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}L(4)&=-10(4)^2+80(4) \\ &\quad-50\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="L(4) = -10(16) + 320 - 50" />
  <BlockMath math="L(4) = -160 + 320 - 50" />
  <BlockMath math="L(4) = 110" />
</MathContainer>

So, the maximum surface area of the box is <InlineMath math="110 \text{ cm}^2" />.

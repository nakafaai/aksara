The bicycle's speed is **the distance per pedal turn times the number of pedal turns per minute**. The pedalling rate is fixed, so only the distance per pedal turn changes.

# Speed with each gear

**The 24-tooth gear gives 4.4 m per pedal turn**, and the 16-tooth gear gives <InlineMath math="\tfrac{48}{16}\cdot2.2=6.6" /> m.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v_{24}&=50\cdot4.4=220\text{ m/min}=13.2\text{ km/h}\\
v_{16}&=50\cdot6.6=330\text{ m/min}=19.8\text{ km/h}
\end{aligned}" />

# Increase in speed

<BlockMath math="19.8-13.2=6.6\text{ km/h}" />

To turn metres per minute into kilometres per hour, **multiply by 60 and divide by 1,000**. The values 13.2 and 19.8 km/h are the speeds with each gear, not the increase. The value 2.2 km/h assumes the difference per pedal turn is just one circumference, and 4.4 km/h uses the 2.2 m difference with a wrong conversion.

So the speed increases by **6.6 km/h**.

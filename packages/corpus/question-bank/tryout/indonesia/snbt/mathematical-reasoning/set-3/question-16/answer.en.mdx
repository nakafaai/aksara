A short stair usually uses tall steps and narrow treads. But here there's an extra condition: **<InlineMath math="R" /> has to be whole and <InlineMath math="n=\tfrac{300}{R}" /> has to be whole too**. The plan: find the possible <InlineMath math="R" />, then its narrowest tread, then the length.

# A whole step height

The rise rule only allows <InlineMath math="R=15, 16, 17, 18" />. The number of steps <InlineMath math="n=\tfrac{300}{R}" /> has to be a whole number, so **<InlineMath math="R" /> has to divide <InlineMath math="300" /> exactly**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac{300}{15} &= 20 \\
\tfrac{300}{16} &= 18.75 \\
\tfrac{300}{17} &\approx 17.6 \\
\tfrac{300}{18} &\approx 16.7
\end{aligned}" />

Only <InlineMath math="R=15" /> divides exactly, so this stair has to have <InlineMath math="n=20" /> steps. A steeper stair can't be built with whole sizes.

# The narrowest tread

For <InlineMath math="R=15" />, the step rule gives a lower and an upper limit for the tread width.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2\cdot15+T &\ge 62 \quad\Rightarrow\quad T\ge32 \\
2\cdot15+T &\le 66 \quad\Rightarrow\quad T\le36
\end{aligned}" />

The shortest length comes from **the narrowest tread, <InlineMath math="T=32" />**.

# The horizontal length

The floor above doesn't count as a tread, so <InlineMath math="20" /> steps have only <InlineMath math="19" /> treads.

<BlockMath math="19\cdot32=608\text{ cm}" />

Check it: <InlineMath math="20\cdot15=300" /> matches the floor height, and <InlineMath math="2\cdot15+32=62" /> meets the step rule. This is much longer than a stair with a fractional <InlineMath math="R" />, and **that's the price of the whole-size condition**.

Each other option **quietly breaks one condition**.

- <InlineMath math="416\text{ cm}" /> comes from <InlineMath math="R=18" /> with <InlineMath math="17" /> steps and <InlineMath math="T=26" />. Its height is <InlineMath math="17\cdot18=306" />, not <InlineMath math="300" />.

Let the four sorted natural numbers be <InlineMath math="x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4" /> where <InlineMath math="x_1 \le x_2 \le x_3 \le x_4" />.

Based on the problem information, we can form the following equations:

**Median**: Since there are an even number of data points (<InlineMath math="4" />), the median is the average of the two middle values.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&\frac{x_2+x_3}{2}=8 \\ &x_2+x_3=16\end{aligned}" />

**Range**: The difference between the largest and smallest datum is <InlineMath math="10" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&x_4-x_1=10 \\ &x_1=x_4-10\end{aligned}" />

**Mean**: The sum of all data divided by <InlineMath math="4" /> is <InlineMath math="8" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4}{4}=8 \\ &x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4=32\end{aligned}" />

We substitute the value <InlineMath math="x_2 + x_3 = 16" /> into the mean equation to find the relationship between <InlineMath math="x_1" /> and <InlineMath math="x_4" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{align*} x_1 + (x_2 + x_3) + x_4 &= 32 \\ x_1 + 16 + x_4 &= 32 \\ x_1 + x_4 &= 16 \end{align*}" />

Now we have a system of two equations for <InlineMath math="x_1" /> and <InlineMath math="x_4" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{cases} x_4 - x_1 = 10 \\ x_4 + x_1 = 16 \end{cases}" />

By adding both equations, we get:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&2x_4=26 \\ &x_4=13\end{aligned}" />

Substituting the value of <InlineMath math="x_4" /> to get <InlineMath math="x_1" />:

<BlockMath math="x_1 = 13 - 10 = 3" />

To find the middle values, use <InlineMath math="x_2+x_3=16" /> and <InlineMath math="3\le x_2\le x_3\le13" />. A **unique mode** means only one value has the highest frequency.

If <InlineMath math="x_2=3" />, then <InlineMath math="x_3=13" />. The data becomes <InlineMath math="3,3,13,13" />, with two modes. Setting <InlineMath math="x_3=13" /> gives the same case.

If the middle values differ from each other and from the endpoints, all four values are distinct and there is no unique mode. Therefore, the middle pair must be equal:

<BlockMath math="x_2 = x_3 = 8" />

The complete data set is <InlineMath math="3, 8, 8, 13" />. Its only mode is <InlineMath math="8" />, so every condition is satisfied.

Thus, the product of the second and fourth datum is:

<BlockMath math="x_2 \cdot x_4 = 8 \cdot 13 = 104" />

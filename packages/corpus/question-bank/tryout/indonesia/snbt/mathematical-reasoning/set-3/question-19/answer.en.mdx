Because the group price applies to every sheet, the total cost **drops suddenly when an order crosses a group boundary**. Compute the cost near those boundaries.

# Costs near the group boundaries

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
100\text{ sheets}&:\quad 100\cdot500=50{,}000\\
101\text{ sheets}&:\quad 101\cdot400=40{,}400\\
400\text{ sheets}&:\quad 400\cdot400=160{,}000\\
501\text{ sheets}&:\quad 501\cdot300=150{,}300
\end{aligned}" />

An order of 101 sheets is cheaper than 100 sheets, and 501 sheets is cheaper than 400 sheets. The second and fourth statements are true. The example of 100 and 101 sheets also shows that **the total cost does not always rise**, so the third statement is false.

# An order of 300 sheets

<BlockMath math="300\cdot400=120{,}000<150{,}300" />

For 300 sheets, ordering exactly 300 is **cheaper**, so the first statement is false.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **true**.

**Work Rate Analysis**

Work rate is the portion of work completed per unit of time (<InlineMath math="1 \text{ minute }" />). Based on the problem:

Let <InlineMath math="r_1" />, <InlineMath math="r_2" />, and <InlineMath math="r_3" /> be the fractions of the job completed per minute by the three machines. Let <InlineMath math="W" /> be the fraction completed together.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="r_1 = \frac{1}{16}" />
  <BlockMath math="r_2 = \frac{1}{32}" />
  <BlockMath math="r_3 = \frac{1}{32}" />
</MathContainer>

**Combined Work Calculation**

If the three machines work together for <InlineMath math="6\text{ minutes}" />, the total work completed is the sum of their rates multiplied by time:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="W = 6\left(\frac{1}{16} + \frac{1}{32} + \frac{1}{32}\right)" />
  <BlockMath math="W = 6\left(\frac{2}{32} + \frac{1}{32} + \frac{1}{32}\right)" />
  <BlockMath math="W = \frac{4}{32} \times 6" />
</MathContainer>

Simplify the fraction <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{32}" /> to <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{8}" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}W &= \frac{1}{8} \times 6 \\ &=\frac{6}{8}=\frac{3}{4}\end{aligned}" />

In decimal form, <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{4}" /> is equal to <InlineMath math="0.75" />.

Thus, the completed work is <InlineMath math="0.75" /> part.

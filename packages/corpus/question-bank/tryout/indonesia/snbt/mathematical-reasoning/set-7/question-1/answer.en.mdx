#### Count the Meeting Intervals

The initial arrival counts as the first meeting. The fourth meeting therefore follows **three equal intervals**, not four. Let <InlineMath math="T" /> be the time between simultaneous arrivals, in hours.

<BlockMath math="3T=18\quad\Rightarrow\quad T=\frac{18}{3}=6" />

| Meeting | Elapsed time in hours |
| --- | ---: |
| First | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| Second | <InlineMath math="6" /> |
| Third | <InlineMath math="12" /> |
| Fourth | <InlineMath math="18" /> |

#### Check the Possible Period

A joint arrival occurs when the elapsed time is a **common multiple of all three periods**. The smallest positive such time is their least common multiple:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\operatorname{LCM}(2,3,x)&=6 \\ \operatorname{LCM}(6,x)&=6\end{aligned}" />

Every listed value of <InlineMath math="x" /> is a positive integer. It must divide <InlineMath math="6" /> for the joint interval to remain <InlineMath math="6\text{ hours}" />. The divisors are <InlineMath math="1,2,3,6" />. Of the listed values <InlineMath math="5,7,6,8,9" />, only <InlineMath math="x=6" /> works. With that period, all vans return at the elapsed times in the table.

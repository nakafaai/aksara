The linear relation between the reading <InlineMath math="R" /> and the true temperature <InlineMath math="C" /> is fixed by **the two known pairs of data**, just as the Fahrenheit formula is fixed by the freezing and boiling points.

# Finding the thermometer formula

From 0 °C to 100 °C the reading rises from <InlineMath math="-4" /> to 86, an increase of 90. So **each 1 °C raises the reading by 0.9**.

<BlockMath math="R=0.9C-4" />

# Finding the true temperature

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.9C-4&=32\\
0.9C&=36\\
C&=40
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="0.9\cdot40-4=32" />. At this temperature the thermometer **reads lower** than the true value.

The value 32 °C trusts the reading. The value 36 °C corrects only the offset of 4, not the factor 0.9.

So the true temperature is **40 °C**.

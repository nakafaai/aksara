A travel distance of 300 m means 3 blocks, so the intersection <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> must satisfy **<InlineMath math="\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert=3" />**. **Coordinates may be negative**, because the robot can go west and south.

# Intersections on the axes

If **one coordinate is zero**, the intersections are <InlineMath math="(3,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(-3,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(0,3)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,-3)" />, which is 4 intersections.

# Intersections off the axes

If neither coordinate is zero, their absolute values are 1 and 2 in some order, and **each coordinate may be positive or negative**.

<BlockMath math="2\cdot2\cdot2=8" />

The first factor 2 picks the order of 1 and 2, and the next two pick the sign of each coordinate. So there are <InlineMath math="4+8=12" /> intersections, which together form a tilted square around G.

The value 4 counts only the intersections on the axes, or only those to the north-east. The value 8 forgets the intersections on the axes.

So there are **12 intersections** exactly 300 m of travel from G.

You're looking for all the money Ayu receives: her manager's salary plus her part of the shared profit. The plan: work out each owner's capital-months, find the shared profit by taking the deductions in the right order, then take Ayu's part and add her salary.

# Capital months for each owner

Capital-months measure how much money was invested and for how long it worked. **Every time the capital changes, count the stretch before and after the change separately.**

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Ayu} &= 40\cdot3+20\cdot9=120+180=300 \\
\text{Bima} &= 30\cdot6+40\cdot6=180+240=420 \\
\text{Citra} &= 90\cdot8=720
\end{aligned}" />

Ayu has <InlineMath math="40" /> million in from January to March, then <InlineMath math="20" /> million from April to December. Citra only joins at the start of May, so her money works for <InlineMath math="8" /> months, May to December. The capital-months add up to <InlineMath math="1{,}440" />, in the ratio <InlineMath math="300:420:720=5:7:12" />.

# The shared profit

The order matters: **Ayu's salary comes out first, and the reserve is then taken from what's left**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{left after salary} &= 252-12=240 \\
\text{reserve} &= 10\%\cdot240=24 \\
\text{shared profit} &= 240-24=216
\end{aligned}" />

All amounts here are in millions of rupiah.

# What Ayu receives

Ayu's part is <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{24}" /> of the shared profit. **You still have to add her manager's salary**, because the question asks for all the money Ayu receives.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Ayu's part} &= \frac{5}{24}\cdot216=45 \\
\text{all of Ayu's money} &= 45+12=57
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, Bima gets <InlineMath math="\frac{7}{24}\cdot216=63" /> and Citra gets <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{24}\cdot216=108" />. Together that's <InlineMath math="45+63+108=216" />, and <InlineMath math="216+12+24=252" />, exactly the net profit.

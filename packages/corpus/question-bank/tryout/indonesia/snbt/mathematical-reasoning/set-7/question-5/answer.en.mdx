#### Relate Water and Solid Volumes

A **fully submerged solid** displaces water equal to its own volume. Because the container began full, the lost water volume equals the total volume of the balls. Let <InlineMath math="V_c" /> be the cylinder volume and <InlineMath math="V_b" /> one ball's volume, in cubic centimeters.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}V_c&=\pi(20)^2(30)=12{,}000\pi \\ V_b&=\frac43\pi(3)^3=\frac43\pi(27)=36\pi\end{aligned}" />

#### Find and Check the Number of Balls

Subtract the **remaining water from the initial volume**, then divide by the volume of one ball. The common factor <InlineMath math="\pi" /> cancels:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}n(36\pi)&=12{,}000\pi-11{,}388\pi \\ 36n&=612 \\ n&=\frac{612}{36}=17\end{aligned}" />

The result is a positive integer. Check the remaining water volume:

<BlockMath math="12{,}000\pi-17(36\pi)=11{,}388\pi\text{ cm}^3" />

This matches the question, so <InlineMath math="17" /> balls were inserted.

#### Arithmetic Sequence Analysis

The pattern of temperature decrease in the table forms an **arithmetic sequence**. For every <InlineMath math="100 \text{ m}" /> increase in altitude, the temperature drops by <InlineMath math="2^\circ\text{C}" />.

Given:
- The first term (<InlineMath math="U_1" />) at an altitude of <InlineMath math="100 \text{ m}" /> is <InlineMath math="32" />.
- The **common difference** (<InlineMath math="b" />) is <InlineMath math="-2" />.

An altitude of <InlineMath math="800 \text{ m}" /> corresponds to the <InlineMath math="8" />th term (<InlineMath math="U_8" />), and an altitude of <InlineMath math="1200 \text{ m}" /> corresponds to the <InlineMath math="12" />th term (<InlineMath math="U_{12}" />).

The formula for the <InlineMath math="n" />-th term of an arithmetic sequence is:

<BlockMath math="U_n = U_1 + (n-1)b" />

So, the ratio of the numerical Celsius readings at the two altitudes is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}U_8&=32+(8-1)(-2)=32-14=18 \\ U_{12}&=32+(12-1)(-2)=32-22=10 \\ U_8:U_{12}&=18:10=9:5\end{aligned}" />

Therefore, the ratio of the numerical Celsius readings is <InlineMath math="9:5" />.

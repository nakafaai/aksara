Distinguish **a change in temperature** from **the temperature itself**. A change is affected only by the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac95" />, while a temperature is also affected by the added 32.

# Temperature change and the Kelvin scale

If <InlineMath math="C" /> rises by 5, then <InlineMath math="F" /> rises by <InlineMath math="\tfrac95\cdot5=9" />, because the added 32 cancels out. The first statement is true.

From <InlineMath math="K=C+273" />, 300 K is <InlineMath math="300-273=27^\circ\text{C}" />, **not 37 °C**. The second statement is false.

# Comparing and doubling temperatures

Convert 100 °F to Celsius: <InlineMath math="C=\tfrac59(100-32)=\tfrac59\cdot68\approx37.8" />. This is higher than 37 °C, so the third statement is true.

Example: 10 °C is 50 °F, while 20 °C is 68 °F, not 100 °F. Because of **the added 32**, Fahrenheit is not proportional to Celsius. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **false**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **false**.

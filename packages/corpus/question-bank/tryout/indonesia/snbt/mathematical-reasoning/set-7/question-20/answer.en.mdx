#### Determine Amplitude

Amplitude (<InlineMath math="A" />) is **half the vertical distance** from crest to trough. Here that distance is <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="A = \frac{16}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="A = 8 \text{ meters}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Determine the Period

The period (<InlineMath math="T" />) is the time required for **one complete cycle**. Convert <InlineMath math="12\text{ hours }24\text{ minutes}" /> to decimal hours:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="T=\left(12+\frac{24}{60}\right)\text{ h}" />
  <BlockMath math="T=(12+0.4)\text{ h}" />
  <BlockMath math="T = 12.4 \text{ hours}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Choose the Phase and Write the Function

At <InlineMath math="t=0" />, the water crosses the **mean level while rising**. A sine function with no phase shift has exactly that behavior because <InlineMath math="\sin 0=0" /> and its initial slope is positive. Therefore, we can use:

<BlockMath math="h(t) = A \sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{T} t\right)" />

Substitute the values <InlineMath math="A = 8" /> and <InlineMath math="T = 12.4" />:

<BlockMath math="h(t) = 8 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{12.4} t\right)" />

Therefore, a possible model is <InlineMath math="h(t) = 8 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{12.4} t\right)" />.

The curve starts at the mean level and rises. Its crest at <InlineMath math="t=3.1" /> and trough at <InlineMath math="t=9.3" /> are <InlineMath math="9.3-3.1=6.2" /> hours apart, with a vertical difference of <InlineMath math="8-(-8)=16\text{ m}" />.

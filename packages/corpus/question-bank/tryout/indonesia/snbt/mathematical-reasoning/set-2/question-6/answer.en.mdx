To find the distance from point <InlineMath math="Q" /> to line <InlineMath math="BD" />, we will form triangle <InlineMath math="BDQ" /> and calculate its height from the vertex <InlineMath math="Q" />. The first step is to calculate the lengths of all three sides of this triangle.

#### Base diagonal

Line <InlineMath math="BD" /> is the diagonal of the cube's base with edge length <InlineMath math="s = 4" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}BD&=\sqrt{AB^2+AD^2} \\ &=\sqrt{4^2+4^2} \\ &=\sqrt{32}=4\sqrt2\end{aligned}" />

#### Side from the rear vertex

Point <InlineMath math="Q" /> is at the midpoint of <InlineMath math="FG" />. We can find the length of <InlineMath math="DQ" /> by considering the right-angled triangle <InlineMath math="DHQ" />. First, we calculate the length of <InlineMath math="HQ" /> on the top face of the cube:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}HQ&=\sqrt{HG^2+GQ^2} \\ &=\sqrt{4^2+2^2} \\ &=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5\end{aligned}" />

Next, use the Pythagorean theorem on triangle <InlineMath math="DHQ" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}DQ&=\sqrt{DH^2+HQ^2} \\ &=\sqrt{4^2+(2\sqrt5)^2} \\ &=\sqrt{16+20} \\ &=\sqrt{36}=6\end{aligned}" />

#### Side from the front vertex

The length of <InlineMath math="BQ" /> can be calculated from the right-angled triangle <InlineMath math="BFQ" />, with <InlineMath math="BF = 4" /> and <InlineMath math="FQ = 2" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}BQ&=\sqrt{BF^2+FQ^2} \\ &=\sqrt{4^2+2^2} \\ &=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5\end{aligned}" />

#### Perpendicular distance

Now we have triangle <InlineMath math="BDQ" /> with sides <InlineMath math="BD = 4\sqrt{2}" />, <InlineMath math="DQ = 6" />, and <InlineMath math="BQ = 2\sqrt{5}" />. Let <InlineMath math="O" /> be the perpendicular projection of <InlineMath math="Q" /> onto line <InlineMath math="BD" />, the foot of the perpendicular.

Let the length <InlineMath math="DO = x" />, then the length <InlineMath math="OB = 4\sqrt{2} - x" />. The length <InlineMath math="y=QO" /> is the altitude of the triangle and the distance we need. Using the two formed right-angled triangles, we obtain the equation:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}QO^2&=DQ^2-DO^2 \\ &=BQ^2-OB^2\end{aligned}" />

Substituting the side values:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&6^2-x^2 \\ &=(2\sqrt5)^2-(4\sqrt2-x)^2\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&36-x^2 \\ &=20-(32-8\sqrt2x+x^2)\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&36-x^2 \\ &=20-32+8\sqrt2x-x^2\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="36 = -12 + 8\sqrt{2}x" />
  <BlockMath math="48 = 8\sqrt{2}x" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}x&=\frac{48}{8\sqrt2} \\ &=\frac6{\sqrt2}=3\sqrt2\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

After finding <InlineMath math="x = 3\sqrt{2}" />, we substitute it back to find <InlineMath math="y" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}y&=\sqrt{DQ^2-x^2} \\ &=\sqrt{6^2-(3\sqrt2)^2}\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}y&=\sqrt{36-18} \\ &=\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the distance from point <InlineMath math="Q" /> to line <InlineMath math="BD" /> is <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{2}\text{ units}" />.

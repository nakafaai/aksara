All monetary values below are in rupiah. Let <InlineMath math="M" /> be the purchase cost, <InlineMath math="R" /> the total revenue, and <InlineMath math="U" /> the profit.

First, we calculate the total capital (buying price) for <InlineMath math="100" /> durians.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}M&=100\times8{,}000 \\ &=800{,}000\end{aligned}" />

Next, we calculate the total revenue from sales. The sales are divided into two parts: the first <InlineMath math="70" /> fruits and the remaining <InlineMath math="30" /> fruits.

**Revenue from the first <InlineMath math="70" /> fruits:**

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&70\times 10{,}000 \\ &=700{,}000\end{aligned}" />

**Revenue from the remaining <InlineMath math="30" /> fruits:**

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&30\times 7{,}000 \\ &=210{,}000\end{aligned}" />

Thus, the total revenue is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="R = 700{,}000 + 210{,}000" />
  <BlockMath math="= 910{,}000" />
</MathContainer>

Since the total revenue is greater than the total capital, the dealer makes a profit. The profit amount is the difference between revenue and capital:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="U = R - M" />
  <BlockMath math="= 910{,}000 - 800{,}000" />
  <BlockMath math="= 110{,}000" />
</MathContainer>

The profit percentage <InlineMath math="p" /> is the profit divided by the initial purchase cost:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="p = \frac{U}{M} \times 100\%" />
  <BlockMath math="= \frac{110{,}000}{800{,}000} \times 100\%" />
  <BlockMath math="= \frac{11}{80} \times 100\%" />
  <BlockMath math="= 13.75\%" />
</MathContainer>

So, the dealer makes a profit of <InlineMath math="13.75\%" />.

Each **fully submerged** sphere displaces water equal to its own volume. Because the cylinder was initially full, the remaining water volume is the cylinder volume minus the total sphere volume.

Let <InlineMath math="V_t" /> be the cylinder volume. Its radius is <InlineMath math="r_1=12\text{ cm}" /> and its height is <InlineMath math="t=18\text{ cm}" />, so the initial water volume is:

<BlockMath math="V_t = \pi r_1^2 t" />

<BlockMath math="= \pi (12)^2 (18)" />

<BlockMath math="= \pi (144) (18)" />

<BlockMath math="= 2592\pi \text{ cm}^3" />

Each sphere has radius <InlineMath math="r_2=3\text{ cm}" />. Its volume <InlineMath math="V_b" /> is:

<BlockMath math="V_b = \frac{4}{3} \pi r_2^3" />

<BlockMath math="= \frac{4}{3} \pi (3)^3" />

<BlockMath math="= \frac{4}{3} \pi (27)" />

<BlockMath math="= 36\pi \text{ cm}^3" />

# Counting the spheres

Let <InlineMath math="V_s" /> be the **remaining water volume**. It equals <InlineMath math="1980\pi \text{ cm}^3" />, so the number of spheres <InlineMath math="n" /> satisfies:

<BlockMath math="V_s=V_t-nV_b" />

<BlockMath math="1980\pi = 2592\pi - n(36\pi)" />

Divide both sides by <InlineMath math="\pi" /> to simplify the calculation:

<BlockMath math="1980 = 2592 - 36n" />

<BlockMath math="36n = 2592 - 1980" />

<BlockMath math="36n = 612" />

<BlockMath math="n = \frac{612}{36}=17" />

Thus, the cylinder contains <InlineMath math="17" /> spheres. No rounding is needed.

You can check the volume balance after inserting the spheres:

<BlockMath math="1980\pi+17(36\pi)=2592\pi\text{ cm}^3" />

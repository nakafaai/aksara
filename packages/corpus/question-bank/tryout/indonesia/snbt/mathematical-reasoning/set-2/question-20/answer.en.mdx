The amount of medicine is lowest **just before the next dose is taken**. So check those moments for each value of <InlineMath math="T" />.

# The first low point

Just before the second dose, the body holds only what remains of the first dose, <InlineMath math="400\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{T/6}" />. **The 100 mg limit must hold at this point**, so

<BlockMath math="400\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{T/6}\ge100\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\left(\tfrac12\right)^{T/6}\ge\tfrac14\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\tfrac{T}{6}\le2\;\Longleftrightarrow\;T\le12" />

# Checking T equal to 12 and 24

For <InlineMath math="T=12" />, the amount at 18:00 is exactly 100 mg, then it rises to 500 mg after the second dose. Just before the third dose, 12 hours later, it is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{500}{4}=125" /> mg. **The next low point is higher**, because leftover medicine accumulates.

For <InlineMath math="T=24" />, there is no second dose in the first 24 hours, and at hour 18 only 50 mg remain. This value fails.

The values 4, 6 and 8 hours also work, but they are all smaller than 12. The value 6 hours is tempting because it equals the half-life, but the condition is only the 100 mg limit.

So the largest value of <InlineMath math="T" /> that works is **12 hours**.

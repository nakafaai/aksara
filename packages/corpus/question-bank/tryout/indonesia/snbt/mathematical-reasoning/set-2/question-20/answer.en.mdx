**Delivery Schedule Analysis**

We need to find the time interval for all three suppliers to deliver goods simultaneously. The concept used is the Least Common Multiple (LCM).

Given delivery schedules:
*   Oil <InlineMath math="A" />: every <InlineMath math="3\text{ days}" />.
*   Oil <InlineMath math="B" />: every <InlineMath math="4\text{ days}" />.
*   Oil <InlineMath math="C" />: every <InlineMath math="x\text{ days}" />.

The first simultaneous delivery occurred on April <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="2023" />. The <InlineMath math="11" />th simultaneous delivery occurs <InlineMath math="120\text{ days}" /> later.

**Calculating Delivery Interval**

Since the first simultaneous delivery has already occurred, the <InlineMath math="11" />th simultaneous delivery means there is a difference of <InlineMath math="11 - 1 = 10" /> simultaneous delivery intervals. The total elapsed time is <InlineMath math="120\text{ days}" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{120}{10}=12\text{ days}" />

This means all three suppliers deliver goods simultaneously every <InlineMath math="12\text{ days}" />. This is the LCM value of their delivery schedules.

<BlockMath math="\text{LCM}(3, 4, x) = 12" />

**Determining the Unknown Value**

We know that <InlineMath math="\text{LCM}(3, 4) = 12" />. For <InlineMath math="\text{LCM}(12, x) = 12" />, <InlineMath math="x" /> must be a factor of <InlineMath math="12" /> (a number that divides <InlineMath math="12" /> without a remainder).

Checking the available options:
*   <InlineMath math="5, 7, 8, 9" /> are not factors of <InlineMath math="12" />.
*   <InlineMath math="6" /> is a factor of <InlineMath math="12" />.

Among the listed choices, <InlineMath math="x=6" /> is possible. The other positive factors of <InlineMath math="12" /> also satisfy the equation, but they are not listed.

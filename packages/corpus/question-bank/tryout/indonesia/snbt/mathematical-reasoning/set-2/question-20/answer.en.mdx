The amount of medicine is lowest **just before the next dose is taken**. So check those moments for each value of <InlineMath math="T" />.

# The first low point

Just before the second dose, the body holds only what remains of the first dose, <InlineMath math="360\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{T/8}" />. **The 90 mg limit must hold at this point**, so

<BlockMath math="360\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{T/8}\ge90\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\left(\tfrac12\right)^{T/8}\ge\tfrac14\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\tfrac{T}{8}\le2\;\Longleftrightarrow\;T\le16" />

# Checking T equal to 16 and 24

For <InlineMath math="T=16" />, the amount at 22:00 is exactly 90 mg, which **still meets the condition of not less than 90 mg**. After the second dose it becomes 450 mg, and at the end of the first 24 hours it is still <InlineMath math="450\cdot\tfrac12=225" /> mg.

For <InlineMath math="T=24" />, there is no second dose before hour 24, and just before hour 24 only 45 mg remain. This value fails.

The values 4, 8 and 12 hours also work, but they are all smaller than 16. The value 8 hours is tempting because it equals the half-life, but the condition is only the 90 mg limit.

So the largest value of <InlineMath math="T" /> that works is **16 hours**.

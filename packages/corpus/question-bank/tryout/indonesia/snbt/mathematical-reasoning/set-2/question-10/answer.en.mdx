#### Probability from the first box

Let <InlineMath math="A" /> be the event of drawing a red ball from the first box. This box contains <InlineMath math="4+3=7" /> balls, of which <InlineMath math="4" /> are red. Each ball is equally likely to be drawn, so:

<BlockMath math="P(A)=\frac47" />

#### Probability from the second box

Let <InlineMath math="B" /> be the event of drawing a white ball from the second box. This box contains <InlineMath math="7+3=10" /> balls, of which <InlineMath math="3" /> are white. Therefore:

<BlockMath math="P(B)=\frac3{10}" />

#### Probability of both events

The two draws are independent. The result from the first box does not change the probabilities in the second box. Multiply the individual probabilities to find the probability that both events occur:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(A\cap B)&=P(A)P(B) \\ &=\frac47\times\frac3{10} \\ &=\frac{12}{70} \end{aligned}" />

The probability of drawing a red ball from the first box and a white ball from the second box is <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{70}" />.

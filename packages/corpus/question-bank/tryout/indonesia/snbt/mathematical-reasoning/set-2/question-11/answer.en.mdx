Let the candidate answer <InlineMath math="b" /> questions correctly and <InlineMath math="w" /> wrongly, with <InlineMath math="b+w\le40" />. The total score is <InlineMath math="4b-w" />, **four times the correct answers minus the wrong answers**, and each statement is checked with this formula.

# Candidates who answer everything

If every question is answered, <InlineMath math="w=40-b" /> and the score is <InlineMath math="5b-40" />. This score is **always a multiple of 5**, while 102 is not. The first statement is false.

# A score of 159, negative scores and zero

The top score is 160, with 40 correct answers. With only 39 correct, the score is at most <InlineMath math="4\cdot39=156" />. There is no way to get 159, so the second statement is true.

A candidate who answers one question wrongly scores <InlineMath math="-1" />, so a negative score is possible. The third statement is true.

A candidate with 1 correct and 4 wrong answers scores <InlineMath math="4-4=0" />. **A score of 0 also appears without leaving everything blank**, so the fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **true**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **false**.

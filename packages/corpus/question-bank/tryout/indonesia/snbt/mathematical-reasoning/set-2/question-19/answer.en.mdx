The formula <InlineMath math="M(t)=360\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{t/8}" /> is **exponential decay**: over equal time intervals the amount shrinks by the same factor, not by the same difference.

# The decrease per hour is not constant

In the first 8 hours the medicine drops by 180 mg, but in the next 8 hours only by 90 mg. So **the decrease is not constant** and cannot be 45 mg every hour. The first statement is false.

# Computing several times

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
M(24)&=360\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{3}=45\\
M(4)&=360\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{1/2}=\frac{360}{\sqrt2}=180\sqrt2\approx254.6\\
M(40)&=360\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{5}=\frac{360}{32}=11.25
\end{aligned}" />

06:00 the next day is 24 hours after the first dose, so the second statement is true. After 4 hours there are about 254.6 mg. The figure 270 mg **assumes a linear decrease**, half of the 180 mg lost in the first 8 hours. The third statement is false. The fourth statement is true.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **true**.

The formula <InlineMath math="M(t)=400\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{t/6}" /> is **exponential decay**: over equal time intervals the amount shrinks by the same factor, not by the same difference.

# The decrease per hour is not constant

In the first 6 hours the medicine drops by 200 mg, but in the next 6 hours only by 100 mg. So **the decrease is not constant** and cannot be 50 mg every hour. The first statement is false.

# Computing several times

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
M(18)&=400\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{3}=50\\
M(3)&=400\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{1/2}=\frac{400}{\sqrt2}=200\sqrt2\approx282.8\\
M(30)&=400\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{5}=\frac{400}{32}=12.5
\end{aligned}" />

Midnight the next day is 18 hours after 06:00, so the second statement is true. After 3 hours there are about 282.8 mg. The figure 300 mg **assumes a linear decrease**, half of the 200 mg lost in the first 6 hours. The third statement is false. The fourth statement is true.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **true**.

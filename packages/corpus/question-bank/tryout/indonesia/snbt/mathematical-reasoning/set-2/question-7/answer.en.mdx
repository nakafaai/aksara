Every statement rests on the similar triangles of the pole and the child. For a child of height <InlineMath math="h" /> at distance <InlineMath math="x" />, **the shadow length <InlineMath math="s" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="\tfrac{h}{s}=\tfrac{4.8}{x+s}" />**.

# A child 1.6 m tall

<BlockMath math="4.8s=1.6(x+s)\;\Longrightarrow\;3.2s=1.6x\;\Longrightarrow\;s=\tfrac x2" />

The first statement is true. After 10 seconds the child is at <InlineMath math="x=12" />, the shadow is 6 m, and **the tip is at <InlineMath math="12+6=18" /> m**. The second statement is true.

The child moves away 1.2 m every second, so the shadow grows only <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1.2}{2}=0.6" /> m every second. The third statement is false.

# A child 2 m tall

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
4.8s&=2(x+s)\;\Longrightarrow\;2.8s=2x\\
x=7&\;\Longrightarrow\;2.8s=14\;\Longrightarrow\;s=5
\end{aligned}" />

A taller child **has a longer shadow**, 5 m, not 3.5 m. The figure 3.5 m comes from reusing <InlineMath math="s=\tfrac x2" />, which holds only for the 1.6 m child. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **false**.

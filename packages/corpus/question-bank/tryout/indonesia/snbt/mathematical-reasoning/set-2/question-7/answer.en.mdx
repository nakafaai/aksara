Every statement rests on the similar triangles of the pole and the child. For a child of height <InlineMath math="h" /> at distance <InlineMath math="x" />, **the shadow length <InlineMath math="s" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="\tfrac{h}{s}=\tfrac{6}{x+s}" />**.

# A child 1.5 m tall

<BlockMath math="6s=1.5(x+s)\;\Longrightarrow\;4.5s=1.5x\;\Longrightarrow\;s=\tfrac x3" />

The first statement is true. After 12 seconds the child is at <InlineMath math="x=12" />, the shadow is 4 m, and **the tip is at <InlineMath math="12+4=16" /> m**. The second statement is true.

The child moves away 1 m every second, so the shadow grows only <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> m every second. The third statement is false.

# A child 2 m tall

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
6s&=2(x+s)\;\Longrightarrow\;4s=2x\;\Longrightarrow\;s=\tfrac x2\\
x=8&\;\Longrightarrow\;s=4
\end{aligned}" />

A taller child **has a longer shadow**, 4 m, not 3 m. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **false**.

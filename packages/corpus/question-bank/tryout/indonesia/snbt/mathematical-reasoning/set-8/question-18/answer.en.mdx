To determine the last two digits, use the cycles of powers modulo <InlineMath math="100" />. We have

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3^{20}\equiv1\pmod{100}" />
  <BlockMath math="2^{22}\equiv2^2\pmod{100}" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, the residues of powers of <InlineMath math="3" /> repeat every <InlineMath math="20" /> exponents. The same holds for powers of <InlineMath math="2" /> from the second power onward: multiplying both sides of the second congruence by a power of <InlineMath math="2" /> preserves the pattern.

The exponents satisfy <InlineMath math="2026\equiv6\pmod{20}" /> and <InlineMath math="2025\equiv5\pmod{20}" />. Therefore,

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}3^{2026}&\equiv3^6\pmod{100}\\&\equiv29\pmod{100}\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}2^{2025}&\equiv2^5\pmod{100}\\&\equiv32\pmod{100}\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Their sum has remainder <InlineMath math="29+32=61" />.

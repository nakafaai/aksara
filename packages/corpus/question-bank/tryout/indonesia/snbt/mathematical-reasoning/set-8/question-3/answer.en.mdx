All four statements depend on one thing: **how many days one cylinder actually lasts**. A cylinder is only swapped in the morning, so that number is always whole, and gas that isn't enough for a full day is lost. So first work out how long each cylinder lasts, then test each statement.

# How long a cylinder lasts at the current use

The daily use is <InlineMath math="0.3\cdot3+0.1\cdot7=1.6\text{ kg}" />. A cylinder stays as long as its gas covers a full day, so **the number of days is the quotient rounded down**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
5.5\text{ kg}: 3\cdot1.6&=4.8\le5.5 < 4\cdot1.6=6.4 \text{, so } 3\text{ days, } 0.7\text{ kg left}\\
12\text{ kg}: 7\cdot1.6&=11.2\le12 < 8\cdot1.6=12.8 \text{, so } 7\text{ days, } 0.8\text{ kg left}
\end{aligned}" />

# Costs before and after the price rise

The cost per day is the price of a cylinder divided by the days it lasts:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{up to 15 July: } \frac{87{,}000}{3}&=29{,}000 \text{ and } \frac{196{,}000}{7}=28{,}000\\
\text{from 16 July: } \frac{93{,}000}{3}&=31{,}000 \text{ and } \frac{224{,}000}{7}=32{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The first statement is true. Up to <InlineMath math="15" /> July, the <InlineMath math="12\text{ kg}" /> cylinder is cheaper per day, even though its price per kilogram, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{196{,}000}{12}\approx16{,}333" /> rupiah, is higher than <InlineMath math="\tfrac{87{,}000}{5.5}\approx15{,}818" /> rupiah. That happens because **the small cylinder throws away a bigger share of its gas**: <InlineMath math="0.7" /> out of <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ kg}" />, against <InlineMath math="0.8" /> out of <InlineMath math="12\text{ kg}" />.

The second statement is false. From <InlineMath math="16" /> July, the <InlineMath math="12\text{ kg}" /> cylinder costs <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}32{,}000" /> a day, more than the <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ kg}" /> cylinder at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}31{,}000" /> a day. The big cylinder's price went up by <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}28{,}000" />, far more than the small one's rise of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}6{,}000" />.

# A slightly larger daily use

If the small stove runs for <InlineMath math="8\text{ hours}" />, the daily use becomes <InlineMath math="0.9+0.8=1.7\text{ kg}" />. Check how long the <InlineMath math="12\text{ kg}" /> cylinder lasts:

<BlockMath math="7\cdot1.7=11.9\le12 < 8\cdot1.7=13.6" />

The cylinder still lasts <InlineMath math="7" /> days, so the cost stays <InlineMath math="\tfrac{196{,}000}{7}=28{,}000" /> rupiah a day. The third statement is true. The extra use **only shrinks the lost gas**, from <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ kg}" /> to <InlineMath math="0.1\text{ kg}" />, without changing the number of days.

# Gas lost at each swap

From the first step, the <InlineMath math="12\text{ kg}" /> cylinder leaves <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ kg}" />, while the <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ kg}" /> cylinder leaves <InlineMath math="0.7\text{ kg}" />. So **the big cylinder actually loses more gas** at each swap, and the fourth statement is false. The big cylinder does lose a smaller share of what it holds, but the statement compares amounts of gas, not shares.

So the first statement is true, the second is false, the third is true, and the fourth is false.

You're looking for the largest number of patients who can be served. Each patient served uses a different bag, so **the number of patients served can't be more than the number of bags that are actually useful**. Here's the plan: find which bags are useful, count that upper limit, then show a way to hand out the bags that reaches it.

# Bags that suit each patient

Combine the ABO rule and the rhesus rule for each waiting patient group:

| Patient | Suitable bags |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^-" /> | <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^-" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^-" /> | <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^-,\ \mathrm{O}^-" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\mathrm{B}^-" /> | <InlineMath math="\mathrm{B}^-,\ \mathrm{O}^-" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^+" /> | <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^+,\ \mathrm{O}^-" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^+" /> | <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^+,\ \mathrm{A}^-,\ \mathrm{O}^+,\ \mathrm{O}^-" /> |

Several patient groups need O bags at the same time. So **one O bag can't be counted twice** for two different patient groups.

# An upper limit from the useful bags

No patient is B positive or AB, so the <InlineMath math="\mathrm{B}^+" />, <InlineMath math="\mathrm{AB}^+" /> and <InlineMath math="\mathrm{AB}^-" /> bags are no use. A <InlineMath math="\mathrm{B}^-" /> bag only suits the <InlineMath math="\mathrm{B}^-" /> patient, and there's only one, so **at most one of the two** <InlineMath math="\mathrm{B}^-" /> bags gets used. The bags that could be used are:

<BlockMath math="\underbrace{2}_{\mathrm{O}^-}+\underbrace{1}_{\mathrm{A}^-}+\underbrace{1}_{\mathrm{B}^-}+\underbrace{14}_{\mathrm{O}^+}+\underbrace{9}_{\mathrm{A}^+}=27" />

So at most <InlineMath math="27" /> patients can be served.

# A way to reach the limit

Now show that <InlineMath math="27" /> really can be reached:

- Two <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^-" /> bags to two <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^-" /> patients.
- One <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^-" /> bag to one <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^-" /> patient.
- One <InlineMath math="\mathrm{B}^-" /> bag to the <InlineMath math="\mathrm{B}^-" /> patient.
- Fourteen <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^+" /> bags to fourteen <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^+" /> patients.
- Nine <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^+" /> bags to nine <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^+" /> patients.

**Every one of these matches**, and the total is <InlineMath math="2+1+1+14+9=27" /> patients. Check it from the patients' side: the ones left waiting are one <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^-" />, two <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^-" />, two <InlineMath math="\mathrm{O}^+" /> and three <InlineMath math="\mathrm{A}^+" /> patients, that's <InlineMath math="8" /> people. Out of <InlineMath math="35" /> patients, <InlineMath math="35-8=27" /> are served, a match.

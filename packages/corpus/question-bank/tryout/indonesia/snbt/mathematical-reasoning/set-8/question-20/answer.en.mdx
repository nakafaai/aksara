Drawn area changes by **the square of the length ratio**. So compare the areas first, then take the square root to get the length ratio.

# Ratio of areas and lengths

On the tourist map the lake is drawn with area <InlineMath math="2\cdot1.2=2.4\text{ cm}^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{2.4}{0.6}&=4\\
\sqrt4&=2
\end{aligned}" />

Each length on the other map is **half as long** as on the tourist map, so each centimetre stands for twice the distance.

# Scale of the other map

<BlockMath math="2\cdot250{,}000=500{,}000" />

Check: at 1 : 500,000, 1 cm stands for 5 km, so <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}^2" /> stands for <InlineMath math="25\text{ km}^2" />, and <InlineMath math="0.6\cdot25=15\text{ km}^2" />.

The value 1 : 1,000,000 multiplies by the area ratio 4 without taking the square root. The values 1 : 125,000 and 1 : 62,500 divide, although a smaller drawing means each centimetre stands for a longer distance. The value 1 : 250,000 is the tourist map's own scale.

So the scale of the other map is **1 : 500,000**.

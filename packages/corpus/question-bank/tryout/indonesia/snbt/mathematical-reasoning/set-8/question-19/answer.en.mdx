A scale links lengths on the map to real lengths. For this map, **1 cm stands for 250,000 cm, which is 2.5 km**.

# Reading the scale

A distance of 10 km is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{10}{2.5}=4" /> cm on the map. The first statement is true.

250,000 cm is 2.5 km, **not 25 km**, because 1 km is 100,000 cm. The second statement is false.

# Changing the scale

At 1 : 500,000 each centimetre stands for 5 km, twice as far. The same real distance is drawn **half as long as before**, so the third statement is true.

The enlarged photocopy doubles every length on the map, while real distances stay the same. So 2 cm on the copy stands for 2.5 km, and 1 cm for 1.25 km. The scale becomes 1 : 125,000, **not 1 : 500,000**. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **false**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **false**.

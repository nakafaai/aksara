You're looking for the first moment when **the money the organisers receive reaches the production cost** of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000{,}000" />. Here's the plan: find what the organisers receive per ticket in each tier, add it up tier by tier, then find how many normal tickets are still needed.

# Money received per ticket

The organisers receive <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of each ticket price by tier. The discount and the service fee are the platform's business, so **neither changes what the organisers receive**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{early bird: } 90\%\cdot120{,}000&=108{,}000\\
\text{presale: } 90\%\cdot150{,}000&=135{,}000\\
\text{normal: } 90\%\cdot200{,}000&=180{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

# The total after the first two tiers

First find the money collected once all early bird and presale tickets are sold:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
150\cdot108{,}000+300\cdot135{,}000&=16{,}200{,}000+40{,}500{,}000\\
&=56{,}700{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

That's still less than <InlineMath math="100{,}000{,}000" />, so **the break-even point falls in the normal tier**.

# Normal tickets needed

The shortfall is <InlineMath math="100{,}000{,}000-56{,}700{,}000=43{,}300{,}000" /> rupiah. Each normal ticket adds <InlineMath math="180{,}000" /> rupiah, so the number of normal tickets <InlineMath math="k" /> must satisfy:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
180{,}000k&\ge43{,}300{,}000\\
k&\ge240\tfrac{5}{9}
\end{aligned}" />

Tickets come in whole numbers, so you **round up** to <InlineMath math="k=241" />. The total number sold is <InlineMath math="450+241=691" />.

Check both sides. After <InlineMath math="690" /> tickets, the organisers have <InlineMath math="56{,}700{,}000+240\cdot180{,}000=99{,}900{,}000" /> rupiah, still short. After <InlineMath math="691" /> tickets, they have <InlineMath math="56{,}700{,}000+241\cdot180{,}000=100{,}080{,}000" /> rupiah, which reaches the production cost.

# Common wrong answers

A few shortcuts lead to other numbers. If you forget **the ninety percent share for the organisers**, you get <InlineMath math="635" /> tickets, because <InlineMath math="63{,}000{,}000+185\cdot200{,}000=100{,}000{,}000" />. If you round <InlineMath math="240\tfrac{5}{9}" /> down, you get <InlineMath math="690" /> tickets, but the money isn't enough yet. If you subtract the four ticket discount from the organisers' money, your answer comes out too large, because the platform pays that discount.

So at least <InlineMath math="691" /> tickets have to be sold.

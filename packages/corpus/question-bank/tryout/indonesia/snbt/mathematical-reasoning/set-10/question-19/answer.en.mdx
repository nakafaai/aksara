#### Find the base and the height

Use the coordinates <InlineMath math="A=(-3,-2,-2)" />, <InlineMath math="B=(3,-2,2)" />, and <InlineMath math="C=(-3,2,2)" /> from the diagram. You can treat <InlineMath math="AC" /> as the base. First check that **the two sides meeting at B are equal**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}AB^2&=6^2+0^2+4^2=52\\BC^2&=6^2+(-4)^2+0^2=52\end{aligned}" />

So triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is isosceles. Its height from <InlineMath math="B" /> meets the midpoint <InlineMath math="M" /> of <InlineMath math="AC" /> at a right angle:

<BlockMath math="M=\left(\frac{-3+(-3)}2,\frac{-2+2}2,\frac{-2+2}2\right)=(-3,0,0)" />

The base and height follow from the distance formula. Each coordinate difference is a length in centimetres:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}AC&=\sqrt{0^2+4^2+4^2}=\sqrt{32}=4\sqrt2\text{ cm}\\BM&=\sqrt{6^2+(-2)^2+2^2}=\sqrt{44}=2\sqrt{11}\text{ cm}\end{aligned}" />

Now use **half the base times the perpendicular height**:

<BlockMath math="\text{Area of }ABC=\frac12(4\sqrt2)(2\sqrt{11})=4\sqrt{22}\text{ cm}^2" />

#### Check with a cross product

The vectors from <InlineMath math="A" /> are <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}=(6,0,4)" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AC}=(0,4,4)" />. The cross-product magnitude gives the **area of the parallelogram** spanned by these vectors, so divide it by two for the triangle:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}&=(0\cdot4-4\cdot4,\;4\cdot0-6\cdot4,\;6\cdot4-0\cdot0)\\&=(-16,-24,24)\\\left\lVert\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}\right\rVert&=\sqrt{(-16)^2+(-24)^2+24^2}\\&=\sqrt{256+576+576}=\sqrt{1408}=8\sqrt{22}\text{ cm}^2\\\text{Area of }ABC&=\frac12(8\sqrt{22})=4\sqrt{22}\text{ cm}^2\end{aligned}" />

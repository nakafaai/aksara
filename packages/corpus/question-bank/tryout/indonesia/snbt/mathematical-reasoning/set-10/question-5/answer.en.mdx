Let <InlineMath math="p=AB" /> and <InlineMath math="w=BC" />, with lengths in centimetres. The conditions are <InlineMath math="p-w=5" /> and <InlineMath math="p^2+w^2=625" />. Substituting <InlineMath math="p=w+5" /> gives

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}(w+5)^2+w^2&=625 \\ w^2+5w-300&=0 \\ (w+20)(w-15)&=0\end{aligned}" />

Side lengths are positive, so <InlineMath math="w=15" /> and <InlineMath math="p=20" />.

The ratio <InlineMath math="AE:EB=1:3" /> gives <InlineMath math="AE=20/4=5" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="AED" /> is right-angled at <InlineMath math="A" />, so the length of <InlineMath math="ED" /> in centimetres is

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}ED&=\sqrt{AE^2+AD^2} \\ &=\sqrt{5^2+15^2} \\ &=5\sqrt{10}\end{aligned}" />

Thus <InlineMath math="ED" /> has length <InlineMath math="5\sqrt{10}\text{ cm}" />.

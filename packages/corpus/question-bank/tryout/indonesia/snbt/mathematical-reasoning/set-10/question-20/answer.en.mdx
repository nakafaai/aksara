You're looking for the fewest weekly portions that give a net profit of at least <InlineMath math="600{,}000" />, with a fixed mix of dishes. Here's the plan: find the mix from the chart totals, turn it into one **bundle of portions**, find the profit of one bundle, then find how many bundles are enough.

# The mix of the three dishes

Add up the portions of each dish over the five days.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{chicken rice} &= 40+30+20+35+25=150\\
\text{fried noodles} &= 30+25+10+20+15=100\\
\text{orange ice} &= 40+65+40+45+60=250
\end{aligned}" />

The ratio is <InlineMath math="150:100:250=3:2:5" />. For the ratio to be **exactly the same**, weekly sales have to be made of whole bundles of <InlineMath math="3" /> chicken rice, <InlineMath math="2" /> fried noodles, and <InlineMath math="5" /> orange ice, which is <InlineMath math="10" /> portions per bundle.

# Profit of one bundle and the fixed costs

The profit per portion is <InlineMath math="4{,}000" />, <InlineMath math="3{,}000" />, and <InlineMath math="2{,}000" />. A week's rent and wages **don't depend on the number of portions**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{profit of one bundle} &= 3\cdot4{,}000+2\cdot3{,}000+5\cdot2{,}000=28{,}000\\
\text{rent and wages for a week} &= 350{,}000+5\cdot70{,}000=700{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

# How many bundles are enough

Say <InlineMath math="k" /> bundles are sold. The net profit has to be at least <InlineMath math="600{,}000" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
28{,}000k-700{,}000 &\ge 600{,}000\\
28{,}000k &\ge 1{,}300{,}000\\
k &\ge 46.42\ldots
\end{aligned}" />

Bundles have to be whole, so **at least** <InlineMath math="47" /> bundles, which is <InlineMath math="47\cdot10=470" /> portions.

# Checking

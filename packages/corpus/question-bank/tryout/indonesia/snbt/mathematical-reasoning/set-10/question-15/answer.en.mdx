The concentration of a mixture is **the average of the ingredients' concentrations weighted by their volumes**. Water counts as an ingredient at 0%.

# Effect of water

Water contains no juice, so the volume of juice does not change, only the concentration drops. The first statement is false.

Syrup A with water is an average of 60% and 0%, so it **can never be above 60%**. The fourth statement is false.

# Mixing syrups A and B

With equal volumes the concentration is the plain average, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{60\%+20\%}{2}=40\%" />. The second statement is true.

For any ratio of volumes, a weighted average of two numbers **always lies between them**. So a mixture of syrups A and B is always between 20% and 60%. The third statement is true.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **true**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **false**.

Three sides of length <InlineMath math="L" /> and four sides of width <InlineMath math="W" /> must be fenced, so <InlineMath math="3L+4W=76" />. All dimensions are in metres. For <InlineMath math="L" /> to be an integer, <InlineMath math="W\equiv1\pmod3" /> is required.

The combined area in square metres is

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}A(W)&=2W\cdot\frac{76-4W}{3} \\ &=\frac{152W-8W^2}{3}\end{aligned}" />

This downward-opening parabola has its unconstrained maximum at <InlineMath math="W=152/16=9.5" />.

The area decreases as <InlineMath math="W" /> moves farther from <InlineMath math="9.5" />. The nearby candidates satisfying the integer condition are:

| Width | Length | Area |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="16" /> | <InlineMath math="224" /> |
| <InlineMath math="10" /> | <InlineMath math="12" /> | <InlineMath math="240" /> |
| <InlineMath math="13" /> | <InlineMath math="8" /> | <InlineMath math="208" /> |

The permitted value <InlineMath math="W=10" /> is closest to the maximum and gives the greatest combined area, <InlineMath math="240\text{ m}^2" />.

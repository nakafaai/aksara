There are <InlineMath math="4" /> seat pairs with exactly one seat between A and B, <InlineMath math="2" /> orders for A and B, and <InlineMath math="4!" /> arrangements of the others, giving <InlineMath math="4\cdot2\cdot4!=192" /> arrangements.

Number the seats from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="6" />. For each possible A and B seat pair, the number of adjacent pairs among the remaining seats is:

| Seats for A and B | Adjacent pairs |
| --- | ---: |
| <InlineMath math="(1,3)" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(2,4)" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(3,5)" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(4,6)" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |

Across the four possible A-B seat pairs, the remaining seats contain <InlineMath math="2+1+1+2=6" /> adjacent seat pairs for C and D.

Each such pair permits <InlineMath math="2\cdot2\cdot2=8" /> orders of A-B, C-D, and E-F. Thus <InlineMath math="6\cdot8=48" /> arrangements are invalid. The required count is <InlineMath math="192-48=144" />.

Overall plastic use falls only if **the plastic saved on shopping bags is more than the plastic added by bin bags**. Percentages alone are not enough, because <InlineMath math="70\%" /> and <InlineMath math="40\%" /> are taken from different starting amounts, and one bin bag may be thicker or heavier than one shopping bag.

So the information needed is a comparison of the plastic in both kinds of bag in the same unit, such as weight. With it, you can check whether the net change is a saving or an increase.

The other options do not help judge total plastic:

- The price of a bin bag does not show how much plastic is used.
- The number of minimarkets does not change the comparison between plastic saved and plastic added.
- Why residents bring their own bags explains behavior, not the amount of plastic.
- The color of bags has nothing to do with the amount of plastic.

So the information most needed is a comparison of the plastic saved on shopping bags with the plastic added by bin bags.

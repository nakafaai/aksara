The ladder, wall, and floor form **a right triangle** with the ladder as the hypotenuse. Find the height of the top of the ladder with the Pythagorean theorem in both positions.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{initial height} &= \sqrt{250^2-70^2}=\sqrt{62{,}500-4{,}900}=\sqrt{57{,}600}=240 \\
\text{final height} &= \sqrt{250^2-150^2}=\sqrt{62{,}500-22{,}500}=\sqrt{40{,}000}=200
\end{aligned}" />

The top drops from <InlineMath math="240" /> cm to <InlineMath math="200" /> cm, so it slides down <InlineMath math="240-200=40" /> cm.

Note that the foot moves <InlineMath math="150-70=80" /> cm, but the top drops only <InlineMath math="40" /> cm. The two ends need not move by the same amount, so each must be computed on its own. So the top of the ladder slides down <InlineMath math="40" /> cm.

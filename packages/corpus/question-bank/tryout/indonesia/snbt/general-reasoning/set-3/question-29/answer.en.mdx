We count **equally likely pairs of balls**, rather than treating the three colour outcomes as equally likely. Since the order of the two balls does not matter, the total number of pairs is

<BlockMath math="\binom{10}{2}=\frac{10\times9}{2}=45." />

| Pair colours | Number of pairs | Listed payout, thousand rupiah |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Red and red | <InlineMath math="\binom{6}{2}=\frac{6\times5}{2}=15" /> | 30 |
| Red and blue | <InlineMath math="6\times4=24" /> | 15 |
| Blue and blue | <InlineMath math="\binom{4}{2}=\frac{4\times3}{2}=6" /> | 0 |

The table accounts for <InlineMath math="15+24+6=45" /> pairs, matching the total. The expected payout is the average prize weighted by each outcome's probability:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} E&amp;=\frac{30(15)+15(24)+0(6)}{45} \\ &amp;=\frac{450+360+0}{45} \\ &amp;=\frac{810}{45}=18. \end{aligned}" />

The operator applies **the same factor to every prize**, namely <InlineMath math="90\%" />. We therefore multiply the expectation by this factor as well:

<BlockMath math="0.9\times18=16.2=\frac{81}{5}\text{ thousand rupiah}." />

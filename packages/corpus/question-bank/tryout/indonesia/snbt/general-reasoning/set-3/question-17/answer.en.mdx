Let <InlineMath math="M_n" /> and <InlineMath math="M_f" /> be the rents of the nearby and farther rooms. Let their transport costs be <InlineMath math="C_n" /> and <InlineMath math="C_f" />.

The argument is weakened when the extra rent near work exceeds the saving in transport costs:

<BlockMath math="M_n-M_f>C_f-C_n" />

Rearranging gives

<BlockMath math="M_n+C_n>M_f+C_f" />

The farther room then costs less overall. The statement about rent premiums exceeding extra travel costs directly weakens the argument. Preferences, the appeal of low rent, and the practice of comparing costs do not prove that the nearby room is cheapest.

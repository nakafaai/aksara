A claim using **every week** must hold for all four weeks. A single counterexample is enough to disprove it.

#### Compare sales within the same week

| Week | Pashmina compared with square | Bergo compared with square |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="9<12" /> | <InlineMath math="14>12" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="14<15" /> | <InlineMath math="16>15" /> |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="20<21" /> | <InlineMath math="18<21" /> |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="27<30" /> | <InlineMath math="20<30" /> |

Pashmina sales are lower than square headscarf sales in every week. In contrast, **weeks three and four** contradict the claim that bergo sales are always higher than square headscarf sales. That bergo claim is therefore inconsistent.

#### Check the sales pattern and increases

The bergo counts form an **arithmetic sequence**, because the differences between consecutive weeks are equal:

<BlockMath math="16-14=18-16=20-18=2" />

Sales of all three styles also rise from one week to the next. To compare overall increases, subtract the first-week count from the fourth-week count:

| Style | Increase from the first to the fourth week |
| --- | --- |
| Square | <InlineMath math="30-12=18" /> |
| Pashmina | <InlineMath math="27-9=18" /> |
| Bergo | <InlineMath math="20-14=6" /> |

Bergo has the smallest increase. The arithmetic-sequence claim, weekly-increase claim, and smallest-increase claim are all supported. Only the claim that bergo sales always exceed square headscarf sales fails.

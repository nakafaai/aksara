From that, the head of nursing concludes that nurses make mistakes more often after working more than <InlineMath math="7" /> hours and predicts that the night-time error rate will fall once the shift is split in two. The question asks for the assumption that **must** be true, the one whose reversal makes the reasoning collapse.

Focus on the words "more often". They mean the error rate, errors per medication given, is higher in the last hours. Errors piling up in the last hours doesn't by itself mean the rate there is high. If most medicines are given in those hours anyway, you'd expect most errors there too.

Let <InlineMath math="a" /> be the number of medications given from 04.00 to 07.00. The other <InlineMath math="2{,}000-a" /> are given from 21.00 to 04.00, with <InlineMath math="18-12=6" /> errors. The rate in the last hours is higher than before them when:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac{12}{a} &> \frac{6}{2{,}000-a} \\ 12\,(2{,}000-a) &> 6a \\ 24{,}000 &> 18a \\ a &< \tfrac{24{,}000}{18}=\tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 2{,}000 \end{aligned}" />

So the data shows nurses err more often in the last hours only if the medications given from 04.00 to 07.00 are **less than two-thirds** of all night-shift medications. Test it by reversing it. If the last hours handle two-thirds or more, their error rate is equal to or lower than the earlier rate. The data no longer points to fatigue, and the prediction loses its footing. An assumption whose reversal breaks the argument is the required one.

The argument still works above one half, so this condition is too strict.
- What happens to the morning and afternoon shifts isn't needed, because the head of nursing's conclusion is only about night-time.
- Data on nurses working overtime in the morning and afternoon shifts would support the argument, but even if it's false, the night-shift data stands on its own. It helps, but it isn't required.
- The number of medications given may change, because the conclusion is about the error rate, errors per <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> medications, not about the number of errors.

So the required assumption is that the medications given from 04.00 to 07.00 are less than two-thirds of all night-shift medications.

The phrase **at least one** guarantees that a participant drank tea without added sugar. It directly supports the fourth choice without any extra assumption.

| Other choice | Why it is not a necessary conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| Every participant chose tea with sugar | Contradicts the existence of a tea drinker without added sugar |
| Every tea drinker added sugar | Contradicts the existence of a tea drinker without added sugar |
| Nobody drank tea without sugar | Directly denies the stated existence of at least one person |
| Every participant chose tea | The passage only says some participants chose tea, which does not establish all participants |

Distinguish **some** from **all**. For example, two participants could choose tea, one with sugar and one without, while another participant chooses water. This example satisfies every premise but shows that everyone choosing tea is not required.

The necessary conclusion is therefore that at least one participant drank tea without added sugar.

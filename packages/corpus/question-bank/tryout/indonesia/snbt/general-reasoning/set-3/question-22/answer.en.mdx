# Given

**A durian nursery** loses <InlineMath math="10\%" /> of its starting seedlings in month one, transplants <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of the survivors in month two, and sows new seedlings equal to <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of the starting count.

# Asked

The number of seedlings **transplanted to the orchard in month two**.

# Solution

> Express every step as a clean fraction of the original count so you never mix up percentages of the survivors with percentages of the start.

This question uses three percentages, and **each one has a different base**. The <InlineMath math="10\%" /> is taken from the starting number, the <InlineMath math="50\%" /> from the seedlings still alive, and the <InlineMath math="60\%" /> from the starting number again. You only know the final count, <InlineMath math="840" />, so the safest move is to write everything in one unit: the starting number of seedlings.

Let the starting number be <InlineMath math="N" />. Follow the seedlings month by month.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{alive after month one} &= 0.9N \\
\text{moved} &= 0.5 \times 0.9N = 0.45N \\
\text{new seedlings} &= 0.6N \\
\text{final count} &= 0.9N - 0.45N + 0.6N = 1.05N
\end{aligned}" />

The final count is <InlineMath math="840" />, so you can find <InlineMath math="N" /> and then the number moved.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1.05N &= 840 \\
N &= 800 \\
0.45N &= 0.45 \times 800 = 360
\end{aligned}" />

Check by tracing it again: <InlineMath math="800" /> seedlings, <InlineMath math="80" /> die so <InlineMath math="720" /> are left, half of them, <InlineMath math="360" />, are moved so <InlineMath math="360" /> remain, then <InlineMath math="480" /> new ones are added. That gives <InlineMath math="360 + 480 = 840" />, which matches the question.

Each of the other options comes from mixing up a base.

- <InlineMath math="378" /> comes from just adding the percentages: giving a net zero percent change. You then think the starting number was <InlineMath math="840" />, so the number moved is <InlineMath math="378" />.
- <InlineMath math="400" /> comes from taking <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of the starting <InlineMath math="800" />, even though only half of the surviving seedlings were moved.
- <InlineMath math="420" /> comes from taking <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of the final <InlineMath math="840" />.
- <InlineMath math="525" /> comes from treating the new seedlings as <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of what was left after moving. Then the final count is <InlineMath math="1.6" /> times that remainder, so the remainder is <InlineMath math="840 \div 1.6 = 525" />, and since half were moved, the number moved is taken to equal that remainder.

# Answer

So **<InlineMath math="360" />** seedlings were moved to the orchard in the second month.

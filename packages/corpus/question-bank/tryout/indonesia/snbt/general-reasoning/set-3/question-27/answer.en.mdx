Average speed is **total distance divided by total time**, not the average of the two speeds. The outbound and return times differ, so the two speeds do not carry equal weight.

Let the distance from home to the market be <InlineMath math="d" /> km. The travel times are:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t_{\text{out}} &= \frac{d}{14} \\
t_{\text{back}} &= \frac{d}{21} \\
t_{\text{total}} &= \frac{d}{14}+\frac{d}{21}=\frac{3d+2d}{42}=\frac{5d}{42}
\end{aligned}" />

The total distance is <InlineMath math="2d" />, so

<BlockMath math="\bar{v}=\frac{2d}{\frac{5d}{42}}=\frac{84}{5}=16.8\text{ km/h}" />

The <InlineMath math="d" /> cancels, so the result does not depend on the distance. As a check, with <InlineMath math="d=42" /> km the total time is <InlineMath math="3+2=5" /> hours for <InlineMath math="84" /> km, and <InlineMath math="84\div5=16.8" />.

The other options come from these mistakes:

- <InlineMath math="17.5" /> km/h is the plain average of <InlineMath math="14" /> and <InlineMath math="21" />. It is wrong because Raka rides longer at <InlineMath math="14" /> km/h.
- <InlineMath math="8.4" /> km/h comes from dividing the one-way distance by the total time.
- <InlineMath math="21" /> km/h is only the return speed.
- <InlineMath math="35" /> km/h is the sum of the speeds, not an average speed.

So Raka's average speed is <InlineMath math="16.8" /> km/h.

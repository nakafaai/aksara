Six months give only six pairs of temperature and sales. To test the idea, you need **more pairs of temperature and fan sales**, ideally under different conditions, so you can see whether "hot month, high sales" keeps repeating.

Fan sales and temperatures for the same months in earlier years give exactly that kind of data. If hotter years also recorded higher sales, the idea becomes stronger. If not, it weakens.

The other options do not test the link between temperature and fan sales:

- Pool ticket prices relate to pool visitors, not fan sales.
- The number of shop staff says nothing about temperature.
- The best-selling brand gives no information about how temperature affects how many fans are sold.
- The length of the pool closure only explains the pool data for May.

So the most useful extra data are fan sales and average maximum temperatures for the same months in several earlier years.

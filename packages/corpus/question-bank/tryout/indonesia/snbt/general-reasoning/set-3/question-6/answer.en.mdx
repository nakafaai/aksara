Two April-to-June results seem to clash: the number of night-time errors fell from <InlineMath math="18" /> to <InlineMath math="15" />, yet the night-time error rate rose. From January to March, that rate was <InlineMath math="\tfrac{18}{2{,}000}\cdot 1{,}000=9" /> per <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> medications.

Remember that the error rate is a fraction: errors divided by medications given. The top went down, but the fraction went up. That's only possible if **the bottom fell more sharply** than the top. So the right explanation has to cut the number of night-time medications, and cut it by enough.

By how much? Let <InlineMath math="p" /> be the number of night-time medications from April to June. The rate climbs above <InlineMath math="9" /> when:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac{15}{p}\cdot 1{,}000 &> 9 \\ p &< \frac{15{,}000}{9}\approx 1{,}667 \end{aligned}" />

Night-time medications must drop from <InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> to below about <InlineMath math="1{,}667" />, a fall of more than one-sixth, or about <InlineMath math="16.7\%" />. That one-sixth comes from the top of the fraction: errors fell by exactly one-sixth, from <InlineMath math="18" /> to <InlineMath math="15" />, so the bottom has to fall by more than one-sixth for the fraction to rise.

Moving the routine 05.00 medicines to 08.00 cuts night-time medications by about a quarter, roughly from <InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> to <InlineMath math="1{,}500" />. Check it:

<BlockMath math="\frac{15}{1{,}500}\cdot 1{,}000=10>9" />

Both facts hold: errors fall to <InlineMath math="15" /> while the rate rises to about <InlineMath math="10" />. And 08.00 already belongs to the morning shift, so those doses really leave the night-time count.

Here's why the other options fail.

- Fresher nurses explain only the drop in errors. If medications stayed at <InlineMath math="2{,}000" />, the rate would be <InlineMath math="\tfrac{15}{2{,}000}\cdot 1{,}000=7.5" />, a fall. The second fact stays unexplained.
- A more detailed checklist makes more errors get counted, which makes the drop in errors even stranger. As long as medications don't fall, the rate can't rise while the error count falls.
- Medications falling by about <InlineMath math="10\%" /> point the right way but not sharply enough. With about <InlineMath math="1{,}800" /> medications, the rate is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{15}{1{,}800}\cdot 1{,}000\approx 8.3" />, still below <InlineMath math="9" />.
- More medications make the bottom of the fraction bigger, so the rate falls even further. That's the wrong direction.

So the explanation that makes both results true is moving the routine 05.00 medicines to 08.00, which cuts night-time medications by about a quarter.

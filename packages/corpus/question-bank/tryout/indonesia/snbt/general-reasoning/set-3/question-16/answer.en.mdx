The extra condition puts Hamid in chair 7. A statement is *Benar* ("true") only if it holds in **every arrangement that's still valid** after adding that condition. Otherwise it's *Salah*.

First use the structure that holds in every arrangement.

- Rule 1 gives two cases: Asri in chair 1 and Bonar in chair 5, or Bonar in chair 1 and Asri in chair 5.
- Dimas can't sit next to Asri (rule 3), and he can't sit in chair 3 or chair 7 either. If Dimas took one of those, Elok would face him from the other one, and Fajar would need a chair two away from Elok, which means chair 1 or chair 5. Those belong to Asri and Bonar. So Dimas sits next to Bonar.
- Cahya takes Dimas's other neighbouring chair (rule 2), which is chair 3 or chair 7. Elok faces Dimas, so Elok sits next to Asri (rule 4). Fajar is two chairs from Elok (rule 5).

Now put Hamid in chair 7. That chair is taken, so **Cahya must be in chair 3**. Hamid in chair 7 isn't next to Cahya, so rule 7 is safe. Two cases remain.

- Asri in chair 1. Dimas is in chair 4, Bonar in chair 5 and Elok in chair 8. Fajar is two chairs from Elok, so in chair 2 or chair 6, and Galuh takes whichever is left. Galuh's number stays below Hamid's either way, so both ways are valid.
- Asri in chair 5. Bonar is in chair 1, Dimas in chair 2 and Elok in chair 6. Fajar is in chair 4 or chair 8. The empty chairs left are 4 and 8, and Galuh needs a smaller number than Hamid in chair 7. So Galuh takes chair 4 and Fajar takes chair 8.

That gives three arrangements. Columns 1 to 8 are chair numbers, and each person is written by the first letter of their name.

| II | A | G | C | D | B | F | H | E |
| III | B | D | C | G | A | E | H | F |

Now judge each statement.

- Cahya in chair 3 is *Benar*. Cahya sits in chair 3 in all three arrangements, because Hamid already has chair 7.
- Asri in chair 1 is *Salah*. It's true in arrangements I and II, but arrangement III puts Asri in chair 5. The case with Bonar in chair 1 is easy to miss, because there Galuh has to move to chair 4 to stay below Hamid.
- Fajar next to Asri is *Salah*. Only arrangement I puts Fajar next to Asri. In arrangement II Fajar is in chair 6, and in arrangement III Fajar is in chair 8 while Asri is in chair 5.
- Galuh facing Fajar is *Benar*. Their chair pairs are 6 and 2, 2 and 6, and 4 and 8. Each pair differs by <InlineMath math="4" />, so they always face each other.

So the statements about Cahya in chair 3 and about Galuh facing Fajar are *Benar*, while the statements about Asri in chair 1 and about Fajar sitting next to Asri are *Salah*.

First, find the flaw in the chili-sauce argument. The premise is about the parts: every ingredient is the tastiest of its kind. The conclusion is about the whole: the sauce is the tastiest in the village. The argument assumes **whatever is true of each part must be true of the whole**. But how good a sauce tastes also depends on the amounts, the grinding, and how the flavors go together. The best chilies and the best shrimp paste can still make a sauce that's far too salty if the amounts are off. This mistake is called the fallacy of composition.

So look for an argument with both of these features:

1. it runs from the parts to the whole, and
2. the property it moves doesn't automatically carry over to the whole, because the whole depends on how the parts combine.

The band argument has both. Every musician is the best in town on their instrument, so the band is supposed to be the best in town. A band's quality also depends on how tight it plays, its arrangements, and whether the playing styles fit, so the best players don't necessarily make the best band. The pattern matches the chili sauce exactly.

Here's why the other options fail.

- The box argument also runs from parts to whole, but it lacks the second feature. Weight adds up: if every box is under <InlineMath math="20" /> kilograms, <InlineMath math="30" /> boxes must weigh under <InlineMath math="30\cdot 20=600" /> kilograms. This argument is valid, so it has no flaw.
- The vocational-school argument runs the other way: from the whole to each part. The school having the highest average doesn't mean every student has a top score. That's the fallacy of division, the mirror image of composition.
- The futsal argument generalizes from one player to all players. Its flaw is drawing a general rule from one exceptional case, not moving a property from the parts to the whole.
- The village argument compares two different villages. Its flaw is a weak analogy, because the neighboring village may differ in other ways that affect cleanliness.

So the argument whose flaw matches best is the one claiming the band is the best in town because each musician is the best on their instrument.

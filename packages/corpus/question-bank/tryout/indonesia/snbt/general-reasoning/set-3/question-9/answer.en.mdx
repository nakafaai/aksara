#### Tracing the cause-and-effect chain

The passage describes this sequence:

1. Illegal tin mining produces sediment and disrupts river channels.
2. Sedimentation and disrupted channels reduce the rivers' capacity.
3. Lower capacity makes flooding more likely when rainfall raises river flow.

The second choice preserves every link in that sequence. It does not claim that mining is the only possible cause of flooding, nor that flooding must occur every time it rains. It states only that the damage **can increase the risk**, which matches the passage.

#### Rejecting the other choices

- The first reverses the passage by claiming that stopping illegal mining causes damage.
- The third says flooding cannot occur, contradicting the stated risk.
- The fourth denies the threat to communities.
- The fifth claims sediment improves river flow, the opposite of the passage.

Therefore, the best-supported conclusion is: **Mining-related sedimentation and disrupted channels can reduce river capacity and increase rainy-season flood risk.**

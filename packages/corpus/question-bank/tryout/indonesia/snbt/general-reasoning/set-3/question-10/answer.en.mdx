All values refer to the **same portion size**, <InlineMath math="100\,\text{g}" /> of edible fruit. Compare the same nutrient, because protein and fat are given in grams, whereas iron and calcium are in milligrams.

#### Calculate the two combined amounts

For calcium, add pomelo to avocado, then ambarella to jackfruit:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}C_{\text{pomelo+avocado}}&amp;=23+10=33\,\text{mg}\\C_{\text{ambarella+jackfruit}}&amp;=15+20=35\,\text{mg}\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="33<35" />, the choice stating a **lower total calcium content** is correct.

For protein, add the amounts for the pairs specified in the choice:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}P_{\text{pomelo+jackfruit}}&amp;=0.6+1.2=1.8\,\text{g}\\P_{\text{avocado+ambarella}}&amp;=0.9+1.0=1.9\,\text{g}\end{aligned}" />

The protein total for pomelo and jackfruit is lower, rather than higher, so the protein claim is incorrect.

#### Check the claims about every nutrient

A claim about **every nutrient** fails if even one column contradicts it. The table shows counterexamples to the other three choices.

| Claim | Counterexample from the table |
| --- | --- |
| Every jackfruit value exceeds pomelo’s | Jackfruit calcium is <InlineMath math="20<23" /> mg |
| Every avocado value exceeds pomelo’s | Avocado calcium is <InlineMath math="10<23" /> mg |
| Jackfruit has the highest value for every nutrient | Avocado fat is <InlineMath math="6.5>0.3" /> g, ambarella iron is <InlineMath math="2.8>0.9" /> mg, and pomelo calcium is <InlineMath math="23>20" /> mg |

Only the comparison of total calcium content agrees with the data.

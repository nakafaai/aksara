The catch is that another change started in the same July: reminder messages sent to members' phones. The drop could come from the fines being dropped, from the reminders, or from both.

To rule out the reminders, you need a group that **had its fines dropped but never got reminders**. If that group's share of late loans still fell, the reminders can't explain the drop.

Members who never registered a phone number are exactly that group. Their fines were dropped too, but no reminder ever reached them. Their share fell from <InlineMath math="10\%" /> to <InlineMath math="5\%" />, cut in half, much like the overall drop from <InlineMath math="9\%" /> to <InlineMath math="4\%" />. So the drop happens even without reminders, which leaves dropping the fines as the explanation still standing.

Here's why the other options fail.

- Reminder recipients falling from <InlineMath math="9\%" /> to <InlineMath math="3\%" /> had both changes at once. You can't split their drop into a fine part and a reminder part.
- A neighboring library that changed nothing only rules out a seasonal or regional trend. The reminders are still in the running.
- Another library that also dropped fines and started reminders repeats the same mix. A similar result there still doesn't separate fines from reminders.
- The debtors' interviews give a reason why fines could matter, but they don't show that reminders didn't matter. This supports the fine story without knocking out its rival.

So the statement that most strengthens the head librarian's conclusion is that late loans also fell from <InlineMath math="10\%" /> to <InlineMath math="5\%" /> among members who never received a reminder.

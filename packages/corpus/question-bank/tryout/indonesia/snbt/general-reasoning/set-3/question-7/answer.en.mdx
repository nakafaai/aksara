This is a reading-comprehension question. We do not need to decide whether the historical assessment is legally correct today. We only compare each choice with the claims made in the passage.

The passage makes four relevant claims:

1. Several provisions were more lenient than the Corruption Law.
2. The lighter provisions were expected to make corruption more widespread.
3. They were expected to weaken deterrence.
4. Lower minimum fines were expected to make recovery of state losses more difficult.

Four choices repeat one or more of those claims. The choice predicting a decrease in corruption begins with the supported statement that some provisions were more lenient, but then says corruption would **decrease**.

That conclusion reverses the direction stated in the passage:

- **passage: more widespread**
- **choice: decrease**

Therefore, the conclusion least consistent with the passage is: **Some provisions are more lenient than the Corruption Law, and corruption in Indonesia will decrease.**

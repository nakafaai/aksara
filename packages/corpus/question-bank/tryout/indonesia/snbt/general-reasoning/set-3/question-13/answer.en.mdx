This question asks for the negation (*ingkaran*), the statement that's true exactly when the original is false. The original is an if-then statement, and its "then" part joins two things with *dan* ("and"). Both the "if" part and the "then" part also contain quantifiers: *tidak ada* ("no") and *semua* ("all").

Give each part a name so the shape is easy to see.

- <InlineMath math="p" />: no volunteer is absent from the morning roll call.
- <InlineMath math="q" />: the post opens on time.
- <InlineMath math="r" />: all volunteers get a lunch ration.

The original is <InlineMath math="p \Rightarrow (q \wedge r)" />. An if-then statement is false in only one situation: the "if" part happens, but the "then" part doesn't. So **the "if" part stays exactly as it is**, including its *tidak ada*, and only the "then" part gets negated.

<BlockMath math="\neg\big(p \Rightarrow (q \wedge r)\big) \equiv p \wedge \neg(q \wedge r) \equiv p \wedge (\neg q \vee \neg r)" />

The second step uses De Morgan's law: the negation of "<InlineMath math="q" /> and <InlineMath math="r" />" is "not <InlineMath math="q" /> or not <InlineMath math="r" />". Now translate each part back.

- <InlineMath math="\neg q" />: the post doesn't open on time.
- <InlineMath math="\neg r" />: the negation of "all volunteers get a lunch ration" (*semua relawan mendapat jatah makan siang*) is "some volunteer doesn't get a lunch ration" (*ada relawan yang tidak mendapat jatah makan siang*). To refute "all", one volunteer who misses out is enough.

The result: no volunteer is absent from the morning roll call, but the post doesn't open on time or some volunteer doesn't get a lunch ration. Check the meaning: the original really is broken when everyone has shown up but one of its two promises fails.

Each other option misses at one point.

- The version starting with "some volunteer is absent" (*ada relawan yang absen*) also negates the "if" part. But when someone is absent, the original isn't broken at all.
- The version with "doesn't open on time and some volunteer doesn't get a ration" forgets that De Morgan's law turns "and" into "or". It only covers the case where both promises fail together, yet one failure is already enough.
- The version with "all volunteers don't get a ration" (*semua relawan tidak mendapat jatah*) negates the quantifier wrongly. That sentence means no volunteer gets a ration at all, which is far stronger than the negation, which only needs one volunteer.
- The version that still has the if-then form just negates both parts. The negation of an if-then statement isn't an if-then statement anymore. It's an "and" statement.

So the negation is "No volunteer is absent from the morning roll call, but the post doesn't open on time or some volunteer doesn't get a lunch ration" (*Tidak ada relawan yang absen pada apel pagi, tetapi posko tidak dibuka tepat waktu atau ada relawan yang tidak mendapat jatah makan siang*).

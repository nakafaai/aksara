An implication <InlineMath math="p \Rightarrow q" /> is false only when <InlineMath math="p" /> is true and <InlineMath math="q" /> is false. So **the negation of an implication is "p and not q"**:

<BlockMath math="\neg(p \Rightarrow q) \equiv p \wedge \neg q" />

Here <InlineMath math="p" /> is "today is a holiday" and <InlineMath math="q" /> is "all shops in the market are closed". The negation of <InlineMath math="q" /> is not "all shops are open" but **"some shop is not closed"**, because one shop staying open is enough to refute "all shops are closed".

The result: today is a holiday and some shop in the market is not closed.

The other options are wrong:

- "Today is not a holiday and all shops are open" wrongly negates both <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" />.
- "If today is not a holiday, some shop is open" is still an implication, not a negation.
- "Today is a holiday and all shops are open" is too strong. The negation needs only one shop that is not closed.
- "Today is not a holiday or all shops are closed" is another form of the original statement, <InlineMath math="\neg p \vee q" />, so it is equivalent, not the negation.

So the negation is "Today is a holiday and some shop in the market is not closed".

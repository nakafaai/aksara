The passage distinguishes **effects still being studied** from benefits supported strongly enough to promise. Compare the strength of each claim with the limits of the evidence described.

| Claim in a choice | Evidence limit in the passage |
| --- | --- |
| Drinking green tea eliminates acne | Clinical evidence for acne is limited or conflicting |
| Every form of UV-related skin damage is prevented | The positive findings mentioned concern redness under limited UV exposure |
| Green tea is proven to replace established sun protection | The passage provides no evidence that drinking green tea can replace established sun protection |
| Concentrated supplements are safe for everyone because they are herbal | The passage provides no evidence of safety for everyone |

The word **every** extends limited findings into a universal guarantee. Likewise, a plant origin does not establish a supplement’s safety.

The correct choice preserves the evidence limits: oral green tea is being studied for possible skin effects, but the reported findings do not justify promising acne treatment or prevention of photoaging. This conclusion acknowledges the research without turning it into certainty.

Of the four possible arrangements, only **arrangement IV** puts Q on the middle shelf:

| Arrangement | Top shelf | Middle shelf | Bottom shelf |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IV | M, P | N, O, Q | K, L |

You can also derive it directly. If Q is in the middle, P must be on top. The middle shelf already holds O and Q, so only one place is left there and the pair K and L does not fit. K and L cannot go on top either, because only one top place remains after P. So **K and L are at the bottom**. The last top place and the last middle place go to M and N, and since N cannot be on top, M is on top and N in the middle.

In this arrangement, K is on the bottom shelf.

The other options are false in arrangement IV:

- M is on the top shelf, not the middle.
- N is on the middle shelf, not the bottom.
- P is on the top shelf, not the middle.
- L is on the bottom shelf with K, not on top.

So if Q is on the middle shelf, K must be on the bottom shelf.

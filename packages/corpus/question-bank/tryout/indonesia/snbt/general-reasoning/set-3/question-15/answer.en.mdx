The passage distinguishes a **possible effect being studied** from a treatment
that has been **proved** to work.

#### Check the strength of each claim

- The passage does not say that drinking green tea eliminates acne. It says the
  clinical evidence for acne is limited.
- It does not say that every type of UV damage is prevented. The positive
  findings concerned a limited outcome under limited exposure, and other
  results were conflicting.
- It explicitly rejects replacing established sun protection with green tea.
- It gives no basis for calling concentrated supplements universally safe.
  "Herbal" is not evidence of safety.

The supported conclusion is the cautious one: oral green tea is being studied,
but the current evidence does not support a treatment or prevention guarantee.

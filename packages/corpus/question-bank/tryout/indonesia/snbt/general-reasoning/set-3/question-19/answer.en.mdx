We can solve this problem by comparing the performance of the three tools.

#### Determining Performance Ratios

The cutting capability relationships are given as:
1.  <InlineMath math="P = 3Q \rightarrow Q = \frac{1}{3}P" />
2.  <InlineMath math="R = \frac{1}{2}P" />

From these relationships, we can establish a capability (speed) ratio between <InlineMath math="P" />, <InlineMath math="Q" />, and <InlineMath math="R" />. Let <InlineMath math="P = 6" />, then:
-   <InlineMath math="Q = \frac{1}{3}(6) = 2" />
-   <InlineMath math="R = \frac{1}{2}(6) = 3" />

Thus, the speed ratio is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="P : Q : R = 6 : 2 : 3" />
</MathContainer>

#### Calculating Area Cut by Tool R in Three Hours

It is known that tool <InlineMath math="R" /> cuts <InlineMath math="200\text{ m}^2" /> in <InlineMath math="4\text{ hours}" />. We calculate the area <InlineMath math="R" /> can cut in <InlineMath math="3\text{ hours}" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Area}_R(3\text{ hours}) = \frac{3}{4} \times 200 = 150\text{ m}^2" />
</MathContainer>

#### Calculating Area Cut by Tool Q

The speed ratio is <InlineMath math="Q : R = 2 : 3" />. In the same amount of time, the areas cut have the same ratio.

Thus, the area cut by tool <InlineMath math="Q" /> is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Area}_Q = \frac{2}{3} \times \text{Area}_R" />
  <BlockMath math="\text{Area}_Q = \frac{2}{3} \times 150 = 100\text{ m}^2" />
</MathContainer>

So, in <InlineMath math="3\text{ hours}" />, tool <InlineMath math="Q" /> can cut a grass area of **<InlineMath math="100\text{ m}^2" />**.

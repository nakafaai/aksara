This question asks for the conclusion that's **always true whenever all three premises are true**. *Sebagian* ("some") means at least one, and *tidak ada* ("no") means zero.

The method is to follow one person whose existence a premise guarantees, then see what must hold for that person.

1. Premise 3 guarantees at least one person who's in the journalism club and also in the choir. Call that person P.
2. Premise 2 says no grade XI student is in the choir. P is in the choir, so P isn't a grade XI student.
3. Premise 1 says every debate finalist is a grade XI student. Its contrapositive, the equivalent form that swaps both parts and negates them, says: anyone who isn't a grade XI student isn't a finalist. So P isn't a finalist.

P is in the journalism club and isn't a finalist. That's why "some journalism club members aren't debate finalists" must be true.

The other conclusions aren't guaranteed. To show that, it's enough to give a situation where all three premises hold but the conclusion is false. The first situation has just two people, P and Q.

| Person | Finalist | Grade XI | Choir | Journalism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P | no | no | yes | yes |
| Q | yes | yes | no | yes |

All three premises hold: the only finalist, Q, is in grade XI, that grade XI student isn't in the choir, and P is in both journalism and the choir.

- "Some debate finalists aren't journalism club members" is false here, because the only finalist, Q, is in journalism. This option just swaps the subject and predicate of the valid conclusion, but a "some A aren't B" statement can't be turned around.
- "No journalism club member is a debate finalist" is false here too, because Q is in journalism and a finalist. This option stretches "some" into "all".

The second situation has three people, P, R and S.

| Person | Finalist | Grade XI | Choir | Journalism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P | no | no | yes | yes |
| R | yes | yes | no | no |
| S | no | no | yes | no |

All three premises hold again: finalist R is in grade XI, R isn't in the choir, and P is in both journalism and the choir.

- "Some journalism club members are grade XI students" is false here, because the only journalism member, P, isn't in grade XI. This option comes from assuming that "some aren't" automatically means "the rest are".
- "All choir members are journalism club members" is false here, because S is in the choir but not in journalism. This option reverses premise 3 and turns "some" into "all" at the same time.

So the conclusion that must be true is that some journalism club members aren't debate finalists.

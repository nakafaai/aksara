A good explanation must fit **two facts that seem to pull in opposite directions**: on sections with dense hedges, the sidewalk stays cleaner while the traffic lane gets dirtier.

A hedge that acts like a wall blocks the airflow from the traffic lane to the sidewalk. Exhaust particles stay on the road side, so the level in the traffic lane rises while the sidewalk behind the hedge is shielded. The explanation about **blocked air** accounts for both facts at once.

The other options do not fit:

- The number of pedestrians does not add particles to the traffic lane.
- If the plants released much PM2.5, the sidewalk next to the hedge would also be dirtier, yet it stays cleaner.
- Measuring more at night would affect the sidewalk and the traffic lane together, so it cannot explain why only the traffic lane is higher.
- Being farther from the city center tends to lower pollution, and the passage says traffic was similar on all sections.

So the best explanation is that very dense hedges block the movement of air, so exhaust builds up in the traffic lane.

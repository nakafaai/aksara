The extension officer claims that shade trees raise coffee yields. The evidence sits in the table: on the same slope, shaded farms always lead by <InlineMath math="100" /> kilograms per hectare, <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> against <InlineMath math="900" /> on the gentle slope and <InlineMath math="700" /> against <InlineMath math="600" /> on the steep slope. Because each comparison is made within one slope, the slope can no longer explain that lead.

A cause-and-effect claim is weakest when **another cause explains the whole lead**. That other cause has to meet three conditions:

1. it differs between shaded and unshaded farms,
2. it pushes the yield of the shaded farms up, and
3. it's present on both slopes, because the <InlineMath math="100" />-kilogram lead shows up on both slopes.

The statement about pruning meets all three. On both slopes, almost every shaded farm is pruned regularly, almost no unshaded farm is, and regular pruning raises yields. The shaded farms' lead could come entirely from pruning rather than from the shade trees. That cuts the causal link from shade to yield.

Here's why the other options fail.

- Older coffee plants on shaded farms fails condition 2. Older plants bear less fruit, so shaded farms start with a handicap. If they still lead, shade looks even stronger. This statement supports the officer.
- Different fertilizing only on the steep slope fails condition 3. It could explain the lead on the steep slope, but on the gentle slope fertilizing is equal and shaded farms still lead by <InlineMath math="100" /> kilograms. Part of the officer's evidence stays untouched.
- Heavy rain on the steep slope fails condition 1. The rain falls on shaded and unshaded steep-slope farms alike, so it only explains why the steep slope trails the gentle one, not why shaded farms lead on the same slope.
- The lower overall average is the cooperative official's argument. The numbers are <InlineMath math="\tfrac{10\cdot 1{,}000+40\cdot 700}{50}=760" /> against <InlineMath math="\tfrac{40\cdot 900+10\cdot 600}{50}=840" />, and that gap appears because <InlineMath math="40" /> of the <InlineMath math="50" /> shaded farms are on the steep slope. The officer's evidence compares farms on the same slope, so this statement doesn't touch it.

So the statement that most weakens the officer's view is that on both slopes almost every shaded farm is pruned regularly while unshaded farms almost never are.

# Given

The cafeteria table lists **daily cups sold for three drinks** along with each drink price.

# Asked

The **day with the highest total drink revenue**.

# Solution

> Selling the most cups does not guarantee the biggest intake when another day sells a richer mix of higher-priced drinks.

Revenue depends on **cups and prices**, so the day with the most cups need not have the most revenue. Work out each day's revenue divided by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> rupiah:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Monday} &= 40\times9+25\times7+30\times6=360+175+180=715 \\
\text{Tuesday} &= 35\times9+30\times7+35\times6=315+210+210=735 \\
\text{Wednesday} &= 50\times9+20\times7+25\times6=450+140+150=740 \\
\text{Thursday} &= 30\times9+40\times7+30\times6=270+280+180=730 \\
\text{Friday} &= 45\times9+35\times7+20\times6=405+245+120=770
\end{aligned}" />

The chart below draws each day's revenue. **Friday's bar is the tallest**, even though it sold as many cups as Tuesday and Thursday.

# Answer

Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday all sold **<InlineMath math="100" />** cups, but Friday sold more of the expensive drinks. So the highest revenue came on Friday (*Jumat*), **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}770{,}000" />**.

Let <InlineMath math="C" /> and <InlineMath math="V" /> be the masses of chocolate and vanilla powder, in grams, for <InlineMath math="10" /> cakes. Using the ratio <InlineMath math="3:2" /> and <InlineMath math="V=30" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{C}{V}=\frac{3}{2}" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\frac{C}{30}&amp;=\frac{3}{2}\\C&amp;=\frac{3\times30}{2}\\&amp;=45\end{aligned}" />

The cake size and recipe stay the same. Making <InlineMath math="50" /> cakes requires <InlineMath math="50/10=5" /> times as much of each ingredient. Let the required chocolate mass be <InlineMath math="C_{50}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}C_{50}&amp;=45\times5\\&amp;=225\end{aligned}" />

Therefore, <InlineMath math="50" /> cakes require **<InlineMath math="225" /> grams of chocolate powder**.

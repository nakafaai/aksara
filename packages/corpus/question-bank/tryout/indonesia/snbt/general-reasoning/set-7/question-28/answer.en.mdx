The positions of A and B must differ by <InlineMath math="3" /> to leave exactly two reports between them. The possible position pairs are:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(1,4),\ (2,5)" />
  <BlockMath math="(3,6),\ (4,7)" />
</MathContainer>

There are four position pairs, two orders for A and B, and <InlineMath math="5!" /> orders of the other five reports. The number satisfying the A-B spacing is therefore:

<BlockMath math="4\times2\times5!=960" />

Now count the forbidden arrangements with E and F adjacent. Keep the A and B positions occupied, without closing the gaps. The number of available pairs of consecutive original positions is:

| A and B positions | Available pairs |
| :--- | ---: |
| <InlineMath math="(1,4)" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(2,5)" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(3,6)" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(4,7)" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |

Across the four cases there are <InlineMath math="3+2+2+3=10" /> available pairs. E and F have two orders in each pair, and the other three reports have <InlineMath math="3!" /> orders. Including the two orders for A and B:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}2\times10\times2\times3!&=240\end{aligned}" />

Removing the forbidden arrangements leaves <InlineMath math="960-240=720" />. Swapping C and D preserves every other condition, so exactly half put C before D:

<BlockMath math="\frac{720}{2}=360" />

All <InlineMath math="7!" /> arrangements are equally likely. The required probability is:

<BlockMath math="\frac{360}{7!}=\frac{360}{5040}=\frac1{14}" />

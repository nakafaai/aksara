# Given

Next week revenue must reach at least **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}4{,}000{,}000" />**, while **milk coffee and chocolate sales stay the same** as in the table.

# Asked

The **minimum number of lemon cups** that must be sold next week.

# Solution

> Subtract the locked-in revenue from the target, divide the remaining gap by one lemon drink price, and round up to a whole cup.

First find the revenue **already fixed** from coffee milk and chocolate divided by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> rupiah. Coffee milk sold <InlineMath math="200" /> cups and chocolate <InlineMath math="150" /> cups.

<BlockMath math="200\times9+150\times7=1{,}800+1{,}050=2{,}850" />

The rest of the target must come from lemon: <InlineMath math="4{,}000-2{,}850=1{,}150" /> thousand rupiah. If <InlineMath math="n" /> cups of lemon are sold:

<BlockMath math="6n\ge1{,}150\quad\Rightarrow\quad n\ge191.67" />

Cups are whole numbers, so **round up** to <InlineMath math="192" />. Check: <InlineMath math="191" /> cups give <InlineMath math="2{,}850+1{,}146=3{,}996" /> thousand, still short, while <InlineMath math="192" /> cups give <InlineMath math="2{,}850+1{,}152=4{,}002" /> thousand.

# Answer

So at least **<InlineMath math="192" />** cups of lemon must be sold.

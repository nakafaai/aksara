# Given

The table tracks **monthly cat and dog clinic visits**, showing cat visits spiking in March and April while dog visits stayed steady.

# Asked

The conclusion **most strongly supported** by the text and table.

# Solution

> When cat visits jump sharply while dog visits stay flat, the cat-only free vaccine program stands out as the clear driver.

Compare the two columns. Cat visits were about <InlineMath math="115" /> to <InlineMath math="120" /> before the program, then **jumped** to <InlineMath math="310" /> in March and <InlineMath math="280" /> in April. Dog visits, whose service did not change, stayed around <InlineMath math="80" /> to <InlineMath math="90" />. So the jump fits the cat vaccine program best.

The chart below draws both columns of the table. **Only the cat line jumps in March and April**, while the dog line stays flat.

The other options are not supported:

- Dog visits in March and April, <InlineMath math="82" /> and <InlineMath math="88" />, did not fall compared with other months.
- Cat visits in May and June, <InlineMath math="140" /> and <InlineMath math="135" />, are still above January and February, so they did not return exactly.
- The table records visits to one clinic, not the number of cats in the city.
- Dog visits were still recorded in March and April, so the service was not closed.

# Answer

So the best supported conclusion is that **the free vaccine program increased cat visits, especially in March and April**.

# Given

All five rules apply with **only Ayu, Citra, or Dani able to occupy the center post**.

# Asked

The **number of distinct valid guard assignments**.

# Solution

> Split the count cleanly into three cases by who stands at the center post so every legal assignment is counted once.

Split the cases by **who takes the middle post**. Bima cannot be in the middle (rule 4), and Eko cannot be either (Eko in the middle forces Ayu and Bima together). So the middle post goes to Ayu, Citra, or Dani.

**Ayu in the middle**. As in the previous question, there is only one assignment: Bima and Eko north, Citra and Dani south. That is **<InlineMath math="1" />** assignment.

**Citra in the middle**. Ayu and Bima must be apart, so one is north and one is south. Dani and Eko fill the remaining place in the north and the remaining place in the south. If Dani is north, Eko must be south (rule 3), which is allowed. If Dani is south, Eko is north, also allowed. That is <InlineMath math="2\times2=4" /> assignments.

**Dani in the middle**. Citra must go south, so Eko goes north. Ayu and Bima are apart, one north with Eko and the other south with Citra. That is **<InlineMath math="2" />** assignments.

The chart below counts the valid assignments by who takes the middle post. **Bima and Eko never take the middle post**, and the other three bars add up to <InlineMath math="7" />.

# Answer

The total is <InlineMath math="7" /> assignments.

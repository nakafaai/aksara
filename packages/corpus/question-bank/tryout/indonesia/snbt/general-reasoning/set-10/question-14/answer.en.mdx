# Given

Booth rules place **six Grade X and Grade XI booths in spots <InlineMath math="1" /> through <InlineMath math="6" />** with adjacency restrictions.

# Asked

The booth-placement statement that is **certainly true** in every valid layout.

# Solution

> Check which classes can legally occupy booth six until every other candidate hits a rule conflict.

First find which class **can take booth 6**, then cross out those that break a rule.

- X-C cannot, since XI-A must be directly to its right (rule 5).
- X-A cannot, since XI-C must have a higher number than X-A (rule 6).
- XI-C cannot, since it may not be at an end (rule 4).
- XI-A cannot. If XI-A is at <InlineMath math="6" />, X-C is at <InlineMath math="5" />, but X-B must have a higher number than X-C (rule 3).
- XI-B cannot. If XI-B is at <InlineMath math="6" />, X-A is at <InlineMath math="5" /> (rule 2), but XI-C must have a higher number than X-A (rule 6).

The diagram below sums up why each candidate for booth 6 drops out. **Only X-B survives**.

Only X-B is left. The other options hold in some arrangements only. For example, X-C, XI-A, X-A, XI-B, XI-C, X-B meets every rule but puts X-A at <InlineMath math="3" /> and XI-B at <InlineMath math="4" />.

# Answer

So the statement that must be true is that **X-B is at booth 6**.

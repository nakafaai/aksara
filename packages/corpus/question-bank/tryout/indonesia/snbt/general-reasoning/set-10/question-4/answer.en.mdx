# Given

The table tracks **visitors and books borrowed across four villages** in two different months.

# Asked

The statement that **best matches** the combined data across all four villages.

# Solution

> Sum total visitors and total borrowed books across all four villages first, then divide to compare average books per visitor.

First add up **the totals of all four villages**, then compare the averages.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{January} &: \frac{242\text{ books}}{150\text{ visitors}}\approx1.61 \\
\text{June} &: \frac{280\text{ books}}{220\text{ visitors}}\approx1.27
\end{aligned}" />

The chart below draws books per visitor in each village and combined. **The June bar is shorter in every village**, so the combined average falls even though visitors rise.

  ]}
  valueLabel="Books per visitor"
/>

Visitors rose from <InlineMath math="150" /> to <InlineMath math="220" />, while books per visitor fell.

The other options do not fit:

- In Mekarsari, loans fell from <InlineMath math="90" /> to <InlineMath math="85" />.
- In June, Sukamaju had the most visitors (<InlineMath math="64" />), but Mekarsari had the most loans (<InlineMath math="85" />).
- The biggest visitor rise was in Pasirjaya (<InlineMath math="26" />), not Cibeber (<InlineMath math="20" />).
- Loans rose from <InlineMath math="242" /> to <InlineMath math="280" />, about <InlineMath math="16\%" />.

# Answer

So the best fitting statement is that **visitors increased, but the average number of books borrowed per visitor fell**.

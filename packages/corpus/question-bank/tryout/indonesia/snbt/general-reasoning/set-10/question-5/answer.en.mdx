The key word is **can**. Exposure creates a route by which Nia might become infected, but the passage explicitly says that illness is not guaranteed.

Two outcomes remain compatible with the information:

- Nia becomes infected after the exposure.
- Nia does not become infected after the exposure.

The conclusion that Nia will not catch influenza is therefore **possibly true**. The evidence does not make it certain. Calling it definitely false would make the same mistake in the opposite direction by treating a risk as a certainty.

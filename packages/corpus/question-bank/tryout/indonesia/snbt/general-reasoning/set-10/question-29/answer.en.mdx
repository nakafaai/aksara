# Given

From the male students and **<InlineMath math="4" /> female students**, a **<InlineMath math="3" />-member team** must include **at least <InlineMath math="1" /> female student**.

# Asked

The **number of valid ways to form the team**.

# Solution

> Count every possible three-person team first and simply subtract the all-male teams instead of adding three separate cases.

It is easier to count **all teams, then subtract the teams with no girls**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{all teams} &= \binom{9}{3}=84 \\
\text{teams with no girls (all boys)} &= \binom{5}{3}=10 \\
\text{teams with at least 1 girl} &= 84-10=74
\end{aligned}" />

The chart below puts the three counts side by side. **Teams with no girls are only a small part of all teams**, so <InlineMath math="74" /> are left.

Order does not matter, since a team is being chosen.

# Answer

So there are **<InlineMath math="74" />** ways to choose the team.

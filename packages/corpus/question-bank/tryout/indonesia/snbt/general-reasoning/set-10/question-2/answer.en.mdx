# Given

There is a claim that **renovating Cendana Market also drew residents who previously shopped at neither market**.

# Asked

The statement that **most strengthens** the claim of brand-new market shoppers.

# Solution

> Showing that some new visitors previously shopped only at convenience stores proves they did not merely switch from Kenari Market.

This guess needs evidence that some visitors **did not shop at either market before**. Visitors who always used to shop at a minimarket are new to the markets, not shoppers who only moved over from Kenari. That fact supports the guess directly.

The diagram below separates two sources of Cendana's new visitors. **Only the path from the minimarket adds new shoppers to the two markets**.

  description={<>Some of Cendana's new visitors may have moved over from Pasar Kenari, which adds no shoppers to the two markets. Visitors who always used to shop at a minimarket are new shoppers for both markets.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR
    K["Used to shop at Pasar Kenari"] -->|"just switched markets"| C["Cendana's new visitors"]
    M["Always used to shop at a minimarket"] -->|"new to both markets"| C`}
/>

The other options do not strengthen it:

- Roadworks on the way to Kenari give shoppers another reason to switch markets, so they weaken the guess.
- Rising prices tend to make people buy less, not bring in new shoppers.
- Data from other cities do not show what happened in Lembayung.
- Equal weather only makes the two counts comparable, it does not show new shoppers.

# Answer

So the strongest support is that **some new Cendana visitors always used to shop at a minimarket**.

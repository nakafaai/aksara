# Given

A rule chain links **winning the rotating trophy to choosing the field trip destination**, and **Class XI-2 may not choose the destination**.

# Asked

Whether each of the four statements about Class XI-2 is **True** (certainly true) or **False**.

# Solution

> Walk backward with contrapositives from Class XI-2 losing the choice so every forced fact stands out cleanly.

Mark each statement Benar (true, it must follow) or Salah (false, it need not follow). Join the facts into one chain, then use **contraposition**.

<BlockMath math="\text{cleanliness winner}\Rightarrow\text{trophy}\Rightarrow\text{may choose the trip}" />

The diagram below draws that chain together with the wall-magazine branch. **The trophy can come by two roads**, and Class XI-2, which may not choose the trip, holds it by neither.

<Mermaid
  title={<>The chain of the rotating trophy and the study trip</>}
  description={<>The cleanliness winner and the wall-magazine winner both get the rotating trophy, and a trophy holder may choose the trip. Class XI-2 may not choose, so it doesn't hold the trophy and didn't win the cleanliness contest.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR
    K["Cleanliness winner"] --> P["Gets the rotating trophy"]
    M["Wall-magazine winner"] --> P
    P --> T["May choose the trip"]
    X["Class XI-2 may not choose"] -.->|"contrapositive"| N["No trophy, not the cleanliness winner"]`}
/>

**Statement 1** is false. Class XI-2 may not choose the trip, so by the third fact it certainly does not hold the trophy.

**Statement 2** is true. Without the trophy, Class XI-2 certainly did not win the cleanliness contest.

**Statement 3** is true. This is the chain above: a cleanliness winner gets the trophy, and a trophy holder may choose the trip.

**Statement 4** is false. The chain runs one way only. The second fact says the wall magazine winner also gets the trophy, so a class that may choose the trip need not be the cleanliness winner.

# Answer

So statements 2 and 3 are true, while statements 1 and 4 are false.

The conclusion holds only if **no other cause** is more plausible. A shorter, easier test can raise scores even if reading skill has not changed. So the rise may not come from the reading program.

The other options do not weaken it:

- The kind of book chosen offers no other cause for the rise.
- Where the reading happens, in class or in a hall, offers no other cause.
- The start time only describes how the program runs.
- An unchanged math score shows the rise happened only in reading, which tends to support the conclusion.

So the strongest weakener is that the reading test was replaced by a shorter, easier one.

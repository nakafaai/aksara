# Given

Three premises state **all chess club members entered tournaments, all tournament players are at least <InlineMath math="12" /> years old, and some Grade VII students belong to the chess club**.

# Asked

The conclusion that is **certainly true** from those three premises.

# Solution

> Follow the Grade VII chess club members step by step through both all-rules straight to the age-twelve guarantee.

Follow the Grade VII students who are club members through **the chain of premises**.

- By premise 3, some Grade VII students are chess club members.
- By premise 1, those students have played in a tournament.
- By premise 2, those students are at least <InlineMath math="12" /> years old.

The diagram below follows the Grade VII students who are club members. **Each arrow uses one premise**, ending at an age of at least <InlineMath math="12" />.

  title={<>Following Grade VII chess club members through the three premises</>}
  description={<>Some Grade VII students are chess club members. Every club member has played in a tournament, and everyone who has played in a tournament is at least <InlineMath math="12" /> years old.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR
    S["Grade VII students"] -.->|"some, premise 3"| K["Chess club members"]
    K -->|"premise 1"| T["Have played in a tournament"]
    T -->|"premise 2"| U["At least 12 years old"]`}

So at least some Grade VII students are at least <InlineMath math="12" /> years old.

The other options are not certain:

- Premise 3 covers only some Grade VII students, not all.
- Premise 1 says every member has played in a tournament, so the third option contradicts it.
- Premise 2 cannot be reversed: someone aged <InlineMath math="12" /> need not be a club member.
- Grade VII members surely played in a tournament, so the fifth option contradicts the premises.

# Answer

So the conclusion that must be true is that some Grade VII students are at least **<InlineMath math="12" />** years old.

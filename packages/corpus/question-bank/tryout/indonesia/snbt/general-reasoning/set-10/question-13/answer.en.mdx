# Given

The compound statement says **contest registration opens on Monday and closes on Friday**.

# Asked

The exact **negation** of that and-statement.

# Solution

> An and-statement becomes false as soon as even one half fails, so its negation flips and into or.

The statement has the form **"p and q"**, which is true only when both parts are true. Its negation only needs to say that at least one part fails.

<BlockMath math="\sim(p\wedge q)\equiv\,\sim p\vee\sim q" />

The diagram below checks when the negation is true. **One failed part is enough** for the negation to hold.

<Mermaid
  title={<>When the negation of "opens Monday and closes Friday" is true</>}
  description={<>If registration doesn't open on Monday, the negation is true. If it opens on Monday but doesn't close on Friday, the negation is true too. Only if it opens Monday and closes Friday is the negation false.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR
    A{"Opens on Monday?"} -->|"no"| N1["Negation true"]
    A -->|"yes"| B{"Closes on Friday?"}
    B -->|"no"| N2["Negation true"]
    B -->|"yes"| S["Original true, negation false"]`}
/>

With <InlineMath math="p" /> = registration opens on Monday and <InlineMath math="q" /> = registration closes on Friday, the negation is that registration does not open on Monday or does not close on Friday.

The other options do not fit:

- *Not ... and not ...* is too strong, since the negation already holds when just one part fails.
- *Opens on Monday or closes on Friday* denies nothing.
- An *if ..., then ...* form is not the negation of an *and* statement.
- Tuesday and Saturday are just one other case, not the negation.

# Answer

So the negation is that registration does not open on Monday or does not close on Friday.

# Given

Mrs. Ani has **<InlineMath math="600" /> mL of drink at a <InlineMath math="1:5" /> syrup-to-water ratio** and wants a **<InlineMath math="1:4" /> ratio** by adding pure syrup only.

# Asked

How many **milliliters of pure syrup** to add.

# Solution

> The amount of water never changes when you pour in pure syrup, so use that fixed water volume to find the new syrup target.

First split **the syrup and the water** in the <InlineMath math="600" /> mL drink.

<BlockMath math="\text{syrup}=\frac{1}{6}\times600=100\text{ mL},\qquad \text{water}=\frac{5}{6}\times600=500\text{ mL}" />

The water stays the same, so the syrup must become <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> of the water.

<BlockMath math="100+x=\frac{500}{4}=125\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=25" />

The chart below compares the drink before and after the syrup is added. **The water bar stays put, and only the syrup bar rises by <InlineMath math="25" /> mL**.

# Answer

So Bu Ani must add **<InlineMath math="25" />** mL of pure syrup.

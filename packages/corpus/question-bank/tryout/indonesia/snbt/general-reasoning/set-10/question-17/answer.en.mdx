Class X booths are never adjacent and X-B is always at 6, so X-A and X-C take **two non-adjacent numbers from 1 to 4**, not 5.

- X-C at 1, XI-A at 2. X-A at 3 or 4, with XI-B and XI-C filling the rest. That gives 2 arrangements.
- X-A at 1, XI-B at 2. X-C at 3 or 4, XI-A directly to its right, and XI-C in the gap left. That gives 2 arrangements.
- X-A at 2. X-C must be at 4 and XI-A at 5. XI-B must be at 1 or 3, and only XI-B at 1 with XI-C at 3 works. That gives 1 arrangement.

Any other start, such as X-C at 2, always ends in a broken rule.

# Given

Booth X-B stays fixed at **spot <InlineMath math="6" />**, while **X-A and X-C take two non-adjacent spots among <InlineMath math="1" /> through <InlineMath math="4" />**.

# Asked

The **total number of distinct valid booth layouts**.

# Solution

> List the legal non-adjacent pairs for X-A and X-C first, then count how many valid ways the Grade XI booths complete each case.

Class X booths are never adjacent and X-B is always at <InlineMath math="6" />, so X-A and X-C take **two non-adjacent numbers from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="4" />**, not <InlineMath math="5" />.

- X-C at <InlineMath math="1" />, XI-A at <InlineMath math="2" />. X-A at <InlineMath math="3" /> or <InlineMath math="4" />, with XI-B and XI-C filling the rest. That gives <InlineMath math="2" /> arrangements.
- X-A at <InlineMath math="1" />, XI-B at <InlineMath math="2" />. X-C at <InlineMath math="3" /> or <InlineMath math="4" />, XI-A directly to its right, and XI-C in the gap left. That gives <InlineMath math="2" /> arrangements.
- X-A at <InlineMath math="2" />. X-C must be at <InlineMath math="4" /> and XI-A at <InlineMath math="5" />. XI-B must be at <InlineMath math="1" /> or <InlineMath math="3" />, and only XI-B at <InlineMath math="1" /> with XI-C at <InlineMath math="3" /> works. That gives <InlineMath math="1" /> arrangement.

Any other start, such as X-C at <InlineMath math="2" />, always ends in a broken rule.

So **<InlineMath math="5" />** arrangements meet every rule.

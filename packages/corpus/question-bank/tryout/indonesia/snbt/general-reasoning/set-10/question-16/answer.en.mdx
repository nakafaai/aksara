Mark each statement Mungkin (possible) or Tidak mungkin (impossible). There are **five arrangements** that meet every rule (booths 1 to 6 from the left):

- X-A, XI-B, X-C, XI-A, XI-C, X-B
- X-A, XI-B, XI-C, X-C, XI-A, X-B
- X-C, XI-A, X-A, XI-B, XI-C, X-B
- X-C, XI-A, XI-B, X-A, XI-C, X-B
- XI-B, X-A, XI-C, X-C, XI-A, X-B

**Statement 1**: possible. The second and fifth arrangements put X-C at 4.

**Statement 2**: possible. In the second arrangement, XI-B is at 2 and XI-C at 3.

**Statement 3**: impossible. XI-A is always directly right of X-C. If XI-A is at 3, X-C is at 2, so X-A must be at 4 and XI-B at 5. Then XI-C must be at 1, which breaks rules 4 and 6.

**Statement 4**: impossible. XI-C must have a higher number than X-A. If XI-C is at 2, X-A is at 1 and XI-B would also need booth 2, which is already taken.

So statements 1 and 2 are possible, while statements 3 and 4 are impossible.

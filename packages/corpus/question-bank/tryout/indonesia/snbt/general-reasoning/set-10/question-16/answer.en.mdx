# Given

The booth rules leave **five complete valid layouts from spot <InlineMath math="1" /> through <InlineMath math="6" />**.

# Asked

Whether each statement is **possible** or **impossible** under the rules.

# Solution

> With all five legal layouts written down, checking possible versus impossible is as quick as scanning the five rows.

Mark each statement Mungkin (possible) or Tidak mungkin (impossible). There are **five arrangements** that meet every rule (booths 1 to 6 from the left):

- X-A, XI-B, X-C, XI-A, XI-C, X-B
- X-A, XI-B, XI-C, X-C, XI-A, X-B
- X-C, XI-A, X-A, XI-B, XI-C, X-B
- X-C, XI-A, XI-B, X-A, XI-C, X-B
- XI-B, X-A, XI-C, X-C, XI-A, X-B

The chart below reads XI-A's and XI-C's booths in the five arrangements. **XI-A's bar never stops at <InlineMath math="3" />, and XI-C's bar never stops at <InlineMath math="2" />**.

**Statement 1**: possible. The second and fifth arrangements put X-C at <InlineMath math="4" />.

**Statement 2**: possible. In the second arrangement, XI-B is at <InlineMath math="2" /> and XI-C at <InlineMath math="3" />.

**Statement 3**: impossible. XI-A is always directly right of X-C. If XI-A is at <InlineMath math="3" />, X-C is at <InlineMath math="2" />, so X-A must be at <InlineMath math="4" /> and XI-B at <InlineMath math="5" />. Then XI-C must be at <InlineMath math="1" />, which breaks rules 4 and 6.

**Statement 4**: impossible. XI-C must have a higher number than X-A. If XI-C is at <InlineMath math="2" />, X-A is at <InlineMath math="1" /> and XI-B would also need booth 2, which is already taken.

# Answer

So statements 1 and 2 are possible, while statements 3 and 4 are impossible.

Work out what **one piece** needs: <InlineMath math="\frac{300}{12}=25" /> grams of flour and <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{12}=\frac{1}{3}" /> of an egg. For <InlineMath math="n" /> pieces she needs <InlineMath math="25n" /> grams of flour and <InlineMath math="\frac{n}{3}" /> eggs.

There are three conditions:

- Flour: <InlineMath math="25n\le1{,}000" />, so <InlineMath math="n\le40" />.
- Eggs: <InlineMath math="\frac{n}{3}\le15" />, so <InlineMath math="n\le45" />.
- Eggs must be **whole**, so <InlineMath math="\frac{n}{3}" /> is a whole number, meaning <InlineMath math="n" /> is a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" />.

The chart below puts each condition's limit side by side. **The flour gives the tighter limit**, and the whole-egg condition then brings it down to a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" />.

The largest multiple of <InlineMath math="3" /> not above <InlineMath math="40" /> is <InlineMath math="39" />. Thirty-nine pieces need <InlineMath math="975" /> grams of flour and <InlineMath math="13" /> eggs, both available. The answer <InlineMath math="40" /> would need <InlineMath math="13\tfrac{1}{3}" /> eggs, which is not allowed.

So Rina can make at most <InlineMath math="39" /> pieces.

The [WHO explanation of trans fat](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat/) links its consumption to heart-disease risk. The reasoning is:

1. Industrial trans fat raises LDL cholesterol and lowers HDL cholesterol.
2. Those changes increase the risk of heart attack and death from coronary heart disease.
3. Reducing intake therefore reduces exposure to that dietary risk.

The other choices go beyond the passage. Saying that fried foods may contain industrial trans fat does not mean that every fried food does. Reducing one risk factor does not guarantee that heart disease will never occur. The passage also does not say that the LDL and HDL changes cancel each other out, or that limiting intake matters only for people who already have heart disease.

Therefore, the supported conclusion is that **reducing industrial trans-fat intake reduces exposure to a preventable dietary risk for coronary heart disease**.

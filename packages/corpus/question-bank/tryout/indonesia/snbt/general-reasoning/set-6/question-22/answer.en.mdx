After the water is added, the mixture has a volume of <InlineMath math="40+5=45\text{ L}" />. A concentration of <InlineMath math="20\%" /> gives a solute volume of:

<BlockMath math="0.20\times45=9\text{ L}" />

There is no need to recover the volumes of the two original solutions because the diluted mixture's volume and concentration are already known.

Let <InlineMath math="y\text{ L}" /> of the <InlineMath math="45\%" /> solution be added. It contributes <InlineMath math="0.45y\text{ L}" /> of solute. Setting the new concentration to <InlineMath math="30\%" /> gives:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\frac{9+0.45y}{45+y}&=0.30\\9+0.45y&=13.5+0.30y\\0.15y&=4.5\\y&=30\end{aligned}" />

To check, the final volume is <InlineMath math="45+30=75\text{ L}" /> and the solute volume is <InlineMath math="9+13.5=22.5\text{ L}" />. The ratio <InlineMath math="22.5/75=30\%" /> is the required concentration. Therefore, <InlineMath math="30\text{ L}" /> of solution must be added.

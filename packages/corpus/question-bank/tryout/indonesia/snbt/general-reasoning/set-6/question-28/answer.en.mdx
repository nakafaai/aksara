# Given

**Two real numbers** satisfy **<InlineMath math="0 < a < 1" />** and **<InlineMath math="b > 1" />**.

# Asked

Whether each inequality is **Certainly true** or **Not certainly true** for all allowed values.

# Solution

> Multiplying a positive number by a fraction below one shrinks it, and one concrete counterexample immediately disproves a non-guaranteed claim.

Choose Pasti benar (always true) or Tidak pasti benar (not always true). Use inequality facts: **multiplying by a positive number below <InlineMath math="1" /> makes a number smaller**, and dividing by a number above <InlineMath math="1" /> also makes it smaller. To show a statement is not always true, one counterexample is enough.

The chart below tries one pair of values, <InlineMath math="a=0.5" /> and <InlineMath math="b=1.2" />. **The <InlineMath math="a+b" /> bar doesn't reach <InlineMath math="2" />, the <InlineMath math="a:b" /> bar is shorter than <InlineMath math="a" />, the <InlineMath math="a^2" /> bar is shorter than <InlineMath math="a" />, and the <InlineMath math="ab" /> bar is shorter than <InlineMath math="b" />**.

**Statement 1**: not always true. For <InlineMath math="a=0.5" /> and <InlineMath math="b=1.2" />, <InlineMath math="a+b=1.7" />, not more than <InlineMath math="2" />.

**Statement 2**: not always true. Since <InlineMath math="b>1" /> and <InlineMath math="a>0" />, dividing <InlineMath math="a" /> by <InlineMath math="b" /> always makes the value smaller than <InlineMath math="a" />. The statement is always false.

**Statement 3**: always true. Multiply <InlineMath math="a<1" /> by <InlineMath math="a>0" /> to get <InlineMath math="a^2<a" />.

**Statement 4**: always true. Multiply <InlineMath math="a<1" /> by <InlineMath math="b>0" /> to get <InlineMath math="ab<b" />.

# Answer

So statements 3 and 4 are always true, while statements 1 and 2 are not.

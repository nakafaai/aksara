# Given

After cafeteria plates shrank, **more staff went back for seconds**, yet **total food taken per employee still dropped from <InlineMath math="620" /> grams to <InlineMath math="540" /> grams**.

# Asked

The conclusion **most strongly supported** by the passage.

# Solution

> Total grams per person already counts every return trip, so two small plates can still hold less food than one oversized plate.

Look at two changes that seem to pull in opposite directions. More employees took food more than once, yet **the total taken per employee still fell**, from <InlineMath math="620" /> grams to <InlineMath math="540" /> grams, because that figure already includes second and later servings. So smaller plates reduced the food taken even though some employees went back for more.

The other options are not supported:

- Food eaten equals food taken minus waste. Before the change it was <InlineMath math="620-70=550" /> grams, after it <InlineMath math="540-40=500" /> grams. The drop is <InlineMath math="50" /> grams, not <InlineMath math="80" /> grams.
- Only <InlineMath math="25\%" /> took food more than once, not every employee.
- The passage says nothing about the canteen's profit or loss.
- The passage says nothing about afternoon hunger.

# Answer

So the best supported conclusion is that **smaller plates reduce the food taken even though more employees take food more than once**.

Let <InlineMath math="W" /> mean that real farmworker wages rise, <InlineMath math="T" /> that farmers' terms of trade rise, <InlineMath math="P" /> that village poverty increases, and <InlineMath math="I" /> that rural-urban inequality increases.

The model gives this conditional rule:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(\neg W\land\neg T)\Rightarrow(P\land I)" />
  <BlockMath math="\neg W\land\neg T" />
</MathContainer>

The observed condition exactly matches the rule's antecedent. By modus ponens, its consequence follows:

<BlockMath math="P\land I" />

Therefore, **village poverty and rural-urban inequality will both increase** according to the stated model. No additional real-world assumption is needed.

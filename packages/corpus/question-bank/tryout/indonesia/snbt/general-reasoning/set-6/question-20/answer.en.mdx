# Given

Five trees K, L, M, N, and O have distinct heights with **<InlineMath math="N > M > K" /> and O shortest**, leaving only **L** unplaced.

# Asked

The **extra clue** that uniquely fixes the full height order of all five trees.

# Solution

> Four trees already sit in a single chain and only L floats free, so pick the clue that traps L into one exact gap.

The two statements give the order <InlineMath math="N>M>K" /> with O lowest. The only unknown is **where L goes**, which can be anywhere above O: above N, between N and M, between M and K, or between K and O.

"L is lower than K" leaves L only one place, between K and O. The order becomes N, M, K, L, then O from tallest to shortest, and it is certain.

The diagram below draws the full order. **L has only one place, between K and O**, so no tree's position is in doubt anymore.

  title={<>Height order of the five trees once L is shorter than K</>}
  description={<>N is taller than M, M is taller than K, K is taller than L, and L is taller than O, the shortest tree.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR
    N["N"] --> M["M"]
    M --> K["K"]

    L --> O["O, shortest"]`}

The other options are not enough:

- L being taller than O is already known, since O is lowest.
- L below N still leaves three places: between N and M, between M and K, or between K and O.
- L below M still leaves two places: between M and K or between K and O.
- N being tallest still leaves L in one of three places below N.

# Answer

So the information that fixes the order is that **tree L is shorter than tree K**.

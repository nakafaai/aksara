# Given

Dina walks from A to B moving only **east (<InlineMath math="4" /> blocks) and north (<InlineMath math="2" /> blocks)** while avoiding closed **intersection X (<InlineMath math="2" /> east, <InlineMath math="1" /> north)**.

# Asked

The **number of distinct valid routes** from A to B avoiding X.

# Solution

> Count every path from A to B first, then subtract the blocked paths found by multiplying paths from A to X by paths from X to B.

Every route from A to B has <InlineMath math="4" /> steps east and <InlineMath math="2" /> steps north. Count all routes, then **subtract the routes through X**.

All routes: choose which <InlineMath math="2" /> of the <InlineMath math="6" /> steps go north.

<BlockMath math="\binom{6}{2}=15" />

Routes through X: from A to X there are <InlineMath math="2" /> steps east and <InlineMath math="1" /> north, <InlineMath math="\binom{3}{1}=3" /> ways. From X to B it is again <InlineMath math="2" /> east and <InlineMath math="1" /> north, <InlineMath math="3" /> ways. So <InlineMath math="3\times3=9" /> routes pass through X.

<BlockMath math="15-9=6" />

The chart below puts the three counts side by side. **Routes through X take up more than half of all routes**, so only <InlineMath math="6" /> are left.

# Answer

So Dina can choose from <InlineMath math="6" /> routes.

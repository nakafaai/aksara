The form "<InlineMath math="p" /> only if <InlineMath math="q" />" (*hanya jika*) means <InlineMath math="p\Rightarrow q" />: <InlineMath math="q" /> is a **necessary condition** for <InlineMath math="p" />. Here <InlineMath math="p" />: the shop is open and <InlineMath math="q" />: the owner is present, so the statement is "if the shop is open, the owner is present".

The statement equivalent to <InlineMath math="p\Rightarrow q" /> is its **contrapositive**, <InlineMath math="\lnot q\Rightarrow\lnot p" />: if the owner is not present, the shop is not open (*Jika pemiliknya tidak hadir, toko itu tidak buka*).

The other options are not equivalent:

- "If the owner is present, the shop is open" is the converse, <InlineMath math="q\Rightarrow p" />.
- "If the shop is not open, the owner is not present" is the inverse, <InlineMath math="\lnot p\Rightarrow\lnot q" />.
- "The shop is open and the owner is not present" is the negation.
- "The owner is present only if the shop is open" means <InlineMath math="q\Rightarrow p" />, the same as the converse.

So the statement is equivalent to "If the owner is not present, the shop is not open".

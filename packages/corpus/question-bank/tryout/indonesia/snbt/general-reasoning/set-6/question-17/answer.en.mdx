# Given

All five order rules apply with **T limited to first or sixth** and **P and Q always adjacent**.

# Asked

The **number of distinct valid performance orders**.

# Solution

> Split into the two endpoint cases for T and glue PQ into one block so every valid order is counted cleanly.

Split the cases by the position of **T**, since T may only be first or last. P and Q always sit together, so treat PQ as one block.

**Case T last**. Places 1 to 5 hold the block PQ, R, S, and U, with R before S, S not last (already met because T is last), and U not next to R. All possibilities:

| Places 1 to 5 | Meets rule 4? |
| --- | --- |
| P, Q, R, S, U | yes |
| R, P, Q, S, U | yes |
| R, P, Q, U, S | yes |
| R, S, P, Q, U | yes |
| R, S, U, P, Q | yes |
| U, P, Q, R, S | yes |

Other arrangements with R before S fail because U is next to R, for example P, Q, U, R, S or U, R, P, Q, S. That is **<InlineMath math="6" />** orders.

**Case T first**. Places 2 to 6 hold the block PQ, R, S, and U, with S not in place 6. The possibilities are T, P, Q, R, S, U, then T, R, P, Q, S, U, then T, R, S, P, Q, U, and T, R, S, U, P, Q. That is **<InlineMath math="4" />** orders.

The chart below compares the two cases. **The case with T last gives more orders**, because rule 5 is already met there.

Comparing the two bars makes it easy to see that placing T first allows more legal orders than placing T last.

# Answer

The total is **<InlineMath math="6+4=10" />** orders.

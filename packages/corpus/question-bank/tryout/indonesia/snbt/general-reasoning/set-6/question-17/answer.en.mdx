Split the cases by the position of **T**, since T may only be first or last. P and Q always sit together, so treat PQ as one block.

**Case T last**. Places 1 to 5 hold the block PQ, R, S, and U, with R before S, S not last (already met because T is last), and U not next to R. All possibilities:

| Places 1 to 5 | Meets rule 4? |
| --- | --- |
| P, Q, R, S, U | yes |
| R, P, Q, S, U | yes |
| R, P, Q, U, S | yes |
| R, S, P, Q, U | yes |
| R, S, U, P, Q | yes |
| U, P, Q, R, S | yes |

Other arrangements with R before S fail because U is next to R, for example P, Q, U, R, S or U, R, P, Q, S. That is **6** orders.

**Case T first**. Places 2 to 6 hold the block PQ, R, S, and U, with S not in place 6. The possibilities are T, P, Q, R, S, U, then T, R, P, Q, S, U, then T, R, S, P, Q, U, and T, R, S, U, P, Q. That is **4** orders.

The total is <InlineMath math="6+4=10" /> orders.

To judge the cameras' role, we need to compare **with and without cameras** while other things, above all the patrols, stay the same. Streets with cameras and streets without cameras but with the same patrols give exactly that comparison. If theft on camera streets is far lower, the cameras' role shows. If it is about the same, the drop may come from the patrols.

The other options help less:

- Residents' feeling of safety is an opinion, not theft data.
- Camera specifications do not show their effect on theft.
- Data from 2020 do not separate the effect of the cameras from the patrols in 2026.
- The number of patrol members describes the patrols but does not show the cameras' role.

So the most helpful information is a comparison of thefts on streets with cameras and on other streets without cameras but with the same patrols.

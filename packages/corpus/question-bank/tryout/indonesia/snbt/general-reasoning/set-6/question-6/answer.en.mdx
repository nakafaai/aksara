# Given

Stair use **spiked in week <InlineMath math="5" /> and then slid through week <InlineMath math="8" />**, while total trips stayed near <InlineMath math="2{,}700" /> with no other building changes.

# Asked

The **most likely explanation** for that sharp jump followed by a steady fade.

# Solution

> A brand-new sign grabs attention at first, then fades into the background as people get used to seeing it and drift back to the elevator.

The pattern to explain is **a jump in week <InlineMath math="5" /> followed by a gradual decline**, while total trips stayed about <InlineMath math="2{,}700" />. The passage also says nothing else changed in the building.

A new poster catches attention, so many employees try the stairs. After a few weeks the poster becomes an ordinary sight, and some employees return to their old habit of taking the lift. This explains both the jump and the gradual decline.

The other options do not fit:

- Total trips did not rise, so the number of employees did not rise.
- Working from home would lower total trips, but the total stayed the same.
- If the poster had been removed at the start of week <InlineMath math="5" />, stair use would not have jumped that week.
- New stairs in week <InlineMath math="8" /> would raise stair use, yet it fell, and the passage says nothing else changed.

# Answer

So the most likely explanation is that **the poster drew attention while new, but employees gradually got used to it and went back to the lift**.

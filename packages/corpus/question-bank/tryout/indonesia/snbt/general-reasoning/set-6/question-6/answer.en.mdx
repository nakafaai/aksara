Each student appears **once**, classified by their main hobby. We can therefore count students by adding the relevant row or column.

#### Participation by hobby

To count participants in one hobby, add the values **in the same row**. For example, the basketball total is:

<BlockMath math="100+150+120=370" />

Basketball has the largest count in every grade, so it also has the largest overall total. The claim that it is the most popular hobby is supported.

For acting, the total is:

<BlockMath math="40+7+18=65" />

The claim that <InlineMath math="65" /> students like acting is also supported. For painting, the total is:

<BlockMath math="68+54+44=166" />

Therefore, the claim that <InlineMath math="160" /> students like painting does not match the data.

#### Totals and comparisons by grade

To count Grade XII students, add all five hobbies **in the Grade XII column**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&amp;120+80+44+44+18\\&amp;=306\end{aligned}" />

The claimed Grade XII total of <InlineMath math="306" /> is correct. Dance counts in Grades X, XI, and XII are <InlineMath math="75" />, <InlineMath math="78" />, and <InlineMath math="80" />, respectively. Since <InlineMath math="75<78<80" />, Grade X has the fewest dance participants among the three grades.

The only unsupported statement is that <InlineMath math="160" /> students participate in **painting**. The correct total is <InlineMath math="166" /> students.

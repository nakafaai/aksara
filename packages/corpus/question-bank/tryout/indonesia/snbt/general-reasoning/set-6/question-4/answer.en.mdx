# Given

The table tracks **daily stair use before the poster** (around <InlineMath math="300" />), then a **jump to <InlineMath math="700" /> in week <InlineMath math="5" /> followed by weekly declines**.

# Asked

The conclusion **most strongly supported** by the text and table.

# Solution

> Read the shape of the weekly curve as a whole, spotting the sharp jump right when the poster went up and the steady fade right after.

Compare the weeks before and after the poster. Before the poster, stair use was about <InlineMath math="300" /> per day. In week <InlineMath math="5" /> it jumped to <InlineMath math="700" />, then **fell every week**: <InlineMath math="560" />, <InlineMath math="430" />, <InlineMath math="380" />. The poster had its largest effect when new, then gradually weakened.

- Total trips stayed about <InlineMath math="2{,}700" /> each week, for example <InlineMath math="2{,}400+300=2{,}700" /> in week <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="2{,}300+380=2{,}680" /> in week <InlineMath math="8" />.

# Answer

So the best supported conclusion is that **the poster's effect on stair use was largest in its first week and then weakened**.

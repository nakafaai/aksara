The conclusion names plate size as **the cause**. Such a conclusion weakens when **another cause** occurred at the same time and could produce the same result.

If the second four weeks fell in very hot weather, when lunch portions usually shrink, the drop in food taken could come from the weather rather than the plates. Two causes are mixed, so the conclusion weakens.

The other options do not weaken it:

- Easier washing has nothing to do with how much food is taken.
- An unchanged menu and price rule out other causes, so they strengthen the conclusion.
- Liking big plates does not change the amounts recorded.
- Similar results in another canteen support the role of plate size.

So the statement that weakens the conclusion most is that the second four weeks fell in very hot weather, when lunch portions usually shrink.

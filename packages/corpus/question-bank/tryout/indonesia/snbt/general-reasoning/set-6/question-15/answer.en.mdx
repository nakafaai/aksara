# Given

All five performance-order rules apply together with **R performing second**.

# Asked

The statement that **must be true** when R is second.

# Solution

> Locking R into the second slot sharply narrows who can take the first slot and forces the key rule into place.

If R is second, first work out who can be first.

- **U** cannot be first, since it would be just before R (rule 4).
- **S** cannot be first, since S must come after R (rule 2).
- **P** cannot be first, since Q must follow P directly, but second place is R.
- **Q** cannot be first, since P must come directly before Q.

The diagram below sums up why each candidate for first place drops out. **Only T survives**, since T is allowed to present first.

  title={<>Who can present first if R is second</>}
  description={<>U drops out because it would sit just before R. S drops out because it must come after R. P drops out because Q must follow P directly, but R is second. Q drops out because P must come directly before Q. Only T is left.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR

    R --> S["S first: before R, out"]
    R --> P["P first: Q can't be second, out"]

/>

Only **T** remains, so T is certainly first. The orders meeting every rule are T, R, P, Q, S, U, then T, R, S, P, Q, U, and T, R, S, U, P, Q.

The other options are not certain:

- S is third in only two of the three orders.
- U is last in only two of the three orders.
- P is third only in T, R, P, Q, S, U.
- Q is last only in T, R, S, U, P, Q.

# Answer

So what must be true is that T presents first.

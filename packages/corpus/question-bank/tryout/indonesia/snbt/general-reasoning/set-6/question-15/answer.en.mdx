If R is second, first work out who can be first.

- **U** cannot be first, since it would be just before R (rule 4).
- **S** cannot be first, since S must come after R (rule 2).
- **P** cannot be first, since Q must follow P directly, but second place is R.
- **Q** cannot be first, since P must come directly before Q.

Only **T** remains, so T is certainly first. The orders meeting every rule are T, R, P, Q, S, U, then T, R, S, P, Q, U, and T, R, S, U, P, Q.

The other options are not certain:

- S is third in only two of the three orders.
- U is last in only two of the three orders.
- P is third only in T, R, P, Q, S, U.
- Q is last only in T, R, S, U, P, Q.

So what must be true is that T presents first.

# Given

Five rules constrain the **performance order of six participants** from first through sixth, including **T** endpoints and an adjacent pair.

# Asked

The position where **U** can **never** perform.

# Solution

> Drop U into a test slot and watch whether the remaining rules immediately leave T or the paired block with zero legal places.

Check whether U can take second place. If U is second, **T** must be first or sixth.

- If T is first, places 3 to 6 hold P, Q, R, and S. R cannot be third (next to U) and must come before S, so R is fourth or fifth. If R is fifth, S must be sixth, which breaks rule 5. If R is fourth, S cannot be sixth, so S is fifth, and P and Q are left with places 3 and 6, which are **not adjacent**.
- If T is sixth, places 1 and 3 to <InlineMath math="5" /> hold P, Q, R, and S. P and Q cannot sit together at place 1, since place 2 is U. So P and Q take places 3 and 4 or <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />. With P, Q in <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />, R and S fill places 1 and 5, so **R in place 1** is next to U. With P, Q in <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />, R and S fill places 1 and 3, so R is always next to U.

Every case fails, so U **cannot** be second.

Other places are possible for U, for example:

- U first: U, P, Q, R, S, T.
- U third: R, S, U, P, Q, T.
- U fifth: P, Q, R, S, U, T.
- U sixth: T, P, Q, R, S, U.

So U cannot present in second place.

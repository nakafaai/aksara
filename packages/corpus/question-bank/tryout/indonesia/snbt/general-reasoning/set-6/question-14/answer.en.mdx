Check whether U can take second place. If U is second, **T** must be first or sixth.

- If T is first, places 3 to 6 hold P, Q, R, and S. R cannot be third (next to U) and must come before S, so R is fourth or fifth. If R is fifth, S must be sixth, which breaks rule 5. If R is fourth, S cannot be sixth, so S is fifth, and P and Q are left with places 3 and 6, which are **not adjacent**.
- If T is sixth, places 1 and 3 to 5 hold P, Q, R, and S. P and Q cannot sit together at place 1, since place 2 is U. So P and Q take places 3 and 4 or 4 and 5. With P, Q in 3 and 4, R and S fill places 1 and 5, so **R in place 1** is next to U. With P, Q in 4 and 5, R and S fill places 1 and 3, so R is always next to U.

Every case fails, so U **cannot** be second.

Other places are possible for U, for example:

- U first: U, P, Q, R, S, T.
- U third: R, S, U, P, Q, T.
- U fifth: P, Q, R, S, U, T.
- U sixth: T, P, Q, R, S, U.

So U cannot present in second place.

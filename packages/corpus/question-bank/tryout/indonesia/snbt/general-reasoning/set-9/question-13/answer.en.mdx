An implication <InlineMath math="p\Rightarrow q" /> is false in **one situation only**: <InlineMath math="p" /> happens but <InlineMath math="q" /> does not. So its negation is <InlineMath math="p\land\lnot q" />.

With <InlineMath math="p" />: it rains and <InlineMath math="q" />: the road in front of the school floods, the negation is "**It rains and the road in front of the school does not flood**" (*Hujan dan jalan di depan sekolah tidak tergenang*).

The other options are not the negation:

- "If it does not rain, the road does not flood" is the inverse, and it can be true together with the original.
- "If the road does not flood, then it does not rain" is the contrapositive, which is **equivalent** to the original.
- "It does not rain and the road floods" does not refute the original, which says nothing about days without rain.
- "It does not rain or the road floods" is just another form of the original, <InlineMath math="\lnot p\lor q" />.

So the negation is "It rains and the road in front of the school does not flood".

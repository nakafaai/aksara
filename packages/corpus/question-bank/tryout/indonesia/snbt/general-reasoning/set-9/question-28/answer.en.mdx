# Given

**Item A costs <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}80{,}000" />** and **item B costs <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000" />**.

# Asked

Whether each percentage comparison statement is **True** or **False**.

# Solution

> The same twenty-thousand gap gives two different percentages depending on whether you divide by item A or by item B.

Mark each statement Benar (true) or Salah (false). A percentage is always taken **relative to its base**. The price gap is <InlineMath math="20{,}000" />, but the percentage depends on which price is the base.

**Statement 1** is false. A is <InlineMath math="20\%" /> cheaper than B, but B is <InlineMath math="25\%" /> dearer than A.

**Statement 2** is false. After a <InlineMath math="10\%" /> rise the prices are <InlineMath math="88{,}000" /> and <InlineMath math="110{,}000" />, so the gap becomes <InlineMath math="22{,}000" />.

**Statement 3** is true. The ratio is exactly <InlineMath math="125\%" />.

**Statement 4** is true. <InlineMath math="80{,}000+20{,}000=100{,}000" />.

# Answer

So statements 3 and 4 are **true, while statements 1 and 2 are false**.

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> be the numbers of X and Y packages. Both are non-negative integers.

The material and labour limits give:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3x+5y\le42" />
  <BlockMath math="4x+y\le28" />
</MathContainer>

Total profit, in the given units, is:

<BlockMath math="L=8x+9y" />

Each X needs <InlineMath math="4" /> hours, so at most <InlineMath math="7" /> X packages can be made. For each <InlineMath math="x" />, profit increases with <InlineMath math="y" />. It is therefore enough to check the largest <InlineMath math="y" /> satisfying both limits:

| <InlineMath math="x" /> | Largest <InlineMath math="y" /> | Profit |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="8" /> | <InlineMath math="72" /> |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="71" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="79" /> |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> | <InlineMath math="78" /> |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> | <InlineMath math="86" /> |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="85" /> |
| <InlineMath math="6" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="84" /> |
| <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="56" /> |

The highest profit is <InlineMath math="86" /> at <InlineMath math="x=4" /> and <InlineMath math="y=6" />. The next-best pair, <InlineMath math="x=5" /> and <InlineMath math="y=5" />, yields only <InlineMath math="85" />.

The maximum-profit solution satisfies both limits:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3\times4+5\times6=42" />
  <BlockMath math="4\times4+6=22\le28" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, there are **<InlineMath math="6-4=2" /> more Y packages** than X packages.

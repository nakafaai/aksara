# Solution

> Spot the turning point in the table, where extra fertilizer boosts harvest up to a limit before over-fertilizing pulls yields back down.

Look at **the direction of change** in the harvest as fertilizer increases. From <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="150" /> kg, the harvest rises: <InlineMath math="3.0" />, <InlineMath math="4.2" />, <InlineMath math="5.0" />, <InlineMath math="5.1" />. The gains shrink, <InlineMath math="1.2" />, then <InlineMath math="0.8" />, then only <InlineMath math="0.1" /> tons. At <InlineMath math="200" /> kg, the harvest actually falls to <InlineMath math="4.6" /> tons.

The other options do not fit the data:

- "The more, the higher" breaks down at <InlineMath math="200" /> kg.
- The harvest clearly changes with the fertilizer, so fertilizer does matter.
- The highest harvest, <InlineMath math="5.1" /> tons, comes at <InlineMath math="150" /> kg.
- A gain of <InlineMath math="1.2" /> tons happens only from <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="50" /> kg. Later gains are smaller.

# Answer

So the most appropriate conclusion is that more fertilizer raises the harvest up to about **<InlineMath math="150" />** kg, but more than that actually lowers it.

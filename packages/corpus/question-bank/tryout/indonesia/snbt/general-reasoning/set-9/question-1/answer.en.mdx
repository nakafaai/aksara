# Given

Caterpillars fell in **both plot groups**, dropping from <InlineMath math="50" /> to <InlineMath math="20" /> with traps and from <InlineMath math="50" /> to <InlineMath math="30" /> without traps.

# Asked

The conclusion **most strongly supported** by comparing both groups.

# Solution

> Because the untrapped plots also dropped by twenty, pheromone traps may explain the extra drop, not the entire decline.

Compare the two groups of plots. Caterpillars fell in **both** groups: from <InlineMath math="50" /> to <InlineMath math="20" /> in the trap plots and from <InlineMath math="50" /> to <InlineMath math="30" /> in the plots without traps. The drop without traps shows that something other than the traps was at work.

The final gap of <InlineMath math="10" /> caterpillars is the part most likely linked to the traps. So the traps probably played a part, but they do not explain the whole drop.

The other options are not supported:

- The <InlineMath math="60\%" /> drop includes other causes that also lowered caterpillars in the plots without traps, so it is not all due to the traps.
- The passage only says it rained almost every day, without showing rain to be the only cause.
- Caterpillars in the plots without traps fell rather than rose.
- The passage says nothing about the harvest.

# Answer

So the best supported conclusion is that the pheromone traps probably helped reduce caterpillars, but they do not explain the whole drop.

# Given

Dina score <InlineMath math="d" /> and Eko score <InlineMath math="e" /> come with **(1) their average is <InlineMath math="80" />** and **(2) Eko scored <InlineMath math="6" /> below Dina**.

# Asked

Whether statements **(1)** and **(2)** are sufficient to find Dina score.

# Solution

> With two unknown scores, one equation alone leaves endless pairs, while both independent equations together pin down Dina exact score.

Let Dina's score be <InlineMath math="d" /> and Eko's <InlineMath math="e" />. There are **two unknowns**, so two equations are usually needed.

**Statement (1) alone**: not sufficient. <InlineMath math="d+e=160" /> fits many pairs, such as (<InlineMath math="80" />, <InlineMath math="80" />) or (<InlineMath math="90" />, <InlineMath math="70" />).

**Statement (2) alone**: not sufficient. <InlineMath math="e=d-6" /> also fits many pairs, such as (<InlineMath math="70" />, <InlineMath math="64" />) or (<InlineMath math="86" />, <InlineMath math="80" />).

**Both statements together**:

<BlockMath math="d+(d-6)=160\quad\Rightarrow\quad 2d=166\quad\Rightarrow\quad d=83" />

The chart below draws the Eko score that fits each statement for several Dina scores. **The two lines meet only at <InlineMath math="d=83" />**.

# Answer

So the two statements together are **sufficient, but neither alone is**.

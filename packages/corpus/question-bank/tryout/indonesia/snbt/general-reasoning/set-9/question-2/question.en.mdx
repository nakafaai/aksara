According to the IPCC, climate change has already affected food security through higher temperatures, changing rainfall, and more frequent extreme events. The size and direction of the effect vary by crop and region. A higher concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere can stimulate the growth of some crops, but it can also reduce the concentration of important nutrients.

FAO estimates that plant pests and diseases reduce global crop yields by <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="40\%" /> each year. Climate change can alter where pests spread and how damaging they become. However, pest pressure also depends on rainfall, land use, host plants, natural enemies, and farm management.

The IPCC reports that warming has shifted the distributions of many marine species toward the poles and changed fisheries productivity. This is a population-level pattern, not a rule that every tropical fish moves poleward or that one observation alone proves ocean warming. Tropical and subtropical fisheries are projected to experience larger productivity losses than many poleward regions.

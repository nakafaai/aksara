# Given

During the **11:00 to 12:00 window**, patient arrivals jumped from <InlineMath math="5" /> to <InlineMath math="13" /> and the wait time in that hour rose.

# Asked

The **most likely explanation** for why wait times rose between 11:00 and 12:00.

# Solution

> Wait times naturally grow in a formerly quiet hour once online booking lets more patients choose to arrive near noon.

Waiting usually rises when **more patients** arrive at the same hour. At 11.00 to 12.00, patients rose from <InlineMath math="5" /> to <InlineMath math="13" />. Online queuing let patients choose their arrival hour, and some chose to come near noon. More patients at that hour explains the longer wait.

The other options do not explain it:

- The passage does not mention when doctors leave.
- The drop in morning waiting is a true fact, but it does not explain the rise near noon.
- The small rise in daily patients is a fact, but it does not explain why the longer wait happened at that hour.
- Paint color in the waiting room does not affect waiting time.

# Answer

So the most likely explanation is that, through online queuing, more patients chose to come near noon.

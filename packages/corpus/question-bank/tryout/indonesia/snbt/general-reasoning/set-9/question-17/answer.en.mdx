# Given

Umar stays on **floor <InlineMath math="1" /> gardening**, while Rani, Sita, and Tika fill **floors <InlineMath math="2" /> through <InlineMath math="4" /> with the cook directly below the painter**.

# Asked

The **number of distinct valid floor-and-hobby arrangements**.

# Solution

> Count the two adjacent floor slots for the cook-and-painter pair and then check how many valid name assignments fit each slot.

Umar is certainly on floor 1, gardening. Rani, Sita, and Tika take floors 2, 3, and 4 with cooking, painting, and photography. The cook and painter must be on **two adjacent floors** with the cook below, floors 2 and 3 or floors 3 and 4. Split the cases by **who photographs**, since Tika cannot.

**Sita photographs**. As in the previous question, Sita is on floor 2, and Rani and Tika are on floors 3 and 4 with the cook below the painter. That is **<InlineMath math="2" />** arrangements.

**Rani photographs**. Sita and Tika are the cook and painter on two adjacent floors. Rani must be higher than Sita.

- If Sita and Tika are on floors 3 and 4, Rani is on floor 2, lower than Sita. That breaks fact 2.
- So Sita and Tika are on floors 2 and 3, and Rani is on floor 4. The cook on floor 2 and the painter on floor 3 can be Sita and Tika in two ways. That is **<InlineMath math="2" />** arrangements.

The chart below counts the valid arrangements by who photographs. **Tika never photographs**, and the other two bars add up to <InlineMath math="4" />.

# Answer

The total is <InlineMath math="2+2=4" /> arrangements.

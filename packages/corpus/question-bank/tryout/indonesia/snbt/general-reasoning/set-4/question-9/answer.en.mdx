A parallel-reasoning question asks for the same flaw, not the same topic. So first pin down exactly what's wrong with the cooperative head's reasoning, then look for the option whose mistake has the same shape.

Notice when each thing was measured. The farmers are split by their situation **right now**, insured or not. Their crop failures are counted over **the past five years**. That means many of those failures could have happened before the farmer took out insurance. In fact, a failed harvest is a very natural reason for a farmer to sign up, so the next loss is covered.

So the cooperative head reads the cause and effect the wrong way round. He concludes that insurance causes crop failure, but the data fit the opposite direction just as well: crop failure pushes farmers to buy insurance. The mistake happens because the "cause" is measured now while the "effect" is measured in the past.

Now look for the option with the same two features: the groups are split by their current situation, and the events being counted happened in the past, so they could have triggered that current situation. The option about fire extinguishers fits:

- Houses are split by whether they **now** keep a fire extinguisher.
- Small fires are counted over the past five years.
- A house that has had a fire would naturally buy an extinguisher afterwards. So the fire could be the reason the extinguisher is there, not the other way round, just like crop failure and insurance.

Here's why the other options aren't parallel:

- With the improved seed, the seed came first and the harvest was recorded afterwards, so the time order is right. The topic is farming too, but its weakness, if any, is that farmers who chose the seed may differ in other ways, not a reversed direction of cause.
- With umbrellas and buses, both are caused by a third thing happening at the same time, the rain. That also treats a link as cause and effect, but it's a shared cause, not an effect that triggered its own "cause". The telltale feature of the cooperative head's reasoning, events counted before the situation blamed for them, is missing here.
- With the coffee reviews, the mistake is a sample that's too small and unrepresentative, since only three of about two hundred daily customers wrote reviews.
- With the glass plates, two changes happened in the same month and all the credit goes to one of them. That's ignoring another change that happened at the same time.

So the reasoning with the most similar flaw is the neighborhood head's conclusion that fire extinguishers make fires more frequent, because there too a past event may be what caused the present situation.

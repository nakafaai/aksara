#### Establish the sample space

Each shelf contains <InlineMath math="12" /> books, so there are:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="12\times12=144" />
</MathContainer>

equally likely ordered pairs, with the Shelf A book chosen first and the Shelf B book chosen second.

#### Compare the listed outcomes

| Outcome | Favorable pairs | Probability |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Two fiction books | <InlineMath math="5\times3=15" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{15}{144}" /> |
| One fiction and one science book | <InlineMath math="5\times3+3\times3=24" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{24}{144}" /> |
| One science and one history book | <InlineMath math="3\times6+4\times3=30" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{30}{144}" /> |
| Two history books | <InlineMath math="4\times6=24" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{24}{144}" /> |
| Two science books | <InlineMath math="3\times3=9" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{9}{144}" /> |

All probabilities have the same denominator, so compare their numerators. The smallest numerator is <InlineMath math="9" />. Therefore, **selecting two science books** has the smallest probability.

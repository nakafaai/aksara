The question asks for a **valid** conclusion, one that's true in every situation that meets all three statements. The word *sebagian* means at least one.

Start from the only statement that promises a plant actually exists, statement 2. There's at least one plant, call it plant X, that's watered every day and has broad leaves. Now follow plant X.

1. X has broad leaves, so by statement 3, X gets liquid organic fertilizer.
2. X is watered every day. Statement 1 says no plant in the shaded plot is watered every day, so X isn't in the shaded plot.
3. Put the two together: X gets liquid organic fertilizer and isn't in the shaded plot.

A plant like X must exist, so the conclusion "some plants that get liquid organic fertilizer aren't in the shaded plot" is valid.

The other options aren't valid. To reject one, a single situation that meets all three statements but makes that conclusion false is enough. The situation below has three plants.

| Plant | In the shaded plot | Watered every day | Broad leaves | Gets liquid organic fertilizer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| X | no | yes | yes | yes |
| Y | yes | no | yes | yes |
| Z | no | no | no | yes |

This situation meets all three statements. The only plant in the shaded plot, Y, isn't watered every day. X is watered every day and has broad leaves. Every broad-leaved plant, X and Y, gets liquid organic fertilizer.

- No broad-leaved plant in the shaded plot is false because of Y. Statement 2 only talks about some broad-leaved plants, the ones watered every day. You land on this option if you read *sebagian* ("some") as "all".
- Some plants in the shaded plot not getting liquid organic fertilizer is false here, because the only plant in the shaded plot, Y, gets fertilizer. This option swaps the subject and the predicate of the valid conclusion, and a "some ... are not ..." statement can't be swapped like that.
- Some broad-leaved plants not being watered every day can be false. Delete Y from the table. The situation still meets all three statements, and the only broad-leaved plant, X, is watered every day. Remember that "some are watered" doesn't mean "some aren't watered".
- All plants that get liquid organic fertilizer having broad leaves is false because of Z. Statement 3 works in one direction only: broad leaves guarantee fertilizer, but fertilizer doesn't guarantee broad leaves.

So the valid conclusion is that some plants that get liquid organic fertilizer aren't in the shaded plot.

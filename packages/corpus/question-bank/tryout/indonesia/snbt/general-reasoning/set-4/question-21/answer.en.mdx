# Given

A recursive sequence with constant subtraction <InlineMath math="k" /> satisfies **<InlineMath math="U_1 = 6" />, <InlineMath math="U_4 = 22" />, and <InlineMath math="U_6 = 52" />**.

# Asked

The value of the **eighth term** of the sequence.

# Solution

> Express the fourth and sixth terms using the two missing numbers, solve that pair of equations, and step forward to the eighth term.

The question gives you only three terms, and they're far apart: <InlineMath math="U_1=6" />, <InlineMath math="U_4=22" />, and <InlineMath math="U_6=52" />. Two values are still unknown, the second term and the number <InlineMath math="k" /> that gets subtracted. Two unknowns need **two equations**, one from <InlineMath math="U_4" /> and one from <InlineMath math="U_6" />.

Call the second term <InlineMath math="U_2=x" />. Build the first terms by adding the two previous terms and subtracting <InlineMath math="k" />. Remember that <InlineMath math="k" /> is subtracted again at every step:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
U_3 &= U_2+U_1-k = x+6-k \\
U_4 &= U_3+U_2-k = (x+6-k)+x-k = 2x+6-2k
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="U_4=22" />, you get <InlineMath math="x - k = 8" />. That gives you the third term right away as <InlineMath math="14" />.

Now carry on to the fifth and sixth terms with <InlineMath math="k" /> still unknown:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
U_5 &= U_4+U_3-k = 22+14-k = 36-k \\
U_6 &= U_5+U_4-k = (36-k)+22-k = 58-2k
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="U_6=52" />, you get <InlineMath math="58-2k=52" />, so <InlineMath math="k=3" />. From <InlineMath math="x-k=8" />, the second term is <InlineMath math="x=11" />.

Once you know <InlineMath math="k" />, count forward to the eighth term:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
U_5 &= 36-3 = 33 \\
U_7 &= U_6+U_5-3 = 52+33-3 = 82 \\
U_8 &= U_7+U_6-3 = 82+52-3 = 131
\end{aligned}" />

Check the whole sequence from the start: <InlineMath math="6, 11, 14, 22, 33, 52, 82, 131" />. Each term really is the sum of the two before it minus <InlineMath math="3" />, for example <InlineMath math="33" /> from <InlineMath math="14" /> and <InlineMath math="22" />. The first, fourth, and sixth terms also match the given values.

A few wrong answers come up often:

- <InlineMath math="82" /> is the seventh term. You stopped one step too early.
- <InlineMath math="134" /> comes from forgetting to subtract <InlineMath math="k" /> in the last step, which gives <InlineMath math="82+52=134" />.
- If you treat <InlineMath math="k" /> as zero, like a plain Fibonacci sequence, the fourth term <InlineMath math="22" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=8" />. With <InlineMath math="x=8" />, the sixth term would be <InlineMath math="58" />, not <InlineMath math="52" />. So <InlineMath math="k" /> can't be zero.

# Answer

So the eighth term of the sequence is <InlineMath math="131" />.

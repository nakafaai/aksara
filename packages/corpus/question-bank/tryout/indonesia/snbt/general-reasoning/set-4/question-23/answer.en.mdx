The combined pass rate is **total passes divided by total exam takers**, not the average of four percentages. So you need two steps: find the number of exam takers in each district, then add them up.

Step 1. Exam takers are the participants who met the attendance rule, so multiply the third-column percentage by the number registered.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P &: 75\% \times 80 = 60 \\ Q &: 90\% \times 200 = 180 \\ R &: 80\% \times 160 = 128 \\ S &: 60\% \times 100 = 60 \end{aligned}" />

Step 2. Add up the passes and the exam takers, then divide.

<BlockMath math="\frac{51 + 117 + 96 + 57}{60 + 180 + 128 + 60} = \frac{321}{428} = 75.0\%" />

Check: <InlineMath math="75\% \times 428 = 321" />, exactly the number who passed.

The other options come from the wrong denominator or the wrong kind of average.

- <InlineMath math="80.0\%" /> is the plain average of the four district pass rates (<InlineMath math="85\%" />, <InlineMath math="65\%" />, <InlineMath math="75\%" />, <InlineMath math="95\%" />). A plain average gives districts P and S, with only <InlineMath math="60" /> exam takers each, the same weight as Q with <InlineMath math="180" />.
- <InlineMath math="79.3\%" /> is <InlineMath math="428 : 540" />, the share of registered participants who met the attendance rule, not a pass rate.
- <InlineMath math="76.5\%" /> comes from weighting each district's pass rate by registered participants, but a pass rate's denominator is exam takers.
- <InlineMath math="59.4\%" /> is <InlineMath math="321 : 540" />, passes divided by everyone registered.

So the combined pass rate of the four districts is <InlineMath math="75.0\%" />.

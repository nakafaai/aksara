# Given

Ani is on duty on **Monday, <InlineMath math="3" /> August**, the roster advances only on open days, and the lone holiday is **Monday, <InlineMath math="17" /> August**.

# Asked

Who is on duty on **Friday, <InlineMath math="28" /> August**.

# Solution

> Drop every closed day and the public holiday from the calendar span, then use the remainder in the rotation cycle to land on the right name.

The rota only moves on **open days**, so you need the number of open days from <InlineMath math="3" /> August to <InlineMath math="28" /> August, not the number of calendar days.

Step 1. Count calendar days. From <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="28" /> August there are <InlineMath math="26" /> days, counting both ends.

Step 2. Take away the closed days. Since <InlineMath math="3" /> August is a Monday, the Sundays in that range fall on <InlineMath math="9" />, <InlineMath math="16" /> and <InlineMath math="23" /> August. Add the holiday on <InlineMath math="17" /> August and there are <InlineMath math="4" /> closed days.

<BlockMath math="26 - 4 = 22 \text{ open days}" />

Step 3. Find the person. Ani works on open day <InlineMath math="1" />, so the person on open day <InlineMath math="n" /> is set by the remainder of <InlineMath math="n - 1" /> divided by <InlineMath math="5" />.

<BlockMath math="22 - 1 = 21 = 4 \times 5 + 1" />

A remainder of <InlineMath math="1" /> means one step after Ani, which is Bima.

The other answers come from a missed closed day or a misread rule.

- Citra comes from forgetting the holiday on <InlineMath math="17" /> August, which makes <InlineMath math="23" /> open days.
- Eka comes from not taking away the Sundays, which makes <InlineMath math="25" /> open days.
- Ani comes from counting the rota over all <InlineMath math="26" /> calendar days.
- Dodi comes from treating Saturdays as closed too, but the library only closes on Sundays and national holidays.

# Answer

So the person on duty on Friday, **<InlineMath math="28" />** August is Bima.

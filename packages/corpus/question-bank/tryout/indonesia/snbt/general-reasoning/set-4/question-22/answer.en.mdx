# Given

Store P raises the initial price by <InlineMath math="40\%" /> before discounting the marked-up price, while **Store Q cuts <InlineMath math="20\%" /> off the initial price**.

# Asked

The percentage **cut from Store Q current price** needed to match Store P.

# Solution

> Set the starting price to one hundred so every percentage based on a different price turns into plain, easy numbers.

This question uses three different bases for its percentages. The <InlineMath math="40\%" /> rise is taken from the starting price, store P's discount from the raised price, and store Q's discount from the starting price. The cut you're asked for is taken from **the price store Q is charging now**, because that's the price being lowered.

Both stores start at the same price, so say the starting price is <InlineMath math="100" />. It's only a reference, so the percentage you get works for any starting price.

Work out store P's price first. After the <InlineMath math="40\%" /> rise it's <InlineMath math="140" />. The discount is <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of <InlineMath math="140" />:

<BlockMath math="\text{Store P}: \quad 140 - 0.4\times140 = 140-56 = 84" />

Now store Q's price. It also rises to <InlineMath math="140" />, but its discount is <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of the starting price <InlineMath math="100" />, which is <InlineMath math="20" />:

<BlockMath math="\text{Store Q}: \quad 140 - 0.2\times100 = 140-20 = 120" />

The chart below puts the four prices side by side. **Store Q's bar is still far above store P's**, even though both started from the same raised price.

Store Q has to go from <InlineMath math="120" /> down to <InlineMath math="84" />. Compare the gap with store Q's current price:

<BlockMath math="\frac{120-84}{120}\times100\% = \frac{36}{120}\times100\% = 30\%" />

Check it: <InlineMath math="120" /> minus <InlineMath math="30\%" /> of <InlineMath math="120" /> is <InlineMath math="120-36=84" />, exactly store P's price.

Each other option comes from mixing up a base:

- <InlineMath math="20\%" /> comes from thinking that a <InlineMath math="40\%" /> rise and then a <InlineMath math="40\%" /> cut brings store P back to the starting price, or from simply subtracting <InlineMath math="40\%-20\%" />. Store P's cut is taken from <InlineMath math="140" />, so its price actually ends at <InlineMath math="84" />.
- <InlineMath math="25\%" /> comes from taking store Q's discount from <InlineMath math="140" />, which makes Q's price <InlineMath math="112" /> and the cut <InlineMath math="28/112" />. The question says store Q's discount is taken from the starting price.
- <InlineMath math="28\%" /> comes from the same slip plus one more: Q's price is taken as <InlineMath math="112" />, and then the gap of <InlineMath math="28" /> is read against the starting price <InlineMath math="100" />.
- <InlineMath math="36\%" /> uses the right prices, but compares the gap of <InlineMath math="36" /> with the starting price <InlineMath math="100" /> instead of store Q's current price.

# Answer

So store Q has to cut its current price by <InlineMath math="30\%" />.

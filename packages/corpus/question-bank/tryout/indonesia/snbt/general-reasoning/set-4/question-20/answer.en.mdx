The question asks for a statement that's true in **every order that meets all four facts**. The key is fact 4, an "if ..., then ..." sentence whose "then" part turns out to be impossible. Here *lompatan* means each athlete's best jump.

First join facts 1, 2 and 3. Writing <InlineMath math="A > B" /> means that <InlineMath math="A" /> jumped farther than <InlineMath math="B" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Nadia} &> \text{Jihan} > \text{Gilang} > \text{Hesti} \\ \text{Irfan} &> \text{Gilang} \\ \text{Lukas} &> \text{Gilang} \end{aligned}" />

The first row shows that Hesti's jump is shorter than Nadia's. So the "then" part of fact 4, "Hesti jumped farther than Nadia", can't happen.

A true sentence "if P, then Q" never has P happening while Q doesn't. Here Q surely doesn't happen, so P can't happen either. That means Lukas didn't jump farther than Irfan. In other words, Irfan jumped farther than Lukas.

Now count who must be below Irfan: Gilang and Hesti (fact 1), then Lukas (the step above). At least three athletes jumped shorter than Irfan, so Irfan is at worst in place 3.

To check, list every order. Gilang and Hesti always take places 5 and 6, because the other four athletes all jumped farther than Gilang. Places 1 to 4 go to Irfan, Lukas, Nadia and Jihan, with Irfan above Lukas and Nadia above Jihan. That gives six orders, written from the longest jump.

| Order | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | Irfan | Lukas | Nadia | Jihan | Gilang | Hesti |
| II | Irfan | Nadia | Lukas | Jihan | Gilang | Hesti |
| III | Irfan | Nadia | Jihan | Lukas | Gilang | Hesti |
| IV | Nadia | Irfan | Lukas | Jihan | Gilang | Hesti |
| V | Nadia | Irfan | Jihan | Lukas | Gilang | Hesti |
| VI | Nadia | Jihan | Irfan | Lukas | Gilang | Hesti |

Irfan really is in place 1, 2 or 3 in all six orders. The other statements hold in some orders only.

- Irfan jumping farthest fails in orders IV, V and VI. The step above only puts Irfan above Lukas, not above Nadia.
- Lukas jumping shorter than Nadia fails in order I. Fact 4 only ties Lukas to Irfan, not to Nadia.
- Nadia jumping farthest fails in orders I, II and III. Nadia's chain is the longest, but Irfan is never compared with Nadia directly.
- Jihan being in the top three fails in orders I, II and IV. Jihan is only sure to be below Nadia, but Irfan and Lukas can also be above her.

So the statement that must be true is that Irfan's jump is one of the three longest.

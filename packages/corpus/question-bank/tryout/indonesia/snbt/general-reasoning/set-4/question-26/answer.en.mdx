# Given

Rina buys positive counts of notebooks and pens, with statement **(1) total cost** and statement **(2) <InlineMath math="8" /> items with a bill divisible by <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}8{,}000" />**.

# Asked

Whether statements **(1)** and **(2)** are sufficient to answer the question.

# Solution

> Test each statement on its own first, because a single clue is already sufficient whenever it leaves only one valid whole-number pair.

Say Rina buys <InlineMath math="x" /> notebooks and <InlineMath math="y" /> pens, with <InlineMath math="x \ge 1" /> and <InlineMath math="y \ge 1" />. Test **each statement on its own** first, and combine them only if you need to.

Statement (1) alone. The total is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}40{,}000" />, so dividing both sides by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> gives:

<BlockMath math="6x + 4y = 40 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 3x + 2y = 20" />

For <InlineMath math="2y = 20 - 3x" /> to be even and positive, <InlineMath math="x" /> must be even and less than <InlineMath math="7" />. The possible pairs are <InlineMath math="(x, y) = (2, 7)" />, <InlineMath math="(4, 4)" /> and <InlineMath math="(6, 1)" />. That's three possibilities, so statement (1) alone isn't enough.

Statement (2) alone. Rina buys <InlineMath math="8" /> items, so <InlineMath math="y = 8 - x" /> with <InlineMath math="1 \le x \le 7" />. After dividing by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" />, the total is

<BlockMath math="6x + 4(8 - x) = 32 + 2x" />

This total must be divisible by <InlineMath math="8" />. Try every <InlineMath math="x" /> from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="7" />: the totals are <InlineMath math="34, 36, 38, 40, 42, 44, 46" />. Only <InlineMath math="40" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="8" />, when <InlineMath math="x = 4" />. So statement (2) alone **is already enough**: Rina buys <InlineMath math="4" /> notebooks.

The other options come from stopping too early.

- Statement (1) alone is enough: this stops at the first pair found, but there are three pairs.
- Both statements together are enough: combining them does give <InlineMath math="x = 4" />, but statement (2) already settles it without statement (1).
- Each statement alone is enough: this forgets that statement (1) leaves three possibilities.
- Neither statement is enough: this assumes statement (2) gives no total, but the divisibility condition leaves only one value.

# Answer

So statement (2) alone is enough to answer the question, but statement (1) alone isn't.

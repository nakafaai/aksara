Start with **the pair Q and R**, because together they need a whole day. Saturday is already used by P, so Q and R perform on Friday or Sunday. The remaining day, other than Saturday and the Q and R day, goes to two other performers.

Now test whether S can perform on Saturday. If S takes Saturday evening, P takes Saturday afternoon and Saturday is full. Only one day is left for T and U, so they would perform on the same day. That breaks condition 4, since T must perform on an earlier day than U. So **S cannot perform on Saturday**.

In the same way, all possible schedules can be built. S performs in the evening on Friday or Sunday, while T and U take one place on Saturday and one on another day, with T earlier:

| Group | Friday | Saturday | Sunday |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Q and R | P and T | U afternoon, S evening |
| B | T afternoon, S evening | P and U | Q and R |

In group A, T performs on Saturday and U on Sunday. In group B, T performs on Friday and U on Saturday. In each group, Q and R can swap sessions, and so can P with T or U, giving <InlineMath math="8" /> schedules.

The other statements are not certain:

- T performs on Saturday only in group A.
- Q performs in the afternoon in some schedules and in the evening in others.
- P can perform in the afternoon or the evening.
- U performs on Sunday only in group A.

So the statement that must be true is that S does not perform on Saturday.

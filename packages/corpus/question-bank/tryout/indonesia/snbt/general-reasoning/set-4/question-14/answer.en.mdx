#### Compare relative decreases

A large price drop is not necessarily the largest percentage drop. For every option, divide the decrease by its starting price:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{percentage decrease}=\frac{\text{starting price}-\text{ending price}}{\text{starting price}}\times100\%" />
</MathContainer>

| Option | Calculation | Percentage decrease |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Noodle A, <InlineMath math="2019" /> to <InlineMath math="2020" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{3350-3000}{3350}\times100\%" /> | <InlineMath math="10.4\%" /> |
| Noodle B, <InlineMath math="2017" /> to <InlineMath math="2018" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{4500-4250}{4500}\times100\%" /> | <InlineMath math="5.6\%" /> |
| Noodle B, <InlineMath math="2018" /> to <InlineMath math="2019" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{4250-4000}{4250}\times100\%" /> | <InlineMath math="5.9\%" /> |
| Noodle C, <InlineMath math="2019" /> to <InlineMath math="2020" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{2800-2650}{2800}\times100\%" /> | <InlineMath math="5.4\%" /> |
| Noodle A, <InlineMath math="2017" /> to <InlineMath math="2018" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{3500-2500}{3500}\times100\%" /> | <InlineMath math="28.6\%" /> |

The largest value is <InlineMath math="28.6\%" />. Therefore, **Noodle A from <InlineMath math="2017" /> to <InlineMath math="2018" />** had the highest percentage decrease.

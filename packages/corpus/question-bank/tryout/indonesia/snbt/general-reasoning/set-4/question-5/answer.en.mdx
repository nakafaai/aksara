All four statements are about totals and shares, but the table only gives an average for each group. The key to every one is **multiplying the average by the number of traders** in that group to get a total, because the group sizes change between April and July. If you compare only the averages, you can get all four verdicts backwards.

First work out the total daily turnover of each group, in millions of rupiah:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{April, users: } & 30 \times 2.0 = 60 \\ \text{April, non-users: } & 90 \times 1.2 = 108 \\ \text{April, total: } & 60 + 108 = 168 \\ \text{July, users: } & 60 \times 1.8 = 108 \\ \text{July, non-users: } & 60 \times 1.0 = 60 \\ \text{July, total: } & 108 + 60 = 168 \end{aligned}" />

Statement 1 is false (*Salah*). The April average over all traders is the total divided by the number of traders, <InlineMath math="\frac{168}{120}=1.4" />, so <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1.4\text{ million}" />. The figure <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1.6\text{ million}" /> comes from simply averaging <InlineMath math="2.0" /> and <InlineMath math="1.2" />, even though there are three times as many non-users as users.

Statement 2 is false. The April total and the July total are both <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}168\text{ million}" />. Both group averages did fall, but that doesn't mean the total fell, because <InlineMath math="30" /> traders moved from the non-user group into the user group.

Statement 3 is true (*Benar*). The users' combined daily turnover rose from <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}60\text{ million}" /> to <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}108\text{ million}" />. The ratio is <InlineMath math="\frac{108}{60}=1.8" />, which is more than <InlineMath math="1.5" />. The users' average did drop, but the number of users doubled.

Statement 4 is true. The non-users' share in April is <InlineMath math="\frac{108}{168}\approx 64.3\%" />, more than <InlineMath math="60\%" />. If you compare only the averages, <InlineMath math="\frac{1.2}{2.0+1.2}=37.5\%" />, you forget that there were <InlineMath math="90" /> non-users and only <InlineMath math="30" /> users.

So statements 1 and 2 are false, while statements 3 and 4 are true.

# Given

The April and July table lists **average daily revenue and vendor counts** for digital-wallet users and non-users.

# Asked

Whether each of the four statements is **True** or **False** based on the passage and table.

# Solution

> Whenever group sizes change from month to month, multiply each average by its vendor count first so your totals stay exact.

All four statements are about totals and shares, but the table only gives an average for each group. The key to every one is **multiplying the average by the number of traders** in that group to get a total, because the group sizes change between April and July. If you compare only the averages, you can get all four verdicts backwards.

First work out the total daily turnover of each group, in millions of rupiah:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{April, users: } & 30 \times 2.0 = 60 \\ \text{April, non-users: } & 90 \times 1.2 = 108 \\ \text{April, total: } & 60 + 108 = 168 \\ \text{July, users: } & 60 \times 1.8 = 108 \\ \text{July, non-users: } & 60 \times 1.0 = 60 \\ \text{July, total: } & 108 + 60 = 168 \end{aligned}" />

The chart below draws the total daily turnover of both groups. **The two groups swap bar heights, but the sum stays at <InlineMath math="168" /> million**.

Statement 1 is false (*Salah*). The April average over all traders is the total divided by the number of traders, <InlineMath math="1.4" />, so <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1.4\text{ million}" />. The figure <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1.6\text{ million}" /> comes from simply averaging <InlineMath math="2.0" /> and <InlineMath math="1.2" />, even though there are three times as many non-users as users.

Statement 2 is false. The April total and the July total are both <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}168\text{ million}" />. Both group averages did fall, but that doesn't mean the total fell, because <InlineMath math="30" /> traders moved from the non-user group into the user group.

Statement 3 is true (*Benar*). The users' combined daily turnover rose from <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}60\text{ million}" /> to <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}108\text{ million}" />. The ratio is <InlineMath math="1.8" />, which is more than <InlineMath math="1.5" />. The users' average did drop, but the number of users doubled.

Statement 4 is true. The non-users' share in April is about <InlineMath math="64.3\%" />, more than <InlineMath math="60\%" />. If you compare only the averages, giving <InlineMath math="37.5\%" />, you forget that there were <InlineMath math="90" /> non-users and only <InlineMath math="30" /> users.

# Answer

So statements 1 and 2 are **false, while statements 3 and 4 are true**.

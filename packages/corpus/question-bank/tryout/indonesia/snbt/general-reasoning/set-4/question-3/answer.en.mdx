For each statement, ask two things: are the data in the reading enough to settle it, and if they are, does the calculation actually come out true? There are two traps. The leader's table groups the plots by their state at harvest, so you have to move the **four plots that lost their ducks** back to their starting group. On top of that, some statements need a comparison group that the data simply don't have.

Set up the numbers first. Ducks were released on <InlineMath math="20" /> plots at the start, but only <InlineMath math="16" /> still had ducks at harvest, so <InlineMath math="4" /> plots had their ducks taken out. Ducks were only taken out for spraying, so those four plots were sprayed and are listed in the "no ducks" group together with the <InlineMath math="20" /> plots that never had ducks.

Statement 1 can be concluded (*dapat disimpulkan*). There are <InlineMath math="12" /> sprayed plots, and <InlineMath math="4" /> of them started with ducks. That gives <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{12}=\frac{1}{3}" />. If you only look at the <InlineMath math="0" /> in the duck-plot row, you'd think there were none.

Statement 2 can't be concluded (*tidak dapat disimpulkan*). The reading only covers the 2025 dry season. There are no spraying figures from the season before, so there's no comparison group for "less often than before".

Statement 3 can't be concluded. Under the group's rule, every plot that crossed the threshold had to be sprayed. So not a single plot crossed the threshold and was left unsprayed. Without that comparison group, the data can't tell you what the harvest would have been without spraying.

Statement 4 can be concluded. The <InlineMath math="5.0" /> in the table is the average of <InlineMath math="24" /> plots, including the four plots that lost their ducks, which averaged <InlineMath math="4.5" />. Take those four plots out of the total:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{total yield of 24 plots} &= 24 \times 5.0 = 120 \\ \text{total yield of the 4 plots that lost their ducks} &= 4 \times 4.5 = 18 \\ \text{average of the 20 never-duck plots} &= \frac{120-18}{20} = \frac{102}{20} = 5.1 \end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="5.1 > 5.0" />, the statement is certainly true. The average goes up once the four low-yield plots are removed, which you'd miss if you just read the <InlineMath math="5.0" /> off the table.

So statements 1 and 4 can be concluded, while statements 2 and 3 can't.

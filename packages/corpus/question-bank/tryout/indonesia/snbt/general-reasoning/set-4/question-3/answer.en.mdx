The first premise applies to **every dish**. The second guarantees at least one dish without raw vegetables. Choose one such dish. Since it belongs to the menu, it must also taste sour and spicy.

Therefore, at least one dish contains no raw vegetables and tastes sour and spicy. Compare the scope of this conclusion with the other choices:

| Other claim | Why it does not follow |
| --- | --- |
| Some dish is not both sour and spicy | Contradicts the first premise |
| Some dish is neither sour nor spicy | Also contradicts the first premise |
| Every dish contains raw vegetables | Contradicts the existence of a dish without raw vegetables |
| No dish contains raw vegetables | The second premise guarantees at least one, not the entire menu |

For example, a menu could contain two sour and spicy dishes, one with raw vegetables and one without. Both premises would still hold. This example shows why **at least one** cannot be replaced with **all**.

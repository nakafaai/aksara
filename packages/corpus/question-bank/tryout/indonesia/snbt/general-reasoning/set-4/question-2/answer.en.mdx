Box <InlineMath math="1" /> contains <InlineMath math="16" /> toys and Box <InlineMath math="2" /> contains <InlineMath math="15" /> toys. Every ordered draw therefore has the same denominator:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="16\times15=240" />
</MathContainer>

For a pair with two different toy types, both box assignments count. For example, "ball and Barbie doll" includes a ball from Box <InlineMath math="1" /> with a Barbie doll from Box <InlineMath math="2" />, and the reverse assignment.

#### Compare the Five Events

| Pair | Favorable draws | Probability |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Two teddy bears | <InlineMath math="3\times4=12" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{240}" /> |
| Two marbles | <InlineMath math="4\times4=16" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{16}{240}" /> |
| One ball and one Barbie doll | <InlineMath math="5\times3+4\times4=31" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{31}{240}" /> |
| One Barbie doll and one marble | <InlineMath math="4\times4+4\times3=28" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{28}{240}" /> |
| One teddy bear and one ball | <InlineMath math="3\times4+5\times4=32" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{32}{240}" /> |

All denominators are equal, so the largest numerator determines the most likely pair. Since <InlineMath math="32" /> is the largest, the answer is **one teddy bear and one ball**.

The conclusion about blue light rests on the filter experiment: once blue light was filtered, fewer insects were caught. The experiment is strong only if the filter **changes nothing but the blue light**.

If the filter also halves the brightness, the drop in insects could come from a dimmer lamp rather than from the missing blue light. Two causes are mixed, so the conclusion weakens.

The other options do not weaken it:

- Installation cost has nothing to do with why insects are attracted.
- Replacing the traps on the same schedule makes the comparison fairer, so it supports the study.
- The kind of insect caught does not challenge the role of blue light.
- Electricity savings are unrelated to insect attraction.

So the statement that weakens the conclusion most is that the filter also cuts the lamp's brightness roughly in half.

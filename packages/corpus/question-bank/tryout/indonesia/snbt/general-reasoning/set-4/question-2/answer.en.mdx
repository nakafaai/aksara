The duck owners make a cause-and-effect claim: ducks keep the brown planthoppers (*wereng*) down. Their evidence is a single comparison, namely that plots with ducks crossed the spraying threshold less often than plots without ducks. The data do back that comparison. Of the <InlineMath math="20" /> plots with ducks from the start, only <InlineMath math="20-16=4" /> had their ducks taken out for spraying. Of the <InlineMath math="20" /> plots without ducks from the start, <InlineMath math="12-4=8" /> were sprayed. That's <InlineMath math="20\%" /> against <InlineMath math="40\%" />.

The strongest way to weaken a claim like this is to show that the gap could appear **without the ducks doing anything at all**. You need a third factor that meets two conditions. First, it differs between the two groups of plots. Second, it raises the planthopper risk on its own, with or without ducks.

The statement about planting time meets both:

- The first condition holds because every duck plot was planted early, while most plots without ducks were planted three weeks later.
- The second condition holds because even in seasons before anyone used ducks, late-planted plots crossed the threshold more often. So planting time matters even when there are no ducks.

With those two facts, the plots without ducks are expected to cross the threshold more often simply because they were planted later. The gap between <InlineMath math="20\%" /> and <InlineMath math="40\%" /> can be explained entirely by planting time, so the data no longer show that the ducks kept the planthoppers down.

The other options touch the same topic but don't cut that causal link:

- The crop check only shows that planthoppers are a small part of what the ducks eat. A small part of a duck's food can still be a lot of planthoppers, and this statement gives no other cause for the gap between the groups.
- The peak count of <InlineMath math="7" /> per clump isn't compared with anything. Without the counts from plots without ducks, you can't tell whether that number is higher or much lower than on plots without ducks.
- Using the same counting method in both groups actually rules out another explanation, a difference in how the pests were measured. That statement strengthens the owners' view instead of weakening it.
- Fear of duck theft and having no time to herd ducks are reasons for the choice, but neither has anything to do with planthopper risk. It doesn't show that the two groups differ in anything that affects the pests.

So the statement that weakens the claim most is that the duck plots were planted earlier, while late-planted plots have always crossed the planthopper threshold more often, even without ducks.

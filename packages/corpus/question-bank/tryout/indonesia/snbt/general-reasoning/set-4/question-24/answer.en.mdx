# Given

The passage provides **registered entrants, exam takers, and passing candidates** across districts P, Q, R, and S.

# Asked

Whether each of the four statements is **True** or **False** based on the passage.

# Solution

> Keep registered entrants and actual exam takers strictly separate so you always divide by the base each statement names.

The key here is **picking the right denominator** for each statement. A pass rate divides by exam takers, while "share of registered participants" divides by registered participants. First find the exam takers: P <InlineMath math="60" />, Q <InlineMath math="180" />, R <InlineMath math="128" />, S <InlineMath math="60" />.

| District | Passed : exam takers | Passed : registered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P | <InlineMath math="51 : 60 = 85\%" /> | <InlineMath math="51 : 80 = 63.75\%" /> |
| Q | <InlineMath math="117 : 180 = 65\%" /> | <InlineMath math="117 : 200 = 58.5\%" /> |
| R | <InlineMath math="96 : 128 = 75\%" /> | <InlineMath math="96 : 160 = 60\%" /> |
| S | <InlineMath math="57 : 60 = 95\%" /> | <InlineMath math="57 : 100 = 57\%" /> |

Statement 1 is Benar (true). Altogether <InlineMath math="321" /> of <InlineMath math="428" /> exam takers passed, which is <InlineMath math="75\%" />. The <InlineMath math="80\%" /> in the office's report is only the plain average of four percentages.

Statement 2 is Salah (false). S does have the highest pass rate, <InlineMath math="95\%" />, but that divides by exam takers. Compared with registered participants, S is actually the lowest at <InlineMath math="57\%" />, because only <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of its participants could sit the exam.

Statement 3 is Salah (false). Q has the highest attendance share (<InlineMath math="90\%" />), yet its pass rate is the lowest, <InlineMath math="65\%" />.

Statement 4 is Benar (true). District P passed <InlineMath math="51" /> of <InlineMath math="80" /> registered participants, which is <InlineMath math="63.75\%" />, more than <InlineMath math="60\%" />.

# Answer

So statements 1 and 4 are **true, and statements 2 and 3 are false**.

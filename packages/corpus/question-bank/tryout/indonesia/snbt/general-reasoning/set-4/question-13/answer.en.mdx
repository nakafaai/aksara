Rewrite the statement in "if ... then" form. "No late participant is allowed into the exam room" means **if a participant is late, that participant is not allowed in**:

<BlockMath math="\text{late} \Rightarrow \text{not allowed in}" />

An equivalent statement must hold in exactly the same situations. The contrapositive is equivalent to the original:

<BlockMath math="\text{allowed in} \Rightarrow \text{not late}" />

It reads: every participant allowed into the exam room is not late.

The other options are not equivalent:

- "Every participant who is not late is allowed in" is the inverse. The original does not guarantee that punctual participants are allowed in, since they might be refused for another reason.
- "Some late participant is allowed in" is the negation of the original.
- "Some late participants are not allowed in" is weaker. The original says **all** late participants are refused.
- "Everyone not allowed in must be late" is the converse, which is also not equivalent.

So the equivalent statement is "Every participant allowed into the exam room is not late".

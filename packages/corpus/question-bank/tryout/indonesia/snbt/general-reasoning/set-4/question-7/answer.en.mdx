We can prove the conclusion by considering **two possibilities** for the relationship between <InlineMath math="E" /> and <InlineMath math="F" />.

If <InlineMath math="E\neq F" />, the statement <InlineMath math="A=B\lor E\neq F" /> already holds, regardless of the relationship between <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />. Now consider the other possibility, <InlineMath math="E=F" />:

| Step | Result | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use the second premise | <InlineMath math="G=H" /> | This equality follows from <InlineMath math="E=F" /> |
| Use the third premise | <InlineMath math="C=D" /> | <InlineMath math="G\neq H" /> is false, so the other part of the or statement must be true |
| Use the first premise | <InlineMath math="A=B" /> | <InlineMath math="C\neq D" /> is false, so <InlineMath math="A=B" /> must be true |

In either possibility, at least one part of the conclusion is true. Therefore, the necessary conclusion is

<BlockMath math="A=B\lor E\neq F." />

Remember that **or does not mean and**. We have not established that both parts of the conclusion must be true together.

Introduce four propositions:

- <InlineMath math="p" /> means <InlineMath math="A=B" />.
- <InlineMath math="q" /> means <InlineMath math="C\neq D" />.
- <InlineMath math="r" /> means <InlineMath math="E=F" />.
- <InlineMath math="s" /> means <InlineMath math="G=H" />.

The premises become:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="p\lor q" />
  <BlockMath math="\neg r\lor s" />
  <BlockMath math="\neg q\lor\neg s" />
</MathContainer>

#### Eliminate the Middle Propositions

Resolve the first and third premises on <InlineMath math="q" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(p\lor q),(\neg q\lor\neg s)\Longrightarrow p\lor\neg s" />
</MathContainer>

Now resolve that result with the second premise on <InlineMath math="s" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(p\lor\neg s),(\neg r\lor s)\Longrightarrow p\lor\neg r" />
</MathContainer>

Returning to the original statements, <InlineMath math="p\lor\neg r" /> means **<InlineMath math="A=B" /> or <InlineMath math="E\neq F" />**.

The manager's conclusion compares sick leave **before and after** the plants arrived. Such a comparison supports the conclusion only if no other change explains the difference. Test each option by denying it.

Deny the option about seasons: "The drop in sick leave was mainly caused by the change of season." If that were true, for example because April to June fell in a transitional season when many people get ill, the drop would have nothing to do with the plants. The conclusion collapses, so this assumption is **necessary**.

Denying the other options does not break the conclusion:

- Plants can still improve health even if not every employee likes them.
- Different kinds of plants do not stop plants from helping.
- A survey not filled in by everyone concerns comfort, not the sick-leave data behind the health conclusion.
- Next year's plans do not affect whether the conclusion is true.

So the manager's conclusion needs the assumption that the drop in sick leave was not mainly caused by a change of season between the two periods.

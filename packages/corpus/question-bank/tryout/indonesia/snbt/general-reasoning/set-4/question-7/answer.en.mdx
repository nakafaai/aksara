To strengthen a cause-and-effect claim like this, first find the other explanations that could fit, then look for the statement that rules them out.

The reading gives you an important clue: all eight villages were picked because their 2024 theft count was **the highest in the regency**. A number picked right when it's at its peak often falls on its own the next year, even if nothing is done. That's because the highest count in a year is often partly luck, for example one gang of thieves happening to work that village that year. Luck like that doesn't necessarily repeat, so the count can drift back toward normal. There could also be a change across the whole regency, such as more frequent police patrols.

The strongest way to rule out both explanations is a comparison group that faces the same explanations but doesn't get the claimed cause. The three villages whose lamps often go dark after midnight fit perfectly:

- They were picked for the same reason, the highest counts in 2024, so their numbers could also fall on their own.
- They're in the same regency in the same year, so any regency-wide change affects them too.
- Many of their lamps go dark after midnight, and most night-time thefts happen between midnight and dawn. During the risky hours, many streets in those villages are dark.

If theft in those three villages barely changed, then the high counts didn't fall on their own and no general change brought theft down. The drop in the five villages whose lamps stay on until dawn is then best explained by the lamps.

Here's why the other options are weaker:

- Residents feeling safer is a feeling, not theft data. It doesn't rule out any other explanation for the lower count.
- Villages that got no lamps do rule out a regency-wide change. But they weren't picked for having their highest count in 2024, so this statement doesn't rule out that the five villages' high count simply fell back.
- Lamps don't work in the daytime. If daytime theft fell by almost the same percentage, some other cause is at work, so this statement actually weakens the conclusion.
- A few thieves' confessions only support the general idea that darkness invites theft. They don't show that the drop in these five villages came from the lamps rather than from the other explanations.

So the statement that strengthens the conclusion most is that in the three villages whose lamps went dark after midnight, the number of thefts in 2025 was almost the same as in 2024.

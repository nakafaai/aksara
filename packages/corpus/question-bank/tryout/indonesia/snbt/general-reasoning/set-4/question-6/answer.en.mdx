A weakening statement should **reduce the support supplied by a premise**, without having to prove the final policy wrong. The second premise says **only a small number** of the highest-achieving students can study at leading universities abroad. The choice saying **many** such students can already do so directly challenges that reason.

| Other choice | Relationship to the argument |
| --- | --- |
| The education allocation is growing | Consistent with the first premise |
| Many students can attend leading domestic universities | Concerns a different location from the second premise |
| Some students can study abroad | Still compatible with a small number |
| Part of the allocation is unspent | Repeats the first reason and supports the argument |

Thus, the choice about many highest-achieving students being able to study at leading universities abroad is the most direct weakener. It challenges the analyst’s description of educational access, rather than proving that scholarships have no value.

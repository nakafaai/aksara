A weakening statement should reduce the support supplied by a premise. It does not have to prove the final policy wrong.

The second premise says that **only a small number** of the highest-achieving students can study at leading universities abroad. The statement that **many** such students can already do so directly challenges that premise.

#### Why the Other Statements Do Not Weaken It as Directly

- A growing education allocation is consistent with the first premise.
- Access to leading domestic universities does not answer the claim about study abroad.
- "Some students" can study abroad is compatible with "only a small number".
- Incomplete use of the allocation repeats the first premise and therefore supports the argument.

The strongest weakener is therefore: **Many of Indonesia's highest-achieving students can study at leading universities abroad**.

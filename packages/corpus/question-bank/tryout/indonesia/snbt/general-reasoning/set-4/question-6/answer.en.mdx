The question gives you two facts that seem to clash. Both group averages fell, yet not a single trader's turnover went down. You need a situation that makes both true at once.

Start with the second fact. If no trader's turnover fell, a group's average can only fall because **the members of the group changed**. The only change in membership happened in May, when <InlineMath math="30" /> traders moved from the non-user group into the user group. The reading says no trader stopped, left or joined, so nothing else changed.

Now use a simple rule about averages. If a newcomer to a group is below that group's average, the average goes down. If the members leaving a group are above the average of those who stay, that group's average also goes down. Apply this to both groups:

- For the users' average to fall, the traders who joined in May must have lower turnover than the original users.
- For the non-users' average to fall, the traders who left in May must have higher turnover than the traders who still don't use the e-wallet.

Those two conditions are exactly the key: the May traders sit in the middle, below the original users but above the remaining non-users.

Check it with the table, in millions of rupiah. The April total is <InlineMath math="30(2.0)+90(1.2)=168" /> and the July total is <InlineMath math="60(1.8)+60(1.0)=168" />. The totals match and no one's turnover fell, so every trader's turnover stayed the same. That lets you work out the May traders' average:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{turnover of the 30 original users} &= 30 \times 2.0 = 60 \\ \text{turnover of the 60 users in July} &= 60 \times 1.8 = 108 \\ \text{average of the 30 new users} &= \frac{108-60}{30} = 1.6 \end{aligned}" />

The remaining non-users average <InlineMath math="1.0" />. So <InlineMath math="1.0 < 1.6 < 2.0" />, which fits the explanation.

Here's why the other options fall short:

- If the May traders had lower turnover than the remaining non-users, their leaving would raise the non-users' average. The first fact would stay unexplained.
- Turnover that hasn't had time to rise says nothing about the non-user group, and it doesn't say whether those traders are below the original users' average.
- Buyers switching means some non-users lost turnover. That contradicts the second fact.
- If the May traders had higher turnover than the original users, their joining would raise the users' average, not lower it.

So what makes both facts true is that the traders who started using the e-wallet in May had turnover in between: lower than the original users but higher than the traders who still don't use it.

#### Represent the premises

Let:

- <InlineMath math="p" />: Dito completes the prerequisite exercises.
- <InlineMath math="q" />: Dito takes the practice test.
- <InlineMath math="r" />: Dito receives an evaluation report.

The premises are <InlineMath math="p \rightarrow q" />, <InlineMath math="q \rightarrow r" />, and <InlineMath math="\lnot r" />.

#### Reason backward from the missing report

From <InlineMath math="q \rightarrow r" /> and <InlineMath math="\lnot r" />, modus tollens gives <InlineMath math="\lnot q" />. Dito therefore did not take the practice test.

Now combine <InlineMath math="p \rightarrow q" /> with <InlineMath math="\lnot q" />. A second application of modus tollens gives:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}p &\rightarrow q\\ \lnot q&\\ \therefore\ \lnot p&\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, Dito **did not complete the prerequisite exercises**. The premises do not say that he is not a student; they only determine what he did not complete.

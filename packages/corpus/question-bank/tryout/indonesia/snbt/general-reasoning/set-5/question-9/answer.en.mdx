The mayor of City Q is **reasoning by analogy**: what worked in City P is expected to work in City Q. An analogy is strong when the two cities are alike in what **decides the outcome**, here the cause of congestion that a flyover removes.

A flyover helps vehicles going straight through avoid stopping at the red light. If congestion in City Q, as in City P, comes mainly from such vehicles, the flyover will solve the same problem. This similarity is the most relevant.

The other options do not strengthen it:

- City parks are a similarity unrelated to congestion.
- Construction cost does not show whether travel time in City Q will fall.
- Vehicles turning into the market at the corner are not helped by a flyover. This statement weakens the mayor's view.
- The drop in City P's travel time is already given and adds no similarity between the cities.

So the information that strengthens the view most is that, as in City P, congestion at City Q's busiest junction comes mainly from vehicles going straight through.

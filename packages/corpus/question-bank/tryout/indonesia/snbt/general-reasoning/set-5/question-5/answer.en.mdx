# Given

The January and February table lists **<InlineMath math="200" /> enrolled students, students attending at least one session, and average attendance per session**.

# Asked

Whether each of the four statements is **True** or **False** from the text and table.

# Solution

> Keep the three denominators separate at all times, separating all enrolled students, anyone who attended once, and single-session attendance.

Every statement here depends on **the right denominator**. Three groups are easy to mix up: the <InlineMath math="200" /> enrolled students, the students who attended at least one session, and the students who attended one particular session. Also work out the month's total attendances, which is the average per session times <InlineMath math="12" /> sessions.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{January}&=150\cdot12=1{,}800\text{ attendances}\\
\text{February}&=120\cdot12=1{,}440\text{ attendances}
\end{aligned}" />

The chart below puts three groups of students side by side for both months. **The number absent per session goes up, while the number who never attended actually goes down**.

The first statement is true. Students who never attended are the enrolled students minus those who attended at least one session. In January that's <InlineMath math="200-180=20" /> students, and in February only <InlineMath math="200-192=8" /> students.

The second statement is false. It talks about students who attended at least once, so the denominator is <InlineMath math="192" />, not <InlineMath math="200" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1{,}440}{192}=7.5\qquad\frac{1{,}440}{200}=7.2" />

You get <InlineMath math="7.2" /> if you divide by all enrolled students.

The third statement is false. A student absent from a session is an enrolled student who didn't attend it, so the average is <InlineMath math="200-120=80" /> students. The number <InlineMath math="72" /> comes from <InlineMath math="192-120" />, but the <InlineMath math="8" /> students who never attended are absent from every session too.

The fourth statement is true. In January there are <InlineMath math="1{,}800" /> attendances, all belonging to <InlineMath math="180" /> students, so the average is <InlineMath math="1{,}800:180=10" /> sessions per student. If every one of them attended fewer than <InlineMath math="10" /> sessions, the total would be at most <InlineMath math="180\cdot9=1{,}620" />, which is less than <InlineMath math="1{,}800" />. So at least one student attended <InlineMath math="10" /> sessions or more.

# Answer

So the first and fourth statements are true, while the second and third are false.

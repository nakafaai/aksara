The data show <InlineMath math="\frac{45}{60}=75\%" /> of robotics students scored above <InlineMath math="80" />, against only <InlineMath math="\frac{84}{240}=35\%" /> of the others. The principal's proposal assumes that **robotics raised the scores**, so making it compulsory would raise other students' scores.

The robotics students joined **voluntarily**. Students who are already strong in mathematics may be more drawn to robotics. If so, making robotics compulsory would not raise scores. For the proposal to make sense, the score gap must come mainly from the robotics activity, not from differences in who chose to join.

The other options are not required assumptions:

- Robotics would be compulsory, so student interest does not have to be assumed.
- Scores that were already high before joining show that robotics is **not** the cause. This statement weakens the proposal.
- Having enough mathematics teachers has nothing to do with the effect of robotics.
- The proposal can still hold even if some other students take other activities.

So the proposal depends on the assumption that the score gap comes mainly from the robotics activity, not from differences in who chose to join.

# Given

Mrs. Wina has **<InlineMath math="15" /> kg of laundry in the first half of the month** and **<InlineMath math="20" /> kg in the second half**, with vouchers valid for one half only.

# Asked

The **lowest total laundry cost** for the whole month.

# Solution

> A cheaper voucher per kilogram can still waste money if unused kilograms expire at mid-month, so price both halves carefully.

The month splits into two halves, and voucher P only lives for one half. So it isn't enough to look for the lowest price per kg. You have to check **how many kg of each voucher really get used** in each half.

Voucher P costs <InlineMath math="48{,}000:10=4{,}800" /> rupiah per kg, voucher Q costs <InlineMath math="130{,}000:25=5{,}200" /> rupiah per kg, and the normal rate is <InlineMath math="6{,}000" /> rupiah. Voucher P is cheapest, as long as it's used up.

Start with the plans that don't use voucher Q after dividing prices by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> rupiah.

| Half of the month | How to pay | Cost |
| --- | --- | :---: |
| Days <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="15" />, <InlineMath math="14" /> kg | <InlineMath math="1" /> voucher P and <InlineMath math="4" /> kg at the normal rate | <InlineMath math="48+4\cdot6=72" /> |
| Days <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="15" />, <InlineMath math="14" /> kg | <InlineMath math="2" /> vouchers P, <InlineMath math="6" /> kg wasted | <InlineMath math="96" /> |
| Days <InlineMath math="16" /> to <InlineMath math="30" />, <InlineMath math="20" /> kg | <InlineMath math="2" /> vouchers P, used up exactly | <InlineMath math="96" /> |
| Days <InlineMath math="16" /> to <InlineMath math="30" />, <InlineMath math="20" /> kg | <InlineMath math="1" /> voucher P and <InlineMath math="10" /> kg at the normal rate | <InlineMath math="48+60=108" /> |

The cheapest choice in each half gives this total.

<BlockMath math="72+96=168" />

Now compare with the plans that use voucher Q. A voucher Q bought on day <InlineMath math="1" /> covers <InlineMath math="25" /> of the <InlineMath math="14+20=34" /> kg, leaving <InlineMath math="9" /> kg in the second half.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Q and 1 voucher P}&=130+48=178\\
\text{Q and 9 kg at the normal rate}&=130+9\cdot6=184
\end{aligned}" />

Both cost more than <InlineMath math="168" />. The other plans lose too: four vouchers P cost <InlineMath math="4\cdot48=192" />, and no vouchers at all costs <InlineMath math="34\cdot6=204" />.

There are two traps. The big voucher feels like a saving, but it costs more per kg than voucher P. And voucher P doesn't always pay off, because in the first half a second voucher would only be used for <InlineMath math="4" /> kg.

# Answer

So the lowest cost is **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}168{,}000" />**.

Let

- <InlineMath math="P" /> mean "employees resign and receive severance pay".
- <InlineMath math="Q" /> mean "the company closes."

The phrase **exactly one** makes the premise an exclusive disjunction:

<BlockMath math="P\oplus Q." />

The problem states that <InlineMath math="Q" /> occurs. An exclusive disjunction then requires <InlineMath math="\neg P" />.

Therefore, the first outcome, in which employees resign and receive severance pay, **does not occur**.

The first outcome combines two events with "and". Negating that combination does not let you conclude that each event is individually false. Let <InlineMath math="R" /> represent resignation and <InlineMath math="S" /> receipt of severance:

<BlockMath math="\neg P=\neg(R\land S)\equiv\neg R\lor\neg S" />

The problem rules out the combined first outcome. It does not identify who chose closure or establish every detail of severance payments.

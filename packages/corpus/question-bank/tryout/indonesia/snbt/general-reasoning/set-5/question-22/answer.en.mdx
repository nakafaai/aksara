Look for a relation among the four columns that **holds in every complete row**. Adding columns 1 and 2 does not work, for example <InlineMath math="4+6=10\neq14" />. Try the product of columns 1 and 2 and compare it with columns 3 and 4:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3\times5-7 &= 8 \\
4\times6-10 &= 14 \\
5\times3-6 &= 9
\end{aligned}" />

The rule: column 4 equals **the product of columns 1 and 2 minus column 3**. Apply it to the last row:

<BlockMath math="7\times2-?=5\quad\Rightarrow\quad ?=14-5=9" />

Check: <InlineMath math="7\times2-9=5" />, matching column 4. So the number that replaces the question mark is <InlineMath math="9" />.

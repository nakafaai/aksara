The two facts look contradictory because both use the word _attend_. But they measure different things. The average per session measures **how often** students attend, while the second row of the table measures **how many people** attended at all. A good explanation has to push the first measure down and the second one up at the same time.

Work out the month's total attendances first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{January}&=150\cdot12=1{,}800\text{ attendances from }180\text{ students}\\
\text{February}&=120\cdot12=1{,}440\text{ attendances from }192\text{ students}
\end{aligned}" />

There are <InlineMath math="12" /> more people, but <InlineMath math="360" /> fewer attendances. That happens when students who never came before now show up for a few sessions, while many other students skip more sessions than they used to, for example because they feel they can watch the recording. The newcomers raise the head count, and the skipped sessions lower the attendance count.

The other explanations fail on one of the facts.

- Watching a recording several times doesn't change the attendance record at all.
- Being connected for less than <InlineMath math="80" /> minutes only explains why the average per session fell. It gives the number of students who attended at least once no reason to rise.
- The text says the enrolled students in both months are the same <InlineMath math="200" /> students, so there are no new students.
- If the same <InlineMath math="120" /> students attended every session, only <InlineMath math="120" /> students would ever have attended, not <InlineMath math="192" />.

So the best explanation is that students who never attended before started coming to a few sessions, while many other students came to fewer sessions.

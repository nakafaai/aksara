The analyst's explanation depends on a shortage: distribution is disrupted and production cannot keep up with demand. A strong weakener must therefore provide direct evidence that the alleged shortage did not occur.

| Additional fact | Effect on the explanation |
| :--- | :--- |
| More consumers choose eggs | Supports higher demand. |
| The price rises again | Repeats the result but does not test its cause. |
| Distribution remains disrupted | Supports the explanation. |
| Production falls while demand rises | Supports a supply shortage. |
| Output and deliveries meet the extra demand | Directly contradicts the claimed shortage. |

An increase in the number of producers alone would not be enough evidence because output could still fall. The decisive fact is that actual output and deliveries rose enough to cover the additional demand.

Therefore, **egg output and deliveries increase enough to meet the extra demand** is the strongest weakener.

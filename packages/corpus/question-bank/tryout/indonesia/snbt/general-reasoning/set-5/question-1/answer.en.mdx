# Given

The passage and table report **tuna catch, selling prices, and fuel costs for large and small boats** across two fishing seasons.

# Asked

The conclusion **most strongly supported** by the text and table.

# Solution

> Hauling home more fish does not guarantee more profit when the market price falls while fuel expenses climb at the same time.

You're looking for the conclusion the table guarantees, not one that merely sounds reasonable. The key is the net income for a season, which is fish sales minus fuel cost. Work it out for one large boat first after dividing every amount by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> rupiah.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{2024 season}&=100\cdot100\cdot20-100\cdot300=200{,}000-30{,}000=170{,}000\\
\text{2025 season}&=80\cdot160\cdot15-80\cdot500=192{,}000-40{,}000=152{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

One large boat's catch for the season rises from <InlineMath math="100\cdot100=10{,}000" /> kg to <InlineMath math="80\cdot160=12{,}800" /> kg. But **its net income falls** from <InlineMath math="170" /> million to <InlineMath math="152" /> million rupiah. The lower price, the fewer trips and the costlier fuel wipe out the gain from the bigger catch per trip.

The chart below breaks down one large boat's income. **Sales dip only a little, but the fuel cost climbs**, so the net income falls.

Now check the other conclusions one at a time.

- The <InlineMath math="60\%" /> rise only applies to the catch per trip. Net income actually falls, so the group leader's claim is wrong.
- The table only shows that the price fell after the fish aggregating rafts were set up. It doesn't show the cause, so the word _because_ goes beyond the data.
- The group's total catch actually rises. In the 2024 season it's <InlineMath math="320{,}000" /> kg, and in the 2025 season it's <InlineMath math="376{,}000" /> kg.
- One small boat's net income falls from <InlineMath math="70{,}000" /> to <InlineMath math="50{,}000" /> thousand rupiah. That's a drop of <InlineMath math="\tfrac{20}{70}" />, about <InlineMath math="29\%" />, not <InlineMath math="25\%" />. The fuel cost doesn't fall with the price, so net income falls more sharply than the price does.

# Answer

So the best-supported conclusion is that **each large boat brings home more tuna over the season, yet its net income is lower than in the 2024 season**.

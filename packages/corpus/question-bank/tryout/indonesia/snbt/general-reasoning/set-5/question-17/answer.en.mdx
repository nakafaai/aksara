Let <InlineMath math="g" /> be the hypothetical household-consumption growth rate in percent and <InlineMath math="c" /> its contribution in percentage points. Under the stated **proportionality assumption**,

<BlockMath math="c=2.74\left(\frac{g}{5.05}\right)." />

Because <InlineMath math="g<5<5.05" />, the fraction <InlineMath math="\frac{g}{5.05}" /> is less than <InlineMath math="1" />. Therefore,

<BlockMath math="c<2.74." />

Nothing in the assumption changes the investment contribution, proves negative total growth, or requires the consumption contribution to be zero.

Thus, the household-consumption contribution would be **below** <InlineMath math="2.74" /> percentage points.

For example, choose growth of <InlineMath math="g=4.5" /> percent, which satisfies the below-five-percent condition:

<BlockMath math="c=2.74\times\frac{4.5}{5.05}=\frac{12.33}{5.05}\approx2.44" />

The contribution in this example falls but remains positive. This also shows why a zero contribution is not a necessary consequence.

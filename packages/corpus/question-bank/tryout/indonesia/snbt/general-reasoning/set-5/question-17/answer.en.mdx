# Given

Only the original six rules apply when forming a **four-person team** from the candidates.

# Asked

The total **number of distinct valid teams**.

# Solution

> Split every possible team cleanly into two non-overlapping buckets, without Gendis and with Gendis, so every valid team is counted once.

To count every team, you need a split that misses nothing and counts nothing twice. Gendis makes a good dividing point, because rule 4 and rule 6 both mention Gendis. Every team falls into **exactly one** of two groups: teams without Gendis or teams with Gendis.

First group: Gendis isn't chosen.

1. Cempaka must be chosen (rule 4).
2. The team needs a physics member (rule 1). Only Haikal is left, so Haikal is chosen.
3. Haikal is chosen, so Bening is chosen (rule 5).
4. Bening is chosen, so Elvira can't be (rule 3).
5. The fourth member has to come from chemistry (rule 1), so it's Danu or Fauzan. Arka can't join, because only one place is left and Arka requires Danu (rule 2).

This group holds <InlineMath math="2" /> teams.

Second group: Gendis is chosen, so Fauzan is out (rule 6).

- If Haikal is chosen, Bening joins (rule 5), Elvira is out (rule 3), and the chemistry member must be Danu. That gives <InlineMath math="1" /> team: Bening, Danu, Gendis, Haikal.
- If Haikal isn't chosen, pick three people from Arka, Bening, Cempaka, Danu and Elvira. There are <InlineMath math="10" /> ways to pick three out of five. Cross out the ones with neither Danu nor Elvira, the ones with Arka but no Danu, and the ones with Bening and Elvira together.

| Three people besides Gendis | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Arka, Bening, Danu | Allowed |
| Arka, Cempaka, Danu | Allowed |
| Arka, Danu, Elvira | Allowed |
| Bening, Cempaka, Danu | Allowed |
| Cempaka, Danu, Elvira | Allowed |
| Arka, Bening, Cempaka | No chemistry member |
| Arka, Bening, Elvira | Arka without Danu |
| Arka, Cempaka, Elvira | Arka without Danu |
| Bening, Cempaka, Elvira | Bening with Elvira |
| Bening, Danu, Elvira | Bening with Elvira |

This part gives <InlineMath math="5" /> teams.

The chart below sums up the three parts of the count. **The largest part comes from teams with Gendis but without Haikal**, and the three add up to <InlineMath math="8" />.

So there are **<InlineMath math="8" /> teams** that meet all six rules.

Because **digits may repeat**, choosing a digit for one position does not reduce the choices at another. Count the choices allowed by each position’s rule.

| Position | Valid choices | Count |
| :---: | :--- | :---: |
| First | `Z`, `I`, `V`, `A` | <InlineMath math="4" /> |
| Second | <InlineMath math="1,3,9" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |
| Third | <InlineMath math="3,5,7" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |
| Fourth | <InlineMath math="2,3,4" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |

By the multiplication principle,

<BlockMath math="4\times3\times3\times3=108." />

Therefore, <InlineMath math="108" /> different passwords satisfy the rules.

For example, `Z333` is valid: <InlineMath math="3" /> divides <InlineMath math="9" />, is an odd prime, and is allowed in the last position. The digit <InlineMath math="9" /> is allowed in the second position but not the third because it is not prime.

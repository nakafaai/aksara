# Given

Laras buys positive whole-number counts of notebooks and folders, with statement **(1) bill of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}43{,}000" />** and statement **(2) one more notebook than folder**.

# Asked

Whether statements **(1)** and **(2)** are sufficient to answer the question.

# Solution

> A single equation in two positive integers can already pin down a unique pair once you test every allowed whole number.

Call the number of notebooks <InlineMath math="b" /> and the number of folders <InlineMath math="m" />. Both are whole numbers and at least <InlineMath math="1" />. That **whole-number** condition decides the answer, so don't rush to say one equation with two unknowns can never be enough.

Check statement (1) on its own. Dividing every price by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> rupiah gives this equation.

<BlockMath math="4b+9m=43" />

Try every possible value of <InlineMath math="m" />. Since <InlineMath math="9\cdot5=45" /> already goes past <InlineMath math="43" />, the largest <InlineMath math="m" /> can be is <InlineMath math="4" />.

| <InlineMath math="m" /> | <InlineMath math="43-9m" /> | Divisible by <InlineMath math="4" />? |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="34" /> | No |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="25" /> | No |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="16" /> | Yes, <InlineMath math="b=4" /> |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | No |

The chart below draws that table column. **Only at <InlineMath math="m=3" /> is the remainder bar zero**, so only there is the number of notebooks whole.

Only <InlineMath math="m=3" /> gives a whole number for <InlineMath math="b" />. So statement (1) alone already pins it down: Laras bought <InlineMath math="3" /> folders.

Check statement (2) on its own. It only says <InlineMath math="b=m+1" />. Laras could have bought <InlineMath math="2" /> notebooks and <InlineMath math="1" /> folder, or <InlineMath math="5" /> notebooks and <InlineMath math="4" /> folders, and so on. The number of folders isn't fixed, so statement (2) alone isn't enough.

The most common mistake here is to decide statement (1) isn't enough because there's only one equation, then to combine it with statement (2). The combination does give <InlineMath math="m=3" />, but statement (1) already gets there by itself.

# Answer

So statement (1) alone is enough to answer the question, but statement (2) alone isn't.

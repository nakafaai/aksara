The principal's reasoning has three parts:

- A group of people **choose for themselves** to join an activity.
- That group shows better results than the others.
- The activity is then **made compulsory for everyone** in the hope that everyone's results improve.

The healthy cooking class has all three parts: residents joined voluntarily, their blood sugar is lower, and the class will be made compulsory for all residents. It shares the same weakness too: health-minded residents may simply be the ones who chose to join.

The other options follow different patterns:

- Random assignment removes the self-selection problem, so the factory's reasoning is stronger and not parallel.
- The bakery reasoning generalizes from a small sample rather than proposing a compulsory activity.
- The shoe advertisement relies on a famous person.
- The reasoning about Dodi is a valid deduction.

So the most similar reasoning is the plan to make the healthy cooking class compulsory for all residents.

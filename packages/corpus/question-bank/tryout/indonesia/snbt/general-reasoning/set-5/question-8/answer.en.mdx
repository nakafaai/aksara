# Given

The agency head claims **about <InlineMath math="95\%" /> of riders across the entire city wear helmets** based on data from camera intersections.

# Asked

The **main reasoning flaw** behind extending that figure to the whole city.

# Solution

> Riders naturally put on helmets where cameras watch them, so camera-site numbers cannot speak for every unmonitored street in town.

This statement isn't the same as the head's first conclusion. The first one is about the _cause_ of the drop. This one is about the _whole city_. So check where the <InlineMath math="95\%" /> comes from and where it's being carried to.

The figure comes from the camera counts in April.

<BlockMath math="\frac{21{,}000-1{,}050}{21{,}000}=\frac{19{,}950}{21{,}000}=95\%" />

That count only covers the <InlineMath math="12" /> junctions with cameras. Those junctions aren't a neutral sample, because that's exactly where riders know a violation will be recorded and ticketed. Someone who rides bareheaded on back streets may put a helmet on just to pass the cameras, or may avoid those junctions. The head **stretches a result from a special place to every road in the city**, though that place doesn't represent the other roads.

The diagram below draws the leap in that reasoning. **The <InlineMath math="95\%" /> is born in a special place**, then carried to every road in the city without a reason.

  title={<>From the camera count to a claim about the whole city</>}
  description={<>The cameras only count riders at the <InlineMath math="12" /> camera junctions, where a violation is sure to be recorded. There, <InlineMath math="95\%" /> wear helmets. The head carries that figure to every road, including roads without cameras.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR
    K["12 camera junctions: violations always recorded"] --> H["95% of riders there wear helmets"]
    H -.->|"carried over without a reason"| S["95% of riders in the whole city"]
    J["Roads without cameras"] -.->|"never counted"| S`}
/>

The other options aren't flaws in this statement.

- The statement only mentions riders. Passengers aren't checked by the cameras, but they aren't part of the claim either.
- The table gives daily averages, so the number of days in each month doesn't affect the comparison.
- The denominator <InlineMath math="21{,}000" /> is right, because the <InlineMath math="1{,}050" /> riders without helmets in April are part of the <InlineMath math="21{,}000" /> motorcycles that passed in April.
- The police checks are about what caused the drop. That's a flaw in the first conclusion, not in the claim about how things stand across the city now.

# Answer

So the main flaw is that the head treats riders at the camera junctions as if they represented riders on every road in the city.

# Given

A four-character code with no repeats must **start with a letter, end with an even digit, and contain exactly two letters**.

# Asked

The **number of distinct valid codes** that can be formed.

# Solution

> Fill the locked first and last spots first, decide which of the two middle spots gets the second letter, and multiply the remaining choices.

The code has four places. The first and last places are already fixed by rule 1 and rule 2, so what you need to think about is **the two middle places**.

The first character is a letter, and the code must hold exactly two letters (rule 3). The last character is a digit, so the second letter has to be in the middle. That means the two middle places hold one letter and one digit.

Fill the most restricted places first, then multiply the numbers of choices.

1. Last character: an even digit, <InlineMath math="2" /> or <InlineMath math="4" />. That's <InlineMath math="2" /> choices.
2. First character: one of the five letters. That's <InlineMath math="5" /> choices.
3. Where the middle letter goes: second place or third place. That's <InlineMath math="2" /> choices.
4. The middle letter: it can't match the first letter (rule 4). That's <InlineMath math="4" /> choices.
5. The middle digit: it can't match the last digit (rule 4). That's <InlineMath math="3" /> choices.

<BlockMath math="2\cdot5\cdot2\cdot4\cdot3=240" />

Check it another way. Pick the two middle places as an ordered pair: letter then digit gives <InlineMath math="4\cdot3=12" /> ways, and digit then letter gives <InlineMath math="12" /> more, so <InlineMath math="24" /> ways. Then <InlineMath math="5\cdot24\cdot2=240" />.

The wrong answers usually come from one missed step.

- <InlineMath math="120" /> comes from not counting where the middle letter goes, though it can be in the second or third place.
- <InlineMath math="400" /> comes from forgetting rule 4, <InlineMath math="5\cdot2\cdot5\cdot4\cdot2" />.
- <InlineMath math="480" /> comes from letting the last character be any digit, not just an even one.

# Answer

So **<InlineMath math="240" />** different codes can be made.

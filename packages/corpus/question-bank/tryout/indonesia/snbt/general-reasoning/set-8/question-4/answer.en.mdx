# Given

The table for five Grade XII classes lists **average self-study hours and average exam scores** for each class.

# Asked

The conclusion **most strongly supported** by the table.

# Solution

> Line the classes up from fewest to most self-study hours and watch how the average exam scores climb right alongside them.

Order the classes by self-study hours: XII-1 <InlineMath math="(4)" />, XII-4 <InlineMath math="(5)" />, XII-2 <InlineMath math="(6)" />, XII-3 <InlineMath math="(9)" />, XII-5 <InlineMath math="(10)" />. Their average scores rise too: <InlineMath math="68, 70, 72, 80, 83" />. The table shows **a shared tendency**, without settling the cause.

The other options are not supported:

- The table gives class averages, not individual students, and does not prove a cause. *Pasti* (certainly) is too strong.
- High-scoring classes also have high tutoring rates, so the table does not show that tutoring has no effect.
- The table gives no class sizes.
- The rise is not constant. From <InlineMath math="4" /> to <InlineMath math="5" /> hours the score rises by <InlineMath math="2" />, while from <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="9" /> hours it rises by <InlineMath math="8" />, about <InlineMath math="2.7" /> per hour.

# Answer

So the best supported conclusion is that **classes with more average self-study hours tend to have higher average scores**.

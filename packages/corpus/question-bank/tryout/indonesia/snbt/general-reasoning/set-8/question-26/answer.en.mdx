# Given

Two statements give an **orange-to-grape candy ratio of <InlineMath math="3:5" />** and a **total candy count strictly between <InlineMath math="30" /> and <InlineMath math="50" />**.

# Asked

Whether statements **(1)** and **(2)** are sufficient to pinpoint a unique candy count.

# Solution

> If combining both clues still leaves two different multiples of eight inside that range, even both together are not enough.

Test each statement on its own, then **both together**.

**Statement (1) alone**: not sufficient. There are <InlineMath math="3k" /> orange and <InlineMath math="5k" /> grape sweets for some positive whole number <InlineMath math="k" />, but <InlineMath math="k" /> is unknown.

**Statement (2) alone**: not sufficient. Only a range for the total is known, and the ratio is unknown.

**Both statements together**: the total is <InlineMath math="8k" /> with <InlineMath math="30<8k<50" />. The possible totals are <InlineMath math="32" />, <InlineMath math="40" />, and <InlineMath math="48" />, so the orange sweets could number <InlineMath math="12" />, <InlineMath math="15" />, or <InlineMath math="18" />. There is still more than one answer.

# Answer

So statements (1) and (2) are **not sufficient to answer the question**.

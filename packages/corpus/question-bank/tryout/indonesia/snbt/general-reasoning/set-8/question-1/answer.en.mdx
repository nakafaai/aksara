# Given

The taste test records **how many tasters could tell the two drinks apart** and that **<InlineMath math="70\%" /> of those tasters** preferred the new recipe.

# Asked

The conclusion **most strongly supported** by the passage.

# Solution

> Convert conditional percentages into real headcounts first so you never mistake a slice of a subgroup for a share of everyone.

Turn the percentages into **numbers of people**, because the <InlineMath math="70\%" /> applies only to participants who could tell the drinks apart.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{could tell apart} &= 40\%\times200=80 \\
\text{preferred the old drink} &= 70\%\times80=56 \\
\text{preferred the new drink} &= 80-56=24
\end{aligned}" />

The chart below puts each group next to a third of the participants. **The bar for those preferring the old drink is still below the one-third bar**.

<Chart
  title={<>Taste-test groups compared with a third of the participants</>}
  description={<>In all, <InlineMath math="120" /> participants couldn't tell the drinks apart, <InlineMath math="56" /> preferred the old drink, and <InlineMath math="24" /> preferred the new one. A third of the <InlineMath math="200" /> participants is about <InlineMath math="67" /> people.</>}
  categories={["Couldn't tell apart", "Preferred the old drink", "Preferred the new drink", "A third of participants"]}
  bars="apart"
  series={[{ label: "Participants", mark: "bar", values: [(60 * 200) / 100, (70 * 80) / 100, 80 - 56, Math.round(200 / 3)] }]}
  valueLabel="People"
/>

Those who preferred the old drink are <InlineMath math="28\%" /> of all participants, and <InlineMath math="28\%" /> is less than a third.

The other options are not supported:

- Only <InlineMath math="24" /> people, <InlineMath math="12\%" />, preferred the new drink.
- The <InlineMath math="70\%" /> is a share of the <InlineMath math="80" /> who could tell the drinks apart, not of all participants.
- A taste test does not show sales, and *pasti* (certainly) is too strong.
- The passage says nothing about participants' drinking habits.

# Answer

So the best supported conclusion is that **fewer than a third of the participants preferred the old drink**.

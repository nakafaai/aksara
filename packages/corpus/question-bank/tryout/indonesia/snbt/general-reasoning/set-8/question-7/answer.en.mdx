The manager compares two months and concludes that the music **caused** the rise in spending. That conclusion holds only if nothing else changed from September to October that could raise spending, such as a new menu, a promotion, or a different payday pattern. If something like that changed, the rise may not be due to the music.

The other options are not required assumptions:

- A conclusion about average spending does not need every customer to like the music.
- *Selalu* (always) is too strong. Average spending can rise even if not every customer buys pricier bread.
- The staff's taste does not decide whether music changes what customers spend.
- The conclusion compares only September and October, so it does not need prices to have never changed since the shop opened.

So the conclusion depends on the assumption that the rise in October spending was not caused by some other change between September and October.

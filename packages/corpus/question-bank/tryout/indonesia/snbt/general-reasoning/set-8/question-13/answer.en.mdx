Let <InlineMath math="p" />: Rudi works overtime and <InlineMath math="q" />: Rudi has dinner at home. The statement has the form <InlineMath math="\lnot p\Rightarrow q" />.

The implication <InlineMath math="a\Rightarrow b" /> is equivalent to <InlineMath math="\lnot a\lor b" />. With <InlineMath math="a=\lnot p" />:

<BlockMath math="\lnot p\Rightarrow q\;\equiv\;\lnot(\lnot p)\lor q\;\equiv\;p\lor q" />

So the statement is equivalent to "**Rudi works overtime or has dinner at home**" (*Rudi lembur atau makan malam di rumah*). At least one of the two happens.

The other options are not equivalent:

- "Does not work overtime and has dinner at home" is a conjunction that requires both.
- "If he works overtime, he does not eat at home" cannot be concluded. The original says nothing about days with overtime.
- "If he eats at home, he does not work overtime" cannot be concluded either.
- "Does not work overtime or does not eat at home" is <InlineMath math="\lnot p\lor\lnot q" />, different from <InlineMath math="p\lor q" />.

So the statement is equivalent to "Rudi works overtime or has dinner at home".

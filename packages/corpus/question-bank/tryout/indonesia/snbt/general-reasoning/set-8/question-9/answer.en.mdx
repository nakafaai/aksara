# Given

A researcher planted **<InlineMath math="50" /> seeds of a new corn variety in a dry test plot**, and **<InlineMath math="46" /> grew well**.

# Asked

The **most appropriate conclusion** supported by that observation.

# Solution

> Strong results in one dry test plot justify expecting good odds in similar dry soil, not a guarantee across every climate.

The observation shows <InlineMath math="92\%" /> of the seeds grew well **on the dry test plot**. A reasonable generalization reaches only conditions **similar** to those observed, and it is stated as a likelihood, not a certainty.

The diagram below draws how far that observation can be carried. **Only the branch to similar dry land is backed by the data**, and even then as a likelihood.

The other options go beyond the data:

- *All kinds of land in Indonesia* is far broader than the one dry plot observed, and *pasti* (certainly) is too strong.
- The result applies to this new variety, not to *any* variety.
- The test did not try wet land, so it says nothing about wet land.
- The passage does not mention where the seeds came from, so the cause of the four failures cannot be fixed.

# Answer

So the most appropriate conclusion is that **the variety will most likely grow well on dry land similar to the test plot**.

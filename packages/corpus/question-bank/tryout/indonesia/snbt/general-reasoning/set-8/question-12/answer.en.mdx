HR treats contract renewal as a sign of commitment and connects that commitment to mentoring. Its reasoning needs three steps:

1. Participants attend mentoring.
2. Mentoring increases their commitment.
3. That commitment leads them to choose to renew their contracts.

Automatic renewal weakens the last step. If renewal requires no active decision, the <InlineMath math="82\%" /> rate is not a strong measure of participants’ commitment. It may reflect the default renewal process.

Useful career feedback, increased enrollment, and plans for more sessions do not challenge that link. An independent audit can confirm the number without confirming that it measures commitment.

Therefore, the strongest weakening evidence is that **contracts renew automatically unless employees submit an opt-out form**.

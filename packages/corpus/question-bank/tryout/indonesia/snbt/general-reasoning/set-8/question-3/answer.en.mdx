# Given

The taste-test passage gives **total participants, tasters who noticed a difference, and votes for the new or old flavor**.

# Asked

Whether each of the four statements is **True** or **False** based on the passage.

# Solution

> Keep in mind that seventy percent applies only to tasters who spotted a difference, never to the entire pool of participants.

Mark each statement Benar (true) or Salah (false). Count the people in each group. The <InlineMath math="70\%" /> applies to **those who could tell the drinks apart**, not to all participants.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{could not tell} &= 60\%\times200=120 \\
\text{could tell} &= 200-120=80 \\
\text{preferred old} &= 70\%\times80=56 \\
\text{preferred new} &= 80-56=24
\end{aligned}" />

The diagram below splits the <InlineMath math="200" /> participants step by step. **The <InlineMath math="70\%" /> only applies to the branch that could tell the drinks apart**.

<Mermaid
  title={<>How the <InlineMath math="200" /> taste-test participants split</>}
  description={<>Of the <InlineMath math="200" /> participants, <InlineMath math="120" /> couldn't tell the drinks apart and <InlineMath math="80" /> could. Of those <InlineMath math="80" />, <InlineMath math="56" /> preferred the old drink and <InlineMath math="24" /> the new one.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR
    A["200 participants"] -->|"60%"| T["120 couldn't tell apart"]
    A -->|"40%"| D["80 could tell apart"]
    D -->|"70%"| L["56 preferred old"]
    D -->|"30%"| N["24 preferred new"]`}
/>

**Statement 1** is true. <InlineMath math="80" /> participants could tell the drinks apart.

**Statement 2** is false. <InlineMath math="56" /> people preferred the old drink, not more than <InlineMath math="60" />.

**Statement 3** is true. <InlineMath math="24" /> people preferred the new drink.

**Statement 4** is false. <InlineMath math="24" /> people is far fewer than the <InlineMath math="120" /> who could not tell.

# Answer

So statements 1 and 3 are true, while statements 2 and 4 are false.

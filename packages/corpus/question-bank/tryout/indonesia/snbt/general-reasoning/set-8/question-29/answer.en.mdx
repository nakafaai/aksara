# Given

Three-digit numbers must be **divisible by <InlineMath math="5" />** and use **three all-distinct digits**.

# Asked

The **number of valid three-digit numbers**.

# Solution

> Split numbers ending in zero from those ending in five, because zero still cannot take the hundreds place when five is at the end.

Numbers divisible by <InlineMath math="5" /> end in <InlineMath math="0" /> or <InlineMath math="5" />. Count **the two cases** separately, because the condition on the hundreds digit differs.

**Units digit <InlineMath math="0" />**. The hundreds digit can be <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="9" />, <InlineMath math="9" /> choices. The tens digit must differ from both, leaving <InlineMath math="8" /> choices.

<BlockMath math="9\times8=72" />

**Units digit <InlineMath math="5" />**. The hundreds digit cannot be <InlineMath math="0" /> or <InlineMath math="5" />, so there are <InlineMath math="8" /> choices. The tens digit may be <InlineMath math="0" /> but must differ from the hundreds digit and from <InlineMath math="5" />, so there are <InlineMath math="8" /> choices.

<BlockMath math="8\times8=64" />

# Answer

The total is <InlineMath math="72+64=136" /> numbers.

Let <InlineMath math="T" /> mean that the building is a temple, <InlineMath math="D" /> that it has a domed roof, and <InlineMath math="H" /> its height in metres. The premises give necessary properties of a temple:

<BlockMath math="T \Longrightarrow (D \land H>60)" />

They do not give the reverse implication. A building that is <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> tall is compatible with being a temple, but height alone does not **establish that the building is a temple**. The missing roof and function data are directly relevant to the proposed conclusion.

The word **some** in the statement about temples taller than 100 metres does not mean all temples exceed that height. A temple exactly 100 metres tall still fits the premises, but a non-temple of that height is also allowed.

The conclusion is therefore neither proved nor disproved. It is relevant to the information, but **cannot be assessed because the information is insufficient**.

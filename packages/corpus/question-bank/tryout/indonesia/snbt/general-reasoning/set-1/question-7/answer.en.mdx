A necessary assumption is a statement that, **when denied, makes the conclusion collapse**. Test each option by denying it.

Deny the option about cyclists moving: "The drop in accidents happened mainly because cyclists switched to other roads." If that were true, accidents did not really decrease. They only moved elsewhere along with the cyclists, and the claim that bike lanes cut accidents across the city loses its support. Since the denial breaks the conclusion, this statement is a necessary assumption.

Deny each of the other options, and the conclusion can still stand:

- Higher construction costs do not change whether the lanes reduce accidents.
- Main roads do not need to be exactly as busy. Bike lanes can still reduce accidents on quieter or busier roads.
- The conclusion does not require more cyclists. More cyclists could even add chances for accidents.
- The conclusion concerns only cyclist accidents, so motor vehicle accidents need not fall.

So the conclusion requires the assumption that the drop on Jalan Merdeka did not happen mainly because cyclists moved to other roads.

# Given

**The principal's forecast** takes the <InlineMath math="5" />-point gain measured on volunteer applicants and applies it to next year's entire mandatory cohort.

# Asked

The main flaw in extending the principal's estimate to the whole cohort.

# Solution

> A gain measured on eager volunteers cannot automatically be copied onto unwilling students who are forced to attend.

The principal's forecast has two steps. Step one estimates the class's effect from two groups of applicants: <InlineMath math="8 - 3 = 5" /> points. Step two applies that <InlineMath math="5" /> to **the whole year group, now required to attend**. To find the flaw, check which step was handled properly and which one jumps.

Step one is careful enough. The principal compares participants with applicants who didn't get a place, who also wanted to join and also started at <InlineMath math="70" />. The principal also uses gains, not just final scores.

Step two is the jump. The <InlineMath math="5" /> points only apply to students who chose to apply. Half of this year's group, <InlineMath math="90" /> of <InlineMath math="180" /> students, didn't apply, and the table shows they're different: they started lower (<InlineMath math="66" />) and gained little (<InlineMath math="67 - 66 = 1" /> point). There are no data on how much the class helps students like them, especially if they attend only because they must. So the principal carries a result from one group over to a different group under different conditions.

The chart below draws the three groups' gains. **The group that didn't apply barely moves**, yet that group is the half of the year that will be required to attend next year.

The other options blame things already handled, or things that aren't a problem:

- The <InlineMath math="70 - 66 = 4" />-point head start over students who didn't apply plays no part, because the principal never compares participants with that group.
- Comparing with all <InlineMath math="120" /> students who didn't attend would give <InlineMath math="6.5" /> points, but that comparison mixes in students who weren't interested and started lower. The suggestion makes the estimate worse.
- Different group sizes, <InlineMath math="30" /> and <InlineMath math="60" />, don't stop you from comparing averages.
- The <InlineMath math="3" />-point rise of applicants without a place was already subtracted, <InlineMath math="8 - 3 = 5" />.

# Answer

So the main flaw is that the **<InlineMath math="5" />**-point effect was measured only on students who chose to apply, and is then applied to a whole year group that also includes students like those who didn't apply this year.

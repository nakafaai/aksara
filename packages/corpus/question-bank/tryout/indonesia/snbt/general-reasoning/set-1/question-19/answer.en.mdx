The key is reading the word **only** correctly. "Only employees who have worked more than two years apply for long leave" means every applicant for long leave has worked more than two years:

<BlockMath math="\text{applies for long leave} \Rightarrow \text{worked more than two years}" />

Rina applies for long leave, so Rina has worked more than two years. The third premise says no finance employee has worked more than two years. Therefore Rina cannot be a finance employee.

<BlockMath math="\text{Rina applies} \Rightarrow \text{Rina worked more than two years} \Rightarrow \text{Rina is not in finance}" />

The other options are not certain:

- The second premise says some employees with more than two years did not apply. Those employees are not in finance, so not every employee outside finance applied.
- No finance employee has worked more than two years, so none of them can apply for long leave. This statement is certainly false.
- The employees with more than two years who did not apply, described above, are not in finance. This statement is also certainly false.
- "All employees with more than two years apply" directly contradicts the second premise.

So the conclusion that must be true is that Rina is not a finance employee.

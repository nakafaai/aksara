Two cycles run side by side: the weekday repeats every <InlineMath math="7" /> days, and the market day (*pasaran*) repeats every <InlineMath math="5" /> days. So all you need is **how many days lie between 20 July and 1 December**, then the remainder of that number divided by <InlineMath math="7" /> and, separately, by <InlineMath math="5" />. The options use the Indonesian weekday names: *Senin* is Monday, *Selasa* Tuesday, *Rabu* Wednesday, and *Kamis* Thursday.

Count the gap month by month. Remember the month lengths: July has <InlineMath math="31" /> days, August <InlineMath math="31" />, September <InlineMath math="30" />, October <InlineMath math="31" />, and November <InlineMath math="30" />.

| Part | Days |
| --- | --- |
| 21 to 31 July | <InlineMath math="11" /> |
| August | <InlineMath math="31" /> |
| September | <InlineMath math="30" /> |
| October | <InlineMath math="31" /> |
| November | <InlineMath math="30" /> |
| 1 December | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| Total | <InlineMath math="134" /> |

So 1 December is <InlineMath math="134" /> days after 20 July. Now divide that gap by the length of each cycle:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
134 &= 7\times19+1 \\
134 &= 5\times26+4
\end{aligned}" />

A remainder of <InlineMath math="1" /> in the seven-day cycle moves the weekday one step on from Monday, to Tuesday (*Selasa*). A remainder of <InlineMath math="4" /> in the five-day cycle moves the market day four steps on from Pahing: Pon, Wage, Kliwon, Legi. So 1 December falls on Selasa Legi.

Check it another way. The same weekday and market day pair comes back every <InlineMath math="7\times5=35" /> days. After <InlineMath math="3\times35=105" /> days, on 2 November, the date is Senin Pahing again. From 2 November to 1 December is <InlineMath math="29" /> days, with <InlineMath math="29=7\times4+1" /> and <InlineMath math="29=5\times5+4" />. Same result: Selasa Legi.

The other options come from different counting slips:

- Selasa Pon comes from using the remainder <InlineMath math="1" /> from the seven-day cycle for the market day too. The market day needs the remainder after dividing by <InlineMath math="5" />, which is <InlineMath math="4" />.
- Rabu Pahing comes from a gap of <InlineMath math="135" /> days because 20 July itself was counted. A gap from a date doesn't include the starting date.
- Kamis Pon comes from giving September and November <InlineMath math="31" /> days each, which makes the gap <InlineMath math="136" /> days.
- Senin Kliwon comes from stopping at 30 November, <InlineMath math="133" /> days, and forgetting the last day to 1 December.

So in that year, 1 December falls on Selasa Legi, a Tuesday with the market day Legi.

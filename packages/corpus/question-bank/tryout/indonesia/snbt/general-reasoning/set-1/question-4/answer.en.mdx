The student council head's conclusion is about "most of the students who stopped". So the first step is to count the students who stopped and work out what "most" means. Then test each option by denying it: a necessary assumption is a statement whose denial makes the conclusion **certainly false**.

The students who stopped are the ones who bought high-sugar drinks in the week before the labels but not in the week after:

<BlockMath math="240 - 150 = 90 \text{ students}" />

Careful, it isn't <InlineMath math="240 - 180 = 60" />. The <InlineMath math="180" /> includes <InlineMath math="180 - 150 = 30" /> students who started buying high-sugar drinks after the labels, so <InlineMath math="60" /> is only the net change.

Most of <InlineMath math="90" /> students means more than half, so more than <InlineMath math="45" /> students. A student who stopped no longer buys high-sugar drinks, so anything that student buys in the canteen isn't high in sugar. That's why the conclusion needs more than <InlineMath math="45" /> of the students who stopped to still buy drinks in the canteen.

Now deny the key statement: at most <InlineMath math="45" /> of the students who stopped still bought drinks in the canteen. Then at most <InlineMath math="45" /> switched, which isn't most of them, so the conclusion is certainly false. That's why the statement is necessary.

The other options fail the denial test:

- The claim that everyone buying after the labels also bought before would keep all <InlineMath math="90" /> of those students buying drinks, but it's too strong. The conclusion can still hold if some buyers changed, as long as more than <InlineMath math="45" /> of the students who stopped kept buying drinks. A steady total of <InlineMath math="400" /> doesn't guarantee the same people.

- How the <InlineMath math="30" /> new buyers of high-sugar drinks behave doesn't affect a conclusion about the students who stopped.
- The conclusion doesn't need every student who stopped to read the labels. It's enough that more than half of them switched because of the labels.

So the necessary assumption is that more than <InlineMath math="45" /> of the students who stopped buying high-sugar drinks still bought other drinks in the canteen in the week after the labels.

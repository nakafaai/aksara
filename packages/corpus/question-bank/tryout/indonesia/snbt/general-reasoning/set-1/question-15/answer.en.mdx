The extra condition "O faces K" means **O sits in chair 4**. From the full analysis, O sits in chair 4 in only two arrangements:

| Arrangement | Chair 1 | Chair 2 | Chair 3 | Chair 4 | Chair 5 | Chair 6 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | K | L | N | O | M | P |
| II | K | M | N | O | L | P |

You can also derive this directly. O in chair 4 means N is in chair 3, because O sits right after N. The free chairs are 2, 5, and 6. L and M must face each other, so they take chairs 2 and 5. Chair 6 is left for P, and chair 6 is indeed even.

In both arrangements, P sits in chair 6. So **P must sit in chair 6**.

The other options are not certain:

- N sits in chair 3, which is not next to chair 1. This statement is always false.
- L sits in chair 2 in arrangement I but in chair 5 in arrangement II.
- O in chair 4 is next to chairs 3 and 5, while P is in chair 6. This statement is always false.
- M sits in chair 5 in arrangement I but in chair 2 in arrangement II.

So if O faces K, P must sit in chair 6.

The text describes two stages of public protection:

- Before a product enters the market, BPOM evaluates its safety, claimed benefit, and quality.
- After authorization, consumers still check the product information and BPOM continues its supervision.

That rules out the first four options. Authorization does not prove that a product cures every disease, suits every person, or no longer needs a label check. Supervision also does not stop at authorization.

The best conclusion is therefore the fifth option: the circulation and use of herbal products depend on both regulatory evaluation and continuing supervision.

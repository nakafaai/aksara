The text describes two stages of public protection:

- Before a product enters the market, BPOM evaluates its safety, claimed benefit, and quality.
- After authorization, consumers still check the product information and BPOM continues its supervision.

That rules out the four alternatives. Authorization does not prove that every product has a disease-treatment claim. It also does not guarantee suitability for every person or remove the need to check the label. Supervision also does not stop at authorization.

The best conclusion is therefore this: the circulation and use of herbal products depend on both regulatory evaluation and continuing supervision.

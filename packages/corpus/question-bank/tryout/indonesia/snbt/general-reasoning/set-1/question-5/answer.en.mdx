Each statement compares numbers in the table and is either Benar (true) or Salah (false). "Extra height from a fertilizer" means **the height of fertilized seedlings minus the height of unfertilized seedlings with the same watering time**.

The first statement uses morning watering:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Compost: } & 18-12=6\text{ cm} \\
\text{Liquid fertilizer: } & 15-12=3\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="6=2\times3" />, the first statement is **true**.

The second statement compares the mean of all <InlineMath math="40" /> seedlings for each fertilizer. Each cell of the table represents <InlineMath math="20" /> seedlings, so the combined mean equals the mean of the two cells:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Liquid fertilizer: } & \frac{15+22}{2}=18.5\text{ cm} \\
\text{Compost: } & \frac{18+17}{2}=17.5\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="18.5>17.5" />, the second statement is **true**.

The third statement uses evening watering:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Liquid fertilizer: } & 22-11=11\text{ cm} \\
\text{Compost: } & 17-11=6\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Twice the extra height from compost is <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" />, not <InlineMath math="11\text{ cm}" />. The third statement is **false**, even though the numbers are close.

The fourth statement is also **false**. The tallest seedlings, at <InlineMath math="22\text{ cm}" />, come from liquid fertilizer with evening watering. Liquid fertilizer with morning watering reaches only <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" />.

So the first and second statements are true, and the third and fourth are false.

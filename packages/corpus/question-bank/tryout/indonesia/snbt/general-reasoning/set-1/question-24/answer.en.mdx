Mark each statement Benar (true) or Salah (false). Each one hides a condition that's easy to miss, so start with **the change in each warehouse's stock per week**, which is rice in minus rice out minus shrinkage:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Anggrek}: \quad 22-35-3 &= -16 \\
\text{Bakung}: \quad 38-48-1 &= -11 \\
\text{Cempaka}: \quad 15-18-2 &= -5
\end{aligned}" />

With these numbers, the stocks at the end of some weeks are (in tonnes):

| End of week | Anggrek | Bakung | Cempaka |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="6" /> | <InlineMath math="84" /> | <InlineMath math="84" /> | <InlineMath math="66" /> |
| <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="68" /> | <InlineMath math="73" /> | <InlineMath math="61" /> |
| <InlineMath math="8" /> | <InlineMath math="52" /> | <InlineMath math="62" /> | <InlineMath math="56" /> |
| <InlineMath math="9" /> | <InlineMath math="36" /> | <InlineMath math="51" /> | <InlineMath math="51" /> |

**Statement 1** is Benar. The gap between Anggrek and Bakung starts at <InlineMath math="180-150=30" /> tonnes and shrinks by <InlineMath math="16-11=5" /> tonnes a week, so it closes after exactly <InlineMath math="30:5=6" /> weeks. Then both hold <InlineMath math="84" /> tonnes and Cempaka holds <InlineMath math="66" />, all at least <InlineMath math="40" />. There's a second match too: Bakung and Cempaka both hold <InlineMath math="51" /> tonnes in week <InlineMath math="9" />, but Anggrek is down to <InlineMath math="36" /> then. One moment that meets the condition is enough to make the statement true.

**Statement 2** is Salah. The handover cuts Anggrek's rice out to <InlineMath math="31" /> and raises Cempaka's to <InlineMath math="22" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Anggrek}: \quad 22-31-3 &= -12, & 180-8\times12 &= 84 \\
\text{Cempaka}: \quad 15-22-2 &= -9, & 96-7\times9 &= 33
\end{aligned}" />

Anggrek is safe now, but Cempaka is already down to <InlineMath math="33" /> tonnes at the end of week <InlineMath math="7" />, below the safety stock. The statement only looks true if you check Anggrek, the warehouse being helped.

**Statement 3** is Salah. The denominator is the stock at the end of week <InlineMath math="8" />, not the starting stock:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Anggrek}: \quad \frac{3}{52} &\approx 5.77\% \\
\text{Cempaka}: \quad \frac{2}{56} &\approx 3.57\%
\end{aligned}" />

Anggrek's percentage is larger. The statement looks true if you use the starting stock, because <InlineMath math="2/96\approx2.08\%" /> is indeed more than <InlineMath math="3/180\approx1.67\%" />. Anggrek's stock falls much faster, so the comparison flips.

**Statement 4** is Benar. Each week, the total stock of the three warehouses drops by <InlineMath math="32" /> tonnes, and <InlineMath math="3+1+2=6" /> tonnes of that is shrinkage:

<BlockMath math="\frac{6}{32} = 18.75\% > 10\%" />

The statement looks false if you compare shrinkage with all the rice going out, <InlineMath math="6/101\approx5.9\%" />. What it asks for is the share of the drop in stock, and that drop already has the incoming rice taken off.

So statements 1 and 4 are Benar, and statements 2 and 3 are Salah.

The director claims that the office relocation caused many employees to resign. To assess this claim, compare the timing of the relocation plans with the employees' decisions.

The strongest challenge is that most resignation letters had already been submitted before the relocation was announced. Those earlier decisions weaken the claim that the announcement prompted the resignations. This evidence does not establish the actual cause of the resignations, but it directly challenges the director's proposed causal connection.

A more distant office and a commute that takes twice as long provide reasons why relocation might encourage employees to leave. Lower rent and the expiry of the former lease explain why the company moved, not why employees resigned.

Therefore, the evidence that resignation letters were submitted before the relocation announcement most strongly weakens the director's argument.

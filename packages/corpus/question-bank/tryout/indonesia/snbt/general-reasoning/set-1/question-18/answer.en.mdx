The question hands you one strong rule: of the four statements, exactly one is true. You could try all four suspects one at a time, but Lutfi's statement gives a faster route.

Lutfi doesn't talk about the culprit. He talks about Kirana's statement. "Kirana is lying" is true exactly when Kirana's statement is false, and false exactly when Kirana's statement is true. So **of Kirana and Lutfi, exactly one is always telling the truth**. Since only one statement in total is true, that one true statement must belong to Kirana or Lutfi. That means Joko's statement and Maya's statement are both false.

Now put those two false statements to work.

1. Joko said "the culprit is Lutfi or Maya". That's false, so the culprit is neither Lutfi nor Maya. Joko or Kirana is left.
2. Maya said "the culprit is not me and not Kirana". A statement joined by "and" is false when at least one of its parts is false. So the culprit is Maya or Kirana.

The only suspect who appears in both steps is Kirana. Kirana is the culprit. That makes Kirana's statement "the culprit is Joko" false, and Lutfi's statement "Kirana is lying" true.

The other options fail because of these slips.

So Kirana is the culprit, and the only true statement is Lutfi's.

A statement is Benar (true) only if it **holds in all four arrangements** that satisfy the conditions. Otherwise it is Salah (false).

| Arrangement | Chair 1 | Chair 2 | Chair 3 | Chair 4 | Chair 5 | Chair 6 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | K | L | N | O | M | P |
| II | K | M | N | O | L | P |
| III | K | P | L | N | O | M |
| IV | K | P | M | N | O | L |

**Statement 1** is true. N sits in chair 3 in arrangements I and II and in chair 4 in arrangements III and IV. Chair 5 is impossible, because O would then need chair 6, which is always taken.

**Statement 2** is true. Facing chairs differ by <InlineMath math="3" />, an odd number. Adding or subtracting an odd number always turns even into odd and odd into even. Since L and M face each other, one sits in an even chair and the other in an odd chair. You can see this in the pairs 2 and 5 and 3 and 6.

**Statement 3** is false. In arrangements I and II, O sits in chair 4, an even chair.

**Statement 4** is false. In arrangements I and II, P in chair 6 faces N in chair 3, not L or M.

So statements 1 and 2 are true, and statements 3 and 4 are false.

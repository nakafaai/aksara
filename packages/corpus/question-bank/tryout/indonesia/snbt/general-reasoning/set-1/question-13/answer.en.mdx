The easiest comparison keeps the **decimal factor** <InlineMath math="0.44" /> the same. First rewrite the target:

<BlockMath math="0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} = 0.44 \times \frac{34}{36}" />

#### Rewrite each option

**Multiplying one factor and dividing the other** by the same number does not change a product. The multiplier must be nonzero. For the first option, multiply the decimal factor by four and divide the fractional factor by four:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}0.11\times\frac{35}{9}&amp;=(0.11\times4)\times\left(\frac{35}{9}\div4\right)\\&amp;=0.44\times\frac{35}{36}\end{aligned}" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 0.11 \times \frac{35}{9} &amp;= 0.44 \times \frac{35}{36} \\ 0.22 \times \frac{17}{9} &amp;= 0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} \\ 0.33 \times \frac{34}{27} &amp;= 0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} \\ 0.55 \times \frac{34}{45} &amp;= 0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} \\ 0.66 \times \frac{17}{27} &amp;= 0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The four forms with a coefficient other than <InlineMath math="0.11" /> equal the target. The form with coefficient <InlineMath math="0.11" /> has the same positive factor <InlineMath math="0.44" />, but <InlineMath math="\frac{35}{36} > \frac{34}{36}" />. Therefore, the only greater number is <InlineMath math="0.11 \times \frac{35}{9}" />.

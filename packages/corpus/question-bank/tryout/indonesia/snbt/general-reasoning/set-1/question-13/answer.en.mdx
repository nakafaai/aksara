The easiest comparison keeps the decimal factor <InlineMath math="0.44" /> the same. First rewrite the target:

<BlockMath math="0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} = 0.44 \times \frac{34}{36}" />

#### Rewrite each option

Multiplying one factor and dividing the other by the same number does not change a product. Applying that idea gives

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 0.11 \times \frac{35}{9} &= 0.44 \times \frac{35}{36} \\ 0.22 \times \frac{17}{9} &= 0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} \\ 0.33 \times \frac{34}{27} &= 0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} \\ 0.55 \times \frac{34}{45} &= 0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} \\ 0.66 \times \frac{17}{27} &= 0.44 \times \frac{17}{18} \end{aligned}" />

The four forms with a coefficient other than <InlineMath math="0.11" /> equal the target. The form with coefficient 0.11 has the same positive factor 0.44, but <InlineMath math="\frac{35}{36} > \frac{34}{36}" />. Therefore, the only greater number is <InlineMath math="0.11 \times \frac{35}{9}" />.

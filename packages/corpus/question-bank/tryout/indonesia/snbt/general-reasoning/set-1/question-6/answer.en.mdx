A good explanation must fit **the whole pattern in the data**, not just one number. Two facts need to be explained together:

1. With liquid fertilizer, evening watering gives seedlings <InlineMath math="7\text{ cm}" /> taller than morning watering.
2. For unfertilized and composted seedlings, watering time hardly matters, and morning watering is even slightly better.

The cause must therefore act only on liquid fertilizer and depend on when it is applied. The statement about nitrogen meets both conditions. Liquid fertilizer goes in with the watering. Applied in the morning, the solution sits on the soil surface as the soil heats up toward midday, so part of the nitrogen escapes into the air. Applied in the evening, the solution soaks in overnight before the soil heats up. Compost is mixed into the soil from the start, so it is not affected.

The other options do not fit the pattern:

- Watering time does not change how long the sun shines. Even if it did, unfertilized and composted seedlings would also grow taller, and they do not.
- How fast compost releases nutrients explains a difference between fertilizer types, not between watering times for liquid fertilizer.
- More careful measuring in the evening would help every group, and the passage says all seedlings received the same amount of water.
- The variety's water needs apply to all seedlings, so they cannot explain why only the liquid-fertilizer group differs so much.

So the best explanation is the loss of nitrogen from liquid fertilizer during the hot midday when it is applied in the morning.

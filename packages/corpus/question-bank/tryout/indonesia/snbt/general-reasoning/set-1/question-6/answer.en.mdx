Two facts seem to clash. High-sugar packs fell from <InlineMath math="480" /> to <InlineMath math="360" />, while total packs stayed at <InlineMath math="800" />. Yet the total sugar in all drinks sold rose from about <InlineMath math="15.6" /> kg to <InlineMath math="16.6" /> kg. The right explanation has to keep **both facts true at once**, without denying either of them.

The clue is how the label is written: grams per <InlineMath math="100" /> ml. That number is a sugar concentration, not the amount of sugar in one pack. The sugar in one pack also depends on how much the pack holds.

Compare one pack of each kind:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Dropped: } & \frac{11\text{ g}}{100\text{ ml}} \times 200\text{ ml} = 22\text{ g} \\ \text{Replacement: } & \frac{6\text{ g}}{100\text{ ml}} \times 600\text{ ml} = 36\text{ g} \end{aligned}" />

Each time a student swaps one high-sugar pack for one replacement pack, the number of packs stays the same and high-sugar packs drop by one, but the sugar sold goes up by <InlineMath math="36 - 22 = 14" /> grams. So both facts can be true together. That drink is only the most popular replacement, so not every swap adds <InlineMath math="14" /> grams, which is why a total rise of about <InlineMath math="1" /> kg still fits.

The other options don't explain both:

- Students buying more packs contradicts the table, because total packs stayed at <InlineMath math="800" />.
- A price rise for high-sugar drinks explains only why high-sugar packs fell, not why sugar rose.
- Drinks brought from home aren't counted, because the biology teacher counted only drinks sold in the canteen.
- Picking the lowest sugar number per <InlineMath math="100" /> ml isn't enough. If the packs are the same size, a lower concentration means less sugar, so the rise stays unexplained.

So the best explanation is that the replacement drink has a low sugar concentration per <InlineMath math="100" /> ml but comes in a much bigger pack, so each pack holds more sugar than the high-sugar drink students gave up.

The passage gives two clues:

- Growth is represented by several measurements, including more than weight.
- Those measurements are interpreted against age-appropriate curves and followed over time.

The conclusion about several measurements and their pattern over time combines both ideas. The other options turn one measurement into a complete diagnosis, treat any increase as automatically appropriate, or claim that growth charts replace all other health assessment. None of those claims appears in the text.

Therefore, growth assessment uses several suitable measurements and follows their development over time.

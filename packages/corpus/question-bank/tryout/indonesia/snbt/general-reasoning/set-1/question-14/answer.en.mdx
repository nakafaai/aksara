For a "must be true" question, find **every arrangement that satisfies the conditions** and choose the statement that holds in all of them.

K sits in chair 1, so chair 4 faces K. Chairs 2 and 6 are next to K, so N can only sit in chair 3, 4, or 5. L and M must take a pair of facing chairs: chairs 2 and 5 or chairs 3 and 6. The pair 1 and 4 is impossible because chair 1 belongs to K.

**Case L and M in chairs 2 and 5**. The free chairs are 3, 4, and 6. N must be in chair 3 or 4. If N were in chair 4, O would need chair 5, which is taken. So N is in chair 3, O in chair 4, and P in chair 6. Chair 6 is even, so condition 5 holds.

**Case L and M in chairs 3 and 6**. The free chairs are 2, 4, and 5. N must be in chair 4 or 5. If N were in chair 5, O would need chair 6, which is taken. So N is in chair 4, O in chair 5, and P in chair 2.

L and M can swap places in each case, which gives four arrangements:

| Arrangement | Chair 1 | Chair 2 | Chair 3 | Chair 4 | Chair 5 | Chair 6 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | K | L | N | O | M | P |
| II | K | M | N | O | L | P |
| III | K | P | L | N | O | M |
| IV | K | P | M | N | O | L |

P always sits in chair 6 or chair 2. Both chairs are next to chair 1, so **P is certainly next to K**.

The other statements hold only in some arrangements:

- N faces P in arrangements I and II, but not in III and IV, where N in chair 4 faces K.
- L sits in an even chair only in arrangement I.
- O faces K in arrangements I and II, but sits in chair 5 in III and IV.
- In arrangement I, M in chair 5 is next to O in chair 4.

So the statement that must be true is that P sits next to K.

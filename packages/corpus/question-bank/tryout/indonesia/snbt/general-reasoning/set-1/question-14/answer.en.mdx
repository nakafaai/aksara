We need the one value that falls outside the open interval <InlineMath math="\frac{144}{29} < x < 5" />.

#### Rewrite the lower bound

Write the lower bound as a number just below <InlineMath math="5" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{144}{29} = \frac{145 - 1}{29} = \frac{145}{29} - \frac{1}{29} = 5 - \frac{1}{29}" />
</MathContainer>

The interval is therefore

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="5 - \frac{1}{29} < x < 5" />
</MathContainer>

#### Rewrite the answer choices

Each numerator is one less than five times its denominator:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac{139}{28} &= 5-\frac{1}{28} \\ \frac{149}{30} &= 5-\frac{1}{30} \\ \frac{154}{31} &= 5-\frac{1}{31} \\ \frac{159}{32} &= 5-\frac{1}{32} \\ \frac{164}{33} &= 5-\frac{1}{33} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

For denominators greater than <InlineMath math="29" />, the fraction <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{n}" /> is smaller than <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{29}" />. Subtracting that smaller amount from <InlineMath math="5" /> produces a value between the lower bound and <InlineMath math="5" />. Thus, the options with denominators <InlineMath math="30" /> through <InlineMath math="33" /> all satisfy the inequality.

By contrast, <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{28} > \frac{1}{29}" />, so

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="5-\frac{1}{28} < 5-\frac{1}{29}" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, <InlineMath math="\frac{139}{28}" /> lies below the lower bound and is the only value that does not satisfy the inequality.

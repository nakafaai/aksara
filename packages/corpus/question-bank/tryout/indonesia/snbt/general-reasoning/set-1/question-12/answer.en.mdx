The two clocks begin with the same reading. Because the slow clock loses <InlineMath math="5\text{ minutes}" /> every real hour, its delay grows by <InlineMath math="5\text{ minutes}" /> per hour.

#### Find the delay needed for matching dials

A <InlineMath math="12\text{-hour}" /> dial repeats after <InlineMath math="12\text{ hours}" />. The first time the hands can display the same reading again is therefore when the slow clock is exactly <InlineMath math="12\text{ hours}" /> behind:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="12 \text{ hours} = 12 \times 60 \text{ minutes} = 720 \text{ minutes}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Find how long the delay takes to accumulate

Let <InlineMath math="t" /> be the elapsed real time in hours. The accumulated delay is <InlineMath math="5t\text{ minutes}" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 5t &= 720 \\ t &= \frac{720}{5} \\ t &= 144\text{ hours} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the clocks first show the same reading again after <InlineMath math="144\text{ hours}" />, or <InlineMath math="6\text{ days}" />.

Start from the fact that is certain and draw conclusions step by step. Write the statements as symbols:

- <InlineMath math="p" />: the warehouse receives a rice delivery on Monday
- <InlineMath math="q" />: the staff reweigh every sack
- <InlineMath math="r" />: the stock report is sent late
- <InlineMath math="s" />: the warehouse head calls the supplier

The premises are <InlineMath math="p \Rightarrow q" />, <InlineMath math="q \Rightarrow r" />, <InlineMath math="\neg r \vee s" />, and the fact <InlineMath math="\neg s" />.

**Step 1** uses the disjunction. In <InlineMath math="\neg r \vee s" />, at least one part must be true. Since <InlineMath math="s" /> is false, <InlineMath math="\neg r" /> must be true. So **the stock report is not late**.

**Step 2** uses modus tollens twice. From <InlineMath math="q \Rightarrow r" /> and <InlineMath math="\neg r" />, we get <InlineMath math="\neg q" />. From <InlineMath math="p \Rightarrow q" /> and <InlineMath math="\neg q" />, we get <InlineMath math="\neg p" />.

<BlockMath math="\neg s,\; \neg r \vee s \;\Rightarrow\; \neg r \;\Rightarrow\; \neg q \;\Rightarrow\; \neg p" />

So the warehouse did not receive a rice delivery on Monday.

The other options are wrong:

- Reweighing every sack contradicts <InlineMath math="\neg q" />.
- A late report contradicts <InlineMath math="\neg r" />.
- The premises say nothing about other days, so a delivery on another day may or may not have happened.
- The premises only concern this week, so nothing follows about next week.

So the conclusion that is certainly true is that the warehouse did not receive a rice delivery on Monday this week.

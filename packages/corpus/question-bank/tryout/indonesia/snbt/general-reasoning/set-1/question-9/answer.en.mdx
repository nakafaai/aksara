The head of the department picked **the road sections with the most crashes** in the previous year, then compared the numbers before and after the speed bumps went in, with no comparison sections. That's where the weakness is.

Sections picked because their numbers were the highest in one year tend to have lower numbers the next year, even if nobody touches them. Part of a very high count comes from chance, such as bad weather or a few careless drivers that year, and chance like that rarely repeats exactly. This is called **regression to the mean**. Without comparison sections that were just as bad but got no speed bumps, you can't separate the drop of almost half from regression to the mean.

So the flaw has two parts. A group is picked because it's at the extreme, and then the improvement afterwards is credited to an action, with nothing to compare against.

The hospital argument has the same pattern. The nurses were picked because they were late most often last month, and this month's drop in lateness is credited to the written warning. Without comparison nurses, part of that drop could be regression to the mean alone.

Now check the other options.

- The taxi company drew lots for which drivers got training and compared them with the other drivers. The lottery and the comparison group close exactly that gap, so this argument isn't flawed in the same way.
- The station survey is flawed because passengers at one station may not represent the whole city. That's a different weakness, a biased sample.
- The workshop did pick the pumps that used the most power, but it drew lots for half of them and compared the two groups. Since both groups dropped by about the same amount, the conclusion that cleaning saves little power actually makes sense. Those pumps show regression to the mean directly.
- The traffic light only compares before and after. That's similar, but the junction wasn't picked for having the highest numbers, so the key part of the pattern, picking extreme cases, is missing.

So the reasoning whose weakness is most similar is the hospital's argument about written warnings for the nurses who were late most often.

The conclusion under test is causal: white paint causes a lower room temperature. A causal conclusion is weakened most by **another cause that affects only one group**, because that cause could produce the temperature difference.

The statement about tree shade does exactly this. The painted houses belong to owners who signed up, so the two groups are not guaranteed to be alike in other ways. Here <InlineMath math="30" /> of the <InlineMath math="40" /> white-roofed houses stand under large trees, compared with only <InlineMath math="8" /> of the <InlineMath math="40" /> other houses. Shade keeps sunlight off the roof, so much of the <InlineMath math="2.8^{\circ}\text{C}" /> difference could come from the trees rather than the paint.

The other statements do not weaken the conclusion:

- The price of the paint says nothing about whether it lowers the temperature.
- Some residents of unpainted houses using fans rarely does not change the room temperature the officers measured.
- Nearly equal temperatures before painting strengthen the conclusion, because the difference only appeared after the roofs were painted.
- A larger difference on sunny days fits the way reflective paint works, by reflecting sunlight, so it strengthens the conclusion.

So the statement that weakens the conclusion most is that far more white-roofed houses are shaded by large trees.

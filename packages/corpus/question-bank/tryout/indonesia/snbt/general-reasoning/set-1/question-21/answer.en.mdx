The gaps between terms aren't constant, so look for a pattern in the **differences at several levels**: the differences between terms, then the differences of those differences, and so on.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{terms} &: 3,\ 4,\ 7,\ 15,\ 31,\ 58 \\
\text{first differences} &: 1,\ 3,\ 8,\ 16,\ 27 \\
\text{second differences} &: 2,\ 5,\ 8,\ 11 \\
\text{third differences} &: 3,\ 3,\ 3
\end{aligned}" />

The second differences aren't constant, but they always go up by <InlineMath math="3" />. So the next second differences are <InlineMath math="14" />, <InlineMath math="17" />, <InlineMath math="20" />, and so on.

Now build the sequence from the bottom up. Add each new second difference to the last first difference, then add that result to the last term:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
27+14 &= 41, & 58+41 &= 99 \\
41+17 &= 58, & 99+58 &= 157 \\
58+20 &= 78, & 157+78 &= 235
\end{aligned}" />

# Given

**The number sequence** starts with <InlineMath math="3, 4, 7, 15, 31, 58" /> and continues by the same rule.

# Asked

The first term in the sequence that exceeds <InlineMath math="200" />.

# Solution

> When first differences keep changing, take differences of differences until a steady pattern appears, then build back up term by term.

This question asks for two things: find the rule of the sequence, then keep extending it until a term goes past <InlineMath math="200" />. The gaps between terms aren't constant, so look for a pattern in the **differences at several levels**: the differences between terms, then the differences of those differences, and so on.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{terms} &: 3,\ 4,\ 7,\ 15,\ 31,\ 58 \\
\text{first differences} &: 1,\ 3,\ 8,\ 16,\ 27 \\
\text{second differences} &: 2,\ 5,\ 8,\ 11 \\
\text{third differences} &: 3,\ 3,\ 3
\end{aligned}" />

The second differences aren't constant, but they always go up by <InlineMath math="3" />. So the next second differences are <InlineMath math="14" />, <InlineMath math="17" />, <InlineMath math="20" />, and so on.

Now build the sequence from the bottom up. Add each new second difference to the last first difference, then add that result to the last term:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
27+14 &= 41, & 58+41 &= 99 \\
41+17 &= 58, & 99+58 &= 157 \\
58+20 &= 78, & 157+78 &= 235
\end{aligned}" />

The seventh term, <InlineMath math="99" />, and the eighth term, <InlineMath math="157" />, are still not more than <InlineMath math="200" />. The ninth term, <InlineMath math="235" />, is the first one that goes past it.

Check it the other way round. The new first differences are <InlineMath math="41, 58, 78" />, and the gaps between them are <InlineMath math="17" /> and <InlineMath math="20" />. Both continue the pattern <InlineMath math="2, 5, 8, 11, 14" />, which rises by <InlineMath math="3" />, so the work is consistent.

Two wrong shortcuts are common. If you treat the second difference as a constant <InlineMath math="11" />, the sequence becomes <InlineMath math="96, 145, 205" />, and you'd answer <InlineMath math="205" />. If you stop at the very next term, you get <InlineMath math="99" />, but <InlineMath math="99" /> hasn't passed <InlineMath math="200" /> yet.

# Answer

So the first term of the sequence that's greater than **<InlineMath math="200" />** is **<InlineMath math="235" />**.

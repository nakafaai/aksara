You're asked to separate the statements the four rules guarantee from the ones that are merely possible. A statement is *pasti benar* (certainly true) if no situation that obeys every rule can make it false. If you can build such a situation, the statement is *tidak pasti benar* (not certainly true).

Two words decide everything. The first is *hanya* ("only") in rule 2. "Studio A only screens films that passed curation" means **every film in Studio A has passed curation**. It does not say that every curated film is screened in Studio A. The second is *sebagian* ("some") in rule 4, which means at least one film, possibly all of them.

Rewrite the four rules in everyday words.

- Rule 1: a film that isn't screened in Studio A is screened in Studio B.
- Rule 2: a film screened in Studio A has passed curation.
- Rule 3: an animated film never passes curation.
- Rule 4: at least one film in Studio A runs longer than <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes. Call that film X.

Statement 1 is certainly true. Film X is screened in Studio A, so by rule 2 it passed curation. Film X also runs longer than <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes. So at least one curated film runs longer than <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes. People often call this uncertain because the "some" in rule 4 talks about Studio A, not about curated films. The word "only" in rule 2 is what carries film X across.

Statement 2 isn't certainly true, because it flips rule 2 around. Picture film Y, which passed curation, isn't animated, and is screened in Studio B. Film Y obeys all four rules, since rule 2 only limits what Studio A shows. So a curated film can sit in Studio B.

Statement 3 isn't certainly true. The long films you're guaranteed are the ones in Studio A, like film X, and those are indeed not animated, because they passed curation and animated films never do. But no rule says anything about how long animated films are. An animated film of <InlineMath math="25" /> minutes screened in Studio B obeys every rule, and it breaks statement 3.

Statement 4 is certainly true. Take any animated film. By rule 3 it didn't pass curation. By rule 2, Studio A only screens curated films, so it isn't screened in Studio A. By rule 1, a film that isn't in Studio A is in Studio B.

So statements 1 and 4 are certainly true, while statements 2 and 3 are not certainly true.

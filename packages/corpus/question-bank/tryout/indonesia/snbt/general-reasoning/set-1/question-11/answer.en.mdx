You need to separate the statements the five conditions guarantee from the ones that are merely possible. A statement is **certainly true** if no arrangement that obeys every condition makes it false. If you can build such an arrangement, the statement isn't certainly true.

Two words decide a lot. "Hanya" ("only") in condition 4 means every student who gets a committee shirt is on the student council, not that every council member gets a shirt. "Sebagian" ("some") in conditions 3 and 5 means at least one person.

Rewrite the five conditions in plain words.

- Condition 1: anyone who passes the preliminary round joins the intensive training. Its contrapositive: anyone who doesn't join the training didn't pass the preliminary round.
- Condition 2: the intensive training and the debate contest never share a person.
- Condition 3: at least one debater is on the student council.
- Condition 4: anyone who gets a committee shirt is on the student council.
- Condition 5: at least one student passed the preliminary round and got a committee shirt.

Statement 1 is certainly true. Take the debater on the council from condition 3. By condition 2, that student doesn't join the training. By the contrapositive of condition 1, that student didn't pass the preliminary round. So some council member didn't pass the preliminary round.

Statement 2 isn't certainly true. Condition 4 only says shirt holders are council members, and no condition stops a council member who debates from getting a shirt. Try this arrangement: Dinda is on the council, debates, and gets a shirt, but doesn't join the training and didn't pass the preliminary round. Add Raka, who passed the preliminary round, joins the training, gets a shirt and is on the council, so condition 5 holds. All five conditions are obeyed, yet a shirt holder debates.

Statement 3 isn't certainly true. Condition 4 only runs one way, from getting a shirt to being on the council. Condition 5 only guarantees that some of the students who passed got a shirt. Another council member could pass the preliminary round without getting a shirt, and no condition would be broken.

Statement 4 is certainly true. Take the student from condition 5 who passed the preliminary round and got a shirt. By condition 1, that student joins the intensive training. By condition 4, that student is on the council. So some trainee is on the student council.

So statements 1 and 4 are certainly true, while statements 2 and 3 aren't.

A conclusion is Benar (true) only if it **holds in every situation that satisfies all four premises**. If even one situation satisfies the premises but makes the conclusion false, the conclusion is Salah (false).

**Conclusion 1** is true. Premise 4 says Dimas passed the basic training (pelatihan dasar), and premise 1 says everyone who passed may serve at the post.

**Conclusion 2** is false. Premise 2 says only that **some** volunteers allowed at the post are health workers. Dimas may or may not be one of them.

**Conclusion 3** is true. By premise 2, some volunteers allowed at the post (posko) are health workers (tenaga kesehatan). By premise 3, those health workers do not serve in the public kitchen (dapur umum). So some volunteers allowed at the post do not serve in the public kitchen.

**Conclusion 4** is true. Premise 3 can be written as "if someone is a health worker, they do not serve in the public kitchen". Its contrapositive is equivalent:

<BlockMath math="(p \Rightarrow \neg q) \equiv (q \Rightarrow \neg p)" />

Here <InlineMath math="p" /> means "is a health worker" and <InlineMath math="q" /> means "serves in the public kitchen". So if Dimas serves in the public kitchen, Dimas is not a health worker.

**Conclusion 5** is false. It reverses premise 1. Premise 1 does not forbid volunteers without the basic training from serving at the post for some other reason.

So conclusions 1, 3, and 4 are true, and conclusions 2 and 5 are false.

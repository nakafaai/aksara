# Given

**Two study-room tariffs** for Room P and Room Q round any partial hour upward to a full hour, with a daily cap of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}72{,}000" /> for Room Q.

# Asked

Whether each statement comparing Room P and Room Q costs is true or false.

# Solution

> Round every session up to the next whole hour first, then cap Room Q at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}72{,}000" /> per day before comparing costs.

Mark each statement Benar (true) or Salah (false). Two rules make this question tricky. First, **time is rounded up to whole hours**, so <InlineMath math="3" /> hours <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes is charged as <InlineMath math="4" /> hours. Second, room Q has a daily cap of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}72{,}000" />. So turn every length of stay into charged hours first, and only then compare the costs.

Let <InlineMath math="c" /> be the time after rounding up. Measured directly in rupiah (thousand-rupiah units), the two cost formulas are

<BlockMath math="P=\begin{cases} 40, & c\le4 \\ 40+8(c-4), & c>4 \end{cases} \qquad Q=\min(10c,\ 72)" />

The symbol <InlineMath math="\min" /> means the smaller of the two values. This table holds every value you'll need (using thousand-rupiah units):

| <InlineMath math="c" /> (hours) | Room P | Room Q |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> | <InlineMath math="10" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> | <InlineMath math="20" /> |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> | <InlineMath math="30" /> |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="48" /> | <InlineMath math="50" /> |
| <InlineMath math="6" /> | <InlineMath math="56" /> | <InlineMath math="60" /> |
| <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="64" /> | <InlineMath math="70" /> |
| <InlineMath math="8" /> | <InlineMath math="72" /> | <InlineMath math="72" /> |
| <InlineMath math="9" /> | <InlineMath math="80" /> | <InlineMath math="72" /> |
| <InlineMath math="10" /> | <InlineMath math="88" /> | <InlineMath math="72" /> |

The chart below draws both costs by charged hours. **The two lines meet twice, at <InlineMath math="4" /> hours and at <InlineMath math="8" /> hours**, and after <InlineMath math="8" /> hours the Q line goes flat because of the daily cap.

**Statement 1** is Benar. Any stay longer than <InlineMath math="8" /> hours is charged as at least <InlineMath math="9" /> hours. Room P then costs at least <InlineMath math="40+8\times5=80" /> thousand and keeps rising, while room Q stays capped at <InlineMath math="72" /> thousand. If you forget the cap, Q seems to cost <InlineMath math="90" /> thousand or more, and you'd wrongly call the statement false.

**Statement 2** is Salah. A stay of <InlineMath math="5" /> hours <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes is charged as <InlineMath math="6" /> hours, so it costs <InlineMath math="40+8\times2=56" /> thousand, which isn't less than <InlineMath math="50" /> thousand. Without rounding, the cost looks like just over <InlineMath math="48" /> thousand, and the statement seems true.

**Statement 3** is Salah. One counterexample is enough. A stay of <InlineMath math="3" /> hours <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes is less than <InlineMath math="4" /> hours but is charged as <InlineMath math="4" /> hours. Room Q then costs <InlineMath math="40" /> thousand, the same as room P, so Q isn't cheaper. Q is cheaper for stays of at most <InlineMath math="3" /> hours, but not for every stay under <InlineMath math="4" /> hours.

**Statement 4** is Benar. A stay between <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="8" /> hours, such as <InlineMath math="7" /> hours <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes, is charged as <InlineMath math="8" /> hours. Room P costs <InlineMath math="40+8\times4=72" /> thousand, and room Q's <InlineMath math="80" /> thousand is cut by the cap to <InlineMath math="72" /> thousand. They're equal. Without rounding and the cap, the two costs only match at exactly <InlineMath math="4" /> hours, so the statement looks false.

# Answer

So statements 1 and 4 are Benar, and statements 2 and 3 are Salah.

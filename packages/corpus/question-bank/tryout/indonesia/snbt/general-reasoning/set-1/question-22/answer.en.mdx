Let <InlineMath math="x" /> litres be the volume of the <InlineMath math="20\%" /> solution. The volume of the <InlineMath math="50\%" /> solution is then <InlineMath math="50-x" /> litres.

Adding pure water raises the total volume to <InlineMath math="55" /> litres **without adding solute**. With volume concentrations and additive volumes, the amount of solute must be the same before and after the addition.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 0.2x+0.5(50-x) &amp;= 0.4(55) \\ 0.2x+25-0.5x &amp;= 22 \\ -0.3x &amp;= -3 \\ x &amp;= 10 \end{aligned}" />

The volume of the <InlineMath math="50\%" /> solution is therefore <InlineMath math="50-10=40" /> litres.

Check the result using the volumes you found:

<BlockMath math="\frac{0.2(10)+0.5(40)}{10+40+5}=\frac{2+20}{55}=\frac{22}{55}=0.4=40\%" />

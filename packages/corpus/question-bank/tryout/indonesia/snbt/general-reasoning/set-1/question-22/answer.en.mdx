# Given

**Two sewing teams** Merah and Biru average <InlineMath math="14" /> and <InlineMath math="29" /> garments per tailor per day, with known combined averages before and after a transfer.

# Asked

The Biru team's new average output per tailor per day after the transfer.

# Solution

> Use the combined average like a balance scale to uncover both team sizes first before recalculating Biru's new mean.

The new average of the Biru (blue) team depends on how many people are in it, but **the team sizes aren't given**. So you have to find them first from the two combined averages you do know. The main tool is that total output equals average times team size.

Step one: find the size of the Merah (red) team before the move. Call it <InlineMath math="m" />. After <InlineMath math="4" /> tailors averaging <InlineMath math="32" /> join, the Merah average becomes <InlineMath math="20" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{14m+4\times32}{m+4} &= 20 \\
14m+128 &= 20m+80 \\
48 &= 6m \\
m &= 8
\end{aligned}" />

Step two: find the size of the Biru team before the move. Call it <InlineMath math="b" />. The average over all tailors is <InlineMath math="24" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{14\times8+29b}{8+b} &= 24 \\
112+29b &= 192+24b \\
5b &= 80 \\
b &= 16
\end{aligned}" />

Step three: find the new Biru average. The team's original total is <InlineMath math="29\times16=464" /> pieces. The four tailors who move take <InlineMath math="4\times32=128" /> pieces with them, and <InlineMath math="16-4=12" /> people are left:

<BlockMath math="\frac{464-128}{12} = \frac{336}{12} = 28" />

Check with the overall average. The move doesn't add or remove any tailors, so the overall average must stay <InlineMath math="24" />. Indeed, the combined average stays <InlineMath math="24" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{20 \times 12 + 28 \times 12}{24} = \frac{576}{24} = 24" />

The chart below compares the averages before and after the move. **The Merah team rises by <InlineMath math="6" /> pieces, but the Biru team only drops by <InlineMath math="1" />**, and the overall average stays at <InlineMath math="24" />.

The other options come from a skipped step:

- <InlineMath math="29" /> pieces assumes the Biru average doesn't change. But the tailors who left were above the Biru average, so the average of those who stay has to drop.
- <InlineMath math="21" /> pieces comes from still dividing <InlineMath math="336" /> by <InlineMath math="16" />, as if the four tailors were still in the Biru team.
- <InlineMath math="23" /> pieces comes from assuming Biru falls by as much as Merah rose, <InlineMath math="29-6" />. The teams aren't the same size, so the changes aren't equal either.
- <InlineMath math="26" /> pieces comes from assuming both teams have <InlineMath math="8" /> people, which ignores the combined average of <InlineMath math="24" />.

# Answer

So after the move, the Biru team's average output is <InlineMath math="28" /> pieces per person per day.

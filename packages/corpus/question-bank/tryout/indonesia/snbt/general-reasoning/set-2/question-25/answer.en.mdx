# Given

**The Made family aims** to keep its June bill per resident at or below the Hasan family's May bill per resident using whole kilowatt-hours.

# Asked

The fewest kilowatt-hours the Made family must cut from its May usage.

# Solution

> Find Hasan's cost per resident first, scale to Made's household size, and work backward through the tariff tiers to the largest valid whole kWh.

This question joins two rows of the table. Hasan's row gives you **a limit on the bill per resident**, and you then apply that limit to Made's row and work backwards until you reach kWh.

Step one: find the Hasan family's May bill per resident. Hasan used <InlineMath math="2{,}495-2{,}385=110\text{ kWh}" />, and <InlineMath math="4" /> people live there.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Hasan's bill} &= 40(1{,}200)+70(1{,}600)+25{,}000 = 185{,}000 \\
\text{per resident} &= 185{,}000\div4 = 46{,}250
\end{aligned}" />

Step two: turn that into a limit on the Made family's bill. <InlineMath math="6" /> people live in Made's home, so Made's June bill can be at most <InlineMath math="6\times46{,}250" /> rupiah. The fixed charge still has to be paid, so the usage cost can be at most what's left.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Made's bill} &\le 6\times46{,}250 = 277{,}500 \\
\text{Made's usage cost} &\le 277{,}500-25{,}000 = 252{,}500
\end{aligned}" />

Step three: work backwards from rupiah to kWh, block by block. If the first <InlineMath math="120" /> kWh are used in full, they cost Rp<InlineMath math="176{,}000" />. That's still below <InlineMath math="252{,}500" />, so the rest of the money goes to Block III kWh.

<BlockMath math="\frac{252{,}500-176{,}000}{2{,}200} = \frac{76{,}500}{2{,}200} \approx 34.8" />

Since usage is recorded in whole kWh, Block III can hold at most <InlineMath math="34" /> kWh. Rounding up to <InlineMath math="35" /> would already break the limit. So Made's June usage can be at most <InlineMath math="120+34=154\text{ kWh}" />.

Check the boundary by working out the bill per resident for <InlineMath math="154" /> kWh and <InlineMath math="155" /> kWh.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
154\text{ kWh} &: \frac{176{,}000+34(2{,}200)+25{,}000}{6} = \frac{275{,}800}{6} \approx 45{,}967 \le 46{,}250 \\
155\text{ kWh} &: \frac{176{,}000+35(2{,}200)+25{,}000}{6} = \frac{278{,}000}{6} \approx 46{,}333 > 46{,}250
\end{aligned}" />

<InlineMath math="154" /> kWh still works, and <InlineMath math="155" /> kWh doesn't. Made used <InlineMath math="4{,}238-4{,}068=170\text{ kWh}" /> in May, so the cut must be at least <InlineMath math="170-154=16" /> kWh.

Some shortcuts that go wrong:

- Comparing usage per resident instead of bill per resident: Hasan uses <InlineMath math="110\div4=27.5" /> kWh per person, which would cap Made at <InlineMath math="6\times27.5=165" /> kWh and need a cut of only <InlineMath math="5" /> kWh. That's wrong because Made's extra kWh sit in the pricier Block III.
- Leaving out the fixed charge for both families: the limit becomes a usage cost of Rp<InlineMath math="240{,}000" />, Made's usage is capped at <InlineMath math="149" /> kWh, and the cut becomes <InlineMath math="21" /> kWh.
- Rounding <InlineMath math="34.8" /> up to <InlineMath math="35" /> gives <InlineMath math="155" /> kWh and a cut of <InlineMath math="15" /> kWh, but <InlineMath math="155" /> kWh is already over the limit.

# Answer

So the Made family has to cut its usage by at least <InlineMath math="16" /> kWh.

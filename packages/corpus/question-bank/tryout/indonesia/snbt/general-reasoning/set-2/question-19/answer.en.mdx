This is a disjunctive syllogism: one of two alternatives must occur, and the first alternative is ruled out.

Let:
- <InlineMath math="p" /> = The drainage ditch is cleaned during the neighborhood work day.
- <InlineMath math="q" /> = Reusable items are collected during the neighborhood work day.

Premise <InlineMath math="1" />: Sunday's work day involves cleaning the drainage ditch or collecting reusable items (<InlineMath math="p \lor q" />).

Premise <InlineMath math="2" />: Rain prevents the drainage-ditch work (<InlineMath math="\sim p" />).

The conclusion from these two premises is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
  \text{Premise 1} &: p \lor q \\
  \text{Premise 2} &: \sim p \\
  \hline
  \text{Conclusion} &: q
  \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Because <InlineMath math="p" /> is false while <InlineMath math="p \lor q" /> remains true, <InlineMath math="q" /> must be true. Therefore, **reusable items were collected during Sunday's neighborhood work day**.

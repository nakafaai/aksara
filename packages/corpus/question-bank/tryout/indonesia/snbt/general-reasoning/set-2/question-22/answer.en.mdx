# Given

**A two-stage debate selection** reports pass percentages taken from different base groups across stage one, resits, and stage two.

# Asked

What percentage of resit qualifiers passed stage two.

# Solution

> Pick a clean base of <InlineMath math="100" /> total applicants so every percentage turns into an easy whole count of people.

Each percentage in this problem comes from **a different group**: <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of all participants, half of those who failed stage 1, <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of those who passed directly, and <InlineMath math="52\%" /> of all participants again. The safest move is to turn everything into numbers of people from one shared starting group.

Since every figure is a percentage, you can assume there were <InlineMath math="100" /> participants at the start. Work out the size of each group first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{passed directly} &= 60\%\times100 = 60 \\
\text{failed stage 1} &= 100-60 = 40 \\
\text{passed the retake} &= \tfrac12\times40 = 20
\end{aligned}" />

Of the <InlineMath math="60" /> who passed directly, <InlineMath math="75\%" /> passed stage 2.

<BlockMath math="75\%\times60 = 45" />

Altogether, <InlineMath math="52\%\times100=52" /> people passed stage 2. Anyone who didn't come through the direct route must have come through the retake.

<BlockMath math="52-45 = 7" />

Now watch the divisor. The question asks for a percentage **of those who passed through the retake**, and that's <InlineMath math="20" /> people.

<BlockMath math="\frac{7}{20}\times100\% = 35\%" />

Check it backwards: if <InlineMath math="35\%" /> of <InlineMath math="20" /> people pass, that's <InlineMath math="7" /> people. Together with the <InlineMath math="45" /> from the direct route, that makes <InlineMath math="52" />, which matches <InlineMath math="52\%" /> of <InlineMath math="100" /> participants.

The diagram below traces <InlineMath math="100" /> participants through both stages. **The right divisor is the <InlineMath math="20" /> people who passed through the retake**, not all the participants.

The other options come from a wrong divisor or a wrong average:

- <InlineMath math="7\%" /> stops at <InlineMath math="7" /> people out of the original <InlineMath math="100" />. It hasn't been divided by the retake group yet.
- <InlineMath math="17.5\%" /> divides <InlineMath math="7" /> by <InlineMath math="40" />, everyone who failed stage 1. But half of them dropped out at the retake and never sat stage 2.
- <InlineMath math="55\%" /> first finds the stage-2 pass rate for everyone in stage 2, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{52}{80}=65\%" />, then treats <InlineMath math="65\%" /> as a plain average of <InlineMath math="75\%" /> and the answer. That average should be weighted <InlineMath math="60" /> people to <InlineMath math="20" /> people, not equally.
- <InlineMath math="65\%" /> is the stage-2 pass rate for both routes combined, not for the retake route alone.

# Answer

So **<InlineMath math="35\%" />** of the participants who passed through the retake went on to pass stage 2.

A negation must be true **exactly when the original statement is false**. The original statement is false as soon as one passing vehicle fails to meet both conditions together.

Work in two steps:

1. The negation of "every passing vehicle meets the condition" is "**some** passing vehicle does not meet the condition".
2. The condition has the form "pays the toll **and** has an electronic card". The negation of a conjunction is the disjunction of the negations: "does not pay the toll **or** does not have a card".

<BlockMath math="\neg(p \wedge q) \equiv \neg p \vee \neg q" />

The result: some vehicle passing on that road does not pay the toll or does not have an electronic card.

The other options are wrong:

- The option starting "All vehicles ... do not pay the toll and do not have a card" keeps "all" instead of switching to "some" and negates "and" incorrectly.
- The option "Some vehicle ... does not pay the toll and does not have a card" is too narrow. A vehicle that pays but has no card already makes the original statement false.
- Vehicles that do not pass on the road are not part of the original statement.
- "No passing vehicle pays the toll or has a card" is far stronger than a negation.

So the negation is "Some vehicle passing on that road does not pay the toll or does not have an electronic card".

A necessary assumption is a statement that has to be true for the conclusion to stand. The way to test one is the **negation test**: flip the statement. If the conclusion falls apart, the statement is necessary. If the conclusion could still be true, the statement only helps at most.

Start with the marine affairs head's evidence. Work out the living seedlings per kilometer of coast and the survival rate in each village.

| Village | Living seedlings per km | Survival rate | Drop in retreat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Karangsari | <InlineMath math="2{,}000 : 2 = 1{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="10\%" /> | <InlineMath math="0.2" /> m |
| Muarabaru | <InlineMath math="3{,}500 : 1 = 3{,}500" /> | <InlineMath math="70\%" /> | <InlineMath math="2.5" /> m |
| Pasirkuning | <InlineMath math="6{,}000 : 3 = 2{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="50\%" /> | <InlineMath math="1.5" /> m |
| Lontar | <InlineMath math="1{,}600 : 2 = 800" /> | <InlineMath math="20\%" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> m |

This table only shows that dense mangroves and a slowing shoreline appear together. The head reads it in one direction: dense mangroves cause the slowdown. But survival rates that differ so much, from <InlineMath math="10\%" /> to <InlineMath math="70\%" />, open up the reverse direction: **on a coast that was already calmer, seedlings survive more easily**, so the mangroves end up dense. If that reverse link were the only one, planting more in Lontar wouldn't slow its shoreline at all.

Now test each option.

- The mangroves helped cut the retreat, not merely resulted from a calm coast. Negation: dense mangroves didn't help cut the retreat at all and only appeared because the coast was already calm. Then making Lontar's mangroves dense changes nothing. The conclusion collapses, so this statement is necessary.
- Lontar can plant <InlineMath math="20{,}000" /> seedlings. The conclusion has the form "if its living seedlings reach <InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> per kilometer, then ...", so it doesn't need Lontar to actually manage that. The number is off anyway. Lontar needs <InlineMath math="2 \times 2{,}000 = 4{,}000" /> living seedlings. If its <InlineMath math="1{,}600" /> current seedlings stay alive, it's <InlineMath math="2{,}400" /> short, and at a <InlineMath math="20\%" /> survival rate it only needs to plant <InlineMath math="2{,}400 : 0.2 = 12{,}000" /> seedlings.
- A survival rate as high as Muarabaru's is also only about how to meet the "if" condition. With a lower rate, Lontar just plants more, and the conclusion stays intact.
- No breakwaters in Muarabaru and Pasirkuning. Negation: there were breakwaters. The mangroves could still have helped cut the retreat alongside them, so the conclusion doesn't necessarily collapse. This statement helps, but it's too strong to be necessary.
- <InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> as the lowest density that works. Negation: a lower density works too. The conclusion only talks about at least <InlineMath math="2{,}000" />, so it's still intact.

So the necessary assumption is that dense mangroves in Muarabaru and Pasirkuning helped cut the retreat there, rather than merely resulting from a coast that was already calm.

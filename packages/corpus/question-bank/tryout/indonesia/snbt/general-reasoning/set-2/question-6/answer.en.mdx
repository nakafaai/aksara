Two facts seem to clash. First, in all four villages the shoreline retreat slowed down or stayed the same. Second, the land lost along the whole north coast of the regency went up. The explanation you want makes both facts true at once, and naming a cause that points the right way isn't enough. It also has to be big enough.

Think of the north coast as two parts: the coast of the four villages, and the coast outside them. The land lost along the whole coast is the sum of those two parts. If the first part shrinks, the total can only grow if the second part loses extra land that's bigger than that shrinkage.

First, work out the shrinkage in the four villages. Land lost per year is the average retreat times the length of coast, with <InlineMath math="1\text{ km} = 1{,}000\text{ m}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{before: } 4.0(2{,}000) + 3.0(1{,}000) + 2.5(3{,}000) + 2.0(2{,}000) &= 22{,}500 \text{ m}^2 \text{ per year} \\ \text{after: } 3.8(2{,}000) + 0.5(1{,}000) + 1.0(3{,}000) + 2.0(2{,}000) &= 15{,}100 \text{ m}^2 \text{ per year} \end{aligned}" />

The four villages lose <InlineMath math="22{,}500 - 15{,}100 = 7{,}400\text{ m}^2" /> less each year. So the coast outside them has to **get worse by more than that improvement**: each year it has to lose more than <InlineMath math="7{,}400\text{ m}^2" /> extra.

Second, work out the extra loss in the two options that both say the coast outside the villages got worse.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{near the port: } 3{,}000 \times 4 &= 12{,}000 \text{ m}^2 \text{ per year} \\ \text{other coast: } 40{,}000 \times 0.1 &= 4{,}000 \text{ m}^2 \text{ per year} \end{aligned}" />

The extra loss near the port, <InlineMath math="12{,}000\text{ m}^2" />, is bigger than <InlineMath math="7{,}400\text{ m}^2" />, so the regency's total rises by about <InlineMath math="4{,}600\text{ m}^2" /> a year. Both facts are then true together. The extra loss along the <InlineMath math="40" /> km of other coast is only <InlineMath math="4{,}000\text{ m}^2" />, so the regency's total still falls by about <InlineMath math="3{,}400\text{ m}^2" /> a year. A long stretch with a small change loses out to a short stretch with a big one.

The other options don't do the job:

- The fact that most of the coast lies outside the four villages is needed, but it isn't enough. If the coast outside kept retreating at its old rate, the regency's total would still fall by <InlineMath math="7{,}400\text{ m}^2" /> a year.
- Lots of dead seedlings in Karangsari and Lontar only explains why those two villages barely improved. A rate that hardly changes can't push the total up.
- Why the four villages were chosen explains the choice of location, not the rise in the total. If anything, if the parts that used to erode fastest are now slower, the total should fall even more.

So the best explanation is that about <InlineMath math="3" /> km of coast near the new port retreats <InlineMath math="4" /> m a year faster, because that extra loss is big enough to cancel and outweigh the improvement in the four villages.

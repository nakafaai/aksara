The question asks us to continue the **shortest cycle of changes** consistent with the data. The predictions use that assumption. Five sales figures alone do not guarantee the same pattern in real sales.

#### Find the next sales figures

Calculate the **difference between consecutive years**. For example, shirts fall from <InlineMath math="50" /> to <InlineMath math="35" />, so the first change is <InlineMath math="35-50=-15" />. Apply the same subtraction to each pair of years.

| Item | Known changes | Cycle | Prediction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shirts | <InlineMath math="-15,-15,+50,-15" /> | <InlineMath math="-15,-15,+50" /> | <InlineMath math="55-15=40" /> |
| Pants | <InlineMath math="0,-10,-10,0" /> | <InlineMath math="0,-10,-10" /> | <InlineMath math="40-10=30" /> |
| Suits | <InlineMath math="+15,-10,+15,-10" /> | <InlineMath math="+15,-10" /> | <InlineMath math="50+15=65" /> |

#### Check the choices

The first statement says **less than**, but shirts and pants add up to exactly the stated boundary:

<BlockMath math="40+30=70,\qquad 70\not<70" />

The other four statements follow from these two differences:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}40-30&amp;=10\\65-30&amp;=35\end{aligned}" />

Shirts exceed pants by ten, or equivalently pants are ten fewer than shirts. Suits exceed pants by thirty-five, or equivalently pants are thirty-five fewer than suits. The first option is therefore the incorrect prediction.

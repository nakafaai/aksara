The stock at the end of a month depends on **all earlier months**, not just that month's production. Compute each month-end stock in turn with

<BlockMath math="\text{end stock}=\text{starting stock}+\text{production}-\text{sales}" />

The table rows run from *Januari* (January) to *Juni* (June), with *Produksi* (production) and *Penjualan* (sales).

| Month | Calculation | End stock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January | <InlineMath math="10+30-25" /> | <InlineMath math="15" /> |
| February | <InlineMath math="15+40-30" /> | <InlineMath math="25" /> |
| March | <InlineMath math="25+25-35" /> | <InlineMath math="15" /> |
| April | <InlineMath math="15+35-20" /> | <InlineMath math="30" /> |
| May | <InlineMath math="30+30-40" /> | <InlineMath math="20" /> |
| June | <InlineMath math="20+20-25" /> | <InlineMath math="15" /> |

The largest stock is <InlineMath math="30" /> wardrobes at the end of April.

February is a trap because its production is the largest, <InlineMath math="40" />. But the February end stock is only <InlineMath math="25" />, because sales were also high and the month started with less stock. March, May, and June sold more than they produced, so stock fell in those months.

So the stock of wardrobes is largest at the end of April.

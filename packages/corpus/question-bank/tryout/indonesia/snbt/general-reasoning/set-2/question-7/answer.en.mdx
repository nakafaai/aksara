The app's claim is causal: studying on the app for more than two hours a day **makes** students pass. A figure of <InlineMath math="85\%" /> from a single group cannot support such a claim. It only means something next to **a comparison group**.

Suppose students who study less than two hours or do not use the app also pass at about <InlineMath math="85\%" />. Then the app shows no effect at all. Without a comparison, the operators cannot tell whether <InlineMath math="85\%" /> is high, ordinary, or low. That is the main flaw.

The other options are not the main flaw:

- Percentages are a fair way to compare groups of different sizes.
- The difficulty of the exam does not decide whether the app causes passing.
- An online survey may be a minor issue, but it matters far less than the missing comparison.
- The passage says the operators counted only users who studied more than two hours, not all users.

So the main flaw is the lack of a comparison with the pass rate of students who studied less than two hours or did not use the app.

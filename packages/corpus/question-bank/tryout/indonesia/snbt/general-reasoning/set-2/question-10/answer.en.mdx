Each box contains <InlineMath math="12" /> fruits. The independent, uniform selections produce <InlineMath math="12\times12=144" /> equally likely pairs.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}1+2+4+5&=12\\4+2+3+3&=12\end{aligned}" />

| Combination | Number of pairs | Probability |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> Large apples | <InlineMath math="1\times4=4" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{144}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> Small apples | <InlineMath math="2\times2=4" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{144}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> Large oranges | <InlineMath math="4\times3=12" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{144}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> Small oranges | <InlineMath math="5\times3=15" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{15}{144}" /> |
| Large apple and small orange | <InlineMath math="3+20=23" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{23}{144}" /> |

For the mixed combination, the large apple may come from either box. The two disjoint cases give <InlineMath math="1\times3=3" /> and <InlineMath math="5\times4=20" /> pairs, for a total of <InlineMath math="3+20=23" />.

Among the answer options, the largest probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{23}{144}" />, for **one large apple and one small orange**.

Each term has two parts, a letter and a number, and each part follows its own rule at the same time. To do arithmetic with the letters, first turn each letter into its position in the alphabet, from <InlineMath math="\text{A} = 1" /> to <InlineMath math="\text{Z} = 26" />.

| Term | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Letter | B | D | G | L | U |
| Letter position | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="12" /> | <InlineMath math="21" /> |
| Number | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="9" /> | <InlineMath math="17" /> |

The first rule is in the numbers. Each number is twice the previous one, minus one.

<BlockMath math="2 \times 2 - 1 = 3,\quad 2 \times 3 - 1 = 5,\quad 2 \times 5 - 1 = 9,\quad 2 \times 9 - 1 = 17" />

The second rule links the letters to the numbers. The jump in letter position from one term to the next equals **the number in the term you're looking at**.

<BlockMath math="2 + 2 = 4,\quad 4 + 3 = 7,\quad 7 + 5 = 12,\quad 12 + 9 = 21" />

Now go two steps further. For the 6th term, the number is <InlineMath math="2 \times 17 - 1 = 33" />, and the letter position is <InlineMath math="21 + 17 = 38" />. The alphabet only goes up to <InlineMath math="26" />, so wrap back around to A by subtracting <InlineMath math="26" />.

<BlockMath math="38 - 26 = 12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{L}, \qquad \text{6th term} = \text{L}33" />

For the 7th term, the number is <InlineMath math="2 \times 33 - 1 = 65" />, and L moves forward by <InlineMath math="33" />, the number in the 6th term.

<BlockMath math="12 + 33 = 45,\quad 45 - 26 = 19 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{S}, \qquad \text{7th term} = \text{S}65" />

Check it another way. Without wrapping, the letter positions are <InlineMath math="2, 4, 7, 12, 21, 38, 71" />, and the gaps between them are <InlineMath math="2, 3, 5, 9, 17, 33" />, exactly the numbers in the sequence. Position <InlineMath math="71" /> wraps around twice: <InlineMath math="71 - 52 = 19" />, which is S. Same answer.

The other options come from these slips:

- <InlineMath math="\text{L}33" /> is the 6th term. You get it if you stop one step too early.
- <InlineMath math="\text{O}65" /> comes from moving the letter by the new term's number instead of the current one: <InlineMath math="21 + 33 = 54" /> gives B, then <InlineMath math="2 + 65 = 67" /> gives O. That rule doesn't fit the data, because B moved by <InlineMath math="3" /> gives E, not D.
- <InlineMath math="\text{T}68" /> comes from treating the numbers as plain doubling, so the next ones are <InlineMath math="34" /> and <InlineMath math="68" />. That rule doesn't fit the data either, because <InlineMath math="2 \times 9 = 18" />, not <InlineMath math="17" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{F}129" /> is the 8th term. You get it if you go one step too far.

So the 7th term of the sequence is <InlineMath math="\text{S}65" />.

The operators claim that **the time spent studying on the app** causes passing. The claim is weakened most by **another cause** that the same group shares.

If users who study more than two hours a day usually also attend in-person tutoring every week, their success could come from the tutoring. Both habits could also stem from the same thing, very committed students. The app may then play no role.

The other options do not weaken the claim:

- The number of registered users says nothing about whether the app causes passing.
- A <InlineMath math="40\%" /> pass rate among users who study less than two hours actually **strengthens** the link between study time on the app and passing.
- Users admitted to private universities do not concern the group behind the claim.
- Material written by experienced teachers tends to support the claim.

So the statement that weakens the claim most is that users who study more than two hours usually also attend in-person tutoring.

A causal claim is strengthened by information that **fits how the cause would work**. If a later start reduces lateness, the benefit should be strongest for students who most need extra time in the morning.

Students who live farthest away need the longest travel time. If lateness dropped most for them, the pattern matches the mechanism "extra morning time helps students arrive on time". This statement strengthens the claim.

The other statements do not strengthen it:

- Stricter penalties in the same year are **another cause** that could also reduce lateness. This statement actually weakens the claim.
- Both groups use varied transport, so it does not explain any difference between them.
- Some students still being late neither refutes nor supports the claim, since lateness never fell to zero.
- How exam scores relate to start time says nothing about what caused lateness to fall.

So the statement that strengthens the claim most is that lateness dropped most among students who live farthest away.

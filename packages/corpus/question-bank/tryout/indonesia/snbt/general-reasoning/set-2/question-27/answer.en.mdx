The key is the **distance the train covers in each event**, not just the time.

When passing the pole, the front of the train moves from the pole until the rear leaves it. That distance equals the train's length, <InlineMath math="150\text{ m}" />, so the speed is

<BlockMath math="v=\frac{150\text{ m}}{10\text{ s}}=15\text{ m/s}" />

When passing the tunnel, the front must cover **the tunnel length plus the train length**. The front enters, travels the whole tunnel, and then must move another train length so the rear comes out:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L+150 &= 15\times40=600 \\
L &= 450\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

The other options come from misreading the distance:

- <InlineMath math="600\text{ m}" /> is the whole distance covered, without subtracting the train length.
- <InlineMath math="750\text{ m}" /> comes from adding the train length to <InlineMath math="600\text{ m}" /> instead.
- <InlineMath math="300\text{ m}" /> comes from subtracting the train length twice.
- <InlineMath math="525\text{ m}" /> comes from subtracting only half the train length.

So the tunnel is <InlineMath math="450\text{ m}" /> long.

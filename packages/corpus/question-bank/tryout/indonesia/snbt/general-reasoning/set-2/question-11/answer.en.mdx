You need to separate the statements the four facts guarantee from the ones that merely could happen. Notice that facts 1, 2 and 4 are only "all" or "none" rules. Rules like that don't promise that even one stall exists. **Only fact 3 guarantees that a stall really exists**, because *sebagian* ("some") means at least one. So every "some" statement that must be true has to rest on that stall.

Call that stall the Melati stall: a student-run stall that sells woven crafts (*anyaman*). Now follow the Melati stall through the other facts.

- Fact 4: the Melati stall sells woven crafts, so it's on the second floor.
- Fact 2: the Melati stall is run by students, so it doesn't accept cashless payment.
- Fact 1 in contrapositive form. The contrapositive of "if <InlineMath math="P" />, then <InlineMath math="Q" />" is "if not <InlineMath math="Q" />, then not <InlineMath math="P" />", and the two always mean the same thing. So a stall that doesn't accept cashless payment can't sell hand-drawn batik (*batik tulis*). That means the Melati stall doesn't sell hand-drawn batik.

In short, the Melati stall is on the second floor, sells woven crafts, doesn't accept cashless payment and doesn't sell hand-drawn batik.

For a statement that isn't guaranteed, one fair that meets all four facts but makes the statement false is enough. Picture a fair with these three stalls.

| Stall | Run by | Sells | Cashless | Floor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Melati | Students | Woven crafts | No | Second |
| Kenanga | Adult artisans | Hand-drawn batik | Yes | First |
| Cempaka | Adult artisans | Woven crafts | Yes | Second |

All four facts hold. The batik stall, Kenanga, accepts cashless payment. The student stall, Melati, doesn't. Melati shows that a student stall selling woven crafts exists. Both woven-craft stalls, Melati and Cempaka, are on the second floor.

Now judge the four statements.

- "No stall selling woven crafts accepts cashless payment" is not certainly true. Fact 2 only bans cashless payment for student stalls. Cempaka sells woven crafts and accepts cashless payment without breaking anything. The trap is carrying the Melati stall's traits over to every woven-craft stall.
- "Some stalls on the second floor don't accept cashless payment" must be true. The Melati stall is on the second floor and doesn't accept cashless payment, and fact 3 guarantees it exists.
- "Some stalls selling woven crafts don't sell hand-drawn batik" must be true. The Melati stall sells woven crafts, and by the contrapositive of fact 1, it doesn't sell hand-drawn batik.
- "Some stalls selling hand-drawn batik are on the second floor" is not certainly true. No fact links batik to the second floor, and in the example above the only batik stall, Kenanga, is on the first floor.

So the second and third statements go under *Pasti benar* (must be true), while the first and fourth go under *Tidak pasti benar* (not certainly true).

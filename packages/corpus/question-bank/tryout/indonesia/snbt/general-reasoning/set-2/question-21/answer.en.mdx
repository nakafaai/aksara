# Given

**The alternating sequence** begins <InlineMath math="2, 4, 5, 11, 10, 18, 17, 25" /> and continues by the same rule.

# Asked

The sum of the <InlineMath math="20" />th and <InlineMath math="21" />st terms.

# Solution

> Split the sequence into odd-position and even-position tracks so you can jump straight to far-out terms without listing them all.

This question asks for two terms far down the list, the <InlineMath math="20" />th and the <InlineMath math="21" />st. You need to find the pattern, then **translate each position** into a position inside that pattern.

The differences between neighbouring terms go up and down: <InlineMath math="+2,\ +1,\ +6,\ -1,\ +8,\ -1,\ +8" />. Differences like these don't form a single pattern. That tells you the list is two sequences woven together. Split it into the terms in odd positions and the terms in even positions.

The odd-position terms (1st, 3rd, 5th, 7th) are <InlineMath math="2,\ 5,\ 10,\ 17" />. Their differences are <InlineMath math="3,\ 5,\ 7" />, growing by <InlineMath math="2" /> each time. These are square numbers plus one: <InlineMath math="k^2 + 1" /> for <InlineMath math="k = 1, 2, 3, 4" />. So the <InlineMath math="k" />th odd-position term is <InlineMath math="k^2+1" />, and it sits in position <InlineMath math="2k-1" />.

The even-position terms (2nd, 4th, 6th, 8th) are <InlineMath math="4,\ 11,\ 18,\ 25" />. Each one adds <InlineMath math="7" />. So the <InlineMath math="k" />th even-position term is <InlineMath math="7k - 3" />, and it sits in position <InlineMath math="2k" />.

Position <InlineMath math="20" /> is even, so that term belongs to the even sequence with <InlineMath math="2k=20" />, which means <InlineMath math="k=10" />. Position <InlineMath math="21" /> is odd, so that term belongs to the odd sequence with <InlineMath math="2k-1=21" />, which means <InlineMath math="k=11" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
U_{20} &= 7(10)-3 = 67 \\
U_{21} &= 11^2+1 = 122 \\
U_{20}+U_{21} &= 67+122 = 189
\end{aligned}" />

Check by extending both sequences. Odd sequence: <InlineMath math="2, 5, 10, \dots, 101, 122" />, and its <InlineMath math="11" />th term really is <InlineMath math="122" />. Even sequence: <InlineMath math="4, 11, 18, \dots, 60, 67" />, and its <InlineMath math="10" />th term really is <InlineMath math="67" />.

Some tempting routes that go wrong:

- Plugging <InlineMath math="20" /> and <InlineMath math="21" /> straight into the formulas gives <InlineMath math="7(20)-3=137" /> and <InlineMath math="21^2+1=442" />, which add up to <InlineMath math="579" />. The positions in the combined list haven't been turned into positions in each sequence.
- Using <InlineMath math="k=10" /> for both terms gives <InlineMath math="67+101=168" />. Position <InlineMath math="21" /> is the <InlineMath math="11" />th odd-position term, not the <InlineMath math="10" />th.
- Swapping the sequences, so the square rule goes to position <InlineMath math="20" /> and the add-<InlineMath math="7" /> rule to position <InlineMath math="21" />, gives <InlineMath math="101+74=175" />.

# Answer

So the <InlineMath math="20" />th and <InlineMath math="21" />st terms add up to **<InlineMath math="189" />**.

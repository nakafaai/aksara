# Given

In Text A, <InlineMath math="18" /> out of <InlineMath math="32" /> students could not explain their essays. Applying the same proportion to a cohort of <InlineMath math="160" /> students, **half** of those initially unable to explain become able to explain after implementing Text B's proposal.

# Asked

The number and percentage of cohort students who can now explain their essays after Text B's proposal is applied.

# Solution

Calculate the **final count and share of students** who can explain their essays step by step.

## Initial split and newly able students

Find the **initial split and newly able students**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{18}{32} \times 160 = 90" />
<BlockMath math="160 - 90 = 70" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{2} \times 90 = 45" />
</MathContainer>

## Final count and percentage

Add the improving students to obtain the **final count and cohort percentage**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="70 + 45 = 115" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{115}{160} \times 100\% = 71{,}875\% \approx 72\%" />
</MathContainer>

Check why the other options fail:

| Distractor | Error source |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="70" /> students | Gives only the baseline before Text B's proposal takes effect. |
| <InlineMath math="45" /> students | Counts only the students who improved, omitting the initial <InlineMath math="70" />. |
| <InlineMath math="115" /> students at <InlineMath math="56\%" /> | Right count, wrong share because <InlineMath math="115" /> of <InlineMath math="160" /> is about <InlineMath math="72\%" />. |
| <InlineMath math="105" /> students | Mistakenly adds half of <InlineMath math="70" /> instead of half of <InlineMath math="90" />. |

> Take half only of the group initially unable to explain, then add that gain to the students who could already explain from the start.

# Answer

Now **<InlineMath math="115" /> students** can explain their essays, which is roughly **<InlineMath math="72\%" />** of the cohort.

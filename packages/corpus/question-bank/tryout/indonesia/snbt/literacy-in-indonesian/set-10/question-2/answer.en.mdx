The question asks for a conclusion that doesn't go beyond the counts at the estuary. The key is in two counts taken at different times.

Paragraph 5 gives two pairs of figures. At high tide, the count on the pond walls fell from <InlineMath math="1{,}200" /> to <InlineMath math="450" /> birds. At low tide, the count on the mud only shifted from <InlineMath math="1{,}100" /> to <InlineMath math="1{,}050" />, and **"luas lumpur tidak berubah"** (the mudflat area didn't change).

Combine the two counts. Almost as many birds feed on the mud at low tide, so they haven't left the estuary. Only the birds roosting on the walls at high tide fell sharply. So **many birds still feed here but roost elsewhere**. Paragraph 2 explains the cost: if the roost is far, **"sebagian cadangan yang baru dikumpulkan habis untuk terbang pulang-pergi"** (part of the reserves just gathered is spent flying back and forth). The sandbar across the estuary is about <InlineMath math="6" /> kilometres away, so that's a real possibility.

Why the other options are wrong:

- The population more than halved: <InlineMath math="450" /> out of <InlineMath math="1{,}200" /> is indeed a fall of more than half, but it's a count at one roost, not the population.
- The road reduced prey: the text doesn't say prey declined, and the mudflat area is unchanged.

So the safe conclusion is that birds still feed at the estuary, but many roost elsewhere, which can drain their fat reserves if the new roost is far.

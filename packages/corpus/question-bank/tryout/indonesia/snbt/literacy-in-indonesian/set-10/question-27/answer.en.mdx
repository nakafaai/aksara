Stanza one says Grandfather **wound the clock every Monday morning, seven turns, no more**, while **counting softly** like someone praying.

- The late hands appear after Grandfather has died, and Mother refuses to fix them.
- *Berdoa* (praying) is only a comparison for how he counted.
- The guest reluctant to leave is an image for the clock, not something Grandfather did.
- The poem says **no more** than seven turns, so not as many as possible.

So every Monday morning Grandfather wound the clock seven turns while counting softly.

# Given

The passage offers three reasons why Malay was chosen as the unifying language. The question asks which **shared ideal links the second and third explanations** and why.

# Asked

The shared ideal connecting the second and third explanations for choosing Malay and the reason behind that link.

# Solution

Place the second and third explanations side by side to isolate their common principle.

- **Second explanation**. Javanese speech levels **"require speakers to choose words according to the listener's social rank"**, whereas Malay **"better suited a national movement seeking to treat all citizens as equals"**.
- **Third explanation**. The text notes that **"choosing Javanese could look like favoring one ethnic group"**.

Both points rest on the **ideal of equality**: equality among individuals regardless of status and equality across ethnic groups.

Check why the other options fail:

| Distractor | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Simplicity of bazaar Malay | Not presented as the second or third explanation. |
| Largest number of native speakers | Explicitly not the basis, since Javanese had the most native speakers yet was not chosen. |
| Lexical purity | The passage states Indonesian **"absorbed many words"** from other languages. |
| Use by the colonial government | Mentioned as historical background rather than the second or third explanation. |

> Equality guided the choice both between individuals of different social ranks and between different ethnic groups.

# Answer

Both the second and third explanations reflect the **ideal of equality**, since speech levels mark rank and choosing one group's tongue could appear to privilege that group.

# Given

In the teacher's reasoning, the comparison group with no extra classes estimates how much scores rise on their own:

- **Comparison group**: mean score rose from <InlineMath math="46" /> to <InlineMath math="52" />.
- **Extra-class group**: initial mean score was <InlineMath math="45" />.

# Asked

The predicted second-test mean for the extra-class students had they skipped the class, following the teacher's reasoning.

# Solution

Take the **comparison group gain** as the expected no-treatment rise, then apply it to the extra-class group's starting mean.

## Natural gain and predicted second score

Compute the comparison group's gain and add it to the starting mean of <InlineMath math="45" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="52 - 46 = 6" />
<BlockMath math="45 + 6 = 51" />
</MathContainer>

As the teacher explains, the lowest-scoring students tend to move upward **"on their own"** by that amount even without extra instruction.

Check why the other options fail:

| Distractor | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Staying flat at <InlineMath math="45" /> | The teacher explicitly notes lowest scores tend to rise even with no intervention. |
| Attributing all <InlineMath math="10" /> points to regression | The class effect is separated from regression by comparing against the <InlineMath math="6" />-point control gain. |
| Dropping below <InlineMath math="45" /> | Highest scorers tend to drop, whereas lowest scorers move upward toward the mean. |
| Using <InlineMath math="52" /> directly | <InlineMath math="52" /> started from <InlineMath math="46" />, not from <InlineMath math="45" />. |

> Apply the comparison group's natural gain to the treatment group's own baseline rather than copying the comparison group's final score.

# Answer

Following the teacher's reasoning, their **second-test average without extra classes** would be roughly <InlineMath math="51" />.

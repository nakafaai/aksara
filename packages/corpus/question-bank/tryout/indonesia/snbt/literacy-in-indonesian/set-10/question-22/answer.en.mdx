# Given

The passage notes that the Kedukan Bukit Inscription is **"dated <InlineMath math="683" /> CE"** and **"written in Old Malay"**. The question asks for the **most defensible conclusion** and **its exact limit**.

# Asked

The most defensible conclusion from the Kedukan Bukit Inscription and the exact boundary of that evidence.

# Solution

Separate what a single inscription proves from claims that overreach the evidence.

- **Valid conclusion**. The year <InlineMath math="683" /> CE falls in the **seventh century**, proving that **Malay was already used for written records in the region by the seventh century**.
- **Limit of the evidence**. A single inscription only attests to the language used in that text and **does not reveal the everyday mother tongue of all inhabitants**.

Check why the other options overreach:

| Distractor | Why it overreaches |
| --- | --- |
| Modern Indonesian equals Old Malay | The text explicitly says modern Indonesian **"is not the same as bazaar Malay"**, let alone Old Malay. |
| Malay was every resident's mother tongue | One inscription cannot establish the daily speech of an entire kingdom. |
| Malay is older than other world languages | The text makes no comparison of language ages worldwide. |
| No other language was written | One artifact never proves the absence of writing in other languages. |

> One dated inscription proves written use at that time, yet never establishes the everyday mother tongue of an entire population.

# Answer

The inscription shows **Malay was used in writing by the seventh century**, with the limit that **one inscription cannot establish the everyday language of all residents**.

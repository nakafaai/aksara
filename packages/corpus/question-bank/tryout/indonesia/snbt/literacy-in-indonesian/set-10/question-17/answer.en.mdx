In April two things changed at once: **the weather became drier** and **bikes were added**. To separate them, you need a comparison that has the same change in weather but **a fixed number of bikes**. If trips in that similar city also rose in April, the rise was probably due to the weather.

- Bikes broken in June do not explain the rise in April.
- Opinions about the bikes' colour concern neither the weather nor the number of bikes.
- January data alone contain no change between wet and dry seasons.
- The population does not change from month to month, so it cannot separate the two effects.

So the most useful data are the monthly trips in a similar city that kept its 200 bikes.

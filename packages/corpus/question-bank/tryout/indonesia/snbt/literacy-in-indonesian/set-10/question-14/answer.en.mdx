# Given

The author of Text A suspects that <InlineMath math="18" /> students' essays were generated by an AI app because those students could not explain them orally. The question asks which finding would **most weaken** that suspicion.

# Asked

The finding that, if true, would most weaken the author of Text A's suspicion of AI-written essays.

# Solution

Text A's inference rests on one causal link: **failing to explain an essay proves the student did not write it**. The strongest way to undercut that link is to show **the same failure happens on essays unmistakably written by the students themselves**.

- **Strongest weakener**. If the same students also fail to explain essays they wrote in class right in front of the teacher, weak oral explanations no longer point to AI use. Another cause, such as nervousness or difficulty speaking, explains the result.

Check why the other options fail to weaken the claim:

| Distractor | Effect on the suspicion |
| --- | --- |
| Detector flags the essays as AI-written | Supports the suspicion instead, even though Text B calls detectors **"unreliable"**. |
| Other students have different writing styles | Does not address why these <InlineMath math="18" /> students could not explain their essays. |
| AI tools are easy to use at home | Makes AI use more plausible rather than less. |
| Essays use terms not yet taught | Strengthens the suspicion, matching students who **"did not know the meaning of terms they wrote"**. |

> The sharpest way to undercut a causal suspicion is showing the exact same failure occurs when the suspected cause is definitely absent.

# Answer

The finding that most weakens the suspicion is that **the same students also fail to explain essays they wrote themselves in class**.

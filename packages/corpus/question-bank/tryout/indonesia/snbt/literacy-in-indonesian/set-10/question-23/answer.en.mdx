# Given

The first paragraph introduces the <InlineMath math="683" /> CE **Kedukan Bukit Inscription**, and the second describes Malay as a port lingua franca. The third then poses the puzzle of why Malay was chosen over Javanese despite far fewer native speakers.

# Asked

The function of the first and second paragraphs in answering the puzzle raised in the third paragraph.

# Solution

Trace which part of the fourth paragraph's answer directly draws on the facts laid out in the first two paragraphs.

- **Foundation in the opening paragraphs**. Malay had written roots going back to <InlineMath math="683" /> CE and served **"for centuries"** as a port intermediary used by traders across ethnic groups.
- **Link to the answer**. The first explanation in the fourth paragraph states that Malay **"already crossed ethnic and island boundaries as a language of trade and administration"**. Thus **the opening two paragraphs lay the factual groundwork for that first explanation**.

Check why the other options fail:

| Distractor | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Showing Malay had more native speakers | The third paragraph explicitly says Javanese had **"far more"** native speakers. |
| Preparing the Javanese speech-level point | Speech levels appear only in the fourth paragraph with no setup in the opening two. |
| Equating modern Indonesian with bazaar Malay | The text insists modern Indonesian **"is not the same as bazaar Malay"**. |
| Proving Srivijaya mandated Malay for every group | The passage mentions no such mandate. |

> Opening historical paragraphs often lay down the factual groundwork that directly powers the first explanation later in the passage.

# Answer

The first two paragraphs **build the foundation for the first explanation**, establishing that **Malay was already widely known and used across ethnic groups and islands**.

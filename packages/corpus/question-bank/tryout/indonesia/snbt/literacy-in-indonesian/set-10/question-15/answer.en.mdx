# Given

The school wants **one policy most likely to be accepted by both the author of Text A and the author of Text B**. Every option includes oral explanations, paired with different extra conditions.

# Asked

The single policy most likely to be accepted by both the author of Text A and the author of Text B.

# Solution

Both authors already agree on oral explanations: Text A uses them, and Text B recommends them **"just as the author of Text A did"**. The deciding factor is therefore the **extra condition**, which must not conflict with either author's stated position.

Test each extra condition against both authors:

| Extra condition | Authors' positions |
| --- | --- |
| Relying on AI detectors | Rejected by Text B, which calls detectors **"unreliable"**. |
| Imposing a blanket ban | Rejected by Text B as **"difficult to enforce"**. |
| Broadly permitting AI use | Rejected by Text A, which wants a ban, while Text B only permits it **"for some assignments"** under strict rules. |
| Grading based on neatness | Rejected by Text A because homework trains thinking **"not producing neat writing"**, and Text B insists **"students' thinking must be graded"**. |
| Oral explanation without contested add-ons | **Acceptable to both**, since it centers on student understanding without forcing a rejected clause. |

> A workable consensus keeps what both authors endorse and strips away every extra condition that either author rejects.

# Answer

The policy most likely acceptable to both authors is **requiring oral explanation without any extra provision that either author rejects**.

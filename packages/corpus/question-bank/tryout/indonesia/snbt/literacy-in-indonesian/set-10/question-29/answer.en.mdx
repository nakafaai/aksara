# Given

Regression to the mean occurs when a case is **selected for an extreme score** on one measurement and part of that extreme reflects **temporary luck** that does not repeat. On the next measurement, the score tends to drift back toward average without needing another cause.

# Asked

All situations explainable by regression to the mean as described in the passage.

# Solution

Test each scenario against both hallmarks of regression to the mean: **selection on an extreme value** and **transient random variation**.

| Scenario | Verdict | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top goal-scoring player in a season | **Explained** | Selected for an outlier season partly aided by luck, so the next season naturally tends to be more ordinary. |
| Road segment with the most accidents | **Explained** | Singled out after an unusually bad year, so counts tend to drop closer to average even with no intervention. |
| Seeking treatment at peak headache pain | **Explained** | Treatment starts right at the extreme peak, so pain tends to ease on its own by the next day. |
| Random assignment to study groups | **Not explained** | Random assignment does not select extreme performers, so differences can be linked to study time. |
| A child growing taller | **Not explained** | Height rises through biological growth rather than extreme values returning toward average. |

> Regression to the mean requires selection based on an extreme noisy score, not natural physical growth or randomized group assignment.

# Answer

Regression to the mean explains **the top-scoring player**, **the highest-accident road segment**, and **treating a headache at its worst peak**.

Regression to the mean happens when something is **picked for an extreme value** on one measurement, and part of that extremity is **luck** that doesn't repeat. On the next measurement the value tends to drift towards the average without any other cause.

**The top scorer: explained**. They were picked for their most outstanding season, partly due to luck. The next season tends to be more ordinary.

**The road with the most accidents: explained**. It stood out because of a bad year. With no change at all, the number tends to fall towards the average.

**The worst headache: explained**. People see a doctor precisely when the pain is at its worst. The next day it tends to ease on its own.

**Random assignment to study groups: not explained**. Random assignment doesn't pick students for extreme scores, so the difference can be put down to study time.

**Children's height: not explained**. Height increases through growth, not because an extreme value returns to the average.

So regression to the mean can explain the top scorer, the road with the most accidents, and the people treated at the worst point of a headache.

# Given

In the debate over Rp<InlineMath math="100" /> trillion in subsidies across <InlineMath math="50" /> million households (<InlineMath math="10" /> million per quintile), the two extreme groups show:

- **Bottom quintile**: receives <InlineMath math="8\%" /> of total subsidies with average annual spending of Rp<InlineMath math="20" /> million.
- **Top quintile**: receives <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of total subsidies with average annual spending of Rp<InlineMath math="160" /> million.

# Asked

The most accurate assessment of the debate between subsidy supporters and critics along with the calculation supporting it.

# Solution

Test both sides of the debate using **two distinct metrics**: rupiah received per household and share of household spending.

## Rupiah per household

Compute the **per-household subsidy** for the bottom and top groups:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Bottom} = \frac{8\% \times 100\text{ trillion}}{10\text{ million}} = 0{,}8\text{ million}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{Top} = \frac{40\% \times 100\text{ trillion}}{10\text{ million}} = 4\text{ million}" />
</MathContainer>

## Share of annual spending

Compare each rupiah subsidy against annual spending:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Bottom} = \frac{0{,}8}{20} \times 100\% = 4\%" />
<BlockMath math="\text{Top} = \frac{4}{160} \times 100\% = 2{,}5\%" />
</MathContainer>

**Both sides use accurate figures while measuring different things**. Supporters are right that the subsidy matters more to poor household budgets (<InlineMath math="4\%" /> versus <InlineMath math="2{,}5\%" />), while critics are right that far more rupiah flow to the wealthiest households (Rp<InlineMath math="4" /> million versus Rp<InlineMath math="800" /> thousand).

Check why the other options fail:

| Distractor | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Rp<InlineMath math="800" /> thousand is the largest | That amount is actually the smallest among all groups. |
| Subsidy means nothing to the bottom group | <InlineMath math="4\%" /> of annual spending is substantial for low-income budgets. |
| Subsidy per household is uniform | It ranges widely from Rp<InlineMath math="800" /> thousand to Rp<InlineMath math="4" /> million. |
| Simplified table cannot assess the debate | The table clearly exposes the different yardsticks used by both sides. |

> Two opposing camps can both cite valid math when one measures household budget share and the other measures absolute rupiah received.

# Answer

Both sides rely on **accurate calculations while measuring different quantities**: **budget share (<InlineMath math="4\%" /> versus <InlineMath math="2{,}5\%" />)** versus **rupiah received per household (Rp<InlineMath math="800" /> thousand versus Rp<InlineMath math="4" /> million)**.

Paragraph one shows that the table on the back of the pack is **rarely read**, because shoppers choose in a few seconds. Paragraph two gives the example of Chile, where **front-of-pack warnings** were followed by lower purchases. The last paragraph ends with a **call to make such warnings mandatory**. All three paragraphs lead to one idea.

- The author does not ask for the table to be removed, only says it is rarely read.
- The passage says purchases **fell**, not that sugary drinks disappeared from the market.
- The author criticises the voluntary logo because high-sugar products still carry no mark.
- The author does not tell shoppers to learn arithmetic. The point is information they can grasp at a glance.

So the main idea is that simple front-of-pack warnings are more useful and should be made mandatory.

# Given

At the end of the story, Rani sits on the station bench holding the same small cake and murmurs, **"The four o'clock train is never late"**. The question asks what **repeating her father's line shows** and **how its meaning has changed** for her.

# Asked

What Rani's repetition of her father's sentence signifies and how its meaning has shifted for her.

# Solution

A complete answer must address both Rani's gesture and the deeper shift in meaning.

- **Meaning of the gesture**. Rani arrives with **"the same small cake"** and sits on the bench where she once waited. Repeating her father's words is **her way of remembering him**.
- **Shift in meaning**. Years earlier, the remark sounded like a crossing guard's quiet pride. Now Rani knows from the newspaper clipping that on her birthday afternoon a rider broke through the gate and a guard **"helped lift the victim off the tracks"**. **"It is people who are late"** now also encompasses her father being late because he stopped to help.

Check why the other options fail:

| Distractor | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Rani wants to become a crossing guard | The story mentions no such ambition, and the sentence's meaning clearly shifts. |
| Rani is mocking the young guard | She smiles and answers calmly with no trace of sarcasm. |
| Her father was negligent | The clipping points to her father helping a victim rather than neglecting his duty. |
| Rani was angry or often late | Nothing in the story suggests she resented her father or was habitually late. |

> The very same words take on deeper meaning once hidden facts reveal why a loved one arrived late.

# Answer

Rani repeats the line to **remember her father**, and the words now carry the realization that **his lateness came from helping someone in urgent need**.

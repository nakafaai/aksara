Some statements have to be inferred from scattered clues, not from one sentence.

**Rani's father was most likely the guard who helped: can be concluded**. The accident happened **"pada sore hari ulang tahunnya itu"** (on the afternoon of her birthday), the guard who helped came **"dari pos terdekat"** (from the nearest post) and worked **"di tengah hujan"** (in the rain), and Rani's father came home late **"dengan seragam basah"** (with his uniform wet). *Most likely* fits, because the guard isn't named.

**The way the barrier is operated has changed: can be concluded**. It used to be lowered by **"memutar engkol besi"** (turning an iron crank). Now **"engkolnya sudah diganti tombol"** (the crank has been replaced by a button) and the barrier lowers itself.

**Rani brings the cake to remember that day: can be concluded**. She brings **"kue kecil yang sama"** (the same small cake) and sits on the bench where she used to wait.

**The young guard knows Rani's father: can't be concluded**. The guard **"tidak mengenal nama ayahnya"** (doesn't know her father's name).

**Rani's father forgot her birthday: can't be concluded**. He had promised to come home early that day. He was late because of something else, not because he forgot.

So what can be concluded is that Rani's father was most likely the guard who helped the victim, that the way the barrier is operated has changed, and that Rani brings the cake to remember her birthday back then.

# Given

A local government may reclaim only one of three mudflats along a flyway segment:

- **Mudflat P**: <InlineMath math="900" /> hectares, the sole stopover within flight range from the north.
- **Mudflat Q**: <InlineMath math="400" /> hectares, with two replacement mudflats within flight range.
- **Mudflat R**: <InlineMath math="600" /> hectares, with one replacement, but reclaiming it also removes the only roost near that replacement.

# Asked

Which mudflat causes the smallest harm if reclaimed according to the author's criteria on substitutes and safe roosts.

# Solution

The author sets **two core criteria** to judge reclamation damage: position in the stopover chain and nearby roost safety.

- **Stopover-chain position**. A site that **"has many substitutes within flight range may be lost with small damage"**, whereas losing **"the only stopover in a segment can sever the journey of thousands of birds"**. Losing P causes the greatest harm.
- **Nearby roost availability**. The author warns that **"a wide mudflat is not necessarily useful without a safe roost nearby"**. Reclaiming R also cripples its replacement site.

Thus **Mudflat Q** carries the smallest loss because two replacements remain within flight range, provided safe roosts near those replacements stay intact.

Check why the other options fail:

| Distractor | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Picking P or judging by area alone | Area is not decisive because removing P breaks the stopover chain. |
| Picking R | R's replacement loses its only roost, making that mudflat far less useful. |
| Treating distance as irrelevant | Birds can only use stopovers reachable with their remaining fuel. |
| Picking Q due to zero loss or smallest area | The author calls the loss **"small"**, not zero, and replacements rather than size drive the judgment. |

> A mudflat's ecological importance depends on reachable substitutes and safe nearby roosts rather than sheer hectare size.

# Answer

Reclaiming **Mudflat Q** causes the smallest harm because two substitutes remain within flight range, as long as roosts near those substitutes stay safe.

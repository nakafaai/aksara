The right design has to meet **two conditions** from the teacher.

- **A same-time comparison group**. What can be credited to the classes is the **"selisihnya dengan kelompok pembanding"** (gap with the comparison group).
- **Random selection**. The teacher warns this year's comparison group was **"tidak dipilih secara acak"** (not chosen at random). Random splitting means both groups regress to the mean alike and don't differ in other ways.

Each other option fails one condition.

- Top scorers tend to fall through regression, so they're no fair comparison for low scorers.
- Counting only students who attend diligently spoils the random split, since diligent students may differ in other ways.
- Students whose schedules clashed aren't chosen at random, however many there are.
- This year's <InlineMath math="10" /> points isn't a same-time comparison and is still mixed with regression.

So the best design splits low scorers at random into attending and non-attending groups, then compares the two groups' rises.

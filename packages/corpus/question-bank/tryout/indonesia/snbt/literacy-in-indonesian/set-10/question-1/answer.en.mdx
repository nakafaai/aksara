# Asked

The role of citing the bar-tailed godwit flight record in the author's reasoning and the statement that directly supports it.

# Solution

A correct answer must match both parts: what the extreme record does in the argument and which statement immediately reinforces it.

- **Function of the record**. The author stresses that the figure **"shows the upper limit of one species, not the habit of most shorebirds"**. In the fourth paragraph, the author rejects the idea that birds can simply move to another mudflat, because they can only use stopovers within reach.
- **Direct support**. The next sentence adds that **"most species, including those reaching Indonesian coasts, travel the flyway as a chain of hops with several stopovers"**.

| Distractor | Why it fails |

| Proving birds can skip lost stopovers | The record is cited to argue against assuming most birds can skip stopovers, and country counts do not address this. |

> One extreme record marks the upper limit of a single species rather than the typical migration habit of most shorebirds.

# Answer

The record establishes that **one species' extreme limit cannot be generalized to other shorebirds**, and the detail about **a chain of hops with several stopovers** directly reinforces that point.

# Given

After the embankment road opened to motorcycles, two average counts changed:

- **At high tide on pond embankments**: from <InlineMath math="1{.}200" /> birds down to <InlineMath math="450" /> birds.
- **At low tide on the mudflat**: from <InlineMath math="1{.}100" /> birds down to <InlineMath math="1{.}050" /> birds.
- The sandbar across the estuary was not surveyed by the observers.

# Asked

The percentage drops at high tide and low tide along with the most accurate interpretation of their comparison.

# Solution

Compute each percentage drop using the **initial count** as the denominator, then check what the comparison justifies.

## High tide and low tide percentage drops

The **count drop and percentage drop** for each tide are:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="1{.}200 - 450 = 750 \implies \frac{750}{1{.}200} \times 100\% = 62{,}5\%" />
<BlockMath math="1{.}100 - 1{.}050 = 50 \implies \frac{50}{1{.}100} \times 100\% \approx 4{,}5\%" />
</MathContainer>

## Interpreting the two percentages

Birds kept feeding on the mudflat, while high-tide embankment counts fell steeply. Even so, the observers **"were not yet willing to draw conclusions about population"** because the sandbar across the estuary was never counted.

Check why the other options fail:

| Distractor | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Right percentages with a population drop claim | The observation group explicitly refused to draw a population conclusion. |
| <InlineMath math="37{,}5\%" /> and <InlineMath math="95{,}5\%" /> | Those are the remaining proportions, not the percentage decreases. |
| <InlineMath math="167\%" /> and <InlineMath math="4{,}8\%" /> | Both mistakenly divide by the final count instead of the initial count. |
| Using <InlineMath math="50" /> as a percentage | <InlineMath math="50" /> is the raw drop in bird count at low tide, not a percentage. |

> Always divide the drop by the initial count rather than the final count, and avoid population claims when alternative roosts were never surveyed.

# Answer

The drops are about **<InlineMath math="62{,}5\%" /> at high tide** and **<InlineMath math="4{,}5\%" /> at low tide**, showing disturbance mainly affected the roost while leaving the new roosting site unknown.

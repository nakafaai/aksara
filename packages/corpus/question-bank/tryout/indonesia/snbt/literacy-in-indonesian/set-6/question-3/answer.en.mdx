# Given

In **August** (dry season, when El Nino's impact is strongest), **El Nino** coincides with warmer waters off Sumatra and Java and cooler waters in the western Indian Ocean. Paragraph five defines this reversal of positive IOD as **negative IOD**, which "can offset part of the rainfall deficit."

# Asked

The most defensible forecast for August rainfall in western Indonesia.

# Solution

Identify the phase first (**negative IOD**), then honor the text's measured wording ("part of the deficit," neither guaranteeing normal rain nor reversing drought into heavy rain) during dry-season El Nino.

> Negative IOD can soften the rainfall shortage compared with El Nino alone, yet because it offsets only part of the deficit, a drier dry season remains possible.

## Checking the five forecasts against the passage

Testing **IOD sign and the qualifier "part"** filters out all four exaggerated or inverted options.

| Forecast | Alignment with passage | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rainfall deficit may be milder than with El Nino alone, yet drier conditions remain possible | Faithful to "can offset part" during dry season | **Correct** |
| Drought becomes even harsher than El Nino alone | False, that describes positive IOD, not negative IOD | **Incorrect** |
| Rainfall will stay normal | Overstates "part" as a complete offset | **Incorrect** |
| El Nino has little effect because August is peak rainy season | Reverses seasons, August is dry season | **Incorrect** |
| Rain jumps far above normal because IOD overrides El Nino | Goes beyond text, which never claims IOD dominates completely | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The most defensible forecast is that **the rainfall deficit may be milder than when El Nino acts alone, yet a drier dry season still remains possible**.

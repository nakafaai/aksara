# Given

In the kite story, the stiff **fiberglass dragon kite** "only knows how to stand straight or fall" when the wind reverses, whereas **Grandfather's bamboo kite** "tilts first" and finds new balance. The narrator mirrors that shift, moving from rigid yanking to flexibility as he offers to hold the bamboo.

# Asked

The best reading of the contrast between the two kites in the story.

# Solution

Focus on the **single axis of contrast** - how each kite responds to shifting wind - and link it to the narrator's own shift in attitude.

> The two kites embody two ways of meeting change: holding rigid until breaking, or flexing first to regain balance.

## Checking the five symbolic readings

Evaluating **whether each reading centers on responding to change** isolates the exact theme.

| Symbolic reading | Fit with the story | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two ways of meeting change: staying rigid until falling or flexing to adapt | Matches both kites and the narrator's growth | **Correct** |
| Superiority of expensive goods over simple ones | False, the expensive dragon is the one that crashes | **Incorrect** |
| Victory of modern technology over tradition | False, fiberglass fails when the wind turns | **Incorrect** |
| Contrast between two kinds of wind | False, the wind tests both kites equally | **Incorrect** |
| Grandfather's pride in his own craft | False, Grandfather praised the dragon and never mocked it | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The contrast between the two kites best represents **two ways of facing change: holding rigid until you fall, or yielding and adapting**.

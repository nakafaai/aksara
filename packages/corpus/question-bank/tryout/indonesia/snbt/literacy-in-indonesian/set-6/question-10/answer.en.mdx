# Given

**At the** story's end, the narrator and Kakek communicate through tying a slow knot and brief lines that imply more than they literally say.

# Asked

Which statements are genuinely supported by the characters' words and actions at the ending.

# Solution

> Keep what a character literally speaks separate from the quiet meaning carried by their tone and actions.

At the end of the story, both characters speak through actions and through sentences that seem to be about something else. For each statement, separate **what's actually said** from **what's meant**.

The statement about an open confession isn't supported. The narrator thinks: "Aku ingin bilang bahwa layangan naga itu sebenarnya tidak terlalu bagus, tetapi yang keluar dari mulutku lain." (I wanted to say the dragon kite wasn't really that good, but what came out of my mouth was something else.) The confession is never spoken.

The statement about an indirect apology is supported. What does come out is "Besok aku yang pegang bambunya, Kek. Kakek yang bilang kapan berhenti meraut." (Tomorrow I'll hold the bamboo, Kek. You tell me when to stop shaving.) At the start the narrator said the kite "tidak perlu ditimbang" (doesn't need balancing). Now the narrator offers to join in the balancing and hands the decision to Kakek. The earlier attitude is taken back **without the word sorry**.

The statement about the knot is supported. The story says it outright: "Simpulnya ia buat pelan-pelan, supaya aku bisa melihat." (He tied the knot slowly, so I could see.)

The statement about the face isn't supported. The story says the opposite: "Ia melihat tanganku, tidak melihat wajahku." (He looked at my hands, not at my face.) Kakek deliberately doesn't look at the grandchild's face, sparing the grandchild the shame.

The statement about the prettier kite isn't supported. The white kite feels "ringan dan agak jelek" (light and rather ugly), and there's a gluey fingerprint in its corner. It isn't accepted for its looks.

# Answer

**The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The narrator openly admits regret | Tidak |
| The last line is an indirect apology | Ya |
| Kakek ties the knot slowly so the grandchild can learn | Ya |
| Kakek studies the grandchild's face | Tidak |
| The white kite is prettier in the narrator's eyes | Tidak |

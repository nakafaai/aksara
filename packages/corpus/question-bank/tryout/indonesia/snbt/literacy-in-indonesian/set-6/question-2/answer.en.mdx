This question asks for the chance of a severe dry season, not the chance of El Nino. The fourth paragraph gives two hints: El Nino "tidak selalu mengeringkan setiap kabupaten" (doesn't always dry out every regency), and there are severe dry seasons "yang terjadi tanpa El Nino" (that happen without El Nino). So next year has **two roads to a severe dry season**, and both have to be counted.

First turn the regency's record into chances. In an El Nino year, a severe dry season happens with a chance of <InlineMath math="\tfrac{6}{10}=0.6" />. In a year without El Nino, the chance is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2}{20}=0.1" />. The chance of El Nino is <InlineMath math="0.7" />, so the chance of no El Nino is <InlineMath math="1-0.7=0.3" />.

Multiply the chance of each road by the chance of a severe dry season on that road, then add them up:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{severe})&=0.7\times0.6+0.3\times0.1\\
&=0.42+0.03\\
&=0.45
\end{aligned}" />

That's <InlineMath math="45\%" />. The figure makes sense: it's below <InlineMath math="60\%" /> because El Nino isn't certain, but above <InlineMath math="42\%" /> because a year without El Nino can be dry too.

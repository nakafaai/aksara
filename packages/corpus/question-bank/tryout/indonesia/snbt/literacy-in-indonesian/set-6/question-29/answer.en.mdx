# Given

Paragraph four explains that for a child "living in a home where food can run out if not eaten promptly," eating the treat immediately **"is a reasonable choice,"** because wait time reflects a child's assessment of how dependable their environment is.

# Asked

The best way to understand a child from a household that often runs short of food at month's end eating the treat immediately.

# Solution

Apply **paragraph four's framework** directly to the child's background: experience has taught them that waiting can mean ending up with nothing.

> Eating the treat immediately is a rational choice grounded in the child's lived experience that something postponed might never arrive.

## Checking the five interpretations against paragraph four

Testing **each view against paragraph four's rationale** eliminates every contrary framing.

| Interpretation | Fit with paragraph four | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sensible choice based on lived experience that what is postponed might never be received | Matches paragraph four's exact household example | **Correct** |
| Predicts the child will fail in school later | Repeats the simplistic message the passage critiques | **Incorrect** |
| Proves the child lacks innate self-control | Ignores how environment shapes the decision | **Incorrect** |
| Shows the child dislikes the researcher | Never mentioned in the passage | **Incorrect** |
| Shows the child misunderstood the offer | False, paragraph four frames it as a deliberate calculation | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The child's action is best understood as **a reasonable choice based on their experience that something postponed might never be obtained**.

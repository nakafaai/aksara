The last paragraph says liquefaction cannot be prevented by stopping earthquakes, but **its damage can be reduced**. The ways are compacting or draining the soil, setting foundations down to hard layers, and, cheapest of all, **not building on the most vulnerable land**. Palu's no-living zones are an example of the last.

- Earthquakes cannot be stopped, and vibration dampers are not mentioned.
- The passage calls avoiding vulnerable land the cheapest option.
- The government set zones where people may no longer live, so not every site can be rebuilt.
- Draining soil is named as a way to reduce damage, not to worsen it.

So the conclusion that fits best is that damage from liquefaction can be reduced, above all by choosing the right land.

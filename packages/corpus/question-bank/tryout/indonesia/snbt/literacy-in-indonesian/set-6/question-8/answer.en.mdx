# Given

**The story follows** a kite-making grandfather, a white kite already at home, slowing orders, and the narrator's shifting view of Kakek.

# Asked

Whether each statement is true or false based on explicit and quiet clues in the story.

# Solution

> Pay close attention to small details tucked inside sentences because stories often reveal key truths quietly rather than announcing them.

This story rarely says things outright. You have to infer a lot from **small details slipped in** mid-sentence. Look for that detail for each statement.

The statement about Kakek holding back is true. The narrator waits for Kakek to "berkata tentang serat kaca, tentang pabrik, tentang anak yang tidak mau mendengar" (talk about fibreglass, about the factory, about a child who won't listen). All three could be used for a scolding. Then comes a short sentence: "Ia tidak mengatakannya." (He didn't say it.) Kakek has the material but chooses silence.

The statement about the white kite is false. It's the kite "yang semalam ia selesaikan dan belum dipesan siapa pun" (that he finished last night and nobody has ordered yet). The word _semalam_ ("last night") shows the kite was done before the dragon was lost that afternoon. Kakek gives it away, but didn't make it for that purpose.

The statement about orders falling off is true. Three clues point the same way. People used to "antre di teras kami" (queue on our porch), while this year "teras itu sepi" (the porch is quiet). The pile of wax paper "tidak berkurang" (isn't going down). The white kite "belum dipesan siapa pun" (hasn't been ordered by anyone).

The statement about Kakek's way being too slow is false. In the middle of the story the narrator does say "Sekarang aku menganggapnya lambat" (now I think of it as slow). But the last line is "Besok aku yang pegang bambunya, Kek. Kakek yang bilang kapan berhenti meraut." (Tomorrow I'll hold the bamboo, Kek. You tell me when to stop shaving.) The narrator offers to join in balancing the bamboo, the very work called slow earlier, and leaves the call to Kakek. The judgement **has changed**.

# Answer

**The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Kakek holds back from lecturing | Benar |
| The white kite was made specially as a replacement | Salah |
| Orders for Kakek's kites are falling off | Benar |
| The narrator still finds Kakek too slow | Salah |

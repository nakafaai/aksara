# Given

Table 1 **lists available rooms** and peak and low occupancy percentages across three cities of very different sizes.

# Asked

Whether each statement about combined occupancy and rooms filled across the three cities is true or false.

# Solution

> To find combined occupancy across cities of different sizes, add all occupied rooms and divide by all available rooms instead of averaging percentages.

The combined occupancy rate **isn't a plain average** of the three percentages. First add up the occupied rooms and the available rooms across the three cities, then divide. The city with many rooms has to carry more weight.

The first statement is true.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1{,}600+900+200}{4{,}000+1{,}500+500}=\frac{2{,}700}{6{,}000}=45\%" />

The plain average of <InlineMath math="46\tfrac{2}{3}\%" /> gives Lembahdaun the same weight as Tirtasari, though it has far fewer rooms.

The second statement is true. Lembahdaun fills <InlineMath math="500\cdot40\%=200" /> rooms in low season. With <InlineMath math="600" /> rooms and <InlineMath math="200" /> occupied, its occupancy becomes:

<BlockMath math="\frac{200}{600}=33\tfrac{1}{3}\%<35\%" />

The third statement is false. Karangwangi's occupancy rate is higher, but its occupied rooms are <InlineMath math="1{,}500\cdot60\%=900" />, while Tirtasari's are <InlineMath math="4{,}000\cdot40\%=1{,}600" />.

The fourth statement is false, though it's close.

<BlockMath math="\frac{3{,}200+1{,}200+450}{6{,}000}=\frac{4{,}850}{6{,}000}\approx80.8\%" />

That's below <InlineMath math="81\%" />. The plain average <InlineMath math="(80+80+90):3\approx83.3\%" /> would make the statement look true.

# Answer

The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement.

| Statement | Answer |

| Lembahdaun with <InlineMath math="100" /> more rooms falls below <InlineMath math="35\%" /> | Benar |
| Karangwangi fills more rooms than Tirtasari | Salah |
| Combined peak-season occupancy is above <InlineMath math="81\%" /> | Salah |

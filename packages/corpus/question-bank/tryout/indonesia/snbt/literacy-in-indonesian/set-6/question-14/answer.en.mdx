# Given

**Text A insists** companies carry employer duties because they set fares and rate drivers, while Text B closes with a middle-ground proposal keeping partner status.

# Asked

How the writer of Text A would most likely respond to the proposal at the end of Text B.

# Solution

> Measure the rival proposal against Text A's core test, as long as the company sets fares and ratings without employer duties, it falls short.

To predict a writer's response, compare the other side's proposal with **all of that writer's demands and reasons**, then see which parts are met and which aren't.

Text B's proposal holds three things: advance notice of commission changes, an appeal route before an account is cut off, and accident cover while carrying a passenger.

Now compare it with Text A.

| What Text A objects to or demands | Met by Text B's proposal? |
| --- | --- |
| Clear rules before an account is cut off | Yes, through the appeal route |
| Work accident cover | Partly, only while carrying a passenger |
| Commission that can rise at any time | Partly, only announced earlier |
| Fares set by the app with no bargaining | No |
| Bad ratings for refusing orders | No |
| Minimum wage for hours the app is on | No |

The unmet parts are the heart of Text A's reasoning. Its closing sentence says a company that **controls how people work** must carry an employer's obligations. Text B's proposal leaves that control, fares and ratings, in the company's hands. So Text A's writer would most likely find the proposal not enough, while not rejecting what it contains.

The other responses don't fit.

- The proposal has no minimum wage and doesn't touch fares, so it doesn't contain all of Text A's demands.
- Text A actually demands work accident cover.
- Text A never discusses part-time drivers, let alone wants them banned.
- Text A doesn't use the company's losses as a yardstick. It asks the company to carry more obligations.

# Answer

So Text A's writer would most likely find the proposal not enough, because it leaves the company setting fares and rating drivers without carrying an employer's obligations.

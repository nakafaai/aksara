Paragraph two sets out the sequence. Normally the sand grains **rest on one another**, so the ground is strong. During an earthquake the grains are pushed together, but **the water in the gaps cannot escape in time**. Its pressure rises and pushes the grains apart. Once the grains **no longer rest on one another**, the ground loses its strength and flows like a liquid.

- Rain is not mentioned. The water involved is the water already in the gaps.
- The grains do not break. They are only pushed apart.
- Clay is described as more resistant, and the passage does not say it coats the sand.
- The weight of buildings is not given as the cause.

So the ground loses its strength because water pressure in the gaps rises until the sand grains no longer rest on one another.

# Given

A primary school plans daily treat-waiting drills to raise future test scores. Yet paragraph three reports the correlation shrank to **"nearly negligible"** once family background and early ability were controlled, and paragraph five states the link **"runs through family circumstances"** that waiting drills do not change.

# Asked

The primary flaw in the school's waiting-drill plan based on the passage.

# Solution

Pinpoint the **central causal fallacy**: treating an early correlation as direct causation when the underlying driver (family circumstances) remains untouched by practicing waiting.

> The plan mistakes correlation for causation, ignoring that the apparent link largely runs through family circumstances that waiting drills cannot alter.

## Evaluating the five proposed flaws

Checking **which critique comes from paragraphs three and five** rules out every off-text distraction.

| Proposed flaw | Alignment with the passage | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treats early correlation as causation even though the link mostly runs through unchanged family circumstances | Directly captures paragraphs three and five | **Correct** |
| Drills are too short to form a habit | Not discussed in the passage | **Incorrect** |
| Treats are unhealthy for children | Not evaluated in the passage | **Incorrect** |
| Drills only work for Stanford children | Misreads the Stanford sample critique | **Incorrect** |
| Wait duration in the drill is too brief | Longer drills still leave family circumstances unchanged | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The plan's main flaw is that **it treats the correlation in the initial study as cause and effect, even though the relationship largely runs through family circumstances that waiting drills do not change**.

The school's plan rests on one step: if children who wait score higher on average, then **making children wait** will raise scores. That step treats waiting as the cause of high scores. The passage gives two reasons to doubt it.

First, the third paragraph shows the link shrinking until it "hampir tidak berarti" (hardly mattered) once family background and early thinking ability were taken into account. So most of the visible link doesn't come from waiting itself.

Second, the fifth paragraph says the link "berjalan lewat keadaan keluarga yang juga membentuk prestasi anak" (runs through family circumstances that also shape a child's achievement), then closes: "Melatih anak menunggu kudapan tidak dengan sendirinya mengubah keadaan itu." (Training children to wait for a treat doesn't by itself change those circumstances.) Practice at school doesn't change whether a child's family often runs out of food, or whether the adults around the child can be trusted to keep promises. So the practice **touches the symptom, not the cause**.

The other weaknesses don't come from the passage.

- The passage doesn't discuss how long practice takes to form a habit.
- The passage doesn't judge how healthy the treat is.
- The Stanford sample is a problem for the original finding, not a reason the practice only suits Stanford children.
- The length of the wait in practice doesn't change the basic weakness. However many minutes children practise, their family circumstances stay the same.

So the plan's main weakness is that it treats the link in the original finding as cause and effect, when that link largely runs through family circumstances that the practice doesn't change.

# Given

Table 1 **gives available hotel** rooms and peak-versus-low occupancy rates for Tirtasari, Karangwangi, and Lembahdaun.

# Asked

Which conclusion Table 1 best supports regarding occupied rooms and occupancy rate changes between seasons.

# Solution

> A steep percentage drop in a tiny town can mean few rooms, while a smaller rate drop in a huge city can leave far more rooms empty.

The passage separates two measures: **occupancy rate** in percent and **rooms occupied** in rooms. A correct conclusion has to use the right one. Work out both for the change from peak season to low season.

Rooms occupied per night is the number of rooms times the occupancy rate.

| City | Peak | Low | Rooms lost | Drop in occupancy |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Tirtasari | <InlineMath math="3{,}200" /> | <InlineMath math="1{,}600" /> | <InlineMath math="1{,}600" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> points |
| Karangwangi | <InlineMath math="1{,}200" /> | <InlineMath math="900" /> | <InlineMath math="300" /> | <InlineMath math="20" /> points |
| Lembahdaun | <InlineMath math="450" /> | <InlineMath math="200" /> | <InlineMath math="250" /> | <InlineMath math="50" /> points |

Tirtasari loses <InlineMath math="1{,}600" /> occupied rooms a night, far more than the other cities. But its occupancy drops only <InlineMath math="40" /> points, while Lembahdaun drops <InlineMath math="50" /> points. The biggest percentage drop and the biggest loss of rooms **happen in different cities**, because Tirtasari has <InlineMath math="8" /> times as many rooms as Lembahdaun.

Each of the other conclusions falls to one number.

- Lembahdaun does drop most sharply in percent, but it loses only <InlineMath math="250" /> occupied rooms.
- The table has no room rates or costs, so profit can't be judged.
- Tirtasari does fall from <InlineMath math="80\%" /> to <InlineMath math="40\%" />, exactly half. Karangwangi falls from <InlineMath math="80\%" /> to <InlineMath math="60\%" />, not half.
- In Karangwangi, low-season occupancy is <InlineMath math="60\%" />, so only <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of rooms are empty, less than half.

# Answer

So the best-supported conclusion is that **Tirtasari loses the most occupied rooms per night, even though its drop in occupancy rate isn't the largest**.

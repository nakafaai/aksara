# Given

**Text A** argues on behalf of **full-time drivers** spending <InlineMath math="10" /> to <InlineMath math="12" /> hours a day on the road who rely wholly on the app. **Text B** argues from the author's own experience as a **part-time driver** after teaching, rejecting employee status to protect part-time flexibility while merely conceding full-time drivers "might benefit."

# Asked

Text B's main flaw as a reply to Text A's demand for employee status.

# Solution

Compare **who Text A is defending** against **who Text B uses as its benchmark** to spot the mismatch in target groups.

> Text B's main weakness is relying on its author's part-time driving experience to oppose protections sought for full-time drivers who depend entirely on the app.

## Evaluating the five critiques of Text B

Checking **each critique against what both texts actually state** eliminates all four inaccurate charges.

| Proposed flaw | Fit with Text A and Text B | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grounds its reply in part-time experience while Text A addresses full-time drivers wholly dependent on the app | Pinpoints the exact mismatch of target groups | **Correct** |
| Denies the problems cited by Text A | False, Text B says those problems are "indeed real" | **Incorrect** |
| Praises foreign policies | False, Text B cites them as a warning of part-timers losing access | **Incorrect** |
| Fails to compare policy costs | Neither text provides cost data to compare | **Incorrect** |
| Author drives only on weekends | False, the author drives "after finishing teaching" on weekdays too | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

Text B's main flaw is that **its author argues from personal experience as a part-time driver, whereas Text A focuses on drivers who depend entirely on the app for their livelihood**.

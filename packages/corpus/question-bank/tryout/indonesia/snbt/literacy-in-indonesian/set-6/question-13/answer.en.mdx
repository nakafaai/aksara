# Given

**Text B's** fear follows one causal chain: if minimum wages must be paid for **every hour the app is open**, platforms will restrict schedules and caps, pushing **part-time drivers out**.

# Asked

The finding that, if true, most weakens Text B's concern.

# Solution

Select the evidence that **breaks Text B's causal link** while showing that the feared outcome (losing part-time drivers) did not happen.

> A city paying minimum wages only for active-order hours while keeping its part-time driver count unchanged breaks both the company's motive to restrict shifts and the feared exclusion.

## Evaluating the five findings against the chain of Text B

Checking **whether each finding breaks or reinforces the chain** isolates the sole direct counterexample.

| Finding | Effect on Text B's fear | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| City paying minimum wage only during active-order hours saw no drop in part-time drivers | Breaks company motive to cap idle hours and disproves exclusion | **Weakens most** |
| Drivers working over <InlineMath math="10" /> hours daily | Supports Text A without addressing Text B's chain | **Incorrect** |
| Many drivers also work as teachers or staff | Mirrors Text B's own profile, showing more people at risk | **Incorrect** |
| Fees raised without warning | Shared grievance acknowledged by both texts | **Incorrect** |
| Part-time drivers complete fewer orders | Reinforces Text B's fear that companies would cut them | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

Text B's concern is most weakened by **the finding of a city where minimum wages apply only to hours spent fulfilling orders and the number of part-time drivers stayed the same**.

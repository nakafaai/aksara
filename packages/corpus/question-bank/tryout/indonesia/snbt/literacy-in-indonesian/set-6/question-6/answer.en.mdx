# Given

Just as his factory-made dragon kite climbs highest and "other children look up," the narrator **"glances back toward the house"** and notes "from the field our porch was out of sight." Earlier at home he had challenged Grandfather's craft by declaring the factory kite "needs no balancing" and is "already balanced from the factory," while Grandfather sat silently on the porch.

# Asked

What most likely prompted the narrator to glance toward the house at that moment.

# Solution

Connect **the moment of triumph on the field** with **his unanswered boast to Grandfather on the porch** to identify the one audience he was hoping to find.

> The narrator glances toward the porch because he wants Grandfather to witness that the factory-made kite succeeded, even without saying so aloud.

## Checking the five proposed motives

Testing **every motive against adjacent details in the story** rules out all four distractors.

| Proposed motive | Evidence in the story | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wants Grandfather to see the factory kite succeed, though left unspoken | Links his factory-balance boast directly to searching for the porch | **Correct** |
| Fears scolding for leaving without permission | False, Grandfather never shows anger and simply says "Good" | **Incorrect** |
| Needs Grandfather's help with a straining line | False, the kite is still "steady and calm" | **Incorrect** |
| Regrets spending his allowance | False, no guilt appears at the height of winning | **Incorrect** |
| Checking for a wind shift | False, the wind reverses only in the next paragraph unexpectedly | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

He glanced toward the house because **he wanted Grandfather to see that the factory-made kite had succeeded, even though he never said so aloud**.

The narrator doesn't explain the glance. You have to build the reason from two things: **what happens just before**, and **what the narrator is looking for at home**.

Just before, the kite is flying "lebih tinggi daripada layangan siapa pun" (higher than anyone's kite), and "Anak-anak lain menoleh" (the other children turn to look). The narrator is winning. That's the moment of the glance towards the house, followed by: "dari lapangan teras kami tidak kelihatan" (from the field our porch can't be seen). The one on the porch is Kakek (Grandpa) on the bamboo bench. So the narrator is looking for Kakek.

Why Kakek? At home the narrator said the kite "tidak perlu ditimbang" (doesn't need balancing) and is "Sudah seimbang dari pabriknya" (already balanced at the factory). That remark challenged Kakek's way of working, and Kakek didn't answer. Now the kite is proving the remark right. The narrator wants Kakek to **witness that proof**. The words "tidak kelihatan" sound like disappointment that the most important spectator isn't there.

The other motives have nothing to hold on to in the story.

- The story doesn't say the narrator left without a word, and Kakek never seems angry. Kakek only said "Bagus" (nice).
- The string isn't tight yet. The kite is still "tegak dan tenang" (upright and calm), so no help is needed.
- The pocket money appears in the first paragraph as a price, with no sign of regret. Guilt doesn't fit a moment of winning.
- The narrator isn't reading the wind. The wind only turns in the next paragraph, and it comes as a surprise.

So the narrator glances home wanting Kakek to see that the factory kite works, without ever saying so.

# Given

In the **marshmallow-test passage**, paragraph three notes the link shrank by half in a broader sample and became "nearly negligible" after accounting for family background and early cognitive skill. Paragraph four ties wait time to perceived reliability of the child's world, and paragraph five concludes the test **"measures more than self-control"** and its link largely **"runs through family circumstances."**

# Asked

The statement that best summarizes the author's stance on the message that early self-control predicts later success.

# Solution

Choose the summary that **includes both pillars of paragraph five** without exaggerating them into "zero link" or "self-control does not matter."

> The marshmallow test captures more than self-control, and its correlation with later achievement is mostly explained by family background and early cognitive ability.

## Checking the five stance summaries

Matching **each option's strength against the author's measured phrasing** removes all four exaggerations.

| Summary | Fit with paragraphs three through five | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The test measures more than self-control, and its link with achievement is largely explained by family background and early ability | Accurately unites both closing takeaways | **Correct** |
| The link vanished completely to zero | Overstates "nearly negligible" | **Incorrect** |
| Self-control is unimportant | Contradicted by "does not mean self-control is unimportant" | **Incorrect** |
| Original researchers misrecorded data | False, the issue was sample diversity, not recording errors | **Incorrect** |
| Family background always decides every child's outcome | Too absolute, the text discusses averages and tendencies | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The author's stance is best summarized as: **the treat test measures more than self-control, and its link with later achievement is largely explained by family background and early cognitive ability**.

To find what the second mention does, first compare **Lintang Waluku's role in the two paragraphs**. Something mentioned again is usually there to show a change.

In the second paragraph, Lintang Waluku appears as an example of a traditional sign: its position before dawn "dipakai sebagai salah satu tanda saat menyiapkan sawah" (is used as one sign of when to prepare the rice fields). There the stars are a useful sign.

In the fourth paragraph, the same stars appear again, but in a new situation. Lintang Waluku "tetap muncul di tempat yang sama pada waktu yang sama" (still appears in the same place at the same time), while "hujan tidak lagi selalu datang sesudahnya" (rain no longer always follows). As a result, a farmer who sows by the stars may find the seed drying out. A sign that used to be right **can now mislead**. The second mention turns the same example into evidence for the fourth paragraph's main idea: signs tied to the stars don't shift along with the rain.

The other functions don't fit.

- The passage doesn't say farmers know less about the stars. The stars are still recognised, only the rain has shifted.
- The passage says the opposite: the stars stay "tetap" (fixed) in place. It's the rain that changes.
- The passage gives no directions for finding the stars.
- Lintang Waluku was already introduced in the second paragraph, including the note that it belongs to the constellation Orion.

So the mention in the fourth paragraph mainly serves to show that a sign that used to be right can now mislead, because the stars keep to their schedule while the rain shifts.

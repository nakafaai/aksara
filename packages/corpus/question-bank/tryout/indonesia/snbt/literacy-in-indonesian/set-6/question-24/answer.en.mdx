# Given

**Lintang Waluku** appears twice: in paragraph two as a traditional cue for preparing rice fields, and again in paragraph four where it "still appears in the same place at the same time" while rain no longer reliably follows, risking dried-out seedlings.

# Asked

The main function of mentioning Lintang Waluku a second time in paragraph four.

# Solution

Contrast **paragraph two (a useful traditional cue)** with **paragraph four (a fixed cue that can now mislead)** to see why the author brings the same constellation back.

> Revisiting Lintang Waluku in paragraph four shows that a once-reliable sign can now mislead because stars stay on schedule while rainfall shifts.

## Checking the five candidate functions

Testing **how each option explains the contrast between paragraphs two and four** singles out the true role.

| Candidate function | Fit with paragraphs two and four | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shows that a once-accurate sign can now mislead because stars keep their schedule while rain shifts | Explains the deliberate callback accurately | **Correct** |
| Shows farmers forgot how to recognize stars | False, farmers still recognize them, only rain shifted | **Incorrect** |
| Shows stars changed position due to climate change | False, stars "stay in the same place" | **Incorrect** |
| Teaches readers how to locate the constellation | False, no stargazing guide is given | **Incorrect** |
| First introduces Lintang Waluku as part of Orion | False, that introduction already occurred in paragraph two | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The second mention in paragraph four mainly **shows that a sign that used to be accurate can now mislead, because the stars remain on schedule while rainfall has shifted**.

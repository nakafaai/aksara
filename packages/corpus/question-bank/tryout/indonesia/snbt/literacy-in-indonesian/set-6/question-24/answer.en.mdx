Paragraph four says the inscriptions are written in **Old Malay** and are among **the oldest written evidence of Malay**, **the ancestor of Indonesian**. That is why they matter for the history of the Indonesian language.

- The inscriptions are not said to contain a word list.
- I-Tsing was a traveller who wrote records, not the author of the inscriptions.
- Pallava script came from southern India, not Sumatra.
- The language is Old Malay, an ancestor of Indonesian, not standard Indonesian.

So the inscriptions matter because they are among the oldest written evidence of Malay, the ancestor of Indonesian.

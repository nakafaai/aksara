# Given

In paragraph four, the author argues that cues from **living organisms** tend to shift with rainfall because living things **"respond to the weather actually occurring,"** unlike fixed solar and stellar cycles.

# Asked

The finding that, if true, most weakens the claim that living-nature cues tend to shift along with rainfall.

# Solution

Target **the author's biological premise**: if key indicator plants actually respond to **day length (a fixed solar cycle)** instead of rain, they stay just as rigid as constellations.

> If some indicator plants sprout according to unchanging day length rather than rainfall, they do not shift with delayed rain and the author's divide does not hold for all living cues.

## Evaluating the five candidate findings

Testing **whether each finding directly undercuts "responding to actual rainfall"** identifies the sole refutation.

| Finding | Undercuts "living cues track real rain" | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Some indicator plants sprout by unchanging annual day length rather than rainfall | Yes, makes those plant cues as fixed as star positions | **Weakens most** |
| Young farmers no longer know mangsa names | Concerns cultural transmission, not biological response | **Incorrect** |
| Official forecasts occasionally miss | Supports cross-checking rather than weakening living cues | **Incorrect** |
| Bird cues also exist | Adds an example without testing whether it tracks rain | **Incorrect** |
| Farmer groups record nature signs | Matches paragraph five | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The author's claim is most weakened by the finding that **some plants used as signs actually sprout according to day length, which does not vary from year to year, rather than according to rainfall**.

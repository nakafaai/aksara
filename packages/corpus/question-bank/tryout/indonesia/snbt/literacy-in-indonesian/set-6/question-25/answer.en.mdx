The three inscriptions say very different things. Kedukan Bukit **tells of a journey** and the founding of a settlement, Talang Tuo **records the building of a park** for the good of all beings, and Kota Kapur **curses anyone who is disloyal**. That variety shows that kings used inscriptions for many purposes.

- Talang Tuo is about a park, not warfare.
- Cursing rebels shows the king reckoned with the possibility of rebellion.
- Kedukan Bukit was found in Palembang, not Bangka.
- The passage does not link the Talang Tuo park to I-Tsing.

So the three inscriptions most clearly show that kings used inscriptions for many purposes, from recording journeys to demanding loyalty.

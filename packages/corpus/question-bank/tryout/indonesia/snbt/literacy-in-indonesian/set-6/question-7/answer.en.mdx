Kakek's remark is short, so its meaning has to come from **what made the string snap** and from **what Kakek chooses not to say**.

Look at the event first. When the wind turned, the dragon kite dived. The narrator pulled the string, and the kite "menukik lebih dalam" (dived deeper). The fibreglass frame "tidak mau melentur" (wouldn't bend). So the string was pulled from two ends: the hand tugging below, and the stiff kite resisting above. The string was simply in the middle. That's what "cuma ikut" (only follows) means: the string goes along with the pull at both ends.

If the string isn't to blame, the blame lies at its ends, the stiff frame and the tugging hand. Kakek knows that, because the advice that follows is "Jangan ditarik. Ikuti saja." (Don't pull. Just follow it.) Yet Kakek **names nobody**. The narrator waits for a lecture about "serat kaca" (fibreglass), "pabrik" (the factory) and "anak yang tidak mau mendengar" (a child who won't listen), then notes: "Ia tidak mengatakannya." (He didn't say it.) Kakek leaves the conclusion to the grandchild.

The other wordings don't fit.

- Kakek doesn't blame the string's maker. Kakek says the string isn't at fault, and uses it again.
- A string that's still usable is only the literal sense. It doesn't explain the second sentence, "Benang cuma ikut".
- The word _ikut_ ("follow") invites the reading "just following the crowd". But it's the string that follows, not the grandchild, and Kakek avoids digs all through the story.
- Kakek doesn't boast about the home-made kites. The white kite is even called "banyak maunya" (a fussy one).

So Kakek shows that the cause of the break lies with what pulled the string from both ends, not with the string, without naming who's at fault.

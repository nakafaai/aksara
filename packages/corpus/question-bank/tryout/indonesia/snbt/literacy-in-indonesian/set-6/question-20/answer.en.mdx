# Given

Lembahdaun's **tourism head claims** the city is most in demand among peak-season tourists solely because it has the highest occupancy rate.

# Asked

The main flaw in the tourism head's reasoning.

# Solution

> A tiny guesthouse that is almost full still hosts far fewer visitors than a large resort city that is eighty percent full.

The statement has a claim and a reason. The claim: Lembahdaun is the city **most in demand among tourists** in peak season. The reason: its occupancy rate is **the highest**. The main weakness is in the bridge from reason to claim.

An occupancy rate of <InlineMath math="90\%" /> is indeed the highest. But the first paragraph warns that occupancy is a percentage, and "dua kota dengan tingkat hunian yang sama dapat menerima tamu dalam jumlah yang sangat berbeda" (two cities with the same occupancy rate can take in very different numbers of guests). Work out the occupied rooms in peak season.

| City | Occupied rooms per night |
| --- | :---: |
| Tirtasari | <InlineMath math="4{,}000\cdot80\%=3{,}200" /> |
| Karangwangi | <InlineMath math="1{,}500\cdot80\%=1{,}200" /> |
| Lembahdaun | <InlineMath math="500\cdot90\%=450" /> |

Lembahdaun actually fills the fewest rooms. The head of the office **treats a percentage as if it were an amount**. A high occupancy rate in a small city only means its few rooms are nearly full.

The other options don't touch that bridge.

- The claim is specifically about peak season, so leaving out low-season data isn't a weakness.
- Room rates aren't needed to judge how many tourists come.
- The passage says the three cities are in one province.
- The claim compares the three cities in 2024, so Lembahdaun's 2023 data isn't needed.

# Answer

So the main weakness is that the head treats the percentage of rooms occupied as the number of tourists, when Lembahdaun actually has the fewest occupied rooms per night of the three cities.

# Given

In the final two paragraphs on **pranata mangsa**, the author separates fixed astronomical cues ("do not shift") from living-nature cues ("respond to actual weather"), then calls dismissing pranata mangsa as obsolete "hasty" because what falters is **fixed calendar timing**, while **observing nature** remains vital.

# Asked

The author's stance on pranata mangsa amid climate change.

# Solution

Join **paragraph four's split with paragraph five's conclusion** to keep clear what climate change weakens versus what remains valuable.

> Climate change primarily undermines the calendar-bound side of pranata mangsa, while its habit of closely observing nature remains useful.

## Checking the five stance statements

Evaluating **both halves of the author's balanced verdict** removes all four overstated choices.

| Statement of stance | Fit with paragraphs four and five | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Climate change mainly shakes the date-bound part while the habit of observing nature remains useful | Captures both the distinction and closing verdict | **Correct** |
| Completely obsolete because it predates modern forecasts | Explicitly called "hasty" by the author | **Incorrect** |
| All traditional signs fail and must be replaced by forecasts | False, living cues remain useful and cross-check forecasts | **Incorrect** |
| Traditional codification keeps it permanently accurate | False, codified date/star cues can now mislead | **Incorrect** |
| Central Java remains unaffected by climate change | False, text says conditions changed "even in its place of origin" | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The author's stance is that **climate change mainly weakens the part of pranata mangsa reliant on fixed calendar dates, while the habit of observing nature remains useful**.

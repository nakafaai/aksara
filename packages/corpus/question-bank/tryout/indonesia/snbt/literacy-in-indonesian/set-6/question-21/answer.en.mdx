The writer's position is built in the last two paragraphs. The fourth paragraph **sorts the signs** of the pranata mangsa, and the fifth **draws a conclusion** from that sorting. A good summary has to hold both.

In the fourth paragraph, the writer separates two kinds of sign. Signs tied to the sun and stars "tidak ikut bergeser" (don't shift along), so they can mislead when rain comes late. Signs from living things "menanggapi cuaca yang benar-benar terjadi" (respond to the weather that actually happens), so they tend to shift with the rain.

In the fifth paragraph, the writer calls the conclusion that the pranata mangsa is outdated "terburu-buru" (hasty). What's shaken is "anggapan bahwa musim datang dengan teratur pada tanggal yang sama" (the assumption that seasons arrive regularly on the same dates), while "kebiasaan mengamati alam" (the habit of observing nature) is what's "paling dibutuhkan" (most needed). So the position is: climate change **shakes the part that rests on dates**, but the habit of observing stays useful.

The other positions go against the text.

- Outdated because it was made before modern forecasts is the conclusion the writer rejects as hasty.
- The text doesn't say all the signs are wrong. Signs from living things still follow the weather. Nor does the writer propose a replacement, but checking each other.
- The year it was standardised doesn't guarantee accuracy. The fourth paragraph shows standardised signs can mislead.
- The fourth paragraph says conditions "di tempat asalnya pun berubah" (are changing even where it came from), so central Java is affected too.

So the writer's position is that climate change mainly shakes the part of the pranata mangsa that rests on regular dates, while the habit of observing nature stays useful.

# Given

During peak season in **Karangwangi**, star-rated hotels had <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> rooms at <InlineMath math="96\%" /> occupancy in 2023, and <InlineMath math="1{,}500" /> rooms at <InlineMath math="80\%" /> occupancy in 2024.

# Asked

The percentage increase in occupied star-rated hotel rooms per night from peak season 2023 to peak season 2024.

# Solution

Convert both **occupancy rates into occupied rooms** first, then divide the gain by the 2023 baseline (<InlineMath math="960" /> occupied rooms).

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{2023 occupied} &= 1{,}000 \times 96\% = 960\text{ rooms} \\
\text{2024 occupied} &= 1{,}500 \times 80\% = 1{,}200\text{ rooms}
\end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{increase} = \frac{1{,}200 - 960}{960} = \frac{240}{960} = 25\%" />
</MathContainer>

> Even though occupancy fell <InlineMath math="16" /> percentage points as room supply expanded <InlineMath math="50\%" />, occupied rooms actually rose <InlineMath math="25\%" />.

## Tracing the distractor percentages

Checking **base and metric** isolates the exact room-occupancy growth.

| Option | Ratio | What it computes | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="25\%" /> | <InlineMath math="240 : 960" /> | Added occupied rooms divided by 2023 occupied rooms | **Correct** |
| <InlineMath math="24\%" /> | <InlineMath math="240 : 1{,}000" /> | Divides by 2023 total capacity instead of occupied rooms | **Incorrect** |
| <InlineMath math="20\%" /> | <InlineMath math="240 : 1{,}200" /> | Divides by 2024 ending occupied rooms | **Incorrect** |
| <InlineMath math="16\tfrac{2}{3}\%" /> | <InlineMath math="16 : 96" /> | Relative decline in occupancy rate | **Incorrect** |
| <InlineMath math="16\%" /> | <InlineMath math="96 - 80" /> | Percentage-point difference in occupancy | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

Occupied star-rated hotel rooms per night during Karangwangi's peak season rose by **<InlineMath math="25\%" />**.

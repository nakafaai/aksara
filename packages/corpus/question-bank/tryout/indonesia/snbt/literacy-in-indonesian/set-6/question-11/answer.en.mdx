The two writers disagree about drivers' status. But disagreeing about **the remedy** doesn't always mean disagreeing about **which rights need protecting**. Look for the statement with evidence in both texts.

Text A lists, among the rights that come with employee status, "jaminan kecelakaan kerja yang dibayar perusahaan" (work accident cover paid by the company) and "aturan yang jelas sebelum akun diputus" (clear rules before an account is cut off). Text B rejects employee status, but its proposal in the last paragraph includes "jalur banding sebelum akun diputus" (an appeal route before an account is cut off) and "jaminan kecelakaan bagi setiap pengemudi selama ia membawa penumpang" (accident cover for every driver while carrying a passenger). Text B's writer also says accounts cut off without reason "juga merugikan saya" (hurt me too). So both agree that drivers **are entitled to accident cover and clear rules before an account is cut off**. They only differ on how.

The other statements are held by one side only, or by neither.

- Employee status is Text A's proposal. Text B rejects it with "Kami butuh perlindungan, bukan atasan" (we need protection, not bosses).
- Text A doesn't say freedom over hours means nothing. Text A says that freedom "menyempit" (narrows) because of bonus targets. Text B sees it as the main reason for choosing the job.
- Text B only says the company "akan ingin" (would want to) control hours, and part-time drivers "kemungkinan besar" (most likely) won't get a place. The word "certainly" goes beyond Text B, and Text A doesn't discuss it.
- Text A argues against the label "partner". Text B doesn't defend it, since what it defends is the flexibility.

So what both writers agree on is that drivers are entitled to accident cover and to clear rules before their account is cut off.

# Given

In the ride-hailing debate, **Text A** seeks employee status to secure "company-paid accident insurance" and "clear rules before an account is terminated." **Text B** rejects employee status, yet still proposes "an appeal process before account termination" and "accident coverage for every driver carrying a passenger."

# Asked

The statement agreed upon by both Text A's author and Text B's author.

# Solution

Separate **their clash over mechanism (employee status vs independent protections)** from **their shared agreement on core driver rights** stated in both texts.

> Both writers agree that drivers deserve accident coverage and clear due process before deactivation, differing only on whether employee status is the right vehicle.

## Comparing both texts across all five statements

Side-by-side verification of **Text A and Text B** isolates their single shared claim.

| Statement | Text A | Text B | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drivers deserve accident coverage and clear rules before account termination | Agrees (core rights sought) | Agrees (proposed in closing paragraph) | **Both agree** |
| Drivers should be classified as employees | Core demand | Rejected ("We need protection, not bosses") | **Only A** |
| Flexible hours mean nothing at all | Says flexibility has "narrowed" | Calls it the main reason to drive | **Neither** |
| Part-time drivers will certainly be pushed out | Not discussed | Uses "most likely," not "certainly" | **Neither** |
| The "partner" label is accurate | Refuted by A | B defends flexibility, not the label | **Neither** |

# Answer

Both authors agree that **drivers are entitled to accident coverage and clear rules before their accounts are terminated**.

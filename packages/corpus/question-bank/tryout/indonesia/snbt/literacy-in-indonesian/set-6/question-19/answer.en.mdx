# Given

**The text and** table track Karangwangi's rooms and occupancy and explicitly list four kinds of data missing from the report.

# Asked

Which statements about Karangwangi can be concluded from the available report data.

# Solution

> Treat the text's list of missing data as a filter, any claim needing room prices or old-versus-new hotel splits cannot be concluded.

The passage closes with a list of what the report **doesn't contain**: room rates, length of stay, guests per room, and a split between old and new hotels. That list is your test. A statement that needs any of that data can't be concluded.

The statement about low season can be concluded.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Low season 2023}&=1{,}000\cdot70\%=700\text{ rooms}\\
\text{Low season 2024}&=1{,}500\cdot60\%=900\text{ rooms}
\end{aligned}" />

Occupied rooms rise from <InlineMath math="700" /> to <InlineMath math="900" />, even though the occupancy rate falls.

The statement about revenue can't be concluded. Occupied rooms do rise, but revenue also depends on room rates, and the report has none. If rates fell far enough, revenue might not rise.

The statement about every old hotel can't be concluded. The table gives figures for the whole city, and the report "tidak memisahkan data hotel lama dari hotel baru" (doesn't separate old hotels from new ones). The old hotels could have stayed full while the new, still quiet hotels pulled the city's figure down.

The statement about why occupancy fell can be concluded. Occupancy is occupied rooms divided by available rooms. Available rooms rise by <InlineMath math="50\%" />, from <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> to <InlineMath math="1{,}500" />, while peak-season occupied rooms rise only <InlineMath math="25\%" />, from <InlineMath math="960" /> to <InlineMath math="1{,}200" />. When the denominator grows faster than the numerator, the quotient has to fall. That's arithmetic, not a guess.

# Answer

The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Low-season occupied rooms rose | Ya |
| Peak-season revenue was higher | Tidak |
| Every old hotel's occupancy fell | Tidak |
| Occupancy fell because available rooms grew faster | Ya |

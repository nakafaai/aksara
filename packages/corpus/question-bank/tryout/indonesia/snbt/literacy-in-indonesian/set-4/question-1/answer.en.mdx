The author's view has **two parts**, and each has its own conditions.

- **The sea wall proposal**. The author rejects the officials' **calculation**, because the wall **"ikut turun bersama tanah di sekitarnya"** (sinks along with the ground around it). Still, the author writes that **"tanggul memang dibutuhkan untuk menahan pasang dalam waktu dekat"** (a wall is needed to hold back high tides in the near term).
- **The residents' objection**. The author calls it **"sah"** (legitimate) but refuses to delay restrictions, because **"ketinggian yang sudah hilang tidak akan kembali"** (height already lost won't come back). The way out: limits on pumping **"berjalan bersama perluasan air perpipaan"** (go hand in hand with extending piped water).

Three options share the same first part, so the second part decides.

- The author rejects delaying restrictions until pipes reach every home.
- The author doesn't think the wall is unnecessary. It's needed for the near term.
- The author writes that household wells are **"belum tentu penyumbang terbesar"** (not necessarily the biggest contributor). Factory and hotel wells draw far more water per well.
- The author doesn't accept the wall-lifetime calculation. It **"melupakan satu hal"** (forgets one thing): the ground sinking too.

So the author rejects the officials' reasoning but still sees the wall as needed, and accepts the residents' objection as a condition for carrying out the policy, not a reason to delay it.

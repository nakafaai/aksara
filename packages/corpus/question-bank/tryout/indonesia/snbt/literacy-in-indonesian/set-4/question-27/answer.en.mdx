# Given

A store manager concludes from the jam study that cutting product variety **"will definitely raise sales."** Yet the passage notes that a 2010 meta-analysis found an **average effect near zero**, other experiments showed more choices **actually increased buying**, the jam study was **"not wrong"** (just not a universal rule), and the 2015 meta-analysis makes choice overload **more likely** under specific conditions rather than certain.

# Asked

All responses supported by the passage to challenge the manager's claim of certainty.

# Solution

Evaluate all five responses while **keeping "more likely" distinct from "certain"** and respecting the author's note that the jam study itself was not wrong.

> Three responses accurately challenge certainty: the near-zero 2010 average effect, experiments where more choices boosted buying, and 2015 conditions making fewer choices more likely to help.

## Checking the five candidate responses

Matching **each option to the 2010 and 2015 meta-analyses** selects the three valid responses.

| Response | Passage evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The 2010 meta-analysis found an average effect near zero | Directly stated in the passage | **Valid** |
| Other experiments found more choices actually increased purchases | Directly stated in the passage | **Valid** |
| Cutting choices is more likely to help when options are hard to compare | Matches the 2015 meta-analysis's cautious "more likely" | **Valid** |
| The jam study was proven wrong | False, the author states it was "not wrong" | **Invalid** |
| Meeting the 2015 conditions guarantees higher sales | False, those conditions only make overload "more likely" | **Invalid** |

# Answer

The valid responses are **the near-zero average effect in the 2010 meta-analysis**, **experiments in which more choices actually increased buying**, and **the 2015 conditions under which reducing options is more likely to help**.

Paragraph four says that if fares are abolished, service could become less frequent because fare money pays for drivers, fuel, and maintenance. That conclusion holds only if **the lost money is not fully replaced**. If the government always covered the gap from other funds, service need not decline. The argument depends on the assumption that lost fare revenue will not be fully replaced.

- Paragraph three says some new riders used to walk, but it does not claim drivers will never take the bus.
- Fuel prices are not discussed.
- Paragraph four says low-income residents have no choice but the bus, the opposite of all owning vehicles.
- Nothing assumes drivers would work unpaid. The author stresses that drivers must be paid.

So paragraph four depends on the assumption that lost fare revenue will not be fully replaced by other funding.

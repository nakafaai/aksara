The 2015 meta-analysis says overload is **more likely** when options are **hard to compare**, people **don't yet know what they want**, the decision is **complicated**, or people want to choose with **as little effort as possible**. The best design meets as many of those conditions as possible.

- **New workers and insurance plans**. Plans that differ in many terms are **hard to compare**, the decision is **complicated**, and workers new to insurance **don't yet know what they want**. Three conditions are met.

The other options are weaker.

- Repeating the jam experiment adds none of the 2015 conditions. Jam flavours are easy to taste and the decision is simple.
- T-shirts of the same price and style are easy to compare, and the decision is simple.
- Experienced insurance agents do face complicated plans, but they **already know** how to judge them. The "don't know what they want" condition disappears.
- Hurried shoppers meet the "little effort" condition, but near-identical bottled water is easy to compare and the decision isn't complicated.

So the best design is asking new workers who don't understand insurance to choose from <InlineMath math="5" /> or <InlineMath math="30" /> plans that differ in many terms.

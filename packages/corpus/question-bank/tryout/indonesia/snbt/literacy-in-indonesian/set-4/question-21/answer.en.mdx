Text 2's objection rests on one situation: in cities like hers, **pupils' home languages vary**, so a single provincial language does not suit many of them. Information that strengthens the objection must show this situation is **not limited to one classroom** but common in city schools. Data that many urban pupils have a home language different from the provincial one does exactly that.

- Village pupils sharing one language actually suit Text 1's proposal, and Text 2 agrees local lessons fit such areas.
- Enough teachers would make Text 1's proposal easier rather than strengthen Text 2's objection.
- Indonesian marks have nothing to do with the variety of home languages.
- How folk tales are recorded is a technical detail of Text 2's proposal, not the basis of its objection.

So the information that most strengthens Text 2's objection is that many pupils in city schools have home languages different from the provincial language.

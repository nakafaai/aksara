Paragraph three explains that resistance genes can travel on **plasmids**, even between different kinds of bacteria. Its last sentence draws the consequence: resistance that appears in relatively harmless bacteria **can move to bacteria that cause serious disease**. That is why resistance in harmless bacteria still matters.

- Bacteria do not turn into viruses, and the passage never suggests this.
- Nothing says harmless bacteria damage antibiotics.
- The passage does not compare how fast harmless bacteria multiply.
- Toxins produced under antibiotics are not mentioned.

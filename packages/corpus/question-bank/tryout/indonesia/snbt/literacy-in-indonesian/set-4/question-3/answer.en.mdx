The council member concludes the well closures failed because the ground still sank <InlineMath math="4" /> cm a year later. You need a reason from the text that makes those data fit a closure that worked.

The key is in paragraph 2: clay **"melepaskan air dengan sangat lambat, sehingga tanah dapat terus turun bertahun-tahun setelah pemompaan dikurangi, meski lajunya makin kecil"** (releases water very slowly, so the ground can keep sinking for years after pumping is reduced, though more and more slowly). So some sinking after the closures is expected. What matters is the rate. Before the closures the port sank <InlineMath math="6" /> cm a year on average (paragraph 3). A year later it sank <InlineMath math="4" /> cm. A shrinking rate **fits clay compacting more and more slowly**, so one year of data doesn't prove failure.

The other options fail for these reasons:

- The ground recovering when well water rises: this reverses paragraph 2, which says clay **"nyaris tidak mengembang lagi"** (hardly expands again).
- The GPS reading including sea rise: the instruments are swapped. A GPS fixed in the ground measures ground movement. It's the tide station on the pier that mixes the two movements.
- Household wells drawing more water: the direction is reversed. Paragraph 5 says deep wells of factories and hotels **"menyedot jauh lebih banyak air per sumur"** (draw far more water per well).
- The east coast sinking only <InlineMath math="1" /> cm: the figure is right but the place is wrong. East coast data don't explain what happened at the port after the closures.

Sinking that continues at a smaller rate fits clay that keeps compacting slowly, so one year of data can't show the closures failed.

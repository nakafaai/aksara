The experts advise **keeping existing drugs working as long as possible**. According to paragraph two, every use filters bacteria, and unnecessary use speeds up the filtering. Paragraph four names two unnecessary uses: antibiotics for colds and flu, which are caused by viruses, and low doses to make livestock grow faster. Stopping both **reduces needless selection**, so it fits the advice.

- Delaying treatment until new drugs appear contradicts the advice not to wait for new drugs.
- Low doses for livestock are described as giving bacteria many chances to be selected.
- Colds are caused by viruses, so antibiotics are useless there and only speed up selection.
- Never using antibiotics would leave bacterial infections that truly need them untreated. The advice is to keep the drugs working, not to stop treating.

So the step most in line with the advice is to stop using antibiotics for viral illnesses and as growth promoters in livestock.

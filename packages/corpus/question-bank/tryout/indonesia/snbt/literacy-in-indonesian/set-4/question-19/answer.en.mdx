# Given

**The text notes** that only Kota Baru's hospital can run blood tests, while every confirmed case is logged under the patient's home address.

# Asked

Which statements follow about how accurately cases and rates are recorded across subdistricts.

# Solution

> Hard-to-reach testing hides mild cases outside the city, yet outsiders tested in the city are still counted at their own home address.

Two details have to be used together. First, only the hospital in Kota Baru can test blood, so **mild cases outside Kota Baru are more often not recorded**. Second, cases are recorded by **home address**, not by where the patient is treated.

The statement that Kota Baru's rate is closer to the true picture can be concluded. Kota Baru's residents are the easiest to test, so their mild cases are missed less often.

The statement that the true rate in other districts may be higher can be concluded. If some mild cases aren't recorded, the table's figures are smaller than the true ones.

The statement that Kota Baru has many cases because other residents are recorded there can't be concluded. Residents of other districts treated in Kota Baru are still recorded under their own address, not as Kota Baru cases.

The statement about the gap in risk between Cibeureum and Kota Baru can be concluded. In the table, Cibeureum's rate is <InlineMath math="130" /> and Kota Baru's <InlineMath math="80" />. If Cibeureum's true rate is higher because mild cases were missed, while Kota Baru's is nearly complete, the true gap could be bigger than <InlineMath math="50" />.

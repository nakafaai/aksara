# Given

**Total district population** stayed constant at **<InlineMath math="700{,}000" />** in 2023 and 2024, while total cases rose from **<InlineMath math="480" />** (2023) to **<InlineMath math="574" />** (2024). The rate per <InlineMath math="100{,}000" /> residents equals <InlineMath math="(\text{cases} / \text{population}) \times 100{,}000" />.

# Asked

The rise in the district case rate per <InlineMath math="100{,}000" /> residents from 2023 to 2024, and how its percentage increase compares with the percentage increase in total cases.

# Solution

Compute **both years' rates** first, take their difference, and note that because the population denominator stays unchanged, **the percentage rise in rate equals the percentage rise in raw cases**.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{2023 rate} &= \frac{480}{700{,}000} \times 100{,}000 \approx 68.6 \\
\text{2024 rate} &= \frac{574}{700{,}000} \times 100{,}000 = 82
\end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="\Delta\text{rate} \approx 82 - 68.6 = 13.4\text{ per }100{,}000\text{ residents}" />
</MathContainer>

> The district rate rose by about <InlineMath math="13.4" /> per <InlineMath math="100{,}000" /> residents, and its <InlineMath math="19.6\%" /> rise matches the percentage increase in raw cases identically because population stayed constant.

# Answer

The district rate **rose by about <InlineMath math="13.4" /> per <InlineMath math="100{,}000" /> residents**, and in percentage terms **the rise equals the percentage rise in total cases**.

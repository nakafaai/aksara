The author's view shows in **the reasons and the proposal**. People avoid buses mainly because service is unreliable, not because of price, and removing fares could cut the money that pays for service. The proposal is in paragraph five: **discounts for those who need them**, with the remaining money spent on frequent buses, dedicated lanes, and reachable stops. The closing sentence says busy public transport is reliable public transport.

- The author does not want to abolish public transport, only to improve it.
- The author does not propose higher fares, only discounts for certain groups.
- Free transport for everyone is the proposal the author opposes.
- Motorbikes are mentioned as what beats unreliable buses, not as advice.

So the author holds that transport money should go to reliable service, while fare help goes to those who need it.

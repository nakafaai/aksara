# Given

In the village-clinic play, **Bu Warsih** tells Nia, "The letter is only the key, child. The door opens from the inside." She had written the referral letter twenty minutes before seven, yet only handed it over after **Mak Rosnah** (Sumi's mother-in-law) stood up before Rahmat spoke and said, "Take your wife. I am coming."

# Asked

The moment in the script that best represents "the door opened from the inside."

# Solution

Separate both halves of Bu Warsih's metaphor: the **key** is the referral letter and logistics, while the **door opened from the inside** is consent coming from Sumi's own family.

> Mak Rosnah standing up and telling Rahmat "Take your wife. I am coming." is the exact moment the family itself unlocks the decision to leave.

## Mapping five events against the door and key metaphor

Checking **each scene's role** distinguishes the family's consent from Bu Warsih's technical preparations.

| Event in the script | Role in the metaphor | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mak Rosnah stands before Rahmat speaks and says "Take your wife. I am coming." | Consent arising from inside the family itself | **Correct (Door opened from inside)** |
| Udin's ambulance waiting across the bridge | Technical preparation arranged by Bu Warsih | **Incorrect** |
| Referral letter waiting in the drawer since twenty to seven | The key itself, not the door | **Incorrect** |
| Bu Warsih handing the letter to Nia | Action taken after the family already opened the door | **Incorrect** |
| Rahmat arriving at the clinic | Contains no decision yet | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The moment that best represents "the door opened from the inside" is **when Mak Rosnah stands up before Rahmat can speak and says, "Take your wife. I am coming."**.

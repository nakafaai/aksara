# Given

**The port seawall** stands <InlineMath math="1.6" /> metres above highest tide, sea level rises <InlineMath math="0.4" /> cm a year, and the port ground sinks <InlineMath math="6" /> cm a year.

# Asked

After roughly how many years the top of the seawall will sit level with the highest tide if both rates continue.

# Solution

> Picture water climbing up while the wall sinks down with the clay, both motions close the same gap together, so add their rates.

The question asks when the <InlineMath math="1.6" /> metre gap between the top of the seawall and the highest tide runs out. The key is that two movements both eat into that gap.

First movement: the highest tide rises with the sea, <InlineMath math="0.4" /> cm a year by the planners' figure (paragraph 3). Second movement: the top of the seawall sinks, because paragraph 4 says the seawall **"berdiri di atas lempung yang sama dan ikut turun bersama tanah di sekitarnya"** (stands on the same clay and sinks with the ground around it). The seawall runs along the port, and the ground at the port sinks <InlineMath math="6" /> cm a year. The water rises and the seawall sinks, so **the gap shrinks by the sum of the two rates**.

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Gap shrinking per year | <InlineMath math="6+0.4=6.4" /> cm |
| Starting gap | <InlineMath math="1.6" /> m <InlineMath math="=160" /> cm |
| Time | <InlineMath math="160:6.4=25" /> years |

Check: in <InlineMath math="25" /> years the seawall sinks <InlineMath math="150" /> cm and the water rises <InlineMath math="10" /> cm. Together that's <InlineMath math="160" /> cm, the starting gap.

The other options each come from one wrong step:

- About <InlineMath math="27" /> years: only the sinking ground is counted, <InlineMath math="160:6" />. The sea rise is forgotten.
- About <InlineMath math="29" /> years: the sea rise is subtracted, <InlineMath math="160:5.6" />, though both movements shrink the gap.
- About <InlineMath math="114" /> years: the east coast rate is used, <InlineMath math="160:1.4" />, though the seawall is at the port.
- About <InlineMath math="400" /> years: this is the officials' calculation, <InlineMath math="160:0.4" />, which assumes the seawall doesn't sink.

# Answer

With the port's rate unchanged, the top of the seawall is level with the highest tide after about <InlineMath math="25" /> years, far from "centuries".

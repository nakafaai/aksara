Income depends on **two things at once**: how much coffee is produced and the price per kilogram, which the flavour score decides. First find each plot's price, then multiply by its yield.

Plot A, scoring 79, gets Rp70,000. Plots B and C, scoring 82 and 83, get Rp85,000. Plot D, scoring 86, gets Rp110,000.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{A: } 980 \times 70{,}000 &= 68{,}600{,}000 \\ \text{B: } 860 \times 85{,}000 &= 73{,}100{,}000 \\ \text{C: } 820 \times 85{,}000 &= 69{,}700{,}000 \\ \text{D: } 740 \times 110{,}000 &= 81{,}400{,}000 \end{aligned}" />

Plot D earns the most, about Rp81.4 million per hectare, even though it produces the least coffee.

- Plot A produces the most coffee, but its price is the lowest, so its income is actually the smallest.
- Plots B and C fall in between because they share the same price, Rp85,000 per kilogram.
- The incomes of Plots A and D differ widely, so they are not equal.

So the plot with the highest income per hectare is Plot D.

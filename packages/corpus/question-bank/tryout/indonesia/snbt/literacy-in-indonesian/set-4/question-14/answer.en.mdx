The policy has to meet two conditions at once: use STAR's finding, and answer Text B's objection about qualified teachers.

**Starting with the schools whose pupils are poorest: fits**. Text A says the advantage of small classes was **"lebih besar pada siswa dari keluarga kurang mampu"** (bigger for pupils from poorer families), and Text B advises, **"mulailah dari sekolah yang paling membutuhkan"** (start with the schools that need it most).

**Adding small classes as fast as certified teachers are available: fits**. This matches Text B's advice to speed up **"hanya secepat guru yang layak tersedia"** (only as fast as qualified teachers are available), and still shrinks classes as Text A proposes.

**Shrinking all classes at once with uncertified teachers: doesn't fit**. This is the Californian shortcut Text B criticises, where teachers without full certificates ended up concentrated in poor areas.

**Cancelling the class-size cut: doesn't fit**. This doesn't use STAR's finding at all, and Text B doesn't reject small classes either.

**Waiting for a local randomised trial: doesn't fit**. Both writers accept STAR's result. Text B doesn't ask for a new trial, but for a gradual rollout.

So the fitting policies are to shrink classes first at schools whose pupils are poorest, and to add small classes each year as fast as certified teachers can be hired.

# Given

The closing paragraph of the choice-overload passage states that the jam experiment was **"not wrong, yet cannot be turned into a universal rule"** and that the sharper question is **"under what conditions many choices hurt."** The 2015 meta-analysis also ties overload to options that are hard to compare.

# Asked

All statements that can be validly concluded from the passage.

# Solution

Test all five statements against **the author's closing thesis and the 2015 meta-analysis**, rejecting sweeping claims the passage explicitly contradicts.

> Three statements follow directly from the passage, while telling stores to "always" cut choices and calling the jam experiment wrong directly contradict the text.

## Checking all five statements against the text

Matching **each option to closing evidence** isolates the three valid conclusions.

| Statement | Passage evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The better question is under what conditions many choices hurt | Closely matches the passage's closing sentence | **Valid** |
| One famous experiment is not enough for a universal rule | Jam study "cannot be turned into a general rule" given mixed follow-ups | **Valid** |
| Making options easier to compare can lower choice-overload risk | Reduces a key 2015 trigger (hard-to-compare options) | **Valid** |
| Stores should always reduce choices | False, "always" clashes with mixed evidence | **Invalid** |
| The jam study was proven wrong | False, the author states it was "not wrong" | **Invalid** |

# Answer

The valid conclusions are that **the better question is under what conditions many choices hurt**, that **one famous experiment is not enough for a universal rule**, and that **making choices easier to compare can reduce the risk of choice overload**.

# Given

<InlineMath math="500" /> **shoppers pass each** jam stand (<InlineMath math="6" /> flavours vs <InlineMath math="24" /> flavours) using the stopping and buying percentages reported in the passage.

# Asked

How many times more buyers visit the <InlineMath math="6" />-flavour stand and how many times more shoppers stop at the <InlineMath math="24" />-flavour stand.

# Solution

> First find how many passers-by actually stop, then apply the buying rate to those stoppers because nobody buys without stopping first.

First work out **how many people** there are at each stage, then compare. The buying percentage is taken from the visitors **who stopped**, not from everyone who passed.

| Stage | <InlineMath math="24" />-flavour stand | <InlineMath math="6" />-flavour stand |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Stopped | <InlineMath math="60\% \times 500 = 300" /> | <InlineMath math="40\% \times 500 = 200" /> |
| Bought | <InlineMath math="3\% \times 300 = 9" /> | <InlineMath math="30\% \times 200 = 60" /> |

- **Buyers**. <InlineMath math="60 : 9 \approx 6.7" />. The <InlineMath math="6" />-flavour stand has **about <InlineMath math="6.7" /> times** as many buyers.
- **People who stopped**. <InlineMath math="300 : 200 = 1.5" />. The <InlineMath math="24" />-flavour stand stops **<InlineMath math="1.5" /> times** as many visitors.

The other options come from wrong working.

- <InlineMath math="10" /> times comes from <InlineMath math="30\% : 3\%" />, comparing buying percentages without allowing for the <InlineMath math="24" />-flavour stand stopping more people.
- <InlineMath math="0.67" /> times is the comparison the wrong way round, <InlineMath math="200 : 300" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.2" /> doesn't come from any number in the correct working.
- The last option swaps the two comparisons.

# Answer

So the **<InlineMath math="6" />**-flavour stand has about **<InlineMath math="6.7" />** times as many buyers, and the **<InlineMath math="24" />**-flavour stand stops **<InlineMath math="1.5" />** times as many visitors.

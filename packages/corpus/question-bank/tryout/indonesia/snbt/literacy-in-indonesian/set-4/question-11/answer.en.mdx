# Given

**Text A** proposes cutting grade-one classes from <InlineMath math="36" /> to <InlineMath math="24" /> students on the basis of **Project STAR**. Yet Project STAR compared small classes (<InlineMath math="13" /> to <InlineMath math="17" /> students) with regular classes (<InlineMath math="22" /> to <InlineMath math="25" /> students).

# Asked

The additional consideration beyond Text B's objections that most weakens using Project STAR to back Text A's proposal.

# Solution

Compare the **class-size range tested in Project STAR** directly against the **shift proposed by Text A**.

> A <InlineMath math="24" />-student class sits inside STAR's "regular" range (<InlineMath math="22" /> to <InlineMath math="25" />) and <InlineMath math="36" />-student classes were never tested, so STAR does not test shrinking from <InlineMath math="36" /> to <InlineMath math="24" />.

## Checking the five considerations

Testing **novelty beyond Text B and relevance to STAR's evidence** isolates the strongest critique.

| Consideration | Directly undercuts using STAR beyond Text B | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STAR compared <InlineMath math="13" /> to <InlineMath math="17" /> with <InlineMath math="22" /> to <InlineMath math="25" />, not <InlineMath math="36" /> with <InlineMath math="24" /> | Yes, the target size is not even STAR's small-class range | **Correct** |
| Conducted in the United States so completely unusable | Overreaches with "completely unusable" | **Incorrect** |
| Random assignment grouped stronger students together | Misunderstands how randomization works | **Incorrect** |
| California's smaller gains | Already raised by Text B | **Incorrect** |
| Higher total cost | Targets Text A's cost claim, not STAR's evidence | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The consideration that most weakens Text A's use of Project STAR is that **Project STAR compared classes of <InlineMath math="13" /> to <InlineMath math="17" /> students with classes of <InlineMath math="22" /> to <InlineMath math="25" /> students, rather than <InlineMath math="36" /> with <InlineMath math="24" /> students**.

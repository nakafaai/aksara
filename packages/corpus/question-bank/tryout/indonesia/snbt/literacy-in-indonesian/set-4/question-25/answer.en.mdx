The author's view isn't just that Balai Pustaka spread **and** filtered reading. It's that both were **one policy**: spreading was done **in order to** have a say in what people read. The strongest support has to tie the two activities to one aim.

An official's letter calling the expansion of Taman Pustaka, which is **spreading**, a way to suppress "wild reading", which is a **filtering** aim, shows that link directly. Both faces are shown working toward one goal.

The other options show only one face, or don't show the link.

- A list of rejected manuscripts only proves the **filtering** face.
- Lower prices fit the author's account, but don't show the prices were deliberately used to filter. They might be low for other reasons.
- Taman Pustaka libraries in small towns only prove the **spreading** face.
- Farming and health books also show only spreading.

So the strongest support is an official's letter calling the expansion of Taman Pustaka a way to suppress "wild reading".

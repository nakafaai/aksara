# Given

The Balai Pustaka author argues not merely that the agency happened to distribute and filter books, but that **distribution and filtering belonged to one unified policy** (spreading official books deliberately to crowd out "wild" reading).

# Asked

The finding that, if true, most strongly supports the view that distribution and filtering formed a single policy.

# Solution

Select the evidence that **explicitly links the act of distribution to the goal of filtering**, rather than proving only one side in isolation.

> A colonial official's letter describing Taman Pustaka expansion (distribution) as a method to curb "wild" publications (filtering) directly proves both halves served one policy.

## Evaluating the five candidate findings

Testing **whether each finding connects both functions under one goal** identifies the strongest support.

| Finding | Shows distribution | Shows filtering | Connects both into one policy | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Colonial official's letter calling Taman Pustaka expansion a way to curb wild reading | Yes | Yes | Yes, directly | **Strengthens most** |
| List of rejected manuscripts | No | Yes | No | **Incorrect** |
| Lower book prices | Yes | Does not prove filtering intent | Indirect | **Incorrect** |
| List of Taman Pustaka branches in small towns | Yes | No | No | **Incorrect** |
| Agriculture and health pamphlets | Yes | No | No | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The strongest supporting finding is **a colonial official's letter describing the expansion of Taman Pustaka as a way to curb the circulation of wild reading material**.

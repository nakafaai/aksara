The author's view isn't only that Balai Pustaka spread reading matter, or only that it filtered content. It's that **the two were linked in one policy**. The strongest support has to show that link.

An official's letter calling the expansion of Taman Pustaka a way to curb "bacaan liar" links the two directly. Libraries are a tool of **spreading**, and curbing "bacaan liar" is an aim of **filtering**. If an official describes one as a way to achieve the other, they really were one policy.

The other options are wrong.

- Sales figures for *Siti Nurbaya* show only the spreading face. They don't touch the filtering.
- A list of rejected manuscripts shows only the filtering face. The text already says that, and the list doesn't link it to spreading.
- Where Balai Pustaka's staff came from has nothing to do with the link between spreading and filtering.
- "Bacaan liar" still circulating widely suggests the policy may not have worked well, but doesn't show that spreading and filtering were designed together.

So the strongest support is a colonial official's letter calling the expansion of Taman Pustaka a way to curb the circulation of "bacaan liar".

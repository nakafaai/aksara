# Given

At the end of the story, **Pak Harun** writes `"Darto, Pagi Raya"` on the back page of his notebook beneath four other names, two of them crossed out. Earlier Pak Darto paid with exact change and the two exchanged `"Tomorrow I'll bring newspapers again"` and `"I know. That's why I come."`

# Asked

The best-supported interpretation of that back-page list and the part of the story that backs it most strongly.

# Solution

Match **both required parts**: the list tracks **regular customers** (with crossed-out names no longer coming), backed most clearly by **Pak Harun's conversation with Pak Darto**.

> The names record regular customers Pak Harun serves, with lines through those who no longer come, best supported by his heartfelt exchange with Pak Darto.

## Checking interpretation and evidence across the options

Verifying **list meaning and strongest supporting detail** eliminates all four flawed choices.

| Option | Accurate list meaning | Accurate supporting detail | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regular customers served, with crossed-out names no longer coming, backed by Pak Harun and Pak Darto's dialogue | Yes | Yes (shows personal commitment) | **Correct** |
| Customers who owe money | Wrong (Pak Darto pays exact change) | - | **Incorrect** |
| Regular customers, backed by the bus driver leaving | Yes | Wrong (bus driver is not a regular customer) | **Incorrect** |
| List of closing newspapers | Wrong (entries are people's names like Darto) | - | **Incorrect** |
| Crossed out because they switched to phones | Speculation (reason is never stated) | - | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

Those names are **regular customers he serves, with crossed-out lines marking those who no longer come, best supported by Pak Harun's conversation with Pak Darto**.

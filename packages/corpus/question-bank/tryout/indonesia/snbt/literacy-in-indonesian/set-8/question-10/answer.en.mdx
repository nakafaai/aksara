# Given

The story never explicitly states why **Pak Harun** keeps opening his stall even as ledger figures shrink and eleven of fifteen papers go unsold. Yet Bu Sri drops by daily with nasi uduk and change, Pak Darto sits beside the stall without reading, and Pak Harun promises to bring papers again tomorrow while recording Pak Darto's name.

# Asked

All interpretations of why Pak Harun keeps opening his stall that are supported by the story.

# Solution

Infer Pak Harun's motives from **what actually unfolds at the stall**, discarding guesses that clash with his shrinking ledger and his own words.

> The story supports two motives: the stall serves as a gathering place for people he knows well, and he feels bound by a promise to regular customers like Pak Darto.

## Checking the five interpretations against the story

Testing **each motive against stall events and dialogue** isolates the two supported answers.

| Interpretation | Story evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The stall is a meeting place for people he knows well | Bu Sri's daily visits and Pak Darto sitting beside the stall | **Supported** |
| He feels a promise toward regular customers like Pak Darto | Says "Tomorrow I'll bring papers again" and logs Darto's name | **Supported** |
| He believes paper sales will rebound | False, ledger numbers keep shrinking with most papers unsold | **Unsupported** |
| He does not yet know Pagi Raya is folding | False, he tells Bu Sri "I heard too" and knows three weeks remain | **Unsupported** |
| He wants to rival phone news | False, he brushes aside debating the bus driver | **Unsupported** |

# Answer

The story supports that **Pak Harun's stall serves as a meeting place for several people he knows well** and that **he feels a promise toward regular customers such as Pak Darto**.

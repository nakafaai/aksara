# Given

At Pak Harun's newsstand, **Pak Darto** buys a newspaper yet **"does not read it"** and sits watching buses. After sharing that his child will only video-call for Eid followed by a long silence, Pak Harun says "Tomorrow I'll bring newspapers again," and Pak Darto replies "I know. That's why I come."

# Asked

The interpretation of Pak Harun and Pak Darto's exchange best supported by the story.

# Solution

Connect **Pak Darto not reading his paper** with **his child staying away for Eid and Pak Harun's promise to show up again tomorrow**.

> Pak Darto comes for companionship at the stall, and learning his child will not come home makes Pak Harun's promise to keep showing up mean even more.

## Checking the five interpretations against the story

Testing **each reading against both characters' actions and dialogue** removes all four distractors.

| Interpretation | Fit with the story | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pak Darto comes for companionship, and his child's absence makes Pak Harun's promise to be there even more meaningful | Connects unread paper, long silence, and tomorrow's promise | **Correct** |
| Comes for news and wants to save money | False, he does not even read the paper he buys | **Incorrect** |
| Is angry at his child | False, he shares the news calmly without anger | **Incorrect** |
| Pak Harun promises Pagi Raya will keep publishing | False, he says Pagi Raya has only three weeks left | **Incorrect** |
| Pak Darto will stop coming once Pagi Raya closes | False, he comes for company and Pak Harun promises "newspapers" | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The best-supported interpretation is that **Pak Darto comes for companionship at the stall, and his child not coming home makes Pak Harun's promise to keep being there even more meaningful**.

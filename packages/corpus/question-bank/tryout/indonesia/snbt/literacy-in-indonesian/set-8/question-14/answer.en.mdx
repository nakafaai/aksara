The policy has to do two things.

- **Text A's aim**. Indonesian researchers' work should **"ikut membentuk perdebatan dunia"** (help shape global debate), so important findings should appear in English.
- **Text B's objection**. Users at home, such as firefighters and farmers, read in Indonesian, and the law requires Indonesian in scientific publications.

Publishing important findings in English-language journals while providing full Indonesian versions meets both. It also matches Text B's own proposal.

The other options are wrong.

- Requiring English only meets Text A and ignores users at home.
- Requiring Indonesian only ignores Text A's aim, and Text B itself doesn't reject English.
- Text B doesn't ask for citations to be dropped. It only says citations don't measure every benefit.
- Translating only the abstract isn't enough for users who need the full content in Indonesian.

So the most fitting policy is to publish findings important to global debate in English-language journals, and provide full Indonesian versions for users at home.

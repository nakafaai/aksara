The right policy has to pass **three tests** at once.

- **Text A's goal**. Findings must be able to **"ikut membentuk perdebatan dunia"** (help shape the world debate), so important findings need to appear in English.
- **Text B's objection on language**. Users such as firefighters and farmers read **"dalam bahasa Indonesia"** (in Indonesian), and the law **"mewajibkan bahasa Indonesia"** (requires Indonesian) in scientific publishing. So every result needs a full Indonesian version.
- **Text B's objection on assessment**. Citations **"hanya mengukur seberapa sering sebuah artikel dikutip"** (only measure how often an article is cited), so assessment can't rest on citations alone.

Only one policy passes all three. Each of the others fails one test.

- Publishing in English journals with only a summary fails the second test. Users at home get no full version.
- Publishing only in Indonesian fails the first test. Findings don't reach researchers abroad.
- Judging staff by citations alone fails the third test.
- Publishing important findings only in English fails the second test. Important findings, like the peat-fire research, are exactly what Indonesian-reading users need.

So the best policy publishes every result in full in Indonesian with an English abstract, publishes important findings in English too, and assesses usefulness alongside citations.

The key word is **"pura-pura"** (pretends). Bu Sri sees the money but chooses to act as if she doesn't. So she deliberately won't be paid.

Their relationship is also long-standing. Bu Sri has **"sudah dua puluh tahun menitipkan uang receh"** (been leaving her small change with him for twenty years), a sign of trust. The rice ritual repeats **"seperti setiap pagi"** (like every morning): Pak Harun still holds out money, and Bu Sri still refuses it without a word. They keep the habit up as a sign of friendship, without needing to talk about it.

The other options are wrong.

- Bu Sri isn't careless. *Pretends* means she does see the money.
- The story mentions no lost change and no anger. Bu Sri has kept leaving her change with him for twenty years.
- Nothing suggests Pak Harun is only pretending. He holds out money every morning.
- The change is left with him for giving change, not as payment held back. Nothing suggests the rice is used to pressure him.

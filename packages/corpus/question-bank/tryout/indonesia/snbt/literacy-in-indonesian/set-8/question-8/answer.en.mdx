# Given

The story notes **Bu Sri** has entrusted Pak Harun with change coins every morning for twenty years, sets down nasi uduk "as every morning," and "as every morning too" Pak Harun holds out payment that Bu Sri pretends not to see.

# Asked

All conclusions about Pak Harun and Bu Sri's relationship supported by the story.

# Solution

Test all five conclusions directly against **their morning routine details** to keep only the three supported facts.

> The story supports three conclusions: Bu Sri trusts Pak Harun with money, the nasi uduk is a gift rather than a sale, and this exchange happens repeatedly every morning.

## Checking the five conclusions against the story

Matching **each statement to exact text clues** selects the three valid answers.

| Conclusion | Story evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bu Sri trusts Pak Harun with money | Has entrusted him with small change every morning for twenty years | **Supported** |
| The nasi uduk is a gift rather than a sale | Bu Sri pretends not to see the money Pak Harun offers | **Supported** |
| The gesture happens repeatedly | Explicitly described as happening "as every morning" | **Supported** |
| Pak Harun cannot afford to pay | False, he offers payment every morning | **Unsupported** |
| Nasi uduk is payment for holding her change | False, the story never links the two as a trade | **Unsupported** |

# Answer

The story supports that **Bu Sri trusts Pak Harun with money**, that **the nasi uduk is a gift**, and that **this routine occurs repeatedly**.

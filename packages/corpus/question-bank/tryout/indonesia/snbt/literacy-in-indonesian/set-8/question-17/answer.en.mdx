The problem with the current data is that students **choose** their own breakfast habits, and those habits go with other things such as parents' working hours and sleep. If breakfast is given to **randomly chosen classes**, those other differences are spread evenly. A difference in scores between classes that get breakfast and classes that do not is then more likely to come from **breakfast itself**.

- Repeating the survey still relies on habits students choose themselves.
- Feeling "smarter" does not measure quiz scores.
- Year 10 and year 12 students differ in many ways, not only breakfast.
- More quizzes make scores more reliable but do not separate breakfast from the other differences.

So the most suitable design is to give breakfast at school to randomly chosen classes and compare their scores.

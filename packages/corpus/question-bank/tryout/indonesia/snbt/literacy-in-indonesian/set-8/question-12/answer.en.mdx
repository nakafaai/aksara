# Given

**Text A stresses** the citation reach of English publications, while Text B defends Indonesian-language science for wider domestic readers.

# Asked

Which statements can fairly be concluded about where both texts agree and differ.

# Solution

> Text B accepts English's international reach without banning it, while pointing out that citations miss practical readers outside universities.

Watch what Text B agrees with, and don't add views Text A never wrote.

The statement that both writers agree on the reach of English-language articles can be concluded. Text A says the most-read journals are in English, and Text B opens with **"Saya setuju bahwa artikel berbahasa Inggris menjangkau lebih banyak peneliti di luar negeri"** (I agree that English-language articles reach more researchers abroad).

The statement that Text B doesn't reject publishing in English can be concluded. Text B proposes English abstracts and summaries, and English publication **"bila temuannya penting bagi peneliti di luar negeri"** (if the findings matter to researchers abroad).

The statement that Text A calls Indonesian unable to be a language of science can't be concluded. Text A only discusses readership. No judgement of Indonesian's ability appears in Text A. The part about the language's ability is written by Text B as an extra argument.

The statement that citations don't measure usefulness to users who aren't researchers can be concluded. Text B says citations **"hanya mengukur seberapa sering sebuah artikel dikutip peneliti lain"** (only measure how often an article is cited by other researchers), while firefighters and farmers **"jarang mengutip artikel, tetapi merekalah yang memakai hasilnya"** (rarely cite articles, but they're the ones who use the results).

# Answer

The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Both writers agree on the reach of English-language articles | Ya |
| Text B doesn't reject publishing in English | Ya |
| Text A calls Indonesian unable to be a language of science | Tidak |
| By Text B, citations don't measure usefulness to non-researchers | Ya |

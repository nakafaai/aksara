First pin down the author's response. Then look for a finding that supports it directly, not just one on the same topic.

In paragraph 6 the author doesn't accuse the workers of lying, but writes that **"rasa terganggu yang hilang bisa jadi justru tanda bahwa sebagian frekuensi sudah tidak tertangkap lagi"** (losing the sense of being bothered may actually be a sign that some frequencies are no longer picked up). So the claim links two things: **feeling used to it** and **hearing that has already declined**.

The strongest finding has to compare workers who feel used to it with workers who are still bothered, with everything else kept equal. The option about workers with the same length of service does that. Length of service is equal, so length of exposure can't explain the difference. What's left is that feeling used to it goes with a worse hearing threshold, just as the author suspects.

Why the other options are wrong:

- Long-serving versus new workers: this supports damage building up, but doesn't touch feeling used to it at all.
- Being bothered again after leave: this weakens the author, because a habit that can wear off fits psychological getting-used-to better than permanent damage.
- Earplugs: complaints drop because less noise gets in, which has nothing to do with feeling used to it.
- Tinnitus compared with office staff: office staff are exposed to far less noise, so the difference could come from exposure, not from feeling used to it.

So the finding that best supports the author is that among workers with the same length of service, those who feel used to the noise have worse high-frequency hearing thresholds.

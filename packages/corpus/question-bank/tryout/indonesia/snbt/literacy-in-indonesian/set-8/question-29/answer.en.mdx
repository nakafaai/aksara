# Given

Flooding threatens **<InlineMath math="900" /> homes**. Group one sees a **gain frame (saved)**: Plan P (certain <InlineMath math="300" /> saved) or Q (<InlineMath math="1/3" /> chance all saved). Group two sees a **loss frame (damaged)**: Plan R (certain <InlineMath math="600" /> damaged, identical to P) or S (<InlineMath math="1/3" /> chance none damaged, identical to Q).

# Asked

What the majority of each group is most likely to pick according to Tversky and Kahneman.

# Solution

Apply **Tversky and Kahneman's rule**: people prefer certainty under a gain frame (P) yet take risks under a loss frame (S), even though P equals R and Q equals S.

> Most of group one picks the certain plan P while most of group two picks the risky plan S, despite P being identical to R and Q identical to S.

## Mapping all four plans and majority predictions

Comparing **frame, equivalence, and risk preference** yields the exact pair of choices.

| Group | Frame | Certain option | Risky option | Predicted majority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | Gain (saved) | P (<InlineMath math="300" /> saved) | Q (<InlineMath math="1/3" /> all saved) | **P (risk-averse)** |
| Second | Loss (damaged) | R (<InlineMath math="600" /> damaged = P) | S (<InlineMath math="1/3" /> none damaged = Q) | **S (risk-seeking)** |

# Answer

The majority of the first group chooses **P** and the majority of the second group chooses **S**, even though **P is identical to R and Q is identical to S**.

At the start Sari is **ashamed**: in junior high she pretended not to see her mother and did not know whether to laugh or be angry when her mother was called "Mbok Jamu". After taking her mother's place, she sees that her mother **knows every customer's measure and restrictions by heart**. She then closes her textbook, looks at her mother, and asks to be taught to make sinom. These actions show that she now **values** her mother's knowledge and care.

- Sari is never shown as proud of the work before.
- She does not seem afraid. She phones her mother and finishes the morning.
- Nothing suggests she wants to quit her pharmacy studies.
- A plan to sell jamu in the city is not mentioned.

So Sari's change is best described as moving from shame to valuing her mother's knowledge and care.

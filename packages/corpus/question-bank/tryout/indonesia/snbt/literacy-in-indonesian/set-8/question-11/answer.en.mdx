# Given

**Text A** cites the Universitas Tirta Raya study to claim English increases article citations. **Text B** points out that English articles generally come from **better-funded projects and more experienced teams**, confounding language with funding and team experience.

# Asked

The most serious flaw in Text A's evidence and the best additional data to test it.

# Solution

Pair the **confounding flaw (funding and team experience)** with the **controlled comparison** that holds funding and team experience equal across both languages.

> The biggest flaw is that language is tangled with funding and team experience, and the right test compares Indonesian and English articles from equally funded, equally experienced teams.

## Checking the five flaw and test pairs

Testing **both diagnosis and controlled comparison** isolates the single valid pair.

| Flaw and test pair | Accurate flaw | Test isolates language from funding and team | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Language confounded with funding and team experience, tested by comparing articles in both languages with equal funding and teams | Yes | Yes (controls both confounders) | **Correct** |
| Sample of <InlineMath math="400" /> articles too small | Wrong (neither text objects) | Adding universities does not separate funding/teams | **Incorrect** |
| Extending citation counting window | Yes | Still leaves funding and team confounded | **Incorrect** |
| Counting non-academic readers | Yes | Addresses usefulness, not citation cause | **Incorrect** |
| Citations do not measure usefulness | Misses Text A's specific citation claim | Right test paired with wrong flaw | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The most serious flaw is that **language is confounded with funding and team experience**, and the best test is **comparing articles in both languages whose funding and team experience are comparable**.

# Given

In the 1958 Thomas Cup passage, regarding the "spirit alone" tale, the author writes **"The story is moving, yet it hides something"** (acknowledging its appeal and spirit's role while calling it incomplete). Regarding wider meaning, the author states **"The victory's meaning also reached beyond sports"** as national pride.

# Asked

The author's stance toward both the "spirit alone" tale and the view that the victory meant more than sports.

# Solution

Check **both halves together**: the spirit-only tale is treated as **moving yet incomplete**, while the broader meaning beyond sports is **affirmed directly**.

> The author views the "spirit alone" story as moving yet incomplete, while affirming that the 1958 victory meant more than sports alone.

## Checking both stances across the five options

Matching **both parts against the author's wording** eliminates every one-sided distortion.

| Option | Stance on spirit-only story | Stance on meaning beyond sports | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Moving yet incomplete, and affirms the win meant more than sports | Exact | Exact | **Correct** |
| Rejects any meaning beyond sports | Exact | Wrong (expressly affirmed) | **Incorrect** |
| Rejects the spirit story altogether | Wrong (still credits spirit) | Exact | **Incorrect** |
| Accepts the spirit story completely | Wrong ("hides something") | Exact | **Incorrect** |
| Calls it deliberately fabricated and denies national meaning | Wrong on both | Wrong | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The author considers **the story moving yet incomplete, and affirms that the victory meant more than sports alone**.

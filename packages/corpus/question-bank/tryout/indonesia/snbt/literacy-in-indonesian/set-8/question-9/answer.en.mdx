# Given

**The story describes** Pak Harun's newsstand, papers sold and unsold, remaining newspaper titles, customer Pak Darto, and Bu Sri's remarks.

# Asked

Which statements can be concluded from the small numbers and dialogue in the story.

# Solution

> Check the small fractions of papers and titles straight from the text, and verify who actually spoke or bought what.

Two statements need a small calculation, the rest need reading who says what.

The statement about the papers left can be concluded. Pak Harun counts **"sebelas dari lima belas"** (eleven out of fifteen). Two thirds of <InlineMath math="15" /> is <InlineMath math="10" />, and <InlineMath math="11" /> is more than that.

The statement that Pak Darto was the only buyer can't be concluded. The pile shrank by <InlineMath math="15-11=4" />, but Pak Darto bought only **"Pagi Raya, satu"** (one Pagi Raya).

The statement about titles can be concluded. The stall once sold **"dua belas judul koran"** (twelve newspaper titles). After Pagi Raya stops, Pak Harun says **"Masih ada dua judul lagi"** (there are two more titles). That's <InlineMath math="2" /> out of <InlineMath math="12" />, one sixth.

The statement that Bu Sri heard the news from Pak Harun can't be concluded. Bu Sri brings the news herself, and Pak Harun answers **"Saya juga dengar"** (I heard too).

The statement that the stall sells only newspapers can't be concluded. There's **"sebuah toples permen jahe"** (a jar of ginger sweets), and the bus driver buys two.

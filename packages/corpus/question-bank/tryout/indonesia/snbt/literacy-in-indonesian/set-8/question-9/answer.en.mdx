Reserved seats are meant for **children from areas far from any state school**. The proposal is strongest if those children are shown to be **actually shut out** by the distance rule. Data that children from districts without a state senior high school are almost never admitted to one shows the very problem reserved seats would address.

- City parents preferring nearby schools does not concern children from distant areas.
- Rising tutoring costs relate to the original reason for the rule, not to reserved seats.
- Top schools' facilities do not show what happens to children from distant areas.
- How long address checks take concerns a different proposal.

So the strongest support is that children from districts without a state senior high school are almost never admitted to one.

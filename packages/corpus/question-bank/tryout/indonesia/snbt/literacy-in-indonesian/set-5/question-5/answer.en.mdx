# Given

In paragraph two, the author refutes the claim that the Lombok Strait divides fauna because it is "too wide" by pointing to the **Sunda Strait** (tigers and rhinos lived on both Sumatra and Java), concluding that **depth** is the true difference.

# Asked

The unstated assumption that must hold for the Sunda Strait example to refute the "too wide" explanation.

# Solution

Test the argument by **negating the assumption**: if the Sunda Strait were far narrower than the Lombok Strait, defenders of the width explanation could simply say animals crossed because it was narrow, collapsing the rebuttal.

> The Sunda Strait example refutes the "too wide" explanation only if the Sunda Strait is not dramatically narrower than the Lombok Strait.

## Testing the five candidate assumptions

Checking **whether each claim is logically required** isolates the exact hidden premise.

| Candidate assumption | Logical fit with the argument | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Sunda Strait is not much narrower than the Lombok Strait | Required so width cannot explain the difference | **Correct** |
| The Sunda Strait is deeper than the Lombok Strait | Reverses the passage (Sunda Shelf is shallow) | **Incorrect** |
| Tigers and rhinos are identical subspecies | Overly strict, similar land fauna suffices | **Incorrect** |
| Land mammals can never cross water | Contradicts paragraph three (rare accidental crossings) | **Incorrect** |
| Sea levels fell further at the Sunda Strait | Unnecessary to refute the width claim | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The rebuttal depends on the unstated assumption that **the Sunda Strait is not much narrower than the Lombok Strait**.

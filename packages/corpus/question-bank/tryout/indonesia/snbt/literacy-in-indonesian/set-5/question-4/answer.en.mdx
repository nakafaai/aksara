# Given

The Wallace Line author argues that **the deep Lombok Strait blocked land mammals from crossing**. The text also notes that a few shared species do not disprove the line because humans may have transported them, birds cross easily anyway, and strait width is not decisive.

# Asked

The finding that most weakens the author's claim that the deep Lombok Strait blocked land mammals.

# Solution

An effective counter-finding must satisfy **three criteria at once**: involve **land mammals**, cover **most species** (not a lone exception), and date **long before human arrival** to rule out human transport.

> Fossils showing most of Bali's land mammals already lived on Lombok before humans arrived directly undercut the claim that deep water blocked them.

## Checking all five findings against the three criteria

Evaluating **animal group, breadth, and pre-human timing** isolates the strongest counter-evidence.

| Finding | Land mammals | Large share of species | Pre-human timing | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fossils of most Bali land-mammal species on Lombok before human arrival | Yes | Yes | Yes | **Weakens most** |
| One monkey species on both islands | Yes | Only one species | Possibly human-brought | **Incorrect** |
| Dozens of bird species on both islands | No (birds cross easily) | Yes | - | **Incorrect** |
| Strait turns out narrower than thought | Ignores depth | - | - | **Incorrect** |
| Mammals appear only after livestock trade grew | Yes | - | Supports human transport | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The finding that most weakens the author's claim is **fossils showing that most of Bali's land-mammal species already lived on Lombok long before humans arrived**.

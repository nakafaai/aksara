The two texts look opposed, so the point where they meet is easy to miss. For each statement, look for evidence **in both texts**. One text isn't enough.

Start with the daily limit. Text A backs it openly: the decision to limit hikers is "tepat dan seharusnya diambil lebih awal" (right, and should have been taken earlier), on the grounds of litter, edelweiss and safety. Text B turns out to accept it too. Its first paragraph says "pembatasan pendaki bukan hal yang kami tolak" (limiting hikers isn't what we reject), and its proposal in the last paragraph keeps "jumlah pendaki per hari tetap <InlineMath math="400" />" (the number of hikers per day at <InlineMath math="400" />). Text B only challenges "cara kuota itu dibagikan" (the way the quota is handed out). So both writers agree that **the number of hikers per day needs a limit**.

The other statements are held by one writer only, or by neither.

- The yearly number of hikers not needing to fall is Text A's hope. Text B doubts it, saying weekdays "tidak akan terisi oleh anjuran" (won't be filled by advice).
- Text B does complain about city tour operators, but about who gets places and porter work. Neither text says operators damage the ecosystem.
- Neither writer puts income above the mountain. Text A says the quota protects livelihoods, and Text B keeps the load on the mountain the same.
- Moving to weekdays is Text A's advice. Text B rejects it by describing individual hikers as "pekerja dan pelajar" (workers and students) who are only free at weekends.

So what both writers agree on is that the number of hikers in a single day needs to be limited so the mountain isn't damaged.

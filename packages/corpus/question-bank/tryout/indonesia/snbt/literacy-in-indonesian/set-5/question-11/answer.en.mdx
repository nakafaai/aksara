# Given

In the mountain-quota debate, **Text A** backs a daily climber cap to prevent litter, edelweiss damage, and safety risks. **Text B** likewise states "limiting climbers is not what we reject" and keeps "the daily number of climbers at <InlineMath math="400" />," challenging only how slots are distributed.

# Asked

The statement agreed upon by both Text A's author and Text B's author.

# Solution

Look for **shared ground backed by explicit quotes in both texts**, filtering out points held by only one writer or neither.

> Both writers agree that daily climber numbers must be capped to protect the mountain, disagreeing only on how the daily quota is allocated.

## Checking the stances of both texts across all five statements

Side-by-side testing of **Text A and Text B** pinpoints their single shared premise.

| Statement | Text A | Text B | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Daily climber numbers must be capped so the mountain is not damaged | Strongly agrees | Agrees (keeps cap at <InlineMath math="400" />) | **Both agree** |
| Annual total climbers need not fall | A's hope | Doubted by B | **Only A** |
| City tour operators damage the ecosystem | Not stated | B only objects to slot hoarding and porter jobs | **Neither** |
| Income comes ahead of mountain protection | No (quota protects livelihoods) | No (keeps mountain load unchanged) | **Neither** |
| Solo climbers can easily shift to weekdays | A's suggestion | Rejected by B (workers and students have weekends off) | **Only A** |

# Answer

Both authors agree that **the number of climbers per day needs to be limited so the mountain is not damaged**.

# Given

In paragraph one, the author asserts that keroncong **"as a musical genre, with its distinctive ensemble and accompaniment patterns, was unknown in Portugal,"** while openly acknowledging Portuguese origins for the *cavaquinho* instrument and Portuguese creole words.

# Asked

The finding that, if true, most weakens the author's statement that keroncong as a musical genre was unknown in Portugal.

# Solution

Target **the exact thing the author denies** (the full musical genre (ensemble plus accompaniment pattern) existing in Portugal), skipping clues about instruments or language the author already accepts.

> A sixteenth-century Portuguese manuscript describing an ensemble with the same instrument lineup and accompaniment pattern as keroncong directly undercuts the claim that the genre was unknown in Portugal.

## Checking each finding against what the author denies

Separating **already-conceded Portuguese traces** from **proof of the full genre in Portugal** isolates the sole refutation.

| Finding | Counters claim about full genre in Portugal | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sixteenth-century Portuguese manuscript describing a group with keroncong's ensemble and accompaniment pattern | Yes, shows the full genre already existed in Portugal | **Weakens most** |
| *Cavaquinho* is still played in Portugal | Already acknowledged as the instrument's origin | **Incorrect** |
| Portuguese creole words in Tugu songs | Already conceded in paragraph two, lyrics are not accompaniment | **Incorrect** |
| Similar plucked instruments on other islands | Concerns instrument spread only, consistent with the text | **Incorrect** |
| Ancestral traditions of Tugu residents | Says nothing about music inside Portugal | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The finding that most weakens the statement is **a sixteenth-century manuscript from Portugal describing a musical group with the same instrument lineup and accompaniment pattern as keroncong**.

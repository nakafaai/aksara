# Given

**The table records** DO and BOD readings from T1 to T5 in 2021 and 2024, with standards of DO at least <InlineMath math="4" /> mg/L and BOD at most <InlineMath math="3" /> mg/L.

# Asked

Whether each statement is true or false based on the table and water-quality standards.

# Solution

> Remember the two scales point opposite ways because higher DO means healthier oxygen while lower BOD means cleaner water.

Each statement points to particular rows and columns. Read the table with two handles: higher DO is better, with a limit of **at least** <InlineMath math="4" /> mg/L, while lower BOD is better, with a limit of **at most** <InlineMath math="3" /> mg/L.

The statement about BOD in 2021 is true. From T3 to T4 and on to T5, the figures are <InlineMath math="10.0" />, then <InlineMath math="7.0" />, then <InlineMath math="5.0" /> mg/L. Every step goes down.

The statement about the DO condition in 2024 is true. The points downstream of the tofu workshops are T3, T4 and T5. Their DO values are <InlineMath math="5.2" />, <InlineMath math="5.0" /> and <InlineMath math="4.2" /> mg/L. All are at least <InlineMath math="4" /> mg/L. The statement only mentions DO, so the BOD that's still high doesn't make it false.

The statement about DO at T5 is false. The figure falls from <InlineMath math="4.6" /> to <InlineMath math="4.2" /> mg/L.

The statement about BOD at T2 is false. BOD at T2 in 2024 is <InlineMath math="3.0" /> mg/L, and the limit is at most <InlineMath math="3" /> mg/L. A figure **equal to the limit** still meets it and doesn't exceed it.

The statement about the DO gap is true.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Gap in 2021}&=6.6-3.0=3.6\\
\text{Gap in 2024}&=6.4-5.2=1.2\\
\frac{1.2}{3.6}&=\frac13<\frac12
\end{aligned}" />

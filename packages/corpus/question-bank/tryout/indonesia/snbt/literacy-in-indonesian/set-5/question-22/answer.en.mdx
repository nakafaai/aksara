# Given

In paragraph three of the keroncong passage, the author details how the **cello** (a European bowed instrument) is not bowed in European style, but **plucked in patterns imitating the Javanese kendang drum**, taking over a local instrument's musical job.

# Asked

The primary function of this cello-playing detail within the passage.

# Solution

Connect the **cello example** back to the **passage's central thesis** that keroncong was shaped locally in the archipelago even when its instruments arrived from abroad.

> Showing a European cello plucked like a Javanese drum proves that imported instruments were adapted to local musical needs, reinforcing that keroncong took shape in the archipelago.

## Checking the five candidate functions

Testing **how each option relates to the main thesis** isolates the true purpose of the detail.

| Candidate function | Fit with the passage | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shows outside instruments were adapted to local musical needs, reinforcing that keroncong was formed locally | Directly supports the central thesis | **Correct** |
| Shows keroncong moved closer to European music | Reversed, a European instrument takes on a Javanese role | **Incorrect** |
| Shows players disliked the kendang | False, its rhythmic pattern is actively imitated | **Incorrect** |
| Compares which instrument arrived first | False, the cello explicitly arrived later | **Incorrect** |
| Shows players lacked skill to bow a cello | False, plucking is an intentional musical choice | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The detail primarily **shows that imported instruments were used according to local musical needs, strengthening the thesis that keroncong took shape in the Indonesian archipelago**.

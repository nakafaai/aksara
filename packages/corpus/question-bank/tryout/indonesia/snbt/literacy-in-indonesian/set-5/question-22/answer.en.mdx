To find what a detail is for, ask **which big idea it helps**. This passage's big idea is in the first paragraph: keroncong "tumbuh di Nusantara" (grew in the archipelago) even though its instrument came from outside.

Now read the detail about the cello. The cello is a European instrument. If keroncong merely copied Europe, the cello would be bowed "seperti dalam musik Eropa" (as in European music). It isn't. Its strings are plucked "dengan pola yang meniru tabuhan kendang, sehingga sebuah alat Eropa mengambil alih tugas sebuah alat Jawa" (in a pattern that imitates kendang drumming, so a European instrument takes over the job of a Javanese one). The instrument came from outside, but **how it's used is set by the local music**.

That's a small example of the big idea. Like the Portuguese plucked instrument that became the cak and the cuk, the cello was reworked here too. The detail strengthens the idea that keroncong was shaped in the archipelago.

The other functions don't fit the text.

- The detail points the opposite way from moving closer to Europe. A European instrument is made to work like a Javanese one.
- The text doesn't say the kendang fell out of favour. Its pattern is imitated, a sign its sound was still wanted.
- The text doesn't compare which came first. The cello is said to arrive later, with the violin and guitar, while the two small plucked instruments "tetap" (still) mark the sound.
- The text says nothing about the players' ability. Plucking a cello in a kendang pattern is a musical choice, not a sign of inability.

So the detail mainly serves to show that an instrument from outside is used according to the needs of the local music, which strengthens the idea that keroncong was shaped in the archipelago.

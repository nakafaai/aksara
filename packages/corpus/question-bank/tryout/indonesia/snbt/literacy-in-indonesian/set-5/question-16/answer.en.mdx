The agency calls the treatment plant (IPAL) a success, but "success" can mean many things. A conclusion the data supports has to hold up against **the numbers and the standard together**: DO of at least <InlineMath math="4" /> mg/L and BOD of at most <InlineMath math="3" /> mg/L.

Look at T3, the first point downstream of the tofu workshops.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Drop in BOD}&=\frac{10.0-4.5}{10.0}=55\%\\
\text{DO 2024}&=5.2\ge4\\
\text{BOD 2024}&=4.5>3
\end{aligned}" />

BOD falls by more than half. The DO condition is met, but the BOD condition isn't yet. So T3 **improves a lot, but meets only one of the two conditions**. The table fully supports that conclusion.

Each of the other conclusions falls to one number.

- T3 doesn't meet the standard yet, because its BOD is <InlineMath math="4.5" /> mg/L, still above <InlineMath math="3" /> mg/L.
- The number of points meeting both conditions doesn't grow. In 2021 only T1 and T2 pass. In 2024 it's still only T1 and T2, because T3, T4 and T5 fail on BOD. The count stays at two.
- T2 lies upstream of the workshops, at km <InlineMath math="11" />, while the plant releases its water between T2 and T3. Water from the plant never passes T2, so BOD at T2 says nothing about how the plant works.
- T5 is also downstream of the workshops, but its quality gets worse: DO falls from <InlineMath math="4.6" /> to <InlineMath math="4.2" /> mg/L, and BOD rises from <InlineMath math="5.0" /> to <InlineMath math="6.5" /> mg/L. The word _semua_ ("all") is wrong.

So the best-supported conclusion is that BOD at T3 fell by more than half, yet that point meets only one of the two class two conditions.

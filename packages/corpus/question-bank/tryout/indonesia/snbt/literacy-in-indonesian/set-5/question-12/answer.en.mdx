# Given

**Text A and** Text B debate mountain climbing quotas, ecosystem preservation, and fair livelihoods for slope communities.

# Asked

Which text's underlying reasoning matches each general statement best.

# Solution

> Match the guiding principle of each statement to each text's core concern, namely mountain protection in Text A and slope residents' fairness in Text B.

None of these statements is quoted straight from the texts. Each one is **the general view behind a paragraph**. So look for the paragraph that reasons the same way, then see which text it belongs to.

The statement about accepting a loss now to prevent a bigger one later fits Text A. Its last paragraph grants the objection about income, then answers that a damaged mountain "tidak akan didatangi siapa pun" (won't be visited by anyone), so residents' income "hilang seluruhnya" (is lost entirely). A small loss now is accepted to avoid a total loss later.

The statement about a rule that's fair to the mountain but not necessarily to its people fits Text B. Its writer accepts the limit but shows porters' turns falling "dari rata-rata <InlineMath math="4" /> kali sebulan menjadi kurang dari <InlineMath math="2" /> kali" (from an average of <InlineMath math="4" /> a month to fewer than <InlineMath math="2" />).

The statement about empty weekday trails fits Text A, which says hikers "dapat datang pada hari kerja, ketika jalur sekarang nyaris kosong" (can come on weekdays, when the trails are now nearly empty). Text B uses the same fact for the opposite conclusion.

The statement about who gets a place fits Text B. Its core is the sentence "Yang kami persoalkan adalah cara kuota itu dibagikan" (what we question is how the quota is handed out), and both of its proposals settle **who**, while the number stays the same.

The statement about a proposal that looks good on paper fits Text B. Its writer says the advice to switch days "terdengar masuk akal di atas kertas" (sounds sensible on paper), then shows that individual hikers are only free at weekends.

# Answer

The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| A loss now to prevent a bigger loss | Teks A |
| Fair to the mountain, not necessarily to the slope's people | Teks B |
| Empty weekday trails leave room | Teks A |
| Who gets a place matters as much | Teks B |
| A proposal on paper can fail | Teks B |

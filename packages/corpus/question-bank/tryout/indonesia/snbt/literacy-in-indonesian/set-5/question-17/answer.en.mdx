The question asks about the **added** BOD between two points, not the BOD at T3. So you need two steps: work out that added amount for each year, then compare the two.

The added BOD is the BOD at T3 minus the BOD at T2.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Added in 2021}&=10.0-2.5=7.5\text{ mg/L}\\
\text{Added in 2024}&=4.5-3.0=1.5\text{ mg/L}
\end{aligned}" />

Now find what share of the 2021 amount has gone. The denominator is **the 2021 amount**, because that's the starting state.

<BlockMath math="\frac{7.5-1.5}{7.5}=\frac{6.0}{7.5}=80\%" />

Why is that larger than the drop in BOD at T3? In 2024, the water already carries a BOD of <InlineMath math="3.0" /> mg/L when it reaches the tofu workshops. That part doesn't come from the workshops, so it mustn't be counted as their load.

The other answers come from picking the wrong number or the wrong denominator.

- <InlineMath math="55\%" /> is the drop in BOD at T3 alone, <InlineMath math="(10.0-4.5):10.0" />. That figure still mixes the workshops' waste with the BOD already there at T2.
- <InlineMath math="45\%" /> is the 2024 BOD at T3 divided by the 2021 BOD at T3. That's the share left at T3, not the share removed.
- <InlineMath math="20\%" /> is <InlineMath math="1.5:7.5" />, the added amount that's still left. The question asks for the share that went away.
- <InlineMath math="60\%" /> comes from subtracting the 2024 BOD at T2, <InlineMath math="(7.5-3.0):7.5" />, when you should subtract the amount added in 2024.

So the added BOD between T2 and T3 fell by <InlineMath math="80\%" />.

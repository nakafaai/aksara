The official warns that the data **cover only six months of one year**. A pattern from one season could be a coincidence, caused for example by transport costs or traders' stockpiles. To test the link between rainfall and supply, the researcher needs to see whether **supply falls whenever rain is heavy**, over many cases. Rainfall and supply records for several years provide that comparison.

- Prices for July and August add only two months and contain neither supply nor rainfall.
- Prices of large red chillies say nothing about the supply of bird's eye chillies.
- Buyers' opinions about prices do not measure supply.
- The number of traders does not show how much chilli is harvested in heavy rain.

So the most helpful data are rainfall and supply for the same months over several years.

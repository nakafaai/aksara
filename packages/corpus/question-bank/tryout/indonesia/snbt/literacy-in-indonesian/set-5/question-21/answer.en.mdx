# Given

In paragraph one of the keroncong passage, the author calls the label *Portuguese heritage music* **"not wrong, yet easily misleading"**: Portuguese arrivals brought small plucked instruments related to the *cavaquinho*, yet **keroncong as a musical genre** with its ensemble and accompaniment patterns was unknown in Portugal and grew in the Indonesian archipelago.

# Asked

The author's stance on calling keroncong *Portuguese heritage music*.

# Solution

Keep apart the **two origins the author separates** from the start: the origin of the plucked instrument (Portuguese) versus the origin of the genre itself (formed in the archipelago).

> The label holds true for the origin of its plucked instrument, but misleads if taken to mean the musical genre itself was imported ready-made from Portugal.

## Checking the five candidate stances

Comparing **instrument origin versus genre origin** rules out all four one-sided options.

| Candidate stance | Alignment with the passage | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| True for the plucked instrument's origin, wrong if taken to mean the genre itself came from Portugal | Mirrors paragraph one's exact distinction | **Correct** |
| Keroncong originated entirely in Java | False, plucked instruments came from abroad and Kampung Tugu was not Javanese | **Incorrect** |
| Tugu residents preserved the music unchanged | False, the passage traces major shifts through stambul and radio | **Incorrect** |
| Tracing keroncong's origins is impossible | False, the author traces its history in detail | **Incorrect** |
| Accurate before the late nineteenth century only | False, the genre was never known in Portugal at any point | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The author considers the label **true regarding the origin of its plucked instrument, yet wrong if understood to mean that the musical genre itself came from Portugal**.

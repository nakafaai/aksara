Text 2's proposal is a **comparison**: with limited funds, putting public transport first will cut emissions **faster** than subsidising private cars. Data that strengthen it must compare the results of both choices for the same money. Data showing that each rupiah of subsidy for electric buses cuts more emissions than a car subsidy does exactly that.

- Rising sales show the incentive attracts buyers, not that it is the fastest way to cut emissions.
- Maintenance costs have nothing to do with how much emissions fall.
- Where buyers live does not compare buses and cars.
- If coal plants close, Text 2's objection to electric cars actually weakens.

So the data that most strengthen Text 2's proposal show that each rupiah of subsidy for electric buses cuts more emissions than a car subsidy.

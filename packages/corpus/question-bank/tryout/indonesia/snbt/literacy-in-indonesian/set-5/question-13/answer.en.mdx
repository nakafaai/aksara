# Given

**Text B's** first proposal caps any single buyer at **<InlineMath math="6" /> slots** "so the quota cannot be bought up" by city tour operators who normally book dozens of slots at once.

# Asked

The condition that must hold for the <InlineMath math="6" />-slot per-buyer cap to achieve its goal.

# Solution

Spot the **obvious loophole that would defeat a per-buyer cap**: if a tour operator can split its order across many different buyer names, it can still scoop up dozens of slots.

> A cap of <InlineMath math="6" /> slots prevents hoarding only if tour operators cannot easily bypass it by booking under multiple names.

## Testing whether each option is truly required

Negating **each candidate condition** reveals the sole prerequisite for the cap to work.

| Candidate condition | Effect if false | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tour operators cannot easily bypass the cap by booking under many names | Operators keep hoarding dozens of slots, defeating the cap | **Correct** |
| Solo hikers prefer weekdays | Clashes with Text B (they are free only on weekends) | **Incorrect** |
| Village porters are more skilled | Unrelated to preventing bulk booking | **Incorrect** |
| Daily quota is raised | Clashes with Text B (keeps daily cap at <InlineMath math="400" />) | **Incorrect** |
| Past porter income was already adequate | Does not affect whether a <InlineMath math="6" />-slot cap stops hoarding | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The proposal can achieve its goal only if **tour operators cannot easily bypass the cap by booking through many different names**.

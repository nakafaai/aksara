A percentage decrease is measured against **the starting price**, here February's. The decrease is the difference between February and May.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{decrease} &= 74{,}000 - 36{,}000 = 38{,}000 \\ \text{percentage} &= \frac{38{,}000}{74{,}000} \times 100\% \approx 51.4\% \end{aligned}" />

- <InlineMath math="38.0\%" /> just copies the difference in thousands of rupiah without dividing by the starting price.
- <InlineMath math="48.6\%" /> is May's price as a share of February's, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{36}{74}" />, not the size of the decrease.
- <InlineMath math="105.6\%" /> divides the difference by May's price instead of the starting price.
- <InlineMath math="205.6\%" /> is February's price as a percentage of May's.

So the average price of bird's eye chilli fell by about <InlineMath math="51.4\%" /> from February to May.

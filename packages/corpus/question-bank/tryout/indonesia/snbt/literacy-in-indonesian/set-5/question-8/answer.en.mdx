In a story told in the first person, a claim the narrator makes isn't necessarily true. Test the claim against two things around it: **the sentence before** and **the action after**.

The sentence before says young people today are "lebih percaya pada kertas daripada pada orang yang berdiri di sebelahnya" (more trusting of paper than of the person standing next to them). That's a dig. The person standing next to Darto is Pak Wiryo. Pak Wiryo feels trusted less than a notebook. Someone who truly isn't offended has no need for a dig.

The action after: the narrator "hanya memeriksa silinder empat sesudah ia naik ke geladak, supaya ia tidak merasa digurui" (only checked cylinder four after Darto went up on deck, so Darto wouldn't feel lectured). That reason is back to front. If the check happened in front of Darto and the needle turned out right, the one who'd have to admit a mistake is Pak Wiryo. So waiting for Darto to leave **protects the narrator**, not Darto. The next paragraph proves it: "Injektornya memang mulai menetes" (the injector really had started to drip), and the cleaning is done alone.

Put the two together: Pak Wiryo is offended, but denies it.

The other readings don't fit the paragraph.

- If Pak Wiryo truly weren't offended, the dig and the secret check would have no reason.
- Pak Wiryo doesn't refuse to check. The check happens, just not in front of Darto, and nothing is shown openly.
- Darto's reading turns out right, not wrong.
- Darto going up on deck is the moment Pak Wiryo waits for, not the cause of the offence.

So the best reading is that Pak Wiryo is offended but doesn't admit it, as the dig about young people and the secret check of the cylinder show.

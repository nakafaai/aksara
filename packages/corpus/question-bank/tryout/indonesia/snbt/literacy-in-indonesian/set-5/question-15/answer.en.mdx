# Given

**Text A** defends the daily cap on three grounds that all depend on **daily climber volume**: ecosystem protection, rescue safety, and long-term local livelihoods. **Text B** proposes reserving one-quarter of daily slots for groups hiring registered village guides and porters while keeping "the daily climber count fixed at <InlineMath math="400" />."

# Asked

The response Text A's author is most likely to give to Text B's one-quarter reservation proposal.

# Solution

Check whether Text B's proposal **violates any of Text A's three pillars**: daily volume stays unchanged so ecology and safety remain intact, while local livelihoods actually benefit.

> Text A's author has no reason to reject the proposal because it adds zero daily climbers and leaves both ecological and safety protections untouched.

## Checking the five candidate responses

Testing **each response against Text A's three stated concerns** eliminates all four distractors.

| Candidate response | Fit with Text A | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Has no reason to object since daily climbers do not increase, leaving ecology and safety intact | Aligns with all three pillars of Text A | **Correct** |
| Rejects it for adding extra strain to the mountain | False, daily cap stays at <InlineMath math="400" /> | **Incorrect** |
| Rejects it because local income does not matter | False, Text A says the cap protects local livelihoods | **Incorrect** |
| Suggests raising the daily cap | Contradicts the entire thrust of Text A | **Incorrect** |
| Treats it as identical to shifting climbers to weekdays | False, reserving slots for village porters is a different policy | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

Text A's author would most likely **have no reason to reject the proposal, because it does not increase daily climber numbers and therefore leaves Text A's ecological and safety grounds untouched**.

# Given

The story of **Pak Wiryo** scatters clues about the letter in his pocket: it holds "words of thanks, years of service, a date" four days away, the Chief Engineer mentions "a small office gathering next week," spare parts are "someone else's business now," and Pak Wiryo claims he has not had time to finish reading it even though he has memorized the first paragraph and folds it "for tomorrow."

# Asked

The strongest conclusion about the letter's contents and Pak Wiryo's attitude toward it.

# Solution

Join the **content clues** (thanks, years of service, date four days away, office farewell) with the **attitude clues** (memorized the opening yet keeps putting off reading the end).

> The letter notifies Pak Wiryo that his service ends in four days, and he avoids finishing it because he is not ready to accept retiring.

## Checking the five conclusions against all clues

Matching **both letter content and Pak Wiryo's behavior** isolates the only complete reading.

| Conclusion | Matches letter clues | Matches attitude clues | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| His service ends in four days and he is unwilling to accept it yet | Yes (thanks, service length, date in four days) | Yes (memorized opening yet avoids the ending) | **Correct** |
| A reprimand over engine logs | Clashes with "words of thanks" and "years of service" | Does not fit | **Incorrect** |
| A transfer order to another ship | Fails to explain "years of service" and office event | Incomplete | **Incorrect** |
| An award he proudly treasures | Would not be left unfinished for three weeks | Contradicts text | **Incorrect** |
| An invitation to next week's event | Event mentioned by Chief Engineer, letter date is in four days | Inaccurate | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The strongest conclusion is that **the letter informs Pak Wiryo that his service ends in four days, and he is not yet ready to accept that reality**.

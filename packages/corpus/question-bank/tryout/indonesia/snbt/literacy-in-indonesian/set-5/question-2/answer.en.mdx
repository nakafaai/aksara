# Given

Paragraphs <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, and <InlineMath math="4" /> **discuss the Sunda** and Sahul shelves, Wallacea, and how deep sea straits separated island fauna.

# Asked

Which statements follow accurately from the text's explanation of continental shelves and fauna.

# Solution

> Picture land bridges during low sea levels, islands split by deep trenches like Sulawesi and Lombok stayed cut off even then.

You need to separate statements that truly follow from the text from statements that only sound right. The material is in paragraphs 1, 2 and 4.

The statement about Sulawesi and Kalimantan can't be concluded. Paragraph 1 puts the Wallace line "di antara Kalimantan dan Sulawesi" (between Kalimantan and Sulawesi). Paragraph 2 names Kalimantan as part of the Sunda Shelf, while Sulawesi is among the islands that "tidak pernah tersambung dengan daratan Asia maupun Australia" (were never joined to mainland Asia or Australia). Since Kalimantan was one with mainland Asia, Sulawesi was never joined to Kalimantan either.

The statement about the tiger can be concluded. Paragraph 2 describes the Sunda Shelf as shallow seabed linking "Sumatra, Jawa, Kalimantan, dan Bali", and in glacial times that seabed "berubah menjadi daratan luas" (turned into a wide stretch of land), so land animals could walk. The land route from Sumatra to Bali was open.

The statement about the anoa and the babirusa can't be concluded. Paragraph 4 mentions that Sulawesi is wet and thickly forested **like Kalimantan** precisely to show that climate doesn't explain the difference. Kalimantan has wet forest too, but no anoa. The cause the writer chooses is "keterasingan panjang di balik laut dalam" (long isolation behind deep sea).

The statement about a dried-out Lombok Strait can be concluded. Paragraph 2 explains that the Lombok Strait separates the faunas because its floor is "terlalu dalam untuk ikut mengering" (too deep to dry out as well). If its floor had dried, land mammals could have walked from Bali to Lombok, as they did from Java to Bali, so the difference would probably be far smaller.

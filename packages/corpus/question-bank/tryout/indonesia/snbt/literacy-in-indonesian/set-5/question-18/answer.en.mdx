The agency's guess is a cause-and-effect claim: the housing estate is the cause, the worse water at T5 is the effect. The table only shows the effect, the BOD rising from <InlineMath math="4.0" /> mg/L at T4 to <InlineMath math="6.5" /> mg/L at T5 in 2024. It doesn't show **where** along that stretch of <InlineMath math="31-23=8" /> km the rise happens.

There's more than one thing on that stretch: the housing estate and the rice fields. To test the guess about the estate, you have to separate the estate from everything else. The way to do that is to measure the river water **just before and just after** the estate's drain, on the same day. If BOD jumps between those two points, the jump can only come from that drain. If there's no jump, the cause has to be looked for elsewhere. The text itself says this measurement has never been done.

The other data don't separate the estate from the rest.

- Plans for more houses are about the future. They don't test what has already happened.
- A rainy-season measurement is still taken at T5, so it still mixes every source on the stretch. A different season also adds one more thing that changes.
- The BOD of water leaving the treatment plant concerns the stretch from T2 to T3, far upstream of the estate.
- The area of rice fields in 2024 concerns the other source. At most it could rule the fields out, but it doesn't show what comes out of the estate.

So the data most needed is the BOD of the river water just upstream and just downstream of the estate's drain on the same day.

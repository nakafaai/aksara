A difference in views lies in the texts' **conclusions**, not in facts both accept. Text 1 concludes that **tax incentives for buyers of electric cars should be kept**. Text 2 argues that if funds are limited, **public transport and cleaner power plants** should come before subsidies for private cars. They disagree about where incentive money should go.

- Text 1 admits battery-making emissions and Text 2 does not dispute them, so this is not a point of disagreement.
- Text 1 says electric motors are more efficient, and Text 2 does not discuss it.
- Text 2 grants that electric cars have an advantage, though small, so neither text says emissions are exactly equal.
- Whether clean electricity will ever be available is not debated in either text.

So the most basic difference lies in their judgement of where incentive money should go.

# Given

In paragraph three of the Wallace Line passage, the author notes that calling Wallacea a **"transition zone"** fits organisms that cross water easily (birds, bats, flying insects, seeds), yet adds "Even so" that anyone comparing **land mammals** of Bali and Lombok will find it hard to call the shift gradual.

# Asked

The author's stance toward viewing Wallacea as a transition zone rather than a single dividing line.

# Solution

Combine **both halves of paragraph three** to capture the author's **qualified acceptance** split by organism group.

> The author accepts the transition-zone label for species that cross water easily, while keeping the Lombok Strait as a sharp divide for land mammals.

## Checking the five stance summaries

Matching **how each option splits crossers versus land mammals** isolates the exact authorial position.

| Summary of stance | Fit with paragraph three | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accepts transition zone for easy water-crossers while keeping Lombok Strait sharp for land mammals | Captures both halves of paragraph three accurately | **Correct** |
| Rejects it because Wallace's birds shifted sharply | Wrong, paragraph three puts birds among frequent crossers | **Incorrect** |
| Accepts it fully because of Weber's Line | Overreaches, ignores the sharp land-mammal boundary | **Incorrect** |
| Accepts it only for land mammals | Reverses paragraph three completely | **Incorrect** |
| Treats it as undecidable due to human-brought animals | Mixes paragraph five into a different question | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The author **accepts the transition-zone view for organisms that cross water easily, yet still regards the Lombok Strait as a sharp boundary for land mammals**.

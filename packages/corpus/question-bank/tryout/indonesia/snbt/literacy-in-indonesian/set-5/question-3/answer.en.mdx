This question asks about the writer's attitude to a view the writer reports: that Wallacea is better called a "zona peralihan" (transition zone). That attitude isn't written in one sentence, so you need to join several parts of paragraph 3.

First, the writer explains that deep sea works "seperti saringan" (like a sieve). Birds, bats, flying insects and plant seeds cross more often. Second, the writer says the transition-zone view "sejalan dengan pola penyeberangan tadi" (fits that pattern of crossing). For living things that cross easily, the writer agrees the change is gradual. Third, the writer closes with "Meski begitu" (even so): anyone comparing the land mammals of Bali and Lombok "akan sulit menyebut perubahannya berangsur" (would find it hard to call the change gradual).

So the attitude is to **accept it in part**. The view holds for living things that cross easily, but not for land mammals.

The wrong options:

- Rejecting it because the birds Wallace recorded already change sharply uses a detail from paragraph 1. But in paragraph 3 the writer places birds in the group that crosses most often.
- Accepting it fully because the Weber line exists goes too far. The Weber line is only an example of another proposed line, and the writer still calls the change in land mammals sharp.
- Accepting it only for land mammals turns things around. It's for land mammals that the writer rejects the word "berangsur" (gradual).
- Calling it impossible to judge because some animals were brought by people mixes paragraph 5 into another question. Animals brought by people are discussed to explain species found on both sides.

The writer's attitude: the transition-zone view is accepted for living things that cross the sea easily, but the Lombok Strait is still seen as a sharp boundary for land mammals.

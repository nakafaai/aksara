# Given

Paragraph five warns that **keroncong asli** must be read carefully: it denotes a song form with a **standardized bar count and chord sequence** that hardened in the **twentieth century** ("following the canon," not "oldest"), while Tugu community music actually sounds different from keroncong asli.

# Asked

The most accurate conclusion about a group announcing that every song in its concert uses the keroncong asli form.

# Solution

Apply **paragraph five's technical definition** strictly to the ensemble instead of assuming everyday meanings of *asli* ("oldest" or "from Tugu").

> The group follows the twentieth-century canonical bar and chord pattern, and its sound is not necessarily close to Tugu music.

## Checking the five conclusions against paragraph five

Matching **each option to the passage's definition** eliminates every trap built on everyday usage of *asli*.

| Conclusion | Fit with paragraph five | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Follows the canonical form standardized in the twentieth century, and may not sound like Tugu music | Matches all three details in paragraph five | **Correct** |
| Performs the oldest form of keroncong | False, Tugu music is older and differs from keroncong asli | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The best conclusion is that **the group follows a canonical pattern standardized in the twentieth century, and its music is not necessarily similar to Tugu music**.

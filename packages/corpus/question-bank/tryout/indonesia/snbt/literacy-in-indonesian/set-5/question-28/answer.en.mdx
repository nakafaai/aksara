The word _semu_ means looking like the real thing without being it. So the example you want needs two features: it **looks like a growth mindset**, yet it's **missing the core**.

The fourth paragraph gives both features.

- What it looks like: the teacher is "memuji usaha" (praising effort).
- What's missing: the core of the idea is "usaha yang disertai strategi baru dan bantuan ketika jalan pertama buntu" (effort that comes with new strategies and help when the first way is blocked). A student praised for trying "padahal caranya keliru" (though the method is wrong) only learns that results don't matter.

Now look at the teacher who tells a student they've tried hard, when that student has repeated the same method <InlineMath math="3" /> times and is still wrong. The teacher praises effort, so the first feature is there. The student's method is wrong and unchanged, the teacher offers no new strategy, and the teacher walks away without helping. The core is gone, so the second feature is there too.

The other actions don't have both features at once.

- Asking a student to try another way and showing an example strategy is the real growth mindset. Nothing false about it.
- The praise "Kamu memang pintar" (you really are clever) is the closest trap. It praises intelligence as something inborn, so it fits the first view in the first paragraph. It doesn't resemble a growth mindset at all, so it can't be called an imitation of one.
- Explaining how the brain works is the content of the intervention in the national study, not a mistaken way of applying it.
- Extra lessons are real help, and they have nothing to do with praise.

So the example of a false growth mindset is the teacher who comforts a student by praising effort while the student keeps repeating a wrong method, then leaves without offering a new strategy.

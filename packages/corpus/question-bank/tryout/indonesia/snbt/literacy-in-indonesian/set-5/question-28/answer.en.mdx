# Given

In paragraph four, Dweck defines **false growth mindset** as teachers merely **praising effort** even when a student's method is wrong, without offering **new strategies and help** when the first path stalls, teaching students that results do not matter.

# Asked

The teacher action that best exemplifies what Dweck calls false growth mindset.

# Solution

Look for the example showing **both markers together**: surface resemblance to growth mindset (praising effort) combined with missing substance (no new strategy or guidance while the student repeats a wrong method).

> Comforting a student by praising their effort after three repeats of the same wrong method and walking away without a new strategy is textbook false growth mindset.

## Testing all five teacher actions against the criteria

Comparing **surface effort-praise and missing strategy** separates false growth mindset from real growth mindset and fixed mindset.

| Teacher action | Praises effort | Lacks new strategy and guidance | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Comforts a student repeating the same wrong method <InlineMath math="3" /> times by praising effort and leaving without a new strategy | Yes | Yes | **Correct (False growth mindset)** |
| Asks the student to try another method and models a strategy | Yes | No (genuine growth mindset) | **Incorrect** |
| Praises "You are so smart" | No (fixed-mindset praise of innate talent) | - | **Incorrect** |
| Explains how the brain builds new connections | Standard intervention lesson, not misuse | - | **Incorrect** |
| Provides extra tutoring hours | Real help, not empty praise | - | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The clearest example is **the teacher who comforts a student by praising their effort even though the student keeps repeating the same wrong method, and then leaves without offering a new strategy**.

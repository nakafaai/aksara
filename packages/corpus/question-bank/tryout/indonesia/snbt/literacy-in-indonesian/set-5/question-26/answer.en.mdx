The writer's position isn't in any one sentence. The writer weighs two sides through the passage and only decides in the last paragraph. A good summary has to hold **both sides and the verdict**.

First side: the benefit is small and uneven. Lower-achieving students' grades rise "sekitar <InlineMath math="0.1" /> poin" (about <InlineMath math="0.1" /> point), while for higher-achieving students they "hampir tidak berubah" (hardly change). The benefit is also thought to be larger for students from low-income families, and it really is larger in schools with a supportive climate.

Second side: the cost is very low. The intervention is "murah dan singkat" (cheap and short), under <InlineMath math="1" /> hour.

The verdict is in the closing sentence. The measure is "tetap layak dilakukan, asalkan tidak dijadikan pengganti perbaikan yang lebih mahal" (still worth doing, as long as it isn't made a replacement for costlier improvements). The word "asalkan" (as long as) places it as a **supplement**. A summary with small benefit, uneven, cheap and supplement fits all three.

The other statements take one of the conclusions the writer rejects, or turn the passage around.

- Dropping it because the effect is small is the second conclusion in the last paragraph. The writer rejects it because it "mengabaikan biaya" (ignores cost).
- Praising effort is called "pola pikir bertumbuh yang semu" (a false growth mindset) in the fourth paragraph. That's a mistaken way of applying it, not a condition for success.
- Higher-achieving students are hardly helped at all. The ones helped are lower-achieving students.
- Lifting every student's achievement is the first conclusion in the last paragraph, and the writer says "Data tidak mendukungnya" (the data doesn't support it).

So the writer's position is that growth mindset interventions give a small, uneven benefit, but are still worth using as a supplement because they're cheap.

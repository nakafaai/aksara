# Given

In the growth-mindset passage, the author weighs **two sides** before reaching a final verdict: effects are **small and uneven** (roughly <InlineMath math="0.1" /> points for lower-achieving students and nearly zero for high achievers), yet the intervention is **cheap and brief** (under <InlineMath math="1" /> hour), making it "worth doing as long as it does not replace costlier reforms."

# Asked

The statement that best summarizes the author's stance on growth-mindset interventions.

# Solution

Select the summary that captures **all three pillars of the author's verdict**: small uneven benefits, very low cost, and a complementary role rather than a substitute for major reforms.

> Growth-mindset interventions yield small, uneven benefits, yet remain worth using as a low-cost complement alongside broader improvements.

## Checking the five stance summaries

Comparing **each summary against the closing verdict** filters out both extreme conclusions the author rejects.

| Summary | Captures small/uneven effect, low cost, and complement role | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small and uneven benefits, yet worth using as a complement because it is cheap | Yes, all three elements | **Correct** |
| Should be abandoned because the effect is small | Extreme view rejected for ignoring low cost | **Incorrect** |
| Praising effort is the key requirement | False, praising effort alone is called "false growth mindset" | **Incorrect** |
| Helps high-achieving students the most | Reversed, high achievers showed almost no change | **Incorrect** |
| Lifts achievement across all students | Extreme view explicitly said to lack data support | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The author's stance is best summarized as: **growth-mindset interventions produce small and uneven benefits, yet remain worthwhile as a complement because they are inexpensive**.

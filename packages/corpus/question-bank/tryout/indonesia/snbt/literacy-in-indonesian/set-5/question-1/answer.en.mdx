This question asks you to carry two ideas from the text over to new islands: what makes a strait a barrier, and which living things are held back most.

The first idea is in paragraph 2. The writer rejects the "too wide" explanation and concludes that what separates the straits is **their depth**. A strait that dried out in glacial times became a road for land animals. A strait that stayed under water kept them apart. So the land mammals of K and L, whose strait never dried, should differ more than the land mammals of M and N, even though the strait between M and N is far wider.

The second idea is in paragraph 3: deep sea works "seperti saringan, bukan tembok" (like a sieve, not a wall). Birds, bats and plant seeds cross more easily, while land mammals almost only cross "secara kebetulan" (by chance). That's why the difference in plants between K and L tends to be smaller than the difference in their land mammals.

The wrong options:

- Saying M and N differ more because their strait is wider uses the "too wide" explanation, which the writer argues against.
- Saying K and L share not a single species treats deep sea as a wall. The writer calls it a sieve.
- Saying the difference is equally sharp for mammals, birds and plants forgets that each group has a different chance of crossing.
- Adding the condition that the climates of K and L differ a lot uses the climate guess the writer rejects in paragraph 4. The first part is right, but the condition isn't needed.

The guess that fits the text best: the land mammals of K and L differ more than those of M and N, and the plants of K and L differ less than their land mammals do.

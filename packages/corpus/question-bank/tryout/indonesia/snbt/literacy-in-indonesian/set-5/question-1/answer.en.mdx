# Given

In the **Wallace Line** passage, the author argues that **strait depth** (not width) drives separation because deep straits stayed flooded during glacial lows while shallow straits became land bridges, and deep water acts **as a filter rather than a wall** (plants and birds cross more easily than land mammals). Islands K and L sit across a narrow deep strait that stayed submerged, while M and N sit across a wide shallow strait that once dried out.

# Asked

The best-supported prediction about differences in land mammals and plants between K-L and M-N.

# Solution

Apply **both Wallace Line principles together**: deep submerged water divides K-L land mammals more sharply than M-N, while the filter effect keeps K-L plant differences smaller than land-mammal differences.

> Because the K-L strait never dried out, K and L land mammals diverge more sharply than M and N despite the narrower gap, yet plant seeds still cross that deep-water filter more easily.

## Checking the five predictions against passage logic

Evaluating **depth versus width and filter versus wall** rules out all four distractors.

| Prediction | Match with Wallace Line reasoning | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K-L land mammals differ more than M-N land mammals, and K-L plant differences are smaller than K-L mammal differences | Applies both depth and filter principles accurately | **Correct** |
| M and N differ more because their strait is wider | Relies on the "too wide" view refuted by the author | **Incorrect** |
| K and L share not a single species | Treats deep water as a wall rather than a filter | **Incorrect** |
| Differences are equally sharp across mammals, birds, and plants | Ignores differing dispersal abilities | **Incorrect** |
| Requires K and L to have very different climates | Adds the climate hypothesis rejected in paragraph four | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The best-supported prediction is that **land mammals on K and L differ more than those on M and N, and plant differences between K and L are smaller than their land-mammal differences**.

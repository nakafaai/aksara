The function of a passage within a story is judged by **its link to the main event**, which here is Pak Rasyid's decision to stamp Laila's ticket.

The story of his wife explains where his habit of pressing the stamp gently comes from: the last ticket he stamped for her ended in loss. With that background, the reader understands why stamping his daughter's ticket is so hard and why the pause and the firmer press at the end carry meaning. The episode builds the **old fear** he must face when he lets Laila go.

- The story does not explain why he became a ticket clerk. He had already worked for twenty-three years when the episode is mentioned.
- One loss does not mean every voyage is described as dangerous. Many others come back every Lebaran.
- His firmness with passengers is shown by the drunk young man, not by his wife's story.
- The warm ending gives no hint that Laila will share the fate of Pak Rasyid's wife. That option predicts the future without evidence.

So the episode shows that letting Laila go forces Pak Rasyid to face his old fear of loss.

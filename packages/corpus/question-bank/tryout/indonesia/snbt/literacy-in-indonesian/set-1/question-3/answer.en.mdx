The question asks for a share of the **excess** damage, not a share of all of group I's damage. Group III also loses surface, and that damage happens with no treatment at all. So the first step is to find the excess.

Group I's excess surface loss over group III:

<BlockMath math="0.8-0.3=0.5\text{ mm}" />

That excess has two parts. Groups I and II differ only by scrubbing, and groups II and III differ by the lost moss.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{scrubbing part}&=0.8-0.5=0.3\text{ mm}\\
\text{lost-moss part}&=0.5-0.3=0.2\text{ mm}
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="0.3+0.2=0.5" /> mm, the same as the excess. Now divide the scrubbing part by the excess.

<BlockMath math="\frac{0.3}{0.5}=60\%" />

The other options come from the wrong divisor or the wrong part.

- <InlineMath math="37.5\%" /> is <InlineMath math="0.3:0.8" />. It divides by all of group I's damage, but <InlineMath math="0.3" /> mm of that happens without any treatment.
- <InlineMath math="40\%" /> is <InlineMath math="0.2:0.5" />, the lost-moss share, not the scrubbing share.
- <InlineMath math="62.5\%" /> is <InlineMath math="0.5:0.8" />, the excess divided by all of group I's damage.
- <InlineMath math="75\%" /> is <InlineMath math="0.3:0.4" />. The <InlineMath math="0.4" /> isn't in the table. It comes from a wrong subtraction.

So <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of group I's excess surface loss comes from scrubbing.

# Given

According to the bystander-effect passage, the effect shows most strongly when **two conditions hold together**: many other people are present so responsibility diffuses, and **the danger is ambiguous**, since the 2011 meta-analysis found the effect weakens when danger is obvious.

# Asked

All situations in which the bystander effect is most likely to appear strongly.

# Solution

Test every scenario against **both criteria from the passage**: multiple witnesses and an unclear emergency.

> A strong bystander effect requires both diffused responsibility across a crowd and ambiguity about whether an emergency is occurring.

## Checking both conditions across all five situations

Evaluating **crowd presence and ambiguity** separates the two matching situations from the rest.

| Situation | Many others present | Ambiguous danger | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Person lying on a busy platform while commuters wonder if they are ill or asleep | Yes (busy platform) | Yes (unclear if ill or sleeping) | **Correct** |
| Weak man at a packed bus stop whom people assume is just tired | Yes (packed stop) | Yes (mistaken for fatigue) | **Correct** |
| Thick smoke inside a shopping mall | Yes | No, danger is unmistakable | **Incorrect** |
| Pedestrian alone on the street | No, alone | No diffusion of responsibility | **Incorrect** |
| Person shouting in a pool with one lifeguard | No, only one witness | No, danger is clear | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The bystander effect is most likely to appear strongly for **the person lying on a crowded station platform** and **the weak man sitting at a packed bus stop**.

# Given

**A lone bystander** helps with <InlineMath math="80" /> percent probability, while each person in a crowd decides independently with <InlineMath math="40" /> percent probability.

# Asked

The fewest people needed in a crowd so the probability that at least one person helps exceeds <InlineMath math="80" /> percent.

# Solution

> Instead of listing every way someone could help, flip the problem and find the chance that nobody helps at all.

The chance that "at least one person helps" is easiest to find through its opposite: **nobody helps**. Each person doesn't help with probability <InlineMath math="100\%-40\%=60\%" />. Since each person decides independently, the chance that nobody helps is the product of the individual chances.

<BlockMath math="P(\text{at least one helps})=1-(0.6)^n" />

Try each crowd size.

| People | Nobody helps | At least one helps |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="0.36" /> | <InlineMath math="64\%" /> |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="0.216" /> | <InlineMath math="78.4\%" /> |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="0.1296" /> | <InlineMath math="87.04\%" /> |

With <InlineMath math="3" /> people the chance is <InlineMath math="78.4\%" />, still below <InlineMath math="80\%" />. With <InlineMath math="4" /> people it's <InlineMath math="87.04\%" />, already above <InlineMath math="80\%" />. So at least <InlineMath math="4" /> people are needed.

The other options come from a wrong method.

- <InlineMath math="3" /> people comes from adding chances, <InlineMath math="40\%+40\%+40\%=120\%" />, or from reading <InlineMath math="78.4\%" /> as already past <InlineMath math="80\%" />. Chances can't be added, because the total can go over <InlineMath math="100\%" />.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> people does meet the condition, but the question asks for the **smallest** number.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> people comes from raising the chance of helping to the power, <InlineMath math="1-(0.4)^2=84\%" />. It's the chance of **not** helping that gets raised to the power.
- Each person's chance does fall, but that doesn't mean the chance that someone helps falls too. That's the author's point.

# Answer

So the crowd needs at least **<InlineMath math="4" />** people.

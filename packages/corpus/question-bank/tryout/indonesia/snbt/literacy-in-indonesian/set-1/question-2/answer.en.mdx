The key is the weakness of the first design described in paragraph three.

In the first design, the group that slept was tested in the morning and the group that stayed awake was tested in the evening. The author says this result "belum cukup untuk membuktikan peran tidur" (is not enough to prove the role of sleep) because the groups were tested at different times, and offers another possible cause: evening tiredness. The nap design tests both groups **at the same time of day**, so test time no longer differs between them. If the sleeping group still does better, the advantage is far more reasonably credited to sleep.

So the design's purpose is to rule out another explanation, namely the participants' condition at the time of the test.

- The number of word pairs is never named as a problem of the first design.
- The passage does not compare the amount of slow-wave sleep in a nap and in a night's sleep.
- Whether participants knew the hypothesis is not discussed.
- The design neutralises the effect of tiredness, but it does not prove that tiredness has no effect. The author treats tiredness as a real possibility.

By matching the test time, the researchers make sure the score gap does not come from how participants felt when they were tested.

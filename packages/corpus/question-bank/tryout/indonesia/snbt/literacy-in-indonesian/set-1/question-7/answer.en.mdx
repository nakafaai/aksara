Pak Darsa's attitude is hardly ever stated outright. Read it from his actions and from the comparison the narrator uses.

**Unimpressed by the explanation of the sensor: supported**. After Wulan explains that the sensor reads fog as darkness, Pak Darsa nods **"seperti orang yang baru diberi tahu bahwa air itu basah"** (like someone who has just been told that water is wet). The comparison shows the explanation didn't impress him.

**Passing on knowledge a sensor can't read: supported**. Pak Darsa explains what a copper-yellow western sky means for fishers, when the west wind comes and when the gulls fly low. The sensor only reads light and dark.

**Letting Wulan record his knowledge despite keeping his distance at first: supported**. On the morning she arrives, Pak Darsa walks ahead to the tower and says little. That evening, as Wulan types every word, **"Pak Darsa tidak menyuruhnya berhenti"** (Pak Darsa doesn't tell her to stop), and then he explains even more.

**Refusing to help out of anger: not supported**. The story shows no anger. He polishes the lens and writes the logbook as usual while Wulan works downstairs.

**The key can no longer open anything: not supported**. Pak Darsa hands the key over **"untuk pintu"** (for the door). It's the lamp that no longer needs a key, not the tower door.

So the story supports that Pak Darsa was unimpressed by the explanation of the sensor, that he passed on knowledge a sensor can't read, and that he let Wulan record his knowledge although he kept his distance at first.

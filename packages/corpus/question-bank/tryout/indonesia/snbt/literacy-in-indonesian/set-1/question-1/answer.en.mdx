# Given

**The temple wall** trial splits panels into three groups with combinations of scrubbing, biocide spray, and moss loss.

# Asked

Which two effects the trial design cannot separate.

# Solution

> You can only separate two causes when two groups match on everything else and differ in just one treatment.

Two effects can be separated only if **two groups differ in just that one thing**. First lay out each group's treatment.

| Group | Scrubbed | Sprayed with biocide | Moss lost |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| I | yes | yes | yes |
| II | no | yes | yes |
| III | no | no | no |

- **Groups I and II** differ only in scrubbing. So the effect of scrubbing **can** be separated.
- **Groups II and III** differ in **two things at once**: biocide and losing the moss. No group is sprayed while keeping its moss, and no group loses its moss without biocide. So the biocide's own effect on the stone can't be separated from the effect of losing the moss.

The other options are wrong.

- Scrubbing and biocide can be separated, because groups I and II are both sprayed and differ only in scrubbing.
- Scrubbing and losing the moss don't always come together. Group II loses its moss without being scrubbed.
- The panels were split **on each side of the wall** by lottery, so every group is on every side.
- The dead moss in group II is **left until it falls off by itself**, not removed straight away.

# Answer

So the two effects the design can't separate are **the biocide's effect on the stone and the effect of losing the moss, because every panel sprayed with biocide also loses its moss**.

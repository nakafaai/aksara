Notice the leap in Text 1. Its evidence comes from **a single monitor on Jalan Merdeka**, but its conclusion is about **the city's air**. Moving from one road to the whole city is valid only if the drop on Jalan Merdeka is not offset by a rise elsewhere. That is the unstated assumption, and it is exactly what Text 2 attacks when it describes traffic jams on Jalan Kenanga and Jalan Cempaka.

- Rising visitor numbers are not needed for a claim about air.
- Traders' support does not affect whether the air becomes cleaner.
- Cycling and walking both produce no exhaust, so the choice between them does not affect the claim.
- Text 1 compares Sunday mornings with Sunday mornings before the programme, so the monitor's accuracy on weekdays is irrelevant.

So Text 1's claim depends on the assumption that the pollution removed from Jalan Merdeka did not simply move to nearby roads.

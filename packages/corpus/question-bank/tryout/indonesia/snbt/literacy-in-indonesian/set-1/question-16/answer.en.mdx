A campaign can only reduce **avoidable** waste, so the plan must be judged against that column and against the reasons food is thrown away.

In the avoidable column, rice and flour foods rank first at <InlineMath math="820" /> grams. The interviews show the cause is portion sizes that follow habit, so portion planning does address it. Vegetables rank second at <InlineMath math="610" /> grams, but for a different reason: they **wilt before they are cooked**. Planning rice portions does nothing about how vegetables are bought or stored. A sound judgement credits the data's support and also names the plan's limit.

- Vegetables are largest in the total column, but that figure includes <InlineMath math="290" /> grams of unavoidable waste. For avoidable waste, rice is still largest.
- Rice makes up about a quarter of all waste, whether you count its <InlineMath math="860" /> grams or its <InlineMath math="820" /> avoidable grams, and that avoidable part is still the largest single amount that can be reduced.
- Not all rice waste is avoidable. <InlineMath math="40" /> grams are classed as unavoidable.
- Unavoidable waste such as fruit peel will not disappear through a campaign, so a fruit campaign would help less.

So the plan is backed by the data for rice, but it leaves out vegetables thrown away because they wilt.

Read the line together with its neighbours. Before it, the water "menipis" (thins) day after day under the sun. After it, what remains is "hanya putih / yang retak di telapak kakinya" (only white / cracking under his feet), which is salt. Water rising into the sky because of the sun's heat is **evaporation**. Blue stands figuratively for seawater, so "menyerahkan birunya kepada langit" (giving up its blue to the sky) means the seawater evaporates and leaves salt behind.

- A sky growing bluer reads the line literally and ignores the thinning water.
- The subject of the line is the water, not the father. His attitude appears only in the last stanza.
- Tides leave no salt in the pans, and the poem stresses the sun, not the movement of the sea.
- Water soaking into the ground does not go to the sky and leaves no cracking white.

So the line pictures seawater evaporating until only salt remains.

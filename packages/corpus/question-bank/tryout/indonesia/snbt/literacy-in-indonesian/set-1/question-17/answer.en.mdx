Attendance percentages can't simply be added up, because **each school has a different number of students**. So work out the absence days saved at each school, then add them.

At one school, a semester has (number of students) <InlineMath math="\times 100" /> student days. A rise of <InlineMath math="p" /> points in attendance means absences fall by <InlineMath math="p\%" /> of those student days.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Kalirejo}&=400\times100\times8\%=3{,}200\\
\text{Tegalsari}&=100\times100\times2\%=200\\
\text{Sumberan}&=250\times100\times8\%=2{,}000\\
\text{Watugede}&=250\times100\times2\%=500\\
\text{Total}&=5{,}900\text{ days}
\end{aligned}" />

Check with the combined attendance rate. Absences in 2023 were <InlineMath math="13{,}450" /> days out of <InlineMath math="100{,}000" /> student days, and in 2024 only <InlineMath math="7{,}550" /> days. The difference is <InlineMath math="5{,}900" /> days as well.

The other options come from dropping the weights or leaving out schools.

- <InlineMath math="700" /> days counts only Tegalsari and Watugede, <InlineMath math="200+500" />.
- <InlineMath math="4{,}900" /> days subtracts a <InlineMath math="1" />-point rise, like the other schools', on the trial schools' <InlineMath math="100{,}000" /> student days, <InlineMath math="5{,}900-1{,}000" />. That answers a question about the cause, but this question asks for the total change regardless of cause.
- <InlineMath math="5{,}000" /> days uses a plain average. The average of the four percentages rises from <InlineMath math="87\%" /> to <InlineMath math="92\%" />, and then <InlineMath math="5\%" /> is multiplied by <InlineMath math="100{,}000" />. That gives a school of <InlineMath math="100" /> students the same weight as a school of <InlineMath math="400" />.
- <InlineMath math="5{,}200" /> days counts only Kalirejo and Sumberan, <InlineMath math="3{,}200+2{,}000" />.

So absences at the four trial schools fell by <InlineMath math="5{,}900" /> days.

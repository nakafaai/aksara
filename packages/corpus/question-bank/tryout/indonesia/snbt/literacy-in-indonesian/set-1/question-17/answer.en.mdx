The research team's warning names two flaws in the measurement: households **are more careful because they know their waste is weighed**, and some food waste goes unrecorded. Both flaws belong to the measuring method. If waste is measured the same way before and after the campaign, the same flaws appear in both measurements, so **the difference between them** can more fairly be credited to the campaign.

The best approach is therefore to compare weighings before and after the campaign using the same method and the same observation period.

- Comparing a household survey with landfill weights mixes two different methods. The gap could come from the methods rather than the campaign.
- Asking respondents weighs nothing, and their answers can be swayed by the wish to look successful.
- Comparing rice with vegetables after the campaign shows no change from the earlier situation.
- Weighing only the most active households would inflate the result, because that group does not represent all households.

So the campaign is best judged by weighing before and after with the same method and the same observation period.

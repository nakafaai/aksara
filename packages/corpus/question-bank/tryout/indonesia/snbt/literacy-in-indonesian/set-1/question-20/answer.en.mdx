# Given

The **education chief** predicts free lunches across all <InlineMath math="40" /> primary schools will raise district attendance by nearly six points. However, the four trial schools were chosen for having the lowest attendance, the other <InlineMath math="36" /> schools are already at <InlineMath math="93\%" />, and Kalirejo and Sumberan gained a new rural bus route in July 2024.

# Asked

The statement that best explains the flaw in the education chief's projection.

# Solution

Extrapolating four low-attendance schools to the entire district fails for **two reasons at once**: the other schools have very little headroom left to climb six points, and the biggest gains overlap with the new transport route.

> Schools already at <InlineMath math="93\%" /> have little room to jump nearly six points, and the <InlineMath math="8" />-point gains in Kalirejo and Sumberan are confounded with the new rural bus route.

## Evaluating each proposed critique

Checking **each critique against the data** separates the complete diagnosis from false claims.

| Proposed critique | Match with passage facts | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trial schools had the lowest baseline, other schools are already at <InlineMath math="93\%" />, and gains are not separated from the new bus route | Captures both ceiling-effect and confounding flaws | **Correct** |
| Nearly six points is smaller than the gain at other schools | False, other schools rose only <InlineMath math="1" /> point | **Incorrect** |
| The chief miscalculated the nearly six-point rise | False, weighted attendance rose from <InlineMath math="86.55\%" /> to <InlineMath math="92.45\%" /> (<InlineMath math="5.9" /> points) | **Incorrect** |
| The program costs too much district-wide | Costs are never mentioned in the passage | **Incorrect** |
| The four trial schools were picked at random | False, they were selected for lowest prior attendance | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The flaw is best explained by the fact that **the trial schools were selected for having the lowest attendance while other schools already sit at <InlineMath math="93\%" /> with little room to gain six points, and the rise is still tangled with the new rural bus route**.

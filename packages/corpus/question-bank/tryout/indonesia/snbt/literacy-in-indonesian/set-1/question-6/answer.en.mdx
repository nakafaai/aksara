That sentence has to be linked to two other details in the story: **the thirty-one years** and **when the clockwork is removed**.

- **Thirty-one years**. The number matches the time his father handed him the tower key, **"Tiga puluh satu tahun lalu"** (thirty-one years earlier), when his father could no longer turn the crank. So turning the crank is a habit he inherited from his father on his very first night on watch.
- **When the clockwork is removed**. The story says **"besok jam pemutar lensa akan dicabut"** (the lens clockwork would be removed tomorrow). That night the clockwork was still there, and the crank could still be turned.

If the crank could still be turned and he didn't go up, then he **chose** not to. He let go of a habit handed down from his own father before the new machine actually forced him to stop. That night he sat explaining the wind, the gulls and the stairs to Wulan.

The other options are wrong.

- The clockwork comes out **tomorrow**, not that afternoon. This option misreads the order of events.
- The story never says he forgot. "Untuk pertama kalinya dalam tiga puluh satu tahun" (for the first time in thirty-one years) marks a deliberate change, not carelessness.
- The story never says Wulan turned the crank, and she only arrived that morning.
- Nothing shows he wants to make the new lamp fail. That night he shares what he knows with Wulan.

So the best-supported reading is that Pak Darsa chose to let go of the habit inherited from his father before the machine forced him to, because the clockwork was only coming out the next day.

Two things could **force** someone to stop turning the crank. The story even shows one of them in Pak Darsa's father.

- **The machinery is gone**. Once the clockwork is removed, the crank is useless.
- **The body can't manage it**. His father stopped because he was **"sudah batuk-batuk dan tidak sanggup lagi memutar engkol"** (coughing and no longer able to turn the crank).

The right pair has to rule out **both**.

- **"besok jam pemutar lensa akan dicabut"** (tomorrow the lens clockwork will be removed). That night the crank still works, so the machinery doesn't force him to stop.
- That afternoon he **climbs to the lamp room** and polishes the big lens. He can still manage the hundred and twelve stairs, so his body doesn't force him to stop either.

Each other pair rules out only one, or neither.

- Wulan typing on the phone says nothing about whether Pak Darsa could climb.
- Why his father stopped is about his father, not about the crank that night.
- The work schedule Wulan brings says nothing about the crank that night, and sitting downstairs is the event itself, not the reason.
- Handing over the key in the morning doesn't show the crank still worked that night.

So the pair that best shows it was his own choice is that the clockwork is only removed tomorrow, and that afternoon Pak Darsa still climbs to the lamp room to polish the lens.

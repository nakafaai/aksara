Set the name *Soekarno* aside first, since the question says personal names may keep the old spelling. What's left are **ordinary words**, and the only one that differs is *membatja* against *membaca*.

- **Document Q**. *membaca* uses *c*. According to the text, *tj* was only replaced by *c* in EYD in **1972**. So Q was written in 1972 or later.
- **Document P**. *membatja* uses *tj*. The letters *tj* were used in the 1901 spelling **and** kept by the 1947 Ejaan Republik. So P was written before 1972.

Can P be narrowed further? The 1901 spelling and the Ejaan Republik differ only in *oe* and the apostrophe for the glottal stop. P's ordinary words, *membatja koran pagi*, contain neither the sound /u/ nor a glottal stop. So it **can't be settled** whether P comes from before or after 1947.

The other options are wrong.

- The *oe* in *Soekarno* doesn't date anything, because personal names may keep the old spelling.
- *tj* was also used before 1947, so *membatja* doesn't prove P comes from the Ejaan Republik period.
- Q doesn't mix spellings. The *oe* is only in a personal name, and the text says names like that **were still written in official documents** decades after 1972.
- *tj* and *c* aren't a matter of habit. Each follows the official spelling of its time, and the question says both documents follow the official spelling.

So the best conclusion is that P was written before 1972, whether before or after 1947, while Q was written in 1972 or later.

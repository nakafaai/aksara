The answer is in the last two sentences of paragraph two: "Perubahan itu bukan sekadar soal gaya. Bahasa Jawa Kuno memiliki bunyi dan susunan kata yang tidak sama dengan bahasa Sanskerta sehingga juru tulis perlu menyesuaikan tanda-tanda yang mereka pakai." (The change was not merely a matter of style. Old Javanese had sounds and word structures unlike Sanskrit, so the scribes had to adapt the signs they used.) The word **sehingga** (so that, therefore) marks cause and effect: the difference between the languages is the reason for the change.

- No royal ban is ever mentioned.
- Rounder strokes are mentioned, but as a feature of the change, not its reason. The author stresses that the change was **not merely a matter of style**, and the passage never links stroke shape to palm leaves.
- The passage does not say that the priests and traders stopped coming.
- The spread to the Philippines came later and is presented as a result of Kawi's spread, not as a reason for the change.

So the scribes changed the Pallava script because Old Javanese had sounds and word structures different from Sanskrit.

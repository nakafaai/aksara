First pin down the **two errors** in how the 1964 story spread, then find the situation that has both.

- **The facts were exaggerated**. The newspaper said <InlineMath math="38" /> neighbours watched and did nothing. Later checking showed the figure was **"dibesar-besarkan"** (exaggerated): only a few really saw it, and at least one person called the police.
- **One case was taken as proof about everyone**. A single incident was used to conclude that **city people** had stopped caring.

The tourist report has both. The number of traders is blown up from <InlineMath math="3" /> to "puluhan" (dozens), and then that one incident is used to judge every trader in the tourist town.

The other options have only one of the errors, or none.

- The organiser inflates a number but draws no conclusion about a group from one case.
- The pickpocketing really happened. Only the conclusion about all bus stations is wrong, the facts aren't exaggerated.
- The lab result isn't exaggerated. The error is the leap from the lab to every crowd, and the author rejects exactly that conclusion.
- The recording is complete and the conclusion is only about the people in it. Nothing is exaggerated and nothing is generalised.

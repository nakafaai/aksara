# Given

The spread of the 1964 story went wrong in **two ways at once**: the witness count was exaggerated to <InlineMath math="38" /> passive neighbors, and a single incident was generalized as proof that all city dwellers had lost empathy.

# Asked

The situation that commits both of the same errors together.

# Solution

Look for the scenario that combines **factual exaggeration of an event** with **overgeneralizing one incident to an entire group**.

> The matching scenario must both inflate what actually happened and use that single case to judge an entire group of people.

## Checking both errors across every scenario

Testing **both error conditions** side by side singles out the exact parallel.

| Scenario | Facts exaggerated | One case generalizes to whole group | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| News of a tourist "swarmed by dozens of vendors" when there were only <InlineMath math="3" />, used to brand all vendors in the resort town unfriendly | Yes (<InlineMath math="3" /> inflated to dozens) | Yes (all vendors in town) | **Correct** |
| Organizers inflate crowd numbers without judging a group | Yes | No | **Incorrect** |
| Real pickpocketing incident generalized to all terminals | No | Yes | **Incorrect** |
| Accurate lab study stretched to every crowd | No | Yes | **Incorrect** |
| Unedited footage used only to describe people in that video | No | No | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The situation containing both errors is **the news report claiming a tourist was "swarmed by dozens of vendors" when only <InlineMath math="3" /> were there, used as proof that vendors across the tourist town are unfriendly**.

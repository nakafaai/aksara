An analogy question takes two steps: first pin down the error in the 1964 story, then find the situation with the same error.

**Step 1. The error**. Two things happen together.

- **A single incident** is taken as proof about all city people.
- That incident was itself **exaggerated in the reporting**. The text says the figure of <InlineMath math="38" /> witnesses was **"dibesar-besarkan"** (exaggerated), only a few really saw it, and someone called the police.

So the pattern is: one exaggerated case used to draw a general conclusion about a large group of people.

**Step 2. Match it**. The viral video of a motorbike-taxi driver has the same pattern: one incident, exaggerated by editing, then used to conclude that motorbike-taxi drivers across the city don't care about safety.

The other situations have different problems.

- The survey of <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> drivers uses lots of data, not one case. Its problem is drawing a cause from a link between two things, not exaggerating one incident.
- Two experiments with different results are findings that don't agree yet, not a conclusion from one mistaken case.
- Camera data about street quarrels is actually like the 2020 study the author uses to correct the popular picture.
- The mayor who rejects a survey because he dislikes the organisation is attacking the source, not generalising from one case.

So the closest situation is the single viral video, edited and exaggerated, then used as proof that the city's motorbike-taxi drivers no longer care about safety.

A good interpretation compares the stamp at the end with **Pak Rasyid's earlier habit**.

Since his wife left for treatment in the city and came home in a coffin, Pak Rasyid "selalu menekan stempel pelan-pelan, seakan-akan takut kertas itu terluka" (always pressed the stamp gently, as if afraid of hurting the paper). That habit grew from fear of letting go of the people he sent away. With Laila's ticket, he holds the stamp in the air for a while and then presses it harder. The pause shows hesitation, and the firmer press shows a decision made deliberately. His advice about a thick jacket and his hoarser voice confirm that he lets Laila go even though it hurts.

- The story shows no anger. His last words to her are caring.
- The worn, smooth stamp has been that way for years, so it cannot explain the change that morning.
- The queue is restless, but he holds the stamp longer instead of hurrying.
- Nothing suggests Laila will come back before Lebaran.

So the harder press marks Pak Rasyid's decision to let Laila go, painful as it is.

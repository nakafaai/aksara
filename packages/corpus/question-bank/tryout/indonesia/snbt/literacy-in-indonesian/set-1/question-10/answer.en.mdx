# Given

In the lighthouse story, **Pak Darsa** writes *copper-yellow western sky* in the rightmost logbook column never requested by the city office. Wulan's question about that column prompts him to explain tidal warnings for fishers and share his sea knowledge all night.

# Asked

All roles played by the "copper-yellow western sky" column scene in the plot.

# Solution

Evaluate the **narrative function of the logbook column** both for the kind of knowledge it reveals and for the shift it triggers between Pak Darsa and Wulan.

> The logbook column highlights local sea knowledge beyond any light sensor and marks the turning point where Pak Darsa shifts from distance to sharing.

## Evaluating each proposed plot role

Checking **every option against the scene's events** separates the two true roles from the distractors.

| Proposed role | Plot evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shows knowledge the light sensor cannot read | Warns fishers of reversing currents, while the sensor only reads dark and light | **Correct** |
| Marks a shift in attitude toward Wulan | Moves Pak Darsa from aloof silence to sharing winds, gulls, and stairs all night | **Correct** |
| Shows the logbook is obsolete | False, he still writes in it and the sign has "never been far off" | **Incorrect** |
| Proves sky signs beat the sensor in accuracy | False, they serve different jobs and are never ranked | **Incorrect** |
| Explains why the office replaced the keeper | False, the office's motives are never mentioned | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The logbook column scene **reveals sea knowledge that the new lamp's sensor cannot read** and **marks the moment Pak Darsa shifts from keeping his distance to sharing with Wulan**.

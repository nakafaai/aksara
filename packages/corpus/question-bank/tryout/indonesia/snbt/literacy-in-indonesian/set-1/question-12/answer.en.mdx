# Given

**Text B** cites SMA Pelita, a school two kilometers away with no phone ban whose average score also rose from <InlineMath math="70" /> to <InlineMath math="75" />, to argue that switching from essay exams to multiple-choice exams lifted scores at SMA Harapan Bangsa.

# Asked

The finding that, if true, would most weaken Text B's SMA Pelita comparison.

# Solution

Text B's comparison assumes that **the exam format change** drove SMA Pelita's <InlineMath math="5" />-point jump. That evidence weakens most if an **alternative local cause** explains SMA Pelita's rise in that exact year.

> If SMA Pelita began extra tutoring for all graduating students in that same year, its score jump can no longer isolate the effect of the exam format change.

## Checking the direction of each finding

Sorting each option by whether it **weakens, strengthens, or misses** Text B's comparison identifies the best counter-evidence.

| Finding | Effect on SMA Pelita evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMA Pelita started extra tutoring for all final-year students that same exam year | Introduces a competing cause unique to SMA Pelita | **Weakens most** |
| Other schools without bans also gained about <InlineMath math="5" /> points | Reinforces Text B's pattern across schools | **Strengthens** |
| Both schools share very similar surroundings | Makes Text B's comparison even fairer | **Strengthens** |
| Teachers at SMA Harapan Bangsa report calmer classes | Already in Text A and does not address SMA Pelita | **Irrelevant** |
| Students at SMA Pelita still bring phones to school | Only restates the premise of Text B's comparison | **Irrelevant** |

# Answer

The finding that most weakens Text B's evidence is that **SMA Pelita started holding extra tutoring for all final-year students in that same exam year**.

First understand **how Text B's evidence works**. SMA Pelita didn't ban phones, yet its scores also rose, from <InlineMath math="70" /> to <InlineMath math="75" />. Text B supposes that **the change of exam format** raised scores at both schools, and so concludes that most of the rise at SMA Harapan Bangsa may have nothing to do with phones.

That evidence weakens if the rise at SMA Pelita turns out to have **another cause**. Extra lessons for every final-year student in the same year are exactly that. The rise at SMA Pelita then no longer shows how much the exam format did, so it can't be used to explain the rise at SMA Harapan Bangsa.

Watch the direction of each finding.

- Other schools without a ban that also rose by about <InlineMath math="5" /> points actually **support** Text B. The rise seems to happen everywhere, ban or no ban.
- Similar backgrounds also **support** Text B, because they make the comparison fairer.
- Teachers' reports of calmer classes are already in Text A and don't touch SMA Pelita at all.
- SMA Pelita students still carrying phones only confirm that the school had no ban. That's part of Text B's evidence.

So the finding that most weakens the SMA Pelita evidence is that SMA Pelita started extra lessons for every final-year student in the same exam year.

Every option reads two things: the lamp being off and Pak Darsa's smile. Two options share the same first part, so the second part decides.

**The lamp being off**. The image only makes sense once you link it to his father's rule, **"Mercusuar tidak boleh tidur, karena laut tidak pernah tidur"** (A lighthouse must not sleep, because the sea never sleeps). Now the lamp is off in daylight because its sensor reads light, exactly how the new lamp works. The lighthouse may sleep because a machine has taken over the watch. The rule that once bound Pak Darsa **no longer applies**.

**The smile**. The story says he **"tidak tahu mengapa"** (doesn't know why) he smiles. So the smile is a sign of acceptance, but an acceptance he doesn't fully understand yet. The night before, he had already stopped going up to turn the crank.

The other options are wrong.

- The lamp is off in daylight because that's how it works, "Kalau terang, mati" (When it's light, it switches off). Nothing shows it's broken.
- The lamp being off isn't Wulan's fault, and the smile isn't marked as disappointment.
- The third option's first part is right, but its second part contradicts the text. He **doesn't know** why he smiles, so he hasn't fully understood his feelings.
- The story shows no anger at his father. The rule is remembered as the reason for his loyalty.

So the ending means that the rule "a lighthouse must not sleep" no longer binds him, and Pak Darsa's smile shows an acceptance he doesn't fully understand yet.

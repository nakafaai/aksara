The closing image only means something once you link it to his father's rule: **"Mercusuar tidak boleh tidur, karena laut tidak pernah tidur."** (A lighthouse must not sleep, because the sea never sleeps.) For Pak Darsa, a sleeping lighthouse used to mean neglect. Then check three things at the end of the story.

- **Why the lamp is off**. The tower stands **"di bawah matahari"** (under the sun), and Wulan has already explained how the new lamp works: **"Kalau terang, mati."** (When it's light, it goes off.) So the lamp is off because that's how it should be, not because it's broken or neglected.
- **Who guards the sea now**. The new lamp switches itself on whenever it gets dark. The sea is still guarded, just not by Pak Darsa. He no longer carries his father's rule.
- **Why he smiles**. The story says he **"tidak tahu mengapa ia tersenyum"** (doesn't know why he smiles). Any option that gives a sure reason for the smile goes beyond the text.

Three options share the same first part, so the second part decides.

- The new lamp hasn't failed. It's off in daylight because its sensor reads light.
- Nothing shows that Pak Darsa regrets anything. He smiles, and that morning he hands the key to Wulan himself.
- The second part of the third option gives a reason the text doesn't have. The story stresses that he doesn't know why he smiles.
- The sea still needs guarding, since it "tidak pernah tidur" (never sleeps). What changes is the guard, a lamp that switches itself on.

So the best-supported reading is that an image that once meant neglect now feels calm, because the lamp is off in daylight as it should be and Pak Darsa no longer carries his father's rule.

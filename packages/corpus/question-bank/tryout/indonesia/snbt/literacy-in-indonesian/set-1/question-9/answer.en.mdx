First, pin down the claim of paragraph three. The author says rankings **change how students see learning**: helping a classmate feels like hurting yourself, and students near the bottom feel their effort means nothing. Data that strengthen this claim must show different learning behaviour depending on whether rankings exist.

The data on students in ranking schools explaining material to classmates less often test that claim directly. The word "serupa" (similar) matters because it rules out another explanation, such as differences in school quality.

- University admission has nothing to do with how students view learning.
- Parents' preferences concern the taste of report-card readers, not student behaviour.
- Slightly higher exam marks may seem to support the essay in general, but they do not touch peer help or the feeling that effort is pointless.
- The time teachers need to write reports is a cost of the author's proposal, not support for paragraph three.

So the data that most strengthen paragraph three show that students in ranking schools explain material to classmates less often.

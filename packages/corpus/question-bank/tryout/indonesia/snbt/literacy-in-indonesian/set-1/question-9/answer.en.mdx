# Given

At the close of the story, **Pak Darsa** watches the white tower under the sun with its lamp off "like a sleeping lighthouse" and smiles without knowing why, echoing his father's old rule that a lighthouse must never sleep because the sea never sleeps.

# Asked

The interpretation of the closing image that is best supported by the whole story.

# Solution

Connect the final image to **his father's warning**, the solar sensor's daytime rule ("when it's bright, it turns off"), and the narrator's note that Pak Darsa himself does not know why he smiles.

> An image that once meant neglect now feels peaceful, because the lamp is off in daylight as designed and Pak Darsa no longer carries his father's burden.

## Testing each interpretation against the text

Inspecting **the second clause of each option** separates faithful readings from overclaims.

| Core interpretation | Fit with the passage | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What once meant neglect now feels calm because the lamp rests in daylight as designed and he no longer bears the duty | Matches "under the sun", "when it's bright, it turns off", and his handover | **Correct** |
| The new lamp has failed to turn on | Wrong, the sensor deliberately turns it off in daylight | **Incorrect** |
| Pak Darsa regrets giving up the tower | Unsupported, he smiles and hands over the key himself | **Incorrect** |
| States a definite reason for his smile | Goes beyond the text, which says he does not know why he smiles | **Incorrect** |
| The sea no longer needs watching | Wrong, the sea still "never sleeps", the automatic lamp now watches it | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The best-supported interpretation is that **the image that once signified neglect now feels calm, because the lamp is off in daylight as it should be and Pak Darsa no longer carries that duty**.

Paragraph three gives three rules: a consonant **already carries the vowel /a/**, small marks change that vowel, and a special mark silences the vowel. Apply all three to each statement.

| Statement | Answer | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A consonant without marks is read with /a/ | True | The passage says every consonant letter already carries the sound /a/, as in *ka*, *na*, or *ta*. |
| *Ki* uses a completely different letter from *ka* | False | The vowel is changed by adding a small mark to the same letter, not by using a new letter. |
| *Tan* needs a vowel-silencing mark | True | The final *n* must stand without a vowel, and the passage names a special mark for that. |
| Vowels are always separate letters beside the consonant | False | Vowel marks can sit above, below, or beside the letter, and they attach to the consonant. |

The third statement asks you to apply a rule rather than find a matching sentence. Without the silencing mark, the *n* would be read *na*, turning *tan* into *tana*.

# Given

**The text details** letter and punctuation changes across the 1947, 1972, 2015, and 2022 reforms, including alignment with Malaysia.

# Asked

Whether each statement about the scope and limits of the spelling reforms is true or false.

# Solution

> A single sweeping word like every or mainly can flip an almost-true claim into false, so check the exact scope of each reform.

These statements test whether you pair each reform with its exact content and limits. Watch limiting words such as *setiap* (every) and *terutama* (mainly).

The statement about the number of letters is true. In 1947 only two things changed: *oe* became *u*, and the apostrophe for a glottal stop at the end of a syllable became *k*. For 1972, the text lists six replacements: *tj*, *dj*, *j*, *nj*, *sj* and *ch*. The text also says the 1972 reform **"menyentuh lebih banyak huruf"** (touched more letters).

The statement about 2015 and 2022 is false. The text says both **"lebih banyak mengatur tata tulis"** (mostly regulated writing conventions), such as capitals, italics and punctuation, **"daripada mengganti huruf untuk bunyi"** (rather than replacing letters for sounds). The only added letters it names are the diphthong *ei*, and that isn't a consonant sound either.

The statement about the apostrophe is false because of the word **setiap** (every). The text only says the apostrophe for a glottal stop **"di akhir suku kata"** (at the end of a syllable) was replaced by *k*. This statement stretches that rule to every apostrophe.

The statement about Malaysia is false. A shared spelling with Malaysia was the goal of EYD in **1972**. The 1947 change was the Republic's own decision.

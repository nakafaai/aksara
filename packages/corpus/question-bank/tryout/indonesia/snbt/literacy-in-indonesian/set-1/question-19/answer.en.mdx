Text 2's objection is that cars are diverted to Jalan Kenanga and Jalan Cempaka while the monitor sits only on Jalan Merdeka, so **the pollution may simply have moved**. To test that objection, we need to know what happens on the diversion roads.

The right data are air-quality readings on Jalan Kenanga and Jalan Cempaka on Sunday mornings, **before and during** the programme. If pollution there rose by roughly the amount it fell on Jalan Merdeka, Text 2's objection holds. If it barely rose, Text 1's claim is stronger.

- Visitor numbers do not measure pollution at all.
- Weekday carbon monoxide is still measured on Jalan Merdeka, so it says nothing about the diversion roads.
- Street vendors' income concerns the economic objection, not the air objection.
- The number of bicycles shows how the space is used, not whether pollution moved.

So the data most needed are air-quality readings on the diversion roads before and during the programme.

# Given

**Control schools rose** <InlineMath math="1" /> point, lunch-only schools rose <InlineMath math="2" /> points, route-only schools rose <InlineMath math="3" /> points, and schools with both rose <InlineMath math="8" /> points.

# Asked

What conclusion best fits how the lunch programme and the new transport route interact.

# Solution

> Subtract the shared <InlineMath math="1" />-point background rise first so you can see whether combining both programmes beats the sum of each alone.

You now have four kinds of school. To compare them fairly, first take out the **<InlineMath math="1" /> point that happened at every school**, then look at what each treatment adds.

| Kind of school | Rise | Left after removing <InlineMath math="1" /> point |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| No programme, no route | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| Lunches only (Tegalsari, Watugede) | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| Route only (extra data) | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |
| Both (Kalirejo, Sumberan) | <InlineMath math="8" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> |

If the two effects simply added up, the schools with both should show about <InlineMath math="1+2=3" /> points left. They actually show <InlineMath math="7" /> points, **far more** than that sum. So lunches and the transport route seem to strengthen each other. For example, students who can now take the bus may be keener to come because there's lunch, or the other way round.

The other options are wrong.

- The route alone leaves <InlineMath math="2" /> points, far from <InlineMath math="7" />. It doesn't explain the whole rise.
- Schools with lunches only rose <InlineMath math="1" /> point above schools without the programme. The effect is small, but it isn't zero.
- The separate effects add up to <InlineMath math="3" /> points, while the schools with both show <InlineMath math="7" /> points left. They aren't equal. Even if you forget to take out the <InlineMath math="1" /> point, the sum is only <InlineMath math="2+3=5" />, still far from <InlineMath math="8" />.
- The effect of lunches alone, <InlineMath math="1" /> point, is smaller than the effect of the route alone, <InlineMath math="2" /> points.

# Answer

So the rise at schools with both is far larger than the sum of the separate rises, so lunches and the transport route seem to strengthen each other.

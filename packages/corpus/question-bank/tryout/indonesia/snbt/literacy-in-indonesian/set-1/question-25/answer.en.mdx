# Given

Responding to fears about the 1972 spelling reform, the author **concedes real reprinting costs** for books and signs, **rejects the feared loss of comprehension** because young readers still read *tjinta* as *cinta*, and explains that **only the written symbols changed while the words stayed the same**.

# Asked

The situation that most closely mirrors this three-part reasoning pattern.

# Solution

Break the author's response into its **three logical steps** and check which option keeps all three intact without dropping or reversing any step.

> The pattern concedes physical replacement costs, denies the feared reading barrier, and credits that survival to changing only symbols while keeping the words unchanged.

## Matching all three reasoning parts across options

Mapping **each option against the three criteria** highlights the exact structural match.

| Option scenario | Concedes cost | Rejects reading loss | Symbols change, words stay | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| City changes road-sign font at high cost, yet drivers still read old signs because the words stay the same | Yes | Yes | Yes | **Correct** |
| Drivers struggle and fears prove true | Yes | Reversed | No | **Incorrect** |
| Replacement carries zero cost | Missing | Yes | Yes | **Incorrect** |
| Street names themselves are also changed | Yes | No | Words change | **Incorrect** |
| Translating texts into everyday language | Unfocused | No | Words are replaced | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The closest match is **a city replacing the font on all road signs at great expense while drivers can still read older signs because the words remain the same**.

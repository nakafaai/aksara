An analogy question takes two steps: first pin down the **pattern** of the author's answer, then find the situation with the same pattern.

**Step 1. The author's pattern**. The author grants one thing and rejects another.

- There's a real **cost** for printed material: **"buku pelajaran dan papan nama harus diganti"** (textbooks and signs had to be replaced).
- But readers' understanding **isn't cut off**, because only the symbols changed, **"bukan bunyi itu sendiri, apalagi kata-katanya"** (not the sound itself, let alone the words).

So the pattern is: the written form changes, replacing things costs money, but what's written stays the same, so old writing is still understood.

**Step 2. Match it**. Road signs that only change their typeface follow the same pattern. Putting them up costs money, but the words don't change, so drivers still understand the old signs.

The other situations follow different patterns.

- Renamed streets change the **content**, not the typeface, and residents do struggle. That's like the worry the author rejects.
- Dropping cursive lessons cuts off the ability to read old handwriting. That's also like the worry, not the author's answer.
- Reprinting with new covers doesn't change the writing at all, and no reader is at risk of being cut off.
- Unscanned archives become hard to find, so access to old documents really is cut off.

So the closest situation is the city that changes the typeface on its road signs: the cost is high, but drivers still understand the old signs because the words don't change.

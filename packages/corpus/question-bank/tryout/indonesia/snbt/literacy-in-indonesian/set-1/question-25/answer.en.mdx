Break the author's reply down first. The pattern has **three parts**.

- **Grant the cost**. The worry **"ada benarnya untuk urusan cetak-mencetak"** (has some truth where printing is concerned), since textbooks and signs had to be replaced.
- **Deny the feared loss**. Young readers **can still read** *tjinta* as *cinta*.
- **The reason**. Only the **symbols for the sounds** changed, while the sounds and the words stayed.

Signs whose lettering is replaced at great cost but stay readable because the words are the same have all three parts.

The other options drop a part or flip it.

- Drivers taking a long time to adjust and the worry coming true flip the second part. The author rejects that worry.
- A change that costs nothing removes the first part. The author grants the cost.
- If the street names change too, the words **aren't the same any more**. The reason in the third part no longer holds.
- Translating into everyday language changes the **words**, not just the symbols. In the 1972 reform the words stayed the same.

So the most similar situation is the city that replaces the lettering on every sign at great cost, while drivers can still read the old signs because the words are the same.

# Given

**The text and** both tables list student counts, August and October averages, and the teacher's caution about motivation.

# Asked

Which statements follow from the table calculations and the teacher's cautions in the text.

# Solution

> The larger group pulls the combined average closer to its own score, and matching starting scores still never guarantees equal motivation.

Three statements need a calculation, the other two need spotting a word that's too strong.

The statement about the August average can be concluded. The overall average is <InlineMath math="57.8" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{120 \times 62 + 180 \times 55}{300} = 57.8" />

That's <InlineMath math="2.8" /> from <InlineMath math="55" /> but <InlineMath math="4.2" /> from <InlineMath math="62" />.

The statement about double can't be concluded. Tutored students rose <InlineMath math="11" /> points and the others <InlineMath math="9" />, far from double.

The statement about half can be concluded. The second table has <InlineMath math="50+40=90" /> students, and <InlineMath math="50" /> were tutored, more than <InlineMath math="45" />.

The statement that tutoring had no effect at all can't be concluded. The teacher concludes tutoring **"mungkin membantu"** (may help), only far less than the brochure promises.

The statement about motivation can't be concluded. The teacher warns that students with the same starting score are **"belum tentu sama dalam hal lain, seperti semangat belajar"** (not necessarily alike in other ways, such as motivation).

# Answer

**The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The August average is closer to the untutored group | Ya |
| The tutored rise is double | Tidak |
| More than half the second table were tutored | Ya |
| Tutoring had no effect at all | Tidak |
| Same starting score means same motivation | Tidak |

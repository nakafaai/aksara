# Given

In the old-letters story, **Pak Sastro** twice picks a longer translation: "never talked much" instead of "quiet" (which sounds like a fixed trait rather than a patient complaint), and "Mas can now buy his own books" instead of "Mas is now successful" (since Bu Ratmi never wrote "successful" and knew his struggle borrowing books).

# Asked

What both of Pak Sastro's translation choices have in common.

# Solution

Compare **Pak Sastro's reasoning in both moments**: both times he turns down a tidy generic label in order to stay faithful to **Bu Ratmi's own way of seeing her husband**.

> Both times, Pak Sastro rejects a concise generic word in favor of phrasing that preserves how Bu Ratmi viewed her husband.

## Checking the five candidate commonalities

| Candidate commonality | Fits both translation choices | Verdict |

| Rejects concise generic words for phrasing that preserves how Bu Ratmi saw her husband | Matches both "more faithful" and "your grandmother knew that" | **Correct** |
| Avoids offending Dita out of politeness | Never mentioned in the story | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

Both choices share that **Pak Sastro rejects concise, generic words in favor of phrasing that preserves how Bu Ratmi viewed her husband**.

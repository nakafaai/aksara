# Given

IRRI simulations estimate a **C4 rice** yield boost of <InlineMath math="32\%" /> at current atmospheric <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" />, dropping to <InlineMath math="21\%" /> at higher <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" />. The C4 pump's advantage is concentrating <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> around **Rubisco** so photorespiration nearly disappears.

# Asked

Why the smaller C4 yield gain under higher atmospheric <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> makes sense from the passage's explanation of Rubisco.

# Solution

Check how elevated <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> helps **ordinary C3 rice**: a higher <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" />-to-oxygen ratio makes ordinary rice's Rubisco bind oxygen less often, shrinking the penalty the C4 pump fixes.

> Under higher atmospheric <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" />, Rubisco in ordinary rice binds oxygen less often, so the extra advantage of a C4 pump narrows.

## Checking the five explanations against Rubisco behavior

Testing **gas-ratio logic** rules out all four unsupported or inverted options.

| Explanation | Fit with Rubisco explanation | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rubisco in ordinary rice binds oxygen less often, shrinking the C4 pump's relative advantage | Follows directly from the gas ratio | **Correct** |
| The C4 pump shuts down at high <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> | False, the pump still moves <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> | **Incorrect** |
| C4 rice loses more water at high <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> | Never stated in the passage | **Incorrect** |
| Rubisco binds oxygen more often when <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> rises | Reverses the actual direction | **Incorrect** |
| Higher <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> changes leaf temperature and eliminates photorespiration | Unmentioned and overstates zero loss | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The difference makes sense because **at higher <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> levels, Rubisco in ordinary rice binds oxygen less frequently, so the extra advantage of the C4 pump grows smaller**.

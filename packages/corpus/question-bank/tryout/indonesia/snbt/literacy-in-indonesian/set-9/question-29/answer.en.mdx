# Given

An official concludes old houses were built sturdier than modern ones because surviving old houses rarely crack. The survivorship-bias passage recommends asking one key question: **"who or what never made it into the record, and why,"** including fragile old buildings that collapsed long ago.

# Asked

All questions that follow the passage's recommended method to test the official's conclusion.

# Solution

Pick the questions that **track down old houses missing from today's sample** (collapsed or demolished because they were fragile), skipping questions about new homes, material prices, or fresh paint.

> Asking about same-era houses that already collapsed and fragile old houses demolished before inspection directly uncovers the missing data.

## Checking the five questions against the passage method

Testing **which questions search for missing non-survivors** selects the two valid answers.

| Question | Searches for old houses missing from today's record | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How many houses from that era have already collapsed | Yes (finds buildings lost before inspection) | **Matches** |
| Whether fragile old houses were demolished earlier | Yes (asks why they never entered the record) | **Matches** |
| How many new houses are built today | Says nothing about vanished old houses | **Does not match** |
| How building-material prices compare | Misses the survival filter | **Does not match** |
| How often old houses are repainted | Only concerns appearance of surviving houses | **Does not match** |

# Answer

The matching questions ask about **same-era houses that have already collapsed** and **fragile old houses that were already demolished before inspection**.

# Given

**Text A and** Text B debate clearing street vendors from pavements, moving them to Pasar Sentra, stall rent, and vendors returning to the street.

# Asked

Which statements can be concluded about what both texts agree on and dispute.

# Solution

> Text B shares the goal of walkable pavements, yet stresses that foot-traffic location matters more to vendors' survival than a shiny building.

Watch what Text B agrees with and what it questions.

The statement that both writers agree about the pavement can be concluded. Text A says **"Trotoar adalah hak pejalan kaki"** (The pavement belongs to pedestrians), and Text B opens with **"Saya setuju bahwa trotoar harus dapat dilalui pejalan kaki dengan aman"** (I agree that pedestrians must be able to use the pavement safely).

The statement that location matters more than the building's quality can be concluded. Pasar Baru's **"bangunannya juga baru dan rapi"** (building was also new and tidy), yet income still fell, and Text B concludes, **"Bangunan yang bagus tidak berguna jika letaknya jauh dari arus orang"** (A good building is useless if it's far from where people pass).

The statement that vendors must pay rent from the first month can't be concluded. Text A says the stall is **"gratis selama enam bulan pertama"** (free for the first six months).

The statement that more than half the vendors returned to the street can be concluded. <InlineMath math="38" /> of <InlineMath math="60" /> returned, and half of <InlineMath math="60" /> is <InlineMath math="30" />.

The statement that Text B rejects any kind of arrangement can't be concluded. Text B proposes other ways: selling at set hours with <InlineMath math="1.5" /> metres kept clear, or moving the bus stop.

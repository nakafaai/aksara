# Given

**Text B responds** to Text A's points on pedestrian accidents, the <InlineMath math="400" />-metre distance to Pasar Sentra, and walkway space.

# Asked

Which statements match how Text B engages with Text A's goals and numbers.

# Solution

> Both writers can cite the exact same <InlineMath math="400" />-metre figure for opposite points, namely close on a map in Text A versus far from buyers in Text B.

Two statements need the two texts' numbers linked, the rest need a careful read of Text B's stance.

The statement about the goal can be concluded. Text B writes, **"Saya setuju bahwa trotoar harus dapat dilalui pejalan kaki dengan aman"** (I agree the pavement must be safely passable for pedestrians).

The statement that relocation must fail can't be concluded. Text B gives a condition: if traders are moved, the bus stop can be moved **"agar arus orang ikut bergeser ke sana"** (so the flow of people shifts there too).

The statement about <InlineMath math="400" /> metres can be concluded. Text A uses it to show Pasar Sentra is close. Text B uses it to show buyers **"tidak akan berjalan"** (won't walk) that far.

The statement that Text B doubts the accident figure can't be concluded. Text B doesn't discuss it.

The statement about pedestrian space can be concluded. Now pedestrians get **"hanya kebagian sekitar"** (only about) <InlineMath math="1" /> metre. Text B's idea keeps <InlineMath math="1.5" /> metres clear, and Jalan Merdeka's <InlineMath math="3" />-metre pavement meets its width condition.

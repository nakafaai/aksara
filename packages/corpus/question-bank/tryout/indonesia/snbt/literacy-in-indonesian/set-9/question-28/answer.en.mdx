# Given

A student reads several stories of successful college dropouts and concludes dropping out boosts entrepreneurial odds. Under **survivorship bias**, popular stories spotlight survivors while **"people who drop out and fail are rarely interviewed,"** so we must ask about the **"missing data."**

# Asked

The main flaw in the student's reasoning and the data needed to test the claim according to the passage.

# Solution

Pair **survivorship bias (sampling only winners)** with **complete denominator data** comparing the success rate among all dropouts (including failures) against the success rate among graduates.

> The main flaw is looking only at success stories, and testing it requires comparing the share who succeed among all dropouts with the share who succeed among graduates.

## Checking flaw and required data across all five options

Testing **whether missing failures and a graduate baseline are included** isolates the sole valid answer.

| Option | Accurate flaw | Data supply missing failures and baseline | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stories include only winners, tested by comparing success rates among all dropouts versus graduates | Yes | Yes (recovers missing data) | **Correct** |
| Collecting even more dropout success stories | Yes | Wrong (same filter, failures still missing) | **Incorrect** |
| Ignoring family wealth | Not the survivorship-bias flaw highlighted | - | **Incorrect** |
| Recording how many semesters winners attended | Yes | Wrong (still omits failures) | **Incorrect** |
| Dropouts gain more free time | Supports the student rather than faulting the claim | - | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The main flaw is that **those stories include only those who succeeded**, and testing the claim requires **comparing the share who succeed among all dropouts against the share who succeed among graduates**.

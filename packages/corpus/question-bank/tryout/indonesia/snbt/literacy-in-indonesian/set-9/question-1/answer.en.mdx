# Given

Paragraph two of the **C4 rice** passage traces drought in C3 plants: as soil dries, leaves close **stomata** to save water vapor, restricting <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> entry while photosynthetic oxygen builds up (even without temperature change), so **Rubisco** binds oxygen more often and releases carbon back out.

# Asked

The sequence that best explains how drought raises photorespiration in C3 plants.

# Solution

Follow the **four linked steps** in paragraph two and eliminate options inserting temperature change, open stomata, <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> buildup, or PEP carboxylase.

> Stomata close to conserve water, <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> entry drops while oxygen accumulates, and Rubisco binds oxygen more often so carbon is released again.

## Checking the causal chain across all five options

Every **distractor breaks one specific link** in paragraph two's sequence.

| Proposed sequence | Accuracy of every link | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stomata close, <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> struggles to enter while oxygen accumulates, Rubisco binds oxygen more often and releases carbon | Exact match across all steps | **Correct** |
| Relies on rising temperature | Wrong ("even when temperature does not change") | **Incorrect** |
| Stomata open and oxygen escapes | Reversed, stomata close and oxygen builds up | **Incorrect** |
| <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> accumulates inside the leaf | Reversed, <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> cannot easily enter | **Incorrect** |
| PEP carboxylase binds oxygen | Wrong, PEP carboxylase belongs to C4 and ignores oxygen | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The right sequence is that **stomata close, <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> struggles to enter while oxygen builds up, and Rubisco binds oxygen more frequently so part of the carbon is released again**.

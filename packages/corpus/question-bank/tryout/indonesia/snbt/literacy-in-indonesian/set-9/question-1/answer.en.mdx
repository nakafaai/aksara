Paragraph two gives the cause and effect. Peat is hard to burn **as long as it stays wet**. Once canals are dug and the water drained, the peat **dries out** and behaves like a dry sponge: **light, porous, and easy to ignite**.

- Oil from deeper layers is not mentioned.
- Leftover salt is not mentioned.
- Plant sap is not given as a cause.
- Sinking ground is described as a result of drainage, not a cause of fire.

So drained peatland burns easily because dry peat becomes light, porous, and easy to ignite.

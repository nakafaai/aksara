# Given

The story closes with **Pak Sastro** "not yet deciding whether to translate it at all" after realizing Bu Ratmi's letters hold private sacrifices like the sold necklace. Paragraph two had earlier remarked that in translating, **"choosing one option means discarding the other."**

# Asked

What the ending most likely implies and which part of the story supports it best.

# Solution

Connect **Pak Sastro's hesitation over translating the final letter** (deciding what reaches Dita) with paragraph two's theme that every choice of a translator excludes something else.

> The ending implies that translating also means deciding what gets shared with others, echoing paragraph two's line that choosing one equivalent means discarding another.

## Checking ending implication and support across the options

Matching **both deeper theme and textual echo** eliminates all four flawed pairs.

| Option | Ending implication | Strongest textual support | Verdict |

| Deciding what to share, backed by where the letters were found | Exact | Wrong (storage spot does not concern choosing what to share) | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The ending implies that **translating also means deciding what gets shared, aligned with the statement that choosing one equivalent means discarding another**.

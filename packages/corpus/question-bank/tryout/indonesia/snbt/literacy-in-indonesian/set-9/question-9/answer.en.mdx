The middle-way proposal rests on the claim that **lower stakes** keep schools **from teaching to the test**. A finding that schools in countries with low-stakes exams cut subjects less often shows that claim holding in practice.

- Tutored students' scores do not test whether low stakes improve teaching.
- Parents' wishes support the proposal the author rejects.
- Differences in city and village report marks only show the need for a common measure, not the advantage of low stakes.
- Past participation numbers are unrelated to the proposal.

So the strongest support is that schools in countries with low-stakes exams cut subjects less often.

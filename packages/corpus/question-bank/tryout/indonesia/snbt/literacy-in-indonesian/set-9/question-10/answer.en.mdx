# Given

In the story, **Bu Ratmi** writes letters to her mother in Wonogiri: she notices her quiet husband's habit of returning borrowed books unfinished ("Your grandmother knew that"), and quietly sells her necklace to pay rent while asking her mother "not to tell anyone."

# Asked

All interpretations of Bu Ratmi that are supported by the story.

# Solution

Check all five claims against **Bu Ratmi's letters and Pak Sastro's remarks** to isolate the two supported interpretations.

> The story supports that Bu Ratmi noticed her husband's small struggles even though he rarely spoke of them, and shouldered family burdens without telling him.

## Checking the five interpretations of Bu Ratmi

Matching **each option to exact letter details** yields the two supported statements.

| Interpretation | Story evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knew her husband's small struggles even though he rarely talked about them | He "never talked much," yet "your grandmother knew that" about borrowed books | **Supported** |
| Shouldered family burdens without telling her husband | Sold her necklace for rent and asked her mother "not to tell anyone" | **Supported** |
| Disappointed that her husband was not successful | False, she writes warmly of his progress buying his own books | **Unsupported** |
| Addressed the letters to her husband | False, they were written "to her mother in Wonogiri" | **Unsupported** |
| Hoped her granddaughter would become a translator | False, never mentioned and Dita initiated the translation | **Unsupported** |

# Answer

The story supports that **Bu Ratmi noticed her husband's quiet struggles even though he rarely spoke of them** and that **she once shouldered a family burden without telling her husband**.

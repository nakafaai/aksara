Bu Ratmi never appears directly. Readers know her only through the letters and Pak Sastro's explanations.

**She knew her husband's small daily hardships even though he rarely talked: supported**. He was **"tidak pernah banyak bicara"** (never one to talk much). The line about buying his own books points to his habit of returning borrowed books before finishing them. Pak Sastro stresses, **"Nenekmu tahu itu"** (Your grandmother knew that). Ratmi noticed something that small.

**She carried a family burden without telling her husband: supported**. She sold the necklace to pay the rent and asked her mother **"tidak memberi tahu siapa pun"** (not to tell anyone). Pak Sastro only learns of it forty years later.

**She was disappointed he never succeeded: not supported**. Ratmi writes about her husband's progress, being able to buy his own books. That isn't disappointment.

**She wrote to her husband: not supported**. The letters were written **"kepada ibunya di Wonogiri"** (to her mother in Wonogiri).

**She hoped her grandchild would become a translator: not supported**. The story mentions no such hope. Dita is the one who asks for the translation.

So the story supports that Bu Ratmi knew her husband's small daily hardships even though he rarely talked, and that she once carried a family burden without telling him.

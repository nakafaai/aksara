# Given

The pantun *"Air pasang di Teluk Bayur, / perahu nelayan pulang ke tepi. / Jangan sombong walaupun makmur, / ilmu dicari sepanjang hari."* rhymes **a-b-a-b** (*-ur, -i, -ur, -i*). The author states **"sampiran primarily sets up sound"** and any echo with the message is **"loose and not always present,"** calling attempts to find hidden meaning in every sampiran excessive.

# Asked

The best way to understand this stanza's sampiran according to the author's view.

# Solution

Apply **the author's sound-first, loose-echo rule** directly to the *-ur* and *-i* rhymes and the coastal image that has no tight link to humility and learning.

> The sampiran mainly prepares the *-ur* and *-i* end sounds, while any link between its coastal image and the advice on humility and knowledge is at most loose.

## Checking the five interpretations against the author view

Evaluating **rhyme role versus semantic link** isolates the single faithful reading.

| Interpretation | Alignment with author's view | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primarily sets up the *-ur* and *-i* end sounds, with at most a loose link to the advice | Exact match with the author's stance | **Correct** |
| Must encode a hidden symbolic message | Explicitly called "excessive" by the author | **Incorrect** |
| Certainly mirrors the meaning of the advice | "Certainly" clashes with "not always present" | **Incorrect** |
| Fails the a-b-a-b rhyme rule | False, *Bayur/makmur* and *tepi/hari* are cleanly a-b-a-b | **Incorrect** |
| Sampiran matters more than the message | Never claimed by the author | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The sampiran is best understood as **primarily setting up the *-ur* and *-i* end sounds, while the link between its imagery and the advice is at most loose**.

The passage opens by saying batik is defined by **how it is made**: wax-resist dyeing with **malam** (hot wax). Printed cloth is made by machine "**tanpa malam sama sekali**" (without any wax at all). Because it skips that technique, it does not meet the definition of batik, even when the pattern looks similar.

- The passage does not say printed patterns always differ. The cloth carries batik patterns.
- Printed cloth is described as cheaper, not more expensive.
- Where printed cloth is made is not mentioned.
- Colour fastness is not discussed.

So printed cloth is better called batik-patterned textile because it is made without wax, while batik is defined by the wax technique.

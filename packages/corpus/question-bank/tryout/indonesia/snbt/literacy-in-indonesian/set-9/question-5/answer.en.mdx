# Given

Optimistic researchers argue converting rice to C4 is **"just a matter of switching on the right genes in the right cells."** Yet the passage notes C4 pumping also requires **tightly packed, chloroplast-rich bundle-sheath cells** to keep <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> from leaking, whereas rice bundle-sheath cells hold few chloroplasts.

# Asked

The finding that, if true, most weakens the claim that switching on the right genes in the right cells is enough.

# Solution

Select evidence showing that **even with every C4 enzyme turned on**, rice still fails to trap <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> because leaf anatomy remains a bottleneck.

> If rice carrying all C4 enzymes still leaks <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> from its bundle-sheath cells, turning on genes alone is clearly not enough without structural leaf changes.

## Checking the five findings against the single gene claim

Testing **which finding proves enzymes alone fall short** isolates the sole counterexample.

| Finding | Undercuts "genes alone suffice" | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rice expressing all C4 enzymes still loses <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> from its bundle-sheath cells | Yes, points to leaf-anatomy limits beyond enzymes | **Weakens most** |
| C4 enzymes exist across many C3 plants | Supports researchers' optimism | **Incorrect** |
| C4 evolved independently many times | Another pillar of their optimism | **Incorrect** |
| Enzymes are expressed in the target cells | Supports rather than weakens the claim | **Incorrect** |
| Research costs are very high | Concerns budget, not biological sufficiency of genes | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The finding that most weakens that claim is **rice expressing all C4 enzymes that still loses <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> from its bundle-sheath cells**.

# Given

**Text B** doubts the assumption that **"buyers will move along with the vendors."** In Pasar Baru's track record, when buyers did not follow, vendor revenue plunged from Rp<InlineMath math="600{,}000" /> to Rp<InlineMath math="250{,}000" /> and **<InlineMath math="38" /> of <InlineMath math="60" /> vendors returned to street sidewalks** within a year.

# Asked

Who is most likely to be harmed if buyers do not follow the relocated vendors, and why.

# Solution

Trace the **two-step chain from Pasar Baru**: vendors lose income first, and as they drift back onto the sidewalks to survive, pedestrians lose the promised clear sidewalks too.

> Vendors suffer first from collapsing sales, and pedestrians end up harmed as well when returning vendors reclaim the sidewalks.

## Evaluating the five candidate outcomes

Checking **each option against Pasar Baru's precedent** eliminates every off-text or incomplete answer.

| Claimed harmed party | Fit with Text B's Pasar Baru precedent | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vendors, and eventually pedestrians too as returning vendors occupy sidewalks again | Directly follows income drop and <InlineMath math="38/60" /> returning | **Correct** |
| City government budget | Not discussed in either text | **Incorrect** |
| Vendors only, while pedestrians definitely gain | False, struggling vendors return to sidewalks | **Incorrect** |
| Buyers facing higher food prices at Pasar Sentra | Food prices are never mentioned | **Incorrect** |
| Bus riders due to relocated stops | Moving stops is Text B's suggestion, not Text A's plan | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The parties most likely to be harmed are **vendors, and eventually pedestrians too, because vendors returning to the street will occupy the sidewalks once again**.

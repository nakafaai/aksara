Survivorship bias happens when a conclusion is drawn **only from what passed some filter**, while **what fell out isn't visible** in the data. Look for the filter in each case.

**Old motorbikes: biased**. Poor old bikes broke down long ago and are off the road. Only the ones that lasted are seen.

**Funds that still exist: biased**. Funds that lost money were closed and aren't counted, so the average of those left looks better.

**Old songs still played: biased**. Ordinary old songs have been forgotten. The ones still played passed the filter of time.

**Randomly assigned students: not biased**. All students are observed, and random assignment filters no one out of the data.

**Surveying all graduates, including those who didn't go to university: not biased**. This survey deliberately includes the group usually missed.

So the biased reasoning is the conclusion about old motorbikes, the judgement of funds that still exist, and the conclusion about old songs.

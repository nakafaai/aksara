# Given

**Survivorship bias** happens when a conclusion draws **only on items that passed a survival filter**, while **failures vanish from view**.

# Asked

All reasoning examples that exhibit survivorship bias as explained in the passage.

# Solution

Check **whether a hidden filter removes failures** in each scenario, excluding random assignment and full-cohort surveys that track everyone.

> Judging old motorcycles still on the road, surviving mutual funds, and old songs still played on radio all sample only survivors while hiding failures.

## Testing all five cases for a survival filter

Identifying **what dropped out of view** selects the three survivorship-bias cases.

| Case | What vanished from view | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old motorcycles still on the road | Poorly built old motorcycles broke down and disappeared | **Has bias** |
| Mutual funds still operating | Losing funds shut down and dropped out of the average | **Has bias** |
| Old songs still played today | Mediocre old songs were forgotten over time | **Has bias** |
| Random student assignment | No filter, every student is observed | **No bias** |
| Survey of all graduates including non-college ones | Deliberately captures every group without filtering | **No bias** |

# Answer

The cases containing survivorship bias are **conclusions about old motorcycles**, **evaluating only mutual funds that still exist**, and **conclusions about old songs still played today**.

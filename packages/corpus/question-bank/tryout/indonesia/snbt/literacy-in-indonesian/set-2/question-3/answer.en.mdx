# Given

Total mercury in the water is roughly **<InlineMath math="3" /> times** higher upstream than downstream. The team measured predatory-fish methylmercury at <InlineMath math="0.42\text{ mg/kg}" /> upstream and <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ mg/kg}" /> downstream.

# Asked

The downstream predatory-fish level predicted by the official's proportionality assumption, and how the team's actual measurement compares with it.

# Solution

Calculate the **official's prediction** by dividing upstream predatory mercury by <InlineMath math="3" />, then divide the team's downstream measurement by that prediction.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{predicted downstream} = 0.42 : 3 = 0.14\text{ mg/kg}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{ratio} = 0.90 : 0.14 \approx 6.4 \approx 6\text{ times}" />
</MathContainer>

> The official's assumption predicts <InlineMath math="0.14\text{ mg/kg}" />, yet the team's measured <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ mg/kg}" /> is roughly six times higher.

The official's assumption predicts about **<InlineMath math="0.14" /> milligrams per kilogram**, whereas the team's downstream data are **about <InlineMath math="6" /> times that level**.

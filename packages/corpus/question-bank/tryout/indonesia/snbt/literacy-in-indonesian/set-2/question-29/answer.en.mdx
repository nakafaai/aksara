An analogy question takes two steps: first pin down the pattern of the supporters' answer, then find the situation with the same pattern.

**Step 1. The supporters' pattern**. The last paragraph has three parts.

- No option is truly neutral. Even a form with every box empty **"sudah memilih sistem masuk"** (has already chosen opt-in).
- So **not choosing is also a choice**.
- So what needs discussing isn't whether to have a default, but **which default**.

**Step 2. Match it**. A teacher who says that giving no homework is itself a decision about students' study time uses the same pattern. "Giving none" isn't a neutral state but one of the options. So the question moves to what kind of homework.

The other situations use different patterns.

- A crossed-out price is a way to persuade buyers. It says nothing about not choosing being a choice.
- A school that lets students choose for themselves rejects defaults for the sake of freedom, like the critics, not the supporters.
- A helmet law is a rule that forces people, not a default they can change.
- A doctor who writes a default dose because patients are lazy uses the "effort to change" explanation from the third paragraph, not the supporters' reply to the ethical criticism.

So the closest situation is the teacher who says that giving no homework is already a decision, so what needs discussing is what kind of homework.

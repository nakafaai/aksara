The last paragraph names **two limits of the surveys**. First, lichens grow very slowly, so their condition reflects air quality over years, not on the survey day. Second, bark acidity, tree species, and light also affect their growth. For that reason, monitors treat survey results as **an early sign that must be confirmed with measuring instruments**. Instruments are still needed to know pollutant levels at a particular time.

- The passage calls lichen surveys a **cheap** method, the opposite of being more expensive.
- Lichens returned to city parks once the air improved, so surveys can be done in cities.
- The passage does not say people struggle to tell lichen forms apart. Schools and community groups actually use these surveys.
- Paragraph three shows that lichens are sensitive to sulphur dioxide.

So lichen surveys cannot fully replace instruments because lichens reflect conditions over years and are affected by other factors.

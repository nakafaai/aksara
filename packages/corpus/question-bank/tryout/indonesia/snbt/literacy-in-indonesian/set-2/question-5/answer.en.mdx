# Given

The monitoring team notes **two study limits**: fish were caught during only one dry season, and fish body sizes were not recorded even though older, larger fish tend to hold more methylmercury. Meanwhile, the district official relied solely on cleaner swamp water with no fish data at all.

# Asked

The most accurate statement about how these two limits affect the team's rebuttal of the official.

# Solution

Separate what the limits mean for the **exact size of the upstream-downstream gap** from what they mean for the **official's water-only argument**.

> Unrecorded fish sizes leave the exact gap between sites uncertain, yet they cannot rescue an official's conclusion that never rested on fish measurements.

## Evaluating the five interpretations

Checking **each statement against both sides of the debate** identifies the balanced assessment.

| Statement | Fit with passage logic | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leaves the exact site gap uncertain, yet does not save the official's water-only conclusion | Accurately weighs the limit without validating the official | **Correct** |
| Cancels the rebuttal because seasons differed between sites | False, both sites were sampled in the same dry season | **Incorrect** |
| Seasons have no effect on mercury levels | False, the team explicitly lists single-season sampling as a limit | **Incorrect** |
| Comparing the same feeding group controls for body size | False, downstream predators could still be larger than upstream ones | **Incorrect** |
| Proves the official's conclusion right | False, uncertainty does not validate a claim with zero fish data | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

Both limitations **leave the exact size of the difference between sites uncertain, yet do not rescue the official's conclusion based solely on water cleanliness**.

# Given

**The story follows** Laras preparing to leave for university and her mother handing over an unfinished woven cloth with straight lines.

# Asked

Which statements can be concluded from the story's actions and timeline.

# Solution

> Watch small clues about timing and actions because fiction often shows a key fact through what characters do rather than what they declare.

Two statements rest on small clues in the story, and the other two test what the promise said and the order of events.

The statement that the mother never forbids Laras to leave can be concluded. Everything she says that night is about the bus time, a jacket and thread. There's no ban, and her remark about gold thread actually defends Laras leaving.

The statement that the mother cut the cloth before it was finished can be concluded. In the morning there are **"ujung-ujung benang lungsin yang sudah digunting"** (cut ends of warp threads), and Laras knows the cloth was **"masih kurang sejengkal"** (still a hand's span short). The mother herself says, "Bagian ujungnya belum jadi" (The end isn't finished).

The statement about promising not to come home can't be concluded. Laras promises the opposite: **"Aku pulang setiap libur, Bu"** (I'll come home every holiday, Mum).

The statement that the straight-line pattern was woven after Laras left can't be concluded. Laras sees the pattern on the bus, on the cloth her mother handed her before she left. So it was woven **before** she left, that night at the latest.

# Answer

**The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The mother never forbids Laras to leave | Ya |
| The mother cut the cloth before it was finished | Ya |
| Laras promised not to come home until she finished studying | Tidak |
| The straight-line pattern was woven after Laras left | Tidak |

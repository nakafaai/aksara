# Given

**The story portrays** Ibu's attitude toward Laras leaving through her actions, figures of speech, and the unfinished woven cloth.

# Asked

Whether each statement about Ibu's attitude fits the story.

# Solution

> When a character rarely voices feelings directly, read her actions and the gift she hands over as her clearest message.

Ibu (Mother) almost never says how she feels. You have to read it from **what she does** and from **the cloth** she hands over.

- **Statement 1: matches**. Ibu **"sering berdiri lama"** (often stood for a long time) in front of the floor plans in Laras's room. Years later, the end of the cloth has straight lines **"seperti denah yang dulu menempel di dinding kamarnya"** (like the floor plans that used to hang on the bedroom wall). She had been paying attention for a long time, not just that night.
- **Statement 2: doesn't match**. Ibu actually turns down Laras's promise to come home every holiday: **"Jangan berjanji soal benang yang belum ditenun."** (Don't make promises about thread that hasn't been woven.) About the cloth, Ibu says "Kau saja yang selesaikan, entah dengan benang apa" (you finish it, with whatever thread), without saying when or where.
- **Statement 3: matches**. Handing over the roll of cloth, Ibu says, **"Bagian ujungnya belum jadi"** (the end isn't finished), and then **"Kau saja yang selesaikan, entah dengan benang apa."** (You finish it, with whatever thread.) Ibu hands over unfinished cloth and leaves Laras free to choose how.
- **Statement 4: doesn't match**. Ibu herself swaps the bamboo-shoot pattern for floor-plan lines, and she leaves Laras free to choose any thread.
- **Statement 5: matches**. That night Ibu only asks about the bus and says to bring a jacket, and then **"Itu saja."** (That's all.) About Laras leaving, Ibu speaks through the gold-thread figure, and the blessing only shows in the cloth handed over the next morning.

# Answer

The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement.

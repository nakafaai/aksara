The mother almost never says what she feels. Her attitude has to be read from her figurative remarks and her actions, above all the cloth she hands over.

The statement that she hopes Laras will stay doesn't fit. Her remark about gold thread says thread that stays **"terlalu lama digulung dan tidak pernah dipakai"** (wound up too long and never used) spoils. She doesn't hold Laras back.

The statement that she accepts Laras's choice fits. She never forbids it, tells Laras to take a jacket, and weaves a pattern **"seperti denah yang dulu menempel di dinding kamarnya"** (like the floor plans that used to hang on her bedroom wall). That pattern shows she accepts the world Laras has chosen, without having to say it.

The statement that she thinks the tradition no longer matters doesn't fit. She has woven every night for twenty years, and most of the cloth still carries the bamboo-shoot pattern, **"motif yang selalu ditenun keluarga mereka"** (the pattern their family always wove). She changes part of the pattern, she doesn't abandon the tradition.

The statement that she leaves it to Laras how to carry the tradition on fits. She says, **"Kau saja yang selesaikan, entah dengan benang apa"** (You finish it, with whatever thread). The words *entah dengan benang apa* (with whatever thread) give Laras the freedom to choose her own way.

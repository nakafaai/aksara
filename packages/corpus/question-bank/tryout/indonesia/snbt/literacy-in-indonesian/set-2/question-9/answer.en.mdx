The author's reason in paragraph two is a causal chain: unpaid internships cost money, so **only students from well-off families can take them**, and work experience ends up with that group. The finding that weakens it most must show that this gap in participation **does not exist**. If students from low-income families take unpaid internships just as often, cost is not holding them back, and the author's reason loses its basis.

- Stipends at large companies show that some internships are not a problem, but they do not refute the gap in unpaid internships.
- Feeling that the experience was valuable is something the author already accepts, and it says nothing about who can afford to take part.
- Rising room rents make the cost heavier, which strengthens the author's reason.
- A grade requirement is a different filter, not evidence that cost does not hold poorer students back.

So the finding that most weakens the reason is equal participation by low- and high-income students.

The mother almost never says what she feels. Her attitude has to be read from her figurative remarks and her actions, above all the cloth she hands over. Two statements set traps with readings that sound natural but clash with clues in the story.

The statement that she gently tries to talk Laras out of leaving doesn't fit. Her silence and figurative remarks could be mistaken for gentle persuasion. But her remark about gold thread says thread that stays **"terlalu lama digulung dan tidak pernah dipakai"** (wound up too long and never used) spoils. That remark defends Laras leaving, it doesn't hold her back.

The statement that she accepts Laras's choice fits. She never forbids it, tells Laras to take a jacket, and weaves a pattern **"seperti denah yang dulu menempel di dinding kamarnya"** (like the floor plans that used to hang on her bedroom wall). That pattern shows she accepts the world Laras has chosen, without having to say it.

The statement that the bamboo-shoot pattern no longer needs weaving doesn't fit. **"Sebagian besar kain"** (most of the cloth) still carries the bamboo-shoot pattern, the one the family always wove. She changes only the last few spans, she doesn't abandon the family pattern.

The statement that she leaves it to Laras how to carry the tradition on fits. She says, **"Kau saja yang selesaikan, entah dengan benang apa"** (You finish it, with whatever thread). The words *entah dengan benang apa* give Laras the freedom to choose her own way.

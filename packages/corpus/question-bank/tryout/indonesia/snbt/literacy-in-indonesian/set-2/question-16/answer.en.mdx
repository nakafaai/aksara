# Given

At the Karangsari fish auction, **every species' price rose** from January to February: mackerel tuna from <InlineMath math="30" /> to <InlineMath math="33" />, Indian mackerel from <InlineMath math="40" /> to <InlineMath math="41" />, and anchovies from <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="22" /> thousand rupiah per kg. Meanwhile, the cheapest fish (anchovies) jumped from <InlineMath math="10" /> to <InlineMath math="30" /> tons.

# Asked

The conclusion best supported by the passage and auction table.

# Solution

Compare **each species' price change** against the **volume-weighted overall average price** to see how a shift in catch mix pulls the overall average down.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{January average} &= \frac{20(30) + 10(40) + 10(20)}{40} = \frac{1{,}200}{40} = 30\text{ thousand/kg} \\
\text{February average} &= \frac{10(33) + 10(41) + 30(22)}{50} = \frac{1{,}400}{50} = 28\text{ thousand/kg}
\end{aligned}" />

> Every fish species rose in price, yet the overall average fell from Rp<InlineMath math="30{,}000" /> to Rp<InlineMath math="28{,}000" /> because inexpensive anchovies grew to three-fifths of total weight.

## Checking the five candidate conclusions

Testing **species prices, weighted average, and boat counts** rules out all four distractors.

| Conclusion | Match with table data | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every fish type rose in price, yet overall average fell because cheap anchovies became the largest share | Exact fit with all table figures | **Correct** |
| Fish prices plunged across the board | False, every species rose per kg | **Incorrect** |
| Higher total auction value proves fishers fared better | False, participating boats rose from <InlineMath math="50" /> to <InlineMath math="80" /> | **Incorrect** |
| Every species rose by the same percentage | False, tuna and anchovies rose <InlineMath math="10\%" />, mackerel <InlineMath math="2.5\%" /> | **Incorrect** |
| Anchovies contributed the most value because they had the steepest price jump | False, their <InlineMath math="10\%" /> rise matched tuna | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The best-supported conclusion is that **the price of every fish species rose, yet the overall average price fell because inexpensive anchovies became the largest share of total weight**.

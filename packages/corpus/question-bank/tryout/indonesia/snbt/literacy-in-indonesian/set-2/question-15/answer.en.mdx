# Given

Responding to the employment studies cited by Text A, **Text B's author** notes that those studies "mostly observe large formally registered firms" and "may not hold" for micro businesses whose wages take <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of revenue, profit is <InlineMath math="5\%" />, and prices cannot be raised freely.

# Asked

All responses that match Text B's author's view of those studies.

# Solution

Distinguish Text B's measured point about **limited scope of applicability** from sweeping claims the author never makes.

> Text B does not dismiss the studies as wrong for large firms, but argues they leave unanswered what happens to thin-margin micro businesses.

## Checking each response against Text B

Matching **every option to Text B's wording** separates the two accurate views from three distortions.

| Candidate response | Fit with Text B | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| May hold for large formally registered firms | Accurate, B questions transferability rather than truth for big firms | **Fits** |
| Still leaves unanswered the fate of thin-margin micro firms unable to raise prices freely | Captures B's core objection in paragraphs two and three | **Fits** |
| The studies are wrong and raises always cut jobs | False, B never calls them wrong or universal job killers | **Does not fit** |
| Already sufficient for micro businesses | False, B argues the exact opposite | **Does not fit** |
| Irrelevant because wages are spent in another city | False, B never discusses where wages are spent | **Does not fit** |

# Answer

According to Text B, **the studies may hold for large formally registered firms**, yet **they still do not answer what happens to micro businesses with thin margins that cannot raise prices at will**.

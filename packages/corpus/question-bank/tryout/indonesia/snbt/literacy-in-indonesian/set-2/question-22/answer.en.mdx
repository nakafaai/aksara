A single-disaster hypothesis requires all the victims to have died in the **same period**. Compare the radiocarbon results in sentences (12) and (13).

| Genetic ancestry group | Radiocarbon period |
| --- | --- |
| South Asian-related | <InlineMath math="7" />th through <InlineMath math="10" />th centuries CE |
| Eastern Mediterranean and Southeast Asian-related | <InlineMath math="17" />th through <InlineMath math="20" />th centuries CE |

These ranges **do not overlap** and differ by about <InlineMath math="1{,}000\,\text{years}" />. The remains therefore cannot all come from a single death event.

The correct option mentions different periods and genetic ancestry groups. Different ancestry alone would not rule out one disaster, because people of different ancestries can be in the same place. The **difference in dates** is decisive here. The alternatives about an army, an epidemic, or just one skeleton lack support in the passage.

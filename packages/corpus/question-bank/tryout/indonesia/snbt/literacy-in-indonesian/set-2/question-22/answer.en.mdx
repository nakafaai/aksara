# Given

The author judges the **colonial-era** label "plantation laboratory" for the Bogor Botanic Garden as "accurate for part of its work, but not all of it." During colonial times the garden tested four oil-palm seedlings for plantations while also running a late-19th-century laboratory studying non-commercial plants and a herbarium.

# Asked

The pair of details that best supports both halves of the author's colonial-era judgment together.

# Solution

A complete pair must supply **one colonial plantation activity** and **one colonial non-commercial research activity**.

> The four oil-palm seedlings support "accurate for part of its work," while the late-19th-century laboratory studying non-commercial species supports "not all of it" within the same colonial period.

## Testing each pair against both halves

Checking **coverage of both sides and the colonial timeframe** identifies the sole complete pair.

| Pair of details | Supports colonial plantation side | Supports colonial non-plantation side | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Four oil-palm seedlings for plantation seed and the laboratory studying non-commercial species | Yes (1848 oil palms) | Yes (late-19th-century lab) | **Correct** |
| Location beside the palace and founder's birthplace | Does not describe work | Irrelevant | **Incorrect** |
| Herbarium and laboratory | Missing plantation evidence | Both support only the second half | **Incorrect** |
| Oil-palm seedlings and present-day conservation | Yes | Wrong era (conservation is the modern goal) | **Incorrect** |
| Founder's birthplace and the name Buitenzorg | Unrelated to garden work | Unrelated to garden work | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The strongest pair is **the four oil-palm seedlings whose descendants reportedly supplied plantations, paired with the laboratory studying species with no commercial value**.

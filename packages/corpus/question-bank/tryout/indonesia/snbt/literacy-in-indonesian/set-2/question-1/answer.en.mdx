This question asks you to apply the text's explanation to a new place. Two conditions must hold for fish to carry methylmercury, and you need to check both.

- **There is mercury**. Without mercury from the mine, there's nothing to turn into methylmercury.
- **There is a place for the change**. The second paragraph says the change happens **"di dasar perairan yang tenang dan miskin oksigen"** (at the bottom of still, oxygen-poor water), where bacteria turn inorganic mercury into methylmercury. The text also says that in inorganic form, mercury **"tidak mudah masuk ke jaringan hewan"** (doesn't easily enter animal tissue).

Now check the three places.

| Place | Mercury from the mine | Still, oxygen-poor bottom |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| Fast river near the mine | yes, the most | no |
| Still reservoir downstream | yes, less | yes |
| Separate clear lake | no | not stated |

Only the reservoir meets both conditions. Like the swamp in the text, it can make fish more contaminated even though the total mercury in its water is lower.

The other options are wrong.

- High total mercury in the fast river doesn't necessarily mean contaminated fish. Most of the mercury there is still inorganic, the same mistake the official makes in the text.
- A still lake that gets no mine water has no mercury to convert.
- Being close to the source isn't the right measure. In the text, fish near the mine are actually less contaminated.
- The last option picks the right place for the wrong reason. Plant-eating fish don't eat other fish.

So plant-eating fish are most likely to be contaminated in the still reservoir with an oxygen-poor bottom, even though the total mercury in its water is lower.

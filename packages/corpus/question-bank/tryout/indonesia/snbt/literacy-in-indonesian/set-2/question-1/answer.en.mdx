The question asks what a sentence built on **bukan hanya ... melainkan juga** (not only ... but also) means. That pattern keeps the first factor in play and adds a second one that also counts.

The first factor, "berapa banyak merkuri yang ada" (how much mercury there is), still matters. The second, "di mana merkuri itu mengendap" (where the mercury settles), has to be read with the second paragraph. It says that at the bottom of waters that are "yang tenang dan miskin oksigen" (still and low in oxygen), "sebagian bakteri mengubah merkuri anorganik menjadi metilmerkuri" (some bacteria turn inorganic mercury into methylmercury). That's the form algae take up and pass on to fish. The fourth paragraph names the downstream swamp as such a place.

So "where" here isn't about distance from the mine. It means **the conditions where the mercury settles**, because those conditions decide how much mercury turns into the form that enters the food chain.

- The option about the place closest to the mine reads "where" as distance. Yet fish near the mine actually carry less.
- The option saying the amount of mercury doesn't matter reads "bukan hanya" (not only) as if it were "bukan" (not). It also forgets that mud is exactly where mercury becomes methylmercury.
- The option about parts of the fish's body misreads "mengendap" (settles). The sentence is about deposits at the bottom of the water, not inside the fish.
- The option about more mercury in the swamp guesses an amount nobody measured. The text only says water upstream holds about <InlineMath math="3" /> times the total mercury of water downstream.

So the team's statement means that the conditions where mercury settles help decide how much of it becomes methylmercury and enters the food chain.

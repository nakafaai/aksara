# Given

The passage explains that **inorganic mercury** does not easily enter animal tissue. The real hazard starts when bacteria in **calm, oxygen-poor bottom mud** convert it into methylmercury that binds to protein, even where total water mercury is lower.

# Asked

Where plant-eating fish are most likely to carry the highest methylmercury contamination, and why.

# Solution

Compare all three water bodies on the **two conditions for methylmercury production**: mercury inflow from the mining river and calm, oxygen-poor bottom sediment.

> Total inorganic mercury in fast water matters less than calm, oxygen-poor sediment where bacteria convert mercury into bioavailable methylmercury.

## Comparing the three water bodies

Testing **mercury supply and sediment conditions** identifies the highest-risk site clearly.

| Water body | Mercury inflow from mine | Bottom sediment condition | Methylmercury risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Downstream reservoir | Yes, receives mining-river water | Calm and oxygen-poor | **Highest (Correct)** |
| Fast river near the mine | High, but mostly inorganic | Fast-flowing, not calm | **Lower** |
| Clear separate lake | None from the mining river | Lacks mercury source | **Lowest** |

# Answer

Plant-eating fish are most likely to be most contaminated **in the calm reservoir, because its oxygen-poor bottom mud allows bacteria to convert mercury into methylmercury**.

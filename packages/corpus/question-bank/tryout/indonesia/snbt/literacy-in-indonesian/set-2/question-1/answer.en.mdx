Paragraph two gives two reasons that work together. First, lichens have no roots and no protective waxy layer, so water, minerals, and gases **enter directly through the whole surface of the body**. Pollutants dissolved in dew or rain are absorbed with them and build up. Second, a lichen **cannot get rid of those substances by shedding leaves**, as many trees can. The option that contains both reasons is the answer.

- The need for light is mentioned in the last paragraph as a factor in growth, not as the reason for sensitivity to pollution.
- Paragraph one says the opposite: the fungus holds water.
- Bark acidity affects growth, but the passage never says lichens grow only on acidic bark. They also live on rocks and tin roofs.
- The passage does not say nitrogen stops photosynthesis. Some species actually thrive where nitrogen is plentiful.

So lichens are more easily harmed because they absorb substances directly through an unprotected surface and cannot shed leaves to get rid of pollutants.

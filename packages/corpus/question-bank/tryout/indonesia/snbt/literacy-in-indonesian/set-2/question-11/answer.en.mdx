# Given

**Text A** argues that minimum-wage raises are spent immediately at local stalls, markets, and workshops, injecting purchasing power into the district. **Text B** states that most Sukaraja workers work in micro and small businesses and proposes phasing their raise over three years.

# Asked

The strongest objection Text A's author can raise against Text B's phased-raise proposal by combining Text A's reasoning with Text B's own fact.

# Solution

Combine **Text A's spending-boost argument** with **Text B's workforce fact**: phasing raises over three years for micro businesses delays higher wages for the majority of local workers.

> Because most Sukaraja workers are employed in micro and small businesses, a three-year phase-in postpones most of the local purchasing-power boost.

## Comparing the supporting reasons across options

Every option mentions a delayed boost, so **the attached reason** decides which objection holds.

| Reason given in option | Match with Text A and Text B | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text B states most Sukaraja workers work in micro and small businesses | Directly links Text A's mechanism with Text B's fact | **Correct** |
| Workers spend their wages outside the district | False, Text A says the money circulates inside the district | **Incorrect** |
| Most local workers work in factories | False, Text B explicitly says they do not | **Incorrect** |
| Market sales fell in 2022 | False, 2022 market sales rose <InlineMath math="6\%" /> | **Incorrect** |
| Employment studies forbid phased raises | False, those studies address jobs, not local spending size | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The strongest objection is that **the proposal delays most of the purchasing-power boost, because according to Text B most workers in Sukaraja work in micro and small businesses**.

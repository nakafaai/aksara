The main point must contain **the problem the author raises** and **the author's stance on it**. Paragraph two shows that unpaid internships are affordable only for students from well-off families. Paragraph three shows that interns can replace paid employees. The last paragraph proposes limits, a stipend and conditions for academic credit, rather than abolishing internships. The option that holds both problems and that limiting stance is the answer.

- The author does not reject all internships and even describes what a genuine learning internship looks like.
- The author proposes a stipend for transport and meals, not pay equal to permanent staff.
- For small organisations, the author proposes an allowance from the university or local government, not a ban on taking interns.
- Certificates appear only as a promise in the adverts and are not part of the author's proposal.

So the author's main point is that unpaid internships made up of real work should be limited because they narrow opportunities and push out paid workers.

The remark is figurative. Laras has just promised to come home every holiday, and her mother answers with sentences about thread. So the thread stands for the bond between mother and daughter, not real thread. The figure has **two parts**, and each has to be read in the right direction.

**Laras leaving won't break the bond: fits**. Laras going far away is like thread being pulled, and good gold thread **"tidak putus kalau ditarik"** (doesn't snap when pulled).

**Keeping Laras back could waste her: fits**. Thread **"baru rusak kalau terlalu lama digulung dan tidak pernah dipakai"** (only spoils if it stays wound up too long and is never used). Laras held back at home, not used for what she chose, is like thread kept in storage.

**Taking gold thread to Bandung: doesn't fit**. This reads the figure literally, as if the mother were talking about real thread.

**Laras must come home soon: doesn't fit**. This misplaces the second part. What spoils is thread **wound up and never used**, that is, Laras held back, not a bond left behind. Her mother also says, "Jangan berjanji soal benang yang belum ditenun" (Don't make promises about thread that hasn't been woven), so she isn't asking Laras to come back soon.

**The mother is disappointed because the bond will snap: doesn't fit**. This reverses the first part. Good gold thread does **not** snap when pulled.

So the remark means that Laras leaving won't break their bond, and that keeping her at home could waste her.

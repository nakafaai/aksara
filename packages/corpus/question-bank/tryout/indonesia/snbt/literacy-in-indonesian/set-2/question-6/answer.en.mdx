# Given

When Laras promises to come home every holiday, **Mother** refuses the promise and says, "Good gold thread does not snap when pulled. It only ruins if kept rolled up too long and never used."

# Asked

All interpretations that fit the figurative meaning of Mother's remark on the night before Laras leaves for Bandung.

# Solution

Read **both halves of the metaphor** in the context of Laras leaving home: pulling the thread represents distance between them, while leaving it rolled up represents keeping Laras confined at home.

> Mother's metaphor conveys two assurances at once: distance will not break their bond, whereas holding Laras back at home would waste who she can become.

## Checking each interpretation against the metaphor

Testing **both halves of the figure of speech** separates the two valid readings from reversals and literal traps.

| Interpretation | Alignment with Mother's metaphor | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laras's departure will not break their bond | Matches "does not snap when pulled" | **Fits** |
| Holding Laras back at home could waste her potential | Matches "only ruins if kept rolled up too long and never used" | **Fits** |
| Mother tells Laras to pack gold thread for Bandung | Misreads the metaphor literally | **Does not fit** |
| Mother wants Laras to promise holiday visits | False, Mother just told her not to make that promise | **Does not fit** |
| Mother fears distance will sever their bond | Reverses "does not snap when pulled" | **Does not fit** |

# Answer

The fitting interpretations are that **Laras's departure will not break their bond** and that **holding Laras back at home could waste her potential**.

# Given

To narrow the "plantation laboratory" label, the author gives **two colonial counterexamples**: the late-19th-century laboratory studying non-commercial plants and the **herbarium** built for research. Suppose a historian discovers that the laboratory actually researched plantation crops almost exclusively.

# Asked

How that discovery affects the author's qualification of the "plantation laboratory" label.

# Solution

Check the discovery against **both of the author's counterexamples**: the first (the laboratory) collapses so the rebuttal weakens, yet the second (the herbarium) remains untouched so the rebuttal does not collapse completely.

> Losing the laboratory example weakens the author's rebuttal, but the research herbarium remains intact so the qualification does not fall completely.

## Checking each candidate impact

Tracking **which example is undermined and which survives** isolates the exact effect.

| Candidate impact | Fit with the author's argument | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weakens the rebuttal without destroying it completely, because the herbarium is unaffected | Accounts accurately for both independent examples | **Correct** |
| Strengthens the author's rebuttal | False, it removes one of the author's own counterexamples | **Incorrect** |
| Has no effect at all | False, the laboratory is a direct pillar of the rebuttal | **Incorrect** |
| Destroys the rebuttal completely | False, the second example (the herbarium) still stands | **Incorrect** |
| Overturns the oil-palm story | False, the finding does not touch oil-palm history | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The discovery **weakens the author's rebuttal without overturning it completely, because the research herbarium (the author's second example) remains untouched**.

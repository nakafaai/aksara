The managers' reasons come in the fifth paragraph, right after the warning. There are **two** reasons, and the right option has to hold both without adding any claim that isn't there.

- **Narrow genetic diversity**. Each species is **"biasanya diwakili oleh beberapa individu saja"** (usually represented by only a few individuals), so its genetic diversity is far narrower than in the wild.
- **Relationships left behind**. Many plants depend on **"penyerbuk, jamur tanah, atau hewan penyebar biji yang tidak ikut pindah ke kebun"** (pollinators, soil fungi or seed-spreading animals that don't move to the garden).

The other options are wrong.

- The text never says the garden is too small. The problem isn't the garden's size but the few individuals per species and the relationships left in the forest.
- The third option's first part is right, but the text doesn't name that consequence. It mentions narrow genetic diversity, not vulnerability to disease.
- Today's garden is run for conservation, not plantations. The plantation role belongs to the colonial period.
- The text never says the garden's plants can't bear fruit. This option invents a consequence.

So the best reason is that the garden holds only a few individuals of each species and doesn't bring along the pollinators, soil fungi or seed spreaders those plants need.

# Given

Paragraph five identifies **two conservation limits** of botanic gardens: narrow genetic diversity because only a few individuals represent each species, and missing ecological partners (pollinators, soil fungi, and seed-dispersing animals) that do not move to the garden.

# Asked

The measure most likely to address both limitations completely at once.

# Solution

Select the measure that preserves **large populations** for genetic diversity together with **all three biological partners** (pollinators, soil fungi, and seed dispersers).

> Protecting the native forest where large plant populations live alongside their pollinators, soil fungi, and seed-dispersing animals closes both gaps completely.

## Checking how completely each option closes both gaps

Testing **genetic breadth and all three ecological partners** isolates the only complete solution.

| Proposed measure | Restores broad genetic diversity | Includes pollinators, soil fungi, and seed dispersers | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protecting forest where large populations live with pollinators, soil fungi, and seed dispersers | Yes | Yes, all three | **Correct** |
| Planting more individuals inside the garden | Yes | No, ecological partners stay behind | **Incorrect** |
| Bringing pollinators and seed dispersers to the garden | No (still few individuals) | Missing soil fungi | **Incorrect** |
| Storing seeds in a seed bank | Yes | Provides none of the three partners | **Incorrect** |
| Adding more individuals plus soil fungi | Yes | Missing pollinators and seed dispersers | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The measure most likely to address both limitations completely is **protecting the forest where large populations of the plant live together with their pollinators, soil fungi, and seed-dispersing animals**.

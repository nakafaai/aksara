First pin down **the two shortcomings** from the fifth paragraph.

- **Narrow genetic diversity**. Each species is **"biasanya diwakili oleh beberapa individu saja"** (usually represented by only a few individuals).
- **Partners don't move with them**. Many plants depend on **pollinators, soil fungi or seed-dispersing animals** that don't come to the garden.

A step that answers both **fully** has to provide many individuals **and** all three kinds of partner. Protecting the forest where a large population lives with its pollinators, soil fungi and seed dispersers does both.

The other options answer only one shortcoming, or part of one.

- Planting more individuals widens genetic diversity, but the partners are still left in the forest.
- Bringing in pollinators and seed dispersers answers part of the second shortcoming, but the soil fungi are left behind and there are still few individuals.
- A seed bank can store genetic diversity, but it provides no pollinators, fungi or seed dispersers.
- Adding individuals along with soil fungi is nearly complete, but there are still no pollinators or seed dispersers.

So the step most likely to answer both shortcomings fully is protecting the forest where a large population of the plant lives with its pollinators, soil fungi and seed dispersers.

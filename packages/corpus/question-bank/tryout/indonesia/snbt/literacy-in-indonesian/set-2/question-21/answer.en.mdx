Look at what the story does **within its paragraph** and **for the paragraph after it**.

- **Within its paragraph**. Oil palm comes from West Africa, is planted in the garden, and its descendants are said to have become seed for plantations in Sumatra. That's an example of **acclimatisation**, testing plants from another country before wider planting, and the result serves plantations.
- **For the next paragraph**. Because of roles like this, some historians call the garden a **"laboratorium perkebunan"** (plantation laboratory). The author then limits that label: **"tepat untuk sebagian kegiatannya, tetapi tidak untuk seluruhnya"** (accurate for some of its activities, but not all of them).

Three options call it an example of acclimatisation, so the second part decides.

- The author doesn't endorse the label for the garden's whole history. The author points to the laboratory and herbarium, which didn't serve plantations.
- Oil palm wasn't rescued from extinction. It was brought in to be tested and grown, so that's acclimatisation, not conservation.
- "Sering disebut" (often said) only marks the claim as reported, not that the acclimatisation failed. The text never calls it a failure.
- The laboratory was only built at the end of the 19th century, and oil palm is a cash crop, not a species without trade value.

So the story gives an example of acclimatisation serving plantations, and it's also the basis for a label the author then limits.

The right option has to read two things correctly: **what the change in pattern means** and **how the mother shows her attitude**.

**The change in pattern**. Most of the cloth carries the bamboo-shoot pattern, the family's pattern. In the last spans it turns into straight lines **"seperti denah yang dulu menempel di dinding kamarnya"** (like the floor plans that used to hang on her bedroom wall), the world Laras has chosen. The lines stop at the unfinished edge, and the mother says, "Kau saja yang selesaikan" (You finish it). So the tradition doesn't end. It goes on, changed by Laras's life.

**How the mother shows it**. That night she only talks about the bus, a jacket and thread. Her acceptance only shows in the cloth she hands over. Her blessing comes **through the cloth, not through words**.

The other options are wrong.

- The mother changes the pattern, so the tradition isn't copied exactly. The flawless threads show care, not a ban on change.
- The third option's first part is right, but its second part is wrong. The cloth is left unfinished so that **Laras** can finish it, not to reject change.
- The unfinished cloth doesn't stand for a broken tradition. The mother hands it over to be continued.
- The last option's first part is right, but its reason is made up. Mother and daughter speak the same language. The mother chooses silence, she isn't unable to speak.

So the best-supported idea is that a tradition can go on while being changed by the next generation's life, and the mother's blessing comes through the cloth, not words.

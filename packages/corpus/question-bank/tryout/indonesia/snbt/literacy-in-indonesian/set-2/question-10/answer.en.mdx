# Given

In the last few spans of the unfinished songket cloth, **Mother** shifts the traditional bamboo-shoot pattern into sharp architectural lines like the floor plans on Laras's bedroom wall, then hands it over for Laras to finish "with whatever thread."

# Asked

All ideas supported by the pattern change in the cloth and Mother's words when handing it over.

# Solution

Examine both the **symbolic shift in the woven motif** and **Mother's quiet way of giving blessing** through craft rather than explicit words.

> Mother hands down the tradition while making room for Laras's chosen path, expressing her silent blessing through the woven cloth itself.

## Checking each idea against the scene

Testing **every candidate idea against the text** isolates the two supported themes.

| Idea | Textual evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family tradition continues while changing with Laras's chosen life | Traditional motif turns into floor-plan lines handed over for Laras to finish | **Supported** |
| Blessing is not spoken directly but revealed through the cloth | Mother stays brief and figurative, showing acceptance in her weaving | **Supported** |
| Tradition must be copied without change | False, Mother herself changes the motif | **Unsupported** |
| Unfinished cloth symbolizes a broken tradition | False, it is handed over specifically to be continued | **Unsupported** |
| Mother does not share Laras's language | False, they speak the same language and Mother chooses silence | **Unsupported** |

# Answer

The supported ideas are that **the family tradition continues while being reshaped by the life Laras chooses** and that **Mother does not voice her blessing directly, yet her acceptance shows through the cloth she wove**.

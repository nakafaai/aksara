Judge each option on its own by looking at **what the change in pattern means** and **how the mother shows her attitude**.

**The tradition goes on while changing: right**. Most of the cloth carries the bamboo-shoot pattern, the family's pattern. In the last spans it turns into straight lines **"seperti denah yang dulu menempel di dinding kamarnya"** (like the floor plans that used to hang on her bedroom wall), the world Laras has chosen. The lines stop at the unfinished edge, and the mother says, "Kau saja yang selesaikan" (You finish it). The tradition doesn't stop, it changes along with Laras's life.

**The blessing isn't stated outright but shows in the cloth: right**. That night the mother only talks about the bus, a jacket and thread, and even that in figures of speech. She never says she gives Laras her blessing. Her acceptance only shows clearly in the pattern of the cloth she hands over.

**The tradition must be copied exactly: wrong**. The mother changes the pattern. The flawless threads show care, not a ban on change.

**The unfinished cloth stands for a broken tradition: wrong**. The mother hands it over precisely so that Laras can carry it on.

**The mother doesn't speak Laras's language: wrong**. They speak the same language. The mother chooses silence, she isn't unable to speak.

So the ideas supported are that the family tradition goes on while being changed by the life Laras has chosen, and that the mother doesn't state her blessing outright, but her acceptance shows in the cloth she wove.

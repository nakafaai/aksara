# Given

The **cooperative head** saw overall average fish price drop from Rp<InlineMath math="30{,}000" /> to Rp<InlineMath math="28{,}000" /> per kg and claimed "fish prices plunged" to request a floor price. Yet all three species rose in price, while cheap anchovies jumped from one-quarter to three-fifths of total weight.

# Asked

The statement that most accurately evaluates the cooperative head's reasoning.

# Solution

Separate the **arithmetic accuracy of Rp<InlineMath math="28{,}000" /> per kg** from the **faulty interpretation** that fish prices collapsed.

> The arithmetic is right, yet the argument is weak because every fish species actually rose in price and the overall average dipped only because cheap anchovies grew into the largest share.

## Evaluating the five assessments

Checking **each assessment against species prices and mix shift** eliminates every distractor.

| Assessment | Fit with auction data | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weak because all three species rose per kg and the average fell only from a larger share of cheap anchovies | Pinpoints the exact mix-shift fallacy | **Correct** |
| The head miscalculated Rp<InlineMath math="28{,}000" /> per kg | False, <InlineMath math="1.4\text{ billion} : 50\text{ tons}" /> equals Rp<InlineMath math="28{,}000" /> | **Incorrect** |
| Strong because a lower average proves lower prices received | False, every fish type sold for more than in January | **Incorrect** |
| Caused by smaller catch weight per boat | False, catch per boat does not alter per-kg prices | **Incorrect** |
| Indian mackerel prices dropped | False, mackerel rose from <InlineMath math="40" /> to <InlineMath math="41" /> thousand/kg | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The reasoning is **weak, because the per-kilogram price of all three fish species actually rose, and the overall average fell only because the share of inexpensive anchovies expanded**.

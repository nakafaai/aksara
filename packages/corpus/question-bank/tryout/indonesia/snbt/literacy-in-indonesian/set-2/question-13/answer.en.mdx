The main conflict is **the tension that drives the story to its resolution**. It begins when orders pile up and Sekar suggests rushing the inside of each kebaya so that two can be finished a day. Mbah Darsih refuses by showing her **the neat inner stitching**. The resolution comes when Sekar herself sews a hem carefully and her grandmother accepts her. The two sides are speed in meeting orders and a firm commitment to quality.

- Sekar's mother is mentioned only to show the machine's age, and she competes with no one.
- No one fights over the machine. At the end, both sit before the same machine.
- Mbah Darsih keeps sewing orders from the online shop, so she does not reject everything new.
- Buyers arrive from many cities. The problem is too many orders, not too few.

So the main conflict is the pull between filling orders quickly and holding firm to the quality of handwork.

Paragraph two says the difference **lies in the soil where mangroves grow**, then builds a chain of causes. The soil is flooded by seawater twice a day, so it holds almost no oxygen. Without oxygen, dead leaves, twigs, and roots **decompose very slowly**. Those remains pile up layer by layer over hundreds to thousands of years, so most of the carbon ends up in the soil.

- The passage says mangroves absorb carbon dioxide "seperti tumbuhan lain" (like other plants), so absorption speed is not the difference.
- Roots do trap mud, but the passage never names carbon carried from land by seawater as the source of the store.
- Breathing roots are said to slow waves and hold sediment, not to pump carbon dioxide into the soil.
- Waves do not turn mud into carbon. That option mixes up erosion control with carbon storage.

So mangrove soil stores so much carbon because flooding with almost no oxygen makes plant remains decompose very slowly, letting them build up over centuries to millennia.

# Given

In the **Yogyakarta AWED Wolbachia trial** passage, the author makes three linked points: the design compares randomized cluster groups during the same period using dengue-positive proportions, cross-border mosquito movement makes measured efficacy more likely too low than too high, and one city's result may not repeat elsewhere or replace breeding-site control.

# Asked

The statement that best summarizes the author's view of the design's strength, the direction of possible bias in efficacy, and the limits of the conclusion.

# Solution

Check **all three parts of every option**, because each distractor flips or distorts exactly one of the three points.

> The trial is strong because it compares two groups over the same period, its efficacy figure is more likely understated than overstated, and its findings may not carry over to every other city.

## Checking all three elements across the options

Matching **design strength, bias direction, and conclusion limits** against the text isolates the sole complete summary.

| Option | Design strength | Direction of bias | Conclusion limits | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compares two groups in the same period, efficacy more likely too low, may not hold in other cities | Exact | Exact | Exact | **Correct** |
| Claims efficacy is more likely too high | Exact | Reversed | Exact | **Incorrect** |
| Claims the trial only counts raw dengue cases | Wrong (uses proportions) | Exact | Exact | **Incorrect** |
| Claims it proves Wolbachia replaces breeding-site removal | Exact | Exact | Wrong (not tested) | **Incorrect** |
| Describes a before-and-after comparison | Wrong (vulnerable to quiet years) | Exact | Exact | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The best summary is that **the design is strong because it compares two groups during the same period, its efficacy figure is more likely too low, and its results may not automatically hold in other cities**.

# Given

**The text and** table compare technical irrigation share, harvested area, and rice output across three districts in 2022 and drought year 2023.

# Asked

Which conclusion the data best support regarding production decline, harvested area, and yield per hectare.

# Solution

> Total harvest can drop through two separate doors, either fewer hectares get harvested or each hectare produces less grain.

These data separate two ways a dry season cuts production: **harvested area shrinking** and **yield per hectare falling**. Work out both for each district.

| District | Yield 2022 (t/ha) | Yield 2023 (t/ha) | Fall in production |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Sukamaju | <InlineMath math="300:50=6" /> | <InlineMath math="279:45=6.2" /> | <InlineMath math="21:300=7\%" /> |
| Ciranda | <InlineMath math="220:40=5.5" /> | <InlineMath math="150:30=5" /> | <InlineMath math="70:220\approx31.8\%" /> |
| Banyulor | <InlineMath math="150:30=5" /> | <InlineMath math="92:20=4.6" /> | <InlineMath math="58:150\approx38.7\%" /> |

The order of the production falls, <InlineMath math="7\%" />, <InlineMath math="31.8\%" /> and <InlineMath math="38.7\%" />, matches the shrinking share of technical irrigation, <InlineMath math="80\%" />, <InlineMath math="40\%" /> and <InlineMath math="20\%" />.

Now split the source of the fall in Ciranda. The loss from shrinking area is the lost area times the old yield, <InlineMath math="10\times5.5=55" /> thousand tonnes. The loss from the lower yield is <InlineMath math="30\times0.5=15" /> thousand tonnes. Together that's <InlineMath math="70" />, matching the table. So most of the fall comes from area. In Banyulor, <InlineMath math="10\times5=50" /> of the <InlineMath math="58" /> thousand tonnes also comes from area.

The other options are wrong.

- The fall comes mainly from area, not yield per hectare. In Sukamaju, yield per hectare actually rose.
- Sukamaju's production still fell <InlineMath math="7\%" />, so it wasn't "not affected at all".
- Yield per hectare didn't fall by about a fifth. It fell about <InlineMath math="9\%" /> in Ciranda and <InlineMath math="8\%" /> in Banyulor, and rose in Sukamaju.
- In thousand hectares, Ciranda and Banyulor each lost <InlineMath math="10" /> thousand ha. Banyulor is only bigger in percent.

# Answer

So the best-supported conclusion is that **the percentage fall in production grows as technical irrigation narrows, and in Ciranda and Banyulor most of that fall comes from shrinking harvested area**.

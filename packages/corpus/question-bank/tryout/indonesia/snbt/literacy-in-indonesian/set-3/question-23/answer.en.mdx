# Given

Paragraph four of the Java railway passage acknowledges that trains soon carried **small traders and laborers** and spurred towns and markets near stations. Yet the author describes those benefits as "not planned as the primary aim, yet still real," and closes by reaffirming that who gained most "is already clear from the direction of its tracks."

# Asked

The statement that best captures what paragraph four concedes, how it affects the author's conclusion, and how the author characterizes those benefits.

# Solution

Verify **all three components together**, because every distractor misses exactly one component.

> Paragraph four concedes real benefits for small traders and laborers, leaves the main conclusion intact, and frames those benefits as unintended yet genuine.

## Checking all three parts across the options

Matching **concession, effect on thesis, and framing of benefits** identifies the sole accurate statement.

| Option | What is conceded | Effect on conclusion | How benefits are framed | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Benefits for small traders and laborers, does not overturn conclusion, unintended yet real | Exact | Exact | Exact | **Correct** |
| Overturns the author's conclusion | Exact | Wrong (ending reaffirms it) | Exact | **Incorrect** |
| Concedes benefits for plantation owners | Wrong (that is paragraph three) | Exact | Exact | **Incorrect** |
| Rejects calling it the start of modernization | Wrong (author says "not wrong") | - | - | **Incorrect** |
| Frames those benefits as the primary aim | Exact | Exact | Wrong ("not planned as the main aim") | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

Paragraph four **acknowledges benefits for small traders and laborers, does not overturn the conclusion about who benefited most, and portrays those benefits as unintended yet still real**.

In the text, an anchor is a number seen or heard first that becomes the starting point of an **estimate**, even though it says nothing about the real value. Check each case against both conditions: there's an estimate, and the number carries no information.

**The painting auction: an anchor**. The question "more or less than Rp<InlineMath math="50{,}000{,}000" />" has the same form as the questions in the wheel and honey experiments. The number says nothing about the painting's value, yet the bids rise with it.

**The tower's height: an anchor**. The students have to estimate the tower's height, and <InlineMath math="900" /> or <InlineMath math="90" /> metres is named first in a "more or less" question. Their guesses follow the number.

**The tailor friend: not an anchor**. Here Rp<InlineMath math="600{,}000" /> is worked out from the cost of fabric and sewing, so the number does carry information about the jacket's value. Following it is using information, not being pulled by an anchor.

**Two identical shoes: not an anchor**. The buyer isn't estimating any value, only comparing two prices for the same item.

**The market receipt: not an anchor**. The receipt is evidence of a real price elsewhere. The seller adjusts the price because of new information.

So the number works as an anchor in the painting auction and the tower's height.

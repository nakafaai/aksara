# Given

**Text B's author** compares blaming borrowers' literacy alone to "blaming a pedestrian for not seeing a pit deliberately covered with leaves." In the next paragraph, borrowers face **steep borrowing costs** whose figures are **scattered across screens and shown in full only after approval**.

# Asked

The accurate correspondence for "pedestrian," "pit," and "leaves" in Text B's metaphor.

# Solution

Map **all three metaphorical parts** directly onto the actors and mechanisms described in Text B.

> The pedestrian is the borrower being blamed, the pit is the heavy loan cost, and the covering leaves are the scattered cost disclosures revealed completely only after approval.

## Mapping all three parts of the metaphor

Pairing **each metaphor term with its exact role** rules out every swapped option.

| Metaphor term | Exact match in Text B | Why distractors fail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pedestrian | **Borrower** | Not the lending app (the app places the leaves) |
| Pit | **Steep loan cost** | Not government regulation |
| Leaves | **Scattered cost display shown fully only after approval** | Not low literacy and not the one-page disclosure rule |

# Answer

The accurate match is **pedestrian = borrower, pit = steep borrowing cost, and leaves = cost details scattered across screens and shown completely only after approval**.

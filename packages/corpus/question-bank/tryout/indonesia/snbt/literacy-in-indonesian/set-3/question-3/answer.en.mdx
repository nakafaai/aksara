The last paragraph describes almost the same situation: an old pond whose embankment is opened so the tide flows again **can be colonised naturally by mangrove seedlings from nearby trees**. In this question the pond lies next to a mangrove forest, so parent trees are available. The most likely result is natural regrowth.

- Carbon escapes when soil is drained. Letting the tide back in wets the soil rather than drying it.
- Paragraph four criticises dense single-species planting as an example of programmes that failed.
- The passage never says the tide will drown the nearby forest. Mangroves live in tidal flooding.
- Experts stress that the key to recovery is **not the number of seedlings** but the flow of water, and planting is needed mainly where no parent trees remain.

So the most likely result is that mangrove seedlings from nearby trees grow naturally in the old pond.

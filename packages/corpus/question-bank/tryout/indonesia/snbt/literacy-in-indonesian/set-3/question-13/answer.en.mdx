# Given

**Text B** doubts Text A's survey of <InlineMath math="300" /> defaulting borrowers (two-thirds missed a basic interest question) because it lacks a **comparison group** of borrowers who repay on time.

# Asked

The finding that, if true, best defends Text A's conclusion against Text B's objection.

# Solution

To answer Text B directly, the defending finding must **supply the missing comparison group (on-time borrowers)** and show that far fewer of them miss the same interest question.

> If only one-tenth of on-time borrowers miss the same question (versus two-thirds of defaulters), weak understanding of interest is genuinely concentrated among defaulters.

## Evaluating the five candidate findings

Testing **whether each option supplies a contrasting comparison group** isolates the sole direct defense.

| Candidate finding | Adds on-time comparison group | Strengthens Text A | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Only one-tenth of on-time payers fail the same interest question | Yes | Yes (sharp contrast: <InlineMath math="10\%" /> vs <InlineMath math="67\%" />) | **Correct** |
| Borrowers take new loans to pay old ones | No | Offers a competing cause | **Incorrect** |
| Same survey of defaulters in another city | No (still no baseline) | Leaves B's objection intact | **Incorrect** |
| Expanding defaulter sample to <InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> | No (still defaulters only) | Leaves B's objection intact | **Incorrect** |
| Redesigning the app screen | Unrelated to the survey | Matches Text B's proposal | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The strongest defense is the finding that **among borrowers who repay on time, only one-tenth fail to answer the same simple interest question**.

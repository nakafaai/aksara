First understand **Text B's objection**: the survey **"hanya bertanya kepada peminjam yang sudah gagal bayar"** (only asked borrowers who had already defaulted), so nobody knows whether borrowers who pay on time can't answer the interest question either. The survey has **no comparison group**.

A finding that defends A has to supply that comparison **and** show a different result. If only a tenth of on-time borrowers can't answer, against two thirds of defaulters, then poor understanding of interest really is more common among those who default. Text A's conclusion gets stronger.

The other options don't defend A.

- Borrowing to cover loans in other apps points to a **different** cause of default, piling debt. It doesn't defend A, it offers a rival explanation.
- A survey in another city still asks only defaulters. There's still no comparison.
- Raising the sample to <InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> fixes nothing as long as they're all defaulters.
- Changing the app's display is Text B's proposal. It doesn't touch the survey at all.

So the best answer is that among borrowers who pay on time, only a tenth can't answer the same interest question.

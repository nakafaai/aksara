A's conclusion rests on the survey: two thirds of borrowers **who defaulted** couldn't answer a question about interest. Text B points out the gap: the survey has no **comparison**, that is, borrowers who pay on time.

If it turned out that two thirds of on-time borrowers also couldn't answer the same question, a poor grasp of interest would show up **equally in both groups**. A trait shared equally by those who default and those who pay on time can't explain why one group defaults. A's conclusion becomes very weak.

The other findings don't touch the core of A's conclusion.

- Low, irregular income could be another cause, but this finding doesn't show that understanding interest plays **no** role. Both causes can be present together.
- More apps explain why there are more loans, not why some borrowers default.
- Borrowing to pay off another loan could itself result from not understanding interest, so this finding actually fits A.
- Never learning about interest at school supports A, since it strengthens the suspicion of poor understanding.

So the finding that most weakens A's conclusion is that among on-time borrowers, two thirds also can't answer the same interest question.

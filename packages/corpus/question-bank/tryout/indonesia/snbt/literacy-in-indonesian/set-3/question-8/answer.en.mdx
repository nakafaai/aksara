# Given

On the final trip before the bus route closes, **Bu Tarmi** gazes at Warsito's credit page, tears it out, slips it into his shirt pocket, pushes his cash back, and talks with him about "nothing except the price of chili peppers."

# Asked

All interpretations supported by the roadside-eatery scene.

# Solution

Read **the unspoken gestures** within the context of Warsito's last night on the Wonogiri-Jakarta route.

> Tearing out the tab and pushing back the cash forgives the debt as an unspoken farewell, while chatting about chili prices lets both of them avoid painful goodbyes.

## Testing all five interpretations of the scene

Matching **each claim to Bu Tarmi's exact actions** isolates the two supported readings.

| Interpretation | Evidence in the scene | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bu Tarmi erases the debt as an unspoken farewell | Tears out the page, tucks it into Warsito's pocket, and pushes his money back | **Supported** |
| Talking about chili prices helps both avoid saying goodbye | Immediately after a weighty gesture, they stick to trivial small talk | **Supported** |
| The eatery is losing money | False, never stated and clashes with refusing cash | **Unsupported** |
| Bu Tarmi keeps the tab to collect later | False, she hands the torn page directly to Warsito | **Unsupported** |
| Their relationship is purely commercial | False, an ordinary merchant does not tear up a tab and refuse payment | **Unsupported** |

# Answer

The story supports that **Bu Tarmi forgives Warsito's debt as an unspoken farewell** and that **their talk about chili prices serves to avoid saying goodbye aloud**.

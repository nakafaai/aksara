# Given

At eight sharp, **seat number three**, usually taken at the last minute by an elderly woman in a blue headscarf, remains empty even though she said goodbye last Thursday. Warsito walks to the stall for peanuts he never opens, brushes off Ardi's reminder by saying "The terminal clock runs fast," and waits until twelve past eight to release the handbrake.

# Asked

The interpretation of Warsito's excuse and delayed departure best supported by the story.

# Solution

Connect the **excuse about the fast clock** to the preceding paragraph about **seat number three** and the elderly passenger's long-standing last-minute habit.

> Warsito invents an excuse to stall departure because part of him is still waiting for the regular last-minute passenger in the blue headscarf.

## Testing each interpretation against the scene

Comparing **the motive behind the delay** singles out the interpretation anchored in the story.

| Interpretation | Evidence in the story | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Makes an excuse to stall because part of him still waits for the elderly woman who usually boards last | Links directly to empty seat three and the unopened peanuts | **Correct** |
| Waits for unbooked passengers still buying tickets | Unsupported, only eleven seats were sold and no late buyers appear | **Incorrect** |
| Truly believes the terminal clock is fast compared to his watch | Unsupported, no watch of Warsito's is ever mentioned | **Incorrect** |
| Delays to spite Ardi over Pemalang | Unsupported, no link is drawn between Pemalang and the delay | **Incorrect** |
| Wants to arrive in Jakarta during empty traffic | Unsupported, never mentioned in the story | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The best-supported interpretation is that **Warsito makes an excuse to delay departure because part of him is still waiting for the elderly woman in the blue headscarf who used to board at the last minute**.

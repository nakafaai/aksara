Warsito's reply has to be read together with what happens **just before it**, not on its own.

- Every night, an old woman boards **"pada menit terakhir"** (at the last minute) and sits in seat three. Warsito knows she said goodbye last Thursday and will now live in Bekasi for good.
- **"Pukul delapan tepat, kursi nomor tiga kosong"** (At eight sharp, seat three was empty). Warsito then gets off, walks to the stall, and comes back with a packet of peanuts **"yang tidak ia buka"** (that he didn't open). He's filling time, not hungry.
- Only then does he say the terminal clock is fast, and the bus leaves twelve minutes late.

So "the terminal clock is fast" is an excuse to delay. Part of him is still waiting for the habit of past nights, the passenger in seat three, even though he knows she won't come. The delay also fits the last night of a route he has driven for twenty-three years.

The other options are wrong.

- The first option's first part is right, but its reason is made up. The story mentions no passenger who hasn't bought a ticket.
- Nothing shows he truly believes the clock is wrong. The unopened peanuts and the empty seat make more sense as reasons for the delay.
- There's no sign he's annoyed or wants to tease Ardi. At the end he holds Ardi's hand and gives him advice.
- The story doesn't say he wants to arrive at a quiet hour. He drives slowly the whole way.

So the best-supported reading is that Warsito is looking for an excuse to delay, because part of him is still waiting for the seat-three passenger he knows won't come.

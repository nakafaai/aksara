# Given

An official in another city makes **two assumptions at once**: protection starts the moment mosquitoes are released, and Wolbachia can replace breeding-site removal immediately. Yet the passage states that protection can be expected only once the Wolbachia proportion becomes high, and whether Wolbachia can replace breeding-site control was not tested by AWED.

# Asked

The pair of textual rebuttals that accurately counters both assumptions together.

# Solution

Match the **paragraph-two caveat on timing of protection** with the **paragraph-six caveat on replacing breeding-site control**.

> Protection can be expected only after Wolbachia mosquitoes reach a high proportion, and replacing breeding-site control was never tested by the AWED trial design.

## Checking both halves of each rebuttal pair

Testing **every option against exact passage claims** removes all four flawed pairs.

| Rebuttal pair | Counters timing assumption | Counters replacement assumption | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protection waits until Wolbachia proportion is high, and replacing breeding-site control was not tested by AWED | Yes (paragraph two) | Yes (paragraph six) | **Correct** |
| Claims Wolbachia wipes out local mosquitoes | Wrong (mosquitoes are released and mate) | - | **Incorrect** |
| Claims AWED proved both methods must be paired | - | Wrong (passage only says replacement was untested) | **Incorrect** |
| Claims AWED efficacy is overstated | Wrong (more likely understated) | - | **Incorrect** |
| Claims endless re-releases are required | Wrong (once dominant, it persists without re-release) | - | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The accurate pair of rebuttals is that **protection can be expected only after the proportion of Wolbachia mosquitoes becomes high, and whether Wolbachia can replace breeding-site control was not tested by the AWED design**.

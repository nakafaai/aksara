# Given

Paragraph four of the AWED trial passage explains that comparing the **proportion of fever patients testing dengue-positive** resists differences in care-seeking habits, because care-seeking shifts positive and negative fever patients together. Suppose a new clinic opens only in **control clusters**, so residents there visit more often for any mild fever.

# Asked

The effect of the new control-cluster clinic on a raw case-count comparison versus a positive-proportion comparison.

# Solution

Apply **paragraph four's logic** in reverse to increased clinic visits inside control clusters for each of the two metrics.

> More clinic visits in control clusters raise recorded dengue counts there, making raw case counts exaggerate the release's benefit, while positive proportions stay largely stable because dengue-positive and dengue-negative visits rise together.

## Comparing the impact on both metrics

Testing **raw counts against test-positive proportions** isolates the exact outcome.

| Claimed effect | Check under AWED design logic | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Case counts overstate the benefit, while positive proportions stay relatively safe | Control counts rise to widen the gap, yet positive and negative visits rise together | **Correct** |
| Positive proportion rises in control clusters | Wrong, negative fever visits rise alongside positive ones | **Incorrect** |
| Case counts understate the benefit | Reversed, higher control counts make the benefit look bigger | **Incorrect** |
| Positive proportions break down as well | Wrong, proportions were specifically designed to resist care-seeking shifts | **Incorrect** |
| Initial randomization protects raw case counts | Wrong, a clinic opened afterward still distorts raw counts | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The new clinic **causes a raw case-count comparison to overstate the benefit of Wolbachia release, while the comparison of positive-patient proportions remains relatively unaffected**.

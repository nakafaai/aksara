Paragraph two compares two verbs. For the homeland and the nation, the youths **mengaku** (declare) oneness. For language, they do not declare a single language but **menjunjung** (uphold) the language of unity. The author then explains the effect: Indonesian becomes the shared language **without requiring** the youths to give up their regional languages. *Menjunjung* marks Indonesian as a common language, not a replacement for regional ones.

- The author stresses that the youths were not required to abandon regional languages.
- Indonesian became the state language only in the 1945 Constitution, not in the 1928 pledge.
- *Menjunjung* means honouring and raising up the language of unity, not placing regional languages above it.
- The pledge itself records agreement to uphold the language of unity.

So *menjunjung* means that Indonesian became a shared language without erasing regional languages.

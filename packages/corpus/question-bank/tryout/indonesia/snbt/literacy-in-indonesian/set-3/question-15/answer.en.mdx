# Given

**Text A and** Text B debate online loans, residents' financial literacy, total-cost disclosure, and how daily interest compounds over a month.

# Asked

Which statements both the writer of Text A and the writer of Text B agree with.

# Solution

> Verify that both texts actually back the point, if only one writer proposes or accepts it, the answer is immediately No.

The key word is **sekaligus** (both at once). A statement goes in Ya (Yes) only if **both** writers agree with it.

- **Statement 1: Ya**. Text A centres its proposal on financial education. Text B writes, **"Edukasi tetap berguna, tetapi tidak cukup."** (Education is still useful, but not enough.)
- **Statement 2: Tidak (No)**. Text A uses the survey as evidence of the root cause, but Text B says it **"perlu dibaca hati-hati"** (needs careful reading) because it has no comparison group.
- **Statement 3: Tidak**. That's **Text B's** proposal alone. Text A asks for no new rules and even says there are **"sudah banyak"** (already many).
- **Statement 4: Ya**. Text A writes that <InlineMath math="0.4\%" /> interest **"terdengar kecil, padahal dalam sebulan nilainya jauh lebih besar"** (sounds small, though over a month it's far bigger). Text B works it out: that interest comes to Rp<InlineMath math="120{,}000" /> over <InlineMath math="30" /> days.
- **Statement 5: Tidak**. Text A treats every figure as **"terbuka bagi siapa pun yang mau membaca"** (open to anyone willing to read), but Text B says the figures are **"baru terlihat lengkap setelah pinjaman disetujui"** (only seen in full after approval). They disagree.

# Answer

The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Residents' understanding is still useful | Ya |
| The survey is enough to show the root cause | Tidak |
| Require the total cost on one page | Tidak |
| Daily interest is far bigger over a month | Ya |
| Costs are fully visible before approval | Tidak |

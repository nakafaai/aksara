A statement goes in the Ya (Yes) column only if **both** writers agree with it. Watch for statements held by just one side.

The statement that many borrowers struggle to repay has both writers' agreement. A talks about residents who are **"terjerat"** (trapped) and a survey of borrowers who defaulted. B talks about borrowers who have to repay far more than they received, and also discusses borrowers who defaulted.

The statement that oversight should be tightened is held only by B. A writes **"aturan tentang pinjaman daring sudah banyak"** (there are already plenty of rules on online loans), and what's missing is residents' understanding. It's B who asks for **"pengawasan yang benar-benar menindak"** (oversight that really takes action).

The statement that residents' understanding of costs is still useful has both writers' agreement. For A, that understanding is the main key. B also writes, **"Edukasi tetap berguna, tetapi tidak cukup"** (Education is still useful, but not enough). *Still useful* isn't the same as *the root of the problem*, so B agrees with this statement.

The statement that low literacy is the root of the problem is held only by A. A says the survey shows **"akar masalahnya"** (the root of the problem), while B refuses to put the whole problem on residents' literacy.

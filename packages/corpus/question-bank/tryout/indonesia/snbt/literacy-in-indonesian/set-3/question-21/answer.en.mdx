# Given

The Java railway passage concludes that **who benefited most from the first line "is already clear from the direction of its tracks"** (linking inland plantations to Semarang port for export).

# Asked

The pair of finding and reason that most effectively weakens the author's conclusion.

# Solution

The author's inference rests on one bridge: **track direction reveals who uses and gains from the line most**. A strong counter-pair breaks that bridge and states accurately why.

> If most traffic in the early years consisted of ordinary passengers, then laying tracks toward plantation regions no longer shows who used the railway most.

## Checking both halves of each candidate pair

Evaluating **both the finding and its attached reason** eliminates all four flawed pairs.

| Finding and reason pair | Breaks "track direction = biggest beneficiary" | Reason is valid | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Most early traffic was ordinary passengers, because track direction does not reflect who uses it most | Yes | Yes | **Correct** |
| Ordinary passenger traffic, because it proves who built the line | Yes | Wrong (the private builder is already stated) | **Incorrect** |
| Growth of towns along the track | No (already conceded in paragraph four) | Does not weaken | **Incorrect** |
| High export prices in Europe | Actually boosts plantation gains | Contradicts the finding | **Incorrect** |
| Identity of the builder | Does not address who gained most from traffic | Irrelevant | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The strongest pair is **the finding that most traffic in the early years consisted of ordinary passengers, because the direction of the tracks does not reflect who used the line most**.

The line only makes sense once you link it to **the old driver's advice** and to **Ardi's future**.

- **The old driver's advice**. "Penumpang tidak beli cepat, To. Mereka beli sampai" (Passengers don't buy speed, To. They buy arriving). Passengers pay to be taken safely, not to be rushed.
- **Ardi's future**. Ardi will work at a travel agency, where **"telat segini bisa dipotong gaji"** (being this late can get your pay docked). His job will demand speed, all the more on a straight, easy toll road.

Warsito passes on the heart of the old advice in a form that fits Ardi's situation. A straight road and pressure for speed mustn't make Ardi forget that what he carries are **people**, like the old woman in seat three or Bu Tarmi waiting every week. Warsito hands Ardi what was once handed to him.

The other options are wrong.

- Warsito doesn't say toll roads are dangerous, and he doesn't hold Ardi back from the travel agency. He pats his shoulder.
- The third option's first part is right, but its content is the opposite of the advice. Being on time to protect your pay is the travel agency's logic, not the logic of "your passengers are still people".
- The line doesn't blame anyone. Warsito never mentions why the route was closed.
- The route closes next week, so there's no night bus for Ardi to return to. The line doesn't mention boredom either.

So Warsito passes on the old driver's advice in a new form: however fast and easy the road, passengers must still be treated as people.

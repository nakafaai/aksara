# Given

The author's evaluation of Java's first railway spans the passage: calling it the start of modernization is **"not wrong,"** benefits for small traders and laborers were real though unintended, the largest gains went to plantation owners and trading firms, and the original <InlineMath math="1{,}435\text{ mm}" /> gauge was later replaced by <InlineMath math="1{,}067\text{ mm}" />.

# Asked

All statements that match the author's assessment of Java's first railway line.

# Solution

Check each claim against **specific sentences across the passage** to separate the three matching assessments from two direct contradictions.

> The author accepts the line as a start of modernization with real side benefits for local residents while maintaining that plantation owners and trading companies captured the largest gains.

## Checking all five statements against the passage

Mapping **each statement to its paragraph evidence** yields the three supported choices.

| Statement | Passage evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marked the start of Java's modernization | Paragraph one ("not wrong") and closing ("is the start of modernization") | **Matches** |
| Benefits for small traders and laborers were real though not the primary aim | Paragraph four ("not planned as the main aim, yet still real") | **Matches** |
| Biggest gains went to plantation owners and trading firms | Paragraph three ("the largest profit fell into their hands") | **Matches** |
| Designed mainly for residents' travel | False, it "followed exported goods, not residents' travel" | **Does not match** |
| Its original track gauge became Java's standard | False, Java standardized on <InlineMath math="1{,}067\text{ mm}" />, replacing <InlineMath math="1{,}435\text{ mm}" /> | **Does not match** |

# Answer

The matching statements are that **the line was the start of Java's modernization**, that **its benefits for small traders and laborers were real even though not the primary aim**, and that **its largest gains went to plantation owners and trading companies**.

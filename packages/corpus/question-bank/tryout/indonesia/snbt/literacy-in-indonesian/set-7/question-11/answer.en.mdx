# Given

**Text A and** Text B report counts of <InlineMath math="5" />-gram thin shopping bags and <InlineMath math="20" />-gram rolled bin bags in 2022 and 2023.

# Asked

The percentage drop in total plastic weight from both bag types between 2022 and 2023.

# Solution

> One thick bin bag weighs four times as much as a thin bag, so convert all bag counts into tonnes before finding the percentage drop.

Text B asks for the **weight** of plastic to be measured, with bin bags included. A million bags at <InlineMath math="5" /> grams is <InlineMath math="5" /> tonnes, and a million bags at <InlineMath math="20" /> grams is <InlineMath math="20" /> tonnes.

| Type | Weight 2022 | Weight 2023 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Thin bags | <InlineMath math="30\times5=150" /> t | <InlineMath math="6\times5=30" /> t |
| Bin bags | <InlineMath math="2\times20=40" /> t | <InlineMath math="5\times20=100" /> t |
| Total | <InlineMath math="190" /> t | <InlineMath math="130" /> t |

The fall is <InlineMath math="190-130=60" /> tonnes. As a percentage of the starting weight, <InlineMath math="\frac{60}{190}\times100\%\approx32\%" />.

The other options are wrong.

- <InlineMath math="80\%" /> counts only thin bags. The bin bags, whose weight rose from <InlineMath math="40" /> to <InlineMath math="100" /> tonnes, are forgotten.
- About <InlineMath math="66\%" /> is the fall in the number of all bags, from <InlineMath math="32" /> million to <InlineMath math="11" /> million. Number isn't weight, because a bin bag is four times heavier.
- About <InlineMath math="46\%" /> is <InlineMath math="60:130" />. A percentage fall has to be divided by the starting weight, <InlineMath math="190" /> tonnes.
- About <InlineMath math="68\%" /> uses <InlineMath math="130" /> tonnes as the size of the fall, but <InlineMath math="130" /> tonnes is the weight after the levy.

# Answer

So the weight of plastic fell by about <InlineMath math="32\%" />, from <InlineMath math="190" /> tonnes to <InlineMath math="130" /> tonnes.

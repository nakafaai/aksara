# Given

Early in the poem, the speaker recalls Father "reading the sea" from sky color and mud scent with **childhood admiration**. At the close, Father's phone reply ("You just haven't learned to read them yet") plus the poet's note ("It took me a long time to understand Father's answer") lead the speaker to **self-awareness** of still being unable to read life's tides.

# Asked

How the speaker's attitude toward Father develops across the poem, and which moment best marks that shift.

# Solution

Contrast **childhood awe at the start** with **reflective self-awareness at the end**, anchored at Father's phone reply.

> The speaker moves from childhood admiration to recognizing their own inability to read life's "tides," marked directly by Father's reply on the phone.

## Checking the five candidate trajectories

Testing **both endpoints and the turning point** eliminates all four inaccurate readings.

| Candidate trajectory | Fit with poem and poet's note | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Childhood admiration matures into awareness of not yet reading life's "tides," marked by Father's phone reply | Matches opening, ending, and poet's note | **Correct** |
| Turns into disappointment that Father stopped sailing | False, rusted knees are stated without disappointment | **Incorrect** |
| Grows bored with tide schedules | False, the phone clock highlights what is missing in the city | **Incorrect** |
| Doubts Father's wisdom by saying there is no sea | False, that remark is literal and followed by deep reflection | **Incorrect** |
| Understands Father's lesson from the start | False, took a long time to understand | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The speaker's attitude develops **from childhood admiration into awareness that they have not yet learned to read the "tides" of their own life, marked by Father's reply over the phone**.

# Given

**The text and** table compare five-minute recall, confidence estimates, and one-week test scores across three study strategies.

# Asked

Which statements can be concluded accurately from the experiment table and text.

# Solution

> Strategies that feel easiest after five minutes often fade fastest after a week, so keep the five-minute and one-week columns distinct.

Three statements need a careful read of the table, the other two need matching to the text.

The statement about the recall-once group can be concluded. At one week, <InlineMath math="56\%" /> beats <InlineMath math="40\%" />. At five minutes, <InlineMath math="78\%" /> is below <InlineMath math="83\%" />.

The statement about the lowest estimate can't be concluded. The read-four-times group gave the **"perkiraan paling tinggi"** (highest estimate).

The statement about <InlineMath math="21" /> points can be concluded. <InlineMath math="61\%-40\%=21" /> points.

The statement about recall without feedback can't be concluded. The text says recall practice is **"paling berguna jika diikuti umpan balik"** (most useful with feedback), and **"Tanpa umpan balik, jawaban yang keliru dapat ikut teringat"** (without feedback, wrong answers can stick too).

The statement that more recall means more remembered at five minutes can't be concluded. At five minutes the order is reversed: <InlineMath math="83\%" /> with no recall, <InlineMath math="78\%" /> with one, and <InlineMath math="71\%" /> with three.

# Answer

**The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Recall-once leads only at one week | Ya |
| The read-four-times group estimated lowest | Tidak |
| A <InlineMath math="21" />-point gap at one week | Ya |
| Recall without feedback is always better | Tidak |
| More recall, higher five-minute score | Tidak |

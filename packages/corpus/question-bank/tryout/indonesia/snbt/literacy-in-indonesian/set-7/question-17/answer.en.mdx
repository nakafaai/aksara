Under the town's rule, panel power **flowing into the grid does not reduce the bill**. Only panel power **used directly** reduces it, because that electricity need not be bought. House S produced 470 kWh but used only 230 kWh of it directly. Moving power-hungry appliances to the daytime means more panel power is used at once and less is bought at night. That is also the cooperative's advice.

- Adding panels only adds power flowing into the grid, which does not cut the bill.
- Switching panels off would force the house to buy more electricity.
- Running the pump at night adds to purchases from the grid.
- Selling panels reduces the electricity House S produces for itself.

So the step most likely to cut the bill is moving power-hungry appliances from night to daytime.

To measure the effect of one change, look for the part of the data that had **only** that change.

- Corridors A and B had two changes at once: a lower fare and more frequent buses.
- Corridors C and D had only the lower fare, because their frequency didn't change.

So the rise in Corridors C and D is the right data to use. Both rose <InlineMath math="10\%" />: from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="13.2" /> and from <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="6.6" /> thousand passengers.

The other options are wrong.

- The total rise mixes corridors that got only the cheaper fare with corridors that also got more frequent buses.
- Corridor A had two changes at once. Its passenger count after the change doesn't separate them.
- Corridors A and B had the same changes, so the difference between them doesn't show the fare's effect.
- Corridor B also had two changes at once. Having few passengers before the change doesn't alter that.

So the most suitable data are the rises in Corridors C and D, because only the fare changed there.

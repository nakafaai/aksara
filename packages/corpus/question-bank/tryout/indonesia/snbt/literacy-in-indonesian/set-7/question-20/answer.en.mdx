# Given

In the **city bus dataset**, Corridors C and D (fare cut alone) rose <InlineMath math="10\%" /> while Corridors A and B (fare cut plus more frequent buses) rose <InlineMath math="50\%" /> to <InlineMath math="60\%" />. Daily revenue fell from Rp<InlineMath math="180" /> million to Rp<InlineMath math="143.4" /> million because fares fell <InlineMath math="40\%" /> while riders rose only about <InlineMath math="33\%" />, and post-change data cover only November 2024.

# Asked

All conclusions supported by the city-bus passage and table.

# Solution

Verify **each conclusion and its qualifiers** against the table (*likely* and *can* fit the evidence, whereas *certainly* and *at all* clash with it).

> Three conclusions hold: more frequent buses likely drove most of A and B's surge, revenue fell despite more riders, and cutting fares to Rp<InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> could cut revenue even further.

## Checking all five conclusions against the data

Matching **each option to table numbers** selects the three supported claims.

| Conclusion | Table and text evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More frequent buses likely drove most of A and B's gain | A and B jumped <InlineMath math="50\%" /> to <InlineMath math="60\%" />, versus <InlineMath math="10\%" /> on C and D | **Supported** |
| Ticket revenue fell even as ridership grew | Daily revenue dropped from Rp<InlineMath math="180" /> million to Rp<InlineMath math="143.4" /> million | **Supported** |
| A Rp<InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> fare could lower revenue further if ridership rises only slightly | Cutting fares by a third outweighs a <InlineMath math="10\%" /> rider gain | **Supported** |
| The ridership jump will certainly last | False, post-change data span only November 2024 | **Unsupported** |
| Fares had no effect at all on Corridors C and D | False, C and D rose <InlineMath math="10\%" /> from fares alone | **Unsupported** |

# Answer

The supported conclusions are that **more frequent buses likely contributed most of the gain on Corridors A and B**, that **ticket revenue fell despite higher ridership**, and that **a Rp<InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> fare can reduce revenue even further if ridership grows only slightly**.

# Given

Total city-bus ridership rose from **<InlineMath math="36" /> thousand** to **<InlineMath math="47.8" /> thousand** (up <InlineMath math="11.8" /> thousand, nearly one-third), and the mayor credited the entire gain to lower fares. Yet on Corridors C and D, which received **only** the fare cut, ridership grew by **<InlineMath math="10\%" />**.

# Asked

The calculation that best exposes the flaw in the mayor's claim about the fare cut.

# Solution

Apply the **fare-only growth rate (<InlineMath math="10\%" />)** across the <InlineMath math="36" />-thousand baseline to separate the fare share from the <InlineMath math="11.8" />-thousand total gain.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{fare-only estimate} = 36 \times 10\% = 3.6\text{ thousand}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{actual increase} = 47.8 - 36 = 11.8\text{ thousand}" />
</MathContainer>

> Assuming A and B also rose <InlineMath math="10\%" /> from lower fares alone, the fare cut explains roughly <InlineMath math="3.6" /> of the <InlineMath math="11.8" /> thousand new riders (under one-third of the gain).

## Checking the five candidate calculations

Testing **which calculation isolates fare impact from bus frequency** leaves one clear answer.

| Calculation | Isolates fare effect from frequency | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assuming A and B rose <InlineMath math="10\%" />, so fares explain about <InlineMath math="3.6" /> of <InlineMath math="11.8" /> thousand added riders | Yes, shows fares explain less than a third of the surge | **Correct** |
| Computing the <InlineMath math="33\%" /> overall rise | Merely repeats the mayor's unseparated claim | **Incorrect** |
| Using Corridor A's <InlineMath math="60\%" /> jump as fare impact | Wrong, Corridor A also got more frequent buses | **Incorrect** |
| Crediting all of A and B's rise to fares | Wrong, A and B received both changes together | **Incorrect** |
| Citing lower revenue to deny ridership grew | Wrong, ridership grew even as per-ticket price fell | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The best calculation is **assuming Corridors A and B rose by only <InlineMath math="10\%" />, so the fare cut alone explains roughly <InlineMath math="3.6" /> of the <InlineMath math="11.8" /> thousand additional riders**.

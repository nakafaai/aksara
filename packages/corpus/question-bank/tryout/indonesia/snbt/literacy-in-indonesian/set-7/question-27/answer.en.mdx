# Given

In the retrieval-practice study, the **read-four-times** group scored <InlineMath math="83\%" /> after five minutes and <InlineMath math="40\%" /> after one week, while the **read-three-times, recall-once** group scored <InlineMath math="78\%" /> after five minutes and <InlineMath math="56\%" /> after one week, with both spending four total sessions.

# Asked

The accurate comparison of those two groups and its takeaway for a student facing an exam in one week.

# Solution

Compute **the percentage-point gap on both tests** and apply the delayed-test result to an exam one week away.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{five-minute test} : 83\% - 78\% = 5\text{ points (read-4 ahead)}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{one-week test} : 56\% - 40\% = 16\text{ points (read-4 behind)}" />
</MathContainer>

> For an exam a week away, swapping the final rereading session for a single recall session produces far stronger retention (<InlineMath math="56\%" /> vs <InlineMath math="40\%" />).

The question asks for a finding that hits only one explanation, the click as a warning signal. So you need to know what the warning explanation predicts, then find a finding that doesn't fit that prediction but still fits the startle effect and jamming.

Paragraph 5 says such a warning is **"hanya berguna setelah pemangsa belajar dari pengalaman mencicipinya"** (only useful after the predator has learned from tasting it). So a bat that has never eaten this moth has no reason yet to avoid it. If the click really were a warning, this new bat should still catch and eat some moths first.

The finding that bats which **have never tasted the moth** miss from their very first attack clashes with that prediction. The startle effect and jamming need no learning. Paragraph 5 even says jamming **"bekerja sejak serangan pertama"** (works from the first attack). So those two explanations stay safe.

The other options fail for these reasons:

- Silent moths being caught more often is true in paragraph 6, but all three explanations predict it, so none is weakened.
- A click whose effect doesn't fade weakens the startle effect, because startle **"akan memudar ketika kelelawar terbiasa"** (fades as the bat gets used to it). The warning explanation isn't touched.
- A moth that starts clicking while the bat is still far away fits a warning, which is useful when heard before the attack is under way.
- Bats still catching silent moths accurately only shows their sonar is normal. All three explanations agree with this.

The finding that weakens the warning explanation most is that inexperienced bats already fail from their first attack.

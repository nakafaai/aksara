# Given

The passage presents **three explanations for *Bertholdia trigona* clicks**: a bad-taste warning ("useful only after predators learn from tasting it"), startle (fades with habituation), and sonar jamming ("works from the very first attack").

# Asked

The finding that most weakens the bad-taste warning hypothesis without weakening startle or sonar jamming.

# Solution

Look for evidence that **violates the learning requirement of warning signals** while remaining consistent with both startle and jamming, which need no prior tasting experience.

> If naive bats that have never tasted the moth already miss on their very first attack, the warning hypothesis fails while startle and jamming stay intact.

## Testing each finding across all three hypotheses

Mapping **which hypothesis each finding undercuts** isolates the single selective test.

| Finding | Weakens warning hypothesis | Leaves startle and jamming intact | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Naive bats that never tasted the moth already miss on their first attack | Yes (warnings require learned experience) | Yes (neither needs prior tasting) | **Correct** |
| Silenced moths are caught more often | No (predicted by all three) | Yes | **Incorrect** |
| Click effect never fades over repeated trials | No | No (weakens startle instead) | **Incorrect** |
| Moths click while bats are still far away | Actually fits warning signals | - | **Incorrect** |
| Bats accurately catch silenced moths | Merely shows normal sonar | - | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The finding that selectively weakens the warning explanation is **bats with no prior experience tasting the moth already failing on their very first attack**.

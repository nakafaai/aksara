# Given

The popular view pairs a **premise** (moths hear bats from farther away than bats detect moths) with a **conclusion** (moths always win). The author accepts the premise "for most bats," then refutes "always" using the **barbastelle bat**, whose quiet calls shorten its detection range so moths hear it only when it is nearly upon them.

# Asked

The author's stance toward both the premise and the conclusion of that popular view.

# Solution

Separate the author's **acceptance of the premise for most bats** from the **refutation of "always wins" via the quiet barbastelle**.

> The author accepts the hearing-range premise for most bats yet refutes "always wins" through the barbastelle, which trades detection distance to stay unheard.

## Checking the five stance summaries

Verifying **premise, conclusion, and barbastelle trade-off** eliminates all four distortions.

| Summary of stance | Fit with paragraphs three and four | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accepts the hearing-range premise yet refutes "always wins" with the barbastelle trading detection distance for stealth | Exact across all three points | **Correct** |
| Rejects the hearing-range premise | Wrong, the author confirms it holds for most bats | **Incorrect** |
| Accepts both premise and conclusion | Wrong, paragraph four directly refutes "always" | **Incorrect** |
| Claims the barbastelle detects prey from farther away | Reversed, its detection range is short | **Incorrect** |
| Remains neutral without refuting either part | Wrong, "However" and the barbastelle explicitly refute the claim | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The author **accepts the premise about moths' hearing range, yet refutes the "always wins" conclusion using the barbastelle, which trades away detection distance to avoid being heard**.

# Given

**Text A highlights** fewer thin supermarket bags, while Text B adds surging bin-bag sales and argues weight is a better measure.

# Asked

Which statements can be concluded from the figures and stances of both texts.

# Solution

> Text B does not call Text A's count wrong, it shows that counting thin bags alone misses heavier replacement bin bags.

Two statements need a small calculation, the rest need a careful read of Text B's stance.

The statement about <InlineMath math="24" /> million can be concluded. Text A gives <InlineMath math="30" /> million falling to <InlineMath math="6" /> million, a drop of <InlineMath math="30-6=24" /> million. Text B says the <InlineMath math="80\%" /> drop is something it **"tidak saya ragukan"** (doesn't doubt).

The statement that Text B thinks Text A's figures are wrong can't be concluded. Text B says it doesn't doubt them.

The statement about bin bags can be concluded. Sales rose from <InlineMath math="2" /> million to <InlineMath math="5" /> million a year, that's <InlineMath math="5\div2=2.5" /> times, more than double.

The statement about traditional markets can't be concluded. Text A uses only supermarket data, and Text B points out it **"hanya mencakup supermarket"** (covers only supermarkets).

The statement about plastic weight can be concluded. Text B asks **"apa yang sebenarnya turun"** (what actually fell) and asks the city to **"mengukur berat plastik, bukan sekadar jumlah kantong"** (measure plastic weight, not just the number of bags).

# Answer

The table below summarizes the verdict for each statement.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Both texts accept a drop of about <InlineMath math="24" /> million | Ya |
| Text B thinks Text A's figures are wrong | Tidak |
| Bin bags more than doubled | Ya |
| Text A uses traditional market data | Tidak |
| Text B doubts bag numbers as a measure of weight | Ya |

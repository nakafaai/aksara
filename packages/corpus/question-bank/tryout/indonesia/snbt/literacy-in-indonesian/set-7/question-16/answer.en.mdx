# Given

**City bus fares** fell from Rp<InlineMath math="5{,}000" /> in September to Rp<InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> in November 2024, while daily passengers rose from <InlineMath math="36" /> thousand to <InlineMath math="47.8" /> thousand.

# Asked

How weekday bus ticket revenue changed from September to November 2024.

# Solution

> Revenue equals passengers times fare, so multiply out both months first and always divide the change by the starting September revenue.

Ticket revenue is **the number of passengers times the fare**. Both changed, so work out both.

| Month | Passengers | Fare | Revenue per weekday |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| September | <InlineMath math="36{,}000" /> | Rp<InlineMath math="5{,}000" /> | Rp<InlineMath math="180" /> million |
| November | <InlineMath math="47{,}800" /> | Rp<InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> | Rp<InlineMath math="143.4" /> million |

Revenue fell by <InlineMath math="180-143.4=36.6" /> million. As a percentage of September's revenue, <InlineMath math="\frac{36.6}{180}\times100\%\approx20\%" />.

The other options are wrong.

- A rise of about <InlineMath math="33\%" /> is the rise in passengers (<InlineMath math="11.8" /> divided by <InlineMath math="36" />). The lower fare is forgotten.
- A fall of <InlineMath math="40\%" /> is the fall in the fare alone (<InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> divided by <InlineMath math="5{,}000" />). The extra passengers are forgotten.
- The direction is reversed. Passengers rose about <InlineMath math="33\%" />, but the fare fell <InlineMath math="40\%" />, so revenue fell.
- About <InlineMath math="25\%" /> comes from dividing <InlineMath math="36.6" /> by <InlineMath math="143.4" />. A percentage fall has to be divided by the starting revenue, Rp<InlineMath math="180" /> million.

# Answer

So ticket revenue per weekday fell by about <InlineMath math="20\%" />, from Rp<InlineMath math="180" /> million to about Rp<InlineMath math="143" /> million.

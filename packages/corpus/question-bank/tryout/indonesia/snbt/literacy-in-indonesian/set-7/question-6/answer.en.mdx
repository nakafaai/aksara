# Given

In stanza one, "Father **reads** the sea like reading a wall clock" refers to reading sky color and wet-mud scent to know when to sail. At the poem's close, when the speaker says there is no sea in the city, Father answers "Everyone has tides. You just haven't learned to **read** them yet."

# Asked

How the meaning of the word "read" develops from the start to the end of the poem.

# Solution

Compare **what is being read** in stanza one (natural coastal signs) with what is being read at the end (the rising and falling rhythms of one's own life).

> "Reading" expands from recognizing natural signs at sea to know when to act into recognizing the ebbs and flows of life itself.

## Checking the five candidate meanings

Tracking **the shift from sea signs to life's rhythms** rules out all four inaccurate descriptions.

| Candidate shift | Fit with the poem | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expands from reading natural signs for when to act into reading the ebbs and flows of life | Links stanza one and Father's closing reply accurately | **Correct** |
| Shifts from tide tables to phone clocks | False, poet's note says Father "never used tide tables" | **Incorrect** |
| Tells the speaker to watch the phone clock | False, stanza four shows the phone clock cannot tell when to act | **Incorrect** |
| Narrows strictly to ocean tides | Reversed, there is no sea in the city so the meaning widens | **Incorrect** |
| Shifts from baseless guessing to admitting error | False, Father reads real physical signs | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The word "read" evolves **from recognizing natural signs to know when to act in the first stanza into recognizing the rising and falling rhythms of life by the end of the poem**.

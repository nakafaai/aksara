The remark answers the speaker's line **"tidak ada laut di sini, Yah"** (there's no sea here, Dad). So *pasang* (tide) here no longer means rising sea water. It's used figuratively.

Gather the clues.

- For the father, the tide is a rhythm that comes and goes regularly, and family life is **fitted** to it: eating when the water is low, sleeping when it rises.
- In the city the speaker has a clock that's **"tepat sampai detik"** (accurate to the second), but doesn't know when to act.
- The father says the speaker **"belum belajar membacanya"** (hasn't learned to read it yet). What can't be read yet is the rhythm of the speaker's own life, not the sea.

So the remark means every life has a rhythm of rising and falling that has to be recognised, and the speaker hasn't yet recognised the rhythm of life in the city.

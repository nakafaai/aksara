Compare what is "read" in the two places.

- **First stanza**. The father knows when the water comes **"dari warna langit di atas tanjung, dari bau lumpur yang tiba-tiba basah lagi"** (from the colour of the sky over the cape, from the smell of mud suddenly wet again). Reading means recognising signs in nature to know when to set out.
- **End of the poem**. The speaker says **"tidak ada laut di sini"** (there's no sea here), but the father answers **"Semua orang punya pasang"** (everyone has a tide). What's read now isn't the sea but the rise and fall in life. The meaning widens from sea to life.

The other options are wrong.

- The poet's note says **"Ayah tidak pernah memakai tabel pasang"** (my father never used a tide table).
- The father doesn't tell the speaker to watch the phone clock. The fourth stanza shows the phone clock doesn't say when to act.
- There's no sea in the speaker's city, so the meaning isn't narrowed to sea tides.
- The poem never shows the father guessing blindly or admitting his guesses were wrong. He reads real signs.

So "reading" in the first stanza means recognising signs in nature to know when to act, and by the end it widens to recognising the rise and fall in life.

# Given

**The read-four-times group** dropped from <InlineMath math="83" /> percent at five minutes to <InlineMath math="40" /> percent at one week, while the recall-three-times group dropped from <InlineMath math="71" /> percent to <InlineMath math="61" /> percent.

# Asked

What share of its five-minute memory each study group lost after one week.

# Solution

> Do not stop at percentage-point gaps, divide what was forgotten by the five-minute score to see what fraction of initial memory disappeared.

The question asks for the **share of the five-minute memory** that was lost, not the difference in percentage points. So the difference has to be divided by the five-minute figure.

| Group | Difference | Share lost |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Read four times | <InlineMath math="83-40=43" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{43}{83}\approx52\%" /> |
| Read once, recalled three times | <InlineMath math="71-61=10" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{10}{71}\approx14\%" /> |

So the group that read four times lost about half its memory, while the group that recalled three times lost only about a seventh.

The other options are wrong.

- <InlineMath math="43\%" /> and <InlineMath math="10\%" /> are differences in percentage points, not yet divided by the five-minute memory.
- About <InlineMath math="48\%" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{40}{83}" />, the share **left**, not lost. <InlineMath math="86\%" /> is likewise the share left in the other group, <InlineMath math="\frac{61}{71}" />.
- About <InlineMath math="108\%" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{43}{40}" />, divided by the one-week figure. A share lost can't exceed <InlineMath math="100\%" />.
- About <InlineMath math="52\%" /> is right, but <InlineMath math="10\%" /> for the other group is still a point difference.

# Answer

So the group that read four times lost about <InlineMath math="52\%" /> of its five-minute memory, while the group that recalled three times lost about <InlineMath math="14\%" />.

# Given

A hunting bat receives a moth echo **earlier and louder in its right ear**, while **the gap between call and echo grows shorter**. The passage states ear differences reveal direction, call-to-echo delay reveals distance, and approaching prey triggers faster calls ("buzz") because each echo quickly goes stale as the prey turns.

# Asked

What can be inferred about the moth's direction and distance, and how the bat's calls likely change.

# Solution

Map **all three echolocation clues in order** using the exact rules given in the passage.

> An earlier, louder echo in the right ear places the moth to the right, a shorter delay means it is closer, and the bat calls more rapidly because each echo quickly goes stale while the moth turns.

## Checking all three parts across the options

Verifying **direction, distance, and call rate** isolates the only completely accurate option.

| Option | Direction | Distance | Call change | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| To the right, getting closer, calls get more rapid as echoes quickly go stale | Exact | Exact | Exact | **Correct** |
| Moving farther away with sparser calls | Exact | Wrong (shorter gap = closer) | Wrong | **Incorrect** |
| Located on the left side | Wrong (arrived at right ear first) | Exact | Exact | **Incorrect** |
| Louder calls to avoid being heard | Exact | Exact | Wrong (louder calls warn moths) | **Incorrect** |
| Swaps delay for direction and ear difference for distance | Reversed | Reversed | - | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The moth is **to the right and getting closer, and the bat's calls grow more rapid because each echo quickly becomes outdated as the prey keeps turning**.

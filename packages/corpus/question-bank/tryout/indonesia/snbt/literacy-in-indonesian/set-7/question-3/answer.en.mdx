# Given

Flight-room results best match **sonar jamming**: *Bertholdia trigona* floods the bat's ears with **echo-like false clicks** packed tightest **precisely when the bat enters the terminal buzz** and relies most heavily on echoes.

# Asked

The analogous situation that uses the same jamming defense, including its timing.

# Solution

Select the scenario that satisfies **both criteria together**: deploying **false deceptive reflections** to confuse the attacker's sensor and triggering them **at the final stage of attack**.

> A ship releasing metal chaff to create false radar echoes precisely as a missile enters its final approach matches both the jamming mechanism and its exact timing.

## Comparing all five situations against jamming criteria

Testing **mechanism and final-stage timing** separates the exact parallel from the distractors.

| Situation | Confuses sensor with false echoes | Deployed at final attack stage | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ship releases false-echo chaff right at the final stage of missile pursuit | Yes (false radar reflections) | Yes (final pursuit stage) | **Correct** |
| Toxic frog avoided by experienced birds | No (warning signal pattern) | No | **Incorrect** |
| Eyespots making a bird flinch briefly | No (startle pattern) | No | **Incorrect** |
| Predatory fish approaching silently | Attacker stealth (barbastelle), not prey defense | No | **Incorrect** |
| Radio noise broadcast nonstop all day | Yes | No (misses final-stage timing) | **Incorrect** |

# Answer

The situation that best matches sonar jamming is **a ship deploying false reflectors precisely during the final stage of a missile's pursuit**.

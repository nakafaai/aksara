There are two steps. First, find which explanation the text favours. Second, find the situation that copies that explanation's features, including when the defence is used.

Paragraph 6 says the intact moth's clicks were **"paling rapat tepat ketika kelelawar memasuki dengung akhir serangan"** (densest exactly when the bat entered the final buzz of its attack), and that this pattern **"lebih cocok dengan pengacauan"** (fits jamming better). Paragraph 5 describes jamming: the clicks flood the bat's ears **"dengan bunyi yang mirip gema"** (with sounds like echoes) and are most useful **"ketika kelelawar paling bergantung pada gema"** (when the bat depends most on echoes). So two features must be there together: **a false signal that confuses the attacker's sense**, used **in the final stage of the attack**.

Reflective metal chaff makes a missile's radar pick up false reflections. It's released just as the missile enters its final stage, when the missile depends most on its radar. Both features are met.

- A poisonous frog avoided by experienced birds is the warning pattern, which works after the predator has learned.

- A predatory fish moving slowly copies the barbastelle's strategy, an attacker that sneaks up with very weak calls. It's not a prey defence.
- Radio noise does confuse signals, but it's broadcast all day, not concentrated at the decisive moment. The timing feature is missing.

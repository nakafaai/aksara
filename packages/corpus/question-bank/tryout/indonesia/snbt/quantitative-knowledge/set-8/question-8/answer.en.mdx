Measure each hand's position as an angle from 12, clockwise. What is often forgotten is that **the hour hand also moves** during those 40 minutes.

# Positions of the two hands

The minute hand moves <InlineMath math="\tfrac{360^\circ}{60}=6^\circ" /> per minute. The hour hand moves <InlineMath math="\tfrac{360^\circ}{12}=30^\circ" /> per hour, or **<InlineMath math="0.5^\circ" /> per minute**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{minute hand}&:\quad 40\cdot6^\circ=240^\circ\\
\text{hour hand}&:\quad 4\cdot30^\circ+40\cdot0.5^\circ=140^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

# The smaller angle

The difference of the positions is <InlineMath math="240^\circ-140^\circ=100^\circ" />. The angle on the other side is <InlineMath math="360^\circ-100^\circ=260^\circ" />, so **the smaller angle is <InlineMath math="100^\circ" />**.

The angle <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> keeps the hour hand fixed at 4. The angle <InlineMath math="110^\circ" /> uses an hour hand speed of <InlineMath math="0.25^\circ" /> per minute. The angle <InlineMath math="140^\circ" /> is the position of the hour hand, not the difference, and <InlineMath math="260^\circ" /> is the larger angle.

So the smaller angle between the hands is **<InlineMath math="100^\circ" />**.

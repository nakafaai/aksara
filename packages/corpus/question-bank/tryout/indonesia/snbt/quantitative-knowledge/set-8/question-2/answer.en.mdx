The expression <InlineMath math="x^3+y^3" /> can be written with the sum and the product, so it is enough to **find <InlineMath math="xy" /> first**, without finding <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> separately.

# The product from the sum of squares

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x+y)^2&=x^2+y^2+2xy\\
49&=29+2xy\\
xy&=10
\end{aligned}" />

# The sum of cubes

Use **the factorisation of a sum of cubes**, <InlineMath math="x^3+y^3=(x+y)(x^2-xy+y^2)" />.

<BlockMath math="x^3+y^3=7\,(29-10)=7\cdot19=133" />

Check: the two numbers are 2 and 5, and <InlineMath math="8+125=133" />.

The value 203 forgets to subtract <InlineMath math="xy" />, giving <InlineMath math="7\cdot29" />. The value 63 uses <InlineMath math="xy=20" /> because it forgets to halve.

So the value of <InlineMath math="x^3+y^3" /> is **133**.

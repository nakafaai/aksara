Let <InlineMath math="b_1" /> and <InlineMath math="b_2" /> be the numbers of black balls in the two boxes. The first box gives <InlineMath math="b_1=12" />. Because the draws come from different boxes, they are independent:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac{3}{10} &= \frac{b_1}{20}\cdot\frac{b_2}{20} \\ &= \frac{12}{20}\cdot\frac{b_2}{20} \\ b_2 &= 10 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The boxes therefore contain:

- Box <InlineMath math="1" />: <InlineMath math="20 - 12 = 8" /> white balls
- Box <InlineMath math="2" />: <InlineMath math="20 - 10 = 10" /> white balls

Multiplying the two white-ball probabilities gives:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{8}{20}\cdot\frac{10}{20}=\frac{1}{5}" />
</MathContainer>

Since <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{5}" /> is already in lowest terms, <InlineMath math="m=1" /> and <InlineMath math="n=5" />. Hence <InlineMath math="m+n=6" />.

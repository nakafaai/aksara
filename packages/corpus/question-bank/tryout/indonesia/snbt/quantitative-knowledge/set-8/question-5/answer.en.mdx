Let the roots be <InlineMath math="t" /> and <InlineMath math="t^2" />. The sum of the roots is known, so **the sum formula fixes <InlineMath math="t" />**, and then the product of the roots gives <InlineMath math="q" />.

# The root from the sum of roots

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t+t^2&=12\\
t^2+t-12&=0\\
(t+4)(t-3)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

There are **two possibilities**, <InlineMath math="t=3" /> or <InlineMath math="t=-4" />.

# The value of q from the product

| <InlineMath math="t" /> | Roots | <InlineMath math="q=t\cdot t^2" /> |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 3 | 3 and 9 | 27 |
| <InlineMath math="-4" /> | <InlineMath math="-4" /> and 16 | <InlineMath math="-64" /> |

Both pairs do add up to 12, so **both are valid**. The sum of all values of <InlineMath math="q" /> is <InlineMath math="27+(-64)=-37" />.

The values 27 and <InlineMath math="-64" /> use only one possibility. The value 37 has the wrong sign, and <InlineMath math="-91" /> adds the two absolute values with a minus sign.

So the sum of all possible values of <InlineMath math="q" /> is **<InlineMath math="-37" />**.

# Linking the first term and the difference

The <InlineMath math="n" />th term is <InlineMath math="u_n=a+(n-1)b" />. In an arithmetic sequence, a run of consecutive terms adds up to the number of terms times the average of the first and last, because the terms are equally spaced.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
u_1+\dots+u_{10}&=\tfrac{10}{2}\big(a+(a+9b)\big)=10a+45b\\
u_{11}+\dots+u_{15}&=\tfrac{5}{2}\big((a+10b)+(a+14b)\big)=5a+60b\\
10a+45b&=5a+60b\\
a&=3b
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="a\ne0" />, **the difference <InlineMath math="b" /> isn't zero either**. So the terms aren't all zero and the fraction in the question is defined. Every term can now be written with <InlineMath math="b" /> only: <InlineMath math="u_n=3b+(n-1)b=(n+2)b" />.

# The sums of the two groups

Use <InlineMath math="u_n=(n+2)b" /> for the first and last term of each group.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
u_1+\dots+u_{10}&=\tfrac{10}{2}(3b+12b)=75b\\
u_{21}+\dots+u_{30}&=\tfrac{10}{2}(23b+32b)=275b\\
\frac{275b}{75b}&=\frac{11}{3}
\end{aligned}" />

Check the condition: terms <InlineMath math="11" /> to <InlineMath math="15" /> are <InlineMath math="13b" /> to <InlineMath math="17b" />, and they add up to <InlineMath math="\tfrac52(13b+17b)=75b" />. That's **the same as the sum of the first <InlineMath math="10" /> terms**, so <InlineMath math="a=3b" /> is right.

The ant cannot go through the box, so the space diagonal is not its path. The method is **unfolding two faces of the box into one flat plane**, and then the shortest path on that plane is a straight line.

# Three ways to unfold

A path from <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="G" /> crosses two neighbouring faces. When the two faces are unfolded, one edge stays whole and **the other two edges join into one segment**. There are three choices for the edge that stays whole.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{edge 12 whole}&:\quad \sqrt{12^2+(4+3)^2}=\sqrt{144+49}=\sqrt{193}\\
\text{edge 4 whole}&:\quad \sqrt{4^2+(12+3)^2}=\sqrt{16+225}=\sqrt{241}\\
\text{edge 3 whole}&:\quad \sqrt{3^2+(12+4)^2}=\sqrt{9+256}=\sqrt{265}
\end{aligned}" />

# Choosing the shortest

The smallest value is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{193}\approx13.9" />. The shortest path comes when **the longest edge stays whole** and the two short edges are joined.

The length 13 cm is the space diagonal <InlineMath math="\sqrt{144+16+9}" />, which goes through the inside of the box. The length 19 cm walks along the edges <InlineMath math="12+4+3" />. The values <InlineMath math="\sqrt{241}" /> and <InlineMath math="\sqrt{265}" /> come from the other unfoldings.

So the shortest path has length **<InlineMath math="\sqrt{193}\text{ cm}" />**.

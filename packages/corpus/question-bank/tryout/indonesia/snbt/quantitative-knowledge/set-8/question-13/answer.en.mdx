The other solid is the cube without the pyramid <InlineMath math="E.ABD" />. So compute **the volume of the cube minus the volume of the pyramid**.

# Volume of the cut-off pyramid

The base of the pyramid is triangle <InlineMath math="ABD" />, half of the square <InlineMath math="ABCD" />. Its height is the edge <InlineMath math="AE" />, because <InlineMath math="AE" /> **is perpendicular to the base plane**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{ABD}&=\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot6=18\\
V_{\text{pyramid}}&=\tfrac13\cdot18\cdot6=36
\end{aligned}" />

# Volume of the remaining solid

<BlockMath math="V_{\text{rest}}=6^3-36=216-36=180" />

The pyramid **takes one sixth of the cube**, that is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{36}{216}=\tfrac16" />, so the remaining solid is <InlineMath math="\tfrac56" /> of the cube.

The value 36 is the volume of the pyramid, not of the rest. The value 144 appears if the base of the pyramid is taken as the full square, which gives a pyramid of 72. The value 108 assumes the cut splits the cube into two equal halves.

So the volume of the other solid is **<InlineMath math="180\text{ cm}^3" />**.

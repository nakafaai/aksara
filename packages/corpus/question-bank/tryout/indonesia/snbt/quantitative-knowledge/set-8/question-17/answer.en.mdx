The average price of a mixture is **the mean of the prices weighted by the mass** of each kind. So we need the price of each kind and the ratio of their masses.

# Each statement alone

Statement (1) gives both prices but no weights. A mixture of 1 kg of type I and 1 kg of type II has an average price of Rp13,500, while a mixture of 2 kg and 1 kg has an average of Rp13,000. **The result depends on the mass ratio**, so statement (1) alone is not sufficient.

Statement (2) gives the weights but no prices, so the average could be anything. Statement (2) alone is not sufficient.

# Both statements together

Take 2 kg of type I and 1 kg of type II, matching **the mass ratio <InlineMath math="2:1" />**.

<BlockMath math="\bar h=\frac{2\cdot12{,}000+1\cdot15{,}000}{2+1}=\frac{39{,}000}{3}=13{,}000" />

The same ratio, for example 20 kg and 10 kg, gives the same result, so the average price is unique.

So **both statements together are sufficient**, but neither statement alone is.

The interquartile range is **the difference between the upper and lower quartiles**, <InlineMath math="Q_3-Q_1" />. Quartiles can only be read once the data are sorted.

# Sorting and finding the quartiles

<BlockMath math="4,\;7,\;9,\;10,\;13,\;15,\;20" />

There are 7 values, so quartile <InlineMath math="i" /> is the value in position <InlineMath math="\tfrac{i(7+1)}{4}=2i" />. **The lower quartile is the 2nd value** and the upper quartile is the 6th value.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
Q_1&=7,\quad Q_2=10,\quad Q_3=15\\
Q_3-Q_1&=15-7=8
\end{aligned}" />

The same result comes from taking the median of the lowest three values (4, 7, 9) and of the highest three (13, 15, 20).

The value 4 is the semi-interquartile range, half of the interquartile range. The value 16 is the range of all the data, <InlineMath math="20-4" />. The value 3 is the difference between the median and the lower quartile, and 7 is the lower quartile itself.

So the interquartile range of the data is **8**.

You're counting the ways to cover the <InlineMath math="2\times10" /> board with more vertical dominoes than horizontal ones. Here's the plan: first show that **horizontal dominoes always come in pairs**, turn each covering into a row of blocks, then count the cases that meet the condition.

# Horizontal dominoes always come in pairs

Look at the leftmost column that isn't fully covered yet. Suppose its top cell is covered by a horizontal domino, which reaches into this column and the one to its right. The bottom cell of that column can't take a vertical domino, because the top cell is already used. It can't take a horizontal domino reaching left either, because the columns on the left are already full. So the bottom cell is **covered by a horizontal domino on the same two columns**.

That means every covering is a row of two kinds of blocks, from left to right:

- one vertical domino, <InlineMath math="1" /> column wide,
- two stacked horizontal dominoes, <InlineMath math="2" /> columns wide.

Say there are <InlineMath math="k" /> vertical dominoes and <InlineMath math="m" /> horizontal blocks, so there are <InlineMath math="2m" /> horizontal dominoes. In each case there are <InlineMath math="k+m" /> blocks in a row, and you just choose the positions of the <InlineMath math="m" /> horizontal blocks among them.

k=6,\ m=2&:\quad \binom{8}{2}=28\\

The discount and the tax are both percentages, so each can be written as **a multiplier**: a 25% discount means multiplying by 0.75, and a 10% tax means multiplying by 1.1.

# Price after discount and final price

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{after discount}&=200{,}000\times0.75=150{,}000\\
\text{final price}&=150{,}000\times1.1=165{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The tax is computed on **the price after the discount**, not on the original price. The first and third statements are true.

# Effective reduction

The total reduction is <InlineMath math="200{,}000-165{,}000=35{,}000" /> rupiah. Relative to the original price, it is

<BlockMath math="\frac{35{,}000}{200{,}000}\times100\%=17.5\%" />

The figure 15% comes from **simply subtracting the percentages**, <InlineMath math="25\%-10\%" />. That is wrong because the tax is computed on the reduced price, not on the original price. The second statement is false.

# Order of discount and tax

If the tax comes first, the final price is <InlineMath math="200{,}000\times1.1\times0.75" />. Multiplication is commutative, so **the order of the multipliers does not change the result**.

<BlockMath math="200{,}000\times1.1\times0.75=165{,}000" />

The price is the same, not higher. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **false**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **false**.

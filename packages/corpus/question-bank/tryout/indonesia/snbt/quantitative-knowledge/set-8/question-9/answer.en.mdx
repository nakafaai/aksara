On each part of the trip, time equals distance divided by speed. The average speed of the whole trip is **total distance divided by total time**, not the mean of the two speeds.

The value 50 is the plain mean of 60 and 40. The car **spends longer on the fast part**, so the speed 60 weighs more and the average is above 50. The second statement is false.

If all 180 km were driven at 60, the time would be <InlineMath math="\tfrac{180}{60}=3" /> hours. The difference from 3.5 hours is 0.5 hours, or 30 minutes. The third statement is true.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **false**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **false**.

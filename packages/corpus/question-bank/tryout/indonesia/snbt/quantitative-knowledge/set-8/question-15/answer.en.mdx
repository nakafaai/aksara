A line is fixed by **one point and its slope**. The point is the intersection of the first two lines, and the slope comes from the perpendicular condition.

# The intersection point

Add the two equations so that **the variable y cancels**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x+y)+(2x-y)&=5+1\\
3x=6\;\Longrightarrow\;x&=2,\quad y=3
\end{aligned}" />

# Slope of line g

The line <InlineMath math="3x+2y=6" /> can be written <InlineMath math="y=-\tfrac32x+3" />, so its slope is <InlineMath math="-\tfrac32" />. Two lines are perpendicular when **the product of their slopes is <InlineMath math="-1" />**, so the slope of <InlineMath math="g" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac23" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y-3&=\tfrac23(x-2)\\
3y-9&=2x-4\\
2x-3y+5&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="2(2)-3(3)+5=0" />, so the point <InlineMath math="(2,3)" /> lies on the line.

The line <InlineMath math="3x+2y-12=0" /> passes through the right point but is parallel, not perpendicular. The line <InlineMath math="3x-2y=0" /> only flips the sign of the slope, and <InlineMath math="2x+3y-13=0" /> only flips the fraction. The line <InlineMath math="2x-3y-5=0" /> has the right slope but does not pass through <InlineMath math="(2,3)" />.

So the equation of line <InlineMath math="g" /> is **<InlineMath math="2x-3y+5=0" />**.

Two statements can be checked by substitution. The other two need **the inverse formula** or a look at the value that <InlineMath math="f" /> never reaches.

# Direct substitution

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f(4)&=\frac{2\cdot4+1}{4-3}=\frac91=9\\
f(0)&=\frac{0+1}{0-3}=-\frac13
\end{aligned}" />

The first statement is true. The second is false because **the denominator is negative**.

# The inverse formula

Write <InlineMath math="y=f(x)" /> and express <InlineMath math="x" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="y" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y(x-3)&=2x+1\\
xy-2x&=3y+1\\
x&=\frac{3y+1}{y-2}
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{3x+1}{x-2}" /> and <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(5)=\dfrac{16}{3}" />, not 2. Check: <InlineMath math="f\left(\tfrac{16}{3}\right)=\dfrac{35/3}{7/3}=5" />. The third statement is false.

The inverse formula **is undefined at <InlineMath math="y=2" />**. This means no <InlineMath math="x" /> gives <InlineMath math="f(x)=2" />. Trying it, <InlineMath math="2x+1=2x-6" /> gives <InlineMath math="1=-6" />, which is impossible. The fourth statement is true.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **false**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **true**.

This system mixes base-<InlineMath math="4" /> and base-<InlineMath math="2" /> logarithms. Here's the plan: **rewrite every logarithm in terms of two new letters**, find the sum and product of those letters, convert back to <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, then use the condition <InlineMath math="x\ne y" />.

# Making both equations look alike

Let <InlineMath math="p={}^{4}\log x" /> and <InlineMath math="q={}^{2}\log y" />. That means <InlineMath math="x=4^{p}=2^{2p}" />, so <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log x=2p" />. In the same way, <InlineMath math="y=2^{q}=4^{q/2}" />, so <InlineMath math="{}^{4}\log y=\tfrac{q}{2}" />. The rule is: **a base-<InlineMath math="4" /> logarithm is half of the base-<InlineMath math="2" /> logarithm**, because <InlineMath math="4=2^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
p+q&=3\\
2p\cdot\tfrac{q}{2}&=2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad pq=2
\end{aligned}" />

# Finding the pairs

Two numbers with sum <InlineMath math="3" /> and product <InlineMath math="2" /> are the roots of <InlineMath math="t^2-3t+2=0" />, that is, <InlineMath math="(t-1)(t-2)=0" />. So <InlineMath math="(p,q)=(1,2)" /> or <InlineMath math="(p,q)=(2,1)" />. Convert back with <InlineMath math="x=4^{p}" /> and <InlineMath math="y=2^{q}" />, and **notice that <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> use different bases**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(p,q)=(1,2)&:\quad x=4^{1}=4,\quad y=2^{2}=4\\
(p,q)=(2,1)&:\quad x=4^{2}=16,\quad y=2^{1}=2
\end{aligned}" />

# The condition that removes one pair

The pair <InlineMath math="x=y=4" /> satisfies both equations: <InlineMath math="1+2=3" /> and <InlineMath math="2\cdot1=2" />. So it only drops out because of **the condition <InlineMath math="x\ne y" /> in the question**. What's left is <InlineMath math="x=16" /> and <InlineMath math="y=2" />.

Check this pair in the original system.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
{}^{4}\log16+{}^{2}\log2&=2+1=3\\
{}^{2}\log16\cdot{}^{4}\log2&=4\cdot\tfrac12=2
\end{aligned}" />

Both numbers are positive and different, so <InlineMath math="x+y=16+2=18" />.

When the water is transferred, its **volume stays the same** even though the container changes shape. Let <InlineMath math="h" /> be the water height in the cylinder. The hemisphere volume equals the cylinder base area times the water height:

<BlockMath math="\frac12\cdot\frac43\pi r^3=\pi r^2h" />

Since the radius of the sphere is the same as the radius of the cylinder, we can simplify the equation by dividing both sides by <InlineMath math="\pi \cdot r^2" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{2}{3} \cdot r = h" />

Given that the radius <InlineMath math="r = 15 \text{ cm}" />. We substitute this value into the equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{2}{3} \cdot 15 = h" />
  <BlockMath math="h = 10 \text{ cm}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the height of the water in the cylinder is <InlineMath math="10 \text{ cm}" />.

You can also compare the water volume directly with the cylinder base area:

| Quantity | Calculation |
| --- | --- |
| Water volume | <InlineMath math="\frac23\pi(15)^3=2250\pi\text{ cm}^3" /> |
| Cylinder base area | <InlineMath math="\pi(15)^2=225\pi\text{ cm}^2" /> |
| Water height | <InlineMath math="\frac{2250\pi}{225\pi}=10\text{ cm}" /> |

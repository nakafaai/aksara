The balls are drawn together, so **order does not matter** and we use combinations. Each pair of red balls can be combined with each choice of one white ball. The number of ways to choose two red balls is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}C(5,2)&=\frac{5!}{2!3!}\\&=\frac{5\cdot4}{2\cdot1}\\&=10\end{aligned}" />

The number of ways to choose white balls is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}C(4,1)&=\frac{4!}{3!1!}\\&=4\end{aligned}" />

The total number of possible ways to choose the balls is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}C(12,3)&=\frac{12!}{9!3!}\\&=\frac{12\cdot11\cdot10}{3\cdot2\cdot1}\\&=220\end{aligned}" />

Let <InlineMath math="A" /> be the event of drawing <InlineMath math="2" /> red balls and <InlineMath math="1" /> white ball.

The probability of drawing <InlineMath math="2" /> red balls and <InlineMath math="1" /> white ball is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(A) &= \frac{C(5,2)C(4,1)}{C(12,3)} \\ &= \frac{10 \cdot 4}{220} \\ &= \frac{40}{220} \\ &= \frac{2}{11} \end{aligned}" />

The starting sizes aren't known, so here's the plan: write the new volume and surface area in terms of the old sizes, then compare. Keep the difference in mind: **a claim about every size must hold for all sizes, while a claim that some size exists only needs one example**.

# The volume of the box

The volume of a box is the product of its three sizes, so **the scale factors multiply too**.

<BlockMath math="V_{\text{new}}=(2p)(2\ell)\left(\tfrac14t\right)=\left(2\cdot2\cdot\tfrac14\right)p\ell t=p\ell t" />

The volume doesn't change, whatever the starting sizes. The first statement is true.

# The surface area of the box

The surface area of a box is the total area of its <InlineMath math="6" /> faces, <InlineMath math="2(p\ell+pt+\ell t)" />. Each pair of faces changes by a different factor.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{old}}&=2(p\ell+pt+\ell t)\\
L_{\text{new}}&=2\left((2p)(2\ell)+(2p)\left(\tfrac14t\right)+(2\ell)\left(\tfrac14t\right)\right)=2\left(4p\ell+\tfrac12pt+\tfrac12\ell t\right)
\end{aligned}" />

The top and bottom faces become <InlineMath math="4" /> times as big, but the <InlineMath math="4" /> side faces shrink to half. So **the result depends on the shape of the original box**.

For a cube with edge <InlineMath math="s" />, you get <InlineMath math="L_{\text{old}}=6s^2" /> and <InlineMath math="L_{\text{new}}=2\left(4s^2+\tfrac12s^2+\tfrac12s^2\right)=10s^2" />. Since <InlineMath math="10s^2>6s^2" />, the second statement is true.

The third statement says every size, so one counterexample is enough to knock it down. Take a tall box with <InlineMath math="p=\ell=1" /> and <InlineMath math="t=100" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{old}}&=2(1+100+100)=402\\
L_{\text{new}}&=2(4+50+50)=208
\end{aligned}" />

The surface area actually shrinks, so the third statement is false.

# The surface area of the cylinder

The surface area of a closed cylinder is <InlineMath math="2" /> bases plus the curved side, <InlineMath math="2\pi r^2+2\pi rh" />. The bases become <InlineMath math="4" /> times as big, while the curved side stays the same, because <InlineMath math="2\pi(2r)\left(\tfrac12h\right)=2\pi rh" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{new}}&=8\pi r^2+2\pi rh\\
L_{\text{new}}=2L_{\text{old}}&\iff8\pi r^2+2\pi rh=4\pi r^2+4\pi rh\\
&\iff4\pi r^2=2\pi rh\\
&\iff h=2r
\end{aligned}" />

The fourth statement only asks for **one starting size that works**. A cylinder with <InlineMath math="r=1" /> and <InlineMath math="h=2" /> gives <InlineMath math="L_{\text{old}}=2\pi+4\pi=6\pi" /> and <InlineMath math="L_{\text{new}}=8\pi+4\pi=12\pi" />, exactly <InlineMath math="2" /> times as much. The fourth statement is true.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **true**.

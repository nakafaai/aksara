The given fact that <InlineMath math="n" /> is **a positive integer** applies to every statement. This condition often decides whether a statement leaves one value or more.

# Statement one alone

<BlockMath math="n^2-2n-15=(n-5)(n+3)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad n=5\ \text{or}\ n=-3" />

The value <InlineMath math="-3" /> **is not a positive integer**, so only <InlineMath math="n=5" /> remains. Statement (1) alone is sufficient.

# Statement two alone

Since <InlineMath math="32=2^5" /> and the function <InlineMath math="2^n" /> is increasing, the equation <InlineMath math="2^n=32" /> **holds only for <InlineMath math="n=5" />**. Statement (2) alone is sufficient too.

A student who forgets the positivity condition thinks statement (1) leaves two values and wrongly concludes that only statement (2) is sufficient.

So **each statement alone is sufficient** to answer the question.

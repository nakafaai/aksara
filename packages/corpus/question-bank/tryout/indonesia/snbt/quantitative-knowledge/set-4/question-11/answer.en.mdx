# Reflecting point A

The mirror image of <InlineMath math="A(0,5)" /> in the line is <InlineMath math="A'(8,1)" />. Check the two mirror conditions. # Checking the ends of the segment

The best point on the whole line is <InlineMath math="P^*(3,1)" />, but <InlineMath math="x=3" /> isn't between <InlineMath math="5" /> and <InlineMath math="7" />. So **the value <InlineMath math="6" /> can't be reached**.

The distance from a fixed point to a point moving along a straight line always has a valley shape: it falls until the closest point, then keeps rising. The sum of two valley shapes is also a valley, with its bottom at <InlineMath math="P^*" />. Every point of segment <InlineMath math="CD" /> is to the right of <InlineMath math="P^*" />, where the sum keeps rising, so the smallest value is at the point of the segment closest to <InlineMath math="P^*" />, which is <InlineMath math="C(5,5)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
CA&=\sqrt{(5-0)^2+(5-5)^2}=5\\

# The tempting wrong options

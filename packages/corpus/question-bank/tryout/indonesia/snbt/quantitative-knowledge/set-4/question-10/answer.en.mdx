It is easier to take **the area of the square minus three right triangles in the corners**: <InlineMath math="AED" />, <InlineMath math="EBF" />, and <InlineMath math="FCD" />.

# The three corner triangles

Each corner triangle has a right angle, so its area is **half the product of its legs**. The midpoints split each 12 cm side into two 6 cm parts.

| Triangle | Legs | Area |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="AED" /> | <InlineMath math="AE=6" />, <InlineMath math="AD=12" /> | 36 |
| <InlineMath math="EBF" /> | <InlineMath math="EB=6" />, <InlineMath math="BF=6" /> | 18 |
| <InlineMath math="FCD" /> | <InlineMath math="FC=6" />, <InlineMath math="CD=12" /> | 36 |

# Computing the area of triangle DEF

The three corner triangles and triangle <InlineMath math="DEF" /> **cover the square exactly, without overlap**.

<BlockMath math="[DEF]=144-36-18-36=54\text{ cm}^2" />

The value 72 subtracts only the two large triangles and forgets <InlineMath math="EBF" />. The value 90 subtracts only one large triangle and <InlineMath math="EBF" />. The value 108 subtracts only one large triangle, and the value 126 subtracts only <InlineMath math="EBF" />.

So the area of triangle <InlineMath math="DEF" /> is **<InlineMath math="54\text{ cm}^2" />**.

The chart gives the frequency of each score. The mean must **use the frequencies as weights**, while the median is read from the positions of the ordered data.

# Computing the mean

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{4(60)+7(70)+6(80)+3(90)}{20}\\
&=\frac{240+490+480+270}{20}=\frac{1480}{20}=74
\end{aligned}" />

# Finding the median

Order the twenty values from smallest to largest. The median is the mean of the 10th and 11th values. The cumulative frequency is 4 for the score 60 and 11 for the score 70, so **values 5 to 11 are all 70**. The median is 70.

The difference is <InlineMath math="74-70=4" />.

The value 5 appears when the mean is taken as the unweighted mean of the four scores, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{60+70+80+90}{4}=75" />. The value 1 uses a median of 75, the mean of the two middle categories rather than the two middle values. The value 6 uses a median of 80, and the value 10 uses a mean of 80.

So the difference between the mean and the median is **4**.

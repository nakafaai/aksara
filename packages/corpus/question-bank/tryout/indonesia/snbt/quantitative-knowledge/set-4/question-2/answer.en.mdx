# The volume of the whole container

The empty space above the water is a small cone with the same apex, so it's similar to the whole cone. If every length is multiplied by <InlineMath math="k" />, **the volume of a solid is multiplied by <InlineMath math="k^3" />**, because a cone's volume uses three lengths: the radius twice (through the base area) and the height once.

At the start the water reaches half the height, so the empty part is also half the height. Let the volume of the whole container be <InlineMath math="V" /> liters. The empty part holds <InlineMath math="\left(\frac12\right)^3V=\frac18V" />, and the rest is water.

\tfrac78V&=7\\

\end{aligned}" />

# The water at the new level

After filling, the water reaches <InlineMath math="\frac34" /> of the height. That means **the empty part is only <InlineMath math="\frac14" /> of the height**, and its volume is <InlineMath math="\left(\frac14\right)^3" /> of the container.

\text{empty volume}&=\left(\tfrac14\right)^3\cdot8=\tfrac{8}{64}=\tfrac18\text{ liter}\\

\text{water added}&=\tfrac{63}{8}-7=\tfrac{63}{8}-\tfrac{56}{8}=\tfrac78\text{ liter}

The empty space shrank by <InlineMath math="1-\frac18=\frac78" /> liter, which matches the water that went in. Almost every other option comes from **a volume ratio with the wrong power or the wrong direction**.

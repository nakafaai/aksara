The sum of the squared roots can be written with **the sum and product of the roots**, which come straight from the coefficients: <InlineMath math="p+q=7" /> and <InlineMath math="pq=c" />.

# Setting up an equation for c

The square of the sum contains the middle term <InlineMath math="2pq" />, so that term **must be removed as twice the product**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
p^2+q^2&=(p+q)^2-2pq\\
29&=49-2c\\
2c&=20\\
c&=10
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking for real roots

With <InlineMath math="c=10" />, the equation is <InlineMath math="x^2-7x+10=(x-2)(x-5)=0" />. The roots 2 and 5 are real, and <InlineMath math="2^2+5^2=29" />.

The value 20 forgets the factor 2 in <InlineMath math="2pq" />, giving <InlineMath math="49-c=29" />. The value 39 adds <InlineMath math="49+29" /> and halves it because of a sign error. The value 29 takes <InlineMath math="p^2+q^2" /> to be <InlineMath math="c" />, and the value 49 is <InlineMath math="(p+q)^2" />.

So the value of <InlineMath math="c" /> is **10**.

Both regions being compared lose the same rectangle, with area <InlineMath math="s\cdot 2s=2s^2" />. So you just compare **the full circle with the semicircle**. Careful: here the side on the diameter is **the short side** <InlineMath math="AB=s" />, while the long side <InlineMath math="BC=2s" /> stands upright.

# Area of the full circle

All four corners lie on <InlineMath math="L_2" />, so the diagonal of the rectangle is **a diameter of <InlineMath math="L_2" />**, because a right angle always faces a diameter.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(2R_2)^2&=s^2+(2s)^2=5s^2\\
R_2^2&=\tfrac{5}{4}s^2\\
P&=\pi R_2^2-2s^2=\tfrac{5}{4}\pi s^2-2s^2
\end{aligned}" />

# Radius of the semicircle

Points <InlineMath math="C" /> and <InlineMath math="D" /> are at the same height above the diameter, and points of the arc at the same height are symmetric about the center <InlineMath math="O" />. So **the rectangle sits exactly in the middle of the diameter**: point <InlineMath math="B" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{s}{2}" /> from <InlineMath math="O" />, and <InlineMath math="C" /> is <InlineMath math="2s" /> straight above <InlineMath math="B" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="OBC" /> has a right angle at <InlineMath math="B" />, and its hypotenuse is the radius <InlineMath math="R_1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
R_1^2&=\left(\tfrac{s}{2}\right)^2+(2s)^2=\tfrac{17}{4}s^2\\
Q&=\tfrac{1}{2}\pi R_1^2-2s^2=\tfrac{17}{8}\pi s^2-2s^2
\end{aligned}" />

# Comparing

The <InlineMath math="-2s^2" /> part is the same on both sides, so compare <InlineMath math="\tfrac{17}{8}\pi s^2" /> with <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5}{4}\pi s^2=\tfrac{10}{8}\pi s^2" />. Since <InlineMath math="17>10" /> and <InlineMath math="s>0" />, **<InlineMath math="Q" /> is bigger than <InlineMath math="P" />**. With <InlineMath math="\pi\approx3.14" />, that's roughly <InlineMath math="Q\approx4.68s^2" /> and <InlineMath math="P\approx1.93s^2" />.

# Checking the other options

The option <InlineMath math="P>Q" /> comes from putting **the long side on the diameter**, the picture most people imagine first. That way <InlineMath math="R_1^2=s^2+s^2=2s^2" />, so the semicircle leaves only <InlineMath math="\pi s^2-2s^2\approx1.14s^2" />, which is smaller than <InlineMath math="P" />. The option <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> never happens, because <InlineMath math="\tfrac{17}{8}\ne\tfrac{5}{4}" />. The option "not enough information" is wrong, because you don't need the value of <InlineMath math="s" />: both areas are multiples of <InlineMath math="s^2" />.

So <InlineMath math="Q>P" />.

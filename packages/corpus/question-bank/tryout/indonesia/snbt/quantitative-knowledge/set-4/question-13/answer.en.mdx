# Splitting each term

Try subtracting two fractions whose denominators are neighbors.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{k(k+1)}-\frac{1}{(k+1)(k+2)}=\frac{(k+2)-k}{k(k+1)(k+2)}=\frac{2}{k(k+1)(k+2)}" />

That's twice the term we need. So **each term equals half of that difference**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{k(k+1)(k+2)}=\frac12\left(\frac{1}{k(k+1)}-\frac{1}{(k+1)(k+2)}\right)" />

# A formula for the sum

When you add the terms, the second part of each term **cancels the first part of the next one**. Only the first part of the first term and the second part of the last term are left.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S_n&=\frac12\left(\frac{1}{1\cdot2}-\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}\right)\\
&=\frac14-\frac{1}{2(n+1)(n+2)}
\end{aligned}" />

# Solving the inequality

Since <InlineMath math="0.245=\frac14-0.005" />, the condition becomes **the subtracted remainder has to be less than <InlineMath math="0.005" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2(n+1)(n+2)}&<0.005=\frac{1}{200}\\
2(n+1)(n+2)&>200\\
(n+1)(n+2)&>100
\end{aligned}" />

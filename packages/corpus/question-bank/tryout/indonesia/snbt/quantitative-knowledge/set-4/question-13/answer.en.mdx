When the lateral surface is unrolled, the slant height becomes the radius of the sector, and the circumference of the base becomes the arc of the sector. So **the arc length of the sector equals the circumference of the base**.

# Matching the arc length

A sector of radius 9 cm with central angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> has arc length <InlineMath math="\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot2\pi\cdot9" />. The circumference of the base is <InlineMath math="2\pi\cdot3" />, and **the two lengths are set equal**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot2\pi\cdot9&=2\pi\cdot3\\
\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}&=\frac39=\frac13\\
\theta&=120^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking the other options

The central angle is <InlineMath math="360^\circ" /> times **the ratio of the base radius to the slant height**. The value <InlineMath math="240^\circ" /> uses the base diameter, 6 cm, as the numerator. The value <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> uses <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> as the multiplier. The value <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> assumes the surface is always a semicircle, and <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> assumes it is a quarter circle.

So the central angle of the sector is <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.

Let the length and width of the rectangle be <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="l" />. Its area is <InlineMath math="pl" />, so what you need is **the product of <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="l" />**, not necessarily each value.

# Each statement alone

Statement (1) gives <InlineMath math="p+l=14" />. Sides 7 and 7 give area 49, while 10 and 4 give area 40, so **the area is not yet unique**.

Statement (2) gives <InlineMath math="p^2+l^2=100" />. Sides 8 and 6 give area 48, while <InlineMath math="5\sqrt2" /> and <InlineMath math="5\sqrt2" /> give area 50. Statement (2) alone is not sufficient either.

# Both statements together

The square of the sum links the sum, the sum of squares, and **the product** we need.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(p+l)^2&=p^2+l^2+2pl\\
196&=100+2pl\\
pl&=48
\end{aligned}" />

The area is unique, <InlineMath math="48\text{ cm}^2" />, with sides 8 cm and 6 cm.

So **both statements together are sufficient**, but neither statement alone is sufficient.

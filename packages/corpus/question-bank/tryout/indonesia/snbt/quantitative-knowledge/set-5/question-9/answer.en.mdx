The ladder, the wall, and the floor always form a right triangle with hypotenuse 13 m. **The length of the ladder does not change**, so the Pythagorean theorem holds before and after the move.

# The starting position

<BlockMath math="t_{\text{start}}=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{144}=12" />

The top of the ladder starts **12 m above the floor**.

# The position after the move

The top drops 1 m, so it is now 11 m high. The distance from the foot to the wall becomes

<BlockMath math="d=\sqrt{13^2-11^2}=\sqrt{48}=4\sqrt3" />

The foot moves by **the difference between the new and old distances**, <InlineMath math="4\sqrt3-5\approx1.93" /> m.

The value 1 m assumes the foot moves as far as the top drops, although the relation between them is not linear. The value <InlineMath math="4\sqrt3" /> is the new distance to the wall, not yet reduced by 5. The value <InlineMath math="\sqrt{23}" /> applies Pythagoras to the sides 12 and 11, which do not form the right triangle in the problem. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12}{5}" /> scales the 1 m drop by the ratio <InlineMath math="12:5" />.

So the foot of the ladder moves <InlineMath math="\left(4\sqrt3-5\right)" /> m.

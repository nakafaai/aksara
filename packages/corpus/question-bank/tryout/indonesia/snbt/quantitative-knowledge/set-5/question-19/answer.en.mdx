With five coins, more heads than tails means **at least three heads**. There are <InlineMath math="2^5=32" /> equally likely outcomes because the coins are fair.

# Counting the favorable outcomes

The number of ways to get exactly <InlineMath math="k" /> heads is **the number of ways to choose which coins show heads**, <InlineMath math="\binom5k" />.

<BlockMath math="\binom53+\binom54+\binom55=10+5+1=16" />

The probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{16}{32}=\frac12" />.

# The symmetry argument

Five coins cannot show equally many heads and tails, because 5 is odd. So every outcome has more heads **or** more tails. Swapping heads and tails on every coin pairs the two groups one to one, so **the two groups are equal in size**, 16 outcomes each.

The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac5{16}" /> counts only exactly three heads. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{15}{32}" /> forgets the outcome with five heads. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{13}{16}" /> also counts two heads, and the value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{31}{32}" /> is the probability of at least one head.

So the required probability is <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />.

The volume of a pyramid needs its height, but a net only shows flat faces. The plan: read the net to get **the height of each side triangle**, then fold the pyramid and turn that triangle height into the height of the pyramid.

# Reading the net

Let <InlineMath math="O" /> be the centre of the square base and let <InlineMath math="s" /> be the height of each side triangle, the distance from the triangle's tip to the base side it's attached to. In the flat net, each tip lies on the line through <InlineMath math="O" /> that's perpendicular to that side. So **the distance from a tip to <InlineMath math="O" /> is half the base side plus <InlineMath math="s" />**, which is <InlineMath math="3+s" />. The four tips are the same distance from <InlineMath math="O" /> in four perpendicular directions, so that distance is half the diagonal of the big square.

# The height of each side triangle

A square's area is **half the square of its diagonal**. From the area <InlineMath math="128\text{ cm}^2" /> you can get the diagonal.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac12 d^2&=128\\
d^2&=256\\
d&=16\\
3+s&=\tfrac{16}{2}=8\\
s&=5
\end{aligned}" />

# The pyramid's height and volume

After folding, the apex <InlineMath math="T" /> sits right above <InlineMath math="O" />. Let <InlineMath math="M" /> be the midpoint of one base side. Triangle <InlineMath math="TOM" /> has its right angle at <InlineMath math="O" />, with <InlineMath math="OM=3" /> and <InlineMath math="TM=s=5" />. So **the height of a side triangle is the hypotenuse, not the pyramid's height**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
TO&=\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=4\text{ cm}\\
V&=\tfrac13\cdot6^2\cdot4=48\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

Check the slant edge two ways. From a side triangle, the edge is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{5^2+3^2}=\sqrt{34}" />. From the pyramid's height and half the base diagonal <InlineMath math="3\sqrt2" />, the edge is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4^2+18}=\sqrt{34}" />. They match, so the measurements fit together.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12\sqrt{55}\text{ cm}^3" /> comes from taking the distance <InlineMath math="8" /> as <InlineMath math="s" /> **without subtracting half the base side**.
- <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> uses the side triangle's height <InlineMath math="5" /> as the pyramid's height.
- <InlineMath math="144\text{ cm}^3" /> forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> in the pyramid volume formula.
- <InlineMath math="12\sqrt7\text{ cm}^3" /> subtracts half the base diagonal from <InlineMath math="s" />, as if <InlineMath math="s" /> were the slant edge: <InlineMath math="\sqrt{25-18}=\sqrt7" />.

So the volume of the pyramid is <InlineMath math="48\text{ cm}^3" />.

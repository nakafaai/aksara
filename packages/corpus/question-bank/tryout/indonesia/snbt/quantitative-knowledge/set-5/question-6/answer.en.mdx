A quadratic equation with roots <InlineMath math="u" /> and <InlineMath math="v" /> can be written <InlineMath math="x^2-(u+v)x+uv=0" />. So what you need is **the sum and product of the new roots**, which follow from <InlineMath math="\alpha+\beta=3" /> and <InlineMath math="\alpha\beta=1" /> without finding the roots.

# The sum and product of the new roots

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\alpha^2+\beta^2&=(\alpha+\beta)^2-2\alpha\beta=9-2=7\\
\alpha^2\beta^2&=(\alpha\beta)^2=1
\end{aligned}" />

# Building the equation

Put the sum and product into the general form. **The sum of the roots enters with a minus sign**.

<BlockMath math="x^2-7x+1=0" />

A check with the actual roots: <InlineMath math="\alpha=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}" /> gives <InlineMath math="\alpha^2=\frac{7+3\sqrt5}{2}" />, and this value satisfies <InlineMath math="x^2-7x+1=0" />.

The equation with coefficient 9 forgets to subtract <InlineMath math="2\alpha\beta" />, and the one with 11 adds it. The equation <InlineMath math="x^2+7x+1=0" /> has the wrong sign on the sum of the roots. The equation with constant 2 uses <InlineMath math="2\alpha\beta" /> as the product.

So the required equation is <InlineMath math="x^2-7x+1=0" />.

Join the center of the hexagon to its six vertices. This gives **six equilateral triangles with side 4 cm**, and every measurement asked for can be read from them.

# Diagonals

The longest diagonal passes through the center and consists of two circumradii, so its length is <InlineMath math="4+4=8" /> cm. The second statement is true.

The shortest diagonal joins two vertices on either side of a third vertex. The interior angle of a regular hexagon is <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />, so by the cosine rule:

<BlockMath math="d^2=4^2+4^2-2\cdot4\cdot4\cos120^\circ=16+16+16=48" />

This gives <InlineMath math="d=4\sqrt3" />, **not <InlineMath math="4\sqrt2" />**. The first statement is false.

# The incircle and the area

The inradius is the height of one equilateral triangle, <InlineMath math="\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot4=2\sqrt3" />. The value 4 cm is the **circumradius**, so the third statement is false.

<BlockMath math="A=6\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\cdot4^2=24\sqrt3" />

The fourth statement is true.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **true**.

Means cannot be subtracted directly. Turn each mean into **a total score**, then subtract the totals.

# The total of each group

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{total for the whole class}&=25\times70=1750\\
\text{total for the five students}&=5\times86=430\\
\text{total for the other 20 students}&=1750-430=1320
\end{aligned}" />

# The mean of the other group

Divide that total by **the number of students in that group**, which is 20.

<BlockMath math="\bar x=\frac{1320}{20}=66" />

A check with deviations: the five students are on average 16 above 70, an excess of 80 in total. The other 20 students must balance it, on average 4 below 70 each, which gives 66.

The value 52.8 divides 1320 by 25 instead of 20. The value 74 adds 4 instead of subtracting it. The value 70 assumes the mean of the other group does not change. The value 83.2 subtracts 86, not 430, from the class total.

So the mean score of the other 20 students is **66**.

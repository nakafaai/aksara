Both quantities count arrangements of letters under a condition, so work each one out completely, then compare. The key is **how each condition changes which letters are free to move**, and each quantity has one condition that's easy to miss.

# Counting P

The word SEPATU has <InlineMath math="6" /> different letters: the vowels A, E, U and the consonants S, P, T. The first letter has to be a consonant, so there are <InlineMath math="3" /> choices. The other <InlineMath math="5" /> positions hold <InlineMath math="2" /> consonants and <InlineMath math="3" /> vowels. Pick <InlineMath math="3" /> positions for the vowels, fill them in the order A, E, U in just <InlineMath math="1" /> way, then arrange the <InlineMath math="2" /> remaining consonants.

<BlockMath math="P=3\times\binom{5}{3}\times1\times2!=3\times10\times2=60" />

Check it another way. Without the consonant at the front, **locking the order of the vowels works like treating the three vowels as identical**, so there are <InlineMath math="\tfrac{6!}{3!}=120" /> arrangements. The first letter can only be a vowel if it's A, because A has to come before E and U. There are <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5!}{2!}=60" /> arrangements starting with A, so <InlineMath math="120-60=60" /> start with a consonant.

# Counting Q

The word PERMATA is made of P, E, R, M, A, T, A. The first and last letters can only match if that letter appears at least <InlineMath math="2" /> times. **Only the letter A appears more than once**, so both ends have to be A. The <InlineMath math="5" /> middle letters are P, E, R, M, T, all different.

<BlockMath math="Q=1\times5!\times1=120" />

# Comparing

You have <InlineMath math="Q=120" /> and <InlineMath math="P=60" />, so **<InlineMath math="Q" /> is twice <InlineMath math="P" />**, and the correct relationship is <InlineMath math="Q>P" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> comes from forgetting the consonant at the front of <InlineMath math="P" />, which gives <InlineMath math="P=120" />, or from still dividing <InlineMath math="Q" /> by <InlineMath math="2!" />, which gives <InlineMath math="Q=60" /> even though both A's are already used at the ends.
- <InlineMath math="P>Q" /> comes from ignoring the vowel order in <InlineMath math="P" />, which gives <InlineMath math="P=3\times5!=360" />.
- The option that says the information isn't enough is wrong, because both quantities are **definite numbers you can count fully**.

So the arrangements in <InlineMath math="Q" /> are more numerous: <InlineMath math="Q=120" /> and <InlineMath math="P=60" />, which means <InlineMath math="Q>P" />.

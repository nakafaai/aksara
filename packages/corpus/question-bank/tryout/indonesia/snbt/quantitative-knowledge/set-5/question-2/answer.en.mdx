You need <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{p^2}+\tfrac{1}{q^2}+\tfrac{1}{r^2}" />, but the coefficient <InlineMath math="k" /> isn't known yet. The plan: use the condition <InlineMath math="pq=1" /> to **find the third root first** and <InlineMath math="k" />, then build the sum of reciprocal squares from the sums and products of the roots. For <InlineMath math="x^3-5x^2+kx-2=0" />, Vieta's formulas (the links between roots and coefficients) give <InlineMath math="p+q+r=5" />, <InlineMath math="pq+qr+rp=k" />, and <InlineMath math="pqr=2" />.

# The third root and the value of k

The product of all three roots is <InlineMath math="2" />, and <InlineMath math="pq=1" />. So **the condition <InlineMath math="pq=1" /> hands you the third root right away**: <InlineMath math="r=\tfrac{2}{1}=2" />. Since <InlineMath math="2" /> is a root, substitute <InlineMath math="x=2" /> into the equation to get <InlineMath math="k" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2^3-5\cdot2^2+2k-2&=0\\
8-20+2k-2&=0\\
2k&=14\\
k&=7
\end{aligned}" />

The other two roots satisfy <InlineMath math="p+q=5-2=3" /> and <InlineMath math="pq=1" />, so they're the roots of <InlineMath math="t^2-3t+1=0" />. Its discriminant <InlineMath math="9-4=5" /> is positive, so <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" /> are real.

# The sum of reciprocal squares

Squaring a sum of reciprocals creates cross terms. That's why **the sum of reciprocal squares equals the square of the sum of reciprocals minus twice the sum of the cross terms**. Vieta's formulas give both pieces.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac1p+\frac1q+\frac1r&=\frac{pq+qr+rp}{pqr}=\frac72\\
\frac{1}{pq}+\frac{1}{qr}+\frac{1}{rp}&=\frac{p+q+r}{pqr}=\frac52\\
\frac{1}{p^2}+\frac{1}{q^2}+\frac{1}{r^2}&=\left(\frac72\right)^2-2\cdot\frac52=\frac{49}{4}-5=\frac{29}{4}
\end{aligned}" />

# A check by another route

Work out the <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" /> part separately from the <InlineMath math="r" /> part. Since <InlineMath math="pq=1" />, the denominator <InlineMath math="(pq)^2" /> equals <InlineMath math="1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
p^2+q^2&=(p+q)^2-2pq=9-2=7\\
\frac{1}{p^2}+\frac{1}{q^2}&=\frac{p^2+q^2}{(pq)^2}=7\\
7+\frac{1}{2^2}&=\frac{29}{4}
\end{aligned}" />

You get the same result, so **the value you want is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{29}{4}" />**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="7" /> only adds <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{p^2}+\tfrac{1}{q^2}" /> and **forgets the third root's term**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{r^2}=\tfrac14" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{49}{4}" /> is the square of the sum of reciprocals **without subtracting the cross terms**.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{29}{2}" /> comes from dividing <InlineMath math="49-20" /> by <InlineMath math="pqr=2" />, when the denominator should be <InlineMath math="(pqr)^2=4" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{69}{4}" /> comes from adding the cross terms instead of subtracting them.

So the value of <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{p^2}+\tfrac{1}{q^2}+\tfrac{1}{r^2}" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{29}{4}" />.

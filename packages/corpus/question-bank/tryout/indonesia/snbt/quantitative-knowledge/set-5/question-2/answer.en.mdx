Each number is a sum of two square roots, and in every case the numbers under the roots add up to 9. Compare the **squares** of the three numbers, because for positive numbers the order of the squares is the order of the numbers.

# Squaring each number

Use **the square of a sum**, <InlineMath math="(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2=p+q+2\sqrt{pq}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2&=2+7+2\sqrt{14}=9+2\sqrt{14}\\
b^2&=3+6+2\sqrt{18}=9+2\sqrt{18}\\
c^2&=4+5+2\sqrt{20}=9+2\sqrt{20}
\end{aligned}" />

# Comparing the products

The part 9 is the same in all three, so the order is decided by **the product of the two numbers under the roots**: <InlineMath math="14<18<20" />. Hence <InlineMath math="a^2<b^2<c^2" />, and since all three are positive, <InlineMath math="a<b<c" />.

The order <InlineMath math="c<b<a" /> comes from assuming that the roots of the most spread-out numbers, like 2 and 7, give the largest sum. The other orders contradict <InlineMath math="14<18<20" />. In general, for a fixed total, two numbers that are closer together give a larger sum of square roots.

So the order from smallest to largest is <InlineMath math="a<b<c" />.

For a <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> determinant, subtract the product of the other diagonal entries from the product of the main diagonal entries:

<BlockMath math="\begin{vmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{vmatrix}=ad-bc" />

#### Calculating the Left Hand Side Determinant

First, we calculate the determinant of the matrix on the left side:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&\begin{vmatrix}-6&3\\2&4\end{vmatrix}\\&=(-6)(4)-(3)(2)\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= -24 - 6" />
  <BlockMath math="= -30" />
</MathContainer>

#### Calculating the Right Hand Side Determinant

Next, we calculate the determinant of the matrix on the right side which contains the variable <InlineMath math="x" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&\begin{vmatrix}2x&3\\-4x&-1\end{vmatrix}\\&=(2x)(-1)-(3)(-4x)\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= -2x - (-12x)" />
  <BlockMath math="= -2x + 12x" />
  <BlockMath math="= 10x" />
</MathContainer>

#### Determining the Variable Value

Now we equate both determinant results according to the original equation:

<BlockMath math="-30 = 10x" />

Divide both sides by <InlineMath math="10" /> to find the value of <InlineMath math="x" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x = \frac{-30}{10}" />
  <BlockMath math="x = -3" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the value of <InlineMath math="x" /> that satisfies the equation is <InlineMath math="-3" />.

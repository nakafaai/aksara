The two sides of <InlineMath math="2^{x^2-1}=3^{x+1}" /> have different bases, so **you can't just set the exponents equal**. The plan: take the logarithm base <InlineMath math="2" /> of both sides to bring the exponents down, find every solution <InlineMath math="x" />, then work out <InlineMath math="2^x" /> for each one and add.

# Turning it into a quadratic equation

The logarithm base <InlineMath math="2" /> of <InlineMath math="2^{x^2-1}" /> is <InlineMath math="x^2-1" />, and the logarithm of <InlineMath math="3^{x+1}" /> is <InlineMath math="(x+1)\cdot{}^{2}\log3" />. Notice that <InlineMath math="x^2-1=(x+1)(x-1)" /> shares a factor with the right side. **Don't divide both sides by <InlineMath math="x+1" />**, because that factor can be zero. Move everything to one side and factor instead.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-1&=(x+1)\cdot{}^{2}\log3\\
(x+1)(x-1)-(x+1)\cdot{}^{2}\log3&=0\\
(x+1)\left(x-1-{}^{2}\log3\right)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> or <InlineMath math="x=1+{}^{2}\log3" />. For <InlineMath math="x=-1" />, both sides equal <InlineMath math="2^0=3^0=1" />, so this root is valid.

# Working out two to the power x

For the second root, use the rule that **a base raised to a logarithm with that same base gives back the number inside the logarithm**, so <InlineMath math="2^{{}^{2}\log3}=3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2^{-1}&=\tfrac12\\
2^{1+{}^{2}\log3}&=2\cdot2^{{}^{2}\log3}=2\cdot3=6\\
\tfrac12+6&=\tfrac{13}{2}
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking the second root

The second root satisfies <InlineMath math="2^{x-1}=2^{{}^{2}\log3}=3" />. Use that on the left side.

<BlockMath math="2^{x^2-1}=2^{(x-1)(x+1)}=\left(2^{x-1}\right)^{x+1}=3^{x+1}" />

The left side matches the right side, so **both roots are valid and both go into the sum**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6" /> comes from dividing both sides by <InlineMath math="x+1" />, which **loses the root <InlineMath math="x=-1" />**.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> is <InlineMath math="2^{a+b}" /> for the sum of the roots <InlineMath math="a+b={}^{2}\log3" />, but <InlineMath math="2^a+2^b" /> isn't the same as <InlineMath math="2^{a+b}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac92" /> comes from setting the exponents equal as if the bases matched: <InlineMath math="x^2-1=x+1" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=2" /> or <InlineMath math="x=-1" />, then <InlineMath math="4+\tfrac12" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac72" /> comes from forgetting the <InlineMath math="1" /> in the second root, which gives <InlineMath math="x={}^{2}\log3" /> and <InlineMath math="3+\tfrac12" />.

So the sum of <InlineMath math="2^x" /> over all solutions of the equation is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{13}{2}" />.

Rewrite each logarithm in terms of <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log5" />. Three properties are needed: **the logarithm of a product, the logarithm of a power, and swapping the base**.

# Statements with a product

Split 10 and 40 into products of powers of 2 and 5.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
{}^{2}\log10&={}^{2}\log2+{}^{2}\log5=1+p\\
{}^{2}\log40&={}^{2}\log8+{}^{2}\log5=3+p
\end{aligned}" />

The logarithm of a product **becomes a sum**, not a product, so <InlineMath math="1+p\ne2p" /> and <InlineMath math="3+p\ne3p" /> because <InlineMath math="p\ne1" />. The first and fourth statements are false.

# Statements with a power and a base

When the base and the number are both squared, **the value stays the same**, because <InlineMath math="{}^{a^n}\log b^n={}^{a}\log b" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
{}^{4}\log25&={}^{2^2}\log5^2={}^{2}\log5=p\\
{}^{5}\log2&=\frac{1}{{}^{2}\log5}=\frac1p
\end{aligned}" />

The second and third statements are true.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **true**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **false**.

The remainder on division by 6 is known once we know which of the six possible remainders 0 to 5 applies. Check whether each statement **narrows the possibilities down to one**.

# Statement one alone

Numbers that leave remainder 2 on division by 3 include 2, 5, 8, and 11. Their remainders on division by 6 are 2, 5, 2, and 5. Division by 3 **does not separate even from odd numbers**, so both 2 and 5 are possible. Statement (1) alone is not sufficient.

# Statement two alone

Write <InlineMath math="n=12k+5" /> for some integer <InlineMath math="k\ge0" />. Since 12 is a multiple of 6, the part <InlineMath math="12k" /> **is divisible by 6**.

<BlockMath math="n=12k+5=6(2k)+5" />

The remainder is always 5: for example 5, 17, and 29 all leave remainder 5 on division by 6. Statement (2) alone is sufficient. The two statements also agree, because <InlineMath math="12k+5" /> does leave remainder 2 on division by 3.

So **statement (2) alone is sufficient**, but statement (1) alone is not.

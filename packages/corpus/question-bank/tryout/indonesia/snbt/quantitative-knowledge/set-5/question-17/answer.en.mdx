The question compares two absolute values, so first rewrite it in an easier form. The plan: square both sides to get a simple condition on <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, then test each statement with **one example that answers yes and one that answers no**.

# Rewriting the question

Both sides are non-negative, and for two non-negative numbers, the smaller one also has the smaller square. So you can compare the squares.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
|x+y|<|x-y|\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad(x+y)^2<(x-y)^2\\
&\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2+2xy+y^2<x^2-2xy+y^2\\
&\Longleftrightarrow\quad 4xy<0
\end{aligned}" />

So the question is the same as **whether <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> have opposite signs**.

# The first statement alone

Statement (1) only compares the sizes of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, not their signs. The pair <InlineMath math="x=3" /> and <InlineMath math="y=1" /> satisfies (1) and has the same signs, so the answer is no. The pair <InlineMath math="x=3" /> and <InlineMath math="y=-1" /> also satisfies (1) but has opposite signs, so the answer is yes. **Statement (1) alone isn't sufficient**.

# The second statement alone

Cubing always preserves order, so <InlineMath math="x^3>y^3" /> is the same as <InlineMath math="x>y" />. The pair <InlineMath math="x=3" /> and <InlineMath math="y=1" /> gives no, while <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="y=-3" /> gives yes. **Statement (2) alone isn't sufficient**.

# Both statements together

Now <InlineMath math="x>y" /> and <InlineMath math="|x|>|y|" />. From <InlineMath math="|x|>|y|" /> you get <InlineMath math="x^2-y^2>0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x-y)(x+y)&>0\\
x-y>0\quad&\Rightarrow\quad x+y>0
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="x>y" /> and <InlineMath math="x>-y" />, which means <InlineMath math="x" /> is bigger than <InlineMath math="|y|" /> and must be positive. But **the sign of <InlineMath math="y" /> is still unknown**. The pair <InlineMath math="x=3" /> and <InlineMath math="y=1" /> satisfies both statements and gives <InlineMath math="|4|<|2|" />, which is false, so the answer is no. The pair <InlineMath math="x=3" /> and <InlineMath math="y=-1" /> also satisfies both and gives <InlineMath math="|2|<|4|" />, which is true, so the answer is yes. Even together, they aren't sufficient.

The question asks for a conditional probability: **among the people who test positive twice, what fraction is really sick**. The safest way is to picture <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> residents and follow them through the first test and the second test.

# The first test

Out of <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> people, <InlineMath math="10\%" /> are sick and the rest are healthy. The first test is positive for <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of the sick people and **also for <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of the healthy people**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{sick}&=100,\qquad \text{healthy}=900\\
\text{sick and positive}&=90\%\times100=90\\
\text{healthy and positive}&=20\%\times900=180
\end{aligned}" />

# The second test

**The second test only goes to the <InlineMath math="90+180=270" /> people who were positive the first time**. Since the two results are independent for people in the same condition, the positive rates are the same as in the first test.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{sick and positive twice}&=90\%\times90=81\\
\text{healthy and positive twice}&=20\%\times180=36
\end{aligned}" />

# The conditional probability

There are <InlineMath math="81+36=117" /> people who tested positive twice, and <InlineMath math="81" /> of them are sick.

<BlockMath math="P(\text{sick}\mid\text{positive twice})=\frac{81}{117}=\frac{9}{13}" />

Check it with the probability formula, without the <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> people: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.1\times0.9^2}{0.1\times0.9^2+0.9\times0.2^2}=\tfrac{0.081}{0.117}=\tfrac{9}{13}" />. The value is bigger than the probability after one positive test, and that makes sense because **the second test filters out more healthy people**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> **stops after the first test**: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{90}{90+180}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{9}{29}" /> counts the healthy people who test positive the second time out of all <InlineMath math="900" /> healthy people, which gives <InlineMath math="\tfrac{81}{81+180}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{81}{100}" /> is the probability of testing positive twice if you're sick, which is the reverse of what's asked.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{81}{85}" /> ignores that only <InlineMath math="10\%" /> of the residents are sick: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.81}{0.81+0.04}" />.

So the probability that a person who tested positive twice really has the disease is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{9}{13}" />.

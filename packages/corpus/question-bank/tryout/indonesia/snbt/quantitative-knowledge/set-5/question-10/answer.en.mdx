Rewrite the equation of <InlineMath math="g" /> in the form <InlineMath math="y=mx+c" /> so that its gradient and intercepts can be read. A line perpendicular to <InlineMath math="g" /> has **a gradient whose product with the gradient of <InlineMath math="g" /> is <InlineMath math="-1" />**.

# The equations of both lines

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
g:\ 2x-3y=6\quad&\Rightarrow\quad y=\tfrac23x-2\\
h:\ m_h=-\tfrac32\quad&\Rightarrow\quad y=-\tfrac32x+4
\end{aligned}" />

The gradient of <InlineMath math="g" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac23" />, so the first statement is **true**. Line <InlineMath math="h" /> meets the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis where <InlineMath math="y=0" />, at <InlineMath math="x=\tfrac83" />, not 6. The second statement is false.

# The intersection and the triangle area

Substitute the equation of <InlineMath math="h" /> into the equation of <InlineMath math="g" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x-3\left(-\tfrac32x+4\right)&=6\\
\tfrac{13}{2}x&=18\\
x=\tfrac{36}{13},\qquad y&=-\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac{36}{13}+4=-\tfrac{2}{13}
\end{aligned}" />

The intersection has a negative <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate, so it **lies in quadrant IV**, not quadrant I. The third statement is false.

Line <InlineMath math="g" /> meets the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at <InlineMath math="(3,0)" /> and the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis at <InlineMath math="(0,-2)" />. The triangle it forms with the two axes is right-angled with legs 3 and 2, so its area is <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot2=3" />. The fourth statement is true.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **false**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **true**.

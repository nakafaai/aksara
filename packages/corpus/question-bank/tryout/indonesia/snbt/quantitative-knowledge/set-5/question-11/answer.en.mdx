You're looking for the smallest value of <InlineMath math="2x+y" /> under two conditions. The AM-GM inequality (the arithmetic mean is at least the geometric mean) gives a lower bound, but **that bound is only the minimum if its equality case is allowed**. The plan: check the equality case, then turn the problem into a one-variable function on the allowed range.

# A lower bound from AM-GM

For the two positive numbers <InlineMath math="2x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, AM-GM gives the bound below. Equality happens when <InlineMath math="2x=y" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x+y&\ge2\sqrt{2x\cdot y}=2\sqrt{24}=4\sqrt6\\
2x=y\ \text{and}\ xy=12\quad&\Rightarrow\quad x=\sqrt6,\ y=2\sqrt6
\end{aligned}" />

That point gives <InlineMath math="3y=6\sqrt6" />, which is far bigger than <InlineMath math="x=\sqrt6" />, so the condition <InlineMath math="x\ge3y" /> fails. **The AM-GM equality can't happen here**, so <InlineMath math="4\sqrt6" /> isn't the minimum.

# The allowed values of x

From <InlineMath math="xy=12" /> you get <InlineMath math="y=\tfrac{12}{x}" />. Substitute it into the second condition. Since <InlineMath math="x" /> is positive, **you can multiply both sides by <InlineMath math="x" /> without flipping the sign**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x&\ge3\cdot\frac{12}{x}\\
x^2&\ge36\\
x&\ge6
\end{aligned}" />

So you need the smallest value of <InlineMath math="f(x)=2x+\tfrac{12}{x}" /> for <InlineMath math="x\ge6" />.

# The smallest value on that range

Compare <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> with its value at the end of the range, <InlineMath math="f(6)=12+2=14" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f(x)-14&=\frac{2x^2-14x+12}{x}\\
&=\frac{2(x-1)(x-6)}{x}
\end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="x\ge6" />, the factor <InlineMath math="x-1" /> is positive, the factor <InlineMath math="x-6" /> isn't negative, and <InlineMath math="x" /> is positive. So <InlineMath math="f(x)-14\ge0" />, with equality exactly at <InlineMath math="x=6" />. **The minimum sits at the end of the range**, at <InlineMath math="x=6" /> and <InlineMath math="y=2" />, and that pair satisfies <InlineMath math="6\ge3\cdot2" />.

Check with another value in the range: <InlineMath math="x=12" /> and <InlineMath math="y=1" /> give <InlineMath math="2x+y=25" />, which is bigger than <InlineMath math="14" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4\sqrt6" /> is the AM-GM bound whose **equality case breaks the condition <InlineMath math="x\ge3y" />**.
- <InlineMath math="4\sqrt3" /> comes from a wrong AM-GM, <InlineMath math="2x+y\ge2\sqrt{xy}" />, where the coefficient <InlineMath math="2" /> went missing from inside the root.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> is the value of <InlineMath math="x+y" /> at the point <InlineMath math="(6,2)" />, not the value of <InlineMath math="2x+y" />.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> comes from flipping the condition to <InlineMath math="y\ge3x" />, which moves the best point to <InlineMath math="x=2" /> and <InlineMath math="y=6" />.

So the minimum value of <InlineMath math="2x+y" /> is <InlineMath math="14" />.

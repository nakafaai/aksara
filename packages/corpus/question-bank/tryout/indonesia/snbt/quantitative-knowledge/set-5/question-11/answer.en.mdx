The new square cuts off four right triangles at the corners of the large square. Because every side is divided in the same way, the four triangles are **congruent**, each with legs 3 and 1.

# The area of the new square

Two routes give the same result. The side of the new square is the hypotenuse of a corner triangle, or its area is the large square minus **the four corner triangles**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
s^2&=3^2+1^2=10\\
16-4\cdot\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot1&=16-6=10
\end{aligned}" />

# The ratio of the areas

Divide **the area of the new square by the area of the large square**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{10}{16}=\frac58" />

The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> holds when the points are midpoints of the sides, not a <InlineMath math="3:1" /> division. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac9{16}" /> uses only <InlineMath math="3^2" /> as the area of the new square. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac34" /> uses the length ratio <InlineMath math="3:4" /> as the area ratio. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> in the triangle area and so subtracts 12.

So the ratio of the areas is <InlineMath math="\tfrac58" />.

We can solve this problem using the concept of inverse proportion or total workload (man-days).

#### Calculating Workload

The total planned workload is:

<BlockMath math="24\cdot35=840" />

The work is divided into two phases:
1. First phase: <InlineMath math="24\text{ workers}" /> work for <InlineMath math="16\text{ days}" />.
2. Second phase: Half of the workers stop, so <InlineMath math="24 / 2 = 12\text{ workers}" /> remain working for <InlineMath math="P\text{ days}" />.

The equation is:

<BlockMath math="24\cdot35=24\cdot16+12P" />

#### Finding the First Quantity

We solve the equation above to find <InlineMath math="P" /> (the duration for the remaining project).

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="840 = 384 + 12P" />
  <BlockMath math="840 - 384 = 12P" />
  <BlockMath math="456 = 12P" />
  <BlockMath math="P = \frac{456}{12}" />
  <BlockMath math="P = 38" />
</MathContainer>

So, the <InlineMath math="12\text{ workers}" /> need <InlineMath math="38\text{ days}" /> to finish the remaining work. The bridge therefore takes <InlineMath math="16 + 38 = 54\text{ days}" /> in total.

#### Calculating Additional Time

The additional time is the difference between the actual total duration and the original <InlineMath math="35\text{ days}" /> target.

<BlockMath math="(16+38)-35=19" />

Thus, <InlineMath math="19" /> additional days are needed.

Profit is revenue minus all costs. "Making a profit" means the profit is **greater than zero**, not equal to zero.

# Writing the profit function

For <InlineMath math="n" /> items the revenue is <InlineMath math="4500n" /> rupiah and the cost is <InlineMath math="500{,}000+2000n" /> rupiah. **Each item contributes** <InlineMath math="4500-2000=2500" /> rupiah toward the fixed cost.

<BlockMath math="L(n)=4500n-(500{,}000+2000n)=2500n-500{,}000" />

# The condition for a positive profit

<BlockMath math="2500n-500{,}000>0\quad\Rightarrow\quad n>200" />

At <InlineMath math="n=200" /> the profit is exactly zero (the break-even point), so there is no profit yet. The smallest integer greater than 200 is 201, with a profit of <InlineMath math="2500" /> rupiah.

The value 200 is the break-even point, not a profit. The values 111 and 112 divide the fixed cost by the selling price alone and forget the cost per item. The value 250 divides the fixed cost by the cost per item.

So at least **201** items must be made and sold.

The question asks for **a single value of <InlineMath math="x+y" />**. The relation linking the three quantities is <InlineMath math="(x+y)^2=x^2+y^2+2xy" />.

# Each statement alone

Statement (1) is satisfied by <InlineMath math="(2,3)" /> with sum 5 and by <InlineMath math="(1,6)" /> with sum 7. Statement (2) is satisfied by <InlineMath math="(2,3)" /> with sum 5 and by <InlineMath math="(2,-3)" /> with sum <InlineMath math="-1" />. **Each allows different sums**, so neither is sufficient alone.

# Both statements together

<BlockMath math="(x+y)^2=13+2(6)=25\quad\Rightarrow\quad x+y=5\ \text{or}\ x+y=-5" />

Both signs really occur: <InlineMath math="(2,3)" /> gives 5, while <InlineMath math="(-2,-3)" /> also satisfies <InlineMath math="xy=6" /> and <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2=13" /> but gives <InlineMath math="-5" />. **The square root of 25 has two signs**, so the value of <InlineMath math="x+y" /> is not yet unique.

A student who takes only the positive root wrongly concludes that both statements together are sufficient.

So **statements (1) and (2) together are not sufficient** to answer the question.

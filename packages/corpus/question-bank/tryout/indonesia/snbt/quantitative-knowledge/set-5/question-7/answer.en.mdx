You're looking for the area of the region that lies inside all three circles at once. The plan: first work out what that region looks like, then **cut it into pieces whose areas are easy to find**: one triangle and some circular segments (the region between an arc and its chord).

# The shape of the common region

Each radius equals the side of the triangle, so the circle centred at <InlineMath math="A" /> passes through <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="C" />, and the same goes for the other two circles. Triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> lies completely inside all three circles. Beyond side <InlineMath math="BC" />, the region is bounded by arc <InlineMath math="BC" /> of the circle centred at <InlineMath math="A" />. Every point on that arc is at most <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> from <InlineMath math="B" /> and from <InlineMath math="C" />, so the segment beyond <InlineMath math="BC" /> is inside the other two circles as well. So **the common region is triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> plus <InlineMath math="3" /> equal circular segments**.

# The area of one segment

The segment beyond side <InlineMath math="BC" /> is the sector centred at <InlineMath math="A" /> minus triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />. The sector's angle is the angle of an equilateral triangle, <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, so **the sector is one sixth of the circle**.

# The area of the whole region

**Add the triangle and the <InlineMath math="3" /> segments**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A&=9\sqrt3+3\left(6\pi-9\sqrt3\right)\\
&=9\sqrt3+18\pi-27\sqrt3\\
&=18\pi-18\sqrt3
\end{aligned}" />

# A check by another route

The <InlineMath math="3" /> sectors of <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> centred at <InlineMath math="A" />, <InlineMath math="B" />, and <InlineMath math="C" /> also cover the same region. You get the same result. Its value is about <InlineMath math="56.5-31.2=25.3" />, which is bigger than the triangle's area <InlineMath math="9\sqrt3\approx15.6" />. That makes sense, since the region contains the triangle.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\left(18\pi-27\sqrt3\right)\text{ cm}^2" /> only adds the <InlineMath math="3" /> segments and **forgets the triangle in the middle**.
- <InlineMath math="\left(18\pi-9\sqrt3\right)\text{ cm}^2" /> subtracts the triangle just once from the <InlineMath math="3" /> sectors, even though it was counted <InlineMath math="3" /> times.
- <InlineMath math="18\pi\text{ cm}^2" /> just adds the <InlineMath math="3" /> sectors without removing the overlap.
- <InlineMath math="\left(36\pi-18\sqrt3\right)\text{ cm}^2" /> uses a sector angle of <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />, but an equilateral triangle's angle is <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />.

So the area of the region inside all three circles is <InlineMath math="\left(18\pi-18\sqrt3\right)\text{ cm}^2" />.

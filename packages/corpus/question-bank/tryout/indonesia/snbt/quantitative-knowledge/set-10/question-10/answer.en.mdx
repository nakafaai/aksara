First identify the triangle. Since <InlineMath math="20^2+21^2=400+441=841=29^2" />, it is **right-angled with hypotenuse 29**. The inradius is linked to the area by <InlineMath math="L=r\cdot s" />, where <InlineMath math="s" /> is the semi-perimeter.

# Area and semi-perimeter

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L&=\tfrac12\cdot20\cdot21=210\\
s&=\tfrac12(20+21+29)=35
\end{aligned}" />

# The inradius

The formula <InlineMath math="L=r\cdot s" /> comes from **splitting the triangle into three small triangles** with apex at the incentre and height <InlineMath math="r" />.

<BlockMath math="r=\frac{L}{s}=\frac{210}{35}=6" />

For a right triangle, the same result also comes from <InlineMath math="r=\tfrac{a+b-c}{2}=\tfrac{20+21-29}{2}=6" />.

The value 3 divides the area by the whole perimeter instead of half of it. The value 12 is the diameter of the incircle, and 14.5 is the circumradius, half the hypotenuse.

So the inradius of the triangle is **<InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />**.

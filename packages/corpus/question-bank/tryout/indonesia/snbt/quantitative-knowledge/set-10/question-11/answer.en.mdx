# Rewriting as distances

<BlockMath math="f(x)=\lvert x-1\rvert+2\lvert x-4\rvert+3\lvert x-13\rvert" />

So <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> is the sum of the distances from <InlineMath math="x" /> to six points: the point <InlineMath math="1" /> once, the point <InlineMath math="4" /> twice and the point <InlineMath math="13" /> three times. The coefficient means the point **gets counted several times**.

# The slope on each piece

Each distance <InlineMath math="\lvert x-a\rvert" /> drops by <InlineMath math="1" /> per unit while <InlineMath math="x" /> is left of <InlineMath math="a" />, and grows by <InlineMath math="1" /> per unit while <InlineMath math="x" /> is right of <InlineMath math="a" />. The slope of <InlineMath math="f" /> is the sum of all those changes.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x<1:&\quad -1-2-3=-6 \\
1<x<4:&\quad +1-2-3=-4 \\
4<x<13:&\quad +1+2-3=0 \\
x>13:&\quad +1+2+3=+6
\end{aligned}" />

# Adding up the integers

The integers in <InlineMath math="[4,13]" /> are <InlineMath math="4,5,\ldots,13" />, which is <InlineMath math="10" /> numbers. **Both ends count**, because the value there is <InlineMath math="30" /> too.

<BlockMath math="4+5+\cdots+13=\frac{(4+13)\cdot 10}{2}=85" />

# Checking

Sort the six points: <InlineMath math="1,4,4,13,13,13" />. With an even number of points, the total distance is smallest at **every point between the two middle points**, here the third point <InlineMath math="4" /> and the fourth point <InlineMath math="13" />. That matches. Try a few values too: <InlineMath math="f(8)=7+8+15=30" />, while <InlineMath math="f(3)=34" /> and <InlineMath math="f(14)=36" /> are bigger.

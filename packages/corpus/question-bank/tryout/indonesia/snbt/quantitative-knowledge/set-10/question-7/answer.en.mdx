The probability of drawing red is **the number of red balls divided by the total number of balls**. So to know the number of red balls, we need that probability and the total, or equivalent information.

# Statement (1) alone

<BlockMath math="\frac{m}{12}=\frac13\;\Longrightarrow\;m=4" />

The number of red balls is **unique, namely 4**. Statement (1) alone is sufficient.

# Statement (2) alone

Statement (2) only gives the ratio of red to white, <InlineMath math="1:2" />. The bag could hold 1 red and 2 white, 4 red and 8 white, or 10 red and 20 white. **The total number of balls is unknown**, so statement (2) alone is not sufficient.

The two statements agree, because 4 red and 8 white satisfy both.

So **statement (1) alone is sufficient**, but statement (2) alone is not.

Before using the sum and product of roots, check whether the equation factorises. It does: **the form fits <InlineMath math="(x-3)(x-k)" />**, because <InlineMath math="3+k" /> is the sum and <InlineMath math="3k" /> is the product.

# The roots

<BlockMath math="x^2-(k+3)x+3k=(x-3)(x-k)=0" />

The roots are 3 and <InlineMath math="k" />, **whatever k is**.

# The difference condition

The roots differ by 5, so <InlineMath math="k" /> **lies 5 away from 3**.

<BlockMath math="\lvert k-3\rvert=5\;\Longrightarrow\;k=8\;\text{or}\;k=-2" />

Check: for <InlineMath math="k=8" /> the roots are 3 and 8, and for <InlineMath math="k=-2" /> they are 3 and <InlineMath math="-2" />. Both differ by 5.

<BlockMath math="8+(-2)=6" />

The value 8 uses only one possibility. The value 10 is the difference of the two values of <InlineMath math="k" />, not their sum. The value 3 is the fixed root, and 5 is the given difference.

So the sum of all values of <InlineMath math="k" /> is **6**.

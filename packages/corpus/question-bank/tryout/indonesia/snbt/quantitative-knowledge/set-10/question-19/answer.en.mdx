The ball is returned before the second draw, so **the bag is the same for both draws** and the two events are independent. Different colours can happen in two orders.

# The two possible orders

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{red then blue})&=\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac25=\tfrac{6}{25}\\
P(\text{blue then red})&=\tfrac25\cdot\tfrac35=\tfrac{6}{25}
\end{aligned}" />

# Adding the two orders

The two orders cannot happen together, so **their probabilities are added**.

<BlockMath math="P=\tfrac{6}{25}+\tfrac{6}{25}=\tfrac{12}{25}" />

Check: the probability of the same colour is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{9}{25}+\tfrac{4}{25}=\tfrac{13}{25}" />, and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12}{25}+\tfrac{13}{25}=1" />.

The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{13}{25}" /> is the probability of the same colour. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac35" /> appears if the ball is not returned, <InlineMath math="2\cdot\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac24" />. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{21}{25}" /> is the probability of at least one red ball.

So the probability that the two balls have different colours is **<InlineMath math="\tfrac{12}{25}" />**.

All that counts is **who is paired with whom**. Since the pairs have no names, the order in which the pairs appear must not add to the count.

# Forming the pairs one at a time

Take any student, for example the first on the list. They can be paired with 5 others. Of the 4 students left, take the first on the list, who can be paired with 3 others. The last two students **automatically form a pair**.

<BlockMath math="5\cdot3\cdot1=15" />

# Check with combinations

If the pairs were numbered 1, 2 and 3, the count would be <InlineMath math="\binom62\binom42\binom22=15\cdot6\cdot1=90" />. Each unnumbered division appears **<InlineMath math="3!=6" /> times** in that count, once for each order of the numbers.

<BlockMath math="\frac{90}{3!}=15" />

The value 90 treats the pairs as numbered, and 45 only divides 90 by 2. The values 6 and 10 do not count every way of pairing the remaining students.

So the number of ways to divide the six students is **15**.

There are two conditions: all vowels stay together, and the first and last letters are consonants. The plan is to tie all the vowels into one **block**, arrange the inside of the block, decide where the block may go, and then arrange the consonants in the remaining places. Keep in mind that the word has **repeated letters**, so swapping two equal letters doesn't create a new arrangement.

# The letters of the word

The word STATISTIKA has <InlineMath math="10" /> letters. Its vowels are A, A, I, I, which is <InlineMath math="4" /> letters. Its consonants are S, S, T, T, T, K, which is <InlineMath math="6" /> letters. So S appears <InlineMath math="2" /> times, T appears <InlineMath math="3" /> times, A appears <InlineMath math="2" /> times and I appears <InlineMath math="2" /> times.

# Arrangements inside the block

The four vowels are arranged inside the block. Since the A's repeat and the I's repeat, divide by the number of ways to swap equal letters.

<BlockMath math="\frac{4!}{2!\,2!}=\frac{24}{4}=6" />

# Where the block and the consonants go

Treat the block as one object. Now there are <InlineMath math="7" /> objects in a row: the block and six consonants. The first and last letters must be consonants, so **the block can't sit in the first or last place**. It can only go in places two to six, which gives <InlineMath math="5" /> choices. The six consonants fill the other six places, with S repeated twice and T repeated three times.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{consonant arrangements} &= \frac{6!}{2!\,3!}=\frac{720}{12}=60 \\
\text{number of arrangements} &= 5\cdot 60\cdot 6=1{,}800
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking

Without the condition on the first and last letters, the seven objects can be arranged in <InlineMath math="\tfrac{7!}{2!\,3!}=420" /> ways. The block is equally likely to sit in each of the seven places, so **exactly <InlineMath math="5" /> out of <InlineMath math="7" /> parts** keep the block away from the ends. That gives <InlineMath math="420\cdot\tfrac{5}{7}\cdot 6=300\cdot 6=1{,}800" />, the same as before.

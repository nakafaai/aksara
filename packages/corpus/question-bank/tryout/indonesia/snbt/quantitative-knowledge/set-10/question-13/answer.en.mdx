# Where the center is

This circle touches <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="AD" /> inside the corner at <InlineMath math="A" />, so its center is <InlineMath math="O=(r,r)" />. # The radius

Point <InlineMath math="C" /> is on the circle, so its distance to <InlineMath math="O" /> equals <InlineMath math="r" />.

<BlockMath math="OC=\sqrt{(s-r)^2+(s-r)^2}=\sqrt{2}\,\lvert s-r\rvert=r" />

The absolute value gives two cases.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
s-r=\tfrac{r}{\sqrt{2}} &\;\Longrightarrow\; r=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,s}{\sqrt{2}+1}=s(2-\sqrt{2})=(2+\sqrt{2})(2-\sqrt{2})=2 \\
r-s=\tfrac{r}{\sqrt{2}} &\;\Longrightarrow\; r=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,s}{\sqrt{2}-1}=s(2+\sqrt{2})=(2+\sqrt{2})^2=6+4\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}" />

The second circle touches the line <InlineMath math="AB" /> at <InlineMath math="(6+4\sqrt{2},0)" />, far beyond side <InlineMath math="AB" />, which is only <InlineMath math="2+\sqrt{2}" /> long. The problem asks for **touching points on the sides**, so you drop the second circle. The radius is <InlineMath math="r=2" /> and the center is <InlineMath math="O=(2,2)" />.

# The second intersection point

Side <InlineMath math="BC" /> lies on the line <InlineMath math="x=s" />. The distance from <InlineMath math="O" /> to that line is <InlineMath math="s-2=\sqrt{2}" />. The perpendicular from the center to a chord **cuts the chord into two equal halves**, so find half of chord <InlineMath math="CP" /> with the Pythagorean theorem.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac{1}{2}CP &= \sqrt{r^2-(\sqrt{2})^2}=\sqrt{4-2}=\sqrt{2} \\
CP &= 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="C" /> sits at height <InlineMath math="2+\sqrt{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="P" /> sits at height <InlineMath math="2-\sqrt{2}" />. # Checking with the power of a point

The circle touches <InlineMath math="AB" /> at <InlineMath math="T=(2,0)" />, so <InlineMath math="BT=s-2=\sqrt{2}" />. From point <InlineMath math="B" />, **the tangent length squared equals the product of the two pieces of a line** that cuts through the circle, here <InlineMath math="BP" /> and <InlineMath math="BC" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
BT^2 &= BP\cdot BC \\
2 &= BP\cdot(2+\sqrt{2}) \\
BP &= \frac{2}{2+\sqrt{2}}=2-\sqrt{2} \\
CP &= (2+\sqrt{2})-(2-\sqrt{2})=2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}" />

It matches the first method.

# The wrong options

- <InlineMath math="(2-\sqrt{2})\text{ cm}" /> is the length <InlineMath math="BP" />, not <InlineMath math="CP" />.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}" /> is only half the chord, because it wasn't doubled.
- <InlineMath math="(2+\sqrt{2})\text{ cm}" /> assumes the circle also passes through <InlineMath math="B" />, so that <InlineMath math="P=B" />.
- <InlineMath math="(8+6\sqrt{2})\text{ cm}" /> uses the second circle with <InlineMath math="r=6+4\sqrt{2}" />, whose touching points lie outside the sides of the square.

So the length of <InlineMath math="CP" /> is <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}" />.

No iron is gained or lost, so **the volume of the cone equals the volume of the sphere**.

# Equating the volumes

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac43\pi\cdot6^3&=\tfrac13\pi\cdot6^2\cdot t\\
288\pi&=12\pi t\\
t&=24
\end{aligned}" />

# A general check

With equal radii, the equation <InlineMath math="\tfrac43\pi r^3=\tfrac13\pi r^2t" /> gives **a height four times the radius**, <InlineMath math="t=4r" />. For <InlineMath math="r=6" />, the height is 24, matching the result above.

The value 8 forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> in the cone volume, treating it like a cylinder. The value 4 uses <InlineMath math="r^2" /> in the sphere volume. The value 12 takes the cone's height as the sphere's diameter.

So the height of the cone is **<InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}" />**.

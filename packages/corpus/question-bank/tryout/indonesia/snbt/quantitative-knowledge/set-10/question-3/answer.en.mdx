Counting the cases in which either sibling wins can count the case in which both win twice. The complement, where neither sibling is selected, is cleaner.

Removing the two siblings leaves <InlineMath math="2998" /> other participants. Therefore:

Let <InlineMath math="E" /> be the event that neither sibling wins. The event that at least one wins is its complement, <InlineMath math="E^c" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(E) &= \frac{\binom{2998}{20}}{\binom{3000}{20}} \\ &= \frac{2980}{3000}\cdot\frac{2979}{2999} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(E^c) &= 1-P(E) \\ &= 1-\frac{2980\cdot2979}{3000\cdot2999} \\ &= \frac{1993}{149950} \\ &\approx 0.0133 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The probability that at least one sibling wins is approximately <InlineMath math="0.0133" />, or <InlineMath math="1.33\%" />.

Counting the cases in which either sibling wins can count the case in which both win twice. The complement, where neither sibling is selected, is cleaner.

Removing the two siblings leaves <InlineMath math="2998" /> other participants. Therefore:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(\text{neither wins}) &= \frac{\binom{2998}{20}}{\binom{3000}{20}} \\ &= \frac{2980}{3000}\cdot\frac{2979}{2999} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(\text{at least one wins}) &= 1-P(\text{neither wins}) \\ &= 1-\frac{2980\cdot2979}{3000\cdot2999} \\ &= \frac{1993}{149950} \\ &\approx 0.0133 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The probability that at least one sibling wins is approximately <InlineMath math="0.0133" />, or <InlineMath math="1.33\%" />.

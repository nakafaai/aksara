The factors must come from **two different sets**. First, write down the form of each set:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="A = \{-1, -2, -3, \dots\}" />
  <BlockMath math="B = \{2, 4, 6, \dots\}" />
  <BlockMath math="C = \{3, 6, 9, \dots\}" />
</MathContainer>

Now check whether each option can be written as a product using one of the three allowed set pairs: <InlineMath math="A" /> with <InlineMath math="B" />, <InlineMath math="A" /> with <InlineMath math="C" />, or <InlineMath math="B" /> with <InlineMath math="C" />.

Four options can be formed from **members of two different sets**:

| Number | Factorization | Factor membership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="-14" /> | <InlineMath math="(-7)\cdot2" /> | <InlineMath math="-7\in A,\ 2\in B" /> |
| <InlineMath math="-15" /> | <InlineMath math="(-5)\cdot3" /> | <InlineMath math="-5\in A,\ 3\in C" /> |
| <InlineMath math="-18" /> | <InlineMath math="(-3)\cdot6" /> | <InlineMath math="-3\in A,\ 6\in B\cap C" /> |
| <InlineMath math="18" /> | <InlineMath math="2\cdot9" /> | <InlineMath math="2\in B,\ 9\in C" /> |

A **positive product** must use <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="C" />. Using a negative member of <InlineMath math="A" /> with a positive member always gives a negative result. Thus, for positive integers <InlineMath math="m,n" />, every positive product has the form:

<BlockMath math="(2m)(3n)=6mn" />

The number <InlineMath math="14" /> is not a multiple of six, so it cannot be formed.

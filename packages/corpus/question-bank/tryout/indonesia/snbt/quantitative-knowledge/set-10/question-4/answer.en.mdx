You need to get rid of <InlineMath math="a" /> and keep a relation between <InlineMath math="b" />, <InlineMath math="c" /> and <InlineMath math="d" />. The plan is to call the common value of the four powers <InlineMath math="k" />, turn each exponent into a logarithm with base <InlineMath math="k" />, and then use the fact that **<InlineMath math="6" /> and <InlineMath math="12" /> are built from the factors <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />**.

# Turning exponents into logarithms

Let <InlineMath math="2^a=3^b=6^c=12^d=k" />. The number <InlineMath math="k" /> is positive, because every power of a positive number is positive. Also <InlineMath math="k\ne 1" />, since <InlineMath math="2^a=1" /> only happens when <InlineMath math="a=0" />, and <InlineMath math="a" /> isn't zero. So **you're allowed to use logarithms with base <InlineMath math="k" />**.

From <InlineMath math="2^a=k" /> you get <InlineMath math="a=\log_2 k" />. The rule <InlineMath math="\log_p q\cdot\log_q p=1" /> flips the base.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{a} &= \log_k 2, & \frac{1}{b} &= \log_k 3, \\
\frac{1}{c} &= \log_k 6, & \frac{1}{d} &= \log_k 12
\end{aligned}" />

# Two new equations

The logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms. Use <InlineMath math="6=2\cdot 3" /> and <InlineMath math="12=2\cdot 2\cdot 3" />. Notice that **the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> appears twice in <InlineMath math="12" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{c} &= \log_k 2+\log_k 3=\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b} \\
\frac{1}{d} &= 2\log_k 2+\log_k 3=\frac{2}{a}+\frac{1}{b}
\end{aligned}" />

# Removing one variable

Multiply the first equation by <InlineMath math="2" />, then subtract the second equation. That way **the terms <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2}{a}" /> cancel**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{c}-\frac{1}{d} &= \left(\frac{2}{a}+\frac{2}{b}\right)-\left(\frac{2}{a}+\frac{1}{b}\right)=\frac{1}{b} \\
\frac{1}{b} &= \frac{2d-c}{cd} \\
b &= \frac{cd}{2d-c}
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking

Work out the left side again directly with logarithms. It really is <InlineMath math="\log_k 3" />, which is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{b}" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{2}{c}-\frac{1}{d}=2\log_k 6-\log_k 12=\log_k\frac{36}{12}=\log_k 3" />

The denominator <InlineMath math="2d-c" /> can't be zero either, because **<InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{b}=\log_k 3" /> isn't zero** when <InlineMath math="k\ne 1" />.

# The wrong options

Each other option comes from **an elimination step that went the wrong way**.

- <InlineMath math="\dfrac{cd}{c-d}" /> is the value of <InlineMath math="a" />. It shows up when you subtract the two equations directly, which removes <InlineMath math="b" /> instead.
- <InlineMath math="\dfrac{cd}{d-c}" /> comes from that same subtraction with the sign flipped.
- <InlineMath math="\dfrac{cd}{2c-d}" /> swaps the roles of <InlineMath math="c" /> and <InlineMath math="d" />, as if <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{b}=\tfrac{2}{d}-\tfrac{1}{c}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\dfrac{cd}{c+d}" /> copies the pattern <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{c}=\tfrac{1}{a}+\tfrac{1}{b}" /> onto <InlineMath math="b" />, <InlineMath math="c" /> and <InlineMath math="d" />, but that pattern only holds because <InlineMath math="6=2\cdot 3" />.

So <InlineMath math="b=\dfrac{cd}{2d-c}" />.

The factors must come from two different sets. First, write down the form of each set:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="A = \{-1, -2, -3, \dots\}" />
  <BlockMath math="B = \{2, 4, 6, \dots\}" />
  <BlockMath math="C = \{3, 6, 9, \dots\}" />
</MathContainer>

Now check whether each option can be written as a product using one of the three allowed set pairs: <InlineMath math="A" /> with <InlineMath math="B" />, <InlineMath math="A" /> with <InlineMath math="C" />, or <InlineMath math="B" /> with <InlineMath math="C" />.

#### Check each number

1. <InlineMath math="-14" />

    <MathContainer>
       <BlockMath math="-14 = (-7) \times 2" />
    </MathContainer>

    Here, <InlineMath math="-7 \in A" /> and <InlineMath math="2 \in B" />, so this product uses two different sets.

2. <InlineMath math="-15" />

    <MathContainer>
       <BlockMath math="-15 = (-5) \times 3" />
    </MathContainer>

    Here, <InlineMath math="-5 \in A" /> and <InlineMath math="3 \in C" />, so this product also uses two different sets.

3. <InlineMath math="-18" />

    <MathContainer>
       <BlockMath math="-18 = (-3) \times 6" />
    </MathContainer>

    Here, <InlineMath math="-3 \in A" /> and <InlineMath math="6 \in B" />. Since <InlineMath math="6 \in C" /> as well, either <InlineMath math="A" /> with <InlineMath math="B" /> or <InlineMath math="A" /> with <InlineMath math="C" /> works.

4. <InlineMath math="14" />

    <MathContainer>
       <BlockMath math="14 = 2 \times 7" />
    </MathContainer>

    Here, <InlineMath math="2 \in B" />. However, <InlineMath math="7" /> is a positive odd integer that is not divisible by <InlineMath math="3" />. This means:

    - <InlineMath math="7 \notin A" /> because <InlineMath math="A" /> contains negative integers.
    - <InlineMath math="7 \notin B" /> because <InlineMath math="7" /> is odd.
    - <InlineMath math="7 \notin C" /> because <InlineMath math="7" /> is not divisible by <InlineMath math="3" />.

    A positive product cannot use <InlineMath math="A" /> with either positive set because that would produce a negative result. The only remaining pair is <InlineMath math="B" /> with <InlineMath math="C" />, whose product must be divisible by <InlineMath math="6" />. Since <InlineMath math="14" /> is not divisible by <InlineMath math="6" />, it cannot be formed.

5. <InlineMath math="18" />

    <MathContainer>
       <BlockMath math="18 = 2 \times 9" />
    </MathContainer>

    Here, <InlineMath math="2 \in B" /> and <InlineMath math="9 \in C" />, so this product uses two different sets.

Therefore, the number that cannot be obtained from two different sets is <InlineMath math="14" />.

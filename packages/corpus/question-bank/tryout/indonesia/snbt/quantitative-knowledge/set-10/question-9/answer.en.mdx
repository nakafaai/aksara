You need the numerator of <InlineMath math="P_n" /> **after the fraction is simplified**, for every <InlineMath math="n" /> from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="100" />. The plan is to turn the long product into one short fraction, simplify that fraction according to the type of <InlineMath math="n" />, and then count.

# A short form of the product

Each factor splits into two fractions, using the difference of squares <InlineMath math="k^2-1=(k-1)(k+1)" />.

<BlockMath math="1-\frac{1}{k^2}=\frac{k^2-1}{k^2}=\frac{k-1}{k}\cdot\frac{k+1}{k}" />

Collect all fractions of the first kind and all fractions of the second kind. In each group, **the denominator of one fraction cancels with the numerator of the next one**, so only the ends survive.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{4}\cdots\frac{n-1}{n} &= \frac{1}{n} \\
\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{5}{4}\cdots\frac{n+1}{n} &= \frac{n+1}{2} \\
P_n &= \frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{n+1}{2}=\frac{n+1}{2n}
\end{aligned}" />

# Simplifying the fraction

The numbers <InlineMath math="n" /> and <InlineMath math="n+1" /> are consecutive, so their greatest common divisor is <InlineMath math="1" />. That means the only factor that can cancel in <InlineMath math="\tfrac{n+1}{2n}" /> is a factor <InlineMath math="2" />. So **whether <InlineMath math="n" /> is even or odd decides the simplest form**.

- If <InlineMath math="n" /> is even, <InlineMath math="n+1" /> is odd, so nothing cancels. The numerator is <InlineMath math="n+1" />, which is odd.
- If <InlineMath math="n" /> is odd, <InlineMath math="n+1" /> is even, so you divide the top and the bottom by <InlineMath math="2" />. You get <InlineMath math="P_n=\dfrac{(n+1)/2}{n}" />, with numerator <InlineMath math="\tfrac{n+1}{2}" />.

# When the numerator is even

Only odd <InlineMath math="n" /> can work, and the numerator <InlineMath math="\tfrac{n+1}{2}" /> has to be even. That means **<InlineMath math="n+1" /> is a multiple of <InlineMath math="4" />**, so <InlineMath math="n=3,7,11,\ldots,99" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{99-3}{4}+1=25" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}

Even numerators show up exactly at <InlineMath math="n=3" /> and <InlineMath math="n=7" />, which fits the pattern **<InlineMath math="n" /> leaves remainder <InlineMath math="3" /> when divided by <InlineMath math="4" />**.

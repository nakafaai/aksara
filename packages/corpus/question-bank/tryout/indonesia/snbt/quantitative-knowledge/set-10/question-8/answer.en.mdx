Profit is revenue minus cost, and its percentage is **measured against the cost**. The rotten oranges are still part of the cost, but they bring no revenue.

# Revenue and profit

The rotten oranges weigh <InlineMath math="10\%\cdot50=5" /> kg, so **only 45 kg are sold**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{revenue}&=45\cdot16{,}000=720{,}000\\
\text{profit}&=720{,}000-600{,}000=120{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

# Percentage of the cost

<BlockMath math="\frac{120{,}000}{600{,}000}\times100\%=20\%" />

The value <InlineMath math="33\tfrac13\%" /> assumes **all 50 kg are sold**. The value <InlineMath math="16\tfrac23\%" /> divides the profit by the revenue instead of the cost. The value <InlineMath math="25\%" /> compares the price difference per kilogram with the selling price, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{16{,}000-12{,}000}{16{,}000}" />, and <InlineMath math="10\%" /> is the share of rotten oranges.

So the profit as a percentage of the cost is **<InlineMath math="20\%" />**.

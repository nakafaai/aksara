The sample space is 30 equally likely cards, so every probability is **the number of suitable numbers divided by 30**. List the numbers one by one so that none is missed.

# Primes and perfect squares

The primes from 1 to 30 are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 and 29, ten in all. **The number 1 is not prime**. The probability is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{10}{30}=\tfrac13" />, so the first statement is true.

The perfect squares are 1, 4, 9, 16 and 25, five in all. The probability is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5}{30}=\tfrac16" />, not <InlineMath math="\tfrac15" />. The second statement is false.

# Two conditions at once

An even number that is also a multiple of 5 is **a multiple of 10**: 10, 20 and 30. The probability is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{30}=\tfrac{1}{10}" />, so the third statement is true.

The odd numbers greater than 20 are 21, 23, 25, 27 and 29, five in all. The probability is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5}{30}=\tfrac16" />, not <InlineMath math="\tfrac15" />. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **false**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **false**.

A broken duck egg meets **both conditions at once**, so count it only once in the event “duck or broken”. Separate the eggs by type and condition:

| Type | Broken | Intact | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Duck | <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot10=5" /> | <InlineMath math="10-5=5" /> | <InlineMath math="10" /> |
| Chicken | <InlineMath math="\frac14\cdot20=5" /> | <InlineMath math="20-5=15" /> | <InlineMath math="20" /> |

All ten duck eggs qualify, whether broken or intact. Add the five broken chicken eggs. These two groups are disjoint, so:

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{10+5}{10+20}=\frac{15}{30}=\frac12" />

The required probability is <InlineMath math="0.5" />. Inclusion-exclusion gives the same favorable count: <InlineMath math="10+(5+5)-5=15" />.

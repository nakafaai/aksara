From each area, first find **the side length of each shape**, then compute the perimeters.

# Side and perimeter of the equilateral triangle

**The area of an equilateral triangle** with side <InlineMath math="s" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2&=9\sqrt3\\
s^2&=36,\quad s=6\\
P&=3\cdot6=18
\end{aligned}" />

# Side and perimeter of the square

The side of the square is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{25}=5" />, so <InlineMath math="Q=4\cdot5=20" />.

Although the triangle's area, <InlineMath math="9\sqrt3\approx15.6" />, is smaller than the square's, **perimeter and area do not always come in the same order**, so both still have to be computed. Here <InlineMath math="20>18" />.

So the correct relation is **<InlineMath math="Q>P" />**.

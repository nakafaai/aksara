The percentages 40% and 25% are taken from groups of different sizes. So the two percentages **cannot simply be averaged**: each must be weighted by the share of its group.

# The share of each group

Take **100 students**, so there are 60 girls and 40 boys.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{girls with glasses}&=40\%\times60=24\\
\text{boys with glasses}&=25\%\times40=10
\end{aligned}" />

# The overall percentage

The number of students with glasses is **the two groups combined**, <InlineMath math="24+10=34" /> out of 100 students, which is 34%.

The value 32.5% is the plain average of 40% and 25%, which treats the groups as equal in size. The value 31% swaps the percentages of the two groups. The values 24% and 10% each count only one group.

So **34%** of the students wear glasses.

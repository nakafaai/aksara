The circle has no constant term, so the point <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> lies on it. The line <InlineMath math="y=kx" /> always passes through the origin as well, so **the line must touch the circle exactly at the origin**.

# Substituting the line into the circle

Replace <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="kx" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2+k^2x^2-6x+8kx&=0\\
x\left[(1+k^2)x-(6-8k)\right]&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The root <InlineMath math="x=0" /> is the origin. The second root is <InlineMath math="x=\dfrac{6-8k}{1+k^2}" />. The line meets the circle at one point only when **the second root is also zero**.

<BlockMath math="6-8k=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac34" />

# Checking with the radius

The centre of the circle is <InlineMath math="(3,-4)" />, so the radius to the origin has slope <InlineMath math="-\tfrac43" />. A tangent is **perpendicular to the radius**, so its slope is <InlineMath math="\tfrac34" />, because <InlineMath math="-\tfrac43\cdot\tfrac34=-1" />.

The value <InlineMath math="-\tfrac43" /> is the slope of that radius itself, and a line through the centre cuts the circle at two points. The value <InlineMath math="-\tfrac34" /> comes from a sign error in the term <InlineMath math="8kx" />. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac43" /> flips the slope without changing its sign. The line <InlineMath math="y=0" /> cuts the circle at <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(6,0)" />.

So the value of <InlineMath math="k" /> is **<InlineMath math="\tfrac34" />**.

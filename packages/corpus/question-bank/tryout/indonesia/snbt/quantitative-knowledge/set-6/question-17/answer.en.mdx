The question asks for a definite yes or a definite no. Since <InlineMath math="12=2^2\cdot3" />, we need to know whether **the prime factorisation of <InlineMath math="n" /> contains at least two factors of 2 and one factor of 3**.

# Statement (1) alone

Write <InlineMath math="72=2^3\cdot3^2" />. If <InlineMath math="n" /> contains the factor 2 exactly <InlineMath math="a" /> times and the factor 3 exactly <InlineMath math="b" /> times, then <InlineMath math="n^2" /> contains 2 exactly <InlineMath math="2a" /> times and 3 exactly <InlineMath math="2b" /> times. **The exponents of a square are always even**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2a\ge3&\;\Longrightarrow\; a\ge2\\
2b\ge2&\;\Longrightarrow\; b\ge1
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="n" /> has <InlineMath math="2^2\cdot3=12" /> as a factor, and the answer is definitely yes. For example, <InlineMath math="n=12" /> gives <InlineMath math="n^2=144=2\cdot72" />. Statement (1) alone is sufficient.

# Statement (2) alone

The number <InlineMath math="n=6" /> is divisible by 6 but **not divisible by 12**, while <InlineMath math="n=12" /> is divisible by both. The answer can be yes or no, so statement (2) alone is not sufficient.

So **statement (1) alone is sufficient**, but statement (2) alone is not.

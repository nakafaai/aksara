Because the greatest common divisor is 12, the two numbers can be written <InlineMath math="a=12m" /> and <InlineMath math="b=12n" /> with <InlineMath math="m" /> and <InlineMath math="n" /> **sharing no factor other than 1**. In this form, the least common multiple is <InlineMath math="12mn" />.

# Finding the possible pairs

<BlockMath math="12mn=180\quad\Rightarrow\quad mn=15" />

The coprime pairs <InlineMath math="(m,n)" /> with product 15 are <InlineMath math="(1,15)" /> and <InlineMath math="(3,5)" />. **The sum condition picks one of them**.

| <InlineMath math="(m,n)" /> | Numbers | Sum |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="(1,15)" /> | 12 and 180 | 192 |
| <InlineMath math="(3,5)" /> | 36 and 60 | 96 |

# Computing the difference

The numbers are 36 and 60. Check: their greatest common divisor is 12, their least common multiple is 180, and their sum is 96.

<BlockMath math="60-36=24" />

The value 168 is the difference of the other pair, 12 and 180, whose sum is not 96. The value 12 is the greatest common divisor, the value 15 is the product <InlineMath math="mn" />, and the value 36 is the smaller number.

So the difference between the two numbers is **24**.

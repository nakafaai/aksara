Counting the rectangles that avoid both squares directly is hard, because there are so many shapes. It's easier through the **complement**: count every rectangle, then subtract those that contain at least one shaded square. That last part uses the **inclusion-exclusion principle**, so rectangles containing both shaded squares don't get subtracted twice.

# Counting every rectangle

Number the horizontal lines <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="4" /> from the top, so row <InlineMath math="i" /> sits between line <InlineMath math="i-1" /> and line <InlineMath math="i" />. Number the vertical lines <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="6" /> from the left the same way. **Every rectangle is fixed by exactly two horizontal lines and two vertical lines**, so you just choose two of the <InlineMath math="5" /> horizontal lines and two of the <InlineMath math="7" /> vertical lines.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\binom{5}{2}\times\binom{7}{2} &= 10\times21 \\
&= 210
\end{aligned}" />

# Containing the first shaded square

The square in row <InlineMath math="1" />, column <InlineMath math="2" /> lies between horizontal lines <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="1" /> and between vertical lines <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="2" />. A rectangle contains it exactly when **its top line isn't below the square and its bottom line isn't above it**, and the same goes for left and right.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{top} &\in\{0\}, & \text{bottom} &\in\{1,2,3,4\} \\
\text{left} &\in\{0,1\}, & \text{right} &\in\{2,3,4,5,6\} \\
\text{count} &= 1\times4\times2\times5=40
\end{aligned}" />

# Containing the second shaded square

The square in row <InlineMath math="3" />, column <InlineMath math="5" /> lies between horizontal lines <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> and between vertical lines <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />. Use **the same rule for choosing lines**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{top} &\in\{0,1,2\}, & \text{bottom} &\in\{3,4\} \\
\text{left} &\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}, & \text{right} &\in\{5,6\} \\
\text{count} &= 3\times2\times5\times2=60
\end{aligned}" />

# Containing both squares at once

Now the top line has to be above both squares, the bottom line below both, and so on. So **each line has to meet the stricter of the two conditions**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{top} &\in\{0\}, & \text{bottom} &\in\{3,4\} \\
\text{left} &\in\{0,1\}, & \text{right} &\in\{5,6\} \\
\text{count} &= 1\times2\times2\times2=8
\end{aligned}" />

# Putting it together with inclusion-exclusion

The rectangles containing both squares were counted in the <InlineMath math="40" /> and again in the <InlineMath math="60" />, so **subtract that double count once**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{containing at least one square} &= 40+60-8=92 \\
\text{containing no shaded square} &= 210-92=118
\end{aligned}" />

Sanity check: <InlineMath math="92" /> is less than <InlineMath math="40+60=100" />, and the gap is exactly the <InlineMath math="8" /> rectangles that contain both squares. A common slip is stopping at <InlineMath math="210-40-60=110" />, which subtracts those eight rectangles twice.

So the number of rectangles that contain no shaded square is <InlineMath math="118" />.

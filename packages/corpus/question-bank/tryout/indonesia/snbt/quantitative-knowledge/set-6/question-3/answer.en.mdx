Let <InlineMath math="B" /> represent a boy and <InlineMath math="G" /> a girl. Under the stated independence and equal-probability assumptions, all ordered birth outcomes are equally likely.

Before applying the condition, the sample space has <InlineMath math="2^3 = 8" /> outcomes:

<BlockMath math="S_0=\left\{\begin{gathered}BBB, BBG, BGB,\\BGG, GBB, GBG,\\GGB, GGG\end{gathered}\right\}" />

It is given that "at least one child is a girl". This means the event "all children are boys" (<InlineMath math="BBB" />) is impossible.

The new sample space (<InlineMath math="S" />) consists of all possibilities except <InlineMath math="BBB" />:

<BlockMath math="S=\left\{\begin{gathered}BBG, BGB, BGG,\\GBB, GBG, GGB,\\GGG\end{gathered}\right\}" />

The number of elements in the sample space is <InlineMath math="n(S) = 7" />.

Let event <InlineMath math="A" /> be having exactly <InlineMath math="2" /> girls and <InlineMath math="1" /> boy.

From the sample space <InlineMath math="S" />, the outcomes that satisfy this condition are:

<BlockMath math="A = \{BGG, GBG, GGB\}" />

The number of favorable outcomes is <InlineMath math="n(A) = 3" />.

Because the <InlineMath math="7" /> conditional outcomes remain equally likely, the probability is:

<BlockMath math="P(A) = \frac{n(A)}{n(S)} = \frac{3}{7}" />

Therefore, the probability that the family has <InlineMath math="2" /> girls and <InlineMath math="1" /> boy is <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{7}" />.

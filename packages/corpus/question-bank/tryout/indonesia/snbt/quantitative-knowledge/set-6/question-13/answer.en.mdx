The line <InlineMath math="AG" /> is a space diagonal and <InlineMath math="BD" /> is a diagonal of the base. They **don't meet and aren't parallel**, so you can't read the angle straight off one triangle in the box. A clean way is to use coordinates: the angle between two lines is the acute angle between their direction vectors.

# Setting up coordinates

Put <InlineMath math="A" /> at the origin, the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis along <InlineMath math="AB" />, the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis along <InlineMath math="AD" />, and the <InlineMath math="z" />-axis along <InlineMath math="AE" />. Since <InlineMath math="AD=BC=4" />, **every corner can be written straight from the box's measurements**.

<BlockMath math="A(0,0,0),\quad B(2,0,0),\quad D(0,4,0),\quad G(2,4,4)" />

# Finding the direction vectors

A line's direction vector is its end point minus its start point. Then find the lengths of both vectors and **their dot product**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AG} &= (2,4,4), & \lvert\overrightarrow{AG}\rvert &= \sqrt{4+16+16}=6 \\
\overrightarrow{BD} &= (-2,4,0), & \lvert\overrightarrow{BD}\rvert &= \sqrt{4+16}=2\sqrt5 \\
\overrightarrow{AG}\cdot\overrightarrow{BD} &= 2(-2)+4(4)+4(0)=12
\end{aligned}" />

# Taking the acute angle

A line has no front or back. If you use <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{DB}=(2,-4,0)" />, the dot product becomes <InlineMath math="-12" /> and the angle turns obtuse, even though the line is the same. That's why **the angle between two lines is always the acute one**, so you use the absolute value of the dot product.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\cos\theta &= \frac{\lvert\overrightarrow{AG}\cdot\overrightarrow{BD}\rvert}{\lvert\overrightarrow{AG}\rvert\,\lvert\overrightarrow{BD}\rvert} \\
&= \frac{12}{6\cdot2\sqrt5}=\frac{1}{\sqrt5}=\frac{\sqrt5}{5}
\end{aligned}" />

Sanity check: the value lies between <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="1" />, as it should for an acute angle. If the base were a square (<InlineMath math="AB=AD" />), the dot product would be <InlineMath math="-AB^2+AD^2=0" /> and the lines would be perpendicular. Here <InlineMath math="AB\ne AD" />, so the angle isn't a right angle.

# Why the other options fail

The wrong options come from **swapping a line for a different one**.

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac35" /> is the cosine of the angle between <InlineMath math="AC" /> and <InlineMath math="BD" />, which is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12}{2\sqrt5\cdot2\sqrt5}=\tfrac{12}{20}" />. It comes from replacing <InlineMath math="AG" /> with its projection on the base, but the angle with a projection isn't the angle with the original line.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> comes from the cube habit, where <InlineMath math="AG" /> is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="BD" />. That only works when <InlineMath math="AB=AD" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac23" /> is the cosine of the angle between <InlineMath math="AG" /> and the vertical edge <InlineMath math="AE" />, which is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4}{6}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{\sqrt5}{3}" /> is the cosine of the angle between <InlineMath math="AG" /> and the base diagonal <InlineMath math="AC" />, which is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2\sqrt5}{6}" />. The question asks about the base diagonal <InlineMath math="BD" />, not <InlineMath math="AC" />.

So the cosine of the angle between line <InlineMath math="AG" /> and line <InlineMath math="BD" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{\sqrt5}{5}" />.

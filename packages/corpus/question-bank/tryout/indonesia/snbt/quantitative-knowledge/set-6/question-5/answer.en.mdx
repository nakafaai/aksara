The value of <InlineMath math="x" /> isn't given, so you have to check the relation between <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> for **every positive integer**. If the relation changes from one value of <InlineMath math="x" /> to another, the information isn't enough. The method: square twice until the roots disappear, then look at the sign of the difference.

# Comparing the squares

Both quantities are positive. For two positive numbers, the larger one also has the larger square, so **it's enough to compare <InlineMath math="P^2" /> and <InlineMath math="Q^2" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P^2 &= x+2\sqrt{x}\sqrt{x+8}+(x+8)=2x+8+2\sqrt{x^2+8x} \\
Q^2 &= 4(x+3)=4x+12 \\
P^2-Q^2 &= 2\left(\sqrt{x^2+8x}-(x+2)\right)
\end{aligned}" />

# Squaring once more

The sign of <InlineMath math="P^2-Q^2" /> matches the sign of <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x^2+8x}-(x+2)" />. Both of those terms are positive too, so **compare their squares again**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x^2+8x)-(x+2)^2 &= x^2+8x-x^2-4x-4 \\
&= 4x-4 \\
&= 4(x-1)
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="P>Q" /> exactly when <InlineMath math="x>1" />, <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> exactly when <InlineMath math="x=1" />, and <InlineMath math="P<Q" /> only when <InlineMath math="x<1" />.

# Testing every positive integer

The smallest positive integer is <InlineMath math="1" />, and **there the two quantities are equal**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x=1:\quad P &= \sqrt1+\sqrt9=1+3=4 \\
Q &= 2\sqrt4=4
\end{aligned}" />

At <InlineMath math="x=2" /> the difference of squares is <InlineMath math="4(2-1)=4" />, which is positive, so <InlineMath math="P>Q" />. The same holds for every <InlineMath math="x\ge2" />. Because <InlineMath math="x=1" /> gives <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> and <InlineMath math="x=2" /> gives <InlineMath math="P>Q" />, **no single relation can be picked**.

- <InlineMath math="P>Q" /> comes from only trying numbers above <InlineMath math="1" />, which misses the case <InlineMath math="x=1" />.

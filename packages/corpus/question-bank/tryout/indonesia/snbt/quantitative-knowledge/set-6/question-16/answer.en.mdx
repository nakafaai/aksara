Because every worker has the same constant rate, the total amount of work can be measured in worker-days and stays fixed.

Given:
*   <InlineMath math="15\text{ people}" /> can complete the work in <InlineMath math="35\text{ days}" />.
*   The work is desired to be completed in <InlineMath math="3" /> weeks <InlineMath math="= 3 \times 7 = 21\text{ days}" />.

Let <InlineMath math="P" /> be the total number of workers needed to complete the work in <InlineMath math="21\text{ days}" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="15 \times 35 = P \times 21" />
  <BlockMath math="525 = 21P" />
  <BlockMath math="P = \frac{525}{21}" />
  <BlockMath math="P = 25" />
</MathContainer>

So, a total of <InlineMath math="25\text{ workers}" /> are needed.

The question asks for the **additional** workers.
The initial number of workers is <InlineMath math="15" />.

<BlockMath math="25-15=10" />

Thus, <InlineMath math="10" /> additional workers are needed.

First count the numbers divisible by 4 or 6, then remove those that are also divisible by 9. The first step uses **the inclusion-exclusion principle**, because multiples of 12 are counted twice.

# Divisible by 4 or 6

The common multiples of 4 and 6 are **the multiples of their least common multiple**, 12.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\left\lfloor\tfrac{300}{4}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\tfrac{300}{6}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\tfrac{300}{12}\right\rfloor&=75+50-25\\
&=100
\end{aligned}" />

# Those also divisible by 9

A number from that group that is divisible by 9 is a multiple of <InlineMath math="\text{lcm}(4,9)=36" /> or of <InlineMath math="\text{lcm}(6,9)=18" />. Every multiple of 36 is also a multiple of 18, so **counting the multiples of 18 is enough**.

<BlockMath math="\left\lfloor\tfrac{300}{18}\right\rfloor=16" />

The numbers that remain number <InlineMath math="100-16=84" />.

The value 100 forgets to remove the multiples of 9. The value 92 removes only the 8 multiples of 36. The value 76 removes the 8 multiples of 36 and the 16 multiples of 18 together, although the multiples of 36 are already among the multiples of 18. The value 67 removes all 33 multiples of 9 from 1 to 300, including those that were never in the first group.

So the number of such integers is **84**.

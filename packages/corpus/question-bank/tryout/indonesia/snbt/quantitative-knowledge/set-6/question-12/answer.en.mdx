The number of positive factors of <InlineMath math="p_1^{e_1}p_2^{e_2}" /> is <InlineMath math="(e_1+1)(e_2+1)" />, because **each exponent is chosen independently** from 0 up to its highest value.

# Computing both quantities

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P&=(4+1)(2+1)=5\cdot3=15\\
Q&=(3+1)(3+1)=4\cdot4=16
\end{aligned}" />

# Comparing

Since <InlineMath math="16>15" />, **<InlineMath math="Q" /> is greater than <InlineMath math="P" />**, and this comparison is not decided by the sizes of the numbers, <InlineMath math="M=144" /> and <InlineMath math="N=216" />. Both numbers have exponents adding up to 6, but the product <InlineMath math="(e_1+1)(e_2+1)" /> is larger when the exponents are more even.

The option <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> appears when the number of factors is assumed to depend only on the sum of the exponents. So the correct relation is <InlineMath math="Q>P" />.

We already know that the ball drawn is red, so the question asks for **the conditional probability of box I given a red ball**. The sample space shrinks to all the ways of getting a red ball.

# Probability of each red path

A red ball can come from two paths. Multiply the probability of choosing a box by **the probability of drawing red from that box**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{I and red})&=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac35=\tfrac{3}{10}\\
P(\text{II and red})&=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac15=\tfrac{1}{10}\\
P(\text{red})&=\tfrac{3}{10}+\tfrac{1}{10}=\tfrac{4}{10}
\end{aligned}" />

# Conditional probability

Divide the probability of the box I path by **the probability of the whole red event**.

<BlockMath math="P(\text{I}\mid\text{red})=\frac{3/10}{4/10}=\frac34" />

The result makes sense: of the 4 red balls, 3 are in box I, and both boxes hold 5 balls.

The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{10}" /> is the probability of choosing box I and drawing red, before dividing by the probability of red. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac25" /> is the probability of drawing red. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac35" /> is the probability of red once box I is chosen, which reverses the condition. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> ignores the colour of the ball.

So the probability that the red ball came from box I is **<InlineMath math="\tfrac34" />**.

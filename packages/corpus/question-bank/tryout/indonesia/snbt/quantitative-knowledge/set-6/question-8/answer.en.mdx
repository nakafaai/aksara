The largest angle of a triangle **lies opposite the longest side**, here the 19 cm side. All three sides are known, so the angle comes from the cosine rule.

# The cosine rule

Let <InlineMath math="\theta" /> be **the angle opposite the side 19**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
19^2&=5^2+16^2-2\cdot5\cdot16\cos\theta\\
361&=281-160\cos\theta\\
\cos\theta&=\frac{281-361}{160}=-\frac12
\end{aligned}" />

# Finding the angle

A negative cosine means **the angle is obtuse**. The obtuse angle with cosine <InlineMath math="-\tfrac12" /> is <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.

The value <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> appears when the sign of the cosine flips to <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />. The value <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> assumes a right triangle, although <InlineMath math="5^2+16^2=281\ne361" />. The values <InlineMath math="135^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="150^\circ" /> use the cosines <InlineMath math="-\tfrac12\sqrt2" /> and <InlineMath math="-\tfrac12\sqrt3" />, which do not match the computed value.

So the largest angle of the triangle is <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.

The question asks for a definite yes or a definite no. A statement is sufficient when **every group that satisfies it gives the same answer**. Order the ages as <InlineMath math="a_1\le a_2\le\dots\le a_6" />. A mean above <InlineMath math="25" /> means the ages add up to more than <InlineMath math="6\times25=150" />.

# Statement one alone

Statement (1) says <InlineMath math="a_1+a_2+a_3=60" />, but the other three ages are **free as long as they aren't younger than <InlineMath math="a_3" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
20,20,20,40,40,40:\quad \text{sum} &= 180>150 \\
20,20,20,20,20,20:\quad \text{sum} &= 120<150
\end{aligned}" />

The first group answers yes and the second answers no. Statement (1) alone isn't sufficient.

# Statement two alone

With six values, the median is the mean of the third and fourth values, so statement (2) says <InlineMath math="a_3+a_4=60" />. **The youngest ages can be very small**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
30,30,30,30,30,30:\quad \text{sum} &= 180>150 \\
1,1,30,30,30,30:\quad \text{sum} &= 122<150
\end{aligned}" />

There's a yes and a no. Statement (2) alone isn't sufficient.

# Both statements together

Now <InlineMath math="a_1+a_2+a_3=60" /> and <InlineMath math="a_4=60-a_3" />. The age <InlineMath math="a_4" /> can't be smaller than <InlineMath math="a_3" />, so <InlineMath math="60-a_3\ge a_3" />, which means <InlineMath math="a_3\le30" />. The ages <InlineMath math="a_5" /> and <InlineMath math="a_6" /> are at least <InlineMath math="a_4" />. That gives a lower bound for the sum.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{sum} &= 60+a_4+a_5+a_6 \\
&\ge 60+3a_4=60+3(60-a_3) \\
&= 240-3a_3\ge240-90=150
\end{aligned}" />

So the mean is **at least <InlineMath math="25" />**, but the question asks for **more than <InlineMath math="25" />**. The bound <InlineMath math="150" /> really can be reached, when <InlineMath math="a_3=30" /> and <InlineMath math="a_4=a_5=a_6=30" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
15,15,30,30,30,30:\quad \text{sum} &= 150,\ \text{mean }25 \\
20,20,20,40,40,40:\quad \text{sum} &= 180,\ \text{mean }30
\end{aligned}" />

The first group satisfies both statements: the three youngest give <InlineMath math="15+15+30=60" /> and the median is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{30+30}{2}=30" />, but the mean is exactly <InlineMath math="25" />, so the answer is no. The second group satisfies both too, with median <InlineMath math="\tfrac{20+40}{2}=30" />, and the answer is yes. Even both statements together aren't sufficient. The trap is reading the bound at least <InlineMath math="25" /> as more than <InlineMath math="25" />.

So statement (1) and statement (2) are not sufficient to answer the question.

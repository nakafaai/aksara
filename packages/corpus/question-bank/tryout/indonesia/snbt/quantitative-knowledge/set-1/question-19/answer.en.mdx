Two conditions affect each other: Banu and Citra take **two adjacent seats**, while Ayu must sit in one of the middle seats. The safest way is to count by the position of the Banu and Citra pair.

# Counting by the position of the pair

Number the seats 1 to 5. The pair can take seats 1 and 2, 2 and 3, 3 and 4, or 4 and 5. For each position, count the free middle seats for Ayu, then Dodi and Eka fill the remaining two seats in 2 ways.

| Seats of Banu and Citra | Free seats | Seats for Ayu | Arrangements of Ayu, Dodi, Eka |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 and 2 | 3, 4, 5 | 3 or 4 | <InlineMath math="2\times2=4" /> |
| 2 and 3 | 1, 4, 5 | 4 | <InlineMath math="1\times2=2" /> |
| 3 and 4 | 1, 2, 5 | 2 | <InlineMath math="1\times2=2" /> |
| 4 and 5 | 1, 2, 3 | 2 or 3 | <InlineMath math="2\times2=4" /> |

The total is <InlineMath math="4+2+2+4=12" />. **Banu and Citra can swap places**, so each arrangement comes in two orders.

<BlockMath math="12\times2=24" />

# Checking the other options

The value 48 counts arrangements with Banu and Citra adjacent but ignores Ayu's condition, namely <InlineMath math="4!\times2" />. The value 12 forgets that Banu and Citra can swap places. The value 16 counts only the pair in seats 1 and 2 or 4 and 5. The value 36 puts Ayu in one of the 3 middle seats and then assumes the pair **always fits** in the remaining seats, although Ayu can split the free seats apart.

So **24** seating arrangements are possible.

A statement is enough when **every triangle that satisfies it has the same area**. The main tool is the median length formula, which links the three sides to <InlineMath math="AM" />. Check statement (1) on its own, then statement (2) on its own.

# Deriving the median formula

In triangles <InlineMath math="ABM" /> and <InlineMath math="ACM" />, the angles at <InlineMath math="M" /> add up to a straight angle, so **their cosines have opposite signs**. Write the law of cosines for both triangles, with <InlineMath math="BM=CM=\frac{BC}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="\theta=\angle AMB" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AB^2&=AM^2+BM^2-2\cdot AM\cdot BM\cos\theta\\
AC^2&=AM^2+CM^2+2\cdot AM\cdot CM\cos\theta\\
AB^2+AC^2&=2AM^2+\frac{BC^2}{2}
\end{aligned}" />

The third line is the sum of the first two. The cosine terms cancel because <InlineMath math="BM=CM" />.

# Statement one alone

Put <InlineMath math="AB=6" />, <InlineMath math="AC=8" />, and <InlineMath math="AM=5" /> into the median formula.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
36+64&=2\cdot25+\frac{BC^2}{2}\\
BC^2&=100\\
BC&=10
\end{aligned}" />

All three sides are now fixed at <InlineMath math="6" />, <InlineMath math="8" />, and <InlineMath math="10" />, so **the triangle is unique** and so is its area. Since <InlineMath math="6^2+8^2=10^2" />, angle <InlineMath math="A" /> is a right angle and the area is <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot6\cdot8=24\text{ cm}^2" />. Statement (1) alone is enough.

# Statement two alone

From <InlineMath math="BC=10" /> you get <InlineMath math="BM=MC=5=AM" />. Point <InlineMath math="A" /> is <InlineMath math="5" /> away from the midpoint of <InlineMath math="BC" />, so <InlineMath math="A" /> lies on the circle with diameter <InlineMath math="BC" />, and angle <InlineMath math="A" /> is a right angle. But **the two legs aren't fixed**, only <InlineMath math="AB^2+AC^2=100" />.

The first example, <InlineMath math="AB=6" /> and <InlineMath math="AC=8" />, gives an area of <InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}^2" />. The second example, <InlineMath math="AB=AC=5\sqrt2" />, also satisfies (2), since <InlineMath math="50+50=2\cdot25+\frac{100}{2}" />, but its area is <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot5\sqrt2\cdot5\sqrt2=25\text{ cm}^2" />. Two different areas mean statement (2) alone isn't enough.

# Why the other options are tempting

The options that need both statements, or that say neither is enough, come from thinking statement (1) **still needs the angle between <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="AC" />**, when the median already fixes the third side. The option that each statement alone is enough comes from thinking the right angle in statement (2) already fixes the area.

So statement (1) alone is enough to answer the question, but statement (2) alone isn't.

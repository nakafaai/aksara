The word MAMALIA has <InlineMath math="3" /> letters A, <InlineMath math="2" /> letters M, and one each of L and I. Two conditions hold at once: **no two A's side by side and no two M's side by side**. The plan: meet the A condition first with the gap method, then throw out the arrangements where the two M's still touch.

# Meeting the A condition

First arrange the four letters that aren't A, namely M, M, L, and I. The two M's are identical, so the number of arrangements is

<BlockMath math="\frac{4!}{2!}=12" />

These four letters make <InlineMath math="5" /> gaps, counting both ends. Put each A in a different gap, and **no two A's end up side by side**. The three A's are identical, so you just choose <InlineMath math="3" /> of the <InlineMath math="5" /> gaps.

<BlockMath math="12\cdot\binom{5}{3}=12\cdot10=120" />

# Throwing out arrangements where the M's touch

Out of those <InlineMath math="120" /> arrangements, count the ones where the two M's are next to each other. That happens when the two M's are adjacent among the four letters **and** no A goes between them.

Glue MM into one block, then arrange that block with L and I: there are <InlineMath math="3!=6" /> ways. Now there are <InlineMath math="4" /> gaps around these three units, and the gap inside the MM block **can't be used**, because an A there would split the two M's. Choose <InlineMath math="3" /> of the <InlineMath math="4" /> gaps.

<BlockMath math="6\cdot\binom{4}{3}=6\cdot4=24" />

So the number of arrangements meeting both conditions is

<BlockMath math="120-24=96" />

# Checking the result

The word MAMALIA itself qualifies, because the order M, A, M, A, L, I, A has no two equal letters side by side. Listing all <InlineMath math="420" /> arrangements one by one also gives **exactly <InlineMath math="96" /> arrangements** that pass.

# Common mistakes

The answer <InlineMath math="120" /> comes from **forgetting the M condition**. The answer <InlineMath math="192" /> comes from treating the two M's as different letters, which counts every arrangement twice. The answer <InlineMath math="420" /> is the number of all arrangements with no condition at all.

So the number of arrangements with no two equal letters side by side is <InlineMath math="96" />.

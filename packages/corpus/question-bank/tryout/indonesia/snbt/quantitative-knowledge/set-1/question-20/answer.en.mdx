The phrase "given that the product is even" makes this a **conditional probability**. The sample space is no longer all 36 pairs but only the pairs whose product is even.

# The new sample space

A product of two numbers is odd exactly when both numbers are odd. There are <InlineMath math="3\times3=9" /> odd-odd pairs out of 36, so **27 pairs have an even product**. Each pair is equally likely because the dice are fair.

# The required event

There are 15 pairs whose sum is at least 8.

| Sum | Pairs | Count |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 8 | (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2) | 5 |
| 9 | (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3) | 4 |
| 10 | (4,6), (5,5), (6,4) | 3 |
| 11 | (5,6), (6,5) | 2 |
| 12 | (6,6) | 1 |

From these 15 pairs, **remove those with two odd faces**: (3,5), (5,3), and (5,5). That leaves 12 pairs.

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{12}{27}=\frac49" />

The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac13=\tfrac{12}{36}" /> forgets to change the sample space. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac59=\tfrac{15}{27}" /> forgets to remove the three odd pairs from the event. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac23=\tfrac{12}{18}" /> assumes that only half of the pairs have an even product. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac45=\tfrac{12}{15}" /> reverses the condition: it is the probability of an even product given a sum of at least 8.

So the required probability is <InlineMath math="\tfrac49" />.

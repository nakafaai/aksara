What bag B holds at the second draw **depends on the colour of the ball that was moved**. So the plan: find what bag A holds from the given probability, split into two cases by the colour of the moved ball, compute the probability in each case, and combine them with their weights.

# Finding what bag A holds

Say bag A holds <InlineMath math="r" /> red balls. There are <InlineMath math="\binom52=10" /> ways to take two balls from <InlineMath math="5" />, and <InlineMath math="\binom r2" /> ways to take two red ones.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{\binom r2}{10}&=\frac{3}{10}\\
\frac{r(r-1)}{2}&=3\\
r^2-r-6&=0\\
(r-3)(r+2)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

A number of balls can't be negative, so <InlineMath math="r=3" />. Bag A holds **<InlineMath math="3" /> red balls and <InlineMath math="2" /> white balls**.

# Computing the probability in each case

The moved ball is red with probability <InlineMath math="\frac35" />. Bag B then holds **<InlineMath math="3" /> red and <InlineMath math="4" /> white**, <InlineMath math="7" /> balls in all. The moved ball is white with probability <InlineMath math="\frac25" />. Bag B then holds <InlineMath math="2" /> red and <InlineMath math="5" /> white. In both cases there are <InlineMath math="\binom72=21" /> ways to take two balls from <InlineMath math="7" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{two red}\mid\text{red moved})&=\frac{\binom32}{21}=\frac{3}{21}=\frac17\\
P(\text{two red}\mid\text{white moved})&=\frac{\binom22}{21}=\frac{1}{21}
\end{aligned}" />

# Combining the two cases

Each case is **multiplied by the probability that the case happens**, and then the results are added.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{two red})&=\frac35\cdot\frac17+\frac25\cdot\frac1{21}\\
&=\frac{9}{105}+\frac{2}{105}=\frac{11}{105}
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking the result

Count it directly as well. There are <InlineMath math="5" /> choices for the moved ball and <InlineMath math="21" /> choices for the pair from bag B, so there are <InlineMath math="5\cdot21=105" /> equally likely outcomes. The ones that work are <InlineMath math="3\cdot3=9" /> when a red ball is moved and <InlineMath math="2\cdot1=2" /> when a white ball is moved. **The result is the same, <InlineMath math="\frac{11}{105}" />**, and it lies between <InlineMath math="\frac1{21}" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac17" />.

# The wrong options

The option <InlineMath math="\frac1{15}" /> ignores the moved ball, so bag B seems to still hold <InlineMath math="6" /> balls. The option <InlineMath math="\frac3{35}" /> counts only the case where a red ball is moved and forgets to add the white case. The option <InlineMath math="\frac2{21}" /> is the plain average <InlineMath math="\frac12\left(\frac17+\frac1{21}\right)" />, which **ignores the weights <InlineMath math="\frac35" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac25" />**. The option <InlineMath math="\frac17" /> comes from drawing one ball at a time with replacement, which gives <InlineMath math="\frac35\left(\frac37\right)^2+\frac25\left(\frac27\right)^2=\frac{35}{245}" />.

So the probability that both balls drawn from bag B are red is <InlineMath math="\frac{11}{105}" />.

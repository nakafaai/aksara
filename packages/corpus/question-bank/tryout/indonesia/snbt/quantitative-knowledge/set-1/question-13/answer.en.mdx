The volume of water does not change, but the cube takes up part of the space at the bottom of the tank. The first thing to decide is **whether the cube ends up fully under water**, because the formula for the water level differs in the two situations.

# Testing the submerged assumption

The water volume is <InlineMath math="30\times20\times8=4800\text{ cm}^3" />. If the cube were fully submerged, its whole volume would raise the water over the base of <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^2" />.

<BlockMath math="h=\frac{4800+1000}{600}=\frac{29}{3}\approx9.67\text{ cm}" />

This level is **lower than the edge of the cube**, which is 10 cm. So the assumption is wrong, and the top of the cube stays above the water.

# Computing the water level

Because the cube sticks out of the surface, the water **fills only the space around the cube**. The base area available to the water is <InlineMath math="600-10\times10=500\text{ cm}^2" />, and the water volume is still <InlineMath math="4800\text{ cm}^3" />.

<BlockMath math="500h=4800\quad\Rightarrow\quad h=9.6\text{ cm}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="9.6<10" />, so the cube is indeed not fully submerged.

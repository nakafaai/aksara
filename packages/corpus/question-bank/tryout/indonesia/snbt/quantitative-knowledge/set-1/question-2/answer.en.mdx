The three numbers have different bases and different exponents, so they cannot be compared directly. The exponents 30, 20, and 12 are all divisible by 2, which lets you write each number as **the square of a smaller number**.

# Matching the exponents

Write each number as some number squared.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a&=2^{30}=\left(2^{15}\right)^2=32{,}768^2\\
b&=3^{20}=\left(3^{10}\right)^2=59{,}049^2\\
c&=5^{12}=\left(5^{6}\right)^2=15{,}625^2
\end{aligned}" />

For positive numbers, **squaring keeps the order**. Since <InlineMath math="15{,}625<32{,}768<59{,}049" />, it follows that <InlineMath math="c<a<b" />.

# Checking the other orders

The order <InlineMath math="a<b<c" /> looks **only at the bases**, although <InlineMath math="c" /> has the smallest exponent. The order <InlineMath math="c<b<a" /> looks only at the exponents. The orders <InlineMath math="a<c<b" /> and <InlineMath math="b<a<c" /> contradict the comparison of the squared numbers above.

So the order from smallest to largest is **<InlineMath math="c<a<b" />**.

Since <InlineMath math="63=7\times9" /> and 7 and 9 share no factor other than 1, the two remainder conditions fix **exactly one remainder** of <InlineMath math="n" /> modulo 63. Once that remainder is known, the remainder of <InlineMath math="2n+1" /> follows.

# Finding the remainder of n

The condition for division by 9 means <InlineMath math="n=9k+5" /> for some integer <InlineMath math="k\ge0" />. The values <InlineMath math="k=0" /> to <InlineMath math="k=6" /> already cover every possible remainder modulo 63, so check each one against division by 7.

| <InlineMath math="k" /> | <InlineMath math="9k+5" /> | Remainder when divided by 7 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 5 | 5 |
| 1 | 14 | 0 |
| 2 | 23 | 2 |
| 3 | 32 | 4 |
| 4 | 41 | 6 |
| 5 | 50 | 1 |
| 6 | 59 | 3 |

**Only 59** leaves remainder 3 when divided by 7. So <InlineMath math="n=63m+59" /> for some integer <InlineMath math="m\ge0" />.

# Computing the requested remainder

Substitute the general form of <InlineMath math="n" /> into <InlineMath math="2n+1" /> and split off the multiples of 63.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2n+1&=2(63m+59)+1\\
&=126m+119\\
&=63(2m+1)+56
\end{aligned}" />

Because <InlineMath math="0\le56<63" />, the remainder is 56. A check with <InlineMath math="n=59" /> gives <InlineMath math="2(59)+1=119=63+56" />.

The value 59 is the remainder of <InlineMath math="n" /> itself, not of <InlineMath math="2n+1" />. The value 119 has not been reduced, although a remainder on division by 63 must be less than 63. The value 25 comes from swapping the two remainders, which gives <InlineMath math="n=12" />, a number that leaves 5 on division by 7 and 3 on division by 9. The value 11 uses only the condition for division by 9, namely <InlineMath math="2(5)+1" />.

So the remainder when <InlineMath math="2n+1" /> is divided by 63 is **56**.

First compute the measures of the original data, then check **what actually changes** in each modified data set. Ordered from the smallest score, positions 1 to 3 are 5, positions 4 to 8 are 6, positions 9 to 14 are 7, positions 15 to 18 are 8, and positions 19 to 20 are 9.

# Measures of the original data

<BlockMath math="\bar x=\frac{3(5)+5(6)+6(7)+4(8)+2(9)}{20}=\frac{137}{20}=6.85" />

The median of 20 values is the mean of the 10th and 11th values. Both are 7, so the median is 7. **The mean 6.85 is smaller than the median 7**, so the third statement is true.

# Adding new students

With two students scoring 9, there are 22 values and the median is the mean of the 11th and 12th values. Adding at the top end does not move the 7s, which still fill positions 9 to 14. The median stays 7, so the first statement is false.

With three students scoring 6, the frequency of 6 becomes <InlineMath math="5+3=8" />, which **exceeds the frequency of 7**, still 6. The mode becomes 6, so the second statement is false.

# Removing the students who scored five

Without the three students who scored 5, 17 students remain, with total score <InlineMath math="137-15=122" />.

<BlockMath math="\bar x=\frac{122}{17}\approx7.18>7" />

The new mean is **greater than 7**, so the fourth statement is true.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **false**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **true**.

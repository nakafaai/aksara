The inequality <InlineMath math="\lvert A\rvert<B" /> is equivalent to <InlineMath math="-B<A<B" />. This form also forces <InlineMath math="B>0" />, so **both bounds must hold at the same time**.

# Solving both bounds

With <InlineMath math="A=2x-5" /> and <InlineMath math="B=x+1" />, the inequality becomes

<BlockMath math="-(x+1)<2x-5<x+1" />

Solve the right bound and the left bound separately.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x-5&<x+1 &&\Rightarrow\quad x<6\\
2x-5&>-x-1 &&\Rightarrow\quad 3x>4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x>\tfrac43
\end{aligned}" />

**The intersection of the two bounds** is <InlineMath math="\tfrac43<x<6" />. On this interval <InlineMath math="x+1>0" />, so the condition <InlineMath math="B>0" /> holds automatically.

# Adding the integers

The integers in <InlineMath math="\tfrac43<x<6" /> are 2, 3, 4, and 5. **Check the endpoints**: <InlineMath math="x=5" /> gives <InlineMath math="5<6" />, while <InlineMath math="x=6" /> gives the false statement <InlineMath math="7<7" />, and <InlineMath math="x=1" /> gives the false statement <InlineMath math="3<2" />.

<BlockMath math="2+3+4+5=14" />

The value 12 uses only the case <InlineMath math="2x-5\ge0" />, so it loses <InlineMath math="x=2" />. The value 15 rounds the bound <InlineMath math="\tfrac43" /> down and includes <InlineMath math="x=1" />. The value 20 includes <InlineMath math="x=6" />, although the sign is strictly less than. The value 21 makes both of these mistakes.

So the sum of all integer solutions is **14**.

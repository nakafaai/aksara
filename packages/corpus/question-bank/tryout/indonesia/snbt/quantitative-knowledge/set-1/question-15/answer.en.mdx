Triangles <InlineMath math="ABC" /> and <InlineMath math="DEA" /> are similar, with **corresponding vertices** <InlineMath math="A \leftrightarrow D" />, <InlineMath math="B \leftrightarrow E" />, and <InlineMath math="C \leftrightarrow A" />.

Point <InlineMath math="C" /> shares its horizontal coordinate with <InlineMath math="B" /> and its vertical coordinate with <InlineMath math="A" />, so <InlineMath math="C(8,1)" />.

#### Calculating Side Lengths

For triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />, which is a **right triangle** at <InlineMath math="C" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="AC = |8 - 2| = 6" />
<BlockMath math="BC = |4 - 1| = 3" />
</MathContainer>

For triangle <InlineMath math="ADE" />:

<BlockMath math="AE = |1 - (-3)| = 4" />

#### Using Similarity

Since both triangles are similar, the ratios of **corresponding sides** are equal.

<BlockMath math="\frac{AE}{BC} = \frac{AD}{AC}" />

Substitute the known values:

<BlockMath math="\frac{4}{3} = \frac{AD}{6}" />

Multiply both sides by <InlineMath math="6" /> to find the exact length from the similarity ratio:

<BlockMath math="AD = \frac{4 \times 6}{3} = \frac{24}{3} = 8" />

#### Calculating the Similar Triangle Area

Both triangles have right angles (triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> at <InlineMath math="C" />, triangle <InlineMath math="ADE" /> at <InlineMath math="A" />).

The area is half the product of the **perpendicular base and height**.

For triangle <InlineMath math="ADE" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="L_{ADE}=\frac12\times AD\times AE" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 4" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{32}{2}" />
<BlockMath math="= 16" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, the area of triangle <InlineMath math="ADE" /> is <InlineMath math="16" /> square units.

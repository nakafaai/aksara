You can compute both quantities exactly without finding <InlineMath math="x" />. The key is **two helper expressions, <InlineMath math="x+\frac1x" /> and <InlineMath math="x-\frac1x" />**. # Finding the sum of the number and its reciprocal

<BlockMath math="\left(x+\frac1x\right)^2=x^2+2+\frac1{x^2}=3+2=5" />

So <InlineMath math="x+\frac1x=\sqrt5" /> or <InlineMath math="x+\frac1x=-\sqrt5" />. Since **<InlineMath math="x" /> is positive**, <InlineMath math="\frac1x" /> is positive too, so their sum is positive. Only <InlineMath math="x+\frac1x=\sqrt5" /> works.

# Computing the first quantity

Expand the cube of <InlineMath math="x+\frac1x" />. Its two middle terms group into **three times <InlineMath math="x+\frac1x" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\left(x+\frac1x\right)^3&=x^3+3x+\frac3x+\frac1{x^3}=x^3+\frac1{x^3}+3\left(x+\frac1x\right)\\
P=x^3+\frac1{x^3}&=\left(\sqrt5\right)^3-3\sqrt5=5\sqrt5-3\sqrt5=2\sqrt5
\end{aligned}" />

# Computing the second quantity

Square <InlineMath math="x-\frac1x" /> as well.

<BlockMath math="\left(x-\frac1x\right)^2=x^2-2+\frac1{x^2}=3-2=1" />

The value of <InlineMath math="x-\frac1x" /> can be <InlineMath math="1" /> or <InlineMath math="-1" />, because <InlineMath math="x" /> may be bigger or smaller than <InlineMath math="1" />. You really can't pin down that sign. But <InlineMath math="Q" /> is a square, so **the sign disappears once it's squared**.

<BlockMath math="Q=\left(x^2-\frac1{x^2}\right)^2=\left(x+\frac1x\right)^2\left(x-\frac1x\right)^2=5\cdot1=5" />

# Comparing the two quantities

Both numbers are positive, so you can **compare their squares**.

<BlockMath math="P^2=\left(2\sqrt5\right)^2=20<25=5^2=Q^2" />

So <InlineMath math="P<Q" />. As a check, take <InlineMath math="x=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\approx1.618" />. It meets the condition, and it gives <InlineMath math="P\approx4.472" /> and <InlineMath math="Q=5" />.

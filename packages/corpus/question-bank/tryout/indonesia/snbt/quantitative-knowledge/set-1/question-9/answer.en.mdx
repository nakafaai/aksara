The two logarithms in the equation are reciprocals of each other, so the equation turns into a quadratic in one variable. After that, every candidate has to pass **the conditions on the base and the argument**. The plan: write the conditions first, replace the logarithm with one variable, solve each case, and then filter the candidates.

# Writing the conditions

The base of a logarithm must be positive and not equal to <InlineMath math="1" />, and its argument (the number you take the logarithm of) must be positive. Here <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="6-x" /> each act as a base and as an argument.

<BlockMath math="0<x<6,\qquad x\neq1,\qquad x\neq5" />

Any candidate outside these conditions **has to be thrown out**, even if the arithmetic looks neat.

# Replacing with one variable

Swapping the base and the argument turns a logarithm into its reciprocal: <InlineMath math="{}^{b}\log a=\frac{1}{{}^{a}\log b}" />. Let <InlineMath math="u={}^{x}\log(6-x)" />. Then the second term equals <InlineMath math="\frac1u" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
u+\frac1u&=\frac52\\
2u^2-5u+2&=0\\
(2u-1)(u-2)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

So there are **two cases**, <InlineMath math="u=2" /> or <InlineMath math="u=\frac12" />.

# Solving each case

For <InlineMath math="u=2" />, the definition of a logarithm gives <InlineMath math="6-x=x^2" />.

<BlockMath math="x^2+x-6=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad(x+3)(x-2)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=-3\ \text{or}\ x=2" />

For <InlineMath math="u=\frac12" />, the definition gives <InlineMath math="6-x=\sqrt x" />. Square both sides. **Squaring can create a fake candidate**, so you must check the results again.

<BlockMath math="(6-x)^2=x\quad\Rightarrow\quad x^2-13x+36=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad(x-4)(x-9)=0" />

The candidates from this case are <InlineMath math="x=4" /> and <InlineMath math="x=9" />.

# Filtering the candidates

The candidate <InlineMath math="x=-3" /> is thrown out because the base <InlineMath math="x" /> is negative. The candidate <InlineMath math="x=9" /> is thrown out because <InlineMath math="6-9=-3" /> is negative. It also **fails the equation before squaring**, since <InlineMath math="6-9=-3" /> isn't equal to <InlineMath math="\sqrt9=3" />. The candidates <InlineMath math="x=2" /> and <InlineMath math="x=4" /> meet every condition.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x=2:&\quad {}^{2}\log4+{}^{4}\log2=2+\frac12=\frac52\\
x=4:&\quad {}^{4}\log2+{}^{2}\log4=\frac12+2=\frac52
\end{aligned}" />

The sum of the solutions is <InlineMath math="2+4=6" />.

# The wrong options

The option <InlineMath math="2" /> comes from solving only the case <InlineMath math="u=2" />. The option <InlineMath math="3" /> comes from keeping <InlineMath math="-3" />, which gives <InlineMath math="-3+2+4" />. The option <InlineMath math="12" /> comes from checking no condition at all and adding all four candidates: <InlineMath math="-3+2+4+9" />. The option <InlineMath math="15" /> comes from **adding the fake candidate <InlineMath math="9" />**, which gives <InlineMath math="2+4+9" />.

So the sum of all real numbers that satisfy the equation is <InlineMath math="6" />.

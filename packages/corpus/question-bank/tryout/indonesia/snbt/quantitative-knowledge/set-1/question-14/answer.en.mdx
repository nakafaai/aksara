The cross-section of a plane and a cube is the flat shape bounded by every point where the plane cuts an edge of the cube. That shape **isn't necessarily triangle <InlineMath math="PQR" />**, because the plane can cut other edges too. The plan: set up coordinates, find the equation of the plane, find every point where it cuts an edge, and then compute the area of the shape.

# Setting up coordinates

Put <InlineMath math="A" /> at the origin, with **the edges <InlineMath math="AB" />, <InlineMath math="AD" />, and <InlineMath math="AE" /> along the coordinate axes**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A&=(0,0,0),\quad B=(6,0,0),\quad C=(6,6,0),\quad D=(0,6,0)\\
E&=(0,0,6),\quad F=(6,0,6),\quad G=(6,6,6),\quad H=(0,6,6)
\end{aligned}" />

The midpoints are <InlineMath math="P=(3,0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="Q=(6,3,0)" />, and <InlineMath math="R=(3,6,6)" />.

# Finding the equation of the plane

Write the plane as <InlineMath math="ax+by+cz=d" /> and plug in the three points. Point <InlineMath math="P" /> gives <InlineMath math="3a=d" />. Point <InlineMath math="Q" /> gives <InlineMath math="6a+3b=3a" />, so <InlineMath math="b=-a" />. Point <InlineMath math="R" /> gives <InlineMath math="3a-6a+6c=3a" />, so <InlineMath math="c=a" />. With <InlineMath math="a=1" />, **the plane is <InlineMath math="x-y+z=3" />**.

# Finding every cut point

Test the other edge midpoints in <InlineMath math="x-y+z=3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{midpoint of } CG&=(6,6,3):\quad 6-6+3=3\\
\text{midpoint of } HE&=(0,3,6):\quad 0-3+6=3\\
\text{midpoint of } EA&=(0,0,3):\quad 0-0+3=3
\end{aligned}" />

So the plane passes through six edge midpoints: those of <InlineMath math="AB" />, <InlineMath math="BC" />, <InlineMath math="CG" />, <InlineMath math="GH" />, <InlineMath math="HE" />, and <InlineMath math="EA" />. A cube has only six faces, so a cross-section has at most six sides. **The cross-section is a hexagon** with these six midpoints as its corners.

# Computing the area of the hexagon

Two neighbouring midpoints, such as <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />, are <InlineMath math="\sqrt{3^2+3^2+0^2}=3\sqrt2" /> apart. The same work shows all six sides are equal. The centre of the cube, <InlineMath math="(3,3,3)" />, also satisfies <InlineMath math="3-3+3=3" />, and its distance to each corner is <InlineMath math="3\sqrt2" /> as well, for example to <InlineMath math="P" /> through the difference <InlineMath math="(0,-3,-3)" />. So the hexagon **splits into six equilateral triangles** with side <InlineMath math="3\sqrt2" />. The hexagon's area <InlineMath math="L" /> is six times the area of one of them.

<BlockMath math="L=6\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\left(3\sqrt2\right)^2=6\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\cdot18=27\sqrt3" />

# Checking the result

In this hexagon, <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="R" /> are opposite corners, because the midpoint of <InlineMath math="PR" /> is the centre <InlineMath math="(3,3,3)" />. So <InlineMath math="PR=6\sqrt2" /> is a diameter, and angle <InlineMath math="PQR" /> is a right angle. Triangle <InlineMath math="PQR" /> has area <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot3\sqrt2\cdot3\sqrt6=9\sqrt3" />, which is **exactly one third of the hexagon**. That matches the area of two of the six equilateral triangles, <InlineMath math="2\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\cdot18=9\sqrt3" />.

# The wrong options

The option <InlineMath math="\frac{9\sqrt3}{2}\text{ cm}^2" /> is the area of the triangle that only cuts off corner <InlineMath math="B" />, the equilateral triangle through the midpoints of <InlineMath math="AB" />, <InlineMath math="BC" />, and <InlineMath math="BF" /> with side <InlineMath math="3\sqrt2" />. The option <InlineMath math="9\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2" /> treats **triangle <InlineMath math="PQR" /> as the cross-section**. The option <InlineMath math="\frac{27\sqrt3}{2}\text{ cm}^2" /> uses a hexagon side of <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}" />, half an edge. The option <InlineMath math="18\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2" /> treats the cross-section as a rectangle with sides <InlineMath math="PQ" /> and <InlineMath math="QR" />, because angle <InlineMath math="PQR" /> is a right angle.

So the area of the cross-section of plane <InlineMath math="PQR" /> and the cube is <InlineMath math="27\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2" />.

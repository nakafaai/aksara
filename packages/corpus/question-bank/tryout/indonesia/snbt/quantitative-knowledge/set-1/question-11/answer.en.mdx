# Setting up the radius equation

Let <InlineMath math="M" /> be the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AB" />, <InlineMath math="T" /> the midpoint of <InlineMath math="CD" />, and let the center <InlineMath math="O" /> be at distance <InlineMath math="h" /> from <InlineMath math="AB" />. The circle touches <InlineMath math="CD" />, so **the distance from the center to <InlineMath math="CD" /> equals the radius**.

<BlockMath math="r=OT=8-h" />

The radius also equals <InlineMath math="OA" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="OMA" /> has a right angle at <InlineMath math="M" />, with <InlineMath math="AM=4" /> and <InlineMath math="OM=h" />.

<BlockMath math="r^2=OA^2=4^2+h^2" />

# Solving the equation

**Set the two expressions** for <InlineMath math="r^2" /> **equal**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(8-h)^2&=16+h^2\\
64-16h+h^2&=16+h^2\\
48&=16h\\
h&=3
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="r=8-3=5" />. Check: <InlineMath math="OA=\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5" />, which matches <InlineMath math="OT=5" />.

The value 4 is the radius of the circle with diameter <InlineMath math="AB" />, which does not reach <InlineMath math="CD" />. The value <InlineMath math="4\sqrt2" /> is the radius of the square's circumcircle, which cuts <InlineMath math="CD" /> at two points. The value 3 is the distance <InlineMath math="h" />, not the radius. The value 8 comes from replacing <InlineMath math="AM" /> with the whole side, as in <InlineMath math="r^2=8^2+(8-r)^2" />.

So the radius of the circle is **<InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" />**.

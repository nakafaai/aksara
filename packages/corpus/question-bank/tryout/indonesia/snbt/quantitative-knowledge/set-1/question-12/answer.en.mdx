Both quantities can be computed without finding each side first. The key is **the area formula that uses the inradius**, together with the length of the hypotenuse.

# Computing quantity P

For every triangle, **the area equals the inradius times the semiperimeter**, <InlineMath math="A=r\cdot s" />. Here <InlineMath math="s=\tfrac{24}{2}=12" />.

<BlockMath math="r=\frac{A}{s}=\frac{24}{12}=2" />

So <InlineMath math="P=2" />.

# Computing quantity Q

Let the legs be <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b" /> and the hypotenuse <InlineMath math="c" />. The area gives <InlineMath math="ab=48" />. The perimeter gives <InlineMath math="a+b=24-c" />. **The Pythagorean theorem** links the two.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(a+b)^2&=a^2+b^2+2ab\\
(24-c)^2&=c^2+96\\
576-48c&=96\\
c&=10
\end{aligned}" />

The height to the hypotenuse comes from two ways of writing the same area, <InlineMath math="\tfrac12ab=\tfrac12c\,t" />.

<BlockMath math="t=\frac{ab}{c}=\frac{48}{10}=4.8" />

So <InlineMath math="Q=\tfrac12(4.8)=2.4" />. The sides are 6, 8, and 10, which indeed give perimeter 24 and area 24.

Since <InlineMath math="2.4>2" />, **<InlineMath math="Q>P" />** holds. The option saying the information is not enough is wrong, because the perimeter and the area together fix this right triangle.

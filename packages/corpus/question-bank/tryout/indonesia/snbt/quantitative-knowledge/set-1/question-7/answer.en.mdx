The question asks for a definite yes or a definite no. A statement is sufficient only when **every pair of numbers that satisfies it gives the same answer**. To show that a statement is not sufficient, one yes example and one no example are enough.

# Statement one alone

The pair <InlineMath math="x=3,\ y=1" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="9>1" /> and gives <InlineMath math="x>y" />. The pair <InlineMath math="x=-3,\ y=1" /> also satisfies <InlineMath math="9>1" />, but <InlineMath math="x<y" />. Squaring **erases the sign information**, so statement (1) alone is not sufficient.

# Statement two alone

The pair <InlineMath math="x=3,\ y=1" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="x+y>0" /> and gives <InlineMath math="x>y" />. The pair <InlineMath math="x=1,\ y=3" /> also satisfies <InlineMath math="x+y>0" />, but <InlineMath math="x<y" />. A positive sum **does not decide which number is larger**, so statement (2) alone is not sufficient.

# Both statements together

Write statement (1) as a product of two factors.

<BlockMath math="x^2-y^2=(x-y)(x+y)>0" />

A product is positive only when both factors have the same sign. Statement (2) guarantees <InlineMath math="x+y>0" />, so **the factor <InlineMath math="x-y" /> must be positive too**. This gives <InlineMath math="x>y" /> for every pair that satisfies both statements.

So **both statements together are sufficient**, but neither statement alone is sufficient.

You're looking for the area of the small triangle above the trapezoid, but the problem only gives the areas of two triangles inside it. The bridge is **the ratio of the parallel sides**. From the two areas, find the ratio of <InlineMath math="AB" /> to <InlineMath math="CD" />, compute the area of the trapezoid, and then use the similar triangles <InlineMath math="EAB" /> and <InlineMath math="ECD" />.

# Finding the ratio of the parallel sides

Since <InlineMath math="AB\parallel CD" />, angle <InlineMath math="OAB" /> equals angle <InlineMath math="OCD" />, and angle <InlineMath math="OBA" /> equals angle <InlineMath math="ODC" /> (alternate angles). So triangle <InlineMath math="AOB" /> is similar to triangle <InlineMath math="COD" />. The ratio of the areas of two similar shapes **is the square of the ratio of their sides**.

<BlockMath math="\left(\frac{AB}{CD}\right)^2=\frac{9}{25}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{AB}{CD}=\frac35=\frac{AO}{OC}" />

# Computing the area of the trapezoid

Triangles <InlineMath math="AOB" /> and <InlineMath math="BOC" /> both have their top vertex at <InlineMath math="B" />, and their bases <InlineMath math="AO" /> and <InlineMath math="OC" /> lie on one line. They have the same height, so **their areas are in the ratio of their bases**. The same goes for triangles <InlineMath math="AOD" /> and <InlineMath math="COD" />, which share the vertex <InlineMath math="D" />. The symbol <InlineMath math="[\,\cdot\,]" /> means area, in <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
[BOC]&=\frac53\cdot[AOB]=\frac53\cdot9=15\\
[AOD]&=\frac35\cdot[COD]=\frac35\cdot25=15\\
[ABCD]&=9+15+15+25=64
\end{aligned}" />

# Computing the area of the small triangle

Since <InlineMath math="AB\parallel CD" />, triangle <InlineMath math="EAB" /> is similar to triangle <InlineMath math="ECD" /> with side ratio <InlineMath math="3:5" />. Their area ratio is **the square of the side ratio**, which is <InlineMath math="9:25" />. Write <InlineMath math="[EAB]=9k" /> and <InlineMath math="[ECD]=25k" />. The trapezoid is the big triangle minus the small one.

The figure below uses one trapezoid that fits, with <InlineMath math="AB=6" />, <InlineMath math="CD=10" />, and height <InlineMath math="8" />, so you can see how the areas relate. Triangle <InlineMath math="ECD" /> is the small triangle <InlineMath math="EAB" /> plus the trapezoid, so **the difference between the two similar triangles is the area of the trapezoid**.

# Checking the result

The point <InlineMath math="O" /> splits the height in the ratio <InlineMath math="3:5" />, so it's <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> from <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> from <InlineMath math="CD" />. The result **agrees with the ratio method**.

# The similar triangles

Because <InlineMath math="AB\parallel DC" />, angle <InlineMath math="EAB" /> equals angle <InlineMath math="ECD" /> and angle <InlineMath math="EBA" /> equals angle <InlineMath math="EDC" /> (alternate interior angles). So triangle <InlineMath math="ABE" /> is **similar** to triangle <InlineMath math="CDE" /> with scale factor <InlineMath math="AB:DC=12:4=3:1" />.

It follows that <InlineMath math="AE:EC=3:1" /> and <InlineMath math="BE:ED=3:1" />. The ratio of the areas of two similar figures is the square of the scale factor.

<BlockMath math="[ABE]=3^2\times[CDE]=9\times3=27\text{ cm}^2" />

# The two side triangles

Triangles <InlineMath math="DAE" /> and <InlineMath math="DCE" /> share the vertex <InlineMath math="D" />, and their bases lie on the line <InlineMath math="AC" />. They **have the same height**, so the ratio of their areas equals the ratio of their bases, <InlineMath math="AE:EC=3:1" />. The same argument on the line <InlineMath math="BD" /> gives <InlineMath math="[BCE]:[DCE]=BE:ED=3:1" />.

<BlockMath math="[DAE]=[BCE]=3\times3=9\text{ cm}^2" />

The area of the trapezoid is the sum of the four triangles.

<BlockMath math="[ABCD]=27+9+9+3=48\text{ cm}^2" />

The value 30 treats the area ratio as <InlineMath math="3:1" /> instead of <InlineMath math="9:1" />. The value 39 forgets one side triangle. The value 36 gives each side triangle the area of <InlineMath math="CDE" />. The value 54 doubles the area of <InlineMath math="ABE" />.

So the area of trapezoid <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> is **<InlineMath math="48\text{ cm}^2" />**.

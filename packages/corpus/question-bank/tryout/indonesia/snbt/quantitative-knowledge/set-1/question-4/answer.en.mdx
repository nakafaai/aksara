All four statements can be checked without finding each root separately. Use **the sum and product of the roots** from the coefficients of the equation.

<BlockMath math="p+q=6,\qquad pq=k" />

# Finding the value of k

The difference of the roots is linked to their sum and product through the identity for the square of a difference.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(p-q)^2&=(p+q)^2-4pq\\
\left(2\sqrt2\right)^2&=6^2-4k\\
8&=36-4k\\
k&=7
\end{aligned}" />

The statement <InlineMath math="k=7" /> is **true**. The discriminant is <InlineMath math="36-28=8>0" />, so both roots really are real.

# Checking the other three statements

With <InlineMath math="p+q=6" /> and <InlineMath math="pq=7" />, express each quantity through the sum and product of the roots.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
p^2+q^2&=(p+q)^2-2pq=36-14=22\\
\frac1p+\frac1q&=\frac{p+q}{pq}=\frac67\\
p^3+q^3&=(p+q)^3-3pq(p+q)=216-126=90
\end{aligned}" />

The statement <InlineMath math="p^2+q^2=22" /> is **true**. The statement <InlineMath math="\frac1p+\frac1q=\frac76" /> is false because the fraction is flipped: the correct value is <InlineMath math="\frac67" />. The statement <InlineMath math="p^3+q^3=132" /> is false because the value is 90. The number 132 comes from <InlineMath math="(p+q)(p^2+q^2)=6\times22" />, which drops the term <InlineMath math="-pq" /> in <InlineMath math="p^3+q^3=(p+q)(p^2-pq+q^2)" />.

As a check, the roots are <InlineMath math="3+\sqrt2" /> and <InlineMath math="3-\sqrt2" />. Their squares add up to <InlineMath math="(11+6\sqrt2)+(11-6\sqrt2)=22" />.

So the first and second statements are **true**, and the third and fourth statements are **false**.

Each statement needs its own check, and two of them depend on **the numbers left out of a domain**. The plan: build <InlineMath math="f\circ f" /> with its domain, find its range, find <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> with its domain, and then find where the graphs of <InlineMath math="f" /> and <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> meet.

# Building the composition

The value <InlineMath math="f(f(x))" /> exists only when <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> exists and <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> can go into <InlineMath math="f" /> again. So you need **<InlineMath math="x\neq-1" /> and <InlineMath math="f(x)\neq-1" />**. The second condition fails only at <InlineMath math="x=0" />, because <InlineMath math="3x-1=-(x+1)" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f(f(x))&=\frac{3\cdot\frac{3x-1}{x+1}-1}{\frac{3x-1}{x+1}+1}=\frac{3(3x-1)-(x+1)}{(3x-1)+(x+1)}\\
&=\frac{8x-4}{4x}=\frac{2x-1}{x}
\end{aligned}" />

This formula is right, but only for <InlineMath math="x\neq-1" /> and <InlineMath math="x\neq0" />. The first statement includes <InlineMath math="x=-1" />, where <InlineMath math="f(-1)" /> isn't defined. The formula gives <InlineMath math="\frac{-3}{-1}=3" /> there, but <InlineMath math="f(f(-1))" /> doesn't exist. So **the first statement is false**.

# Finding the range of the composition

Write <InlineMath math="f(f(x))=2-\frac1x" />. Since <InlineMath math="x\neq0" />, the value <InlineMath math="\frac1x" /> is never zero, so the output is never <InlineMath math="2" />. Since <InlineMath math="x\neq-1" />, the output <InlineMath math="2-\frac{1}{-1}=3" /> never shows up either, because only <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> gives <InlineMath math="3" />. Every other value <InlineMath math="y" /> is reached by <InlineMath math="x=\frac{1}{2-y}" />, which is neither <InlineMath math="0" /> nor <InlineMath math="-1" /> when <InlineMath math="y\neq2" /> and <InlineMath math="y\neq3" />. So **the range of <InlineMath math="f\circ f" /> is every real number except <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />**, and the second statement is true.

# Finding the inverse and its domain

Write <InlineMath math="y=f(x)" /> and solve for <InlineMath math="x" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="y" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y(x+1)&=3x-1\\
x(y-3)&=-1-y\\
x&=\frac{y+1}{3-y}
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(x)=\frac{x+1}{3-x}" />. The domain of an inverse **is the range of the original function**. The value <InlineMath math="3" /> is never reached by <InlineMath math="f" />, because <InlineMath math="3x-1=3x+3" /> is impossible. On the other hand, <InlineMath math="-1" /> is reached, since <InlineMath math="f(0)=-1" />, so <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(-1)=0" /> exists. The domain of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> is every real number except <InlineMath math="3" />, so the fourth statement is false.

# Finding where the graphs meet

A meeting point satisfies <InlineMath math="f(x)=f^{-1}(x)" /> with <InlineMath math="x\neq-1" /> and <InlineMath math="x\neq3" />. Cross-multiply the two fractions.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{3x-1}{x+1}&=\frac{x+1}{3-x}\\
(3x-1)(3-x)&=(x+1)^2\\
-3x^2+10x-3&=x^2+2x+1\\
4x^2-8x+4&=0\\
4(x-1)^2&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The only solution is <InlineMath math="x=1" />, and <InlineMath math="f(1)=\frac22=1" />. The two graphs **meet at exactly the point <InlineMath math="(1,1)" />**, so the third statement is true.

# Checking the results

Test the composition formula at <InlineMath math="x=2" />. Working it out directly gives <InlineMath math="f(2)=\frac53" /> and <InlineMath math="f\left(\frac53\right)=\frac{4}{\frac83}=\frac32" />. The formula <InlineMath math="\frac{2x-1}{x}" /> also gives <InlineMath math="\frac32" />, so **the formula matches at an allowed point**. Test the inverse too: <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(2)=\frac{2+1}{3-2}=3" />, and indeed <InlineMath math="f(3)=\frac{8}{4}=2" />.

So the first statement is false, the second is true, the third is true, and the fourth is false.

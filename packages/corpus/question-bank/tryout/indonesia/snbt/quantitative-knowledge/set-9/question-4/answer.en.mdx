Computing <InlineMath math="N" /> directly is out of reach, so expand the cube and watch **where each term lands**. With <InlineMath math="T=10^{20}" />,

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
N&=(T-9)^3=T^3-27T^2+243T-729\\
&=\underbrace{(T-27)\,T^2}_{\text{left block}}+\underbrace{243T-729}_{\text{right block}}.
\end{aligned}" />

The left block ends in <InlineMath math="40" /> zeros, while the right block is less than <InlineMath math="10^{23}" />. So **the two blocks don't overlap**, and the digit sum of <InlineMath math="N" /> is the digit sum of both blocks.

# The left block

<InlineMath math="T-27=10^{20}-27" /> is written as <InlineMath math="18" /> nines followed by <InlineMath math="73" />. Multiplying by <InlineMath math="T^2" /> **only adds zeros**, so its digit sum is

<BlockMath math="9\cdot18+7+3=172." />

# The right block and the borrow

This is where the trap sits. <InlineMath math="243T" /> ends in <InlineMath math="20" /> zeros, so subtracting <InlineMath math="729" /> **forces a borrow** from the digit <InlineMath math="3" />:

<BlockMath math="243T-729=242\cdot T+\left(10^{20}-729\right)." />

The part <InlineMath math="10^{20}-729" /> is <InlineMath math="17" /> nines followed by <InlineMath math="271" />. The right block's digit sum is

<BlockMath math="(2+4+2)+9\cdot17+(2+7+1)=8+153+10=171." />

# Checking the result

The total is <InlineMath math="172+171=343" />. **A quick check uses the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="9" />**: <InlineMath math="10^{20}-9\equiv1-0=1" />, so <InlineMath math="N\equiv1" />, and indeed <InlineMath math="343=9\cdot38+1" />.

# The tempting wrong options

- <InlineMath math="163" /> subtracts the digits of <InlineMath math="729" /> from the digits of <InlineMath math="243" />, that is <InlineMath math="172+9-18" />, **without the borrow**.
- <InlineMath math="199" /> adds the digits of <InlineMath math="243" /> and <InlineMath math="729" />, as if <InlineMath math="729" /> were added, that is <InlineMath math="172+9+18" />.
- <InlineMath math="334" /> counts only <InlineMath math="16" /> nines in <InlineMath math="10^{20}-729" />.
- <InlineMath math="352" /> counts <InlineMath math="19" /> nines in <InlineMath math="10^{20}-27" />.

So the sum of all digits of <InlineMath math="N" /> is <InlineMath math="343" />.

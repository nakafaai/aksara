The four terms differ only in the signs of <InlineMath math="b" /> and <InlineMath math="c" />, so track **what happens to one like term** <InlineMath math="a^ib^jc^l" /> with <InlineMath math="i+j+l=8" />. Let its coefficient in <InlineMath math="(a+b+c)^8" /> be <InlineMath math="K>0" />.

# The combined coefficient of one term

Replacing <InlineMath math="c" /> with <InlineMath math="-c" /> multiplies the term by <InlineMath math="(-1)^l" />, and replacing <InlineMath math="b" /> with <InlineMath math="-b" /> multiplies it by <InlineMath math="(-1)^j" />. The combined coefficient is

<BlockMath math="K\left(1-(-1)^l-(-1)^j+(-1)^{j+l}\right)=K\left(1-(-1)^j\right)\left(1-(-1)^l\right)." />

**That product is nonzero only when <InlineMath math="j" /> and <InlineMath math="l" /> are both odd**, and then it equals <InlineMath math="4K" />. If either is even, the term disappears.

# Counting the power pairs

**The sum of two odd numbers is even**, so <InlineMath math="j+l=s" /> with <InlineMath math="s\in\{2,4,6,8\}" />, and <InlineMath math="i=8-s" /> is fixed. For each <InlineMath math="s" />, the odd pairs <InlineMath math="(j,l)" /> are <InlineMath math="(1,s-1),(3,s-3),\dots,(s-1,1)" />, that is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{s}{2}" /> of them.

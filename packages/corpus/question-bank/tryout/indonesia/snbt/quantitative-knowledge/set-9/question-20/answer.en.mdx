In <InlineMath math="(a+b+c)^{10}" />, every triple of whole-number exponents <InlineMath math="(i,j,k)" /> with <InlineMath math="i+j+k=10" /> shows up as **one term with a positive coefficient**. # Signs in the second expansion

Write <InlineMath math="(a-b-c)^{10}=\big(a+(-b)+(-c)\big)^{10}" />. The matching term is <InlineMath math="a^i(-b)^j(-c)^k=(-1)^{j+k}a^ib^jc^k" /> with the same coefficient. After subtracting, the coefficient of <InlineMath math="a^ib^jc^k" /> gets multiplied by <InlineMath math="1-(-1)^{j+k}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
j+k\ \text{even}&\;\Longrightarrow\;1-1=0\\
j+k\ \text{odd}&\;\Longrightarrow\;1-(-1)=2
\end{aligned}" />

So **a term survives only when <InlineMath math="j+k" /> is odd**. Since <InlineMath math="j+k=10-i" /> and <InlineMath math="10" /> is even, that's the same as <InlineMath math="i" /> being odd.

# Counting the surviving terms

For <InlineMath math="i=1,3,5,7,9" />, the number of choices for <InlineMath math="(j,k)" /> is <InlineMath math="11-i" />.

<BlockMath math="10+8+6+4+2=30" />

Check from the other side: the terms with even <InlineMath math="i" /> number <InlineMath math="11+9+7+5+3+1=36" />, all of them cancel, and <InlineMath math="30+36=66" />, matching the original count.

To find the domain of the function, we need to ensure that all components of the function are defined (values inside square roots must be non-negative, and the **denominator cannot be zero**).

There are two conditions to satisfy:

#### Condition on the Numerator

The value inside the square root in the numerator must be **non-negative**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="5 - x \geq 0" />
  <BlockMath math="-x \geq -5" />
  <BlockMath math="x \leq 5" />
</MathContainer>

#### Denominator Must Be Positive

The value inside the square root in the denominator must be **positive** because the denominator cannot equal <InlineMath math="0" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + x - 6 > 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(x + 3)(x - 2) > 0" />
</MathContainer>

The zeros of this inequality are <InlineMath math="x = -3" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 2" />.

The factor signs show when their product is positive:

| <InlineMath math="x" /> | <InlineMath math="x+3" /> | <InlineMath math="x-2" /> | <InlineMath math="(x+3)(x-2)" /> |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="x<-3" /> | − | − | + |
| <InlineMath math="-3<x<2" /> | + | − | − |
| <InlineMath math="x>2" /> | + | + | + |

#### Domain Intersection

Take the **intersection of both conditions**: every value <InlineMath math="x<-3" /> already satisfies <InlineMath math="x\le5" />. The part <InlineMath math="x>2" /> is restricted to <InlineMath math="2<x\le5" />. Exclude <InlineMath math="-3" /> and <InlineMath math="2" /> because the denominator is zero. Include <InlineMath math="5" /> because only the numerator is zero.

<BlockMath math="D_f=(-\infty,-3)\cup(2,5]" />

Write <InlineMath math="a=2m" /> and <InlineMath math="b=2n+1" /> for some integers <InlineMath math="m" /> and <InlineMath math="n" />. In this form each statement can be checked for **all pairs at once**, not just for one example.

# Sum and product

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+b&=2(m+n)+1\\
ab&=2m(2n+1)
\end{aligned}" />

The sum is always an even number plus 1, so it is **always odd**. The first statement is true. The product contains a factor 2, so it is always even. The second statement is false.

# Difference and sum of squares

The square of an even number is divisible by 4, while **the square of an odd number leaves remainder 1 when divided by 4**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2&=4m^2\\
b^2&=4n^2+4n+1=4(n^2+n)+1
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="a^2-b^2=4(m^2-n^2-n)-1" /> is always odd, and the third statement is true. Meanwhile <InlineMath math="a^2+b^2=4(m^2+n^2+n)+1" /> always leaves remainder 1 when divided by 4. For example, <InlineMath math="a=2" /> and <InlineMath math="b=1" /> give 5. The fourth statement is false.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **false**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **false**.

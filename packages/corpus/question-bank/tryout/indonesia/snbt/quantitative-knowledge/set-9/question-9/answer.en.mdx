The exponent <InlineMath math="x^2-2x" /> shows up twice, so this is really **a quadratic inequality in disguise**. The plan: replace <InlineMath math="3^{x^2-2x}" /> with one letter, solve the quadratic inequality, go back to the exponent, then solve two conditions on <InlineMath math="x" />.

# Turning it into a quadratic inequality

Let <InlineMath math="y=x^2-2x" /> and <InlineMath math="t=3^y" />, with <InlineMath math="t>0" />. Since <InlineMath math="9=3^2" />, you get <InlineMath math="9^y=\left(3^y\right)^2=t^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t^2-28t+27&\le0\\
(t-1)(t-27)&\le0\\
1\le t&\le27
\end{aligned}" />

A product of two factors is zero or negative only when <InlineMath math="t" /> lies **between the two zeros**, endpoints included.

# Back to the exponent

The function <InlineMath math="3^y" /> always increases, so the order of the <InlineMath math="t" /> values matches the order of the <InlineMath math="y" /> values. First write both bounds as powers of three: <InlineMath math="1=3^0" /> and <InlineMath math="27=3^3" />. **The bounds apply to the exponent, not to the numbers <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="27" /> themselves**.

<BlockMath math="3^0\le3^{x^2-2x}\le3^3\iff0\le x^2-2x\le3" />

# Two conditions on x

This double inequality holds two conditions, and both must be true at once.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-2x\le3&\iff(x-3)(x+1)\le0\iff-1\le x\le3\\
x^2-2x\ge0&\iff x(x-2)\ge0\iff x\le0\ \text{or}\ x\ge2
\end{aligned}" />

The second condition **throws out the numbers between <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="2" />**. Where both hold is <InlineMath math="-1\le x\le0" /> or <InlineMath math="2\le x\le3" />. The integers there are <InlineMath math="-1" />, <InlineMath math="0" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, and <InlineMath math="3" />, so there are <InlineMath math="4" /> of them.

# Checking the result

For <InlineMath math="x=3" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-1" />, the exponent is <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="t=27" />: <InlineMath math="729-756+27=0" />, which works. For <InlineMath math="x=0" />, the exponent is <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="t=1" />: <InlineMath math="1-28+27=0" />, which works. For <InlineMath math="x=1" />, the exponent is <InlineMath math="-1" /> and <InlineMath math="t=\frac{1}{3}" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{9}-\frac{28}{3}+27=\frac{1-84+243}{9}=\frac{160}{9}>0" />

So **<InlineMath math="x=1" /> really doesn't work**.

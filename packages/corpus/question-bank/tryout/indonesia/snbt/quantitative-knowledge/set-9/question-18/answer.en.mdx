The volume cannot be calculated yet because <InlineMath math="x" /> is unknown. Start with the **surface area** to determine <InlineMath math="x" />.

Let <InlineMath math="l" />, <InlineMath math="w" />, and <InlineMath math="h" /> denote length, width, and height. The surface area formula is:

<BlockMath math="S = 2(lw+lh+wh)" />

Substituting the dimensions gives:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}88&=2\bigl(6x^2+6x+4x\bigr)\\&=12x^2+20x\\0&=12x^2+20x-88\\0&=3x^2+5x-22\\&=(3x+11)(x-2)\end{aligned}" />

The first factor gives <InlineMath math="x=-\frac{11}{3}" />, while the second gives <InlineMath math="x=2" />.

Since <InlineMath math="x>0" />, we take <InlineMath math="x=2" />. The prism therefore measures <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> by <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> by <InlineMath math="2\text{ cm}" />.

Each unit cube has volume <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}^3" />, so the required number equals the prism's volume:

<BlockMath math="6\times4\times2=48" />

The prism needs <InlineMath math="48" /> unit cubes.

Picture **two layers of unit cubes**. Each layer contains <InlineMath math="6\times4=24" /> cubes, giving <InlineMath math="2\times24=48" /> in total.

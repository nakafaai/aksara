Let the smallest number be <InlineMath math="a" />. The seven numbers are <InlineMath math="a,a+1,\dots,a+6" />, so **their sum can be written in terms of <InlineMath math="a" />**.

# Setting up the equation

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+(a+1)+\cdots+(a+6)&=7a+(0+1+\cdots+6)\\
&=7a+21
\end{aligned}" />

The sum **equals four times the smallest number**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
7a+21&=4a\\
3a&=-21\\
a&=-7
\end{aligned}" />

# Check

The numbers are <InlineMath math="-7,-6,-5,-4,-3,-2,-1" />. Their sum is <InlineMath math="-28" />, and <InlineMath math="4\cdot(-7)=-28" />. A negative answer makes sense, because **the sum of seven positive numbers is always more than four times the smallest one**.

The value <InlineMath math="-1" /> is the largest number, and <InlineMath math="-4" /> is the middle one. The values 7 and 3 come from a sign error when moving the 21.

So the smallest number is **<InlineMath math="-7" />**.

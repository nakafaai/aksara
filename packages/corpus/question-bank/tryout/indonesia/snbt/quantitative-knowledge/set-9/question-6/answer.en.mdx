Write <InlineMath math="n=a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)" />. The two factors <InlineMath math="a-b" /> and <InlineMath math="a+b" /> are **always both odd or both even**, because their difference <InlineMath math="2b" /> is even. That gives two steps: sort the numbers by their remainder on division by <InlineMath math="4" />, then check the condition that <InlineMath math="b" /> must be positive.

# The impossible numbers

If both factors are odd, the product is odd. If both are even, the product is a multiple of <InlineMath math="4" />. So **a number with remainder <InlineMath math="2" /> on division by <InlineMath math="4" /> is never a difference of squares**. There are <InlineMath math="25" /> such numbers, <InlineMath math="2,6,10,\dots,98" />.

# Odd numbers and multiples of four

- An odd number <InlineMath math="n=2k+1" /> can be written <InlineMath math="(k+1)^2-k^2" />. **The condition <InlineMath math="b=k\ge1" />** removes <InlineMath math="n=1" />, and indeed <InlineMath math="1=(a-b)(a+b)" /> only works with <InlineMath math="a=1" />, <InlineMath math="b=0" />.
- A multiple of four <InlineMath math="n=4m" /> can be written <InlineMath math="(m+1)^2-(m-1)^2" />. The condition <InlineMath math="b=m-1\ge1" /> removes <InlineMath math="n=4" />, and indeed <InlineMath math="4=2\cdot2" /> only gives <InlineMath math="a=2" />, <InlineMath math="b=0" />.

No other square drops out. For example, <InlineMath math="9=5^2-4^2" /> and <InlineMath math="16=5^2-3^2" />.

# Adding up

The odd numbers that pass number <InlineMath math="50-1=49" />, and the multiples of four that pass number <InlineMath math="25-1=24" />. **The total is <InlineMath math="49+24=73" />**.

Common wrong results are <InlineMath math="75" />, which forgets that <InlineMath math="b" /> must be positive and so counts <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />, and <InlineMath math="50" />, which counts only the odd numbers.

So the number of integers from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="100" /> that are positive square differences is <InlineMath math="73" />.

We determine the **lower and upper bounds** of <InlineMath math="z = x-y" /> separately.

# Upper Bound

Because <InlineMath math="x < y" />, we have:

<BlockMath math="x-y < 0" />

<BlockMath math="z < 0" />

The difference can be arbitrarily close to <InlineMath math="0" />, but it **cannot equal** <InlineMath math="0" /> because <InlineMath math="x \neq y" />.

# Lower Bound

We also know that <InlineMath math="x > 1" /> and <InlineMath math="y < 2" />. Therefore:

<BlockMath math="x-y > 1-y > 1-2" />

<BlockMath math="z > -1" />

The value <InlineMath math="-1" /> is not attained either because **both endpoints are excluded** from the open interval.

Combining both bounds gives:

<BlockMath math="-1 < z < 0" />

Thus, <InlineMath math="z" /> lies between <InlineMath math="-1" /> and <InlineMath math="0" />.

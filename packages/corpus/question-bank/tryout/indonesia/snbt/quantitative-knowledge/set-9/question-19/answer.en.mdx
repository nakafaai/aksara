The point is chosen by area, so the probability is **the area of the favourable region divided by the area of the whole triangle**.

# The favourable region

The line <InlineMath math="x+y=2" /> meets the two legs at <InlineMath math="(2,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,2)" />. The points with <InlineMath math="x+y<2" /> form **a small triangle near the origin** with vertices <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(2,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,2)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{small}}&=\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot2=2\\
L_{\text{large}}&=\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot4=8
\end{aligned}" />

# Probability

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{2}{8}=\frac14" />

The small triangle is similar to the large one with side ratio <InlineMath math="1:2" />, so **their area ratio is <InlineMath math="1:4" />**.

The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> uses the side ratio instead of the area ratio. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac34" /> is the probability of the opposite event, and <InlineMath math="\tfrac18" /> divides by the area of a 4 by 4 square.

So the probability that the point satisfies <InlineMath math="x+y<2" /> is **<InlineMath math="\tfrac14" />**.

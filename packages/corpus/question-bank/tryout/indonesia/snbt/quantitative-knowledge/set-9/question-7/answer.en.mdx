# The angle OAC in both positions

Triangle <InlineMath math="OAC" /> is isosceles since <InlineMath math="OA=OC" />, so <InlineMath math="\angle OAC=\tfrac{180^\circ-\angle AOC}{2}" />.

- **Position <InlineMath math="C_1" />**: the arc <InlineMath math="AC_1=140^\circ-60^\circ=80^\circ" />, so <InlineMath math="\angle OAC_1=\tfrac{180^\circ-80^\circ}{2}=50^\circ" />.
- **Position <InlineMath math="C_2" />**: the arc from <InlineMath math="A" /> through <InlineMath math="B" /> to <InlineMath math="C_2" /> measures <InlineMath math="140^\circ+60^\circ=200^\circ" />, so the minor arc is <InlineMath math="160^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="\angle OAC_2=\tfrac{180^\circ-160^\circ}{2}=10^\circ" />.

The sum is <InlineMath math="50^\circ+10^\circ=60^\circ" />.

- <InlineMath math="70^\circ" /> is the inscribed angle <InlineMath math="ACB" /> for <InlineMath math="C" /> on the major arc, not the angle <InlineMath math="OAC" />.

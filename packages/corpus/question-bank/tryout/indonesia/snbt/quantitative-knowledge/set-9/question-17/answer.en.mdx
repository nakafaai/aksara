A statement is sufficient if **only one positive integer** satisfies it.

# Statement (1) alone

Factorials keep increasing as <InlineMath math="n" /> grows, so **each factorial value is reached by only one positive <InlineMath math="n" />**, except the value 1.

<BlockMath math="5!=120,\qquad 6!=720,\qquad 7!=5{,}040" />

Only <InlineMath math="n=6" /> works. Statement (1) alone is sufficient.

# Statement (2) alone

<BlockMath math="n^2-5n-6=(n-6)(n+1)=0" />

The roots are <InlineMath math="n=6" /> and <InlineMath math="n=-1" />. The condition **<InlineMath math="n" /> is a positive integer** rejects <InlineMath math="-1" />, so <InlineMath math="n=6" />. Statement (2) alone is also sufficient, and it gives the same result as statement (1).

So **statement (1) alone is sufficient, and statement (2) alone is also sufficient**.

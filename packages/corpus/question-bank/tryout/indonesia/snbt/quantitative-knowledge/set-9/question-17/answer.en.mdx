A statement is sufficient when **the number of students who passed both exams has only one possible value**. The key is the base of each percentage: a percent of all students, or a percent of only some of them.

# Statement one alone

The number who passed Mathematics is <InlineMath math="60\%\cdot200=120" />. The second percentage **is taken of the students who passed Mathematics**, not of all students. Students who passed Mathematics and also passed Physics are exactly the students who passed both exams.

<BlockMath math="75\%\cdot120=\frac{3}{4}\cdot120=90" />

There's only one value, so statement (1) alone is sufficient.

# Statement two alone

The number who passed Physics is <InlineMath math="70\%\cdot200=140" />, and the number who passed neither is <InlineMath math="15\%\cdot200=30" />. So <InlineMath math="200-30=170" /> students passed at least one exam. If <InlineMath math="M" /> students passed Mathematics, **the inclusion-exclusion principle** gives

<BlockMath math="\text{passed both}=M+140-170=M-30." />

The value of <InlineMath math="M" /> is unknown. The two situations below both satisfy statement (2), but they give different answers.

| Situation | Mathematics only | Physics only | Both | Neither | Total |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="M=120" /> | <InlineMath math="30" /> | <InlineMath math="50" /> | <InlineMath math="90" /> | <InlineMath math="30" /> | <InlineMath math="200" /> |
| <InlineMath math="M=100" /> | <InlineMath math="30" /> | <InlineMath math="70" /> | <InlineMath math="70" /> | <InlineMath math="30" /> | <InlineMath math="200" /> |

Statement (2) alone isn't sufficient.

# Both statements together

The two statements don't contradict each other: the first situation in the table satisfies both. But statement (1) **is already sufficient on its own**, so statement (2) isn't needed. The trap appears if you read <InlineMath math="75\%" /> as a percent of all students. With that reading, statement (1) only tells you how many passed each exam, and you'd think the two statements must be combined.

So statement (1) alone is sufficient to answer the question, but statement (2) alone isn't.

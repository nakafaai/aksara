Let:
-   <InlineMath math="P(A)" /> = Probability Ali passes
-   <InlineMath math="P(B)" /> = Probability Bima passes
-   <InlineMath math="P(D)" /> = Probability Dika passes

Given:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}P(A)&=0.75\\P(A^c)&=1 - 0.75\\&=0.25\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}P(B)&=0.8\\P(B^c)&=1 - 0.8\\&=0.2\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}P(D)&=0.85\\P(D^c)&=1 - 0.85\\&=0.15\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Because the three outcomes are independent, we multiply the appropriate pass and fail probabilities within each case. Exactly one person passes in one of three mutually exclusive cases:

#### Only Ali passes

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&P(A\cap B^c\cap D^c)\\&=0.75 \cdot 0.2 \cdot 0.15\\&= 0.0225\end{aligned}" />

#### Only Bima passes

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&P(B\cap A^c\cap D^c)\\&=0.8 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 0.15\\&= 0.03\end{aligned}" />

#### Only Dika passes

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&P(D\cap A^c\cap B^c)\\&=0.85 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 0.2\\&= 0.0425\end{aligned}" />

#### Total Probability

Thus, the probability that only one person passes is the sum of these three possibilities:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}&0.0225+0.03\\&\quad+0.0425\\&=0.095\end{aligned}" />

Thus, the required probability is <InlineMath math="0.095" />, or <InlineMath math="9.5\%" />.

The angle between a tangent and a chord subtends the same arc as the inscribed angle on the other side. This is **the tangent-chord theorem**: the two angles are equal.

# The arc being subtended

The <InlineMath math="35^\circ" /> angle between <InlineMath math="\ell" /> and <InlineMath math="AB" /> subtends the minor arc <InlineMath math="AB" />. Angle <InlineMath math="ACB" />, with <InlineMath math="C" /> on the major arc, **also subtends the minor arc <InlineMath math="AB" />**.

# Computing through the central angle

Let the centre be <InlineMath math="O" />. The radius <InlineMath math="OA" /> is perpendicular to the tangent, so <InlineMath math="\angle OAB=90^\circ-35^\circ=55^\circ" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="OAB" /> is isosceles because <InlineMath math="OA=OB" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\angle AOB&=180^\circ-2\cdot55^\circ=70^\circ\\
\angle ACB&=\tfrac12\angle AOB=35^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

An inscribed angle is **half the central angle** on the same arc, and the result agrees with the tangent-chord theorem.

The value <InlineMath math="70^\circ" /> is the central angle <InlineMath math="AOB" />, and <InlineMath math="55^\circ" /> is angle <InlineMath math="OAB" />. The value <InlineMath math="145^\circ" /> is the supplement of <InlineMath math="35^\circ" />, the inscribed angle if <InlineMath math="C" /> were on the minor arc.

So angle <InlineMath math="ACB" /> measures **<InlineMath math="35^\circ" />**.

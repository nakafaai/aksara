The question gives neither the trapezoid's height nor where the chord <InlineMath math="CD" /> lies. **Two parallel chords of one circle can sit across the center or on the same side**, and the condition <InlineMath math="AC>12" /> picks one of them.

# Each chord's distance to the center

The line from the center perpendicular to a chord halves it, so **a chord's distance to the center comes from Pythagoras**. With <InlineMath math="r=\tfrac{65}{8}" />,

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
d_{AB}&=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{65}{8}\right)^2-7^2}=\sqrt{\tfrac{4225-3136}{64}}=\tfrac{33}{8},\\
d_{CD}&=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{65}{8}\right)^2-2^2}=\sqrt{\tfrac{4225-256}{64}}=\tfrac{63}{8}.
\end{aligned}" />

# Two possible heights

If <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="CD" /> lie across the center, the height is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{33}{8}+\tfrac{63}{8}=12" />. If they lie on the same side, it's <InlineMath math="\tfrac{63}{8}-\tfrac{33}{8}=\tfrac{15}{4}" />. # The diagonal condition

In an isosceles trapezoid, the horizontal distance from <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="C" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{14+4}{2}=9" />. **The diagonal condition removes one case**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
h=12&:\quad AC=\sqrt{9^2+12^2}=15>12,\\
h=\tfrac{15}{4}&:\quad AC=\sqrt{9^2+\left(\tfrac{15}{4}\right)^2}=\tfrac{39}{4}<12.
\end{aligned}" />

So the height is <InlineMath math="12" />, and the area is

<BlockMath math="A=\frac{14+4}{2}\cdot12=108\text{ cm}^2." />

# The tempting wrong options

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{135}{4}" /> uses the height <InlineMath math="\tfrac{15}{4}" />, **ignoring the diagonal condition**.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{567}{4}" /> adds the areas of both cases, <InlineMath math="108+\tfrac{135}{4}" />.
- <InlineMath math="216" /> forgets to halve in the trapezoid area formula.
- <InlineMath math="168" /> multiplies the long base <InlineMath math="14" /> by the height <InlineMath math="12" />, as if the shape were a rectangle.

So the area of trapezoid <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> is <InlineMath math="108\text{ cm}^2" />.

The circle through all four vertices also passes through <InlineMath math="A" />, <InlineMath math="B" />, and <InlineMath math="C" />. Through three points that aren't on one line there's **exactly one circle**, so you only need the circumradius of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />. The plan: get the trapezoid's height from the diagonal, find the leg, then use the law of sines.

# The height of the trapezoid

Drop a perpendicular from <InlineMath math="C" /> to <InlineMath math="AB" />, with foot <InlineMath math="E" />. An isosceles trapezoid is symmetric, so the extra length of the bottom side splits evenly on both ends: <InlineMath math="EB=\frac{14-4}{2}=5" />. That makes <InlineMath math="AE=14-5=9" />. **The diagonal's shadow on the base equals the average of the two parallel sides**, <InlineMath math="\frac{14+4}{2}=9" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
CE&=\sqrt{AC^2-AE^2}=\sqrt{225-81}=\sqrt{144}=12\\
BC&=\sqrt{EB^2+CE^2}=\sqrt{25+144}=13
\end{aligned}" />

# The radius from the law of sines

In the right triangle <InlineMath math="BEC" />, the sine of angle <InlineMath math="B" /> is opposite over hypotenuse, <InlineMath math="\sin B=\frac{CE}{BC}=\frac{12}{13}" />. This angle <InlineMath math="B" /> is also angle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> in triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />, and the side opposite it is <InlineMath math="AC" />. **The law of sines** says a side divided by the sine of the opposite angle equals the diameter of the circumcircle.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2R&=\frac{AC}{\sin B}=\frac{15}{12/13}=\frac{65}{4}\\
R&=\frac{65}{8}
\end{aligned}" />

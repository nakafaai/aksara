Both statements seem to be about how big <InlineMath math="x" /> is compared with <InlineMath math="y" />, yet the question asks about the **sign** of <InlineMath math="x" />. So translate each statement into facts about signs first.

# Statement (1) alone

<InlineMath math="\tfrac{x}{y}>1" /> means <InlineMath math="\tfrac{x}{y}" /> is positive, so **<InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> have the same sign**, and <InlineMath math="\left|\tfrac{x}{y}\right|>1" />, so <InlineMath math="|x|>|y|" />. Their shared sign is still unknown:

- <InlineMath math="x=3" />, <InlineMath math="y=1" /> fits, and <InlineMath math="x>0" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=-3" />, <InlineMath math="y=-1" /> also fits, but <InlineMath math="x<0" />.

So statement (1) alone isn't sufficient. The trap is reading <InlineMath math="\tfrac{x}{y}>1" /> as <InlineMath math="x>y" />, though multiplying by a negative <InlineMath math="y" /> flips the inequality.

# Statement (2) alone

Move everything to one side:

<BlockMath math="x^2y-y^3=y\left(x^2-y^2\right)>0." />

This statement **ties down the sign of <InlineMath math="y" />, not the sign of <InlineMath math="x" />**. The pair <InlineMath math="x=2" />, <InlineMath math="y=1" /> fits with <InlineMath math="x>0" />, while <InlineMath math="x=-2" />, <InlineMath math="y=1" /> also fits because <InlineMath math="1\cdot(4-1)>0" />, but <InlineMath math="x<0" />. So statement (2) alone isn't sufficient.

# The tempting wrong options

- Statement (1) alone being sufficient comes from reading <InlineMath math="\tfrac{x}{y}>1" /> as <InlineMath math="x>y" /> and then taking <InlineMath math="x" /> to be positive, **forgetting that <InlineMath math="y" /> may be negative**.
- Statement (2) alone being sufficient comes from dividing both sides of <InlineMath math="x^2y>y^3" /> by <InlineMath math="y^2" /> to get <InlineMath math="x^2>y^2" />, then taking <InlineMath math="x" /> to be positive.
- Each alone being sufficient combines both slips.
- Neither being sufficient comes from testing the statements one by one but never joining them.

So the two statements together are sufficient to answer the question, but neither statement alone is.

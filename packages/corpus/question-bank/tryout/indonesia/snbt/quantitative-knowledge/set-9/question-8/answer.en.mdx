Growth of 10% of the previous year's population is **compound growth**: each year the population is multiplied by 1.1. Let the population at the start of 2025 be <InlineMath math="P" />.

# Finding the starting population

After two years the population is <InlineMath math="P\cdot1.1^2=1.21P" />. The increase is **the final population minus the starting population**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1.21P-P&=4{,}200\\
0.21P&=4{,}200\\
P&=20{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

# Population at the start of 2027

<BlockMath math="1.21\cdot20{,}000=24{,}200" />

Year by year: 20,000 becomes 22,000 at the start of 2026, then 24,200 at the start of 2027. The second year's increase is larger, 2,200, because **the 10% is taken of a population that has already grown**.

The values 20,000 and 22,000 are the populations at the start of 2025 and 2026. The value 21,000 treats the increase as 20% of the starting population, like simple interest. The value 24,000 assumes the second year adds the same as the first.

So the population at the start of 2027 is **24,200 people**.

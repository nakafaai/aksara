# The rule as a shift

Every express parcel already carries one <InlineMath math="9" /> stamp. The rest of the charge can be paid with any number of <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="9" /> stamps, including none at all. So a charge <InlineMath math="M" /> can be paid exactly **if and only if <InlineMath math="M-9" /> can be made without the rule**. In other words, the rule shifts every charge by <InlineMath math="9" />.

# Putting the rule back

A charge <InlineMath math="M" /> can be paid if and only if <InlineMath math="M-9" /> can be made. The largest charge that fails comes from setting <InlineMath math="M-9" /> equal to the largest amount you can't make.

\end{aligned}" />

For every <InlineMath math="M>56" />, the value <InlineMath math="M-9" /> is more than <InlineMath math="47" />, so it can be made, and **the charge <InlineMath math="M" /> can be paid**.

All five options really are charges that can't be paid, so trying to pay each one isn't enough. What decides it is **which charge is the largest**.

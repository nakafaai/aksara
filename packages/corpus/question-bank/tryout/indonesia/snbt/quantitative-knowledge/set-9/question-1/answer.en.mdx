In thousands of rupiah, a payable fee has the form <InlineMath math="7a+9b" /> with <InlineMath math="a\ge1" /> and <InlineMath math="b\ge1" />. **The rule "at least one stamp of each kind" shifts everything**: write <InlineMath math="a=1+a'" /> and <InlineMath math="b=1+b'" /> with <InlineMath math="a',b'\ge0" />, and the fee becomes

<BlockMath math="7a+9b=16+\left(7a'+9b'\right)." />

So a fee <InlineMath math="n" /> can be paid exactly when <InlineMath math="n-16" /> can be written as <InlineMath math="7a'+9b'" /> with no extra rule.

# The gaps of the two stamp values

Without the extra rule, the numbers that can't be written as <InlineMath math="7a'+9b'" /> are the gaps. For two values with no common factor, **the number of gaps is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{(7-1)(9-1)}{2}=24" />** and the largest gap is <InlineMath math="7\cdot9-7-9=47" />. The number <InlineMath math="0" /> isn't a gap, since <InlineMath math="0=7\cdot0+9\cdot0" />.

# Putting the two parts together

A positive fee <InlineMath math="n" /> can't be paid in two situations:

- **Fees below the price of one pair of stamps**, that is <InlineMath math="n<16" />: even one stamp of each kind already costs <InlineMath math="16" /> thousand. That gives <InlineMath math="15" /> values.
- <InlineMath math="n\ge16" /> but <InlineMath math="n-16" /> is a gap: <InlineMath math="24" /> values, from <InlineMath math="17" /> to <InlineMath math="63" />.

# The tempting wrong options

- <InlineMath math="24" /> counts only the gaps of <InlineMath math="7a'+9b'" />, **ignoring the rule of at least one stamp of each kind**.
- <InlineMath math="15" /> counts only the fees below <InlineMath math="16" /> thousand and forgets the gaps above.
- <InlineMath math="47" /> is the largest gap without the rule, and <InlineMath math="63=16+47" /> is the largest fee that can't be paid with the rule. Both **answer the largest value, not how many**.

So the number of fees that can't be paid exactly is <InlineMath math="39" />.

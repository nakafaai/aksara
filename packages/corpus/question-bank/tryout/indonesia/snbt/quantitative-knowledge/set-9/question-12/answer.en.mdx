Every statement uses the circle's centre and radius, so find both in terms of <InlineMath math="c" /> first. After that, each statement **fixes its own value of <InlineMath math="c" />**, and you test what follows with a different reason each time.

# Centre and radius

Complete the square for <InlineMath math="x" /> and for <InlineMath math="y" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-6x+y^2+8y+c&=0\\
(x-3)^2-9+(y+4)^2-16+c&=0\\
(x-3)^2+(y+4)^2&=25-c
\end{aligned}" />

The centre is <InlineMath math="(3,-4)" /> and the squared radius is <InlineMath math="r^2=25-c" />. This equation describes a real circle **only if <InlineMath math="25-c>0" />**, that is <InlineMath math="c<25" />.

# Statement one

For <InlineMath math="c=25" />, the right side becomes <InlineMath math="0" />. A sum of two squares is zero only when both are zero, so the curve is **just the point <InlineMath math="(3,-4)" />**, not a circle with positive radius. This statement is **false**.

# Statement two

A circle touches the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis when the distance from the centre to the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis equals the radius. That distance is <InlineMath math="|-4|=4" />, so <InlineMath math="r=4" /> and <InlineMath math="25-c=16" />, or <InlineMath math="c=9" />. The distance from the centre to the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis is only <InlineMath math="3" />, **less than the radius** <InlineMath math="4" />, so the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis cuts the circle at two points. Check by substituting <InlineMath math="x=0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(0-3)^2+(y+4)^2&=16\\
(y+4)^2&=7\\
y&=-4\pm\sqrt7
\end{aligned}" />

There are two values of <InlineMath math="y" />, so this statement is **true**.

# Statement three

The circle passes through the origin when <InlineMath math="0+0-0+0+c=0" />, so <InlineMath math="c=0" /> and <InlineMath math="r=5" />. The line <InlineMath math="4x-3y=0" /> does pass through the origin, which is on the circle, but **passing through one point of a circle doesn't make a line tangent**. A line is tangent when its distance from the centre equals the radius.

<BlockMath math="d=\frac{|4\cdot3-3\cdot(-4)|}{\sqrt{4^2+3^2}}=\frac{24}{5}<5" />

The distance is less than the radius, so the line cuts the circle at two points: the origin and <InlineMath math="\left(-\frac{42}{25},-\frac{56}{25}\right)" />. The tangent at the origin has to be perpendicular to the radius pointing toward <InlineMath math="(3,-4)" />, which gives <InlineMath math="3x-4y=0" />. This statement is **false**.

# Statement four

The circle touches the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis when <InlineMath math="r=|3|=3" />, so <InlineMath math="25-c=9" /> and <InlineMath math="c=16" />. The distance from the origin to the centre is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5" />, **greater than the radius** <InlineMath math="3" />. Another way: substituting the origin into the left side gives <InlineMath math="c=16>0" />, which signals a point outside the circle. This statement is **false**.

So statement two is true, while statements one, three, and four are false.

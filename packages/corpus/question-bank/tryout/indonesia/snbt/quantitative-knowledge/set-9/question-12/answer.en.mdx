At a constant work rate, the number of workers is inversely proportional to the available time.

Given:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="12 \text{ people} \rightarrow 15 \text{ days}" />
  <BlockMath math="x \text{ people} \rightarrow 9 \text{ days}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Calculating the Number of Workers Needed

The product of workers and days remains constant:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{12}{x} = \frac{9}{15}" />
  <BlockMath math="x = \frac{12 \cdot 15}{9}" />
  <BlockMath math="x = 20" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, <InlineMath math="20\text{ workers}" /> are needed to finish in <InlineMath math="9\text{ days}" />.

#### Calculating the Percentage Increase

This adds <InlineMath math="20-12=8" /> workers. Relative to the original <InlineMath math="12" /> workers, the percentage increase is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{20 - 12}{12} \times 100\% = \frac{8}{12} \times 100\% \approx 66.7\%" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, the workforce must increase by approximately <InlineMath math="66.7\%" />.

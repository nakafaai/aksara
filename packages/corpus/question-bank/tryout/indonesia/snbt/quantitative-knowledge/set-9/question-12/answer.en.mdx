Dividing both sides by <InlineMath math="x" /> is risky because the sign of <InlineMath math="x" /> is unknown. The safe way is **moving everything to one side and factorising**.

# Solving the inequality

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^3-4x&>0\\
x(x-2)(x+2)&>0
\end{aligned}" />

The zeros are <InlineMath math="-2" />, <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="2" />. Check **the sign of the product on each interval**.

| Interval | Sign of <InlineMath math="x(x-2)(x+2)" /> |
| :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> | negative |
| <InlineMath math="-2<x<0" /> | positive |
| <InlineMath math="0<x<2" /> | negative |
| <InlineMath math="x>2" /> | positive |

The solution is <InlineMath math="-2<x<0" /> or <InlineMath math="x>2" />.

For <InlineMath math="x=-1" />, which also works because <InlineMath math="-1>-4" />, we get <InlineMath math="P<Q" />. **Both relations are possible**, so no single relation is certain. Dividing by <InlineMath math="x" /> without checking the sign gives <InlineMath math="x^2>4" /> and then <InlineMath math="x>2" />, and the negative interval is lost.

So the information given is **not sufficient** to decide the relation between <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />.

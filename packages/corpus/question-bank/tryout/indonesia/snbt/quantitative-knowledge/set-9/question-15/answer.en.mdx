Both <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> depend on <InlineMath math="n" />, so first find **every integer <InlineMath math="n" /> that's allowed**. Then compare <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> for each of those values. If the relationship changes from one allowed <InlineMath math="n" /> to another, the information isn't enough.

# The domain of the logarithms

A logarithm is only defined for positive numbers. So <InlineMath math="n>0" /> and <InlineMath math="n-2>0" />, which together give <InlineMath math="n>2" />. **This domain condition applies before you solve the inequality**.

# Solving the inequality

The sum of two logarithms with the same base is the logarithm of the product, and <InlineMath math="3={}^{2}\log8" />. The base <InlineMath math="2" /> is greater than <InlineMath math="1" />, so **the inequality keeps its direction** when you drop the logarithms.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
{}^{2}\log\big(n(n-2)\big)&\le{}^{2}\log8\\
n^2-2n-8&\le0\\
(n-4)(n+2)&\le0\\
-2\le n&\le4
\end{aligned}" />

Combined with <InlineMath math="n>2" />, the only integers left are <InlineMath math="n=3" /> and <InlineMath math="n=4" />. Check the edge: for <InlineMath math="n=4" />, the left side is <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log4+{}^{2}\log2=3" />, which works. For <InlineMath math="n=5" />, the left side is <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log15>{}^{2}\log8=3" />, which doesn't.

# Comparing P and Q

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n=3&\;\Longrightarrow\;P=2^3=8,\quad Q=3^2=9\\
n=4&\;\Longrightarrow\;P=2^4=16,\quad Q=4^2=16
\end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="n=3" /> you get <InlineMath math="Q>P" />, while for <InlineMath math="n=4" /> you get <InlineMath math="P=Q" />. Both values are allowed, so **no single relationship is always true**.

# The tempting wrong options

- <InlineMath math="Q>P" /> **only checks <InlineMath math="n=3" />**. It's true that <InlineMath math="Q" /> never loses to <InlineMath math="P" /> here, but for <InlineMath math="n=4" /> they're equal.
- <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> only checks <InlineMath math="n=4" /> and forgets <InlineMath math="n=3" />.
- <InlineMath math="P>Q" /> ignores the inequality and uses the general fact that <InlineMath math="2^n>n^2" /> for <InlineMath math="n\ge5" />, but here <InlineMath math="n" /> is at most <InlineMath math="4" />.

So the information given isn't enough to decide the relationship between <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />.

A logarithmic inequality has **two conditions working at once**: every argument must be positive, and the inequality itself. If the argument condition is missed, the set of <InlineMath math="n" /> widens and the relation between <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> becomes undecided.

# The argument condition

<InlineMath math="n-3>0" /> and <InlineMath math="n+3>0" />, so **<InlineMath math="n>3" />**. **This condition throws out all the small numbers**.

# Solving the inequality

Combine the two logarithms:

<BlockMath math="{}^{2}\log\big((n-3)(n+3)\big)\le4\iff n^2-9\le16\iff n^2\le25." />

Together with <InlineMath math="n>3" />, **the only integers left are <InlineMath math="n=4" /> and <InlineMath math="n=5" />**.

# The tempting wrong options

- Not enough information comes from **ignoring the argument condition**. From <InlineMath math="n^2\le25" /> alone, numbers like <InlineMath math="n=0" /> or <InlineMath math="n=3" /> get counted, and there <InlineMath math="Q>P" />.
- <InlineMath math="Q>P" /> uses only those small numbers, for example <InlineMath math="n=3" /> with <InlineMath math="8<11" />.
- <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> holds for neither case, since <InlineMath math="16\neq14" /> and <InlineMath math="32\neq17" />. It comes from eyeballing the quantities near <InlineMath math="n=4" />, where the gap is smallest, without computing them.

So the correct relation is <InlineMath math="P>Q" />.

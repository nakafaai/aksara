The event in the question has two conditions at once: **three different floors** and **Ani gets off first**. The plan: pick a sample space whose outcomes are equally likely, count the outcomes that meet both conditions, then check another way.

# The sample space

Each person picks one of <InlineMath math="8" /> floors, and the choices are independent. Record the outcome in order: Ani's floor, Budi's floor, Cici's floor. **These ordered outcomes are the equally likely ones**. Unordered sets of floors aren't equally likely, because one set can come from many different assignments of people.

<BlockMath math="n(S)=8\cdot8\cdot8=512" />

# The outcomes that count

First choose three different floors, in <InlineMath math="\binom{8}{3}=56" /> ways. The lift only goes up, so the person on the lowest floor gets off first. For Ani to get off first, **Ani must get the lowest of the three floors**, which is one way. Budi and Cici fill the other two floors in <InlineMath math="2" /> ways.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(A)&=56\cdot1\cdot2=112\\
P(A)&=\frac{112}{512}=\frac{7}{32}
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking another way

The probability that all three floors differ is <InlineMath math="\frac{8\cdot7\cdot6}{512}=\frac{21}{32}" />. Once the three floors differ, **each person is equally likely** to have the lowest one, so the chance that it's Ani is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{21}{32}\cdot\frac{1}{3}=\frac{7}{32}" />

The result matches.

# The tempting wrong options

- <InlineMath math="\frac{21}{32}" /> uses only the three-different-floors condition and **forgets that Ani must get off first**.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}" /> is the chance that Ani gets off first when three different floors are already certain. But the three different floors are part of the event, with probability <InlineMath math="\frac{21}{32}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{35}{128}" /> lets Budi and Cici get off on the same floor, counting <InlineMath math="7^2+6^2+\dots+1^2=140" /> outcomes where Ani is lowest. That breaks the three-different-floors condition.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{4}" /> computes the chance of three different floors as <InlineMath math="\frac{7}{8}\cdot\frac{6}{7}=\frac{3}{4}" />, as if a chosen floor couldn't be chosen again. Cici still picks from <InlineMath math="8" /> floors, so the second factor is <InlineMath math="\frac{6}{8}" />.

So the probability that the three get off on three different floors and Ani gets off first is <InlineMath math="\frac{7}{32}" />.

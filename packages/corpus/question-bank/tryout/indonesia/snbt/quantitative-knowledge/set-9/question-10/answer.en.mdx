This question hides two things that are easy to miss: **the probability is conditional**, since we already know Ani gets off before Budi, and **Cici may get off together** with someone else on the top floor.

# The new sample space

The condition "Ani's floor is lower than Budi's" **replaces the sample space**. Floor pairs <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> with <InlineMath math="a<b" /> number <InlineMath math="\binom{8}{2}=28" />, and Cici freely picks one of <InlineMath math="8" /> floors, so

<BlockMath math="n(\text{condition})=28\cdot8=224." />

# Counting the event

Since <InlineMath math="a<b" />, the higher of Ani's and Budi's floors is <InlineMath math="b" />. Cici is on the top floor when <InlineMath math="c\ge b" />, so with Budi on floor <InlineMath math="b" /> there are <InlineMath math="9-b" /> choices for Cici and <InlineMath math="b-1" /> choices for Ani. **The count depends on Budi's floor**, so add it up case by case:

<BlockMath math="\sum_{b=2}^{8}(b-1)(9-b)=7+12+15+16+15+12+7=84." />

So the probability is

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{84}{224}=\frac{3}{8}." />

# Checking another way

Separate Cici getting off alone last, <InlineMath math="c>b" />, from Cici getting off with Budi, <InlineMath math="c=b" />. **The alone case** gives <InlineMath math="\sum(b-1)(8-b)=56" /> arrangements, and the with-Budi case gives <InlineMath math="28" />, one for each pair <InlineMath math="(a,b)" />. The total is <InlineMath math="56+28=84" />, the same.

# The tempting wrong options

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" /> counts only Cici getting off alone on the top floor, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{56}{224}" />, **forgetting that Cici may get off with Budi**.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{3}" /> uses the symmetry "each person is equally likely to be last", which ignores shared floors and the condition on Ani and Budi.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{51}{128}" /> ignores the condition, counting <InlineMath math="c\ge\max(a,b)" /> over all <InlineMath math="512" /> arrangements.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{9}{16}" /> only compares Cici with Budi without the condition, <InlineMath math="P(c\ge b)=\tfrac{36}{64}" />.

So the probability that Cici gets off on the highest floor of the three is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{8}" />.

Since <InlineMath math="DE" /> is parallel to <InlineMath math="BC" />, triangle <InlineMath math="ADE" /> is similar to triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />. For similar figures, **the ratio of areas is the square of the ratio of sides**.

# Ratio of sides and areas

**The corresponding sides** are <InlineMath math="AD" /> and <InlineMath math="AB=4+6=10" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{AD}{AB}&=\frac{4}{10}=\frac25\\
\frac{L_{ADE}}{L_{ABC}}&=\left(\frac25\right)^2=\frac{4}{25}
\end{aligned}" />

# Area of quadrilateral DBCE

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{ABC}&=8\cdot\frac{25}{4}=50\\
L_{DBCE}&=L_{ABC}-L_{ADE}=50-8=42
\end{aligned}" />

The quadrilateral <InlineMath math="DBCE" /> is **what remains of the large triangle after removing the small one**, so its area is 42.

The value 50 is the area of the whole triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />. The values 20 and 12 use the side ratio directly without squaring, <InlineMath math="8\cdot\tfrac{10}{4}" /> and <InlineMath math="8\cdot\tfrac64" />.

So the area of quadrilateral <InlineMath math="DBCE" /> is **<InlineMath math="42\text{ cm}^2" />**.

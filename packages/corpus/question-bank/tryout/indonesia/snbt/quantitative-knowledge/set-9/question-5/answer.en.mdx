Let <InlineMath math="A" /> be the event that the number of green balls drawn is **twice the number of white balls drawn**.

There are <InlineMath math="9" /> balls in the box:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="1 \text{ red} + 6 \text{ green} + 2 \text{ white} = 9" />
</MathContainer>

The balls are drawn uniformly at random without replacement, so every unordered selection of <InlineMath math="7" /> balls is equally likely. The number of possible selections is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="n(S) = C^9_7 = \frac{9!}{7!(9-7)!} = \frac{9\cdot8}{2\cdot1}=36" />
</MathContainer>

Let <InlineMath math="w" />, <InlineMath math="g" />, and <InlineMath math="r" /> denote the numbers of white, green, and red balls drawn. The condition is <InlineMath math="g=2w" />, while <InlineMath math="r+g+w=7" />. Since at most two white balls are available, we check <InlineMath math="w=0" />, <InlineMath math="w=1" />, and <InlineMath math="w=2" />.

| White | Green | Red required | Within the available counts? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="7-0-0=7" /> | No, only one red ball is available |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="7-1-2=4" /> | No, only one red ball is available |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="7-2-4=1" /> | Yes |

The number of favorable selections is:

For four green balls, <InlineMath math="C^6_4=\frac{6\cdot5}{2\cdot1}=15" />. Both white balls and the sole red ball must be selected, so each of those choices has one possibility.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="n(A) = C^2_2 \cdot C^6_4 \cdot C^1_1" />
  <BlockMath math="= 1 \cdot 15 \cdot 1" />
  <BlockMath math="= 15" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="P(A) = \frac{n(A)}{n(S)} = \frac{15}{36} = \frac{5}{12}" />
</MathContainer>

The required probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{12}" />.

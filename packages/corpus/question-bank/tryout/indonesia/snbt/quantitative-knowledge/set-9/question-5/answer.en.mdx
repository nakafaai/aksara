You need to compute both quantities. <InlineMath math="Q" /> comes from triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />, which is fully given. <InlineMath math="P" /> comes from the equal-area condition, and that's where the trap is: **two different isosceles triangles with legs <InlineMath math="17\text{ cm}" /> have that area**, and the non-congruence condition picks one of them.

# The height and area of triangle ABC

In an isosceles triangle, the height from the apex <InlineMath math="A" /> lands exactly at the midpoint of the base <InlineMath math="BC" />. So that height, half the base, and the leg <InlineMath math="AB" /> form **a right triangle**, and Pythagoras applies.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
h_{ABC}&=\sqrt{17^2-8^2}=\sqrt{289-64}=\sqrt{225}=15\\
A_{ABC}&=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot16\cdot15=120
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="Q=2\cdot15=30" />.

# Two possible triangles DEF

Let half of the base <InlineMath math="EF" /> be <InlineMath math="u" /> and the height from <InlineMath math="D" /> be <InlineMath math="h" />. Pythagoras gives <InlineMath math="u^2+h^2=17^2=289" />. The area is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}\cdot2u\cdot h=uh" />, so <InlineMath math="uh=120" />. **Add and subtract <InlineMath math="2uh" />** to get perfect squares.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(u+h)^2&=u^2+h^2+2uh=289+240=529\\
(u-h)^2&=u^2+h^2-2uh=289-240=49
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="u" /> and <InlineMath math="h" /> are positive, <InlineMath math="u+h=23" /> and they differ by <InlineMath math="7" />. The only pairs are <InlineMath math="u=8,\ h=15" /> or <InlineMath math="u=15,\ h=8" />, so <InlineMath math="EF=16" /> or <InlineMath math="EF=30" />.

# The non-congruence condition

If <InlineMath math="EF=16" />, triangle <InlineMath math="DEF" /> has sides <InlineMath math="17" />, <InlineMath math="17" />, and <InlineMath math="16" />, exactly like triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />. Two triangles with the same three sides **are always congruent**, so this case is out. What's left is <InlineMath math="EF=30" />, so <InlineMath math="P=30" />.

Check this triangle: base <InlineMath math="30" /> and height <InlineMath math="8" /> give area <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}\cdot30\cdot8=120" />, and the leg is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{15^2+8^2}=\sqrt{289}=17" />. Both fit.

# Comparing and the tempting wrong options

The two quantities are equal, <InlineMath math="P=Q=30" />. That's no accident: in triangle <InlineMath math="DEF" />, half the base and the height swap roles with those of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />, so **half of <InlineMath math="EF" /> equals the height of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />**.

- <InlineMath math="Q>P" /> comes from using <InlineMath math="EF=16" />, the triangle that's congruent to <InlineMath math="ABC" />.
- The "not enough information" option comes from finding both values of <InlineMath math="EF" /> but forgetting the non-congruence condition.
- <InlineMath math="P>Q" /> comes from computing <InlineMath math="Q" /> with the height of triangle <InlineMath math="DEF" />, <InlineMath math="2\cdot8=16" />, but <InlineMath math="Q" /> uses the height of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />.

So the correct relationship is <InlineMath math="P=Q" />, since both equal <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" />.

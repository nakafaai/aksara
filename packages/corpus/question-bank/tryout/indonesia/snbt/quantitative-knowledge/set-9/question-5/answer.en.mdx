Let <InlineMath math="A" /> be the event that the number of green balls drawn is twice the number of white balls drawn.

There are <InlineMath math="9" /> balls in the box:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="1 \text{ red} + 6 \text{ green} + 2 \text{ white} = 9" />
</MathContainer>

Because <InlineMath math="7" /> balls are drawn simultaneously, every unordered selection of seven balls is equally likely. The number of possible selections is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="n(S) = C^9_7 = \frac{9!}{7!(9-7)!} = 36" />
</MathContainer>

Let <InlineMath math="w" />, <InlineMath math="g" />, and <InlineMath math="r" /> denote the numbers of white, green, and red balls drawn. The condition is <InlineMath math="g=2w" />, while <InlineMath math="r+g+w=7" />. Since at most two white balls are available, we check <InlineMath math="w=0" />, <InlineMath math="w=1" />, and <InlineMath math="w=2" />.

#### Case <InlineMath math="w=0" />

Then <InlineMath math="g=0" /> and <InlineMath math="r=7" />. This is impossible because only <InlineMath math="1" /> red ball is available.

#### Case <InlineMath math="w=1" />

Then <InlineMath math="g=2" /> and <InlineMath math="r=7-1-2=4" />. This is also impossible because only <InlineMath math="1" /> red ball is available.

#### Case <InlineMath math="w=2" />

Then <InlineMath math="g=4" /> and <InlineMath math="r=7-2-4=1" />. This case is possible within the available numbers of each color.

The number of favorable selections is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="n(A) = C^2_2 \cdot C^6_4 \cdot C^1_1" />
  <BlockMath math="= 1 \cdot 15 \cdot 1" />
  <BlockMath math="= 15" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="P(A) = \frac{n(A)}{n(S)} = \frac{15}{36} = \frac{5}{12}" />
</MathContainer>

The required probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{12}" />.

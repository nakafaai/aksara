Equal legs and equal area **don't pin down an isosceles triangle uniquely**: two triangles fit, and the obtuse-angle condition picks one. That choice matters, because the two cases give opposite relations between <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />.

# The area of triangle ABC

The altitude from <InlineMath math="A" /> splits the base <InlineMath math="16" /> into two parts of <InlineMath math="8" />, so the height is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{17^2-8^2}=15" /> and **the area is <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot16\cdot15=120" />**.

# Two possible triangles DEF

Let the altitude of triangle <InlineMath math="DEF" /> from <InlineMath math="D" /> be <InlineMath math="h" /> and half the base <InlineMath math="m" />. Then <InlineMath math="m^2+h^2=289" /> and the area is <InlineMath math="mh=120" />. **Both equations are symmetric in <InlineMath math="m" /> and <InlineMath math="h" />**:

<BlockMath math="(m+h)^2=289+240=529,\qquad (m-h)^2=289-240=49." />

So <InlineMath math="\{m,h\}=\{15,8\}" />. The first case is <InlineMath math="m=8" />, <InlineMath math="h=15" />, base <InlineMath math="16" />. The second is <InlineMath math="m=15" />, <InlineMath math="h=8" />, base <InlineMath math="30" />.

# The obtuse-angle condition

An obtuse apex angle means **the square of the base exceeds the sum of the squares of the legs**, that is <InlineMath math="17^2+17^2=578" />. Base <InlineMath math="16" /> gives <InlineMath math="256<578" />, acute. Base <InlineMath math="30" /> gives <InlineMath math="900>578" />, obtuse. So <InlineMath math="EF=30" /> and the height is <InlineMath math="8" />.

Then <InlineMath math="P=30" /> and <InlineMath math="Q=2\cdot8=16" />, so <InlineMath math="P>Q" />.

# The tempting wrong options

- <InlineMath math="Q>P" /> uses the triangle congruent to <InlineMath math="ABC" />, base <InlineMath math="16" /> and height <InlineMath math="15" />, **without checking the obtuse condition**.
- Not enough information comes from finding both cases but not using the obtuse-angle condition to choose.
- <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> takes <InlineMath math="Q" /> as twice the height of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />, that is <InlineMath math="30" />, instead of twice the height of triangle <InlineMath math="DEF" />.

So the correct relation is <InlineMath math="P>Q" />.

The question doesn't give the group sizes at all, and it doesn't give the leaving member's score either. So there are two things to find, and **two conditions in the question pick the answer**: scores go up to <InlineMath math="100" />, and group B started with more than <InlineMath math="5" /> members.

# The ratio of group sizes

The combined mean <InlineMath math="71" /> is <InlineMath math="6" /> away from group A and <InlineMath math="9" /> away from group B. **The group closer to the combined mean has more members**, so

<BlockMath math="n_A:n_B=9:6=3:2,\qquad n_A=3k,\quad n_B=2k,\quad n=5k." />

# The leaving member's score

Call the score <InlineMath math="s" />. A total equals the mean times the number of members, so

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
71\cdot5k-s&=69(5k-1)\\
355k-s&=345k-69\\
s&=10k+69.
\end{aligned}" />

**The score <InlineMath math="s" /> depends on <InlineMath math="k" />**, so both conditions come in here. Scores up to <InlineMath math="100" /> give <InlineMath math="10k+69\le100" />, so <InlineMath math="k\le3" />. Group B having more than <InlineMath math="5" /> members gives <InlineMath math="2k>5" />, so <InlineMath math="k\ge3" />.

# Group B's new mean

With <InlineMath math="k=3" />, group B started with <InlineMath math="6" /> members and a total of <InlineMath math="80\cdot6=480" />, and the leaving score is <InlineMath math="99" />. **Both the total and the size of group B go down**:

<BlockMath math="\bar{x}_B=\frac{480-99}{6-1}=\frac{381}{5}=76.2." />

# Checking the result

At first there were <InlineMath math="15" /> members with a total of <InlineMath math="9\cdot65+480=1065" />, and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1065}{15}=71" />. After the <InlineMath math="99" /> leaves, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{966}{14}=69" />, **matching the new combined mean**.

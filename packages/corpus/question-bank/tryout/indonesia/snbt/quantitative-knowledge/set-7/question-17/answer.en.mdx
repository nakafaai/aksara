The area of a triangle with a right angle at <InlineMath math="C" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot AC\cdot BC" />. So we need **the product of the two legs**, not necessarily each leg.

# Statement (1) alone

The side <InlineMath math="AB" /> is the hypotenuse, so <InlineMath math="BC" /> follows from **the Pythagorean theorem**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
BC&=\sqrt{10^2-6^2}=\sqrt{64}=8\\
L&=\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot8=24
\end{aligned}" />

The area is unique, so statement (1) alone is sufficient.

# Statement (2) alone

Let <InlineMath math="AC=a" /> and <InlineMath math="BC=b" />. The perimeter 24 gives <InlineMath math="a+b=14" />, and Pythagoras gives <InlineMath math="a^2+b^2=100" />. Square the sum to **get the product without finding a and b**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(a+b)^2&=a^2+b^2+2ab\\
196&=100+2ab\\
ab&=48,\quad L=\tfrac12ab=24
\end{aligned}" />

The area is unique again, so statement (2) alone is sufficient. The two statements agree, because the legs 6 and 8 do add up to 14.

So **statement (1) alone is sufficient, and statement (2) alone is also sufficient**.

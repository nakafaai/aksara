The area of a circle is fixed by its radius. So a statement is sufficient if **the radius can be determined uniquely** from it.

# Statement (1) alone

An arc with central angle <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{60}{360}=\tfrac16" /> of the circumference. The arc length is **proportional to its central angle**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac16\cdot2\pi r&=2\pi\\
r&=6,\quad \text{area}=36\pi
\end{aligned}" />

Statement (1) alone is sufficient.

# Statement (2) alone

All four vertices of the square lie on the circle, so **the diagonal of the square is a diameter of the circle**. It passes through the centre and joins two opposite points of the circle.

<BlockMath math="2r=12\;\Longrightarrow\;r=6,\quad \text{area}=36\pi" />

Statement (2) alone is also sufficient, and both statements give the same area.

So **statement (1) alone is sufficient, and statement (2) alone is also sufficient**.

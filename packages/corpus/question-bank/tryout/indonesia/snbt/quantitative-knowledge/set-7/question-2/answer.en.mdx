First make **the bases of both sides** equal. Since <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{27}=\left(\tfrac13\right)^3" />, the inequality becomes a comparison of two powers of <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" />.

# Direction for a base less than one

The function <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac13\right)^t" /> is decreasing, so a smaller exponent gives a larger value. That is why **the inequality sign reverses** when we compare the exponents.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\left(\tfrac13\right)^{x^2-2x}&>\left(\tfrac13\right)^{3}\\
x^2-2x&<3\\
x^2-2x-3&<0\\
(x-3)(x+1)&<0
\end{aligned}" />

# The solution interval

The product <InlineMath math="(x-3)(x+1)" /> is negative **between its two zeros**, so <InlineMath math="-1<x<3" />. The points <InlineMath math="-1" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> are excluded because the sign is strict.

The integers in this interval are 0, 1 and 2. Check with <InlineMath math="x=1" />: the exponent is <InlineMath math="-1" />, so the left side equals 3, and <InlineMath math="3>\tfrac{1}{27}" />.

The value 5 also counts both endpoints. Without reversing the sign, the solution would be <InlineMath math="x<-1" /> or <InlineMath math="x>3" />, which contains infinitely many integers.

So the number of integers that satisfy the inequality is **3**.

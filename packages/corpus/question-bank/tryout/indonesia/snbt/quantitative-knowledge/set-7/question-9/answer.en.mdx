The four positions follow the pattern <InlineMath math="L_1-D_1-L_2-D_2" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\underline{L_1}\;\underline{D_1}\;\underline{L_2}\;\underline{D_2}" />
</MathContainer>

The available vowels are <InlineMath math="\{A,I,U,E,O\}" />. The first digit comes from <InlineMath math="\{1,3,5,7,9\}" />, while the second digit comes from <InlineMath math="\{0,1,2,4,6\}" />.

#### Choose the letters

The second letter is fixed by <InlineMath math="L_2=O" />. Because letters may not repeat and the first letter may not be A, the choices for the first letter are <InlineMath math="L_1\in\{I,U,E\}" />. This gives <InlineMath math="3" /> letter arrangements.

#### Choose the digits

The two digit sets overlap only at <InlineMath math="1" />, so we split the count into two cases.

1. If <InlineMath math="D_2=1" />, then <InlineMath math="D_1\in\{3,5,7,9\}" />. The number of digit pairs is

   <BlockMath math="1\times4=4" />

2. If <InlineMath math="D_2\in\{0,2,4,6\}" />, it cannot equal any odd choice for <InlineMath math="D_1\in\{1,3,5,7,9\}" />. The number of digit pairs is

   <BlockMath math="4\times5=20" />

Therefore, the total number of valid digit pairs is

<BlockMath math="4+20=24" />

#### Count all codes

Multiplying the independent letter and digit choices gives

<BlockMath math="3\times24=72" />

Thus, <InlineMath math="72" /> codes can be formed.

A combined mean is **a mean weighted by the number of students** in each class. The class sizes aren't given, but their ratios can be found from the two combined means you do have. Here's the plan: find the ratio of classes A and B, use it to find the size of class C, then compute the combined mean of B and C.

# The ratio of class A to class B

Let classes A, B, and C have <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" /> students. A class's total score is its mean times its number of students.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
50a+70b&=65(a+b)\\
5b&=15a\\
b&=3a
\end{aligned}" />

That makes sense: <InlineMath math="65" /> is closer to <InlineMath math="70" /> than to <InlineMath math="50" />, so **class B has to be bigger**. Write <InlineMath math="a=k" /> and <InlineMath math="b=3k" />.

# The size of class C

Classes A and B together hold <InlineMath math="4k" /> students with mean <InlineMath math="65" />, so their total is <InlineMath math="260k" />. **The result of the previous step feeds in here** to build the equation for all three classes.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
260k+95c&=75(4k+c)\\
260k+95c&=300k+75c\\
20c&=40k\\
c&=2k
\end{aligned}" />

So the class sizes of A, B, and C are in the ratio <InlineMath math="1:3:2" />.

# The combined mean of class B and class C

Use the class sizes you now know and **weight each mean by its number of students**.

<BlockMath math="\bar x_{BC}=\frac{70(3k)+95(2k)}{3k+2k}=\frac{210k+190k}{5k}=\frac{400k}{5k}=80" />

A check by another route: the total of all three classes is <InlineMath math="75\times6k=450k" />. Take out class A, whose total is <InlineMath math="50k" />, and <InlineMath math="400k" /> is left for <InlineMath math="5k" /> students, a mean of <InlineMath math="80" />.

# The wrong options

- <InlineMath math="90" /> comes from flipping the ratio of A to B to <InlineMath math="3:1" />, which also makes the size of class C wrong, <InlineMath math="4k" />.
- <InlineMath math="85" /> comes from treating <InlineMath math="75" /> as the midpoint of <InlineMath math="65" /> and the combined mean of B and C, that is <InlineMath math="2(75)-65" />.
- <InlineMath math="82.5" /> is the plain average <InlineMath math="\frac{70+95}{2}" />, which **ignores the different class sizes**.
- <InlineMath math="77" /> comes from using the combined mean of A and B, <InlineMath math="65" />, as if it were the mean of class B: <InlineMath math="\frac{65(3)+95(2)}{5}=77" />.

So the combined mean of class B and class C is <InlineMath math="80" />.

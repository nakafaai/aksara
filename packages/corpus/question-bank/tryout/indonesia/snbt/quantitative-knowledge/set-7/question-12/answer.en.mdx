The area of the first triangle is fixed because all three sides are known. The area of the second triangle **depends on the angle between the two known sides**, so check whether it can be smaller and larger than <InlineMath math="P" />.

# Area of the first triangle

Draw the altitude to the side 14. It splits that side into parts 5 and 9, because <InlineMath math="13^2-5^2=15^2-9^2=144" />. So **the altitude is 12 cm**.

<BlockMath math="P=\tfrac12\cdot14\cdot12=84" />

# Range of the second area

If the angle between the sides 13 and 15 is <InlineMath math="\theta" />, then

<BlockMath math="Q=\tfrac12\cdot13\cdot15\cdot\sin\theta=97.5\sin\theta" />

The angle satisfies <InlineMath math="0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ" />. The value of <InlineMath math="Q" /> **can be any positive number up to 97.5**.

- If the third side is 14, the second triangle equals the first and <InlineMath math="Q=84=P" />.
- If <InlineMath math="\theta=90^\circ" />, then <InlineMath math="Q=97.5>P" />.
- If <InlineMath math="\theta=30^\circ" />, then <InlineMath math="Q=48.75<P" />.

All three relations are possible, so the relation between <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> **cannot be determined**.

So the information given is **not sufficient** to decide the relation between <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />.

Each statement uses **a different measure**: a count of families, a percentage change, a mean and a median. Compute each from the table.

# Direction and percentage of change

Use fell for family B (25 to 20) and family D (30 to 27). The others rose, so **only two families decreased**. The first statement is false.

A percentage increase is measured against the July use.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{A}&:\quad \tfrac{6}{18}\approx33\%\\
\text{C}&:\quad \tfrac{3}{12}=25\%\\
\text{E}&:\quad \tfrac{9}{15}=60\%
\end{aligned}" />

The largest percentage increase belongs to family E, not A. Family E rose by 9 cubic metres from **a smaller starting use**, 15, so its percentage is the largest. The second statement is false.

# Mean and median

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\bar x_{\text{July}}&=\frac{18+25+12+30+15}{5}=\frac{100}{5}=20\\
\bar x_{\text{August}}&=\frac{24+20+15+27+24}{5}=\frac{110}{5}=22
\end{aligned}" />

The difference is 2 cubic metres, so the third statement is true.

The July data in order are 12, 15, 18, 25, 30. **The middle value is 18**, so the fourth statement is true.

So the first statement is **false**, the second is **false**, the third is **true**, and the fourth is **true**.

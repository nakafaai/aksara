The safest way to compare two quantities is to **look at the sign of their difference**, <InlineMath math="P-Q" />. Here's the plan: turn that difference into an expression in <InlineMath math="k" /> with Vieta's formulas, then use the condition "two distinct real roots" to pin down the sign.

# The difference of the two quantities

Group <InlineMath math="a^2+b^2-2ab" /> into a perfect square.

<BlockMath math="P-Q=a^2+b^2-2ab-c=(a-b)^2-c" />

For <InlineMath math="x^2-2x+k=0" />, **Vieta's formulas** give <InlineMath math="a+b=2" /> and <InlineMath math="ab=k" />. The squared difference of the roots can be written with their sum and product.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(a-b)^2&=(a+b)^2-4ab=4-4k\\
P-Q&=(4-4k)-(5-5k)=k-1
\end{aligned}" />

# The condition of two distinct real roots

At this point <InlineMath math="k-1" /> looks like it could be positive or negative, and many people stop with "not enough information". But the question says the roots are real and distinct, so **the discriminant has to be positive**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
D=(-2)^2-4(1)(k)&>0\\
4-4k&>0\\
k&<1
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="k<1" />, the difference <InlineMath math="P-Q=k-1" /> is always negative, so <InlineMath math="Q>P" />.

# Checking with examples and another route

For <InlineMath math="k=0" />, the roots are <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="2" />, so <InlineMath math="P=4" /> and <InlineMath math="Q=0+5=5" />. For <InlineMath math="k=-3" />, the roots are <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="-1" />, so <InlineMath math="P=10" /> and <InlineMath math="Q=-6+20=14" />. Both examples give <InlineMath math="Q>P" />.

Another route: <InlineMath math="c=5-5k=\tfrac54(4-4k)=\tfrac54(a-b)^2" />, so <InlineMath math="P-Q=(a-b)^2-\tfrac54(a-b)^2=-\tfrac14(a-b)^2" />. **Distinct roots make <InlineMath math="(a-b)^2>0" />**, so the difference is negative.

The option <InlineMath math="P>Q" /> comes from assuming a sum of squares must be big, <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> would need <InlineMath math="a=b" />, which the question rules out, and "not enough information" comes from leaving out the discriminant condition.

So the correct relationship is <InlineMath math="Q>P" />.

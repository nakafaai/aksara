The two lines are parallel because the coefficients of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> are proportional, <InlineMath math="3:4=6:8" />. The distance formula for parallel lines only works when **both equations use exactly the same coefficients**.

# Matching the coefficients

Multiply the first equation by 2, **including its right-hand side**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
6x+8y-24&=0\\
6x+8y+1&=0
\end{aligned}" />

# The distance formula

For the lines <InlineMath math="ax+by+c_1=0" /> and <InlineMath math="ax+by+c_2=0" />, the distance is **the difference of the constants divided by the length of the normal vector**.

<BlockMath math="d=\frac{\lvert c_1-c_2\rvert}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}=\frac{\lvert-24-1\rvert}{\sqrt{6^2+8^2}}=\frac{25}{10}=2.5" />

Check: the point <InlineMath math="(4,0)" /> lies on the first line, and its distance to the second line is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{\lvert6\cdot4+8\cdot0+1\rvert}{10}=\tfrac{25}{10}=2.5" />.

The value 1.3 doubles the coefficients but forgets to double the constant 12. The value 2.3 uses <InlineMath math="\lvert-24+1\rvert" /> because of a sign error. The value 2.4 is the distance from the origin to the first line. The value 2.2 divides <InlineMath math="\lvert12-1\rvert" /> by 5 without matching the coefficients.

So the distance between the lines is **2.5 units**.

Let <InlineMath math="F" /> represent Farhat's salary and <InlineMath math="A" /> represent Abas's salary.

The initial ratio of their salaries is given as <InlineMath math="2 : 3" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="F : A = 2 : 3 \Rightarrow F = \frac{2}{3}A" />
</MathContainer>

After both salaries increase by <InlineMath math="40{,}000" /> rupiah, their new ratio is <InlineMath math="5 : 7" />. Because every amount is a multiple of one thousand, we can calculate in thousands of rupiah. In these units, the increase is <InlineMath math="40" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(F + 40) : (A + 40) = 5 : 7" />
</MathContainer>

Write the ratio as a fraction:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{F + 40}{A + 40} = \frac{5}{7}" />
</MathContainer>

Cross-multiplication gives:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="7(F + 40) = 5(A + 40)" />
</MathContainer>

Now substitute <InlineMath math="F = \frac{2}{3}A" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="7\left(\frac{2}{3}A + 40\right) = 5(A + 40)" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac{14}{3}A + 280 = 5A + 200" />
</MathContainer>

Collect the terms containing <InlineMath math="A" /> on one side and the constants on the other:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="280 - 200 = 5A - \frac{14}{3}A" />
  <BlockMath math="80 = \frac{15}{3}A - \frac{14}{3}A" />
  <BlockMath math="80 = \frac{1}{3}A" />
</MathContainer>

Multiplying both sides by <InlineMath math="3" /> gives the value of <InlineMath math="A" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="A = 80 \times 3" />
  <BlockMath math="A = 240" />
</MathContainer>

Because <InlineMath math="A" /> was measured in thousands of rupiah, Abas's current salary is <InlineMath math="240{,}000" /> rupiah.

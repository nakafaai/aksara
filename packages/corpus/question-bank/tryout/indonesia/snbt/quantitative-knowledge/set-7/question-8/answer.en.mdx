Compare the **two groups of animal keepers**:
- People raising goats: <InlineMath math="K = 450" />
- People raising chickens: <InlineMath math="A = 300" />
- Total population: <InlineMath math="S = 600" />

Let <InlineMath math="M" /> and <InlineMath math="m" /> denote the maximum and minimum numbers of people who raise both animals.

#### Maximum Number of People Raising Both

The maximum number of people raising both animals is the size of the smaller group. This happens if **all members of the smaller group** are also members of the larger group.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}M&=\min(K,A)\\&=\min(450,300)\\&=300\end{aligned}" />

#### Minimum Number of People Raising Both

The minimum overlap occurs when the **union is as large as possible**, namely 600 people. Adding the group sizes gives <InlineMath math="450+300=750" />. The excess <InlineMath math="750-600" /> counts people included twice:

<BlockMath math="m=\max(0,K+A-S)" />

Substitute the known values:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}m&=\max(0,750-600)\\&=\max(0,150)\\&=150\end{aligned}" />

Both bounds can be attained by the following population allocations.

| Bound | Goats only | Both | Chickens only | Neither |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M | 150 | 300 | 0 | 150 |
| m | 300 | 150 | 150 | 0 |

#### Sum of Maximum and Minimum

We are asked to find the **sum of the maximum and minimum values**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}M+m&=300+150\\&=450\end{aligned}" />

Thus, the sum of the maximum and minimum number of people raising both animals is <InlineMath math="450" />.

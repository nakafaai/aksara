For three geometric terms, write the middle term as <InlineMath math="a" /> and the other two as <InlineMath math="\tfrac{a}{r}" /> and <InlineMath math="ar" />. With this choice **the ratio cancels in the product**, so the middle term is found at once.

# The middle term from the product

<BlockMath math="\frac{a}{r}\cdot a\cdot ar=a^3=216\;\Longrightarrow\; a=6" />

# The ratio from the sum

Put <InlineMath math="a=6" /> into the sum, then multiply both sides by <InlineMath math="r" /> to get **a quadratic equation in <InlineMath math="r" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac6r+6+6r&=26\\
6r^2-20r+6&=0\\
3r^2-10r+3&=0\\
(3r-1)(r-3)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The value <InlineMath math="r=3" /> gives the sequence 2, 6, 18, and <InlineMath math="r=\tfrac13" /> gives the same numbers in reverse order, 18, 6, 2. Both contain the same numbers, so the answer is unique.

Check: <InlineMath math="2+6+18=26" /> and <InlineMath math="2\cdot6\cdot18=216" />. The difference between the largest and the smallest number is <InlineMath math="18-2=16" />.

The value 18 is the largest number itself. The value 20 is the sum of the two outer terms, and 26 is the sum of all three terms.

So the difference between the largest and the smallest number is **16**.

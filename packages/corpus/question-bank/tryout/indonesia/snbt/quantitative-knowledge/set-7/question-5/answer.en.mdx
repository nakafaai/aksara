Write the three terms as <InlineMath math="\tfrac{a}{r}" />, <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="ar" />. The key is **dividing the sum of squares by the sum of the terms**, because the quotient is an expression very close to the sum of the terms.

# The sum of squares identity

For three geometric terms,

<BlockMath math="\frac{a^2}{r^2}+a^2+a^2r^2=\left(\frac ar+a+ar\right)\left(\frac ar-a+ar\right)" />

You can check the right side by multiplying out: the cross terms cancel. So **the sum of squares divided by the sum** equals <InlineMath math="\tfrac ar-a+ar" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac ar+a+ar&=38\\
\frac ar-a+ar&=\frac{532}{38}=14
\end{aligned}" />

# Finding the terms

Subtract the two equations: <InlineMath math="2a=24" />, so <InlineMath math="a=12" /> and **the sum of the two outer terms** is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12}{r}+12r=26" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
12r^2-26r+12&=0\\
6r^2-13r+6&=0\\
(3r-2)(2r-3)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The value <InlineMath math="r=\tfrac32" /> gives 8, 12, 18, and <InlineMath math="r=\tfrac23" /> gives the same numbers in reverse order. Check: <InlineMath math="8+12+18=38" /> and <InlineMath math="64+144+324=532" />.

The difference between the largest and the smallest number is <InlineMath math="18-8=10" />.

The value 12 is the middle term, 14 is the quotient <InlineMath math="532:38" />, 18 is the largest term, and 26 is the sum of the two outer terms.

So the difference between the largest and the smallest number is **10**.

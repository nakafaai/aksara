The question hands you extra information, so what you need is a **conditional probability**: the chance of one event when another event is known to have happened. Call <InlineMath math="G" /> the event "the numbers on the first two cards add up to an even number" and <InlineMath math="E" /> the event "the first even card shows up on the third draw". Here's the plan: find <InlineMath math="P(G)" />, find the chance that <InlineMath math="E" /> and <InlineMath math="G" /> both happen, then divide.

<BlockMath math="P(E\mid G)=\frac{P(E\cap G)}{P(G)}" />

# When the first two cards add up to an even number

There are <InlineMath math="5" /> odd cards and <InlineMath math="5" /> even cards. The sum of two whole numbers is even **exactly when both are odd or both are even**. Without replacement, the second card comes from the <InlineMath math="9" /> cards that are left.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{odd, odd})&=\tfrac{5}{10}\cdot\tfrac49=\tfrac29\\
P(\text{even, even})&=\tfrac{5}{10}\cdot\tfrac49=\tfrac29\\
P(G)&=\tfrac29+\tfrac29=\tfrac49
\end{aligned}" />

# The event you're asked about

The first even card shows up on the third draw means the order is odd, odd, even. Two odd cards first give an even sum, so **the event <InlineMath math="E" /> already sits inside <InlineMath math="G" />** and <InlineMath math="E\cap G=E" />. After two odd cards are gone, <InlineMath math="8" /> cards are left, and <InlineMath math="5" /> of them are even.

<BlockMath math="P(E\cap G)=\tfrac{5}{10}\cdot\tfrac49\cdot\tfrac58=\tfrac{100}{720}=\tfrac{5}{36}" />

# Computing the conditional probability

**Divide the joint probability by the probability of the condition**, because the condition becomes the new sample space.

<BlockMath math="P(E\mid G)=\frac{5/36}{4/9}=\frac{5}{36}\cdot\frac94=\frac{45}{144}=\frac{5}{16}" />

A check by another route: once <InlineMath math="G" /> has happened, odd-odd and even-even are equally likely (each <InlineMath math="\tfrac29" />), so each has chance <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />. In the odd-odd case, the third card is even with chance <InlineMath math="\tfrac58" />, while in the even-even case the first even card has already appeared earlier. That gives <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac58=\tfrac{5}{16}" />.

# The wrong options

- <InlineMath math="\frac{13}{16}" /> comes from reading "exactly on the third draw" as "by the third draw at the latest", which also counts the even-even case: <InlineMath math="\tfrac12+\tfrac{5}{16}=\tfrac{13}{16}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac58" /> comes from assuming the first two cards must be odd, which forgets the even-even case.
- <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> is the chance that the third card is even when **the word "first" is ignored**: <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac58+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac38=\tfrac12" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac14" /> comes from treating the draws as with replacement, so every draw is even with chance <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />: <InlineMath math="\frac{(1/2)^3}{1/2}=\frac14" />.

So the probability that the first even card shows up exactly on the third draw is <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{16}" />.

An angle bisector divides the opposite side in **the ratio of the two sides that form the angle**. This is the angle bisector theorem.

# The ratio from the angle bisector theorem

The bisector from <InlineMath math="A" /> meets <InlineMath math="BC" />, so <InlineMath math="D" /> divides <InlineMath math="BC" /> in the ratio <InlineMath math="AB:AC" />. The segment <InlineMath math="DC" /> **pairs with <InlineMath math="AC" />**, because both meet at <InlineMath math="C" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{BD}{DC}=\frac{AB}{AC}=\frac69=\frac23" />

# Dividing BC

The side <InlineMath math="BC=10" /> splits into <InlineMath math="2+3=5" /> equal parts, and **<InlineMath math="DC" /> takes three of them**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
BD&=\tfrac25\cdot10=4\\
DC&=\tfrac35\cdot10=6
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="4+6=10" /> and <InlineMath math="4:6=2:3" />.

The length 4 cm is <InlineMath math="BD" />, the result of swapping the paired sides. The length 5 cm splits <InlineMath math="BC" /> in half, as a median would, not a bisector. The length 4.5 cm is half of <InlineMath math="AC" /> and 3 cm is half of <InlineMath math="AB" />.

So the length of <InlineMath math="DC" /> is **<InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />**.

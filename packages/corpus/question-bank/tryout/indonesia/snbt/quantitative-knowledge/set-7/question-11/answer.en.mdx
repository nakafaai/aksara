The radii are not known separately, but the area of the ring needs only **the difference of the squared radii**, <InlineMath math="\pi(R^2-r^2)" />.

# A right triangle at the point of tangency

Let the centre be <InlineMath math="O" />, the chord <InlineMath math="AB" />, and the point of tangency <InlineMath math="T" />. The radius <InlineMath math="OT" /> of the small circle is **perpendicular to the chord at the point of tangency**. A line from the centre perpendicular to a chord bisects it, so <InlineMath math="AT=8" />.

Triangle <InlineMath math="OTA" /> is right-angled at <InlineMath math="T" />, with <InlineMath math="OA=R" /> and <InlineMath math="OT=r" />.

<BlockMath math="R^2=r^2+8^2\;\Longrightarrow\;R^2-r^2=64" />

# Area of the ring

<BlockMath math="L=\pi R^2-\pi r^2=\pi(R^2-r^2)=64\pi" />

The result **does not depend on the size of either circle**, as long as a 16 cm chord touches the small circle.

The value <InlineMath math="256\pi" /> uses the whole chord 16 instead of its half. The value <InlineMath math="32\pi" /> takes half of <InlineMath math="64\pi" />, as if computing a triangle area. The value <InlineMath math="128\pi" /> computes <InlineMath math="\pi\cdot16\cdot8" />, and <InlineMath math="192\pi" /> subtracts <InlineMath math="64\pi" /> from <InlineMath math="256\pi" />.

So the area between the two circles is **<InlineMath math="64\pi\text{ cm}^2" />**.

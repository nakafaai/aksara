The volume depends on the radius of the hole, and that radius is hidden in the surface area information. Here's the plan: write the change in surface area as an equation in <InlineMath math="r" />, solve it, **throw out the radius that makes the hole too wide for the cube**, then compute the volume.

# The change in surface area

Let the radius of the hole be <InlineMath math="r" /> centimetres. Drilling changes the surface in two ways. **Two circles disappear** from two faces of the cube, each with area <InlineMath math="\pi r^2" />, and **the wall of the hole appears**, which is the curved surface of a cylinder as tall as the cube's edge, with area <InlineMath math="2\pi r\times9" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
-2\pi r^2+18\pi r&=40\pi\\
r^2-9r+20&=0\\
(r-4)(r-5)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The second line comes from dividing both sides by <InlineMath math="-2\pi" />. So <InlineMath math="r=4" /> or <InlineMath math="r=5" />.

# Choosing the possible radius

The hole has to fit inside a face of the cube, a <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" /> by <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" /> square, so **the diameter of the hole must be less than <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" />**, that is <InlineMath math="r<4.5" />. The radius <InlineMath math="r=5" /> gives a diameter of <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" />, wider than the cube itself, so it's rejected. Only <InlineMath math="r=4" /> is left.

Notice that the rejected radius doesn't give a negative volume: <InlineMath math="729-225\pi" /> is still positive, because <InlineMath math="225\pi<225\times3.2=720" />. So this mistake **only shows up if you check whether the hole fits**.

# Computing the volume

The volume is **the cube's volume minus the volume of the cylinder that was removed**. The cylinder is as tall as the cube's edge, <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V&=9^3-\pi(4)^2(9)\\
&=729-144\pi
\end{aligned}" />

Check: for <InlineMath math="r=4" />, the change in area is <InlineMath math="-2\pi(16)+18\pi(4)=-32\pi+72\pi=40\pi" />, as given.

# The wrong options

- <InlineMath math="(729-16\pi)\text{ cm}^3" /> comes from writing the cylinder's volume as just <InlineMath math="\pi r^2" />, **forgetting to multiply by the height** <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}" />.
- <InlineMath math="(729-36\pi)\text{ cm}^3" /> comes from treating <InlineMath math="4" /> as the diameter, so the radius becomes <InlineMath math="2" /> and the cylinder's volume <InlineMath math="\pi(2)^2(9)" />.
- <InlineMath math="(729-72\pi)\text{ cm}^3" /> comes from subtracting the wall area <InlineMath math="2\pi(4)(9)" /> instead of the cylinder's volume.
- <InlineMath math="(729-225\pi)\text{ cm}^3" /> uses <InlineMath math="r=5" />, but a hole <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> wide doesn't fit inside the cube.

So the volume of the solid after drilling is <InlineMath math="(729-144\pi)\text{ cm}^3" />.

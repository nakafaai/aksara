The two spheres are linked through the edge of the cube. Let the edge be <InlineMath math="s" />. The volume of a sphere is proportional to the cube of its radius, so it is enough to **compare the radii and cube the ratio**.

# The two radii

The small sphere touches two opposite faces of the cube, so **its diameter equals the edge** and <InlineMath math="r=\tfrac{s}{2}" />.

The large sphere passes through two opposite vertices of the cube, so **its diameter equals the space diagonal** of the cube.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{space diagonal}&=\sqrt{s^2+s^2+s^2}=s\sqrt3\\
R&=\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}
\end{aligned}" />

# Ratio of the volumes

The volume ratio is **the cube of the ratio of the radii**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{V_{\text{large}}}{V_{\text{small}}}=\left(\frac Rr\right)^3=\left(\sqrt3\right)^3=3\sqrt3" />

The ratio <InlineMath math="\sqrt3:1" /> is the ratio of the radii, and <InlineMath math="3:1" /> is the ratio of the surface areas. The ratios <InlineMath math="27:1" /> and <InlineMath math="9:1" /> appear if the ratio of the radii is taken as 3, or if 3 is squared.

So the ratio of the volume of the large sphere to the small sphere is **<InlineMath math="3\sqrt3:1" />**.

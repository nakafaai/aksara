Three students are chosen at once, with no order, so the sample space is counted with **combinations**. At least 2 girls means exactly 2 girls or exactly 3 girls.

# Sample space and event

<BlockMath math="n(S)=\binom{10}{3}=120" />

Count **each case separately**, then add, because the two cases cannot happen together.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{exactly 2 girls}&:\quad \binom42\binom61=6\cdot6=36\\
\text{exactly 3 girls}&:\quad \binom43=4\\
n(A)&=36+4=40
\end{aligned}" />

# Probability

The probability is **the number of outcomes in the event divided by the size of the sample space**.

<BlockMath math="P(A)=\frac{40}{120}=\frac13" />

The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac{44}{125}" /> treats each choice as independent with a fixed probability <InlineMath math="\tfrac25" /> for a girl, as if the same student could be chosen again. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac23" /> is the probability of the opposite event, at most 1 girl. The values <InlineMath math="\tfrac25" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac35" /> are the probabilities of a girl or a boy in a single choice.

So the probability of choosing at least 2 girls is **<InlineMath math="\tfrac13" />**.

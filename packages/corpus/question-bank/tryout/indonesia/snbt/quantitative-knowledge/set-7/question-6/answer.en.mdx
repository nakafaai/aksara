Combine the two logarithms with the rule **a sum of logarithms is the logarithm of the product**, and remember that every argument of a logarithm must be positive.

# Condition on the arguments

For both logarithms to be defined, <InlineMath math="x+2>0" /> and <InlineMath math="x-4>0" />. Both hold together exactly when <InlineMath math="x>4" />, so **every solution must be greater than 4**.

# Solving the equation

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
{}^{2}\log\big[(x+2)(x-4)\big]&=4\\
(x+2)(x-4)&=2^4=16\\
x^2-2x-24&=0\\
(x-6)(x+4)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The root <InlineMath math="x=-4" /> **is rejected** because it fails <InlineMath math="x>4" />, since <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log(-2)" /> is undefined. Only <InlineMath math="x=6" /> remains.

Check: <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log8+{}^{2}\log2=3+1=4" />.

The value 2 adds both roots without checking the condition. The value <InlineMath math="-4" /> is the rejected root, and <InlineMath math="-6" /> comes from a sign error in the factorisation. The value 9 appears if the sum of logarithms is turned into the logarithm of a sum, <InlineMath math="(x+2)+(x-4)=16" />.

So the sum of all solutions is **6**.

There are three unknowns but only two equations, so <InlineMath math="x" />, <InlineMath math="y" />, and <InlineMath math="z" /> are indeed not fixed individually. The question, however, asks only for **their sum**, and that sum can still be fixed.

# Adding the two equations

Look at the coefficients: <InlineMath math="1+3" />, <InlineMath math="2+2" />, and <InlineMath math="3+1" /> are all 4. So **the sum of the two equations** contains <InlineMath math="x" />, <InlineMath math="y" />, and <InlineMath math="z" /> with equal coefficients.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x+2y+3z)+(3x+2y+z)&=14+10\\
4x+4y+4z&=24\\
x+y+z&=6
\end{aligned}" />

# Making sure a solution exists

The sum is meaningful only if some numbers really satisfy the system. One example is <InlineMath math="x=1" />, <InlineMath math="y=2" />, and <InlineMath math="z=3" />: <InlineMath math="1+4+9=14" /> and <InlineMath math="3+4+3=10" />, with sum 6. **Every other solution gives the same sum**, because the sum follows directly from the two equations.

The option *tidak dapat ditentukan* (cannot be determined) comes from the belief that three unknowns always need three equations. The value 24 stops before dividing by 4, and the value 12 divides only by 2. The value 4 comes from the difference of the equations, <InlineMath math="2z-2x=4" />, which is not the sum of the three unknowns.

So the value of <InlineMath math="x+y+z" /> is **6**.

A combined mean is a weighted mean, so what matters is **the ratio of the group sizes**, not the sizes themselves. Let the numbers of students in groups A, B, and C be <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" />, and let the mean of group C be <InlineMath math="x" />. The total score of a group equals its mean times its size.

# The ratio of groups A and B

The total score of A and B together equals their combined mean times <InlineMath math="a+b" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
50a+80b&=60(a+b)\\
20b&=10a\\
a&=2b
\end{aligned}" />

So **group A is twice as large as group B**, even though neither size is known.

# Two equations for group C

Combining B and C gives the first equation. Combining all three groups, after replacing <InlineMath math="a" /> with <InlineMath math="2b" />, gives the second.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
80b+xc=86(b+c) &\implies xc=6b+86c\\
100b+80b+xc=78(3b+c) &\implies xc=54b+78c
\end{aligned}" />

Both right sides equal <InlineMath math="xc" />, so **set the two right sides equal**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
6b+86c&=54b+78c\\
8c&=48b\\
c&=6b
\end{aligned}" />

Substitute <InlineMath math="c=6b" /> into the first equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x\cdot 6b&=6b+86\cdot 6b=522b\\
x&=87
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking with sample group sizes

Take <InlineMath math="b=1" />, so <InlineMath math="a=2" /> and <InlineMath math="c=6" />. The total scores are <InlineMath math="100" />, <InlineMath math="80" />, and <InlineMath math="6\cdot 87=522" />. The combined mean of A and B is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{180}{3}=60" />, of B and C it's <InlineMath math="\tfrac{602}{7}=86" />, and of everyone it's <InlineMath math="\tfrac{702}{9}=78" />. **All three combined means match**, and the same ratio gives the same result for any value of <InlineMath math="b" />.

# Checking the other options

The option that it can't be determined comes from thinking the group sizes must be known, but **the ratio is already locked** by the combined means. The option <InlineMath math="104" /> simply averages the three means, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{50+80+x}{3}=78" />. The option <InlineMath math="92" /> treats groups B and C as equal in size, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{80+x}{2}=86" />. The option <InlineMath math="96" /> treats group C as the same size as A and B together, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{60+x}{2}=78" />.

So the mean exam score of group C is <InlineMath math="87" />.

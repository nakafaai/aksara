We know:
-   The actual area is <InlineMath math="810 \text{ m}^2" />.
-   The actual width is <InlineMath math="18 \text{ m}" />.
-   A length of <InlineMath math="3 \text{ cm}" /> in the drawing represents <InlineMath math="90 \text{ m}" /> in reality.

#### Finding the actual length

Using the rectangle area formula <InlineMath math="A = p \times \ell" /> gives

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="810 = p \times 18" />
  <BlockMath math="p = \frac{810}{18}" />
  <BlockMath math="p = 45 \text{ m}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Applying the scale

Let <InlineMath math="d" /> be the length in the drawing. The units in the scale are different, so we keep them visible in the proportion:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{d}{45 \text{ m}} = \frac{3 \text{ cm}}{90 \text{ m}}" />
  <BlockMath math="d = \frac{3 \text{ cm}}{90 \text{ m}} \times 45 \text{ m}" />
  <BlockMath math="d = 1.5 \text{ cm}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the rectangle is <InlineMath math="1.5 \text{ cm}" /> long in the drawing.

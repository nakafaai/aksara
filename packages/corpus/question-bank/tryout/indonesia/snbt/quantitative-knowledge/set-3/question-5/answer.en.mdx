Both <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> get smaller as <InlineMath math="n" /> grows, and for <InlineMath math="n=1" /> they're very close. So a few test values won't settle it: you need **a comparison that holds for every <InlineMath math="n" />**. The plan is to rewrite <InlineMath math="P" /> as a fraction with numerator <InlineMath math="1" /> and then compare the denominators.

# Rationalizing the first quantity

Multiply the numerator and the denominator of <InlineMath math="P" /> by its conjugate, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P&=\frac{\left(\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt{n}\right)\left(\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}\right)}{\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}}\\
&=\frac{(n+1)-n}{\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}}
\end{aligned}" />

Now <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> both have numerator <InlineMath math="1" /> and a positive denominator. For fractions like that, **the smaller denominator gives the larger value**. So you only need to compare <InlineMath math="\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}" /> with <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4n+2}" />.

# Comparing the two denominators

Both denominators are positive, so they're in the same order as their squares. The square of the denominator of <InlineMath math="P" /> contains <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{n^2+n}" />, and that term is **always less than <InlineMath math="2n+1" />** because its square is smaller.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\left(2\sqrt{n^2+n}\right)^2=4n^2+4n&<4n^2+4n+1=(2n+1)^2\\
\left(\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}\right)^2=2n+1+2\sqrt{n^2+n}&<2n+1+(2n+1)=4n+2
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}<\sqrt{4n+2}" />. The denominator of <InlineMath math="P" /> is smaller, so <InlineMath math="P>Q" /> for every positive integer <InlineMath math="n" />.

# Checking with an example

For <InlineMath math="n=1" />, <InlineMath math="P=\sqrt{2}-1\approx 0.414" /> and <InlineMath math="Q=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\approx 0.408" />. Indeed <InlineMath math="P>Q" />, but the gap is only about <InlineMath math="0.006" />, so **a rough estimate can easily mislead you**, and the algebra above is what decides it.

# Checking the other options

The option <InlineMath math="Q>P" /> comes from **treating <InlineMath math="Q" /> as the upper bound** <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}" />, the well-known bound for <InlineMath math="\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt{n}" />, but <InlineMath math="Q=\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{2}}}" /> is smaller than that bound. The option <InlineMath math="P=Q" /> comes from treating <InlineMath math="\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}" /> as <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{n+\tfrac{1}{2}}" />, as if the root of an average were the average of the roots. The option that the information isn't enough comes from thinking the order might change for large <InlineMath math="n" />, but the inequality above holds for every <InlineMath math="n" />.

So for every positive integer <InlineMath math="n" />, the quantity <InlineMath math="P" /> is greater than <InlineMath math="Q" />.

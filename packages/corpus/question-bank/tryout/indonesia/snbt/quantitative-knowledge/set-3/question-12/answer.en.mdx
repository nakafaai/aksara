The piece that comes off is a pyramid with its apex at a corner of the block. **Its three edges from that corner are mutually perpendicular**, with lengths 4 cm, 6 cm, and 8 cm, the edges of the block.

# The volume of the removed pyramid

Take as base the right triangle with legs 4 cm and 6 cm. The height of the pyramid over that base is the 8 cm edge, because that edge is perpendicular to the base plane.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{pyramid}}&=\tfrac13\cdot\left(\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot6\right)\cdot8\\
&=\tfrac13\cdot12\cdot8=32
\end{aligned}" />

This is <InlineMath math="\tfrac16" /> of the volume of the block, because **the base is half a face of the block and the pyramid formula adds a factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" />**.

# The remaining volume

The remaining volume is **the volume of the block minus the pyramid**.

<BlockMath math="V=4\cdot6\cdot8-32=192-32=160\text{ cm}^3" />

The value 128 forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> in the triangular base and so subtracts 64. The value 96 forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> and so subtracts half the block. The values 176 and 184 subtract too little, as if the pyramid were only <InlineMath math="\tfrac1{12}" /> or <InlineMath math="\tfrac1{24}" /> of the block.

So the remaining volume is **<InlineMath math="160\text{ cm}^3" />**.

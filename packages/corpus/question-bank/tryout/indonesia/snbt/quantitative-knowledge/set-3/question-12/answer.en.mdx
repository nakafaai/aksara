Pouring does not change the volume of the water. So **the volume of the cone equals the volume of water in the cylinder**, and the water height in the cylinder is the only unknown.

# The volume of water

<BlockMath math="V=\tfrac13\pi r^2t=\tfrac13\pi(6)^2(10)=120\pi\text{ cm}^3" />

# The water height in the cylinder

The water in the cylinder forms a smaller cylinder of radius 4 cm and height <InlineMath math="h" />. Set the volumes equal, then **divide by the base area of the cylinder**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\pi(4)^2h&=120\pi\\
16h&=120\\
h&=7.5
\end{aligned}" />

The value 22.5 forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> in the cone volume. The value 15 scales the height by the radius ratio <InlineMath math="\tfrac64" /> and also forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" />. The value 5 scales by the radius rather than its square, as in <InlineMath math="\tfrac13\cdot10\cdot\tfrac64" />. The value 2.5 divides the correct result by 3 once more.

So the water in the cylinder stands **<InlineMath math="7.5\text{ cm}" />** high.

We know that <InlineMath math="\angle BOC = 50^\circ" />.
Because <InlineMath math="AB" /> is a diameter, the points <InlineMath math="A" />, <InlineMath math="O" />, and <InlineMath math="B" /> are collinear. Therefore, <InlineMath math="\angle AOB" /> is a straight angle of <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.

We first find <InlineMath math="\angle AOC" />:

Write <InlineMath math="\alpha=\angle AOC" /> and <InlineMath math="\beta=\angle OAC" /> for the angles in the calculation.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\alpha + 50^\circ = 180^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="\alpha = 180^\circ - 50^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="\alpha = 130^\circ" />
</MathContainer>

Consider triangle <InlineMath math="\triangle AOC" />.
Since <InlineMath math="O" /> is the center of the circle, <InlineMath math="OA" /> and <InlineMath math="OC" /> are radii of the circle.
Therefore, <InlineMath math="OA = OC" />, so triangle <InlineMath math="\triangle AOC" /> is an isosceles triangle.

In an isosceles triangle <InlineMath math="\triangle AOC" />, the base angles are equal, i.e., <InlineMath math="\angle OAC = \angle OCA" />.

The sum of angles in a triangle is <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\beta + \beta + \alpha = 180^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="2(\beta) + 130^\circ = 180^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="2(\beta) = 180^\circ - 130^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="2(\beta) = 50^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="\beta = \frac{50^\circ}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="\beta = 25^\circ" />
</MathContainer>

So, the measure of <InlineMath math="\angle OAC" /> is <InlineMath math="25^\circ" />.

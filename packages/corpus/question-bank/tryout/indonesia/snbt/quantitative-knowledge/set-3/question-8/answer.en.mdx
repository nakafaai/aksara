A statement is sufficient when it **forces one answer**, either definitely odd or definitely even. Both statements are about divisibility by <InlineMath math="4" />, so check <InlineMath math="n" /> by its remainder when divided by <InlineMath math="4" />.

# Why four remainders are enough

If you replace <InlineMath math="n" /> with <InlineMath math="n+4" />, both expressions change by a multiple of <InlineMath math="4" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(n+4)^2+3(n+4)&=\left(n^2+3n\right)+4(2n+7)\\
(n+4)^2+(n+4)+2&=\left(n^2+n+2\right)+4(2n+5)
\end{aligned}" />

So **whether each expression is divisible by <InlineMath math="4" /> depends only on the remainder of <InlineMath math="n" />** when divided by <InlineMath math="4" />. It's enough to try <InlineMath math="n=0" />, <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, and <InlineMath math="3" />.

# Statement one alone

Statement (1) holds for remainder <InlineMath math="0" /> and remainder <InlineMath math="1" />, so **<InlineMath math="n" /> can be even or odd**. For <InlineMath math="n=4" />, the value <InlineMath math="16+12=28" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="n" /> is even. For <InlineMath math="n=1" />, the value <InlineMath math="1+3=4" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="n" /> is odd. Statement (1) alone isn't sufficient.

# Statement two alone

Statement (2) holds for remainder <InlineMath math="1" /> and remainder <InlineMath math="2" />, so **both answers are still possible**. For <InlineMath math="n=2" />, the value <InlineMath math="4+2+2=8" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="n" /> is even. For <InlineMath math="n=1" />, the value <InlineMath math="1+1+2=4" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="n" /> is odd. Statement (2) alone isn't sufficient.

# Both statements together

The only remainder that satisfies both is <InlineMath math="1" />, so <InlineMath math="n=4m+1" /> for some integer <InlineMath math="m" />, and **<InlineMath math="n" /> must be odd**. For example, with <InlineMath math="n=5" /> the values <InlineMath math="25+15=40" /> and <InlineMath math="25+5+2=32" /> are both divisible by <InlineMath math="4" />, and <InlineMath math="5" /> is indeed odd.

The choice "statement (1) alone is sufficient" usually comes from thinking <InlineMath math="n(n+3)" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="4" /> only for even <InlineMath math="n" />, but <InlineMath math="n=1" /> works too. The choice "neither statement is sufficient" comes from not checking where the two sets of remainders overlap.

So the two statements together are sufficient to show that <InlineMath math="n" /> is odd, but neither statement alone is.

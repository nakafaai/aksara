Because every denominator is positive, we can compare each pair exactly by cross-multiplying. This avoids rounding repeating decimals.

**First comparison:** <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{7} > \frac{1}{3}" />

<BlockMath math="3 \times 3 = 9 > 7 = 1 \times 7" />

The first statement is true.

**Second comparison:** <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{5} > \frac{2}{9}" />

<BlockMath math="1 \times 9 = 9 < 10 = 2 \times 5" />

The second statement is false.

**Third comparison:** <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{3} > \frac{7}{8}" />

<BlockMath math="2 \times 8 = 16 < 21 = 7 \times 3" />

The third statement is false.

**Fourth comparison:** <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{5} > \frac{5}{6}" />

<BlockMath math="3 \times 6 = 18 < 25 = 5 \times 5" />

The fourth statement is false.

**Fifth comparison:** <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{9} > \frac{3}{4}" />

<BlockMath math="5 \times 4 = 20 < 27 = 3 \times 9" />

The fifth statement is false. Therefore, the only true statement is <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{7} > \frac{1}{3}" />.

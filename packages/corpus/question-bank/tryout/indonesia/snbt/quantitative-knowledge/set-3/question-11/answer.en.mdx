The graph of <InlineMath math="f" /> is a downward parabola with its vertex at <InlineMath math="x=a" />. The maximum on the interval sits at the vertex **only if the vertex is inside the interval**. Otherwise, the maximum sits at the end of the interval closest to the vertex. So work through three cases based on where <InlineMath math="a" /> is.

# Values at the ends and at the vertex

Compute the three values that could be the maximum.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f(-1)&=-1-2a+1=-2a\\
f(2)&=-4+4a+1=4a-3\\
f(a)&=-a^2+2a^2+1=a^2+1
\end{aligned}" />

A downward parabola rises to the left of its vertex and falls to the right of it. That's **why the maximum moves to an end** when the vertex is outside the interval.

# Three cases for the vertex

Solve each case, then **check whether the result fits that case's own condition**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a\le -1&:\quad -2a=5 \implies a=-\tfrac{5}{2}\ \text{(fits)}\\
-1<a<2&:\quad a^2+1=5 \implies a=2\ \text{or}\ a=-2\ \text{(both outside the case)}\\
a\ge 2&:\quad 4a-3=5 \implies a=2\ \text{(fits)}
\end{aligned}" />

In the first case, the vertex is left of the interval, so <InlineMath math="f" /> falls across the whole interval and the maximum is <InlineMath math="f(-1)" />. In the third case, the vertex is to the right or exactly at <InlineMath math="x=2" />, so the maximum is <InlineMath math="f(2)" />. The only possible values are <InlineMath math="a=-\tfrac{5}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="a=2" />, and their sum is <InlineMath math="-\tfrac{5}{2}+2=-\tfrac{1}{2}" />.

# Checking each parameter value

For <InlineMath math="a=-\tfrac{5}{2}" />: <InlineMath math="f(-1)=5" /> and <InlineMath math="f(2)=-13" />, with the vertex outside the interval, so the maximum is <InlineMath math="5" />. For <InlineMath math="a=2" />: <InlineMath math="f(2)=5" /> and <InlineMath math="f(-1)=-4" />, so the maximum is <InlineMath math="5" />. For <InlineMath math="a=-2" />, the vertex is at <InlineMath math="x=-2" />, outside the interval, so **the maximum on the interval is only <InlineMath math="f(-1)=4" />**, not <InlineMath math="5" />. The value <InlineMath math="a=-2" /> really has to go.

# Checking the other options

The option <InlineMath math="0" /> uses only the vertex formula <InlineMath math="a^2+1=5" /> and **accepts both <InlineMath math="a=2" /> and <InlineMath math="a=-2" /> without checking the interval**. The option <InlineMath math="-\tfrac{5}{2}" /> works all three cases but still adds <InlineMath math="a=-2" />, giving <InlineMath math="-\tfrac{5}{2}-2+2" />. The option <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{2}" /> counts <InlineMath math="a=2" /> twice, once from the vertex case and once from the right-end case. The option <InlineMath math="2" /> misses the case with the vertex left of the interval.

So the sum of all possible values of <InlineMath math="a" /> is <InlineMath math="-\tfrac{1}{2}" />.

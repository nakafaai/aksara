The number <InlineMath math="3+2\sqrt2" /> has the conjugate <InlineMath math="3-2\sqrt2" />, and their product is 1. This fact **determines <InlineMath math="\frac1x" />**, which three of the statements need.

<BlockMath math="\left(3+2\sqrt2\right)\left(3-2\sqrt2\right)=9-8=1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac1x=3-2\sqrt2" />

The fourth statement is false because the sign in front of <InlineMath math="2\sqrt2" /> must be negative.

# The square root and the sum with the reciprocal

Square <InlineMath math="1+\sqrt2" /> to test the first statement.

<BlockMath math="\left(1+\sqrt2\right)^2=1+2\sqrt2+2=3+2\sqrt2" />

Since <InlineMath math="1+\sqrt2" /> is positive and its square equals <InlineMath math="x" />, <InlineMath math="\sqrt x=1+\sqrt2" /> is true. The sum of <InlineMath math="x" /> and its reciprocal is also quick, because **the root terms cancel**.

<BlockMath math="x+\frac1x=\left(3+2\sqrt2\right)+\left(3-2\sqrt2\right)=6" />

The second statement is true.

# The sum of the squared reciprocals

Square <InlineMath math="x+\frac1x" /> and subtract the middle term. That middle term is <InlineMath math="2\cdot x\cdot\frac1x=2" />, **not zero**.

<BlockMath math="x^2+\frac1{x^2}=\left(x+\frac1x\right)^2-2=36-2=34" />

The third statement is false. The number 36 appears when <InlineMath math="\left(x+\frac1x\right)^2" /> is taken to equal <InlineMath math="x^2+\frac1{x^2}" />.

So the first statement is **true**, the second is **true**, the third is **false**, and the fourth is **false**.

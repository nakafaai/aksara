Let the largest number be <InlineMath math="L" /> and the smallest <InlineMath math="K" />. The change in the mean gives **the sum of these two numbers**, and their difference is already known.

# The sum of the largest and smallest

The ten numbers add up to <InlineMath math="10\times20=200" />. The remaining eight add up to <InlineMath math="8\times19=152" />. **The difference of the two totals** is the sum of the two numbers removed.

<BlockMath math="L+K=200-152=48" />

# Solving the system

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L+K&=48\\
L-K&=30\\
2L&=78\quad\Rightarrow\quad L=39,\ K=9
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="39-9=30" />, and the remaining eight numbers add up to 152 with mean 19, which sits sensibly between 9 and 39.

The value 48 is the sum <InlineMath math="L+K" /> before it is split. The value 30 is the difference, and the value 9 is the smallest number. The value 35 computes the total of the eight numbers with mean 20, which gives <InlineMath math="L+K=40" />.

So the largest number is **39**.

The double layer is where the folded flap lies on top of the rest of the paper. The plan: find the crease, find the flap, see where the flap lands after folding, and **keep only the part that still lies on the paper**.

# The equation of the crease

Every point on the crease stays still when you fold, so its distance to <InlineMath math="A" /> equals its distance to <InlineMath math="M" />. Set up coordinates <InlineMath math="A(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="B(16,0)" />, <InlineMath math="C(16,6)" />, and <InlineMath math="D(0,6)" />, so <InlineMath math="M(8,6)" />. The crease is **the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AM" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2+y^2&=(x-8)^2+(y-6)^2\\
0&=-16x+64-12y+36\\
4x+3y&=25
\end{aligned}" />

This line meets side <InlineMath math="AB" /> at <InlineMath math="P\left(\tfrac{25}{4},0\right)" /> and side <InlineMath math="CD" /> at <InlineMath math="Q\left(\tfrac{7}{4},6\right)" />. On side <InlineMath math="AD" /> it would need <InlineMath math="y=\tfrac{25}{3}>6" />, which is off the paper.

# The flap that gets folded

The flap is **the part on the same side of the crease as <InlineMath math="A" />**, the quadrilateral <InlineMath math="APQD" />. It's a trapezoid with parallel sides <InlineMath math="AP=\tfrac{25}{4}" /> and <InlineMath math="DQ=\tfrac{7}{4}" /> and height <InlineMath math="6" />.

<BlockMath math="\text{area}(APQD)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{25}{4}+\tfrac{7}{4}\right)\cdot 6=24" />

# Where the flap lands

Folding reflects the flap across the crease. The points <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> stay put, <InlineMath math="A" /> lands on <InlineMath math="M" />, and <InlineMath math="D" /> lands on its mirror image <InlineMath math="D'" />.

<BlockMath math="D'=\left(\tfrac{56}{25},\tfrac{192}{25}\right),\qquad \tfrac{192}{25}=7.68>6" />

So **corner <InlineMath math="D'" /> sticks out past side <InlineMath math="CD" />**. The folded flap is the quadrilateral <InlineMath math="MPQD'" />, and the side <InlineMath math="QM" /> lies right on the edge <InlineMath math="CD" />. The part of the flap that still lies on the paper is the triangle <InlineMath math="PQM" />, and the triangle <InlineMath math="QD'M" /> hangs over the edge.

# The double layer area

The triangle <InlineMath math="PQM" /> has its base <InlineMath math="QM" /> on side <InlineMath math="CD" />, and its height is the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to that side.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
QM&=8-\tfrac{7}{4}=\tfrac{25}{4}\\
\text{area}(PQM)&=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{25}{4}\cdot 6=\tfrac{75}{4}
\end{aligned}" />

Check: the overhanging triangle <InlineMath math="QD'M" /> has base <InlineMath math="\tfrac{25}{4}" /> and height <InlineMath math="\tfrac{192}{25}-6=\tfrac{42}{25}" />, so its area is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{21}{4}" />. Then <InlineMath math="\tfrac{75}{4}+\tfrac{21}{4}=24" />, **exactly the whole flap**.

# Checking the other options

The option <InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}^2" /> counts the whole flap and **forgets that a corner sticks out past the edge**. The option <InlineMath math="\tfrac{21}{4}\text{ cm}^2" /> is the overhanging part, not the part on the paper. The option <InlineMath math="\tfrac{75}{2}\text{ cm}^2" /> drops the <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" /> in the triangle area. The option <InlineMath math="\tfrac{15}{2}\text{ cm}^2" /> uses the crease length <InlineMath math="PQ=\tfrac{15}{2}" /> as if it were an area.

So the double layer area is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{75}{4}\text{ cm}^2" />.

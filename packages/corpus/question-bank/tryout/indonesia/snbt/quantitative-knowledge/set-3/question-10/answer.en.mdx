The sample space is every choice of three points from the seven, including choices that **don't form a triangle**. The plan: count the sample space, then sort each choice by where <InlineMath math="O" /> sits relative to its triangle.

# The size of the sample space

The order of choosing doesn't matter, so the number of choices is a combination.

<BlockMath math="\binom{7}{3}=\frac{7\cdot 6\cdot 5}{3\cdot 2\cdot 1}=35" />

These <InlineMath math="35" /> choices are equally likely, so the probability will be **the number of suitable choices divided by <InlineMath math="35" />**.

# Choices that include the centre

There are <InlineMath math="\binom{6}{2}=15" /> choices containing <InlineMath math="O" /> and two vertices. Three of them are collinear, namely <InlineMath math="O" /> with an opposite pair <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="D" />, <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="E" />, or <InlineMath math="C" /> and <InlineMath math="F" />, so they don't form a triangle. The other <InlineMath math="12" /> are triangles that **make <InlineMath math="O" /> a vertex**, not a point inside. None of these <InlineMath math="15" /> choices works.

# Choices of three vertices

There are <InlineMath math="\binom{6}{3}=20" /> triangles whose vertices come from the hexagon. Group them by their sides.

| Type of triangle | Count | Where <InlineMath math="O" /> is |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Uses a long diagonal, such as <InlineMath math="ABD" /> | <InlineMath math="3\cdot 4=12" /> | on a side |
| Three neighbouring vertices, such as <InlineMath math="ABC" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> | outside |
| Equilateral, namely <InlineMath math="ACE" /> and <InlineMath math="BDF" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | inside |

A long diagonal passes through <InlineMath math="O" />, so a triangle that uses that diagonal as a side has <InlineMath math="O" /> right on its side. Three neighbouring vertices lie on one side of the line joining the two outer ones, and that line misses <InlineMath math="O" />, so <InlineMath math="O" /> is outside. **Only the equilateral triangles <InlineMath math="ACE" /> and <InlineMath math="BDF" />** contain <InlineMath math="O" /> inside, because the centre of each of them is exactly <InlineMath math="O" />.

# The probability and a check

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{2}{35}" />

Check that every choice is counted: <InlineMath math="3" /> collinear choices, <InlineMath math="12" /> with <InlineMath math="O" /> as a vertex, <InlineMath math="12" /> with <InlineMath math="O" /> on a side, <InlineMath math="6" /> with <InlineMath math="O" /> outside, and <InlineMath math="2" /> with <InlineMath math="O" /> inside. The total is <InlineMath math="3+12+12+6+2=35" />, **the same as the sample space**.

# Checking the other options

The option <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{10}=\tfrac{2}{20}" /> uses only the <InlineMath math="20" /> vertex triangles and **forgets that <InlineMath math="O" /> can be chosen too**. The option <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{16}=\tfrac{2}{32}" /> removes the <InlineMath math="3" /> collinear choices from the sample space, even though those choices can still happen. The option <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2}{5}=\tfrac{14}{35}" /> also counts the <InlineMath math="12" /> triangles with <InlineMath math="O" /> on a side. The option <InlineMath math="\tfrac{26}{35}" /> counts every triangle that touches <InlineMath math="O" />, that is <InlineMath math="2+12+12" />.

So the probability that the triangle contains <InlineMath math="O" /> in its interior is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2}{35}" />.

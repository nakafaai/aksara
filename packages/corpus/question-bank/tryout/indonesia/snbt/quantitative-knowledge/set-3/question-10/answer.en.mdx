Quadrilateral <InlineMath math="DBCE" /> is triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> minus triangle <InlineMath math="ADE" />. So you only need the area of <InlineMath math="ADE" />, which **shares angle <InlineMath math="A" />** with triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />.

# Area ratio with a shared angle

The area of a triangle can be written as <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot\text{side}\cdot\text{side}\cdot\sin A" />. Since angle <InlineMath math="A" /> is the same, the area ratio is **the product of the ratios of the two sides that enclose that angle**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{[ADE]}{[ABC]}=\frac{\tfrac12\,AD\cdot AE\sin A}{\tfrac12\,AB\cdot AC\sin A}=\frac{AD}{AB}\cdot\frac{AE}{AC}" />

From <InlineMath math="AD:DB=1:2" /> we get <InlineMath math="\frac{AD}{AB}=\frac13" />, and from <InlineMath math="AE:EC=3:1" /> we get <InlineMath math="\frac{AE}{AC}=\frac34" />.

<BlockMath math="[ADE]=\frac13\cdot\frac34\cdot48=12" />

# Computing the quadrilateral

The quadrilateral is **what remains of the triangle after <InlineMath math="ADE" /> is removed**.

<BlockMath math="[DBCE]=48-12=36\text{ cm}^2" />

The value 12 is the area of triangle <InlineMath math="ADE" />, not yet subtracted from 48. The value 30 reads <InlineMath math="AD:DB=1:2" /> as <InlineMath math="\frac{AD}{AB}=\frac12" />. The value 44 uses <InlineMath math="\frac{AE}{AC}=\frac14" />, which is the part <InlineMath math="EC" />, not <InlineMath math="AE" />. The value 24 takes only half the area of the triangle.

So the area of quadrilateral <InlineMath math="DBCE" /> is **<InlineMath math="36\text{ cm}^2" />**.

The question asks for **a single value of <InlineMath math="x+y" />**. A statement is enough only if every pair <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> that satisfies it gives the same sum. A useful tool is the identity <InlineMath math="(x+y)^2=(x-y)^2+4xy" />, but a square root always leaves two signs.

# Statement one alone

With <InlineMath math="x>0" /> and <InlineMath math="xy=-12" />, the value of <InlineMath math="x" /> is a positive divisor of <InlineMath math="12" /> and <InlineMath math="y=-\tfrac{12}{x}" />. The pair <InlineMath math="(3,-4)" /> gives <InlineMath math="x+y=-1" />, while the pair <InlineMath math="(12,-1)" /> gives <InlineMath math="x+y=11" />. **There's more than one value**, so statement (1) alone isn't enough.

# Statement two alone

From <InlineMath math="(x-y)^2=49" />, the difference is <InlineMath math="x-y=7" /> or <InlineMath math="x-y=-7" />, with infinitely many pairs. The pair <InlineMath math="(8,1)" /> gives <InlineMath math="x+y=9" />, while the pair <InlineMath math="(9,2)" /> gives <InlineMath math="x+y=11" />. **The value isn't unique**, so statement (2) alone isn't enough.

# Both statements together

Put both statements into the identity.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x+y)^2&=(x-y)^2+4xy=49+4(-12)=1\\
x+y&=1\ \text{or}\ x+y=-1
\end{aligned}" />

Now check whether both signs really happen with <InlineMath math="x>0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x-y=7&:\quad x(x-7)=-12\ \Rightarrow\ x^2-7x+12=0\ \Rightarrow\ x=3\ \text{or}\ x=4\\
x-y=-7&:\quad x(x+7)=-12\ \Rightarrow\ x^2+7x+12=0\ \Rightarrow\ x=-3\ \text{or}\ x=-4
\end{aligned}" />

The second case drops out because <InlineMath math="x>0" />. The first case gives the pair <InlineMath math="(3,-4)" /> with <InlineMath math="x+y=-1" /> and the pair <InlineMath math="(4,-3)" /> with <InlineMath math="x+y=1" />. So **the condition <InlineMath math="x>0" /> removes one case but doesn't fix the sign of <InlineMath math="x+y" />**. There are still two values.

So **statement (1) and statement (2) aren't enough** to answer the question, even together.

The points <InlineMath math="D" /> and <InlineMath math="H" /> both lie on <InlineMath math="AB" />, so <InlineMath math="DH" /> is **the difference of their distances** from <InlineMath math="A" />. The plan: find all three sides from the incircle, then find <InlineMath math="AH" /> from similar triangles, then subtract.

# Tangent lengths

Let the incircle have center <InlineMath math="O" /> and touch <InlineMath math="BC" /> at <InlineMath math="E" /> and <InlineMath math="CA" /> at <InlineMath math="F" />. **The two tangent segments from one point to a circle are equal**, so <InlineMath math="AF=AD=6" /> and <InlineMath math="BE=BD" />. Let <InlineMath math="BD=y" />.

The angle at <InlineMath math="C" /> is a right angle, and the radii <InlineMath math="OE" /> and <InlineMath math="OF" /> are perpendicular to the sides they touch. So <InlineMath math="CEOF" /> is a square with side <InlineMath math="3" />, which gives <InlineMath math="CE=CF=3" />. That makes <InlineMath math="AC=6+3=9" />, <InlineMath math="BC=y+3" />, and <InlineMath math="AB=6+y" />.

# The sides from Pythagoras

The triangle has its right angle at <InlineMath math="C" />, so <InlineMath math="AB" /> is **the longest side, the hypotenuse**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
9^2+(y+3)^2&=(y+6)^2\\
81+y^2+6y+9&=y^2+12y+36\\
54&=6y\\
y&=9
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="BC=12" /> and <InlineMath math="AB=15" />. Check the radius with the right-triangle formula <InlineMath math="r=\tfrac{AC+BC-AB}{2}=\tfrac{9+12-15}{2}=3" />, which matches the problem.

# Where the foot of the altitude lies

Triangles <InlineMath math="ACH" /> and <InlineMath math="ABC" /> are similar, because both have a right angle and they share the angle at <InlineMath math="A" />. Matching sides give <InlineMath math="\tfrac{AH}{AC}=\tfrac{AC}{AB}" />, so **<InlineMath math="AH" /> comes from the square of the side <InlineMath math="AC" />**, not from the height <InlineMath math="CH" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AH&=\frac{AC^2}{AB}=\frac{81}{15}=\frac{27}{5}\\
DH&=AD-AH=6-\frac{27}{5}=\frac35
\end{aligned}" />

This subtraction is right because <InlineMath math="\tfrac{27}{5}<6" />, so <InlineMath math="H" /> lies between <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="D" />. Check from the side of <InlineMath math="B" />: <InlineMath math="BH=\tfrac{BC^2}{AB}=\tfrac{144}{15}=\tfrac{48}{5}" /> and <InlineMath math="BD=9" />, so <InlineMath math="DH=\tfrac{48}{5}-9=\tfrac35" />. Same result.

Drawing the triangle at its true size shows the order of the points. Going from <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="B" />, the foot of the altitude <InlineMath math="H" /> comes first and then the touch point <InlineMath math="D" />, so **<InlineMath math="DH" /> is the short piece between them**.

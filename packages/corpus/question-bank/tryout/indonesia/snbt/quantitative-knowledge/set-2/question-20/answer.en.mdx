Use an antiderivative for each integral. A definite integral equals the antiderivative at the upper limit minus its value at the lower limit.

#### Evaluating the first integral

An antiderivative of <InlineMath math="3x+5" /> is:

<BlockMath math="F(x)=\frac32x^2+5x" />

Its values at the two limits are:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
F(3)&=\frac{27}{2}+15 \\
&=\frac{57}{2} \\
F(1)&=\frac32+5 \\
&=\frac{13}{2}
\end{aligned}" />

Therefore:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
I_1&=F(3)-F(1) \\
&=\frac{57}{2}-\frac{13}{2} \\
&=\frac{44}{2}=22
\end{aligned}" />

#### Evaluating the second integral

An antiderivative of <InlineMath math="2x-3" /> is:

<BlockMath math="G(x)=x^2-3x" />

With lower limit <InlineMath math="2" />, this gives:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="G(2)=4-6=-2" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
I_2&=G(b)-G(2) \\
&=b^2-3b-(-2) \\
&=b^2-3b+2
\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Finding the upper limit

The two integrals add to <InlineMath math="28" />, so:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
I_2&=28-I_1 \\
&=28-22=6
\end{aligned}" />

Substitute the expression found for <InlineMath math="I_2" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
b^2-3b+2&=6 \\
b^2-3b-4&=0 \\
(b-4)(b+1)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Thus, <InlineMath math="b=4" /> or <InlineMath math="b=-1" />. The condition <InlineMath math="b>0" /> excludes the negative value.

At <InlineMath math="b=4" />, the second integral is <InlineMath math="16-12+2=6" />. Adding the first gives <InlineMath math="22+6=28" />, as required. Therefore, the answer is <InlineMath math="b=4" />.

# Finding the inverse function

Write <InlineMath math="y=f(x)" /> and express <InlineMath math="x" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="y" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y(x-2)&=2x+1\\
xy-2y&=2x+1\\
x(y-2)&=2y+1\\
x&=\frac{2y+1}{y-2}
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{2x+1}{x-2}" />, **exactly the same as <InlineMath math="f(x)" />**, and its domain is also <InlineMath math="x\ne2" />. The third statement is true.

# What a self-inverse function implies

Because <InlineMath math="f^{-1}=f" />, applying <InlineMath math="f" /> twice **returns the input**. A direct check: <InlineMath math="f(5)=\frac{11}{3}" /> and <InlineMath math="f\left(\frac{11}{3}\right)=\frac{25/3}{5/3}=5" />. The fourth statement is true.

The equation <InlineMath math="f(x)=2" /> means <InlineMath math="2x+1=2(x-2)=2x-4" />, which gives <InlineMath math="1=-4" />. No <InlineMath math="x" /> works, because 2 is the horizontal asymptote of the graph of <InlineMath math="f" />. The first statement is false.

So the first statement is **false**, and the second, third, and fourth statements are **true**.

Count all triangles, then **subtract the triangles that use a side of the octagon**. Split those by how many octagon sides they use, so that none is counted twice.

# All triangles

**No three vertices** of a regular octagon **are collinear**, so every choice of three vertices forms a triangle.

<BlockMath math="\binom83=\frac{8\cdot7\cdot6}{3\cdot2\cdot1}=56" />

# Triangles that use an octagon side

A triangle with **two octagon sides** is formed by three consecutive vertices. There are 8 choices for the middle vertex, so there are 8 such triangles.

A triangle with **exactly one octagon side** is formed by one side of the octagon and a third vertex that is not next to either end of that side. For each side, its two ends and their two other neighbors are ruled out, which leaves <InlineMath math="8-4=4" /> vertices.

<BlockMath math="8\times4=32" />

The number of triangles with no octagon side is

<BlockMath math="56-8-32=16" />

The value 56 counts all triangles. The value 48 removes only the triangles with two octagon sides. The value 40 counts the triangles with at least one octagon side, the opposite of what is asked. The value 32 counts the triangles with exactly one octagon side.

So **16** such triangles can be formed.

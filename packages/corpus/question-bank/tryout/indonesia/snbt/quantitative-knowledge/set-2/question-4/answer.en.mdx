Both equations are symmetric in <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, so **let <InlineMath math="s=x+y" /> and <InlineMath math="p=xy" />**. The second equation factors as <InlineMath math="xy(x+y)" />.

<BlockMath math="s+p=11,\qquad sp=30" />

# Finding s and p

The numbers <InlineMath math="s" /> and <InlineMath math="p" /> add up to 11 and multiply to 30, so they are the roots of <InlineMath math="t^2-11t+30=0" />, namely 5 and 6. There are **two possibilities**: <InlineMath math="(s,p)=(5,6)" /> or <InlineMath math="(s,p)=(6,5)" />.

Each possibility must give real values of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />. They are the roots of <InlineMath math="t^2-st+p=0" />.

| <InlineMath math="(s,p)" /> | Equation | <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> | <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2=s^2-2p" /> |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="(5,6)" /> | <InlineMath math="t^2-5t+6=0" /> | 2 and 3 | <InlineMath math="25-12=13" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(6,5)" /> | <InlineMath math="t^2-6t+5=0" /> | 1 and 5 | <InlineMath math="36-10=26" /> |

# Choosing the largest value

Both pairs are real and satisfy the system. Check the second pair: <InlineMath math="1+5+5=11" /> and <InlineMath math="1^2(5)+1(5^2)=30" />. The **largest** value is 26.

The value 13 uses only the first possibility. The values 25 and 36 are <InlineMath math="s^2" />, which forgets to subtract <InlineMath math="2p" />. The value 31 subtracts <InlineMath math="p" /> instead of <InlineMath math="2p" />.

So the largest possible value of <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2" /> is **26**.

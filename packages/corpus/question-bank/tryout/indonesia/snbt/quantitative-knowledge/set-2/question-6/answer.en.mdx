Form four-digit numbers from <InlineMath math="1,2,3,4,5,6" /> with **no repeated digits**. Only the units digit determines whether a number is odd, so choose that digit first.

#### Counting Choices for Each Position

The units position requires an **odd digit**, namely <InlineMath math="1,3,5" />. Once a digit has been used, it cannot be selected again. None of the available digits is zero, so the thousands position has no additional restriction.

| Selection order | Position | Choices | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Units | <InlineMath math="3" /> | Choose 1, 3, or 5 |
| 2 | Thousands | <InlineMath math="6-1=5" /> | One digit has been used |
| 3 | Hundreds | <InlineMath math="6-2=4" /> | Two digits have been used |
| 4 | Tens | <InlineMath math="6-3=3" /> | Three digits have been used |

#### Multiplying the Choices

Use the **multiplication rule** because every choice at one stage can be followed by every remaining choice at the next stage. Choosing the units digit first does not change its position in the final number.

<BlockMath math="N=3\times5\times4\times3=180" />

For example, after choosing 5 for the units, choose 1 for the thousands, 2 for the hundreds, and 3 for the tens. The result is <InlineMath math="1235" />, an odd number with four distinct digits. Therefore, <InlineMath math="180" /> numbers satisfy the conditions.

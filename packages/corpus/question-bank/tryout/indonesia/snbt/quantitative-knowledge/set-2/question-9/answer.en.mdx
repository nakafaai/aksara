The base <InlineMath math="AB=4+9=13\text{ cm}" /> is known, so what you need is **the height <InlineMath math="CD" />** to that base.

# Two similar small triangles

The altitude from the right angle splits triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> into triangles <InlineMath math="ADC" /> and <InlineMath math="CDB" />. Angle <InlineMath math="ACD" /> and angle <InlineMath math="CBD" /> both complete angle <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />, so the two small triangles are **similar**. Corresponding sides are proportional.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{AD}{CD}&=\frac{CD}{DB}\\
CD^2&=AD\cdot DB=4\cdot9=36\\
CD&=6
\end{aligned}" />

The height <InlineMath math="CD" /> is the **geometric mean** of 4 and 9, not their arithmetic mean.

# Computing the area

<BlockMath math="A=\tfrac12\cdot AB\cdot CD=\tfrac12\cdot13\cdot6=39\text{ cm}^2" />

Check with the legs, because **two ways of computing the area must agree**: <InlineMath math="AC=\sqrt{4^2+6^2}=2\sqrt{13}" /> and <InlineMath math="BC=\sqrt{9^2+6^2}=3\sqrt{13}" />, so <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot2\sqrt{13}\cdot3\sqrt{13}=39" />.

The value 42.25 uses the arithmetic mean <InlineMath math="6.5" /> as the height. The values 26 and 58.5 use <InlineMath math="AD" /> or <InlineMath math="DB" /> as the height. The value 78 forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />.

So the area of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is **<InlineMath math="39\text{ cm}^2" />**.

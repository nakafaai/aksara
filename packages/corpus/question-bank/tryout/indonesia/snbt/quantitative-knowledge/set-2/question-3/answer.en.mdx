Both quantities depend on <InlineMath math="x" />, and <InlineMath math="x" /> can be any number between 0 and 1. Compare them through **the sign of their difference**.

# Simplifying the difference

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P-Q&=\frac{x}{1+x}-x^2\\
&=\frac{x-x^2(1+x)}{1+x}\\
&=\frac{x\left(1-x-x^2\right)}{1+x}
\end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="0<x<1" />, the factor <InlineMath math="x" /> and the denominator <InlineMath math="1+x" /> are positive. So the sign of <InlineMath math="P-Q" /> **is the sign of <InlineMath math="1-x-x^2" />**.

# Testing two values

The expression <InlineMath math="1-x-x^2" /> is zero at <InlineMath math="x=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\approx0.618" />, positive to the left of that point, and negative to the right. Two examples show that **the relation changes**.

| <InlineMath math="x" /> | <InlineMath math="P=\frac{x}{1+x}" /> | <InlineMath math="Q=x^2" /> | Relation |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> | <InlineMath math="\tfrac13\approx0.33" /> | <InlineMath math="\tfrac14=0.25" /> | <InlineMath math="P>Q" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\tfrac45" /> | <InlineMath math="\tfrac49\approx0.44" /> | <InlineMath math="\tfrac{16}{25}=0.64" /> | <InlineMath math="Q>P" /> |

At <InlineMath math="x\approx0.618" /> the two quantities are even equal. A single value of <InlineMath math="x" /> cannot decide the relation for every <InlineMath math="x" />.

So **the information given is not enough** to decide the relation between <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" />.

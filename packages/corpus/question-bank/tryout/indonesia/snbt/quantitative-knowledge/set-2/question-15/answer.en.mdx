Every statement can be checked directly from the coordinates. Side <InlineMath math="AB" /> lies on the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, so **the height of the triangle to <InlineMath math="AB" /> is the <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate of <InlineMath math="C" />**, which is 4.

# Area and side length

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A&=\tfrac12\cdot AB\cdot h=\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot4=12\\
AC&=\sqrt{(2-0)^2+(4-0)^2}=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5
\end{aligned}" />

The first and third statements are **true**.

# The angle at C

The angle at <InlineMath math="C" /> is a right angle if the vectors <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{CA}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{CB}" /> are perpendicular, that is, if **their dot product is zero**.

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{CA}\cdot\overrightarrow{CB}=(-2,-4)\cdot(4,-4)=-8+16=8\ne0" />

The angle at <InlineMath math="C" /> is not a right angle, so the second statement is false.

# The circumcenter

The circumcenter is where the perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet. The perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AB" /> is the line <InlineMath math="x=3" />. The perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AC" /> passes through the midpoint <InlineMath math="(1,2)" /> and is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="AC" />, whose gradient is 2, so its gradient is <InlineMath math="-\tfrac12" />. Substitute <InlineMath math="x=3" /> into that bisector.

<BlockMath math="y-2=-\tfrac12(3-1)\quad\Rightarrow\quad y=1" />

The circumcenter is <InlineMath math="(3,1)" />. **Check the distances**: <InlineMath math="OA^2=9+1" />, <InlineMath math="OB^2=9+1" />, and <InlineMath math="OC^2=1+9" /> are all 10. The point <InlineMath math="(3,2)" /> is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{13}" /> from <InlineMath math="A" /> but <InlineMath math="\sqrt5" /> from <InlineMath math="C" />, so the fourth statement is false.

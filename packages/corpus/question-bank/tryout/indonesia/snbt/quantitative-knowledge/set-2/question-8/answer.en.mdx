For a three-term arithmetic sequence, the quickest approach is to write the terms **symmetrically around the middle term**: <InlineMath math="u-b" />, <InlineMath math="u" />, and <InlineMath math="u+b" /> with common difference <InlineMath math="b>0" />.

# The middle term from the sum

<BlockMath math="(u-b)+u+(u+b)=3u=18\quad\Rightarrow\quad u=6" />

The original sequence is <InlineMath math="6-b" />, 6, and <InlineMath math="6+b" />. After the changes, **the new sequence** is <InlineMath math="7-b" />, 6, and <InlineMath math="13+b" />.

# The geometric condition

In a geometric sequence, **the square of the middle term equals the product of the first and third terms**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
6^2&=(7-b)(13+b)\\
36&=91-6b-b^2\\
b^2+6b-55&=0\\
(b+11)(b-5)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Because the difference is positive, <InlineMath math="b=5" />. The original sequence is 1, 6, and 11. Check: the new sequence 2, 6, and 18 is indeed geometric with ratio 3.

<BlockMath math="1\times6\times11=66" />

The value <InlineMath math="-510" /> comes from <InlineMath math="b=-11" />, which gives 17, 6, and <InlineMath math="-5" /> with a negative difference. The value 216 is the product of the new geometric sequence, <InlineMath math="2\times6\times18" />. The value 108 mixes the two sequences, as in <InlineMath math="1\times6\times18" />.

So the product of the three original numbers is **66**.

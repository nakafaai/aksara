You can turn the question into a question about a sign. Write the distance from each data value to the median: <InlineMath math="a=x_3-x_1" />, <InlineMath math="b=x_3-x_2" />, <InlineMath math="c=x_4-x_3" />, and <InlineMath math="d=x_5-x_3" />. All four are non-negative because the data are sorted. **The mean minus the median equals the mean deviation from the median**, so

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\bar x-x_3&=\frac{(x_1-x_3)+(x_2-x_3)+(x_4-x_3)+(x_5-x_3)}{5}\\
&=\frac{(c-b)+(d-a)}{5}
\end{aligned}" />

So the mean is bigger than the median exactly when <InlineMath math="(c-b)+(d-a)>0" />. Statement (1) means <InlineMath math="c=b" />, and statement (2) means <InlineMath math="d>a" />.

# Statement one alone

From <InlineMath math="c=b" />, the difference becomes <InlineMath math="\tfrac{d-a}{5}" />, and **the sign of <InlineMath math="d-a" /> is still unknown**. The data <InlineMath math="2,\ 9,\ 10,\ 11,\ 19" /> satisfy statement (1) with mean <InlineMath math="\tfrac{51}{5}>10" />, so the answer is yes. The data <InlineMath math="1,\ 9,\ 10,\ 11,\ 12" /> also satisfy statement (1), but their mean is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{43}{5}<10" />, so the answer is no. Statement (1) alone isn't enough.

# Statement two alone

From <InlineMath math="d>a" />, the term <InlineMath math="d-a" /> is positive, but <InlineMath math="c-b" /> can be negative and pull the mean down. So **a top end that reaches farther doesn't guarantee a mean above the median**. The data <InlineMath math="2,\ 10,\ 10,\ 10,\ 19" /> satisfy statement (2), since <InlineMath math="19-10=9>8=10-2" />, and their mean is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{51}{5}>10" />. The data <InlineMath math="2,\ 8,\ 10,\ 10,\ 19" /> also satisfy statement (2), but their mean is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{49}{5}<10" />. Statement (2) alone isn't enough.

# Both statements together

Combine <InlineMath math="c=b" /> and <InlineMath math="d>a" />. The term <InlineMath math="c-b" /> **drops out because it's zero**, and the remaining term is positive.

<BlockMath math="\bar x-x_3=\frac{0+(d-a)}{5}>0" />

For every data set that satisfies both statements, the mean is definitely bigger than the median. The answer is always yes.

So **both statements together are enough**, but neither statement alone is enough to answer the question.

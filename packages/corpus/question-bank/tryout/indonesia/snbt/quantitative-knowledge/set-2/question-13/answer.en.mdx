The empty space you're asked about is **the volume of the frustum minus the volume of the ball**. The plan: find the ball's radius from a cross-section of the cone, then find the small cone's volume by similarity, then subtract.

# The radius of the ball

Cut the cone straight through its axis. The cross-section is an isosceles triangle with base <InlineMath math="12" />, height <InlineMath math="8" />, and legs <InlineMath math="\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10" />. The ball's cross-section is **the incircle of that triangle**, so the ball's radius is the triangle's area divided by half its perimeter.

<BlockMath math="r=\frac{\tfrac12\times12\times8}{\tfrac12(12+10+10)}=\frac{48}{16}=3" />

# The small cone above the ball

The top of the ball sits at height <InlineMath math="2r=6" />, so the small cone above it has height <InlineMath math="8-6=2" />. The small cone is similar to the big one with scale factor <InlineMath math="k=\tfrac28=\tfrac14" />, and **volume scales with the cube of the scale factor**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{big}}&=\tfrac13\pi\times6^2\times8=96\pi\\
V_{\text{small}}&=\left(\tfrac14\right)^3\times96\pi=\tfrac{3}{2}\pi
\end{aligned}" />

Check it directly: the small cone's radius is <InlineMath math="\tfrac64=\tfrac32" />, so its volume is <InlineMath math="\tfrac13\pi\times\tfrac94\times2=\tfrac32\pi" />. Same result.

# The empty space in the frustum

The ball lies between heights <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="6" />, so **the whole ball is inside the frustum**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{frustum}}&=96\pi-\tfrac32\pi=\tfrac{189}{2}\pi\\
V_{\text{ball}}&=\tfrac43\pi\times3^3=36\pi\\
V_{\text{empty}}&=\tfrac{189}{2}\pi-36\pi=\tfrac{117}{2}\pi\\
\frac{V_{\text{empty}}}{V_{\text{ball}}}&=\frac{117}{72}=\frac{13}{8}
\end{aligned}" />

# Traps in the other options

- <InlineMath math="1:1" /> uses the length scale <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> for volume, so the small cone wrongly becomes <InlineMath math="24\pi" /> and the empty space <InlineMath math="96\pi-24\pi-36\pi=36\pi" />, the same as the ball.
- <InlineMath math="3:2" /> uses the area scale <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac14\right)^2" /> for volume, so the small cone wrongly becomes <InlineMath math="6\pi" /> and the empty space <InlineMath math="54\pi" />.
- <InlineMath math="5:3" /> uses **the whole cone** instead of just the frustum, so the empty space becomes <InlineMath math="96\pi-36\pi=60\pi" />.
- <InlineMath math="21:8" /> forgets to subtract the ball and compares <InlineMath math="\tfrac{189}{2}\pi" /> with <InlineMath math="36\pi" />.

So the ratio of the empty space in the frustum to the volume of the ball is <InlineMath math="13:8" />.

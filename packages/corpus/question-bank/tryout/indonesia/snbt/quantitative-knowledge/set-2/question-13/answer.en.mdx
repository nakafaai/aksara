The cutting plane separates a small cone at the top from the lower part that contains the base. The volume of the lower part is **the volume of the whole cone minus the volume of the small cone**.

# The similar small cone

The small cone shares the apex of the whole cone and has height <InlineMath math="8-4=4\text{ cm}" />, half the original height. The two cones are **similar with scale factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />**, so the base radius of the small cone is 3 cm.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{whole}}&=\tfrac13\pi(6)^2(8)=96\pi\\
V_{\text{small}}&=\tfrac13\pi(3)^2(4)=12\pi
\end{aligned}" />

The small cone has <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac12\right)^3=\tfrac18" /> of the volume of the whole cone, because volume scales with the cube of the scale factor.

# Computing the lower part

The lower part is **what remains of the whole cone after the small cone is removed**.

<BlockMath math="V=96\pi-12\pi=84\pi\text{ cm}^3" />

The value <InlineMath math="48\pi" /> assumes that cutting at half the height also halves the volume. The value <InlineMath math="72\pi" /> uses the area ratio <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> as the volume ratio and so subtracts <InlineMath math="24\pi" />. The value <InlineMath math="12\pi" /> is the small cone, and <InlineMath math="96\pi" /> is the whole cone.

So the volume of the part containing the base is **<InlineMath math="84\pi\text{ cm}^3" />**.

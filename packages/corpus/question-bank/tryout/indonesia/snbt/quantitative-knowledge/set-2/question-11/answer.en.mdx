# The central angle of each segment

The lengths <InlineMath math="AB" />, <InlineMath math="AP" />, and <InlineMath math="BP" /> are all 4 cm, because each circle passes through the other center. So triangle <InlineMath math="ABP" /> is **equilateral** and angle <InlineMath math="PAB=60^\circ" />. The same argument for <InlineMath math="Q" /> gives the central angle <InlineMath math="PAQ=120^\circ" />.

# The area of one segment

A segment is a sector minus triangle <InlineMath math="APQ" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{\text{sector}}&=\frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ}\cdot\pi\cdot4^2=\frac{16\pi}{3}\\
A_{\triangle APQ}&=\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot4\cdot\sin120^\circ=4\sqrt3\\
A_{\text{segment}}&=\frac{16\pi}{3}-4\sqrt3
\end{aligned}" />

The overlap consists of **two segments**.

<BlockMath math="A=2\left(\frac{16\pi}{3}-4\sqrt3\right)=\frac{32\pi}{3}-8\sqrt3" />

The option <InlineMath math="\frac{16\pi}{3}-4\sqrt3" /> counts only one segment. The option <InlineMath math="\frac{32\pi}{3}-4\sqrt3" /> doubles the sector but subtracts only one triangle. The options <InlineMath math="\frac{32\pi}{3}-16\sqrt3" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac{16\pi}{3}-8\sqrt3" /> subtract the rhombus <InlineMath math="APBQ" /> from each sector, although only triangle <InlineMath math="APQ" /> should be subtracted.

So the area of the overlap is **<InlineMath math="\left(\frac{32\pi}{3}-8\sqrt3\right)\text{ cm}^2" />**.

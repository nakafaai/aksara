"The second red ball comes on the third draw" means two things happen together: **exactly one red ball appears in the first two draws**, and the third draw is red.

# The two possible orders

The first red ball can come on the first or the second draw. Since there is no replacement, each probability uses what is left in the bag.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{R, W, R})&=\frac4{10}\cdot\frac69\cdot\frac38=\frac{72}{720}\\
P(\text{W, R, R})&=\frac6{10}\cdot\frac49\cdot\frac38=\frac{72}{720}
\end{aligned}" />

The two orders are **mutually exclusive**, so their probabilities add.

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{72+72}{720}=\frac{144}{720}=\frac15" />

# The wrong options

The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac1{10}" /> counts only one order. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac3{10}" /> is the probability of exactly two reds in three draws, without requiring the second red to be third. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac25" /> is only the probability that the third draw is red. The value <InlineMath math="\tfrac8{15}" /> stops at the probability of **exactly one red in the first two draws**.

So the required probability is <InlineMath math="\tfrac15" />.

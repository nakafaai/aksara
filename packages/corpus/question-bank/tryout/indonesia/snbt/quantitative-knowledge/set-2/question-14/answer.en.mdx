A line is tangent to a circle exactly when **the distance from the center to the line equals the radius**. So first find the center and radius of the circle.

# Center and radius

Complete the square in <InlineMath math="x" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-6x+y^2+5&=0\\
(x-3)^2-9+y^2+5&=0\\
(x-3)^2+y^2&=4
\end{aligned}" />

The center is <InlineMath math="(3,0)" /> and the radius is 2. Watch the sign: the term <InlineMath math="-6x" /> puts the center at <InlineMath math="x=3" />, **not at <InlineMath math="x=-3" />**.

# The tangency condition

Write the line as <InlineMath math="mx-y+2=0" /> and use the formula for the distance from a point to a line.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{\lvert 3m-0+2\rvert}{\sqrt{m^2+1}}&=2\\
(3m+2)^2&=4\left(m^2+1\right)\\
9m^2+12m+4&=4m^2+4\\
5m^2+12m&=0\\
m(5m+12)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The values are <InlineMath math="m=0" /> or <InlineMath math="m=-\tfrac{12}{5}" />. The value <InlineMath math="m=0" /> gives the line <InlineMath math="y=2" />, which does touch the top of the circle at <InlineMath math="(3,2)" />. **The sum of the two values** is <InlineMath math="-\tfrac{12}{5}" />.

The option 0 sees only the horizontal line <InlineMath math="y=2" />. The option <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12}{5}" /> uses the center <InlineMath math="(-3,0)" /> because of a sign error. The option <InlineMath math="-\tfrac{5}{12}" /> flips the fraction. The option 3 appears when the center <InlineMath math="(-3,0)" /> is combined with a squared radius of 5 read from the constant term.

So the sum of all values of <InlineMath math="m" /> is <InlineMath math="-\tfrac{12}{5}" />.

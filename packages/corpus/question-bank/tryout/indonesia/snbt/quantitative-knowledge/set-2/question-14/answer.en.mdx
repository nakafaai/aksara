We need to determine exactly when <InlineMath math="a_n" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="5" />. Checking a few terms can suggest a pattern, but it does not prove that the pattern continues. We therefore derive a formula for the entire sequence.

#### Finding the First Term

Starting from <InlineMath math="a_4 = 85" /> and working backwards gives

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="a_3 = \frac{85 - 1}{4} = 21" />
  <BlockMath math="a_2 = \frac{21 - 1}{4} = 5" />
  <BlockMath math="a_1 = \frac{5 - 1}{4} = 1" />
</MathContainer>

#### Deriving a Formula for Every Term

Multiplying the recurrence by <InlineMath math="3" /> and adding <InlineMath math="1" /> turns it into a geometric recurrence:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3a_{n+1} + 1 = 3(4a_n + 1) + 1" />
  <BlockMath math="3a_{n+1} + 1 = 4(3a_n + 1)" />
</MathContainer>

Because <InlineMath math="3a_1 + 1 = 4" />, repeated multiplication by <InlineMath math="4" /> gives

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3a_n + 1 = 4^n" />
  <BlockMath math="a_n = \frac{4^n - 1}{3}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Checking Divisibility by Five

Modulo <InlineMath math="5" />, we have <InlineMath math="4 \equiv -1" />. Therefore

<BlockMath math="3a_n \equiv (-1)^n - 1 \pmod{5}" />

- If <InlineMath math="n" /> is even, then <InlineMath math="(-1)^n - 1 = 0" />, so <InlineMath math="a_n \equiv 0 \pmod{5}" />.
- If <InlineMath math="n" /> is odd, then <InlineMath math="(-1)^n - 1 \equiv 3 \pmod{5}" />, so <InlineMath math="a_n \equiv 1 \pmod{5}" />.

Here we may cancel the factor <InlineMath math="3" /> because <InlineMath math="3" /> has a multiplicative inverse modulo <InlineMath math="5" />. Thus, <InlineMath math="a_n" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="5" /> exactly when <InlineMath math="n" /> is even.

Among <InlineMath math="1, 29, 73, 364" /> and <InlineMath math="473" />, only <InlineMath math="364" /> is even. Hence the required value is <InlineMath math="n = 364" />.

A line is tangent to a circle exactly when **the distance from the center to the line equals the radius**. So first find the center and radius of the circle.

# Center and radius

Complete the square in <InlineMath math="x" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2+4x+y^2-1&=0\\
(x+2)^2-4+y^2-1&=0\\
(x+2)^2+y^2&=5
\end{aligned}" />

The center is <InlineMath math="(-2,0)" /> and the radius is <InlineMath math="\sqrt5" />. Watch the sign: the term <InlineMath math="+4x" /> puts the center at <InlineMath math="x=-2" />, **not at <InlineMath math="x=2" />**.

# The tangency condition

Write the line as <InlineMath math="mx-y+3=0" /> and use the formula for the distance from a point to a line.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{\lvert -2m-0+3\rvert}{\sqrt{m^2+1}}&=\sqrt5\\
(3-2m)^2&=5\left(m^2+1\right)\\
9-12m+4m^2&=5m^2+5\\
m^2+12m-4&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The discriminant is <InlineMath math="144+16>0" />, so there are two tangent lines. **The sum of the two values of <InlineMath math="m" />** comes from the coefficients of the quadratic: <InlineMath math="-\tfrac{12}{1}=-12" />.

The option 12 uses the center <InlineMath math="(2,0)" /> because of a sign error. The option <InlineMath math="-4" /> is the product of the two values of <InlineMath math="m" />, not their sum, and the option 4 is that product with the sign flipped. The option <InlineMath math="-6" /> divides the sum by 2.

So the sum of all values of <InlineMath math="m" /> is <InlineMath math="-12" />.

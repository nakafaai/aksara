A mean changes because the total changes. The safest approach is **to work with the total height**, not with the means.

# The total height at each stage

A total is always **the mean times the number of children**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{start}&=12\times150=1800\\
\text{after one child leaves}&=11\times149=1639\\
\text{after one child joins}&=12\times150.5=1806
\end{aligned}" />

The child who left is <InlineMath math="1800-1639=161\text{ cm}" /> tall, and the child who joined is <InlineMath math="1806-1639=167\text{ cm}" /> tall.

<BlockMath math="167-161=6\text{ cm}" />

# A shortcut

The group has 12 children at the start and at the end. So **the difference between the two children equals the change in the total height** from start to end, <InlineMath math="1806-1800=12\times(150.5-150)=6" />. The mean of 149 in the middle is not needed for the answer.

The value 0.5 is the difference between the first and last means, not yet multiplied by the number of children. The value 5.5 multiplies that difference by 11, although the final group has 12 children. The values 1 and 1.5 only compare means, namely <InlineMath math="150-149" /> and <InlineMath math="150.5-149" />.

So the difference in height between the two children is **<InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />**.

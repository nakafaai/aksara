<InlineMath math="(1)" /> During a composting workshop, several groups mixed all materials at once because they did not understand the sequence of brown and green layers.

<InlineMath math="(2)" /> Across the first seven days, a mean of 23 groups per session arranged the layers in the correct order.

<InlineMath math="(3)" /> That figure did not rule out the influence of facilitators' spoken explanations, so the team treated it as the basis for a hypothesis rather than proof of a cause.

<InlineMath math="(4)" /> During trial sessions, every table received an illustrated card showing the order of brown and green materials.

<InlineMath math="(5)" /> During comparison sessions, groups used the spoken guidance and the old instruction sheet.

<InlineMath math="(6)" /> Workshop duration, number of facilitators, and material kits were kept the same in both conditions.

<InlineMath math="(7)" /> Under those conditions, the mean number of groups arranging the layers correctly was 31 in the trial, 23 at baseline, and 25 in the comparison sessions.

<InlineMath math="(8)" /> Some participants also said that the card helped them check the sequence, although those spontaneous comments did not represent every group.

<InlineMath math="(9)" /> The team therefore concluded only that the cards were promising in the context of this short workshop.

**The team changed only one factor: cards showing the order of brown and green materials.**

“One factor” requires a singular noun, and “only” already supplies the restriction. A colon can introduce the factor directly, so adding “namely” before another colon would duplicate that function. The sentence is therefore grammatical and precise without an extra limiting word or mismatched punctuation.

(1) When mangrove seedling trays reached the junction leading to several restoration plots, volunteers often hesitated before the next step.

(2) For seven days, the organisers counted the seedlings that reached the intended plot without being redirected.

(3) The baseline averaged 20 seedlings a day, but changes in the tide and differences in volunteer experience meant that the cause of the wrong turns remained uncertain.

(4) On trial days, the team attached a waterproof planting-site label to every seedling tray.

(5) On comparison days, the earlier process remained in place: destinations appeared only on the dispatch sheet.

(6) Distribution hours, staffing levels, and the number of seedlings moved were kept the same under both conditions.

(7) Under those conditions, the trial value reached 27, above the baseline of 20 and the comparison value of 22.

(8) Short interviews pointed in the same direction; several volunteers found it easier to match trays with planting plots, although that judgement was subjective.

(9) Nevertheless, the team limited its conclusion to the short trial at that one nursery.

(10) The next test will run for longer under the same measurement rules and include different tidal conditions.


Consider the following four summary sentences, which have been placed out of order.

(1) In that trial, the value for seedlings arriving without redirection reached 27, above the comparison value of 22.

(2) However, the short trial at one nursery still limited the conclusion, so the team planned a longer test under the same measurement rules in different tidal conditions.

(3) On the basis of that hypothesis, the team tested planting-location labels.

(4) The baseline produced a value of 20 and was used to form a hypothesis, not to establish a cause.

Which order forms the most coherent summary?

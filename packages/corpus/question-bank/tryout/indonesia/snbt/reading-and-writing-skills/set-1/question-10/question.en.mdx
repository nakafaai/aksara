<InlineMath math="(1)" /> Healthy, resilient ecosystems help reduce disaster risk. <InlineMath math="(2)" /> According to the United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction (UNDRR), environmental degradation can be both a driver and a consequence of disasters. <InlineMath math="(3)" /> Such degradation reduces the environment's capacity to meet social and ecological needs. <InlineMath math="(4)" /> The Sendai Framework recognizes poor environmental management, ecosystem degradation, and unsustainable use of natural resources as drivers of risk.

<InlineMath math="(5)" /> Environmental degradation is a decline in environmental quality. <InlineMath math="(6)" /> This decline is evident, for example, in damaged soil, polluted water and air, and biodiversity loss. <InlineMath math="(7)" /> Ecosystem damage can also reduce people's vulnerability and exposure to hazards. <InlineMath math="(8)" /> Protecting and restoring ecosystems therefore requires planning, governance, and risk-informed investment.

Sources: adapted from UNDRR, ["Nature for Resilience"](https://www.undrr.org/implementing-sendai-framework/sendai-framework-action/nature-resilience) and ["Underlying Disaster Risk Drivers"](https://www.undrr.org/terminology/underlying-disaster-risk-drivers).

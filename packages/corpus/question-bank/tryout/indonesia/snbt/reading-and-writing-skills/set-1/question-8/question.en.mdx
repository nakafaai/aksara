(1) A sarcophagus is a stone burial container that generally has a receptacle and a lid. (2) On Samosir Island, sarcophagi are among the megalithic remains associated with Toba Batak death traditions. (3) The Tomok sarcophagus is rectangular, widens at the top, and resembles a traditional *solu bolon* boat. (4) In Bali, more than one hundred sarcophagi have been recorded at various locations. (5) The boat form of the Tomok sarcophagus is interpreted as a vehicle carrying the deceased to the spirit world.

(6) Its receptacle has an opening used to store bones. (7) Figures carved on the front often depict a guardian against harmful influences. (8) Other figures on the lid (interpreted as people close to the deceased are carved in various poses. (9) [...], the sarcophagus faces Mount Pusuk Buhit or Lake Toba, possibly reflecting a belief that mountains are sacred places for spirits.

Sources: adapted from the Directorate of Cultural Protection, ["Sarkofagus Tomok, Khas Tanah Batak"](https://kebudayaan.kemdikbud.go.id/dpk/sarkofagus-tomok-khas-tanah-batak/); Dalimunthe and Wiradnyana, ["Archaeological Object as Tourism in Samosir Island"](https://ejournal.brin.go.id/kapata/article/view/11854); and the Bali Cultural Heritage Conservation Center, ["Tradisi Megalitik di Bali"](https://kebudayaan.kemdikbud.go.id/bpcbbali/tradisi-megalitik-di-bali/).
The count of sarcophagi in Bali is also documented by Bagyo Prasetyo in [*Megalitik, Fenomena yang Berkembang di Indonesia*](https://repositori.kemendikdasmen.go.id/4657/).

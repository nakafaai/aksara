(1) In a school breakfast programme, many students chose their meal only when they reached the serving table, so some options ran out while others were left over.

(2) During the first seven days, a mean of 23 servings per session were collected as recorded.

(3) That match could vary with attendance and menu popularity, so the team formed only a hypothesis about the timing of orders.

(4) During trial sessions, students booked their menu one day in advance.

(5) During comparison sessions, choices were still made at the serving table that morning.

(6) The menu, serving time, staff numbers, and number of prepared portions were kept the same in both conditions.

(7) Servings collected as ordered reached a mean of 30 in trial sessions, compared with 23 at baseline and 25 in comparison sessions.

(8) Students found advance booking clearer, but absence after booking could still leave food unused.

(9) The team therefore did not claim that advance booking always reduced waste or offset the effects of changes in attendance.

(10) A longer test will record attendance and leftovers while retaining the same measure of order matching.


Consider the following four summary sentences, which have been placed out of order.

(1) In that trial, a mean of 30 servings were collected as ordered, above the comparison mean of 25.

(2) However, the effect of absence on leftovers remained uncertain, so the team planned a longer test recording attendance and leftovers with the same order-matching measure.

(3) On the basis of that hypothesis, the team tested ordering menus one day in advance.

(4) The baseline produced a value of 23 and was used to form a hypothesis, not to establish a cause.

Which order forms the most coherent summary?

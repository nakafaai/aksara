**The team changed only one factor: use of a checklist before recording.**

**One factor** has singular agreement, and **only** states the restriction once. The colon follows a complete clause and introduces the factor: using a checklist before recording.

*One factors* has incorrect number agreement, while *merely only* repeats the restriction. A colon after *namely* is unnecessary here; a comma can introduce that explanatory phrase. A semicolon cannot connect the main clause to the noun phrase that follows in the last distractor.

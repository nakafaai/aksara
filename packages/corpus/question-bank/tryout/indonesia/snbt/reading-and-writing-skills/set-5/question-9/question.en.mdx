(1) In a street-tree survey, two observers often assigned different categories to the same damage because written descriptions such as “moderate” and “severe” were not specific enough.

(2) During seven days of baseline observation, pairs of observers agreed on a mean of 19 records per session.

(3) Agreement could vary with tree species and observer experience, so the baseline supported only a working hypothesis.

(4) During trial sessions, the team added sample photographs for every condition category.

(5) During comparison sessions, observers used only the previous written descriptions.

(6) Route length, observer pairs, and recording forms were kept the same in both conditions.

(7) Mean agreement reached 26 records in the trial, higher than the baseline of 19 and the comparison mean of 21.

(8) Observers said the photographs made comparisons easier, but the available examples showed only a small number of tree species.

(9) Given that limitation, the team did not claim that the photographs would improve consistency across the whole district.

(10) A follow-up will include more tree species while retaining the same measure of observer agreement.


Consider the following four summary sentences, which have been placed out of order.

(1) In that trial, mean agreement reached 26 records, above the comparison mean of 21.

(2) However, because the sample photographs covered few tree species, the team planned to test more species using the same measure of agreement.

(3) On the basis of that hypothesis, the team tested example photos for each condition category.

(4) The baseline produced a value of 19 and was used to form a hypothesis, not to establish a cause.

Which order forms the most coherent summary?

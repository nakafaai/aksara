<InlineMath math="(1)" /> Sago has long been used as a staple carbohydrate in parts of eastern Indonesia. <InlineMath math="(2)" /> Starch extracted from the trunk can be prepared as traditional foods such as papeda and kapurung. <InlineMath math="(3)" /> An Indonesian Ministry of Agriculture publication explains that sago starch can also be used for noodles, vermicelli, and cakes or as a substitute for wheat starch. <InlineMath math="(4)" /> This versatility gives sago a role in efforts to diversify local food.

<InlineMath math="(5)" /> In <InlineMath math="2016" />, the Ministry of Agriculture, the Food and Agriculture Organization, and the Southeast Sulawesi provincial government began a project to promote the use of sago starch. <InlineMath math="(6)" /> Indonesia's <InlineMath math="2023" /> Agricultural Census covered seven agricultural subsectors. <InlineMath math="(7)" /> The sago project developed technical capacity in production and starch extraction. <InlineMath math="(8)" /> It also sought to increase sago's added value through a sustainable and economically viable value chain. <InlineMath math="(9)" /> A business unit called Sagu Meambo Food was formed, with production focused in Konawe and South Konawe and sales based in Kendari. <InlineMath math="(10)" /> These efforts show that food diversification involves both processing innovation and the development of a viable local value chain.

**The team changed only one factor: genre signs on each table.**

**One** requires the singular noun **factor**, and **only** expresses the restriction once. The colon follows a complete clause and introduces the named factor.

*One factors* breaks number agreement, while *merely only* repeats the restriction. The colon after *namely* is unnecessary in this construction; commas can set off the explanatory phrase. A semicolon also cannot join the main clause to *namely genre signs on each table*, which is not an independent clause.

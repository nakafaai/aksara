(1) A student exhibition occupied three rooms linked by branching corridors, and some visitors returned to the entrance before completing the full route.

(2) During the first seven days, a mean of 21 visitors per session reached the final room.

(3) That number could be affected by crowding or by the appeal of particular works, so it did not prove that route directions caused the problem.

(4) During trial sessions, direction arrows were placed at every corridor junction.

(5) During comparison sessions, the corridors had no additional arrows.

(6) The arrangement of works, exhibition hours, staff numbers, and entrance were kept the same in both conditions.

(7) The mean of 30 visitors completing the route in trial sessions was above the baseline mean of 21 and the comparison mean of 23.

(8) Several visitors said the arrows helped, but the team had not recorded which junctions caused the most difficulty.

(9) The result therefore supported only a temporary improvement at that exhibition, not a rule for every gallery.

(10) The next test will run for longer and record where visitors turn back while retaining the same route-completion measure.


Which sentence most logically develops the idea after sentence (3)?

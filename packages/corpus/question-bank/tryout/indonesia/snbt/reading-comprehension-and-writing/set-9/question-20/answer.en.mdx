# Given

After **six months of group tooth-brushing**, cavity counts dropped across all four grades, while sentences (5) and (6) note **the smallest decline in Grade III**, where some students often arrived late and missed brushing.

# Asked

The best conclusion supported by both the passage and the table.

# Solution

Join **the table-wide downward trend with the explanation for Grade III's smaller improvement**. Every class improved, and the smallest drop occurred in the class whose students frequently missed the morning sessions.

> A sound conclusion unifies the broad pattern in the data with the passage's explanation for the outlier row.

## Checking all five candidate conclusions

| Candidate conclusion | Evidence check against text and table | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Group brushing was followed by fewer cavities in every grade, with the smallest drop where students often missed it | Matches all four table rows plus sentences (5) and (6) | **Correct** |
| No student has cavities anymore | False because every grade still has <InlineMath math="5" /> to <InlineMath math="12" /> students with cavities | **Wrong** |
| Grade III students are the laziest at brushing at home | The text mentions arriving late at school, not home habits | **Wrong** |
| Letters to parents improved students' teeth | Sentence (7) says letters are only planned for the future | **Wrong** |
| Fewer students were examined after six months | The table lists a single examined count per grade | **Wrong** |

# Answer

The best conclusion is that **group tooth-brushing was followed by fewer students with cavities in all classes, with the smallest drop in the class where some students often missed the sessions** (*Kegiatan sikat gigi bersama diikuti turunnya siswa bergigi berlubang di semua kelas, dan penurunan paling kecil terjadi di kelas yang sebagian siswanya sering melewatkan kegiatan itu*).

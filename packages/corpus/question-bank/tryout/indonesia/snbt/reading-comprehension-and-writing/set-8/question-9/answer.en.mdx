# Given

Sentence (2) of the *infiltration-well* passage inserts **a faulty comma between subject and predicate**: <Highlight>Air hujan yang jatuh di halaman, langsung mengalir ke selokan karena sebagian besar halaman rumah sudah ditutup semen</Highlight>.

# Asked

The properly punctuated revision of sentence (2).

# Solution

Apply **two Indonesian EYD comma rules**. Never separate a subject (*Air hujan yang jatuh di halaman*) from its predicate (*langsung mengalir*) with a comma, and never place a comma before a trailing *karena* subordinate clause, leaving the sentence cleanly without any commas.

> Standard Indonesian EYD omits commas both between subject and verb and before a subordinate clause that follows the main clause.

## Auditing comma placement across all five options

| Candidate revision | EYD comma check | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Air hujan yang jatuh di halaman langsung mengalir ke selokan karena sebagian besar halaman rumah sudah ditutup semen.* | Keeps subject, verb, and trailing *karena* clause cleanly unsplit | **Correct** |
| *... di halaman, langsung mengalir ke selokan, karena ...* | Two wrong commas: splits subject from verb and precedes *karena* | **Wrong** |
| *Air hujan, yang jatuh di halaman ...* | Splits the noun from its restrictive modifier *yang jatuh di halaman* | **Wrong** |
| *... mengalir ke selokan, karena ...* | Unneeded comma before trailing *karena* clause | **Wrong** |
| *... di halaman langsung, mengalir ke selokan ...* | Splits adverb *langsung* from verb *mengalir* | **Wrong** |

# Answer

The correct punctuation revision is ***Air hujan yang jatuh di halaman langsung mengalir ke selokan karena sebagian besar halaman rumah sudah ditutup semen***.

# Given

Sentence (8) reads: *"Bedeng cabai, yang dikelilingi kenikir, mulai ramai didatangi kepik pada minggu keenam."*

Sentence (3) specifies kenikir was planted around only **three of the six chili beds**, and sentence (10) contrasts those three beds with the other three.

# Asked

The punctuation fix that brings sentence (8) in line with the rest of the passage.

# Solution

Under EYD V, a pair of commas around a *yang* clause marks **non-restrictive extra information**, falsely implying every chili bed had kenikir. Removing **both commas together** turns the clause into a restrictive modifier picking out only those three beds.

> A *yang* clause without commas narrows down a subgroup, whereas enclosing commas treat the description as true of the whole group.

## Removing the enclosing comma pair in sentence eight

Sentence (8) currently reads <Highlight>Bedeng cabai, yang dikelilingi kenikir, mulai ramai didatangi kepik pada minggu keenam.</Highlight>

Dropping both commas forms the clean subject **bedeng cabai yang dikelilingi kenikir** followed straight by the predicate **mulai ramai didatangi** with no stray comma splitting subject and predicate.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Dropping only the second comma** leaves an unpaired opening comma and breaks syntax.
- **Dropping only the first comma** leaves a single comma illegally separating subject and predicate.
- **Fronting *Pada minggu keenam*** still keeps both commas around the *yang* clause.
- **Moving the clause behind *kepik*** makes the bugs, not the beds, surrounded by kenikir.

# Answer

The correct revision is **"Bedeng cabai yang dikelilingi kenikir mulai ramai didatangi kepik pada minggu keenam."**

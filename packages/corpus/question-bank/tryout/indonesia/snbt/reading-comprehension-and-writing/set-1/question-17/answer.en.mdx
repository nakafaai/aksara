# Given

Injuries from snapped fighting-kite strings fell from **<InlineMath math="14" /> people** last year (sentence 3) to **<InlineMath math="3" /> people** this year (sentence 9).

From that drop, the committee concludes in sentence (11) that **banning glass-coated string** made the festival safer.

# Asked

The statement that most strongly weakens the committee's conclusion in sentence (11).

# Solution

To weaken a causal claim, find **another factor that also changed this year** and could lower injuries without the ban. Relocating the kite-fighting contest far away from spectators supplies that exact competing cause.

> A cause-and-effect conclusion weakens most when a separate change explains the same drop on its own.

## Finding the competing cause for fewer injuries

Sentence (3) identifies how people got hurt: <Highlight>tergores benang layang-layang aduan yang putus lalu jatuh ke arah penonton</Highlight> ("cut by fighting-kite strings that snapped and fell toward spectators").

If the fighting contest **moved to an end of the beach closed to spectators** this year, snapped strings fell far from crowds regardless of string type, undermining credit for the ban.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Most last-year injuries coming from glass-coated string** strengthens the case that banning it mattered.
- **A longer-open first-aid post** catches more minor cuts, making only <InlineMath math="3" /> injuries even more impressive.
- **More visitors this year** raises exposure, so fewer injuries supports the ban even more.
- **Barring the two rule-breakers** proves enforcement worked, reinforcing the conclusion.

# Answer

The strongest weakener is that **this year the kite-fighting contest moved to an end of the beach closed to spectators, while last year it was held right in front of the spectator rope** (*Tahun ini, lomba aduan dipindahkan ke ujung pantai yang tertutup bagi penonton, sedangkan tahun lalu lomba itu digelar tepat di depan tali pembatas*).

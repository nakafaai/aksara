# Given

Sentence (3) already reports **fourteen visitors** injured last year, and sentence (9) already reports **only three visitors** injured this year.

Sentence (10) then comments that the figure is much smaller than last year's count, before sentence (11) gives the committee's conclusion.

# Asked

The sentence that should be removed to make the passage more concise without losing any information.

# Solution

Test every candidate on two checks: whether **other sentences already supply its content** and whether **no later sentence depends on it**. Sentence (10) is the only sentence passing both checks.

> A sentence that merely restates a comparison already obvious from two stated numbers and anchors no later reference can be cut safely.

## Testing sentence ten on redundancy and flow

Sentence (10) reads <Highlight>Angka itu jauh lebih kecil daripada jumlah korban luka pada festival tahun lalu.</Highlight> ("That figure is far smaller than last year's injury count.")

Readers already have **<InlineMath math="3" />** in sentence (9) and **<InlineMath math="14" />** in sentence (3). Nothing after sentence (10) refers back to it, and sentence (11) connects smoothly straight after sentence (9).

## Why the other four sentences must stay

- **Sentence (4)** is the sole definition of *gelasan* (ground glass and glue paste).
- **Sentence (9)** is the only place stating this year's figure of **<InlineMath math="3" />** injuries.
- **Sentence (11)** adds a new idea: the committee's causal claim crediting the ban.
- **Sentence (13)** adds new facts about morning-only north-zone hours and fishermen's boats.

# Answer

The sentence that should be removed is **sentence (10)** (*kalimat (10)*).

# Given

Sentence (10) reports a week-twelve aphid gap: an average of **<InlineMath math="6" /> per plant** in the three kenikir beds versus **<InlineMath math="35" />** in the other three beds.

Sentence (11) states all six beds were **watered and fertilized on the same schedule and amounts**, right before sentence (12) credits predatory bugs.

# Asked

The author's main purpose in including sentence (11).

# Solution

Look at where sentence (11) sits: directly between the **aphid gap** (10) and the **causal claim** (12). Keeping watering and fertilizer identical rules out better care as an alternative cause of the gap.

> In a comparison or experiment passage, stating that other conditions were held constant serves to rule out rival explanations.

## Checking the role of sentence eleven in the argument

The author establishes a controlled baseline with <Highlight>Selama semester itu, keenam bedeng disiram dan dipupuk dengan jadwal dan takaran yang sama</Highlight>. ("Throughout that semester, all six beds were watered and fertilized on the same schedule and amounts.")

Without sentence (11), a reader could ask whether the kenikir beds simply received better care. Sentence (11) closes that loophole so kenikir and the bugs remain the sole difference.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Explaining why bugs visited specific beds** fails because uniform care cannot explain a difference.
- **Showing students stayed diligent** misreads a between-bed control as a comment on student effort.
- **Proving watering and fertilizer do not affect aphids** goes beyond the text, as neither factor was varied.
- **Admitting a study weakness** happens only in sentence (13), not in sentence (11).

# Answer

The author includes sentence (11) **to rule out the possibility that the aphid gap between beds was caused by different care** (*menutup kemungkinan bahwa selisih kutu daun antarbedeng disebabkan oleh perbedaan perawatan*).

The managers' conclusion in sentence (14) is that the grain loans from the barn are what freed residents from depending on middlemen. Their evidence is in (10), (12) and (13): among a hundred member households, the number in debt fell from forty to twelve, just when rice prices were rising.

The question asks for the assumption that **must** be true, not just one that helps. The most reliable tool is the negation test: flip the statement and see whether the managers' conclusion collapses. If it collapses, the statement really has to be true. If the conclusion can still stand, the statement is only support.

| Statement | Negated | Effect on the conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Among those who stopped owing, some had borrowed grain | None of those who stopped owing ever borrowed grain | Collapses, because a loan can't free people who never took one |
| Those who borrowed grain no longer owe | Some borrowers still owe | Stands, because the loans could free most borrowers without freeing all |
| Among those still in debt, some never borrowed | All of those still in debt had borrowed | Stands, because those twelve households don't decide why the others stopped |
| Rice was more expensive than before | Rice wasn't more expensive | The conclusion survives without it, and sentence (13) already says rice prices were rising, so it isn't a hidden assumption |
| Another village succeeded with a barn | No other village succeeded | Stands, because the conclusion is only about this village |

Only the first statement passes the negation test. It's also deliberately weak: it only needs some household that stopped owing to have borrowed grain. A required assumption is usually that modest, because the conclusion needs only the most basic condition.

Two traps are the slipperiest. The claim that every borrower stopped owing sounds like the heart of the argument, but it's too strong, and the conclusion can be true without it. The claim about rice prices feels important because the managers use it as evidence, but the conclusion doesn't depend on it, and sentence (13) already says prices were rising, so it isn't a hidden assumption.

So the assumption that must be true is that, among the households that stopped owing middlemen, some had borrowed grain from the barn.

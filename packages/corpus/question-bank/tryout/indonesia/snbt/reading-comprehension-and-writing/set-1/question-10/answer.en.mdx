# Given

Paragraph one (sentence 3) states staff expected **elderly patients would struggle most with the app**.

Paragraph three reports a less crowded waiting room in (10), elderly patients successfully using the app or counter in (11) and (12), and **young patients** ignoring notifications in (13) and (14).

# Asked

The role of the third paragraph in the whole passage.

# Solution

Read paragraph three **against the expectation set up in paragraph one**. It judges how well the app worked while flipping the opening assumption, since the real trouble came from young patients instead of elderly ones.

> A paragraph's function emerges from how it answers or overturns the setup introduced earlier in the passage.

## Tracing the three moves in paragraph three

Paragraph three turns the initial expectation around by noting <Highlight>sebagian pasien muda tidak bergeming ketika pemberitahuan waktu panggilan masuk ke ponselnya</Highlight> ("some young patients ignore the notification when it arrives").

1. **Sentence (10)** evaluates the result: the waiting room is no longer crowded, even on Mondays.
2. **Sentences (11) and (12)** refute the fear that elderly patients would struggle most.
3. **Sentences (13) and (14)** show the real setback comes from young patients ignoring alerts.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Proving the waiting-room problem is solved** ignores sentences (13) and (14) and overstates with *membuktikan*.
- **Backing up the expectation about elderly patients** reverses sentence (11).
- **Explaining late-arrival rules** belongs to paragraph two, not paragraph three.
- **Proposing to keep the counter** adds a recommendation absent from sentence (12).

# Answer

Paragraph three **evaluates the app's outcome while showing that the problem actually came from the group nobody worried about in paragraph one** (*menilai hasil aplikasi antrean sekaligus menunjukkan bahwa masalah yang muncul justru datang dari kelompok yang tidak dikhawatirkan pada paragraf pertama*).

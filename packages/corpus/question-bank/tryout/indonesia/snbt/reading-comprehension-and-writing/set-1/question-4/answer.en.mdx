A sentence deserves deletion when two things happen at once: its content is already somewhere else, and its presence breaks the reference chain between other sentences. On the other hand, a sentence that looks repetitive or off-topic has to stay if another sentence depends on it.

So ask two questions about each candidate: is its content new, and does any sentence need it?

| Sentence | New content? | Does anything need it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (3) debts to middlemen paid off by selling grain | Yes, this is where it first appears | Yes, *lingkaran itu* ("that circle") in (4) needs this step |
| (9) the reserve helps six families | Yes, an example of the reserve in use | It's tied to the "reserve" in (8) and shows the reserve really gets used |
| (10) forty out of a hundred households in debt | Yes, the number before the barn | Yes, *jumlah itu* ("that number") in (12) points to this forty |
| (11) most of them pay off debts by selling grain while prices are low | No, it combines the content of (3) and (1) | Nothing |
| (13) the drop came while rice prices rose | Yes | Yes, *hal itu* ("that fact") in (14) points to it |

Sentence (11) **repeats the ideas of sentences (3) and (1)** in other words: debts are paid off by selling grain right after the harvest, while the price is low. On top of that, (11) sits between two sentences that belong tightly together. The phrase *Dua tahun kemudian* ("two years later") in (12) counts from the barn's revival in (10), and "that number" points to the forty in (10). With (11) in the middle, "that number" can be read as "most of them", and "two years later" seems to count from a harvest. Delete (11), and (10) runs straight into (12).

Why the other candidates aren't the answer:

- Sentence (3) looks like (11), but (3) is the original. Without (3), *lingkaran itu* in (4) loses the step that closes the circle.
- Sentence (10) also sounds like a repeat of (3), since both are about debts to middlemen. The difference is that (10) carries the number forty that "that number" in (12) needs.
- Sentence (9) seems to wander off into pests, but it shows the reserve from (8) actually being used. Deleting it only removes an example, it doesn't make the text more cohesive.
- Sentence (13) looks like an aside about rice prices, yet *hal itu* in (14) points to it.

So the sentence you should delete is sentence (11).

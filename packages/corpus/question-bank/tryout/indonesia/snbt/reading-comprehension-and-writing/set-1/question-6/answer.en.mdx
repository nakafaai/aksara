A proposed edit counts as *tepat* ("correct") only when two things are true: the original sentence really has an error, and the edit fixes it without breaking anything else. So for each edit, first check whether the sentence has a problem, then judge the fix.

Two edits both delete commas, yet they get opposite verdicts. The key is what the *yang* clause ("which/who" clause) between the commas does. EYD V uses commas to enclose extra information. A *yang* clause between commas just adds a remark about everything the noun names, while a *yang* clause without commas **narrows** the noun down to only some of them.

| Proposed edit | Verdict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delete both commas in (4) | Tidak tepat (incorrect) | Sentence (4) is about one particular counter, the one that used to be the only place to get a number. So the clause *yang dulu menjadi satu-satunya tempat mengambil nomor* ("which used to be the only place to get a number") is extra information and belongs between commas. The original is already right. |
| Replace *membuat* ("make") with *menjadikan* ("render") in (10) | Tidak tepat (incorrect) | The verb isn't the problem. *Dengan adanya aplikasi itu* ("with the existence of that app") starts with the preposition *dengan*, so it's an adverbial, and the sentence still has no subject. |
| Delete both commas in (12) | Tepat (correct) | With the commas, sentence (12) says that all elderly patients have no smartphone. But sentence (11) says that nearly a third of app registrants are elderly patients using their own smartphones. The clause has to narrow the group. |
| Delete *tidak* ("not") before *bergeming* in (13) | Tepat (correct) | In the KBBI dictionary, *bergeming* means "to stay still, not to move at all". So *tidak bergeming* actually means "to move, to respond". Patients who respond to the notification wouldn't keep showing up late, so the sentence means *bergeming* ("not react"). |

Look closely at the third edit. It's correct only because sentence (11) exists. If the text didn't mention elderly patients using their own phones, the commas in (12) wouldn't contradict anything. That's the condition you miss if you read sentence (12) on its own.

For sentence (10), a real fix would drop *Dengan adanya* so that *aplikasi itu* ("that app") becomes the subject, or drop *membuat* so that *ruang tunggu* ("the waiting room") becomes the subject.

So the edits to sentences (4) and (10) are incorrect, and the edits to sentences (12) and (13) are correct.

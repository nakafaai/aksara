# Given

Sentence (10) records that **forty out of a hundred barn member households** owed middlemen each lean season, and sentence (12) says two years later **that number fell to twelve**.

Sentence (13) adds that **that drop happened just when rice prices were rising**, and sentence (14) calls **hal itu** ("that fact") the managers' proof.

# Asked

What the phrase *hal itu* in sentence (14) refers to.

# Solution

Follow the reference chain **backward from sentence (14) to sentence (10)** so no link drops out. Keeping the timing detail from sentence (13) intact is what separates the right option from the close traps.

> A shell phrase like *hal itu* points to the entire preceding clause, including the timing condition used as evidence.

## Tracing the reference chain backward

Sentence (13) states <Highlight>Penurunan itu terjadi ketika harga beras justru sedang naik.</Highlight> ("That drop happened just when rice prices were rising.")

| Pointer | Direct link | Full meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *hal itu* in (14) | Whole of sentence (13) | The drop **together with rising rice prices** |
| *penurunan itu* in (13) | Sentence (12) | Member households in debt fell from forty to twelve |
| *jumlah itu* in (12) | Sentence (10) | The hundred barn member households, not the whole village |

## Why the other four options fail

- **During the barn's two years** stops at sentence (12) and drops the rising rice prices in sentence (13).
- **Across the whole village** widens the scope beyond the hundred member households.
- **Size of each household's debt** swaps a count of households for a money amount.
- **Grain loans freeing residents** is the conclusion after *membuktikan* ("proves"), not the referent before it.

# Answer

*Hal itu* refers to **the fall in the number of member households owing middlemen just when rice prices were rising** (*turunnya jumlah rumah tangga anggota yang berutang kepada tengkulak justru ketika harga beras sedang naik*).

The phrase *hal itu* ("that fact") in sentence (14) points back to the content of sentence (13). But sentence (13) depends on other references itself, so you have to follow the chain all the way.

1. Sentence (13): "Penurunan itu terjadi ketika harga beras justru sedang naik" ("That drop happened just when rice prices were rising"). *Hal itu* refers to the whole sentence, the drop **together with when it happened**.
2. *Penurunan itu* ("that drop") points to the change in sentence (12): *jumlah itu tinggal dua belas* ("that number fell to twelve").
3. *Jumlah itu* ("that number") points to sentence (10): forty out of a hundred households that are barn members and owe middlemen. Sentence (11) does sit between the two, but it holds no number that later falls to twelve.

Put the three together. What fell is **the number of member households** in debt, not the size of their debts, and it fell just when rice prices were rising. The part about rising rice prices matters, because that's what the managers use as proof.

Each wrong option breaks exactly one link:

- The fall in member households in debt "during the barn's two years" stops at sentence (12) and drops the rice-price condition from sentence (13).
- The fall "across the whole village" widens the scope. The data in (10) and (12) cover only the hundred member households.
- The shrinking "size of each household's debt" swaps the number of households for an amount of money. Sentence (12) counts households, not debts.
- Grain loans freeing the residents is the conclusion that comes after the word *membuktikan* ("proves"), not the thing referred to before it.

So *hal itu* refers to the fall in the number of member households in debt to middlemen, just when rice prices were rising.

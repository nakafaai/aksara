# Given

Three sentences in the kite-festival passage hold grammar faults: sentence (2) lacks a main verb because of *yang*, sentence (6) doubles the purpose word with *agar supaya*, and sentence (8) misuses the location preposition *di garis pantai* for distance.

Each of four edits is **Tepat** ("Correct") only if it removes the fault without introducing a new error or shifting meaning.

# Asked

Whether each of the four proposed sentence edits is **Tepat** or **Tidak tepat**.

# Solution

Test every edit against **both criteria**: the original flaw disappears and the intended meaning survives. Deleting *yang* in (2) and deleting *agar* in (6) pass both tests, while *pada garis pantai* (8) and *sehingga* (6) fail.

> A sentence edit fails if it swaps one wrong preposition for another location word or turns a purpose clause into a result clause.

## Evaluating all four proposed sentence edits

The passage contains <Highlight>Festival tahun lalu yang menampilkan lebih dari dua ratus layang-layang</Highlight>, <Highlight>agar supaya mereka tidak tergoda</Highlight>, and <Highlight>dua puluh meter di garis pantai</Highlight>.

| Proposed edit | Verdict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delete *yang* in sentence (2) | **Tepat** | Restores *menampilkan* as main predicate cleanly |
| Change *di* to *pada* in sentence (8) | **Tidak tepat** | Distance requires *dari garis pantai*, not location word *pada* |
| Change *agar supaya* to *sehingga* in (6) | **Tidak tepat** | Shifts purpose to consequence, clashing with sentence (7) |
| Delete *agar* in sentence (6) | **Tepat** | Cuts redundancy while keeping standard purpose word *supaya* |

# Answer

Proposals **one and four** are **Tepat**, while proposals **two and three** are **Tidak tepat**.

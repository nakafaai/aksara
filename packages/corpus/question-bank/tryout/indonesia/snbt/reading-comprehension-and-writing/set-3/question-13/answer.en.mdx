# Given

Sentence (12) mentions shoppers who used to buy from **travelling vendors** (*pedagang keliling*).

Sentence (13) reports **some of those vendors lost customers and chose to rent stalls inside the market**, and sentence (14) states **"They (*Mereka*) must complete hygiene training before being allowed to sell."**

# Asked

Whom the pronoun *Mereka* ("They") in sentence (14) refers to.

# Solution

Step back **two links**: *Mereka* (14) continues sentence (13)'s exact subject (*some of those vendors who lost customers and chose to rent stalls*), and *pedagang itu* ("those vendors") points to *pedagang keliling* in sentence (12). Needing training *before selling* also fits new stall tenants alone.

> A pronoun like *Mereka* carries the exact restricted subject of the preceding sentence rather than the broader group named earlier.

## Tracing the two step chain for Mereka

Sentences (13) and (14) link directly: <Highlight>sebagian pedagang itu kehilangan pelanggan dan memilih menyewa lapak di dalam pasar. (14) Mereka wajib mengikuti pelatihan kebersihan sebelum boleh berjualan.</Highlight>

Linking *pedagang itu* to sentence (12) identifies **travelling vendors who lost customers and rented stalls inside the market**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Residents who bought fish** do not rent stalls or apply to sell.
- **All travelling vendors** drops the word *sebagian* ("some") in sentence (13).
- **Existing market vendors** are already selling inside and gained customers instead.
- **Travelling vendors who kept roaming** never rented market stalls.

# Answer

*Mereka* refers to **travelling vendors who lost customers and then rented stalls inside the market** (*pedagang keliling yang kehilangan pelanggan lalu menyewa lapak di dalam pasar*).

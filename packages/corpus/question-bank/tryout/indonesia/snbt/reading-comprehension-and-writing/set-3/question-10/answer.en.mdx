# Given

In year one only **<InlineMath math="3" />** of <InlineMath math="20" /> learners stayed (sentence 4). After the workshop **reordered its curriculum** (sentences 5 and 6), **<InlineMath math="12" />** of <InlineMath math="18" /> stayed (sentence 9).

Right before sentence (9), sentence (8) notes: **"The number of sessions and teachers in year two stayed the same as in year one."**

# Asked

The author's main purpose in writing sentence (8).

# Solution

Notice sentence (8) sits right before ***Hasilnya*** ("As a result") in sentence (9). Holding sessions and teachers constant rules out extra meetings or extra staff as rival causes, leaving the **reordered learning sequence** as the strongest explanation.

> Noting that competing variables stayed unchanged between two years strengthens the causal link to the one variable that did change.

## Checking the control role of sentence eight

The author rules out rival factors with <Highlight>Jumlah pertemuan dan jumlah pengajar pada tahun kedua tetap sama dengan tahun pertama</Highlight>.

With sessions and teachers held fixed, the retention jump from <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="12" /> points cleanly to the new learning sequence in sentences (5) and (6).

## Why the other four options fail

- **Workshop could not afford more sessions** guesses a reason never stated in the text.
- **Explaining fewer year-two applicants** fails because sentence (8) never discusses applicant counts.
- **Teaching method stayed unchanged** is false, since the learning sequence changed in sentence (5).
- **Comparing teacher workload** is never mentioned and misses the paragraph's causal argument.

# Answer

The author writes sentence (8) mainly **to rule out rival explanations for higher retention so the changed learning sequence stands as the strongest explanation** (*menyingkirkan penjelasan lain bagi naiknya jumlah peserta yang bertahan sehingga perubahan urutan belajar menjadi penjelasan yang paling kuat*).

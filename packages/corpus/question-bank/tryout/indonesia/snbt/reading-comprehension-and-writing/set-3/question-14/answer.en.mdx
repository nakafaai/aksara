# Given

Paragraph two holds two internal clashes: sentence (7) says crushed ice ***menghentikan*** ("stops") spoiling yet sentence (8) says **quality still drops even under ice**, and sentence (9) says vendors are ***dianjurkan*** ("advised") to wear gloves yet sentence (10) imposes a **fine** for not wearing them.

# Asked

The word-choice edits that remove **every** contradiction in paragraph two.

# Solution

Fix **both clashes together**: since fish quality still drops under ice, ice only ***memperlambat*** ("slows") spoiling rather than stopping it, and since skipping gloves triggers a fine, wearing them is ***diwajibkan*** ("required") rather than merely advised.

> When a prompt asks to fix *all* contradictions in a paragraph, scan every pair of adjacent sentences instead of stopping after the first flaw.

## Resolving both contradictions in paragraph two

Paragraph two clashes twice: <Highlight>Es serut menghentikan pembusukan</Highlight> against <Highlight>mutunya tetap menurun walaupun selalu tertutup es</Highlight>, plus <Highlight>dianjurkan memakai sarung tangan</Highlight> against <Highlight>dikenai denda untuk setiap pelanggaran</Highlight>.

| Faulty word | Clash in next sentence | Exact fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *menghentikan* in (7) | Quality still drops in (8) | ***memperlambat*** ("slows") |
| *dianjurkan* in (9) | Fined for violations in (10) | ***diwajibkan*** ("required") |

## Why the other four options fail

- ***Mengakhiri*** ("ends") keeps the exact same clash as *menghentikan*.
- ***Disarankan*** ("recommended") keeps the exact same clash as *dianjurkan*.
- Fixing **only one word** leaves the other contradiction unresolved.

# Answer

The complete fix replaces ***menghentikan* in sentence (7) with *memperlambat* and *dianjurkan* in sentence (9) with *diwajibkan*** (*menghentikan pada kalimat (7) diganti memperlambat dan dianjurkan pada kalimat (9) diganti diwajibkan*).

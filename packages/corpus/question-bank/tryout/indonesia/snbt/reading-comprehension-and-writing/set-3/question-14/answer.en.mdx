A cause-and-effect conclusion gets stronger **when other possible causes are ruled out**. If this year's rainy season were milder, fewer absences could come from the weather, not the bridge. Saying the rain and the current were as strong as in earlier years rules that out, so the bridge is the most likely cause.

So the strongest support is that this year's rainfall and current matched earlier years.

- A school bus gives another cause, so it weakens the conclusion.
- The bridge's colour has nothing to do with attendance.
- The engineer's experience does not show the bridge changed attendance.
- If pupils from other villages, who do not use the bridge, also missed school less, there is some general cause, so the conclusion weakens.

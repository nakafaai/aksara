# Given

Sentence (6) reads: *"Setiap pagi, petugas pasar memeriksa apakah pedagang sudah menaburkan ikan dagangannya dengan es serut dan apakah air bekas pencucian ikan sudah dialiri ke saluran tertutup."*

Both affixed verbs (*menaburkan* and *dialiri*) **swap the Indonesian suffixes** *-kan* and *-i*.

# Asked

The accurate affixed-verb correction for sentence (6).

# Solution

Apply the *-i* versus *-kan* object rule cleanly. **Fish** (*ikan*) receive the crushed ice, requiring ***menaburi***, whereas **wash water** (*air bekas pencucian*) is the liquid moved into the drain, requiring ***dialirkan***.

> Suffix *-i* takes the receiving surface as object (*menaburi ikan*), whereas suffix *-kan* takes the moved substance (*air dialirkan ke saluran*).

## Fixing both verb suffixes in sentence six

Sentence (6) reverses both suffixes at once: <Highlight>menaburkan ikan dagangannya dengan es serut dan apakah air bekas pencucian ikan sudah dialiri ke saluran tertutup</Highlight>.

1. *ikan dagangannya* receives crushed ice: ***menaburi ikan dagangannya dengan es serut***.
2. *air bekas pencucian ikan* is routed into the drain: ***dialirkan ke saluran tertutup***.

## Why the other four options fail

- **menaburi + dialiri** leaves the second verb wrong since moved water needs *-kan*.
- **menaburkan es serut + dialiri** fixes the first half yet keeps wrong *dialiri*.
- **menaburkan ikan + dialirkan** fixes the second half yet still scatters the fish.
- **saluran tertutup sudah dialirkan air** swaps the passive subject to the receiving drain, which would need *dialiri*.

# Answer

The correct sentence is **"Setiap pagi, petugas pasar memeriksa apakah pedagang sudah menaburi ikan dagangannya dengan es serut dan apakah air bekas pencucian ikan sudah dialirkan ke saluran tertutup."**

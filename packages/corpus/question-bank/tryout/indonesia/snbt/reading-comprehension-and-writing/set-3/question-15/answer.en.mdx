# Given

Paragraph one describes a **muddy, foul-smelling market** (sentences 1 and 2), is interrupted by sentence (3) about **cheaper fish prices than supermarkets**, and then sentence (4) says ***"Keadaan itu (That situation) drove residents to travelling vendors even though market prices were cheaper."***

# Asked

Which sentence should be deleted to improve coherence without breaking any reference in another sentence.

# Solution

Sentence (3) strays from market filth and misdirects ***Keadaan itu*** ("That situation") in sentence (4) onto cheap prices. Deleting **sentence (3)** reconnects *Keadaan itu* straight to the muddy, smelly market in (1) and (2) while breaking zero references.

> An off-topic sentence both breaks paragraph unity and hijacks the pointer phrase at the start of the next sentence.

## Checking unity and reference flow around sentence three

Sentence (3) interrupts the cause-and-effect link: <Highlight>Harga ikan di pasar itu umumnya lebih murah daripada harga di toko swalayan karena pedagang membelinya langsung dari nelayan. (4) Keadaan itu membuat banyak warga beralih</Highlight>.

Removing sentence (3) keeps the cheaper-price detail inside sentence (4) and restores *Keadaan itu* to the filthy market.

## Why the other four sentences must stay

- **Sentence (4)** supplies the backstory of residents switching to travelling vendors referenced in (12).
- **Sentence (11)** anchors *pembeli tambahan itu* ("those additional shoppers") in (12).
- **Sentence (12)** anchors *pedagang itu* ("those vendors") and *Akibatnya* ("As a result") in (13).
- **Sentence (13)** anchors *Mereka* ("They") in (14).

# Answer

The sentence that should be removed is **sentence (3)** (*kalimat (3)*).

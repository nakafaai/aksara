# Given

Five scrambled sentences (P) through (T) continue sentence (13) (submitting the proposal to the RW): (R) RW discusses the proposal at a meeting, (T) at that meeting Block A doubts clay soil drainage, and (Q) Block D objects to high cost.

Sentence (S) says the RW asks one week to answer **both objections**, and (P) says one week later **the last-mentioned objection** is answered by lending concrete molds to cut cost.

# Asked

The sequence of sentences that forms a coherent paragraph.

# Solution

The tie-breaker sits inside **(P)**: lending molds solves **cost (Block D in Q)** and calls it ***keberatan yang disebut terakhir*** ("the last-mentioned objection"). Therefore **(Q) must come after (T)**, locking the order **(R), (T), (Q), (S), (P)**.

> In paragraph ordering, check the closing sentence for sequence pins like *the last-mentioned objection* that fix the order of earlier pairs.

## Locking the full pointer chain

Sentence (P) settles the order decisively: <Highlight>Sepekan kemudian, keberatan yang disebut terakhir dijawab dengan tawaran pinjaman cetakan cincin beton dari paguyuban sehingga warga cukup membeli semen dan pasir.</Highlight>

1. **(R)** links to *pengurus RW* and *usulan itu* in (13) and introduces the meeting.
2. **(T)** opens *Dalam rapat itu* ("In that meeting") with objection one (Block A's clay soil).
3. **(Q)** gives objection two (Block D's cost) so it is the last-mentioned objection.
4. **(S)** refers to *kedua keberatan itu* ("both objections") and asks for one week.
5. **(P)** opens *Sepekan kemudian* ("A week later") and solves cost (Q).

## Why the other four orders fail

- **(R), (Q), (T), (S), (P)** and **(Q), (R), (T), (S), (P)** make Block A's clay soil the last objection, which concrete molds cannot fix.
- **(R), (T), (Q), (P), (S)** puts the answer before the request for a week.
- **(R), (T), (S), (Q), (P)** mentions *both objections* when only one has been stated.

# Answer

The coherent paragraph order is **(R), (T), (Q), (S), (P)**.

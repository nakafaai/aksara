This question asks for a conclusion that combines sentences (11) and (12). The key is **which group the two-thirds is taken from**.

Step 1: work out the extra buyers. Sentence (11) says daily buyers rose from about four hundred to about six hundred.

<BlockMath math="600 - 400 = 200" />

So there are about <InlineMath math="200" /> extra buyers.

Step 2: apply two-thirds to the right group. Sentence (12) isn't about all buyers. It's about *pembeli tambahan itu* ("those extra buyers"). So you multiply two-thirds by <InlineMath math="200" />, not by <InlineMath math="600" />.

<BlockMath math="\tfrac{2}{3} \times 200 \approx 133" />

Step 3: match it to the options. About <InlineMath math="133" /> of the daily buyers in July are people who used to buy fish from street vendors. That's more than a hundred. Check the margin too: two-thirds of the increase only drops to <InlineMath math="100" /> if the increase is just <InlineMath math="150" />, far below <InlineMath math="200" />. So even though the numbers in sentence (11) are estimates, *more than a hundred* stays safe.

Now check the other options.

- Two-thirds of all daily buyers uses the wrong group. The fraction applies to the extra buyers, not to all six hundred.
- Street vendors losing about two hundred buyers treats the whole increase as coming from them, when only two-thirds of it did. The passage also never says how many customers the vendors had before or after.
- A third of the extra buyers never having bought fish goes beyond the passage. Sentence (12) only tells you that this third didn't buy from street vendors before. They could have bought fish at a supermarket.
- The rise being caused mainly by vendors moving into the market flips cause and effect. According to sentence (13), vendors rented stalls because they had already lost customers, so their move was a result of the buyers moving.

So the correct conclusion is that in July, more than a hundred of the market's daily buyers were people who used to buy fish from street vendors.

This question asks how sentence (8) relates to sentence (7). The connector at the start of sentence (8) has to show **its link to the sentence right before it**, not the general feel of a paragraph about problems.

Step 1: read sentence (7). The homebound bus leaves at 14.00, *sedangkan* ("whereas") after-school activities don't end until 16.00. The contrast is already inside sentence (7) itself, marked by *sedangkan*.

Step 2: read sentence (8). Students in those activities have to take a motorbike taxi home again. The reason is clear: when the activities end at 16.00, the bus left two hours earlier. So sentence (8) is a direct result of the situation in sentence (7).

Step 3: pick the connector that signals a result: *Akibatnya* ("As a result"). Sentence (8) becomes *Akibatnya, siswa yang mengikuti kegiatan itu harus kembali naik ojek untuk pulang.* The *Namun* ("However") in the passage is wrong because sentence (8) doesn't go against sentence (7). Taking a motorbike taxi fits perfectly with a bus that has already left.

Now check the other options.

- *Sebaliknya* ("On the contrary") introduces a situation opposite to the one before. Sentence (8) doesn't reverse sentence (7), it carries it forward.
- *Meskipun demikian* ("Even so") says something still happens despite the previous situation. It makes the same mistake as *Namun*: students take an ojek not *despite* the schedule but *because of* it.
- *Selain itu* ("Besides that") adds another, separate point of equal weight. Sentence (8) isn't a loose extra point, it's the outcome of sentence (7).
- *Dengan kata lain* ("In other words") restates the same content in different words. Sentence (7) never mentions an ojek, so sentence (8) brings new content.

So the right connector for sentence (8) is *Akibatnya*, because that sentence states the result of the schedule in sentence (7).

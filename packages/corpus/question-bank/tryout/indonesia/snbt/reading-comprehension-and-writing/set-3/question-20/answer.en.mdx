The table shows that **theft reports fell as watch turnout rose**. But the sharpest fall, from $$7$$ to $$3$$, came from February to March, exactly when the street lamps were replaced. So the data cannot separate the effect of the watch from the effect of the lamps. A sound conclusion admits both.

So the best conclusion is that theft reports fell as more residents joined the watch, while the brighter lamps may also have played a part.

- Calling the watch the only cause ignores the lamps replaced in March.
- Calling the lamps the only cause ignores the fall from January to February, before the lamps changed.
- Reports fell after the schedule was shared, they did not rise.
- The table stops in May, so there is no basis for predicting the year's end.

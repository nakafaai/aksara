A coherent paragraph is built by following **the pointing words in each sentence**. A word like _itu_ ("that, those") or a phrase like _setelah kering_ ("once dry") always points to something already mentioned. So a sentence that contains one has to come after the sentence it points to.

First, find the opening sentence. An opener can't point to another sentence.

| Sentence | Pointing word | What it points to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (P) | _buku-buku yang basah itu_ ("those wet books") | the books splashed by waves in (Q) |
| (Q) | none | the opener |
| (R) | _setelah kering_, _buku-buku itu_ ("once dry", "those books") | the drying of the books in (P) |
| (S) | _tanda itu_ ("that mark") | the red star mark in (R) |
| (T) | _keterangan itu_ ("that note") | the note under the mark in (S) |

Only (Q) points nowhere, so (Q) opens the paragraph. It introduces the problem: books get wet from splashing waves.

Next, link the chain. (P) mentions _buku-buku yang basah itu_ and tells how the books are dried, so (P) comes right after (Q). (R) opens with _setelah kering_. Books can only be dry after they've been dried, so (R) has to follow (P). This time order is the second condition you mustn't skip. (R) introduces the star mark, then (S) mentions _tanda itu_ and introduces the note. Finally, (T) mentions _keterangan itu_, so (T) closes the paragraph.

That gives (Q), (P), (R), (S), (T). Check it backwards: every pointing word now has its referent in the sentence just before it.

Why the other orders are wrong:

- (Q), (R), (P), (S), (T) attaches _setelah kering_ straight to (Q). Then the books are lent out again before the paragraph even says they were dried.
- (Q), (P), (S), (R), (T) uses _tanda itu_ before the star mark is introduced in (R).
- (Q), (P), (R), (T), (S) uses _keterangan itu_ before the note is mentioned in (S).
- (P), (Q), (R), (S), (T) opens with _buku-buku yang basah itu_, but no wet books have been mentioned yet.

So the most coherent order is (Q), (P), (R), (S), (T).

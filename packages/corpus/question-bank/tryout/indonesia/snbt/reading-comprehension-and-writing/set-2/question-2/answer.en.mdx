The phrase _cara ini_ ("this method") points back to a method mentioned earlier. Sentence (12) also names what the method achieved: the share of the load that was never borrowed in a month fell from about a third to less than a tenth. So **the right referent has to explain that result**, not just be the method that sits closest.

Trace the sentences before sentence (12) one by one.

1. Sentence (11) says each crate is marked with a coloured ribbon. That is a method, and it's the nearest one. But a ribbon only keeps the crates from getting mixed up. It doesn't change which books are on board, so it can't make more books get borrowed.
2. Sentence (10) says the volunteers pack two crates with different contents for the two areas. The phrase _temuan itu_ ("that finding") in this sentence points back to sentences (8) and (9): adults downstream look for recipe and carpentry books, while children upstream love science comics. If each crate matches those interests, every village gets books people actually want. As a result, fewer books go unborrowed. This is the method that explains the result in sentence (12).
3. Sentence (7) describes recording the titles. Recording only produces data. That data becomes useful once it's used to pack the crates in sentence (10).

The reference chain runs from _cara ini_ in sentence (12) to sentence (10), and then from _temuan itu_ back to sentences (8) and (9).

Why the other options don't fit:

- Marking crates with ribbons is the nearest method, but it has nothing to do with which books get borrowed.
- Recording the titles stops at the start of the chain. Recording alone doesn't change what's on the boat.
- Sending more science comics to every village ignores the split between downstream and upstream. The downstream villages mostly want recipe and carpentry books.
- Cutting the number of books misreads sentence (12). What fell is the share of books nobody borrowed, not the number of books loaded.

So _cara ini_ refers to packing different crates for the downstream and upstream villages, matched to the books borrowed most in each area.

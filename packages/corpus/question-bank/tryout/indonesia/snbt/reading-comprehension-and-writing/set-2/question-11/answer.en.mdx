# Given

Sentence (5) reads: *"Nilai pembicara yang menyalahkan aturan batas waktu tujuh menit untuk setiap pidato akan dikurangi oleh juri."*

In KBBI, ***menyalahkan*** means blaming or calling something wrong, whereas ***menyalahi*** means violating or breaking a rule.

# Asked

The right affixed-word correction for sentence (5) together with its accurate reason.

# Solution

Verify **both the replacement word and its grammatical reason**. Judges deduct points for breaking the time limit, so *menyalahkan* becomes ***menyalahi*** ("violates"), where suffix *-i* marks the target rule violated, not repeated action.

> Indonesian *-kan* versus *-i* verbs can differ sharply in meaning: *menyalahkan* ("blame") versus *menyalahi* ("violate").

## Fixing the affixed verb in sentence five

Sentence (5) states <Highlight>Nilai pembicara yang menyalahkan aturan batas waktu tujuh menit untuk setiap pidato akan dikurangi oleh juri</Highlight>.

Because judges penalize speakers who break the seven-minute rule, ***menyalahi*** ("violate") replaces *menyalahkan* with the exact meaning required.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Replacing with *mempersalahkan*** fails because *mempersalahkan* still means blaming, not violating.
- **Keeping *menyalahkan*** fails because it does not mean breaking a rule.
- **Replacing with *menyalahi* because *-i* means repeated action** pairs the right word with a false reason.
- **Replacing with *mempersalahkan* because *menyalahkan* is non-standard** is doubly wrong: *menyalahkan* is standard in KBBI and *mempersalahkan* has the wrong sense.

# Answer

The right fix is ***menyalahkan* replaced with *menyalahi* because the intended meaning is that the speaker violates the rule** (*menyalahkan diganti menyalahi karena yang dimaksud adalah pembicara melanggar aturan itu*).

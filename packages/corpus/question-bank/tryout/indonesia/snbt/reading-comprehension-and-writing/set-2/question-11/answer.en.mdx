Sentence (5) reads _Nilai pembicara yang menyalahkan aturan batas waktu tujuh menit untuk setiap pidato akan dikurangi oleh juri._ Each option offers two things at once: a replacement form and a reason. A fix only counts when **both the form and the reason are right**.

Start with what the sentence means to say. The judges cut points as a penalty, and in a competition the speaker who gets penalised is the one who breaks a rule, for example by talking for more than seven minutes. So the sentence is about breaking the time-limit rule.

Now check what the word actually means. In KBBI, _menyalahkan_ means "to declare or consider wrong" or "to put the blame on someone". Read literally, the speaker loses points for thinking the rule is wrong. The meaning has shifted, so the word has to go.

The sense "to break a rule" belongs to _menyalahi_. KBBI defines _menyalahi_ as "to go against, to deviate from, not to follow (a rule)". Here the suffix _-i_ makes the rule the target of the action, the thing being broken. The fixed sentence is _Nilai pembicara yang menyalahi aturan batas waktu tujuh menit untuk setiap pidato akan dikurangi oleh juri_, a speaker who breaks the seven-minute limit loses points, and the reason is that the sentence means breaking the rule.

Check the other options.

- Switching to _mempersalahkan_ uses the right reason but the wrong form. KBBI defines _mempersalahkan_ as "to put the blame on someone" or "to consider wrong". It means the same as _menyalahkan_, so the sentence still doesn't say "break".
- Leaving sentence (5) as it is rests on the mistaken idea that _menyalahkan_ means "to break a rule". That meaning belongs to _menyalahi_, not _menyalahkan_.
- Switching to _menyalahi_ because _-i_ marks a repeated action has the right form for the wrong reason. A repeated sense does show up in words like _memukuli_ ("to hit again and again"), but _menyalahi aturan_ doesn't mean breaking a rule many times. Here _-i_ points to the thing being broken.
- Switching to _mempersalahkan_ because _menyalahkan_ is supposedly non-standard is wrong twice. _Menyalahkan_ is a standard KBBI word, and _mempersalahkan_ still means "to blame".

So the right fix is to replace _menyalahkan_ with _menyalahi_, because the sentence means that the speaker breaks the time-limit rule.

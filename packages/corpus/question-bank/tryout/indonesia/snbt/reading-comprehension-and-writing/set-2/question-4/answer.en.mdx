The main idea of a paragraph is **the point that ties all its sentences together**, not what one sentence says. The second paragraph runs from sentence (7) to sentence (12). Look at the job of each sentence first.

| Sentence | Content | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (7) | limited load, volunteers record the titles borrowed | problem and first step |
| (8) and (9) | adults downstream and children upstream borrow different books | finding from the records |
| (10) | volunteers pack different crates for the two areas | main action |
| (11) | crates get ribbons so they aren't mixed up | technical detail |
| (12) | unborrowed books drop from a third to less than a tenth | result |

All the sentences follow one line: the load is limited, borrowing data is collected, the crates are matched to it, and the result shows. The main idea has to hold the three key parts: problem, action and result. The option that holds all of them says the volunteers work around the boat's limited load by matching each crate to what readers in each area want, so fewer books travel without being borrowed.

Why the other options are wrong:

- The different tastes of adults downstream and children upstream are true according to sentences (8) and (9), but that's only the finding. The action and the result are missing.
- The coloured ribbons are only a detail from sentence (11).
- The drop in unborrowed books is only the result in sentence (12). Its cause isn't mentioned.
- The volunteers don't carry only children's favourites. Sentence (10) mentions two crates with different contents, and the downstream crate carries books for adults.

So the main idea of the second paragraph is that the volunteers work around the boat's limited load by matching each crate to what readers in each area want, so fewer books travel without being borrowed.

The committee's conclusion is in sentence (12): the new way of announcing the motion makes speakers respond to their opponents directly more often. Its only evidence is the figure in sentences (10) and (11), which rose from <InlineMath math="40\%" /> last year to <InlineMath math="65\%" /> this year. The statement that weakens this most gives **another explanation for the rise**, one that would push the figure up even if the new format did nothing.

Sentence (13) already shuts some alternatives down: the schools, the number of rounds and the judges were the same in both competitions. So any alternative still open has to be about something that changed between the two years.

The statement about how the percentage was counted points to exactly that kind of change. Last year the figure came from the judges' free-form notes. Free notes easily miss responses that really happened, so <InlineMath math="40\%" /> may be too low. This year every score sheet has a required column, so every response gets recorded. Part of the jump to <InlineMath math="65\%" /> could therefore come from the new way of recording, not from the new way of announcing the motion. The two figures can't be compared directly any more, and the committee's evidence falls apart with them.

The other statements don't weaken the conclusion.

- A preference the judges have had for a long time can't explain a change. Sentence (13) says the judges were the same in both years, so that preference affected last year's figure and this year's figure in the same way.
- Teams with coach-written scripts responding least often actually supports the committee. Once scripts can't be prepared, it makes sense that direct responses go up.
- Complaints that thirty minutes is too short attack how comfortable the rule is, not the link between the rule and the rise.
- A percentage counted from every speech in every round in both years makes sure both figures come from comparable groups of speeches. That removes a doubt, so the committee's conclusion gets stronger.

So what weakens the committee's conclusion most is that last year the percentage came from the judges' free notes, while this year every score sheet has a required column on direct responses to the opponent.

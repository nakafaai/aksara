# Given

Three sentences in the used-oil passage are tested: sentence (2) joins two separate habits with *sehingga* ("so that"), sentence (4) transitions to drains with *adapun* ("as for"), and sentence (5) starts with preposition *Bagi* ("For"), leaving the verb without a subject.

# Asked

Whether each of the four proposed sentence edits is **Tepat** ("Correct") or **Tidak tepat** ("Incorrect").

# Solution

Read each edited sentence in full. Sentence (2) needs **comma + *sedangkan*** ("whereas") for two contrasting habits, and sentence (5) regains its subject only by **deleting *bagi***, never by adding a comma.

> Putting a comma after a prepositional phrase never creates a missing subject, whereas removing the preposition restores the subject.

## Testing all four sentence edits

The originals read <Highlight>Sebagian jelantah itu dipakai lagi untuk menggoreng sehingga sisanya dibuang ke selokan.</Highlight> and <Highlight>Bagi warga yang menyetor jelantah ke bank sampah kelurahan mendapat imbalan</Highlight>.

| Proposed edit | Verdict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Replace *sehingga* with *, sedangkan* in (2) | **Tepat** | Contrasts two distinct habits properly under EYD V |
| Replace *adapun* with *akibatnya,* in (4) | **Tidak tepat** | Dumping oil in drains is not an effect of reheating oil |
| Add a comma after *kelurahan* in (5) | **Tidak tepat** | Keeping *bagi* still leaves *mendapat* without a subject |
| Delete *bagi* in sentence (5) | **Tepat** | Makes *Warga yang menyetor...* the clear subject of *mendapat* |

# Answer

Proposals **one and four** are **Tepat**, while proposals **two and three** are **Tidak tepat**.

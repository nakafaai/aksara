This question has two conditions. The chosen word must **really carry the wrong meaning in its sentence**, and its replacement must make the sentence say what the text intends. So check the original word first, then check the replacement.

Start with sentence (7): _Lapisan minyak di bagian atas kemudian dijual ke sebuah pabrik yang mengelolanya menjadi biodiesel_ ("The top layer of oil is then sold to a factory that _mengelolanya_ into biodiesel"). According to KBBI, _mengelola_ means to control, run or manage, as in managing a company or a project. It doesn't mean turning a material into something else. But the factory in sentence (7) turns the oil into biodiesel. For that meaning, the right word is _mengolah_ ("to process"), which means working a material so it becomes a different product or a better one. So _mengelolanya_ should become _mengolahnya_: _pabrik yang mengolahnya menjadi biodiesel_ ("a factory that processes it into biodiesel").

Now check the other proposals one by one.

- _Mengurusnya_ ("manages it") does replace the faulty word, but _mengurus_ means the same as _mengelola_. A factory that manages oil into biodiesel still makes no sense. The mistake just moves to another word.
- _Menyetor_ ("to hand in") in sentence (5) is already right. _Menitipkan_ means leaving something with someone for safekeeping, so you'd collect it again later. That clashes with the payment per litre the residents receive.
- _Berulang kali_ ("again and again") in sentence (3) is already right, because sentence (2) is about used oil being reused. _Terlalu lama_ ("for too long") shifts the meaning to one long heating, so the link to the habit of reusing oil disappears.
- _Dijernihkan_ ("made to look clear") in sentence (13) is already right. _Dimurnikan_ ("purified") means cleaned of what's mixed in, as if the oil became safe. That weakens the writer's health warning in sentence (14).

So the word whose meaning doesn't fit is _mengelolanya_ in sentence (7), and the right replacement is _mengolahnya_.

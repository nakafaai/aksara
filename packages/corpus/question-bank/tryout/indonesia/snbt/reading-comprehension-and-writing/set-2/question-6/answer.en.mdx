# Given

Sentence (11) states travelling collectors accept used oil as is, **including oil still mixed with water** and frying crumbs.

Sentence (8) explains that **water in oil can turn part of the oil into soap** during biodiesel production.

# Asked

The most valid conclusion about used oil bought by travelling collectors.

# Solution

Join the two cross-paragraph facts from **sentence (11)** and **sentence (8)**. Because collectors' oil can still contain water, turning it straight into biodiesel without removing water risks turning part of it into soap.

> A sound cross-paragraph inference preserves the passage's hedges (*part*, *can/risk*) instead of inflating them into absolutes.

## Linking paragraph two and paragraph three

The two facts connect directly: <Highlight>menerima jelantah apa adanya, termasuk yang masih bercampur air</Highlight> ("accept used oil as is, including oil still mixed with water") and <Highlight>air dalam minyak dapat membuat sebagian minyak berubah menjadi sabun dalam proses pembuatan biodiesel</Highlight> ("water in oil can turn part of the oil into soap during biodiesel production").

Combining them supports one exact conclusion: **if turned straight into biodiesel without separating its water first, part of that oil risks turning into soap**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Paying more because they skip filtering** guesses an unstated pricing reason.
- **Containing more water than waste-bank oil** invents a comparison absent from the text.
- **Biodiesel plant rejecting collectors' oil** is never mentioned, as sentence (12) says the destination is unknown.
- **Definitely clarified and resold** inflates *sebagian* ("some") and *diduga* ("suspected") in sentence (13) into certainty.

# Answer

The best conclusion is that **if made directly into biodiesel without first separating its water, part of that oil risks turning into soap** (*Jika langsung dibuat menjadi biodiesel tanpa dipisahkan airnya lebih dulu, sebagian jelantah itu berisiko berubah menjadi sabun.*).

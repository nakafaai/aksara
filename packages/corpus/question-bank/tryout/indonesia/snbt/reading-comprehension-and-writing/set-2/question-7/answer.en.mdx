You can see the purpose of a sentence from **its role among the sentences that follow it**, not only from what it says. So read sentence (10) together with sentences (11) to (14).

First, look at the content and the signal in sentence (10). It says the programme doesn't reach every food stall yet because some stall owners sell their used oil to roaming collectors who pay more. The word _memang_ ("admittedly, it's true that") signals that the writer is admitting something that doesn't help their own view. This move is called a **concession**: you admit a weakness first, then make your main point.

Next, see where the paragraph goes after that.

- Sentence (11): the collectors take used oil as it is.
- Sentence (12): the collectors never say where the oil goes.
- Sentence (13): some of the oil is suspected of being resold as loose cooking oil.
- Sentence (14): if that suspicion is true, the slightly higher price is paid dearly with the health of people who buy fried snacks.

Sentences (11) to (14) expose the downside of that higher price. So sentence (10) admits the programme's shortcoming and then serves as the stepping stone for the writer to show the risk behind the collectors' price.

Why the other options are wrong:

- The writer doesn't call for stopping the programme. Sentence (14) actually shows that selling to collectors does more harm.
- Sentence (10) doesn't explain why the collectors pay more. Reselling as loose cooking oil only appears in sentence (13), and even there it's a suspicion, not the reason for the price.
- No sentence suggests raising the payment. The writer actually treats the price gap as not worth the risk.
- _Belum menjangkau semua warung_ ("doesn't reach every stall yet") only means some stalls haven't joined. It says nothing about how many have joined, let alone most of them.

So the writer's purpose in sentence (10) is to admit the programme's shortcoming before showing the risk behind the roaming collectors' higher price.

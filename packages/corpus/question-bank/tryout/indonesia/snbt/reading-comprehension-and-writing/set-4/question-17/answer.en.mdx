# Given

Five closing sentences for the *cascara* passage are scrambled: (R) a city café first orders ten packs, (Q) **that first order** sells out in three days, (T) **that café then** orders five times as much, (S) **that surge** overwhelms the drying racks, and (P) **to solve it**, the group recruits two neighbouring farms.

# Asked

The sentence sequence that produces a coherent closing paragraph.

# Solution

Follow the backward pointers from the standalone opener **(R)** to the closing solution **(P)**: initial order (R): sold out (Q): fivefold reorder (T): surge overwhelms racks (S): solution recruiting neighbours (P).

> Sequential pointers (*that first order*, *that surge*, *to solve it*) lock each sentence directly behind the event it names.

## Chaining all five closing sentences in order

Sentence (S) requires a sharp order increase right before it: <Highlight>Lonjakan itu membuat kelompok kewalahan karena para-para mereka hanya cukup untuk kulit dari panen sendiri</Highlight>.

1. **(R)** introduces *a city café* and its initial ten-pack order.
2. **(Q)** follows *that first order* selling out in three days.
3. **(T)** introduces the fivefold jump in orders the next month.
4. **(S)** names that fivefold jump *lonjakan itu* ("that surge") causing overload.
5. **(P)** answers that overload with *untuk mengatasinya* ("to overcome it").

## Why the other four sequences fail

- **R - T - Q - S - P** places *that surge* after selling out instead of after the fivefold jump.
- **Q - R - T - S - P** opens with *that first order* before any order was mentioned.
- **R - Q - S - T - P** places *that surge* before orders ever increased.
- **R - Q - T - P - S** puts *to solve it* before the problem appears.

# Answer

The coherent sequence is **R - Q - T - S - P**.

A main idea is one statement that every sentence in the paragraph explains. A detail that shows up in only one sentence, or an idea from another paragraph, isn't this paragraph's main idea.

Look at what each sentence in the second paragraph does.

1. Sentence (4) introduces a habit of the volunteer team that at first looks excessive.
2. Sentence (5) spells the habit out: pushing a pipe in, spraying water and monitoring the temperature, even though the fire on the surface is out.
3. Sentence (7) explains how the temperature is measured.
4. Sentence (8) says when they leave: once the temperature at every measuring point equals that of the peat around it.

All of those sentences explain one thing: the team **doesn't leave until it's sure the fire inside the peat is out**, and that certainty comes from wetting and measuring. The statement that holds the habit, the method and the aim together is the main idea.

The other statements hold only a part, or miss.

- Measuring temperature with a soil thermometer is just the content of sentence (7).
- Spraying water until the lower layer is wet is just one of the three activities in sentence (5).
- Fire that can flare up again is the first paragraph's idea. The second paragraph opens with _oleh karena itu_ ("that's why"), which means it holds the action that grows out of that idea.
- Sentence (4) says the habit _mula-mula tampak_ ("at first seems") excessive. The word _tampak_ ("seems") marks a first impression, and the rest of the paragraph shows the habit is well founded.

So the main idea is that the volunteer team only leaves burned land after making sure, by wetting the peat and measuring its temperature, that the fire inside the peat is truly out.

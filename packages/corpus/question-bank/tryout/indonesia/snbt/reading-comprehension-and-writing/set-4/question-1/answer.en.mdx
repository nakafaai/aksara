# Given

Sentence (13) reports that on the windswept **eastern slope**, <InlineMath math="40" /> of <InlineMath math="60" /> air-layered trees fell, while only <InlineMath math="2" /> of <InlineMath math="30" /> grafted trees fell.

Sentence (14) adds that on the **sheltered western slope**, almost no air-layered or grafted trees fell during that windy season.

# Asked

The author's main purpose in adding sentence (14) to the passage.

# Solution

Compare both slopes side by side: without strong wind (western slope), both tree types survive alike. Only under strong wind (eastern slope) does the grafted seedlings' taproot advantage stand out. Sentence (14) clarifies **the condition under which that advantage appears**.

> Adding a sheltered baseline highlights the exact condition required for an advantage to become visible.

## Contrasting the eastern and western slopes

Sentence (14) sets up the contrast directly: <Highlight>Di lereng barat yang terlindung bukit, hampir tidak ada pohon cangkok ataupun pohon sambungan yang tumbang pada musim angin itu</Highlight>.

Because strong wind is the sole difference between the two slopes, sentence (14) shows the grafted trees' edge in sentence (13) emerges specifically in windy sites.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Refuting grafted trees' resistance** misreads the contrast, which actually sharpens wind's role.
- **Taproots do not matter** fails because taproots were simply untested on the calm western slope.
- **Western air-layered trees are stronger** mistakes hill shelter for stronger roots.
- **Unsuitable site for air-layered trees** flips the slopes, since the western slope is safe for them.

# Answer

The author adds sentence (14) mainly **to show that the grafted seedlings' advantage in sentence (13) appears only in places hit by strong wind** (*menunjukkan bahwa keunggulan bibit sambungan pada kalimat (13) baru tampak di tempat yang diterpa angin kencang*).

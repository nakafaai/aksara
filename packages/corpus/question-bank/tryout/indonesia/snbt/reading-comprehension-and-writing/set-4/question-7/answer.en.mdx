A proposal is right when two things hold: the original sentence really has a problem, and the proposal **fixes it without creating a new one**. Check both for every proposal.

**First proposal**: Right. Sentence (8) uses the passive form with a pronoun agent, _mereka tinggalkan_ ("they leave", literally "by them left"). In this form the agent has to sit directly in front of the verb, and words like _akan_ ("will"), _sudah_ ("already") or _belum_ ("not yet") go before the agent. The pattern is: adverb, then agent, then verb. So _akan mereka tinggalkan_ is correct, while _mereka akan tinggalkan_ slips _akan_ into the wrong place.

**Second proposal**: Right. The reduplicated form _bahu-membahu_ ("shoulder to shoulder") already means helping one another. Adding _saling_ ("each other") in front repeats the same meaning. With _saling_ removed, the sentence is still complete: _para relawan bahu-membahu menyekat parit-parit kecil_.

**Third proposal**: Not right. The reason given is wrong. The word _relawan_ ("volunteer") isn't plural by itself, since _seorang relawan_ ("a volunteer") is fine too. It's _para_ that marks there being many volunteers, and _para relawan_ is the correct form. What would be excessive is _para relawan-relawan_, where the plural is marked twice. This trap feels right because it looks like the second proposal, but here no meaning is repeated.

**Fourth proposal**: Not right. Sentence (11) compares two things: damp peat is _lebih sulit terbakar daripada_ ("harder to burn than") dry peat. A comparison takes _daripada_ ("than"). The word _dari_ ("from") is for origin, as in _relawan dari sebuah desa_ ("volunteers from a village"). Sentence (11) is already correct, so this proposal would damage it.

So the first and second proposals are right, while the third and fourth aren't.

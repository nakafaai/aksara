# Given

The coffee *cascara* passage has **one grammatical flaw per target sentence**: sentence (11) (*Kulit yang dijemur...*) lacks a predicate due to *yang*, sentence (13) (*baik pada pagi hari dan sore hari*) breaks the paired conjunction, and sentence (14) (*Menurut ketua kelompok mengatakan bahwa...*) mixes two patterns leaving *mengatakan* subjectless.

# Asked

Whether each of the four proposed edits for sentences (11), (13), and (14) is **Tepat** ("Correct") or **Tidak tepat** ("Incorrect").

# Solution

Verify whether each edit fixes the real error. Changing *dan* to ***maupun*** (*baik ... maupun*) in (13) and deleting ***menurut*** in (14) fix the exact flaws, while swapping *agar*/*supaya* in (11) and deleting *bahwa* in (14) leave both syntax defects untouched.

> Pair *baik* strictly with *maupun*, and never blend *Menurut X, ...* ("According to X,") with *X mengatakan bahwa ...* ("X says that...").

## Checking all four edits on the cascara passage

The originals read <Highlight>Kulit yang dijemur di atas para-para bambu agar tidak bercampur</Highlight>, <Highlight>baik pada pagi hari dan sore hari</Highlight>, and <Highlight>Menurut ketua kelompok mengatakan bahwa</Highlight>.

| Proposed edit | Verdict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Replace *dan* with *maupun* in (13) | **Tepat** | Restores the standard pair *baik ... maupun ...* |
| Replace *agar* with *supaya* in (11) | **Tidak tepat** | The real error in (11) is *yang*, not *agar* |
| Delete *bahwa* in (14) | **Tidak tepat** | *Menurut ... mengatakan* still lacks a subject |
| Delete *menurut* in (14) | **Tepat** | Restores *Ketua kelompok* as subject of *mengatakan* |

# Answer

Proposals **one and four** are **Tepat**, while proposals **two and three** are **Tidak tepat**.

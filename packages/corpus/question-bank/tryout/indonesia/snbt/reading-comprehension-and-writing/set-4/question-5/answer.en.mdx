# Given

The passage states **sentence (10) on uniform-seedling risk**: *"Jika seluruh bibit berasal dari satu pohon induk, satu jenis penyakit dapat merusak semuanya sekaligus."* ("If all seedlings come from one parent tree, one disease can destroy them all at once.")

# Asked

Which single-word substitution does **not** change the meaning of sentence (10).

# Solution

Check all five semantic pieces in sentence (10): condition (*jika*), totality (*seluruh*), possibility (*dapat*), damage (*merusak*), and simultaneity (*sekaligus*). Only replacing ***seluruh*** with ***semua*** ("all") keeps every piece unchanged.

> A valid meaning-preserving swap must keep modality, timing, and conditional force intact.

## Testing all five word swaps in sentence ten

Sentence (10) reads <Highlight>Jika seluruh bibit berasal dari satu pohon induk, satu jenis penyakit dapat merusak semuanya sekaligus</Highlight>.

Swapping *seluruh bibit* to ***semua bibit*** keeps the exact universal scope with zero shift in nuance.

## Why the other four swaps shift meaning

- ***Dapat* ("can"): *akan* ("will")** inflates possibility into certainty.
- ***Sekaligus* ("all at once"): *berturut-turut* ("one after another")** changes simultaneous loss to sequential loss.
- ***Merusak* ("destroy"): *menyerang* ("attack")** drops the resulting damage.
- ***Jika* ("if"): *karena* ("because")** turns a hypothetical into reality, clashing with sentence (8)'s cap.

# Answer

The substitution that leaves sentence (10)'s meaning unchanged is **replacing *seluruh* with *semua*** (*kata seluruh diganti dengan semua*).

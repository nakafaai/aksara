A coherent paragraph has two marks: every sentence adds something new, and every reference word finds what it points to straight away. The sentence to remove is the one that **breaks both marks**.

Check sentence (6): _Api di bawah permukaan gambut memang dapat memunculkan nyala baru setelah api di atasnya padam._ ("Fire below the peat surface can indeed start new flames after the fire above it is out.")

- Its content is already in the first paragraph. Sentence (2) says fire creeps inside the peat layer, and sentence (3) says land that looks extinguished can flare up again. Sentence (6) adds nothing.
- Sentence (7) opens with _suhu itu_ ("that temperature"). Its referent is _suhu gambut_ ("the peat's temperature") at the end of sentence (5). Sentence (6) stands between them and doesn't mention temperature at all, so _suhu itu_ loses its grip.

Try reading without sentence (6): _... dan memantau suhu gambut selama sedikitnya tiga hari. Suhu itu diukur dengan termometer tanah ..._ The reference connects directly.

The other four options point to sentences that are still needed, or give a wrong reason.

- Sentence (4) introduces the habit that the following sentences spell out. _Mula-mula tampak berlebihan_ ("at first seems excessive") doesn't clash with the paragraph, because the paragraph explains why that impression is wrong.
- Sentence (5) is the core of the paragraph. Three activities in one sentence isn't a fault, as long as their forms are parallel.
- Sentence (7) explains how the temperature in sentence (5) is monitored, and _titik ukur_ ("measuring point") in sentence (8) leans on it.
- Sentence (8) doesn't repeat sentence (5). Sentence (5) says what's done, while sentence (8) says when the work counts as finished.

So the sentence to remove is sentence (6), because it repeats the first paragraph and cuts _suhu itu_ in sentence (7) off from its referent.

# Given

Paragraph one (sentences 2 and 3) already explains that **subsurface peat fire can reignite** after the surface looks out.

In paragraph two, sentence (5) ends with ***pemantauan suhu gambut*** ("monitoring peat temperature"), sentence (6) repeats subsurface reignition, and sentence (7) opens with ***Suhu itu*** ("That temperature").

# Asked

Which sentence should be removed from paragraph two for coherence, along with the right reason.

# Solution

Sentence (6) fails two coherence rules at once: it **repeats paragraph one** with zero new facts, and it **wedges between *suhu gambut* in (5) and *Suhu itu* in (7)**, severing the reference. Deleting sentence (6) restores direct flow.

> An intrusive mid-paragraph sentence shows two clear signs: repeating an earlier paragraph and breaking a *that X* pointer in the next sentence.

## Checking sentence six between sentences five and seven

Sentence (6) cuts straight across the temperature link: <Highlight>suhu gambut selama sedikitnya tiga hari. (6) Api di bawah permukaan gambut memang dapat memunculkan nyala baru setelah api di atasnya padam. (7) Suhu itu diukur dengan termometer tanah</Highlight>.

Removing sentence (6) reconnects *Suhu itu* immediately to *suhu gambut* in sentence (5).

## Why the other four sentences must remain

- **Sentence (4)** introduces the habit, and *at first seems* fits the explanation that follows.
- **Sentence (5)** supplies the core steps, and listing three parallel actions is valid.
- **Sentence (7)** details the temperature check anchoring *titik ukur* ("measuring points") in (8).
- **Sentence (8)** defines when the job ends rather than repeating (5).

# Answer

Remove **sentence (6), because it repeats paragraph one and separates *suhu itu* in sentence (7) from its referent** (*kalimat (6), karena mengulang isi paragraf pertama dan memisahkan suhu itu pada kalimat (7) dari rujukannya*).

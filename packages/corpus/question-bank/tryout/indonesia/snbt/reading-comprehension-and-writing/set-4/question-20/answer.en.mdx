_Keberatan itu_ ("that objection") points to an objection already laid out. To find it, you need two answers from sentence (8): **who** objects, and **why**.

Start with who. Sentence (8) says the request was turned down because _mereka_ ("they") are paid by the weight of the cherries picked. Sentence (7) mentions two groups of people: the pickers and the committee members. The word _mereka_ sits closer to _para pengurus_ ("the committee members"), but the ones paid by the weight picked are the pickers. The committee members only weigh. So _mereka_ means the pickers.

Now the why. Sentence (8) builds it like this: pay is worked out from the weight of the cherries, so sorting means slower work and less pay. Their objection is **extra work that cuts into their earnings**.

Sentence (9) confirms the chain. The objection only went away once fully red cherries were paid more per kilogram. The higher price makes up for the pay lost to slower work.

The other options miss on one of those two questions.

- The group leader is the one making the request, not the one objecting.
- The committee members come from reading _mereka_ onto the nearest word. The passage mentions no objection from them.
- Fully red cherries being hard to find isn't in the passage.
- Pay per kilogram being too low is the closest trap, because the way out really was raising the price per kilogram. But the pickers didn't complain about the rate. They complained about sorting, which lowers how much they pick.

So _keberatan itu_ refers to the pickers' objection that sorting the cherries slows their work and so cuts their pay.

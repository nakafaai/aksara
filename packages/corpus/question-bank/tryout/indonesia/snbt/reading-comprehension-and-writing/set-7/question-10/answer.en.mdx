# Given

Sentence (8) of the *sign-language* passage makes a sweeping claim by the subdistrict head: <Highlight>kini semua pekerja di wilayah kelurahan itu sudah dapat melayani warga tuli</Highlight> (now all workers in the subdistrict can already serve deaf residents).

# Asked

The statement that most strongly weakens that conclusion.

# Solution

Challenge **the universal claim *all workers* by showing a huge gap in coverage**. If only thirty people have taken the class while thousands work in the subdistrict, the vast majority of workers have never been trained, directly undermining the claim about *all* workers.

> A sweeping claim using *all* is weakened most decisively by showing that the trained sample is only a tiny fraction of the population.

## Evaluating all five statements

| Candidate statement | Effect on the claim about *all workers* | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only thirty people joined the class while thousands work in the subdistrict | Shows almost all workers remain untrained | **Weakens most** |
| Deaf volunteers teach patiently | Says nothing about how many workers attended | **Irrelevant** |
| Many participants find the class enjoyable | Reflects feelings of attendees, not total reach | **Irrelevant** |
| The hall also runs a Sunday writing class | Unrelated to sign-language service | **Irrelevant** |
| Participants learn finger-spelling | Merely repeats a detail from sentence (4) | **Irrelevant** |

# Answer

The statement that weakens the conclusion most is ***Peserta kelas bahasa isyarat baru berjumlah tiga puluh orang, sedangkan pekerja di wilayah kelurahan itu ribuan orang***. (Only thirty people have attended the class, whereas thousands work in that subdistrict).

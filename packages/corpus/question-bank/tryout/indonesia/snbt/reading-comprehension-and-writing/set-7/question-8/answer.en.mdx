# Given

Sentence (6) says participants once felt unsure when meeting <Highlight>pengunjung tuli di tempat kerja mereka</Highlight> (deaf visitors at their workplaces), and sentence (7) adds that they can now greet and ask what *pengunjung itu* (those visitors) need.

# Asked

The referent of *pengunjung itu* (those visitors) in sentence (7).

# Solution

Trace **the demonstrative *itu* back to its nearest matching noun phrase** in sentence (6). The phrase *pengunjung itu* directly refers to **the deaf visitors participants meet at their workplaces**.

> A demonstrative phrase such as *pengunjung itu* points back to the exact noun phrase just introduced in the preceding sentence.

## Checking all five candidate referents

| Candidate referent | Role in the passage | Match for *pengunjung itu* | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deaf visitors participants meet at their workplaces | Introduced directly in sentence (6) | Exact antecedent | **Correct** |
| Saturday visitors to the community hall | Never mentioned in the passage | Absent from text | **Wrong** |
| Deaf volunteers teaching the class | Instructors from sentence (2) | Teachers, not workplace visitors | **Wrong** |
| Local subdistrict head | Quoted speaker in sentences (5) and (8) | Official, not a visitor | **Wrong** |
| Students taking the sign-language class | Participants listed in sentence (3) | Learners, not the visitors served | **Wrong** |

# Answer

The phrase *pengunjung itu* refers to **the deaf visitors whom participants meet at their workplaces** (*pengunjung tuli yang ditemui peserta di tempat kerja mereka*).

# Given

The *indigo-batik* passage links **river pollution** directly to synthetic dye runoff in sentence (2), then notes in sentence (8) that natural *daun tom* soaking water does not pollute the river and serves as garden fertilizer.

# Asked

The most likely outcome if every artisan in Desa Sidoluhur switches to *daun tom* dye.

# Solution

Follow **the explicit cause-and-effect chain** stated in sentences (2) and (8), rejecting predictions that contradict the text or invent new market shifts.

> A valid inference extends the passage's stated cause-and-effect link: removing the sole stated source of pollution makes the river cleaner.

## Linking the pollution source and its clean replacement

Sentence (2) names the cause: <Highlight>sisa air pewarna itu dibuang ke sungai sehingga air sungai berwarna gelap dan berbau</Highlight> (leftover synthetic dye darkened and fouled the river).

Sentence (8) confirms the replacement is clean: <Highlight>Sisa air rendaman daun tom juga tidak mencemari sungai sebab dapat dipakai sebagai pupuk bagi kebun warga</Highlight> (it does not pollute the river and becomes garden fertilizer).

| Option | Alignment with passage evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| River water becomes cleaner | Synthetic runoff stops while natural runoff is non-polluting | **Most likely** |
| Synthetic batik price doubles | Unsupported, sentence (7) says natural batik sells at double price | **Unsupported** |
| Making batik becomes faster | Contradicts sentence (5), natural dyeing takes much longer | **Contradicts text** |
| Tourists stop visiting | Contradicts sentence (7), tourists actively seek natural batik | **Contradicts text** |
| Gardens run short of fertilizer | Contradicts sentence (8), soaking water adds fertilizer | **Contradicts text** |

# Answer

The most likely outcome is that **the river water in Desa Sidoluhur becomes cleaner** (*Air sungai di Desa Sidoluhur menjadi lebih bersih*).

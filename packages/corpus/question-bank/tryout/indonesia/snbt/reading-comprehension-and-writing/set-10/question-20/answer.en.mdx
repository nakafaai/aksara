# Given

The *running-club* table shows **average run times improving every month** from <InlineMath math="34" /> to <InlineMath math="29" /> minutes, while **attendance briefly dipped in April** from <InlineMath math="30" /> to <InlineMath math="28" /> because of school exams.

# Asked

The best conclusion supported by both the passage and the table.

# Solution

Capture **both table columns faithfully without hiding April's dip or guessing the outcome of June's race**. Average run time improved steadily throughout regular training, even though attendance dipped in April.

> A sound data conclusion reflects both series accurately and avoids speculating about future events.

## Checking all five candidate conclusions

| Candidate conclusion | Evidence check against text and table | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Average times kept improving with regular training, even though attendance dipped in April | Matches every table row plus sentences (5) and (6) | **Correct** |
| Attendance rose every month since January | False because attendance fell in April (<InlineMath math="30" /> to <InlineMath math="28" />) | **Wrong** |
| Members taking exams ran more slowly | They skipped practice rather than running slower | **Wrong** |
| The club is guaranteed to win the provincial race | The June race has not happened yet | **Wrong** |
| Average time stayed unchanged since January | False because it improved from <InlineMath math="34" /> to <InlineMath math="29" /> minutes | **Wrong** |

# Answer

The best conclusion is that **members' average time kept improving during regular training, even though the number of members attending practice dipped in April** (*Rata-rata waktu anggota terus membaik selama latihan rutin, meskipun jumlah anggota yang ikut latihan sempat berkurang pada bulan April*).

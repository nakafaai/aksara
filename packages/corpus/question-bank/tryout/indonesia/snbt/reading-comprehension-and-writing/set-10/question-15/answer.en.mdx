# Given

Sentences (3), (4), and (6) of the *larva-monitors* passage detail **the monitors' preventive routine**: checking water containers weekly, draining any with larvae, and catching larvae even in <Highlight>tempat minum burung yang jarang diganti airnya</Highlight> (a bird water dish whose water was rarely changed).

# Asked

The most likely outcome if the monitors stop checking water containers.

# Solution

Follow **the direct consequence of stopping container checks and draining**. Because larvae quickly appear in standing water that is rarely changed, stopping inspections means **mosquito larvae can breed again in undrained water containers**.

> Removing an active preventive step directly allows the targeted hazard to re-emerge.

## Evaluating all five predicted outcomes

| Proposed outcome | Support in the passage | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mosquito larvae can breed again in undrained water containers | Direct effect of halting inspections and draining in sentences (3), (4), and (6) | **Correct** |
| Monitoring cards become more complete | Without checks, no new findings get recorded | **Wrong** |
| Dengue cases vanish immediately | Opposite of stopping a prevention program | **Wrong** |
| The rainy season grows shorter | Weather does not depend on larva monitors | **Wrong** |
| Health volunteers stop reporting to the clinic | Nothing in the text suggests reports would stop | **Wrong** |

# Answer

The most likely outcome is that **mosquito larvae can breed again in water containers that are not drained** (*Jentik nyamuk dapat kembali berkembang di wadah air yang tidak dikuras*).

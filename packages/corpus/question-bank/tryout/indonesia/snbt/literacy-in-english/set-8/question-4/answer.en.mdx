The passage notes that night singing "had often been blamed on street lighting". Finding that **daytime noise predicted it better** puts a rival explanation ahead of the usual one. Robins may sing at night to avoid daytime noise rather than because they are confused by light.

- A better prediction from noise does not prove light has no effect at all, and the study concerned robins only.
- Robins' eyesight is not discussed.
- The study found robins in noisy places were "more likely" to sing at night, not that they sang only at night everywhere.
- The study does not compare Britain with other countries.

So it matters because it challenges the common explanation that lighting causes night singing.

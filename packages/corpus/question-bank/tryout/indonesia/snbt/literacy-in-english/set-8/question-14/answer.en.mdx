# Given

The table reports Harbour (<InlineMath math="20{,}000" /> guests, <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> answers, <InlineMath math="950" /> positive) and Station (<InlineMath math="30{,}000" /> guests, <InlineMath math="6{,}000" /> answers, <InlineMath math="5{,}490" /> positive), with <InlineMath math="92\%" /> advertised for **guests who answered**.

# Asked

Which conclusions can be validly drawn from the passage and the table?

# Solution

> A hotel chain advertises that <InlineMath math="92\%" /> of guests who answered its online survey last year rated their stay "good" or "excellent".

Check each statement against the **correct column** (guests, answers, or positive ratings).

## Testing all five conclusions

Compute each hotel's response rate and the worst-case chain-wide positive rate:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Response rates:} &\quad \text{Station } \frac{6{,}000}{30{,}000} = 20\%, \quad \text{Harbour } \frac{1{,}000}{20{,}000} = 5\% \\ \text{Worst-case all-guest positive rate:} &\quad \frac{6{,}440}{50{,}000} = 12.88\% \approx 13\% \end{aligned}" />

| Candidate conclusion | Data check | Valid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Station guests four times as likely to answer as Harbour guests | <InlineMath math="20\% \div 5\% = 4" /> | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="92\%" /> is a share of answers, not all guests | Advert states <Highlight>of guests who answered</Highlight> | **Yes** |
| Harbour rests on more answers than Station | Harbour <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> vs Station <InlineMath math="6{,}000" /> | **No** |
| If all non-answerers were unhappy, about <InlineMath math="13\%" /> positive | <InlineMath math="6{,}440 / 50{,}000 \approx 13\%" /> | **Yes** |
| Station had fewer guests than Harbour | Station <InlineMath math="30{,}000" /> vs Harbour <InlineMath math="20{,}000" /> | **No** |

Both false choices simply **reverse the two hotels** in the table: Station had more guests (<InlineMath math="30{,}000" /> vs <InlineMath math="20{,}000" />) and far more answers (<InlineMath math="6{,}000" /> vs <InlineMath math="1{,}000" />).

# Answer

The conclusions that can be drawn are:

- Station's guests were four times as likely as Harbour's guests to answer the survey.
- The <InlineMath math="92\%" /> is a share of answers, not a share of guests.
- If every guest who did not answer had been unhappy, about <InlineMath math="13\%" /> of guests would have rated their stay good or excellent.

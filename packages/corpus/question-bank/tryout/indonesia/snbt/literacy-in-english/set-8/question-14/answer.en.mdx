Injuries per 10,000 trips fell steadily: 4.0, about 3.3, 2.5, and 2.0. The number of injuries rose until month 3, but trips rose faster, so **the risk per trip fell**. The official's notes, however, give rival explanations, such as riders' growing experience, and the injury count covers only two hospitals. The fall is clear, its **causes are not**.

- Injuries went from 14 to 18 after the change, not halved.
- The injury rate fell in month 3, from about 3.3 to 2.5.
- The rate per trip fell, so scooters did not become more dangerous per trip.
- Trips kept rising after the speed limit was lowered.

So the best-supported conclusion is that injuries per trip fell over the pilot, though the reasons are not yet clear.

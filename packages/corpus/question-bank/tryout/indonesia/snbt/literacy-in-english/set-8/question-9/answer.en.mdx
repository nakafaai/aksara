# Given

Text B blames **exempt ride-hailing cars driving empty between passengers** for average centre speeds falling almost back to their pre-charge level.

# Asked

Would each of the five findings, if true, **weaken** Text B's explanation (**Yes** or **No**)?

# Solution

> Meanwhile ride-hailing cars spend much of their time driving empty between passengers, so average speeds in the centre have fallen almost back to where they were before the charge.

A finding weakens Text B's causal claim if it shows the **slowdown occurring without ride-hailing cars** or introduces a **rival cause** over the same two-year period.

## Testing each finding against the stated cause

| Finding | Effect on Text B's explanation | Weakens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | Speeds fell equally where ride-hailing cars are rare (cause absent, effect present) | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | Biggest drops occur when ride-hailing cars peak (strengthens Text B) | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | Closing a lane on every main road offers a rival cause for slower speeds | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | Driving empty a third of the time supports <Highlight>much of their time</Highlight> | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | Slower buses merely restate the slowdown without pointing to another cause | **No** |

Watch Statement <InlineMath math="5" /> carefully: reporting slower buses confirms that centre speeds dropped, which **neither introduces a rival cause nor separates** the slowdown from ride-hailing cars.

# Answer

- Statement <InlineMath math="1" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="2" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="3" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="4" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="5" />: **No**

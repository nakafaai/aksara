# Given

Across both hotels there were **<InlineMath math="50{,}000" /> guests**, **<InlineMath math="7{,}000" /> survey answers** (<InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> at Harbour, <InlineMath math="6{,}000" /> at Station), and **<InlineMath math="6{,}440" /> positive ratings** (<InlineMath math="950" /> at Harbour, <InlineMath math="5{,}490" /> at Station). Answering was optional, and a manager noted unhappy guests were less likely to reply.

# Asked

Which of the five statements are supported by the passage and the table (**Yes** or **No**)?

# Solution

> One of the chain's managers pointed out that guests with a complaint usually raise it at the front desk before they leave, and that, in her experience, those guests are less likely to answer the survey afterwards.

Keep **survey answers** strictly separate from **all hotel guests**, and check response bias direction carefully.

## Checking each statement with exact ratios

Compute response rates and minimum chain-wide satisfaction from the table:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Response rate} &= \frac{7{,}000}{50{,}000} = 14\% \quad (\text{Harbour: } 5\%, \text{ Station: } 20\%) \\ \text{Minimum satisfied guests} &= \frac{6{,}440}{50{,}000} = 12.88\% \approx 13\% \end{aligned}" />

| Statement | Verification | Supported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | Harbour <InlineMath math="95\%" /> (<InlineMath math="950/1{,}000" />) beats Station <InlineMath math="91.5\%" /> (<InlineMath math="5{,}490/6{,}000" />) | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | Only <InlineMath math="7{,}000" /> of <InlineMath math="50{,}000" /> (<InlineMath math="14\%" />) answered | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="6{,}440" /> of <InlineMath math="50{,}000" /> is about <InlineMath math="13\%" /> (a guaranteed floor) | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | Only <InlineMath math="5\%" /> of Harbour guests answered, so all-guest happiness is unknown | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | Missing unhappy guests makes <InlineMath math="92\%" /> overstate, not understate, satisfaction | **No** |

Watch Statement <InlineMath math="4" /> versus Statement <InlineMath math="1" />: Harbour had a higher share **among answers**, yet with <InlineMath math="95\%" /> of its guests silent we cannot conclude its guests overall were happier.

# Answer

- Statement <InlineMath math="1" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="2" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="3" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="4" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="5" />: **No**

Paragraph 4 explains that Ibn Battuta **had lost most of his notes** and that much of the book was told **from memory years after the events**. Memory over many years can blur details or mix up places, which is less reliable than notes written at the time. Paragraph 5 adds that some passages seem borrowed from other writers.

- The passage does not say Ibn Juzayy travelled with him.
- The sultan asked for a record of the journeys, not for praise of particular rulers.
- He had studied law, which makes illiteracy very unlikely, and the passage never suggests it.
- The language of the book is not discussed.

So parts of the *Rihla* may be less reliable because much of it was told from memory years later, after most notes were lost.

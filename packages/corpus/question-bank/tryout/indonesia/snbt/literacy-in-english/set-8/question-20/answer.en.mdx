Paragraph 2 claims that Ibn Battuta's learning "opened doors to rulers, scholars, and generous hosts". The Pasai visit **illustrates this**: the sultan received him warmly, debated religious questions with scholars, and gave him a ship. The visit is a concrete example of the general point.

- The China account is among the doubted parts, and Pasai does not prove it reliable.

- How he lost his notes is not explained by the Pasai visit.
- Ibn Juzayy shaped the *Rihla*, not the sultan of Pasai.

So the author includes the Pasai visit to give a concrete example of the welcome his learning brought him.

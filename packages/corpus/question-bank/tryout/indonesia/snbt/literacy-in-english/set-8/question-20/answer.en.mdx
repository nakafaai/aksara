# Given

The narrator first braces for danger with keys between her fingers, then hears her mother say Mr Lin lives two streets past theirs, and finally reflects on **the grey coat under the streetlight and which way he had walked when he left**.

# Asked

How does the narrator's understanding of the man evolve across the whole story?

# Solution

> I put my keys back in the bowl. I thought about the grey coat under the streetlight, and about which way he had walked when he left.

Track **all three stages** of her realization right through the final sentence, not stopping at her mother's explanation.

## Following her shift to the final line

At first he is a frightening stranger on the night bus and dark lane. Next, her mother reframes him as a quiet protector. Finally, remembering <Highlight>which way he had walked when he left</Highlight> makes her suspect he went back towards the main road, taking **extra trouble for her** beyond what her mother realised.

The distractors miss this closing nuance. Nothing suggests she dismisses his care as meaningless. Accepting her mother's "two streets past ours" ignores her final thought questioning that route. Mr Lin's quiet wave shows he sought no thanks, and she feared rather than ignored him.

# Answer

Her understanding changes **from a stranger she feared to someone who quietly protected her, perhaps at more trouble to himself than her mother knew**.

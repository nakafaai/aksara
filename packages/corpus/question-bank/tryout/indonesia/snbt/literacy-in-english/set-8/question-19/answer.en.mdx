# Given

Throughout the walk home, the narrator interprets the man's actions as threatening until her mother reveals he was **asked to watch over her to the gate**.

# Asked

Which detail, read on its own, fits both the narrator's fear and the truth about Mr Lin equally well so that only the mother's explanation settles its meaning?

# Solution

> When I got off at the bottom of Mill Lane, he got off too. The lane is long and has a single streetlight about halfway up. I walked faster. So did he, though he kept the same distance behind me the whole way, never closer and never further, like a dog that has been told to heel.

Look for a **genuinely ambiguous action**: something both a threatening stalker and a silent protector following the mother's request would equally do.

## Testing ambiguity across both readings

Getting off at her stop and <Highlight>walking up the lane behind her</Highlight> fits a stranger tailing her home just as naturally as it fits Mr Lin carrying out her mother's request to see her safely up Mill Lane. Nothing inside that action itself decides between the two readings.

The other details are **not equally balanced**. Waiting openly under the streetlight and waving goodbye already reads as protective. Her mother's hospital job is part of the explanation itself. Walking back down the lane removes threat and is actually challenged by the mother's account, while keys between the narrator's fingers show only her own fear.

# Answer

The detail that fits both readings equally well is that **he got off at her stop and walked up the lane behind her**.

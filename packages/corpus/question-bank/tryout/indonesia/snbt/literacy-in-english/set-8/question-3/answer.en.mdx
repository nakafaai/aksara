Each statement needs its own evidence. "Yes" means the passage supports it, and "No" means it goes beyond what the passage shows.

The seagrass statement is No. Paragraph 5 only reports what divers find side by side: "fewer corals, snails and urchins and more seagrass and fleshy algae." It never says the missing animals ate the seagrass, so that cause is a guess the passage doesn't make.

The oyster statement is Yes. Paragraph 2 says aragonite "dissolves more readily than calcite" and that oysters "build their first larval shell from aragonite before switching largely to calcite as adults." Join the two facts and the larvae carry the more fragile mineral. Paragraph 6 agrees that species whose **early life stages depend on aragonite** will feel the cost first.

The vent statement is Yes. Paragraph 5 says larvae "spawned in normal water elsewhere can drift in and settle." Those animals skipped the acidic water during their most fragile stage, so a vent population may look healthier than one raised in acid from the start. The word "may" keeps the claim inside the evidence.

The faster-growing species statement is No. Those results come from tanks, and paragraph 4 says tanks differ from the sea, for example in **generous feeding** and in testing one factor at a time. A tank result can't be carried straight into the open sea.

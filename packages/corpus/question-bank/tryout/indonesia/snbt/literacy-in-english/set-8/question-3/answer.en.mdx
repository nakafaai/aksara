# Given

Harlow wrote that Gage <Highlight>could not keep to his plans</Highlight>, whereas later research revealed he drove a stagecoach in Chile, **handling a team of horses on mountain roads to a fixed timetable**.

# Asked

Beyond simply showing that Gage held a job, what does the specific kind of work he did in Chile suggest?

# Solution

> Gage worked for some years as a stagecoach driver in Chile, a job that meant handling a team of horses on mountain roads to a fixed timetable, before he died in <InlineMath math="1860" />.

Link the exact demands of stagecoach driving directly back to the **specific ability Harlow said Gage had lost**.

## Matching job demands to the report

Managing a team of horses on mountain roads while sticking to <Highlight>a fixed timetable</Highlight> demands steady self-control and planning. That directly parallels and counters Harlow's earlier observation that Gage <Highlight>could not keep to his plans</Highlight>.

The other options push the evidence too far. Later recovery years afterwards does not prove Harlow was wrong about the **early months** right after the accident, does not mean Gage recovered from every single effect, does not show his friends were wrong all along, and cannot prove the front of the brain plays no part in planning.

# Answer

The work suggests **that he could keep to a fixed timetable and manage a team of horses, the kind of planning Harlow reported he could no longer do**.

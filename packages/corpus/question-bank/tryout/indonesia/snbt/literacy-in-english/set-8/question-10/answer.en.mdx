Paragraph 4 answers the tradition argument: consumers who value traditional meat "will remain free to buy it", and with clear labels "no one will eat it by mistake". Labelling therefore lets people who **prefer farmed meat avoid** cultivated meat, which removes one reason for a ban.

- Labels identify cultivated meat. They do not disguise it as traditional.
- Price is not linked to labelling.
- Safety is a matter for regulators' review, not labels.
- Labels are aimed at consumers, not at changing what farmers produce.

So, according to paragraph 4, cultivated meat should be clearly labelled to let people who prefer farmed meat avoid it.

# Given

Before the charge, **<InlineMath math="100" /> cars** entered the centre each weekday and almost none were ride-hailing cars. Text A states total cars entering are now **<InlineMath math="18\%" /> below** the pre-charge level, and Text B states ride-hailing cars make up **one third** of cars entering now.

# Asked

Combining both texts, about how many private cars enter the centre each weekday now?

# Solution

> They now make up a third of the cars entering the centre. That means private cars are down by about <InlineMath math="45\%" />, not <InlineMath math="18\%" />.

Apply **Text A's percentage drop** to find today's total cars, then remove **Text B's one-third ride-hailing share** from that new total.

## Finding current private cars

Subtract <InlineMath math="18\%" /> from <InlineMath math="100" /> to get all cars entering today, then keep two thirds of that number for **current private cars**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{All cars now} &= 100 - 18 = 82 \\ \text{Ride-hailing cars now} &= \frac{1}{3} \times 82 \approx 27 \\ \text{Private cars now} &= 82 - 27 = 55 \end{aligned}" />

| Figure | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="82" /> | All cars entering now (private plus ride-hailing) |
| <InlineMath math="27" /> | Ride-hailing cars entering now (<InlineMath math="1/3" /> of <InlineMath math="82" />) |
| <InlineMath math="55" /> | Private cars entering now (<InlineMath math="45\%" /> below <InlineMath math="100" />) |

This also confirms Text B's own check (<InlineMath math="55" /> out of <InlineMath math="100" /> is down <InlineMath math="45\%" />). The distractors <InlineMath math="67" /> and <InlineMath math="62" /> mistakenly subtract a third of the old <InlineMath math="100" /> or a quarter of <InlineMath math="82" />.

# Answer

About **<InlineMath math="55" />** private cars enter the centre each weekday now.

The question asks where Text 2 agrees with Text 1's rule and where it parts ways. Text 2 does both, so look for an answer that keeps **both halves**.

Agreement: Text 2 says the insurer recovers the money "from the manufacturer" when "the crash was caused by a fault in the system," and that "the maker still ends up paying for its defects." So the maker still pays in the end.

Disagreement, in two places. First, Text 2 rejects making victims "claim from the maker," because they'd face "far more lawyers and far more information." The owner's insurance pays first. Second, "Nor should the owner vanish from the picture": skipping updates, ignoring warnings or driving in heavy snow are the owner's choices. Text 1 wants the maker liable "whenever" the system is engaged, so this is a real disagreement.

- Owners pay because they chose to buy: that's the insurers' argument that Text 1 reports and rejects. Text 2 never uses it, and it keeps the maker paying for defects.
- Accepts the rule in full: Text 2 says the victim's goal "points to a different rule," and it also gives owners a share of responsibility.
- Handovers as the owner's job: Text 2 never mentions handovers. Only Text 1 does.
- Makers can't see the software's decisions: this flips Text 2, which says the maker has "far more information" than a victim.

So Text 2 agrees that the maker should finally pay for system faults, but it rejects the maker as the first party victims claim from and keeps a role for the owner's choices.

The author rejects both arguments for bans: safety is "exactly what food regulators exist to judge", and tradition is protected because people stay free to buy farmed meat. The proposed alternative is **review like any new food**, **clear labelling**, and **careful sales that are studied**. The author opposes bans, not caution.

- The author does not call for replacing farmed meat, and says traditional meat stays available.
- Waiting for decades of eating is the bans' safety argument, which the author rejects.
- Paying farmers to produce it is not mentioned.
- The author says judging safety is exactly what regulators are for.

So the author's position is that cultivated meat should be reviewed and labelled rather than banned outright.

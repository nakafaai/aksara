The passage says the lesson was "taken from the first months after the accident and stretched to fit the rest of his life". So a matching case needs **three parts**: an early observation, a claim that it is permanent, and later evidence against that claim.

- Years of forgetting make "permanent" a fair description. Nothing is stretched.
- Calling it temporary fits the later return to work. That's the opposite of stretching.
- Later tests confirm the claim, so the permanent label rests on evidence.
- A report written at the time describes the early weeks without stretching them at all.

So the matching case is the patient whose first weeks were described as permanent although the patient later returned to a demanding job.

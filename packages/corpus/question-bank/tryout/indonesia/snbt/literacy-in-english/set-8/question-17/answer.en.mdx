# Given

On Mill Lane, the man matched the narrator's pace while keeping an exact gap behind her, <Highlight>like a dog that has been told to heel</Highlight>. Later her mother reveals she had asked Mr Lin to watch over her until the gate.

# Asked

Read together with what comes later, what does this comparison suggest?

# Solution

> I walked faster. So did he, though he kept the same distance behind me the whole way, never closer and never further, like a dog that has been told to heel.

Focus on the core action inside the simile: a dog told to heel holds its exact spacing **because it is obeying a set instruction**.

## Connecting the simile to the request

Saying he kept a fixed gap <Highlight>like a dog that has been told to heel</Highlight> subtly foreshadows obedience rather than aggression. Once we learn her mother asked him <Highlight>to keep an eye on me as far as the gate</Highlight>, the simile makes clear that he maintained that respectful distance deliberately to carry out the mother's request.

The other choices misread the image. A dog at heel is obeying a command, not hunting prey. Nothing implies Mr Lin feared the narrator, nothing ties hospital porter work to trailing people, and standing openly under the streetlight shows her mother never told him to stay hidden.

# Answer

The comparison suggests **that he kept a fixed distance on purpose, as if following an instruction, which fits the task her mother had given him**.

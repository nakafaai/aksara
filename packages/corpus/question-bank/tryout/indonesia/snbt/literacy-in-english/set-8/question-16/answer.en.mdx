Paragraph 2 explains it: Ibn Battuta "had studied Islamic law", and a trained judge was **valued and well paid** in many Muslim communities. He worked as a judge in Delhi and in the Maldives, and "his learning opened doors to rulers, scholars, and generous hosts".

- His family's wealth is not mentioned.
- He travelled by ship but is not described as a captain. The sultan of Pasai provided him with a ship.
- The sultan of Morocco asked him to record his journeys after he returned, not before he left.
- The *Rihla* was written after the journeys, so rulers could not have read it during them.

So his training in Islamic law, which made him valued as a judge, helped him gain support.

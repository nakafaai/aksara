You're looking for two differences that both make the tank **kinder than the sea**, so the tank would show less harm than the animals would really suffer.

Sort each difference from paragraph 4 by the direction it pushes:

| Difference | Effect on the tank result |
| --- | --- |
| Abrupt rise in carbon dioxide | Harsher, since the real ocean changes "over decades" and gives time to acclimatise |
| No time to adapt over generations | Harsher, for the same reason |
| Generous feeding | Kinder, since "a well-fed animal can spend more energy" controlling its shell chemistry |
| One factor at a time | Kinder, since the sea adds "warmer water and falling oxygen" |
| Steady level | Kinder, since an animal that copes with a steady level "may still suffer during the worst hours of a real night" |

Only one option pairs two kinder differences: generous feeding and the steady level.

- Feeding plus no time to adapt: the second half makes the tank harsher, so this pair points both ways.
- Abrupt rise plus steady level: the abrupt rise makes the tank harsher.
- Abrupt rise plus one factor at a time: the abrupt rise again pulls the other way.
- One factor at a time plus drifting larvae: drifting larvae are the weakness of the vent sites in paragraph 5, not a feature of tanks.

That's what "cut both ways" means here: some differences make a tank overstate harm and others make it understate harm. The pair that both make a tank show less harm is generous feeding and the steady carbon dioxide level.

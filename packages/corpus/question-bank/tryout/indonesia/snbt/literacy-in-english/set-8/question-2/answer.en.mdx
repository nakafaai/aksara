# Given

Harlow reported that Gage's friends saw personality changes after the <InlineMath math="1848" /> accident, while later research showed Gage worked for years as a **stagecoach driver in Chile**. Researchers only suggest that routine perhaps helped him recover.

# Asked

Which sentence would be most accurate for a textbook writer who wants to keep Gage as an example?

# Solution

> Some researchers now suggest that Gage recovered more than the famous story allows, perhaps because the steady routine of his later work helped him relearn how to manage himself.

An accurate textbook summary must preserve **both halves of the evidence** without claiming more certainty than the record supports.

## Keeping both halves and cautious tone

The passage keeps Harlow's account of early behavioural change alongside Gage's later demanding work handling horses on mountain roads to a fixed timetable, while carefully using cautious words like <Highlight>suggest</Highlight> and <Highlight>perhaps</Highlight>. Saying the change **may not have been permanent** fits every known fact.

The other choices overreach or contradict the record. His friends never retracted their remarks. Nothing proves his later routine cured him. Claiming he changed for good contradicts the Chile years, and fights and heavy drinking have no firsthand source.

# Answer

The most accurate sentence is: **Gage's behaviour seems to have changed after the injury, but he later held a demanding job for years, so the change may not have been permanent**.

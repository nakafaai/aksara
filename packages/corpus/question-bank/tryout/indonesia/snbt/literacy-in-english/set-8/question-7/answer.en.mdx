You need to apply Text 2's view to an example that only Text 1 uses. So ask two things: what does Text 2 say about design faults, and what does it say about owners?

On design faults, Text 2 agrees with the lift example. When "the crash was caused by a fault in the system," the insurer recovers the money "from the manufacturer." A design fault is exactly that case, so Text 2 wouldn't blame the owner for it.

On owners, Text 2 adds what the lift example leaves out. An owner can "fail to install a safety update, ignore repeated warnings, or switch the system on in conditions it was never approved for, such as heavy snow." Those are **the owner's own choices**, and a homeowner whose lift fails from a design fault made no such choice. So Text 2 would say the comparison works for design faults but not for crashes the owner's choices helped cause.

- People who never bought the lift: that's a real difference, but Text 2 never argues about who uses a product.
- Victims claiming directly from the maker: Text 2 argues the opposite, with the owner's insurance as "the first payer."
- Premiums that ignore use: Text 2 wants a premium "that rises with" risky choices.
- The homeowner chose to buy: that's the insurers' argument in Text 1. Text 2 points to specific choices like ignoring warnings, not to the purchase.

So Text 2's author would accept the lift comparison for design faults but reject it for crashes where the owner ignored warnings or used the system outside its approved conditions.

# Given

**Text A** defends the congestion charge using the <InlineMath math="18\%" /> drop in total cars entering the centre. **Text B** backs the charge too, but distinguishes private cars (<InlineMath math="45\%" /> down) from exempt ride-hailing cars and raises concerns about centre speeds.

# Asked

Which of the five statements would **both writers** accept (**Yes** or **No**)?

# Solution

> The fall in traffic is real, and I support the charge.

A statement earns **Yes** only if **both Text A and Text B** endorse it. Check each claim against both passages.

## Checking shared agreement across both texts

| Statement | Text A | Text B | Both accept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | Cars entering are <Highlight><InlineMath math="18\%" /> below</Highlight> pre-charge level | <Highlight>The fall in traffic is real</Highlight> | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | Applies <InlineMath math="18\%" /> to all cars, not private cars | Private cars down <InlineMath math="45\%" />, <Highlight>not <InlineMath math="18\%" /></Highlight> | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | Says <Highlight>The charge works</Highlight> | Says <Highlight>I support the charge</Highlight> | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | Silent on current average speeds | Mentions speeds fell almost back | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | Never mentions ride-hailing cars | Argues they should pay the charge | **No** |

Notice how Statements <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> fail the **two-writer test**: even though Text B states both clearly, Text A never addresses current centre speeds or ride-hailing cars.

# Answer

- Statement <InlineMath math="1" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="2" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="3" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="4" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="5" />: **No**

**Text A**

Two years ago the city began charging drivers a daily fee to enter the centre on weekdays. In the first year the number of cars entering fell by <InlineMath math="25\%" />, and buses ran faster. Traffic has crept back a little since, but the number of cars entering is still <InlineMath math="18\%" /> below its level before the charge. The charge works, and its income now pays for three new bus routes.

**Text B**

The fall in traffic is real, and I support the charge. But the creep back is not what the director thinks. Most of it comes from ride-hailing cars, which were almost unknown in the city before the charge and which the city chose to exempt from it. They now make up a third of the cars entering the centre. That means private cars are down by about <InlineMath math="45\%" />, not <InlineMath math="18\%" />. Meanwhile ride-hailing cars spend much of their time driving empty between passengers, so average speeds in the centre have fallen almost back to where they were before the charge. The charge should apply to ride-hailing cars too.

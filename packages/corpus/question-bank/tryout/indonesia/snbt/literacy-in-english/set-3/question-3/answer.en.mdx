# Given

**Lab P** and **Lab Q** use identical cups, freezers, and thermometers, and both time **cooling to zero degrees**. The sole difference is thermometer depth: Lab P places it near the bottom (hot cup wins), while Lab Q places it near the top (cold cup wins).

# Asked

Which conclusion is best supported by the passage?

# Solution

Isolate the **single variable that differs** between the two labs (thermometer location) and match it to the <InlineMath math="2016" /> finding in paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />.

> When equipment and the zero-degree endpoint are identical, opposing results point to thermometer placement sensitivity rather than proof of the Mpemba effect.

## Applying the thermometer finding

Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> directly explains this split: <Highlight>a small change in where the thermometer sat in the water was enough to produce an apparent Mpemba effect or to erase one, which may explain why earlier results disagreed</Highlight>.

Because sensor placement alone can manufacture or erase an apparent win, **neither lab's result on its own proves the effect is real**.

## Why the other options fail

- **Lab P confirmed the effect** ignores that placement can produce a false apparent effect, so Lab P has no priority over Lab Q.
- **Different freezing events** does not apply because both labs timed the exact same event (reaching zero degrees).
- **Supercooling below zero** cannot explain results where both timers stopped at zero degrees.
- **Effect only near the bottom** is never stated in the passage.

# Answer

**The disagreement may come from where the thermometers sat, so neither result shows on its own that the effect is real**.

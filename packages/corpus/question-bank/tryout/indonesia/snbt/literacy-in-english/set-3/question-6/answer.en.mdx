# Given

**Text A** converts <InlineMath math="40" /> million fewer carrier bags (<InlineMath math="8" /> grams each) into <Highlight>some <InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes of plastic that no longer enters our homes and bins every year</Highlight>. **Text B** counters that households bought about <InlineMath math="120" /> tonnes more plastic bin liners after free carrier bags disappeared.

# Asked

Which claim would the writer of Text B endorse while the council in Text A would dispute it?

# Solution

Apply **both tests** carefully: Text B must actively endorse the claim, and Text A must actually **contradict** it rather than merely staying silent on the topic.

> Text B explicitly challenges the council's leap from avoided carrier-bag weight to total plastic saved, whereas Text A treats that exact weight as plastic no longer used.

## Matching both writers positions

Text B directly targets the council's <InlineMath math="320" />-tonne headline: <Highlight>the council's figure of <InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes measures the bags that were not handed out, not the plastic that was not used</Highlight> and adds <Highlight>The plastic saved is real, but it is smaller than the bag count suggests</Highlight>.

Because Text A equates the weight of avoided carrier bags with the plastic no longer entering homes and bins, **Text B endorses** that the bag weight overstates the plastic saved while **Text A disputes** it.

## Why the other options fail

- **Plastic use has fallen** is accepted by both writers: Text B says <Highlight>The plastic saved is real</Highlight>.
- **Households now buy bin liners** is the trap: Text B states it, while Text A never mentions bin liners at all (silence is not disputing).
- **No earlier campaign acted as quickly** is Text A's boast, unaddressed by Text B.
- **Extending the charge to paper bags** is Text A's plan, which Text B explicitly declines to judge.

# Answer

**The weight of the carrier bags no longer handed out overstates how much plastic the city has stopped using**.

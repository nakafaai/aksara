A consumer research group surveyed 2,000 adults in one country about how much meat they ate. Respondents were asked whether they had eaten less meat this year than in the previous year and, if so, what their main reason was. The results are summarised by age group in the table.

Across all respondents who said they ate less meat, one in five named concern for the environment as their main reason. The research group published its findings under the headline "Health drives the move away from meat".

The survey was carried out in a year when meat prices rose sharply. A large supermarket chain reported that, over the same period, the average price of its meat products rose by about 9 percent while the total weight of meat it sold fell by about 3 percent. The research group noted that respondents described their own eating habits and that the survey did not ask how much less meat they ate.

The passage keeps returning to one obstacle. Paragraph 2 names the assumption that hieroglyphs were **mystical symbols**, which gave scholars "little reason to look for sounds". Young advanced partly but kept that view for native words. Champollion showed that hieroglyphs were **a working script** of mixed signs, and only "once scholars accepted" this did readings fall into place. The main point is that hieroglyphs became readable once scholars **dropped that belief** and treated them as a mixed working script.

- The passage says nothing about returning the stone.
- Young's progress is called important, but the decisive step is credited to Champollion.
- Greek was one of the three versions, but the passage does not rank languages in ancient Egypt.
- Coptic survived in church services. Champollion studied it but did not invent it.

So the main point is that hieroglyphs were read once scholars saw them as a mixed working script rather than mystical symbols.

# Given

The narrator states several assumptions and observations across the story, **two of which** her father later corrects on the bus home.

# Asked

Which beliefs held by the narrator turn out to be mistaken? Select all that apply.

# Solution

Match each option to the narrator's own words and check whether the story later **overturns** that belief or leaves it standing as fact.

> Both mistaken beliefs stem from reducing her role to mere vocabulary translation: her father neither struggled with long words nor lacked a real need for her presence at the clinic.

## Testing each of the narrator beliefs

| Narrator's belief | Verdict | Story check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Father wanted help with the long words | **Mistaken** | She opens <Highlight>I assumed he meant the long ones</Highlight>, which he overturns on the bus: <Highlight>the doctor's words had never been the problem</Highlight>. |
| Father had not needed her at the clinic at all | **Mistaken** | She tells him <Highlight>he had not needed me at all</Highlight>, yet he did need her as a buffer against the doctor's judgement (<Highlight>When you tell me, I hear the news</Highlight>). |
| Father speaks the doctor's language well | **Confirmed** | Proven both by arguing with the tax office and answering the doctor directly in her language. |
| Doctor spoke to her rather than to her father | **Confirmed** | Direct observation (<Highlight>The doctor spoke to me, not to him</Highlight>), never contradicted. |
| Father enjoys arguing with the tax office | **Confirmed** | Stated directly (<Highlight>with great pleasure</Highlight>) and never overturned. |

# Answer

- **That her father wanted help with the long words**.
- **That her father had not needed her at the clinic at all**.

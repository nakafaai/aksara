"One in five" applies to **respondents who said they ate less meat**, not to all 2,000 people. First find that group, then take one fifth of it.

From the table, <InlineMath math="168 + 210 + 182 + 70 = 630" /> respondents said they ate less meat.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{5} \times 630 = 126" />

- 42 is the highest group percentage, not a number of people.
- 200 would be one tenth of all 2,000 respondents, a fraction the passage never uses.
- 400 is one fifth of all 2,000 respondents, which applies the fraction to the wrong group.
- 630 is everyone who ate less meat, before taking one fifth.

So about 126 respondents named the environment as their main reason.

# Given

The question holds **the two cups of water completely unchanged** and asks what experimental changes alone could flip which cup wins the freezing race.

# Asked

Which two factors from the passage could make the same pair of cups win in one experiment and lose in another?

# Solution

Filter strictly for **measurement choices** rather than physical changes to the water or container.

> Even when two identical cups are left untouched, changing which freezing milestone is timed or nudging the thermometer position can reverse which cup appears to win.

## Measurement factors in the passage

Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> highlights the choice of milestone: <Highlight>Some experiments timed how long the water took to reach zero degrees. Others timed the first crystals of ice, or the moment the whole sample was solid. These are different events, and a sample that wins one race can lose another</Highlight>.

Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> adds probe placement: <Highlight>a small change in where the thermometer sat in the water was enough to produce an apparent Mpemba effect or to erase one</Highlight>. Both choices change **how the race is measured** without altering the cups.

## Why the other options fail

- **Evaporation and dissolved gas** are physical mechanisms inside the water, not measurement choices.
- **Warming the water beforehand** alters the cups themselves instead of leaving them unchanged.
- **Switching to an ice-cream mixture** replaces the contents of the cups.
- **Warm water rising to the surface** is a convective process inside the water, not a measurement choice.

# Answer

**Timing a different event, such as the first ice instead of reaching zero degrees, and placing the thermometer in a slightly different spot**.

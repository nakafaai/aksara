A hiking compass responds to **the horizontal direction** of the field: its needle points to magnetic north. Paragraph 2 describes the test that separated the two ideas. Researchers reversed **only the vertical part** of the field and left the horizontal direction unchanged. The robins still turned around, as if north and south had swapped. A hiking compass would not have changed at all, so the birds must be reading the dip of the field lines instead.

- Birds in cages show that something other than the sky guides them, but not how the magnetic sense works.
- The angle of field lines near the equator is background information, not an experimental result about birds.
- Flying alone and at night explains why the question is puzzling, not how the compass works.
- The 2012 finding about beak cells concerns where the sensor is, not how it reads the field.

So the deciding finding is that robins turned around when only the vertical part of the field was reversed.

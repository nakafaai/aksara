The condition "with nothing about the cups themselves changed" is the filter. You need two causes that change **how the race is measured**, not what is in the cups.

The third paragraph gives the first: experiments timed different events, reaching "zero degrees", "the first crystals of ice" or "the moment the whole sample was solid", and "a sample that wins one race can lose another". The last paragraph gives the second: "a small change in where the thermometer sat in the water was enough to produce an apparent Mpemba effect or to erase one". Both leave the cups untouched.

- Evaporation and dissolved gas are properties of the water itself. They are proposed reasons why hot water might freeze first, not ways of measuring.
- The first half is right, but warming the water changes the cups.
- The thermometer half is right, but swapping water for an ice-cream mixture changes what is in the cups.
- Warm water rising is another property of the water, one of the proposed mechanisms.

The two things are timing a different event and placing the thermometer in a slightly different spot.

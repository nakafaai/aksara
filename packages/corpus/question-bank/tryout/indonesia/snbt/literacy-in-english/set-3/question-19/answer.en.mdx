Paragraph 4 says that because Champollion knew Coptic, "the late form of the Egyptian language", he could "guess the sounds of many Egyptian words and **test his readings** against them". Coptic gave him a living record of **the sounds of Egyptian**, which mattered once he saw that many signs stood for sounds.

- Scholars could already read Greek before Champollion's work, according to paragraph 1.
- The passage does not say Coptic told him where cartouches were. He examined copies from older temples.
- Champollion's work disproved the mystical view rather than proving it.
- Nothing says Coptic let him translate Demotic directly. His method relied on comparing and testing readings.

So Coptic helped Champollion guess how Egyptian words had sounded and test his readings against them.

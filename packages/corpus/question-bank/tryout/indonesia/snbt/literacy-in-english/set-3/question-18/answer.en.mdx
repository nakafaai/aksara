Each statement needs its own line from the story, and one asks what the narrator knows rather than what the reader can work out.

Statement 1. He did ask for help "with the words", but that's the narrator's reading of why he wanted her there. He speaks the language "well enough to argue with the tax office", answers the doctor "in her language", and on the bus says "the doctor's words had never been the problem". No.

Statement 2. "The doctor spoke to me, not to him, from the start." Yes.

Statement 3. He says "When she tells me, I hear the news and I watch her decide what kind of man I am." The trouble is **being judged**, not the words. Yes.

Statement 4. On the bus the narrator asks why he answered the tablet question himself, and his reply is about himself, his own conduct, "not about my heart". Nothing shows her realising anything about the tablets. The reader works it out later, from **the drawer**. No.

Statement 5. "He answered her directly, in her language, before I could open my mouth." Yes.

So statements 2, 3 and 5 are Yes, and statements 1 and 4 are No.

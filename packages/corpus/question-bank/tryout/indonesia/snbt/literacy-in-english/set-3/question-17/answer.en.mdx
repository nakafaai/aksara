# Given

The father speaks the local language <Highlight>well enough to argue with the tax office</Highlight> and explains <Highlight>the doctor's words had never been the problem</Highlight>. What hurts him is <Highlight>When she tells me, I hear the news and I watch her decide what kind of man I am</Highlight>.

# Asked

Which situation is most similar to what the father fears when the doctor speaks to him directly?

# Solution

Match **both core elements** of the father's experience: **full comprehension of the language** paired with **feeling personally judged** by the speaker.

> The father understands every medical term without difficulty, yet dreads watching an authority figure judge his character rather than simply delivering information.

## Matching the parallel scenario

The job applicant shares **both exact features**: he understands every question completely (no language barrier), yet feels the interviewer judging him as a person by his accent instead of evaluating his answers.

## Why the other options fail

- **Immigrant unable to follow a fast-talking official** depicts a genuine language barrier, which is what the narrator wrongly assumed at first.
- **Patient who dreads hearing bad news** fears the medical news itself, whereas the father easily hears the same news from his daughter.
- **Student facing an overly hard exam** deals with academic difficulty rather than personal judgement.
- **Tourist unable to read a menu** again represents lack of language comprehension.

# Answer

**A job applicant who understands every question but feels the interviewer judging him by his accent rather than his answers**.

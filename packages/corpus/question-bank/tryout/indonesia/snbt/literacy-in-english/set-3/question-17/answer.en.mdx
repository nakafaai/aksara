Paragraph 3 ends with Young's limitation: he believed sound signs were used "only to spell foreign names like Ptolemy", while native Egyptian words kept a purely pictorial meaning. Paragraph 4 shows Champollion finding **Egyptian kings' names**, Ramesses and Thutmose, spelled partly with sound signs, so "foreign names were not the only ones written phonetically". The difference is **whether sound signs reached beyond foreign names**.

- The passage does not divide the scripts between the two scholars this way. Champollion's key evidence came from temple cartouches.
- Young already worked out sound values, so he did not see hieroglyphs as purely mystical, and Champollion showed the signs were mixed, not only sounds.
- Champollion examined cartouches copied from older temples. Nothing says Young visited Egyptian temples.
- The names are reversed: Young matched Ptolemy, and Champollion recognised Ramesses and Thutmose.

So the main difference is that Young limited sound signs to foreign names, while Champollion showed they also spelled Egyptian names.

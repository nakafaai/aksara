# Given

Text A reports **<InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes** of carrier bags no longer handed out each year, while Text B reports a rise of about **<InlineMath math="120" /> tonnes** per year in plastic bin-liner sales.

# Asked

Using both texts' figures, about how much plastic does the charge save each year, and what fraction of the council's figure is that?

# Solution

Subtract the **extra bin-liner plastic** from the council's gross bag weight, then divide the net saving by <InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes.

> Removing one hundred and twenty tonnes of replacement bin liners from three hundred and twenty tonnes of avoided carrier bags leaves two hundred tonnes saved, which simplifies to five eighths.

## Computing net plastic saved and its fraction

| Component | Annual weight | Share of council figure |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Carrier bags not handed out (Text A) | <InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes | <InlineMath math="\frac{8}{8}" /> |
| Extra bin liners bought (Text B) | <InlineMath math="120" /> tonnes | <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{8}" /> |
| Net plastic saved | <InlineMath math="200" /> tonnes | <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{8}" /> |

Computing the **net saving** and simplifying its **fraction**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Net plastic saved} &= 320 - 120 = 200 \text{ tonnes} \\ \text{Share of council figure} &= \frac{200}{320} = \frac{5}{8} \end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="200" /> tonnes, three-eighths** gives the right weight but picks the **cancelled fraction** (<InlineMath math="\frac{3}{8}" />) instead of the remaining share.
- **<InlineMath math="120" /> tonnes** is the added bin-liner plastic rather than the net saving.
- **<InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes** ignores Text B's bin-liner offset completely.
- **<InlineMath math="440" /> tonnes** adds <InlineMath math="120" /> instead of subtracting it.

# Answer

**About <InlineMath math="200" /> tonnes, roughly five-eighths of the council's figure**.

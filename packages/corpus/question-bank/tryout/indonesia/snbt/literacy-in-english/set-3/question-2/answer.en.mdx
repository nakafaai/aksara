Read the phrase in context: birds "might literally see the field as a pattern overlaid on their normal vision". The pattern adds to what the birds already see rather than removing it, because their **normal vision** is still there. Something overlaid is **placed on top of** something else, like a transparent sheet over a picture.

- "Hidden behind" would put the pattern out of sight, but the sentence says the birds see it.
- "Replaced by" would mean normal vision disappears, yet the sentence keeps "their normal vision".
- "Reflected off" describes light bouncing, not one image added to another.
- "Blurred into" would make the pattern unclear, but the sentence suggests a visible pattern.

So "overlaid" means laid on top of.

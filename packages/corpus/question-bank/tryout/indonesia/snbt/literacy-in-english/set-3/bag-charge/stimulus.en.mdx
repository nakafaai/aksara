The charge has worked, and I would not want it removed. But the council's figure of <InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes measures the bags that were not handed out, not the plastic that was not used. Many households used to take their carrier bags home and use them a second time to line kitchen bins. When the free bags disappeared, those households did not stop lining their bins. They bought bin liners instead. Sales data from the city's three largest supermarket chains show that sales of plastic bin liners rose after the charge by about <InlineMath math="120" /> tonnes a year, and bin liners are made thicker than carrier bags. The plastic saved is real, but it is smaller than the bag count suggests. The council should report its results by weight, bin liners included, before it claims a figure. Whether paper bags are better for the environment than plastic ones is a separate question, and I will not try to settle it here.

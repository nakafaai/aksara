Separate **shares** from **numbers of people**, and check what the survey actually asked.

The first statement is true: 42 percent is the highest rate in the table.

The second needs counts, not rates. In the 45 to 64 group, <InlineMath math="0.28 \times 650 = 182" /> people said they ate less meat. In the 30 to 44 group, <InlineMath math="0.35 \times 600 = 210" /> did. Since 182 is fewer than 210, the statement is false.

The third is false because the most common reason in the 18 to 29 group was cost.

The fourth is false. The survey asked whether people ate **less** meat, not whether they stopped eating it, and it did not ask how much less.

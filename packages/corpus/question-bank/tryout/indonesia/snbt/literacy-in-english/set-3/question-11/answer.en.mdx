The groups are **different sizes**, so you cannot simply average the four percentages. First find how many people in each group said they ate less meat, then divide the total by <InlineMath math="2{,}000" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 0.42 \times 400 &= 168 \\ 0.35 \times 600 &= 210 \\ 0.28 \times 650 &= 182 \\ 0.20 \times 350 &= 70 \end{aligned}" />

<BlockMath math="168 + 210 + 182 + 70 = 630 \qquad \frac{630}{2{,}000} \times 100\% = 31.5\%" />

- <InlineMath math="31.25" /> percent is the plain average of <InlineMath math="42" />, <InlineMath math="35" />, <InlineMath math="28" />, and <InlineMath math="20" />. It treats the groups as if they were the same size.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> percent and <InlineMath math="42" /> percent are the lowest and highest group rates, not the overall rate.
- <InlineMath math="35" /> percent is the rate for the <InlineMath math="30" /> to <InlineMath math="44" /> group only.

So <InlineMath math="31.5" /> percent of all respondents said they ate less meat.

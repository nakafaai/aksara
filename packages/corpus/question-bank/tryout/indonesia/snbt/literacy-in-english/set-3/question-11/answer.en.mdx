The groups are **different sizes**, so you cannot simply average the four percentages. First find how many people in each group said they ate less meat, then divide the total by 2,000.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 0.42 \times 400 &= 168 \\ 0.35 \times 600 &= 210 \\ 0.28 \times 650 &= 182 \\ 0.20 \times 350 &= 70 \end{aligned}" />

<BlockMath math="168 + 210 + 182 + 70 = 630 \qquad \frac{630}{2{,}000} \times 100\% = 31.5\%" />

- 31.25 percent is the plain average of 42, 35, 28, and 20. It treats the groups as if they were the same size.
- 20 percent and 42 percent are the lowest and highest group rates, not the overall rate.
- 35 percent is the rate for the 30 to 44 group only.

So 31.5 percent of all respondents said they ate less meat.

# Given

The bus table shows **Northline** ran <InlineMath math="800" /> urban trips (<InlineMath math="560" /> on time) and <InlineMath math="200" /> rural trips (<InlineMath math="180" /> on time), while **Southway** ran <InlineMath math="200" /> urban trips (<InlineMath math="130" /> on time) and <InlineMath math="800" /> rural trips (<InlineMath math="680" /> on time).

# Asked

What were both operators' overall on-time rates last year, and how do they compare with each operator's urban and rural rates?

# Solution

Compute each operator's **overall on-time percentage** out of <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> trips, then place it beside the **separate urban and rural rates** (Simpson's paradox).

> Northline led Southway by five percentage points on urban routes and five percentage points on rural routes, yet Southway scored seven points higher overall.

## Overall rates beside route rates

| Operator | Urban on time | Rural on time | Overall on time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Northline | <InlineMath math="\frac{560}{800} = 70\%" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{180}{200} = 90\%" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{560 + 180}{1{,}000} = 74\%" /> |
| Southway | <InlineMath math="\frac{130}{200} = 65\%" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{680}{800} = 85\%" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{130 + 680}{1{,}000} = 81\%" /> |

Checking **both totals** directly confirms the reversal:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Northline overall} &= \frac{560 + 180}{800 + 200} = \frac{740}{1{,}000} = 74\% \\ \text{Southway overall} &= \frac{130 + 680}{200 + 800} = \frac{810}{1{,}000} = 81\% \end{aligned}" />

Even though Southway leads overall (<InlineMath math="81\%" /> vs. <InlineMath math="74\%" />), **Northline leads on both route types** (<InlineMath math="70\% > 65\%" /> urban and <InlineMath math="90\% > 85\%" /> rural).

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="80\%" /> and <InlineMath math="75\%" />** take an unweighted average of each operator's two route rates, ignoring the <InlineMath math="800" />-to-<InlineMath math="200" /> trip split.
- **Southway ahead on each kind of route** reverses the route-level rates: Northline beat Southway on both urban and rural routes.
- **<InlineMath math="70\%" /> and <InlineMath math="85\%" />** quote single route rates instead of overall totals.
- **Northline <InlineMath math="81\%" /> and Southway <InlineMath math="74\%" />** swaps the two operators' overall scores.

# Answer

**Northline <InlineMath math="74\%" /> and Southway <InlineMath math="81\%" />, even though Northline was ahead on each kind of route**.

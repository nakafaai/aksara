Each place tells you something, and you need both: the drawer is about **the past**, the windowsill about **the future**.

**The drawer**. The box "had sat since his previous appointment months before" in the kitchen drawer. A box that stays put for months isn't being opened every morning, so his "Every day" at the clinic was probably not true.

**The windowsill**. He puts it "beside his reading glasses, where he would see it every morning". He places it where he can't miss it, which suggests he means to start taking the tablets.

- If he'd been taking them every day, the box wouldn't have sat in the drawer for months.
- The box had been in the drawer since the previous appointment, months before today. The gap started long before this visit.
- Nothing suggests he's putting on a show for the narrator. He moves the box "where he would see it every morning", a change for himself.
- A second box is never mentioned. That's an invented explanation.

Together, the drawer and the windowsill suggest that he had not been taking the tablets regularly since his previous appointment, and that he now means to start.

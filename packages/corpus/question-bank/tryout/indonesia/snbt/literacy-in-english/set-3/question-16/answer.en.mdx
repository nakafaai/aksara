# Given

In the final paragraph, the father takes his box of blood-pressure tablets out of the **kitchen drawer** (where it had sat untouched since his previous appointment months earlier) and places it on the **windowsill beside his reading glasses**.

# Asked

Taken together, what do the kitchen drawer and the windowsill suggest?

# Solution

Read the **two locations as a before-and-after pair**: the drawer reveals his past habit, while the windowsill signals his new resolve.

> Leaving the tablet box hidden in a drawer for months shows his clinic answer of taking them every day was untrue, while moving it beside his reading glasses signals he now intends to start.

## Reading both objects together

- **The kitchen drawer (the past)**: The box <Highlight>had sat since his previous appointment months before</Highlight>, showing he had **not been taking the tablets regularly** despite telling the doctor "Every day".
- **The windowsill (the future)**: Placing the box <Highlight>beside his reading glasses, where he would see it every morning</Highlight> shows a quiet personal decision to **begin taking them daily**.

## Why the other options fail

- **Had been taking them every day** directly conflicts with the box sitting untouched in the drawer for months.
- **Stopped only after today's visit** contradicts the months-long gap since his previous appointment.
- **Moving the box only to fool the narrator** is unsupported: placing it beside his own reading glasses for every morning is a practical reminder for himself.
- **Taking tablets from a second box** invents a detail never mentioned in the story.

# Answer

**That he had not been taking the tablets regularly since his previous appointment, and now means to start**.

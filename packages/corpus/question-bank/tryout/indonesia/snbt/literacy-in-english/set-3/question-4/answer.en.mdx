The argument rests on a **contrast between the two candidate sensors**. Weak electromagnetic noise "should have no effect on an iron-based magnet", yet it stopped the robins from orienting, and **blocking the noise restored** their sense of direction. A sensor that weak noise can disturb fits the delicate chemical reactions proposed for cryptochrome much better than an iron magnet. That is why the experiment supports the eye-based idea.

- The experiment did not compare day and night.
- The aluminium screens blocked noise. They did nothing to iron in the birds.
- The robins could not orient in the huts until the screens were added, so the huts were not better than the open air.
- Cryptochrome is described as a retinal protein, and the experiment did not locate it anywhere.

So the experiment is strong support because noise too weak to affect a magnet disrupted the birds, and blocking it restored their orientation.

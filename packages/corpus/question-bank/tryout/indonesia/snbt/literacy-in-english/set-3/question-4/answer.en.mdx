# Given

In <InlineMath math="1969" />, Mpemba and Osborne published a paper reporting **hot water freezing first**. In <InlineMath math="2016" />, British researchers timed **water cooling to zero degrees** and found no sign that hot water got there first.

# Asked

Does the <InlineMath math="2016" /> finding contradict Mpemba and Osborne's <InlineMath math="1969" /> paper?

# Solution

Compare **what each study actually timed** using paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> and the cautious disclaimer at the end of paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />.

> Timing cooling to zero degrees and timing actual freezing are distinct physical events, so finding no head start to zero does not automatically refute freezing first.

## Comparing the two endpoints

Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> stresses that reaching zero degrees and freezing solid are <Highlight>different events, and a sample that wins one race can lose another</Highlight>.

Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> confirms the <InlineMath math="2016" /> study measured only <Highlight>the time taken for water to cool to zero degrees</Highlight> and explicitly adds <Highlight>Their study does not show that hot water can never freeze first in any freezer</Highlight>. Therefore, the <InlineMath math="2016" /> result does **not necessarily** contradict <InlineMath math="1969" />.

## Why the other options fail

- **Yes, <InlineMath math="2016" /> found hot water did not freeze first** swaps the endpoints: <InlineMath math="2016" /> timed cooling to zero, not freezing.
- **Yes, <InlineMath math="1969" /> thermometers were placed wrongly** invents a claim never made about the <InlineMath math="1969" /> apparatus.
- **No, both found hot water hit zero first** contradicts the text stating <InlineMath math="2016" /> found no sign of a hot-water lead.
- **Not necessarily, because <InlineMath math="1969" /> tested ice-cream mixtures** picks the right verdict for the wrong reason: the <InlineMath math="1969" /> paper reported on hot **water** after laboratory tests.

# Answer

**Not necessarily, because the 1969 paper reported hot water freezing first, while the 2016 study timed only cooling to zero degrees, a different event**.

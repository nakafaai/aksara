The research group admits that respondents **described their own eating habits**. Self-reports can differ from behaviour, for example when people want to sound healthier. Evidence that shows **what people actually bought**, such as purchase records from both years, tests the self-reports against behaviour and could also show **how much less** meat they ate, which the survey did not ask.

- Another self-report about enjoying meat is still a self-report, and it asks a different question.
- Doctors' advice describes what people should do, not what they did.
- New vegetarian restaurants say little about whether these respondents ate less meat.
- The list of reasons is already in the survey and does not test whether people ate less.

So the best evidence would be records of each household's meat purchases over both years.

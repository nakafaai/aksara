Paragraph 3 explains the concern: "firms that expected the change to suit them were more likely to sign up", and sectors where staff must be present around the clock were missing. If the participants were **chosen by their own expectations**, their good results may not tell us how **other kinds of employers** would fare. That is why voluntary participation weakens the case for a rule that applies everywhere.

- Pay levels in volunteer companies are not compared with other companies.
- The passage never says voluntary trials are illegal in any sector.
- The author does not accuse companies of reporting false results.
- The length of the trials is not discussed.

So voluntary participation matters because firms expecting to benefit may have joined, so the results may not apply to others.

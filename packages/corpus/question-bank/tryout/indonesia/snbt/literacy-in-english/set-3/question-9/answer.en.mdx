Paragraph 2 says the encouraging results "usually depended on companies cutting unnecessary meetings and redesigning how work was done". A compressed week keeps **the same forty hours** in four longer days and "offers none of those changes". Without the redesign, and with longer days, the benefits may not appear, and people "may" end up **more tired, not less**.

- Paragraph 2 does not say compressed weeks lower pay. The pay point concerns reduced weeks, which keep pay the same.
- Hospitals are discussed in paragraph 3 as sectors missing from the trials, not as users of compressed weeks.
- Cutting meetings is part of the redesign that helped. Nothing says it harmed communication.
- The passage does not say compressed weeks were never tested. It says the good results came mostly from reduced weeks.

So a compressed week may fail because it keeps total hours the same and skips the redesign of work behind the good results.

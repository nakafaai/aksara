# Given

Text B's **<InlineMath math="120" />-tonne** bin-liner rise comes only from <Highlight>the city's three largest supermarket chains</Highlight>. The prompt assumes those chains sell **about half** of the city's bin liners and other shops saw the same rise.

# Asked

Under that assumption, what would follow for the net plastic saved across the whole city each year?

# Solution

**Double** the supermarket chains' <InlineMath math="120" />-tonne rise to cover the entire city first, then subtract that city-wide total from the council's <InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes.

> If three supermarket chains represent only half of city bin-liner sales, the true city-wide jump is two hundred and forty tonnes, cutting net plastic savings down to eighty tonnes.

## Scaling bin liner sales to the whole city

| Scope | Carrier bags avoided | Bin-liner rise | Net plastic saved |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Three chains only (Text B) | <InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes | <InlineMath math="120" /> tonnes | <InlineMath math="200" /> tonnes |
| Whole city (scaled by <InlineMath math="2" />) | <InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes | <InlineMath math="240" /> tonnes | <InlineMath math="80" /> tonnes |

Calculating the **city-wide bin-liner jump** and revised **net saving**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Whole-city bin-liner rise} &= 2 \times 120 = 240 \text{ tonnes} \\ \text{Revised plastic saved} &= 320 - 240 = 80 \text{ tonnes} \end{aligned}" />

This <InlineMath math="80" />-tonne saving is much smaller than the <InlineMath math="200" /> tonnes implied by Text B's partial data alone.

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="260" /> tonnes** halves <InlineMath math="120" /> instead of doubling it, going the wrong direction when scaling up from half the market.
- **Still <InlineMath math="200" /> tonnes** ignores that the other half of the city's shops also sold extra bin liners.
- **<InlineMath math="560" /> tonnes** adds <InlineMath math="240" /> tonnes instead of subtracting replacement plastic.
- **Zero tonnes** would require extra bin liners to reach <InlineMath math="320" /> tonnes, whereas <InlineMath math="240" /> tonnes still leaves <InlineMath math="80" /> tonnes saved.

# Answer

**It would be about <InlineMath math="80" /> tonnes, much less than the figure implied in Text B**.

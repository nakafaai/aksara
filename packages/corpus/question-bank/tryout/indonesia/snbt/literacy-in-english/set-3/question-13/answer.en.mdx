# Given

Both operators ran <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> trips total. **Rural trips** are much easier to run on time (<InlineMath math="90\%" /> and <InlineMath math="85\%" />) than **urban trips** (<InlineMath math="70\%" /> and <InlineMath math="65\%" />), where <Highlight>traffic lights, roadworks and school traffic often hold buses up</Highlight>.

# Asked

Why does Southway hold the higher overall rate (<InlineMath math="81\%" /> vs. <InlineMath math="74\%" />) even though Northline beats it on both route types?

# Solution

Recognise the overall rate as a **weighted average** dominated by whichever route type carries <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of an operator's trips.

> Because rural trips are on time far more often for both operators and account for four fifths of Southway's schedule, route weighting easily outweighs Northline's five-point lead on each route type.

## Weighting mechanism in the table

Southway runs <InlineMath math="800" /> of its <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> trips on open rural roads (<InlineMath math="85\%" /> on time), pulling its overall rate up to <InlineMath math="81\%" />. Northline runs <InlineMath math="800" /> of its <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> trips in congested city traffic (<InlineMath math="70\%" /> on time), pulling its overall rate down to <InlineMath math="74\%" />.

Thus, Southway's lead occurs because **most of Southway's trips are rural**, and rural trips run on time far more often for both operators.

## Why the other options fail

- **Southway ran more trips in total** is false: both operators ran exactly <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> trips.
- **Northline's rural trips run late more often** reverses the table: Northline's <InlineMath math="90\%" /> beats Southway's <InlineMath math="85\%" />.
- **Drivers can afford to be less careful** invents driver behaviour never mentioned in the passage.
- **Urban trips count twice** is false: every trip carries the same single weight in the <InlineMath math="1{,}000" />-trip denominator.

# Answer

**Most of Southway's trips are rural, and rural trips run on time far more often for both operators**.

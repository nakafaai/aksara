The headline presents health as **the** driving force. Two facts in the passage complicate that. In the 18 to 29 group, which had **the highest rate** of eating less meat, the most common reason was **cost**. And the survey took place in a year when meat prices rose sharply, while a supermarket sold less meat by weight as prices climbed. Health may matter a great deal, but cost seems to play a larger role than the headline admits.

- Health was the most common reason in three of the four groups, so it was certainly given.
- The table shows the opposite: younger respondents were more likely to report eating less meat.
- Two thousand respondents is not described as too few.
- Environmental concern was named by one in five, not by most.

So the headline overstates the findings because the group most likely to cut meat cited cost, in a year when prices rose.

Each reading takes a remark either literally or in the sense the story supports. Check each against what happens around it.

Statement 1. This reads "remember" literally, as if wages were paid late. The sentence before it, "Prices go up every month", sets up a contrast about **rising**, not about payday. No.

Statement 2. The narrator watches the hands and finds "They don't do anything. It's the screw." Grandma's two settings never cheat anyone: exact, or giving extra. The advice points her toward what Grandma does, not toward a trick against customers. No.

Statement 3. Prices rise "every month". Wages rise only "when they remember", like a forgetful person, so **rarely and late**. That's why families living on wages, like the nurse's daughter with her coins, need the generous setting. Yes.

Statement 4. The daughter still pays with "a handful of coins". Generous means more rice than the needle shows, not rice for nothing. No.

Statement 5. "Honest" is the needle on zero, "generous" is the needle below zero. Neither gives less, and when the narrator's mistakes did give less, Grandma sent her after the customers with a second cone. Yes.

So statements 3 and 5 are Yes, and statements 1, 2 and 4 are No.

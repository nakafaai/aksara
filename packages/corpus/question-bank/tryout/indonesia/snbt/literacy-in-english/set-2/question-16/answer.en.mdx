You need one moment for **honest** and one for **generous**, and the scale has to be doing each.

**Honest**. Before weighing the rice of the man "in a good coat", Grandma gives the screw a quarter turn, "and the needle sat exactly on zero". A needle on zero with an empty tray weighs correctly.

**Generous**. When the nurse's daughter comes in with "a handful of coins, which she counted twice", Grandma "turned it back", to the setting the narrator found at noon, a hair below zero. Every reading then comes out too low, so the customer gets more rice than the needle shows.

- Both moments here show the generous setting. The empty-tray test and the turn back for the daughter are the same half.
- The zero for the man is the honest half, but the inspector's record isn't generosity. He comes when the market is shut.
- Calling out weights early proves nothing about the setting, and the narrator's mistakes gave **less** rice, which is neither half. Grandma corrected them.
- A wink is gossip, not the scale at work, and the quarter turn alone is only the honest half.

The pair that shows both halves is the needle sitting exactly on zero for the man in the good coat and Grandma turning the screw back for the nurse's daughter.

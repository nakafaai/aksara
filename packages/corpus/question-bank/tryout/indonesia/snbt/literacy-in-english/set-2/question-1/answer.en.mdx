The question asks why one part of the radar study hits Wenner's idea harder than the other part. Wenner said recruits find food by smell.

Start with the bees that left from the hive. Paragraph 5 says most of them "flew fairly straight to the area of the advertised feeder". Wenner had said recruits use "the odour of the site itself" (paragraph 3). A bee homing in on that smell would also end up near the feeder. So this first result **fits both explanations** and can't pick between them.

Now the released bees. They "flew the bearing the dance had given, as though still starting from home, and so missed the feeder". A bee chasing the feeder's smell has no reason to fly a fixed compass line that carries it past the food. Only a bee holding the dance's direction in its head does that. So the second result **fits only the dance explanation**, and that's why it counts more.

Why the other options don't work:

- Reaching the feeder sooner: the released bees missed the feeder, so this flips the result.
- No scent left to follow: the passage never says they lost the scent, and the key fact is the bearing that led them past the food.
- A long search near the feeder: that search belongs to the bees that left from the hive, and the delay was the critics' evidence against von Frisch.
- Repeating Gould: the passage never compares the two bearings, and Gould's test used painted ocelli and a lamp, a different design.

The released bees matter more because flying toward the feeder suits a dance-reader and a scent-follower alike, while flying the dance's bearing from a new spot and missing the food suits only a dance-reader.

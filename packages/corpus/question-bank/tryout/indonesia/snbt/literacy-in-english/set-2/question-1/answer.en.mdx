The question removes one cost on purpose: this warbler never cracks its own eggs. So you need a cost of rejecting that has nothing to do with clumsy ejecting, and then you need to know when that cost wins.

The third paragraph gives it: "Rejection also has a cost when the warbler is wrong", because a host that rejects too readily "will sometimes throw out one of its own eggs that happens to look unusual". That's **a cost of judgement**, not of technique. The last paragraph says when it matters most: "Where cuckoos are rare, a warbler that accepts an odd-looking egg is usually accepting its own." Put the two together. In such a reed bed, most odd eggs are the warbler's own, so a habit of rejecting would **mostly throw out its own eggs**, even with perfect ejecting.

In the experiment, scientists moved **one gene** from one tardigrade species into human cells, and those cells then suffered **noticeably less** DNA damage from X-rays. If a single protein still protects DNA after being moved into a completely different species, then that protein supplies at least part of the tardigrade's protection, and it does not need the rest of the tardigrade's body to work.

- The experiment compares human cells with and without the gene, not human cells with tardigrade cells.
- The protein protected against X-ray damage. Drying was not tested in this experiment.
- The passage says "one tardigrade species" produces the protein, so nothing supports "all species".
- "Noticeably less" damage is not "no damage". That option overstates the result.

So the most reasonable inference is that at least part of the tardigrade's radiation protection comes from a single protein that still works in another species' cells.

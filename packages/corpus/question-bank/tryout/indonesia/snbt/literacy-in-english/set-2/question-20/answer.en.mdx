# Given

In the final paragraph, the narrator operates the screw the following Thursday, turns it **the wrong way twice** so two customers receive less rice than promised, and <Highlight>Grandma made me run after them both with a second cone</Highlight>.

# Asked

What does this final paragraph add to the reader's understanding of Grandma?

# Solution

Read the final incident alongside Grandma's earlier principle (<Highlight>It is honest for some, and generous for others</Highlight>) to see how **strictly one-directional** her rule is.

> Sending the child chasing after two short-changed customers with extra rice proves Grandma's flexible scale bends strictly toward generosity and never tolerates giving anyone less.

## What the mistake reveals

Earlier we see Grandma choose between exact weight (zero) and extra weight (below zero). The final paragraph stress-tests that code through an accident: when the narrator turns the screw the wrong way and gives <Highlight>less rice than the needle had promised</Highlight>, Grandma refuses to let the mistake stand.

Her ethic therefore runs in **one direction only**: a customer may receive more than shown, never less, regardless of who caused the mistake.

## Why the other options fail

- **Losing the stall money to teach a lesson** misses the point: the wrong turn cheated the customers, not the stall, and Grandma's goal was making the customers whole.
- **Generosity only when it costs nothing** is contradicted by sending out two free extra cones at the stall's expense.
- **Trusting the narrator more than her daughter** has no comparison anywhere in the text.
- **No longer worrying about the inspector** is unsupported, while the inspector is never mentioned in the final paragraph.

# Answer

**Her rule works in one direction only: a customer may get more than the needle shows but never less, whoever makes the mistake**.

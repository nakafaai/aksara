Paragraph 3 states the author's view directly. Historians "can say with confidence which example is the oldest to survive", but "a claim about the very first can change whenever new evidence comes to light". A first, in this view, is **provisional**: it rests on **surviving evidence** and can be revised.

- The passage separates being first from being widely used: *Jikji* is older, yet Gutenberg's printing spread more widely.
- The 1234 book is known only from a record, and the author treats it as uncertain rather than as equal to a surviving copy.
- The author describes the problem, not a reason to give up. Identifying the oldest survivor is called something historians can do with confidence.
- *Jikji* is held in Paris and is still recognised as the oldest surviving metal-type book.

So the author would agree that a claim to be the first of its kind depends on surviving evidence and may change when new evidence appears.

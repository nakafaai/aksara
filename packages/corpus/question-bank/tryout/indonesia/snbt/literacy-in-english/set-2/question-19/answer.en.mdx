Paragraph 4 opens with "The idea of movable type is older still", placing Bi Sheng's eleventh-century clay type **before** *Jikji* (1377) and Gutenberg. It then adds a limitation: clay type was fragile, and woodblock printing "remained the usual method in China for centuries". The paragraph does two jobs: it pushes the **idea** further back in time and explains why that early version **did not take over**.

- The paragraph does not connect Bi Sheng to the making of *Jikji*, which the monks printed.
- The paragraph describes woodblock printing in China, not how woodblocks were carved in Korea.
- Clay type is called fragile. Nothing claims it printed more clearly.
- Shen Kuo is mentioned only as the scholar who described the method.

So paragraph 4 shows that movable type as an idea predates both *Jikji* and Gutenberg, while noting why it did not replace woodblock printing in China.

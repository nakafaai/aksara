# Given

At supper, the narrator reveals Grandma's brass screw, and the mother reacts by laughing and citing **thirty years of Monday inspections**. Earlier that morning, the mother also gave specific advice.

# Asked

Which details suggest the narrator's mother already knew what Grandma does with the scale before being told? Select all that apply.

# Solution

Separate **insider clues** pointing directly to the hidden mechanism and inspection timing from generic market gossip or unsupported reactions.

> Telling the child to ignore the needle, laughing instead of worrying at "It's the screw", and knowing why Monday inspections never catch the scale all reveal prior insider knowledge.

## Checking all five details

| Detail | Verdict | Reasoning from the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tells narrator to watch Grandma's hands, not the needle | **True** | Steering attention off the needle (<Highlight>Watch her hands, not the needle</Highlight>) assumes knowing the needle does not tell the whole story and Grandma's hands adjust it. |
| Says the scale is not to be trusted | **False** | Everyone in the market repeats that same line (fish seller and baker included), so it shows no private knowledge. |
| Knows the inspector comes on closed Mondays | **True** | Linking <Highlight>Thirty years</Highlight> of clean checks to <Highlight>Mondays, when the market is shut</Highlight> shows she already knew why the scale is never caught. |
| Is surprised the trick lies in a screw | **False** | Only the narrator is surprised (by the laugh), while the mother shows zero surprise and responds calmly with inspection facts. |
| Laughs rather than looking troubled at "It's the screw" | **True** | Hearing a confession of scale-adjusting with <Highlight>My mother laughed</Highlight> rather than alarm fits someone already in on the secret. |

# Answer

- **On the narrator's first morning, she tells her to watch Grandma's hands rather than the needle**.
- **She knows that the inspector comes on Mondays, when the market is shut**.
- **She laughs, rather than looking troubled, when the narrator says, "It's the screw"**.

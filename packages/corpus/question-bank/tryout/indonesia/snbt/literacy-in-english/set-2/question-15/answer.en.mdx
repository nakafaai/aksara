Combine three pieces of evidence. The questionnaire shows that 45 percent of responding evening visitors had not visited in the previous year, so the evening hours did reach **some recent non-users**. Daytime visits fell by 50 per weekday, which fits the council member's idea that some regular users moved their visits. The head librarian offers a seasonal explanation for the same fall. With the data available, **neither explanation can be ruled out**, so the honest conclusion keeps both the gain and the uncertainty.

- 45 percent is not 100 percent, and the questionnaire asked about the previous twelve months, not about ever having visited.
- The total rose from 510 to 590 visitors per weekday.
- It was the council member, not the head librarian, who linked the daytime fall to the evening hours.
- The passage gives the cost but does not say who paid it.

So the best-supported conclusion is that the evening hours drew some recent non-users, while the data cannot show how many evening visits were moved from the daytime.

# Given

In paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />, the writer opens with <Highlight>This changes what the "failure" of a host means</Highlight>, placing the word **failure** inside quotation marks.

# Asked

Which earlier idea do these quotation marks call into question, and on what grounds?

# Solution

Check both parts of each option: **which earlier idea** is challenged and **which reason** the final paragraph actually gives.

> Quotation marks around a single term signal distance from a previous label, showing that accepting an egg is not blindly losing an arms race when tolerance is the smarter bet.

## Idea challenged and grounds in the text

Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> gives the textbook view being questioned: <Highlight>the textbook account was that the hosts are simply fooled</Highlight>, picturing an arms race the cuckoo is winning.

Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> then supplies the exact grounds: <Highlight>Where cuckoos are rare, a warbler that accepts an odd-looking egg is usually accepting its own, so a habit of tolerance costs little</Highlight>. Calling acceptance a "failure" mislabels what is actually a **sensible bet** in low-risk habitats.

## Why the other options fail

- **Rejecting every cuckoo egg** overstates paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />, which says warblers reject "far more often", not always.
- **Costly rejection** is never questioned by the writer, while it is used to explain why hosts tolerate odd eggs.
- **Poor egg matching** contradicts paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />, which still holds that cuckoo eggs often match host eggs.
- **Most hosts rejecting before hatching** is never claimed, nor is the chick's egg-ejecting behaviour doubted.

# Answer

**The idea in the second paragraph that accepting hosts are simply fooled, on the grounds that acceptance can be a sensible bet where cuckoos are rare**.

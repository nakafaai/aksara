# Given

The city plans to install cameras at **<InlineMath math="60" /> new sites** selected the same way as the first <InlineMath math="40" />, with the same baseline of <InlineMath math="12" /> injury crashes per site over three years (falling to <InlineMath math="7" /> raw). Text B estimates the cameras' own effect at <Highlight>roughly half the raw before-and-after fall</Highlight>.

# Asked

About how many injury crashes would the new cameras themselves prevent across those <InlineMath math="60" /> sites over three years?

# Solution

Chain **three steps** while keeping the three-year window intact: raw fall per site, Text B's halving correction, and scaling to <InlineMath math="60" /> sites.

> Half of the five-crash raw fall per site equals two and a half crashes prevented per site, which across sixty sites gives one hundred and fifty prevented crashes over three years.

## Three step calculation

Using Text A's three-year counts (<InlineMath math="12" /> before, <InlineMath math="7" /> after) and Text B's **half-of-raw estimate**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Raw fall per site (3 years)} &= 12 - 7 = 5 \text{ crashes} \\ \text{Camera effect per site} &= \frac{1}{2} \times 5 = 2.5 \text{ crashes} \\ \text{Total prevented across 60 sites} &= 60 \times 2.5 = 150 \text{ crashes} \end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="300" />** (<InlineMath math="60 \times 5" />) **omits Text B's halving correction** for regression to the mean.
- **<InlineMath math="50" />** (<InlineMath math="150 \div 3" />) converts to a per-year count even though the question asks for three years.
- **<InlineMath math="100" />** (<InlineMath math="40 \times 2.5" />) uses the original <InlineMath math="40" /> sites instead of the <InlineMath math="60" /> new sites.
- **<InlineMath math="420" />** (<InlineMath math="60 \times 7" />) counts crashes remaining after installation rather than crashes prevented.

# Answer

**About <InlineMath math="150" />**

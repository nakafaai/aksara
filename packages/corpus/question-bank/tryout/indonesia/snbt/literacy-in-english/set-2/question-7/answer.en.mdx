Paragraph 2 defines parts pairing precisely: each major component carries a digital code **registered to one particular device**, so even a part from an identical model may trigger warnings or lose functions **until the company's software approves it**. The matching situation must involve a genuine part from the same model being rejected by the device's software. The phone that shows a warning after receiving a screen from an identical phone fits that definition.

- Selling parts only to authorised centres is a different practice named earlier in the paragraph.
- Unusual screws are a physical barrier, not a digital code.
- A paid manual restricts information, which is not pairing.
- A glued battery is also a physical barrier mentioned separately in the paragraph.

So the example of parts pairing is the phone that disables a feature after receiving a genuine screen from an identical phone.

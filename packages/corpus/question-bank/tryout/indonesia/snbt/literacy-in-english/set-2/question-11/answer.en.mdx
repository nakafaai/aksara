# Given

In <InlineMath math="2024" />, the Westbank clinic table records **<InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> appointments (<InlineMath math="120" /> missed)** for signed-up patients and **<InlineMath math="6{,}000" /> appointments (<InlineMath math="600" /> missed)** for patients who did not sign up.

# Asked

What share of the clinic's missed appointments in <InlineMath math="2024" /> belonged to signed-up patients, and how does it compare with their share of all appointments?

# Solution

Compute each fraction using its **own denominator** (total missed appointments vs. total appointments), then compare the two shares directly.

> Signed-up patients accounted for one sixth of all missed appointments against one third of all appointments, so their share of missed appointments was roughly half their share of total visits.

## Comparing the two shares

| Metric (<InlineMath math="2024" />) | Signed-up patients | Clinic total | Signed-up share |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Missed appointments | <InlineMath math="120" /> | <InlineMath math="720" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{6} \approx 17\%" /> |
| All appointments | <InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="9{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3} \approx 33\%" /> |

Total <InlineMath math="2024" /> appointments equal <InlineMath math="3{,}000 + 6{,}000 = 9{,}000" />, and total missed appointments equal <InlineMath math="120 + 600 = 720" />. Computing **both proportions**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Share of missed appointments} &= \frac{120}{720} = \frac{1}{6} \approx 17\% \\ \text{Share of all appointments} &= \frac{3{,}000}{9{,}000} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 33\% \end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{6}" /> is exactly half of <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}" />, their <InlineMath math="17%" /> share of missed appointments is **roughly half** of their one-third share of all appointments.

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="4%" />** (<InlineMath math="120 \div 3{,}000" />) is the signed-up group's own missed rate, not its share of all missed appointments.
- **<InlineMath math="33%" />** mistakes their share of all appointments for their share of missed appointments.
- **<InlineMath math="17%" />, about the same** gets the number right but miscompares one sixth with one third.
- **<InlineMath math="20%" />** divides <InlineMath math="120" /> by the non-signed-up group's <InlineMath math="600" /> instead of the clinic total <InlineMath math="720" />.

# Answer

**About <InlineMath math="17%" />, roughly half of their share of about a third of all appointments**.

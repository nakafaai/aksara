# Given

The **Westbank clinic table** gives <InlineMath math="9{,}000" /> appointments (<InlineMath math="810" /> missed, <InlineMath math="9%" />) in <InlineMath math="2023" />, and <InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> signed-up (<InlineMath math="120" /> missed, <InlineMath math="4%" />) plus <InlineMath math="6{,}000" /> non-signed-up (<InlineMath math="600" /> missed, <InlineMath math="10%" />) in <InlineMath math="2024" />. In <InlineMath math="2023" />, those groups missed **<InlineMath math="5%" />** and **<InlineMath math="11%" />**.

# Asked

Which conclusions can be drawn from the passage and the table? Select all that apply.

# Solution

Verify each candidate conclusion with **exact arithmetic**, paying close attention to the **starting base** of every percentage change.

> Percentage decreases are always measured against the original starting figure rather than the final figure, immediately ruling out both inflated percentage-drop options.

## Verification of all five options

| Conclusion | Verdict | Calculation from data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same number of appointments in <InlineMath math="2023" /> and <InlineMath math="2024" /> | **True** | <InlineMath math="2023" /> had <InlineMath math="9{,}000" /> and <InlineMath math="2024" /> had <InlineMath math="3{,}000 + 6{,}000 = 9{,}000" />. |
| Missed appointments fell by more than <InlineMath math="20%" /> | **False** | Missed visits dropped from <InlineMath math="810" /> to <InlineMath math="720" />, a fall of <InlineMath math="\frac{90}{810} \approx 11%" />. |
| Non-sign-ups missed at more than twice sign-ups' rate in both years | **True** | <InlineMath math="2023" />: <InlineMath math="11% > 2 \times 5% = 10%" />, and <InlineMath math="2024" />: <InlineMath math="10% > 2 \times 4% = 8%" />. |
| Signed-up patients held about a third of <InlineMath math="2024" /> appointments | **True** | <InlineMath math="\frac{3{,}000}{9{,}000} = \frac{1}{3}" /> of all <InlineMath math="2024" /> appointments. |
| Signed-up missed rate fell by a quarter from <InlineMath math="2023" /> to <InlineMath math="2024" /> | **False** | Moving from <InlineMath math="5%" /> to <InlineMath math="4%" /> is a drop of <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{5} = 20%" /> (a fifth, not a quarter). |

# Answer

- **The clinic had the same number of appointments in 2023 as in 2024**.
- **In both years, patients who did not sign up missed appointments at more than twice the rate of patients who did**.
- **Patients who signed up accounted for about a third of all appointments in 2024**.

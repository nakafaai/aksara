The two explanations predict different things. The council member says daytime visits fell **because of the evening hours**: regular users moved their visits later. The head librarian says daytime visits fell **because of the season**: summer holidays always bring a dip. To tell them apart, you need a summer **without** evening hours. Last year's June to August figures, when the library still closed at 6 p.m., provide that test. If daytime visits dipped just as much then, the season explains the fall. If they did not, the evening hours probably drew visitors away from the daytime.

- Evening borrowing says nothing about why daytime visits fell.
- Visitors' opinions about opening hours do not separate the two causes.
- The library's yearly budget is unrelated to the cause of the fall.
- The number of people who work until five or six explains the demand for evening hours, not the daytime drop.

So the most useful information is last summer's daytime visitor numbers, from a time when the library closed at 6 p.m.

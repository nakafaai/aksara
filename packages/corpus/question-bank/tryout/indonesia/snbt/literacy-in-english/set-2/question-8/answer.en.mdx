# Given

In **Text A**, the <InlineMath math="40" /> camera sites averaged <InlineMath math="12" /> injury crashes in the three years before installation and <InlineMath math="7" /> in the three years after. In **Text B**, studies correcting for regression to the mean found <Highlight>the effect of fixed cameras remained real but came out at roughly half the raw before-and-after fall</Highlight>.

# Asked

Assuming Text B's studies apply to this city, what is the most reasonable estimate of the crash drop caused by the cameras themselves?

# Solution

Compute the **raw percentage drop** from Text A first, then take **roughly half** as described in Text B.

> Text B halves the raw before-and-after drop rather than subtracting the city-wide trend, turning a forty-two percent raw drop into roughly twenty-one percent.

## Halving the raw crash drop

From <InlineMath math="12" /> crashes down to <InlineMath math="7" /> crashes per site, the **raw drop** and its **halved estimate** are:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Raw fall} &= \frac{12 - 7}{12} = \frac{5}{12} \approx 41.7\% \approx 42\% \\ \text{Camera effect} &\approx \frac{1}{2} \times 42\% = 21\% \end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="42%" />** is the raw fall before applying Text B's regression-to-the-mean correction.
- **<InlineMath math="34%" />** subtracts the <InlineMath math="8%" /> city-wide fall, which follows Text A's method rather than Text B's half-of-raw correction.
- **<InlineMath math="8%" />** is the city-wide fall across all roads, not the camera effect.
- **<InlineMath math="0%" />** contradicts Text B, which says the camera effect <Highlight>remained real</Highlight>.

# Answer

**About <InlineMath math="21%" />, roughly half of the fall that the counts show**.

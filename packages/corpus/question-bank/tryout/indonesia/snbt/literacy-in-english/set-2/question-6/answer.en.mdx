# Given

**Text A** treats the gap between the <InlineMath math="40%+" /> crash drop at <InlineMath math="40" /> camera sites and the <InlineMath math="8%" /> city-wide drop as the cameras' achievement. **Text B** argues those sites were chosen after an unusually bad run of crashes and would drop by chance anyway (**regression to the mean**).

# Asked

Which statement would the writer of Text B accept while the writer of Text A would reject?

# Solution

Apply **both filters** at once: Text B must endorse the statement, and Text A's core comparison must fail if the statement is true.

> Text A assumes camera sites would have matched the city's modest decline without cameras, whereas Text B argues sites picked after an extreme spike naturally fall faster even with no intervention.

## Comparing both writers on the key claim

Text B states explicitly: <Highlight>A site picked straight after a bad stretch will therefore tend to have fewer crashes afterwards even if nothing is done, a pattern statisticians call regression to the mean</Highlight>.

Because the whole city was not selected after an unusual crash spike, **Text B accepts** that the camera sites would probably have fallen by more than the city even without cameras. **Text A rejects** this, because Text A claims <Highlight>The gap between those two figures is the clearest measure we have of what the cameras achieved</Highlight>.

## Why the other options fail

- **Crashes fell at camera sites** is accepted by both writers. Text B opens with <Highlight>Nobody should doubt that the crash counts at the camera sites went down</Highlight>.
- **Effect as large as counts suggest** reverses the writers: Text A accepts it, Text B rejects it.
- **City comparison measures camera achievement** also reverses the writers: it is Text A's claim, rejected by Text B.
- **Cameras had no effect** is rejected by both writers. Text B writes the effect <Highlight>remained real</Highlight>.

# Answer

**Even without cameras, the camera sites would probably have fallen by more than the city as a whole**.

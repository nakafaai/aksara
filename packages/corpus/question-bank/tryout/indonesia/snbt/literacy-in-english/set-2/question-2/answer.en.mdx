# Given

Five candidate conclusions about reed warblers must be evaluated as **Yes** or **No** using the passage on cuckoos and host rejection.

# Asked

Which of the five statements can be concluded from the passage?

# Solution

Each statement combines two ideas from the passage, so **verify both halves** directly against the text before marking Yes or No.

> A conclusion holds only when the passage supports every link in the claim without reversing experimental evidence or turning a tendency into an absolute rule.

## Evaluation of each statement

| Statement | Verdict | Supporting evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" />. Field experiments confirm hosts fail to notice cuckoo eggs | **No** | Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> opens <Highlight>Field experiments complicate this picture</Highlight> and adds <Highlight>The hosts are not blind</Highlight>. |
| <InlineMath math="2" />. A warbler that has not seen a cuckoo may keep an odd egg where cuckoos are common | **Yes** | Warblers <Highlight>reject far more often when they have just seen a cuckoo near the nest than when they have not</Highlight>, so without a sighting acceptance remains possible. |
| <InlineMath math="3" />. Egg matching is the main evidence that hosts weigh risks | **No** | Egg matching supports the textbook arms-race view in paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />, not the risk-weighing evidence in paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />. |
| <InlineMath math="4" />. Risk of discarding own unusual eggs makes acceptance pay where cuckoos are rare | **Yes** | Paragraphs <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> state overly eager hosts throw out their own unusual eggs, while where cuckoos are rare an odd egg is usually their own. |
| <InlineMath math="5" />. If cuckoos returned in England, rejection should rise within years | **Yes** | Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> notes <Highlight>Individual birds seem to adjust their caution to the risk around them</Highlight> faster than natural selection alone. |

# Answer

Statements **<InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="4" />, and <InlineMath math="5" />** are **Yes**, while statements **<InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />** are **No**.

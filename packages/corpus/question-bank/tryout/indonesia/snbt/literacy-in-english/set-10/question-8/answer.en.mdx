"Cannot see" here isn't about eyesight. You need the sentences around the phrase to know what stays hidden.

The paragraph says stories from families "for whom it works" are what we hear most, "since families for whom it went badly are far less likely to write about it." Then comes the phrase, followed by a colon and its own definition: "those whose parents have neither the time nor the training that the success stories take for granted." Paragraph 2 adds why: the studies rely on volunteers, and families doing well "have more reason to volunteer."

So the children we "cannot see" are the ones **whose results never reach us**, above all those whose parents lack what the success stories had. What's hidden is how these children are doing.

- Being rarely seen in public is the literal reading. Text 1 even shows homeschooled children out at co-ops and the library.
- Families who never told anyone come from paragraph 1, which is about counting. The last paragraph is about how children fare, and its colon names the group by the parents' time and training.
- Children who went back to school also come from paragraph 1, and they aren't homeschooled any more.
- Bullied children who flourish are the success stories. They're the group we do hear about.

The phrase means homeschooled children whose progress goes unreported, especially those whose parents lack the time or training the success stories assume.

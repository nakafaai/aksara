# Given

The closing sentence of **Text 2** warns that before deciding **how much oversight homeschooling needs**, researchers must learn about "the children we currently cannot see", whose parents lack the time or training assumed in success stories.

# Asked

Which specific proposal in Text 1 does that closing sentence push back against?

# Solution

> Before deciding how much oversight homeschooling needs, we should learn much more about the children we currently cannot see: those whose parents have neither the time nor the training that the success stories take for granted.

Match the researcher's focus on **deciding oversight rules** directly to the one policy proposal made in Text 1.

## Matching oversight policy to the parent proposal

Only one statement in Text 1 proposes how to handle rules and oversight: <Highlight>the burden of proof should fall on those who want to tighten the rules</Highlight>. By insisting we first learn about unseen children before deciding how much oversight is needed, Text 2 pushes directly against **shifting the burden of proof** onto advocates of stronger rules.

The other options are not policy proposals about oversight. Co-op classes and soccer address friendships. Census figures from <InlineMath math="5.4\%" /> to <InlineMath math="11.1\%" /> measure prevalence. "Study after study" is evidence challenged earlier in Text 2, and knowing her own children's habits is her reply about teacher training.

# Answer

The sentence pushes back against the proposal **that the burden of proof should fall on those who want to tighten the rules**.

# Given

The ant-foraging passage explains how **slow pheromone fading** locks colonies onto an old route, how faster evaporation helps switching, and warns that one evolutionary claim remains unproven.

# Asked

Which statements about pheromone evaporation and ant route choice can be concluded from the passage.

# Solution

**Each statement needs its** own evidence, and most of them join two paragraphs.

> Footprints in wet cement stay forever and trap everyone on the old path, while footprints in light chalk fade fast enough to let the group switch to a better shortcut.

## Checking each claim against the ant experiments

**Statement <InlineMath math="1" />**. Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> says the old route kept its lead partly because the pheromone "fades slowly enough for the old route to keep its lead." Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> adds that raising evaporation "makes a colony more willing to abandon an old route for a newly opened better one." Faster fading on the long branch removes part of that lead, so switching becomes more likely. The word "probably" keeps the claim as careful as the passage. Yes.

**Statement <InlineMath math="2" />**. Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> calls this idea "attractive but **far from established**." A claim that studies have shown it goes beyond the passage. No.

**Statement <InlineMath math="3" />**. Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> explains the first choice by timing alone: short round trips get re-marked sooner. Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> then says fading "does little to explain that head start, but it matters a great deal once a trail exists." That's exactly the contrast in the statement. Yes.

**Statement <InlineMath math="4" />**. Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> sets a ceiling on fast fading: "If the scent vanishes too quickly, no trail lasts long enough to recruit anyone." The statement says the same thing more cautiously, with "might". Yes.

# Answer

**Only the claim calling an unproven hypothesis established fails. the other statements follow directly from the colony findings**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Faster fading would make switching in the delayed test more likely | Yes |
| Studies have shown faster trails in species with moving food | No |
| Evaporation matters more for dropping a route than for the first choice | Yes |
| Scent that vanishes too fast may recruit no one | Yes |

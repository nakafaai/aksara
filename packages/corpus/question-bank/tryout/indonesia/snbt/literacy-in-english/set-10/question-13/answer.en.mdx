# Given

**The passage gives quarterly** cancellations, starting subscriber totals, an analyst's exact churn definition, a price rise, and Lumen's claim of healthy sorting.

# Asked

Which statements about Lumen's churn rates and rival explanations can be concluded.

# Solution

Keep three things apart: what the table lets you **calculate**, what the analyst's **definition** asks for, and what each side **claims** about causes.

> A single cancellation total cannot tell you whether a customer left because the ticket got pricier or because their free trial just ended.

## Separating calculations definitions and unproven causes

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. The four divisions give <InlineMath math="20 \div 400 = 5\%" />, <InlineMath math="22 \div 440 = 5\%" />, <InlineMath math="60 \div 480 = 12.5\%" /> and <InlineMath math="58 \div 580 = 10\%" />. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />. The analyst defines churn as the share "of the subscribers at the start of a quarter" who cancel. But the passage says nobody published "how many came from people who had signed up during the same quarter in which they cancelled". The cancellations column may mix both groups, so dividing it by the starting count only **approximates** her rate. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />. The analyst says churn more than doubled, and she also says that two changes on the same day "could not be told apart". Her own point means the figures cannot show that the price caused the rise. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />. The company's account needs the share of cancellations that came from trial accounts, and "neither side published" that figure. The table neither confirms nor refutes it. No.

# Answer

**The four churn ratios and the approximation caveat hold, while neither side's causal story is proven by the table**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Churn rates of <InlineMath math="5\%" />, <InlineMath math="5\%" />, <InlineMath math="12.5\%" /> and <InlineMath math="10\%" /> | Yes |
| The table only approximates the analyst's rate | Yes |
| The price rise doubled churn | No |
| The table confirms "healthy sorting" | No |

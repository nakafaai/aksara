A physics class investigated whether the arrangement of cells changes the terminal voltage across a fixed load. They used two <InlineMath math="1.5\,\text{V}" /> cells from the same pack, the same resistor, meter, leads, room temperature, and ten-second measurement interval. They also rotated the cells between arrangements to reduce the effect of one cell consistently occupying the same position.

| Condition | Mean terminal voltage |
| --- | ---: |
| One cell | <InlineMath math="1.46\,\text{V}" /> |
| Two cells in series | <InlineMath math="2.78\,\text{V}" /> |
| Two cells in parallel | <InlineMath math="1.44\,\text{V}" /> |

The series arrangement produced nearly twice the voltage of the other arrangements, but the class did not call this a universal rule. The students had tested only one resistance and had not measured each cell's internal resistance. Their data therefore could not establish how the arrangements would compare in current, power, or operating time under different loads.

The report defined *potential difference* as energy transferred per unit charge between two points. For a follow-up, the class proposed measuring both voltage and current across several resistors while also estimating each cell's internal resistance.


What role does the definition of *potential difference* play in the passage's reasoning?

The parent offers three kinds of evidence: her own children, the Census figures, and test-score studies. The question asks what Text 2 does with each of them.

Text 2 accepts the first two in part. It says "The surge in homeschooling since 2020 is real," and that "many homeschooled children do very well, which I do not doubt." Then it turns to "The larger problem": the test-score studies rely on volunteers, so they "cannot tell us how a typical homeschooled child performs, or whether learning at home is the reason the high scorers score high." In paragraph 3 it adds that stories from families "for whom it works" are the evidence we'd expect to hear most.

So Text 2 **keeps the facts but rejects the causal step**. Neither the studies nor one family's story shows that homeschooling causes the good results.

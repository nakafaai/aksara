The author agrees with the refusal to register images made **entirely** by AI, because **a prompt only makes a request** while the system makes the creative choices. Yet the author also says an illustrator who repaints, combines, and arranges AI material **has made real creative choices** that the law should protect. The position is that protection should follow **the creative choices a person makes**.

- The author says explicitly that not everything touched by AI should go unprotected.
- Cameras are mentioned to show that a tool doing much of the work does **not** rule out protection.
- The author argues that a prompt writer is **unlike** a photographer.
- The training question is left open, and no ban is proposed.

So the author's main position is that copyright should follow a person's creative choices, not a prompt alone.

You need a case with the same three parts as the author's reading of the stuck colony.

The parts come from paragraphs 3 and 5. First, the choice comes from **copying what others already do**: ants follow "whichever trail smells strongest". Second, that copying keeps a poor route in use after a better one appears, since the colony "can stay with a poor route long after a better one appears." Third, the habit usually pays: "where good food sources tend to stay put, sticking with a working route is usually worth more."

The villagers match all three. Each walks where most others walk, they stay on the worn path after a shorter one exists, and the habit prevents wrong turns while the well doesn't move.

- The hiker compares the trails and makes a personal mistake. In the passage, "No ant had to compare the two lengths," and the result isn't anyone's error.
- The shop is held by a contract, a rule from outside. Nothing in that case grows stronger through use, and the habit brings no benefit.
- The stadium crowd moving to the faster exit is the success story of paragraph 2, where the better option wins. It isn't the stuck case.
- The courier firm checks every morning, which is the "constantly checking for a better one" that the author says is usually worth less.

The closest match is the villagers who keep the worn path because everyone follows it, a habit that helps while the well stays put.

# Given

Paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> interprets a stuck ant colony as a system that **follows existing traffic**, stays on a worse route after a shortcut appears, and **benefits from that reliability** whenever the target stays in one place.

# Asked

Which everyday situation most closely matches the author's interpretation of the stuck ant colony?

# Solution

> Yet where good food sources tend to stay put, sticking with a working route is usually worth more than constantly checking for a better one.

Look for an analogy containing **all three pieces**: individuals follow where others already go, the group stays on an older longer path after a shorter one opens, and the habit **usually pays off** because the destination stays fixed.

## Matching all three traits of the stuck colony

Villagers who keep walking a worn path to the well after a shorter path is cleared match every element: <Highlight>each walks where most others walk</Highlight>, they stay with the working older route, and the habit prevents wrong turns while <Highlight>the well stays put</Highlight>.

The other cases miss key parts of this **collective trade-off**. A hiker misreading a map and sticking out of pride is an individual mistake, while a binding supplier contract is an external rule rather than self-reinforcing following.

Stadium crowds shifting to a faster exit match the successful short-path case, and daily courier rerouting matches the costly constant rechecking the author contrasts with ants.

# Answer

The closest match is: **villagers keep walking a worn path to the well after a shorter one is cleared, since each walks where most others walk, a habit that prevents wrong turns while the well stays put**.

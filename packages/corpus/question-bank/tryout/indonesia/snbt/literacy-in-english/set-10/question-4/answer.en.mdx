# Given

In the delayed bridge test, most Argentine ant colonies stayed on the long branch after a shortcut was added. Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> explains this through **lingering pheromone on the old branch** that fades too slowly for the new shortcut to outcompete.

# Asked

Which hypothetical finding would most seriously weaken that pheromone explanation for Argentine ant colonies staying on the long branch?

# Solution

> The usual explanation is that the few ants that wandered onto the new branch laid too little pheromone to compete with the old route's scent, and that this species' trail pheromone fades slowly enough for the old route to keep its lead.

Test the causal claim by **removing the proposed cause** (the old scent) and seeing whether the effect (staying on the long branch) still happens just as often.

## Removing the scent while the effect remains

If researchers <Highlight>wiped off</Highlight> the old branch's scent the moment the shortcut was added and colonies **still stayed on the long branch just as often**, then lingering pheromone could not be what held them there. Some other mechanism, such as spatial memory, would have to explain their persistence.

Every other choice leaves the explanation intact or strengthens it. Switching far more often after wiping off the scent directly confirms the explanation. Fast-evaporating simulations failing to settle illustrate paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />'s ceiling. A few colonies taking the long branch from the start fits "In most trials", and another species' memory does not test Argentine ants here.

# Answer

The finding that would most seriously weaken the explanation is that **in a repeat of the delayed test, colonies stayed on the long branch just as often when its scent was wiped off at the moment the shortcut was added**.

You need details that are **about the father** and that **show a feeling** the narrator never names. A detail that belongs to someone else, or that only sets the scene, doesn't count.

- **Shows it**. Stopping the engine halfway and drifting in silence is a pause, a last look, with no words. It shows the moment matters to him.
- **Shows it**. "A bad knee" is an excuse, and the narrator offers it without comment. He avoids the opening because watching it would be hard.
- **Doesn't.** The cake shows what the townspeople feel, not what the father feels.
- **Doesn't.** The three summers of building set the scene. They say nothing about him.
- **Shows it**. Counting the first cars under his breath is the habit of thirty-one years carrying on. It shows how hard it is for him to let the work go.

The narrator shows his feelings through three details: the silent drift on the last crossing, the bad-knee excuse, and counting the first cars on the bridge.

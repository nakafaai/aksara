# Given

The narrator's father never verbally expresses sadness or grief over the ferry's closure, so the reader must infer his feelings from **his own deliberate choices and quiet actions**.

# Asked

Which pair of details best reveals what the father feels about the ferry's end without his stating it?

# Solution

> Halfway over, he cut the engine and let us drift, and for a minute there was no sound but the water slapping the hull. I waited for him to say something. He didn't.

Both details in the winning pair must be **actions chosen by the father himself** that indirectly reveal his attachment and reluctance to let go.

## Checking both halves for the father actions

Cutting the engine to <Highlight>let us drift</Highlight> in silence on the last crossing shows him savouring one final wordless moment on the river, and staying home from the bridge opening with a <Highlight>bad knee</Highlight> excuse shows he cannot bring himself to celebrate the bridge replacing his ferry.

Every other option includes at least one detail that is **not the father's own action**: a woman's cake and the council man's speech come from others, selling the ferry for parts is a business fact, the bridge's three-summer growth is setting, and the two boys or test lights happen around him.

# Answer

The pair of details that best shows his unstated feelings is: **he cuts the engine halfway over on the last crossing and lets the boat drift in silence, and the next day he stays home from the opening with a "bad knee"**.

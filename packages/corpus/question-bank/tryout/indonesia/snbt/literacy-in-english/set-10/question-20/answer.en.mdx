The story **shows and never states**, so check each statement against what the father does.

Statement 1. He never says what he feels. The drift, the bad knee and the counting all leave it to the reader. Yes.

Statement 2. The narrator never judges the bridge. She reports the council man's view and her father's silence, and leaves it there. No.

Statement 3. He makes a twelfth crossing "because, he said, the timetable had never said anything about a last day". He uses the ferry's own rules to stretch its last day. Yes.

Statement 4. He reads the whole ledger back "for a long time" and keeps it beside him the next morning. That is the opposite of deciding it never mattered. No.

Statement 5. He watches and counts the cars, but he stayed home from the opening with an excuse. Watching isn't the same as fully accepting. No.

So statements 1 and 3 are Yes, and statements 2, 4 and 5 are No.

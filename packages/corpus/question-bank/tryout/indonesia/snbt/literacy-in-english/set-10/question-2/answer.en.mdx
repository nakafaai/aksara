# Given

When both bridge branches opened together, ants spread randomly at first. Ants on the shorter branch returned sooner, adding a second coat of pheromone earlier so traffic snowballed onto it. The passage says the comparison **was made, in effect, by the clock**.

# Asked

Which situation works in the exact same way as a comparison made "by the clock"?

# Solution

> But those that happened to take the short branch got back to the nest sooner, so the short branch received its second coat of pheromone, on the return trip, before the long one did.

Match **two essential mechanisms**: nobody consciously compares or measures the routes, and **shorter trip duration** builds a stronger physical trail faster.

## Matching the clock mechanism to the lawn paths

When walkers on the shorter path across a lawn finish sooner and cross again sooner, that path wears bare first and later walkers simply follow <Highlight>the bare path without measuring either</Highlight>. Just like the ants, **time does the sorting** and positive feedback amplifies the head start.

The other cases break the mechanism. Choosing a better view or a gardener measuring with stones involves deliberate comparison. Copying experienced walkers relies on social imitation rather than trip speed, and more walkers starting on the longer path creates a head start from random initial numbers rather than finishing sooner.

# Answer

The matching situation is: **walkers on the shorter of two paths across a lawn finish sooner and cross again sooner, so it wears bare first, and later walkers follow the bare path without measuring either**.

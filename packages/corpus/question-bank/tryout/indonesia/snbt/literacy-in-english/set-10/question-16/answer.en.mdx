# Given

On the ferry's final day after thirty-one years, the father speaks flatly on the phone, nods past the council man's clichés, makes a twelfth crossing, cuts the engine mid-river in silence, reads his ledger back to the first year, and skips the bridge opening with a **"bad knee"**.

# Asked

Which conclusions about the father on the ferry's last day are suggested by the narrator's account?

# Solution

> Halfway over, he cut the engine and let us drift, and for a minute there was no sound but the water slapping the hull. I waited for him to say something. He didn't.

Read the father's **quiet actions and silences** rather than expecting spoken declarations of emotion.

## Evaluating all five character claims

| Claim | Story evidence | Suggested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marks the occasion in his own quiet ways, not others' words | Nods past clichés, makes a <Highlight>twelfth crossing</Highlight>, drifts silently, reads ledger | **Yes** |
| Openly resents riders who came only for the last day | Lets two boys ride free four times, showing warmth rather than resentment | **No** |
| Looking forward to retiring and relieved the bridge is done | Flat weather-voice, silent drift, and skipping opening show quiet grief, not relief | **No** |
| More moved by the ending than his manner lets on | Silent drift, reading <Highlight>back to the first year</Highlight>, counting cars from window | **Yes** |
| Believes the new bridge will be unsafe in high water | His high-river remark mocks the council man's ignorance of the boat, not bridge safety | **No** |

Notice the **central contrast** between his restrained manner and his deeply felt actions: he never gives a speech, yet every quiet gesture reveals how much the thirty-one years meant to him.

# Answer

The narrator's account suggests:

- He marks the occasion in his own quiet ways rather than in the words that others use for it.
- He is more moved by the ending than his manner lets on.

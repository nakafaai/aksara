The ledger appears three times: as a daily habit, at the final entry, and on the windowsill the next day. Each statement needs its own evidence.

Statement 1. The narrator "had never once seen him read it", and when he turns the pages back she calls it "something I had never seen him do". Yes.

Statement 2. In the last scene the ledger lies "closed on the sill" while he counts the first cars "under his breath", the same kind of counting he wrote down for thirty-one years. The closed book and **the counting habit** tie the new scene to his old work. Yes.

Statement 3. The story never says why he kept the ledger. A company rule is invented. No.

Statement 4. "River quiet" is simply that night's entry, like every entry's "state of the river". Nothing compares it with other years or seasons. No.

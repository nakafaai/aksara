# Given

For thirty-one years the father wrote **the time, the number of passengers, and the state of the river** into a green ledger that the narrator had never once seen him read until the final night. Next morning, the closed ledger rests on the windowsill as he counts the first cars crossing the bridge.

# Asked

Based on the story, can each of the five statements about the green ledger be inferred (**Yes** or **No**)?

# Solution

> Between crossings he wrote each trip into the green ledger he kept under the wheel: the time, the number of passengers, the state of the river. I had seen that ledger all my life and had never once seen him read it.

Verify every statement against the **exact details of how he used, read, and kept the ledger**.

## Evaluating all five ledger statements

| Statement | Direct story evidence | Inferred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | Narrator had <Highlight>never once seen him read it</Highlight> before that night | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <Highlight>the ledger closed on the sill</Highlight> beside him links the ending to his decades of entries | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | Story never says the ferry company required the ledger | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | Every entry recorded <Highlight>the state of the river</Highlight>, so "river quiet" is his standard format | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | Counting cars <Highlight>under his breath</Highlight> continues the passenger-counting habit of the ledger | **Yes** |

Notice how **Statements <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="4" />, and <InlineMath math="5" />** trace the ledger's quiet symbolism from his routine format ("river quiet") to the closing image of counting bridge cars with the closed ledger on the sill.

# Answer

- Statement <InlineMath math="1" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="2" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="3" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="4" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="5" />: **Yes**

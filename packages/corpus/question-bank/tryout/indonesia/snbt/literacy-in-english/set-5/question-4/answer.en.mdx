Naloxone **blocks the body's own opioid chemicals**. When it was added, much of the placebo's pain relief **disappeared**. If blocking those chemicals removes the relief, the relief must partly depend on them. That is why the passage concludes that expectation "can trigger real chemistry in the brain".

- Naloxone took relief away, so it is not acting as a painkiller here.
- The patients reported less pain after the placebo, which assumes they had pain.
- The pills were dummy treatments. Their effect came from the body's own chemicals, not hidden drugs.
- The result shows the opposite: expectation changes brain chemistry.

So the result most strongly suggests that part of a placebo's relief depends on chemicals the body itself releases.

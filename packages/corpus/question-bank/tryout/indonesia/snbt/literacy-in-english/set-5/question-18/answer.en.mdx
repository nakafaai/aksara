Paragraph 4 says the system succeeded "partly for a reason its builders did not fully understand". The sewers were built **to remove a smell**, because builders believed in miasma. Cholera, however, spreads through **water**, as Snow had shown. By keeping sewage away from drinking water, the sewers removed a route for cholera anyway. They worked against the disease **even though the reason for building them was mistaken**.

- The success came from cutting a water route, which supports Snow, not the miasma theory.
- In 1866 cholera struck hardest where the system did not yet reach, which supports the sewers' value.
- Snow traced an outbreak. The sewers were Bazalgette's design.
- The passage says nothing about the smell growing worse in the east.

So paragraph 4 suggests that the sewers reduced cholera even though they were built for a mistaken reason.

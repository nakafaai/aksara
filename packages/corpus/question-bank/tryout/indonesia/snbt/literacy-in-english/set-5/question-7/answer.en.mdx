# Given

Text <InlineMath math="1" /> **opposes building new large** nuclear reactors and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> supports them, while sharing several factual premises about timing and costs.

# Asked

Which views are held by both writers together.

# Solution

A statement gets Yes only if **both writers hold it**. If one writer holds it and the other rejects it or never says it, the answer is No.

> Two people arguing over which bus to take can still agree that the first bus always arrives late and that getting there early matters most.

## Checking whether both texts agree on each view

**Late and over budget**. Text <InlineMath math="1" /> says the recent reactors "opened years behind schedule and far over their first budgets." Text <InlineMath math="2" /> replies, "The overruns are real." Both hold it, so Yes.

**Batteries already enough**. Text <InlineMath math="1" /> says batteries "cannot yet carry a whole grid through a windless winter week." Its hope that storage "may well have closed the gap" is about the future, not now. Text <InlineMath math="2" /> doubts storage too. Neither writer holds this, so No.

**Later units cheaper**. Only Text <InlineMath math="2" /> hints at this, with "Whether the fourth is depends on whether we build a fourth." Even there it's a condition. Text <InlineMath math="1" /> never says it. One-sided, so No.

**Early cuts worth more**. Text <InlineMath math="1" />: "a tonne not emitted in <InlineMath math="2030" /> does more good than a tonne avoided in <InlineMath math="2045" />." Text <InlineMath math="2" />: "early cuts do count most." Both hold it, so Yes.

**Plant-by-plant comparison is fair**. This is the trap. Text <InlineMath math="2" /> says "I do not dispute it" about the plant-by-plant numbers, but then adds, "A grid, though, is not a pile of separate plants." Text <InlineMath math="2" /> accepts the numbers and **rejects them as the basis for the choice**. So No.

# Answer

Both writers agree on recent reactor overruns and on the higher value of cutting emissions this decade, while differing on series discounts and cost comparisons.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Recent reactors were late and over budget | Yes |
| Batteries can already carry a windless spell | No |
| A series of one design lowers later costs | No |
| Early emission cuts are worth more | Yes |
| Plant-by-plant cost is a fair basis | No |

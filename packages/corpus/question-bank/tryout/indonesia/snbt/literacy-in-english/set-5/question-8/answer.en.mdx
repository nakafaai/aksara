# Given

In paragraph <InlineMath math="1" /> of **Text 2**, the writer explains nuclear overruns via **lost workforce skills** after a generation-long pause and states <Highlight>Whether the fourth is depends on whether we build a fourth</Highlight>.

# Asked

Which tram-line scenario follows the exact same reasoning?

# Solution

Look for **both links in Text 2's learning-curve logic**: a **first project runs late and over budget due to decades without building**, and **later projects become cheaper only by continuing to build locally** so crews regain expertise.

> Rebuilding lost domestic expertise requires keeping projects going so crews learn from the first build, matching planners who argue later tram lines get cheaper only if the city keeps building them.

## Matching the learning curve logic

Text 2 traces overruns to **atrophied local skill**: <Highlight>Most Western countries stopped building reactors for a generation, and the skills left with the workers who retired. A first reactor built by crews who have never built one will be slow and costly. Whether the fourth is depends on whether we build a fourth</Highlight>.

The matching tram case shares the **exact same cause and remedy**: no local firm has built a line in decades, so continuing to build is what brings later costs down.

## Why the other options fail

- **Stop after one line because a second costs just as much** rejects the learning curve instead of applying it.
- **Hire a foreign firm for every later line** imports outside skill instead of rebuilding experience through repeated projects.
- **Worldwide tram-car prices falling** credits an external market drop rather than learning by building.
- **First three lines opened on time** lacks any first-time cost overrun caused by missing skills.

# Answer

**A city's first tram line runs late and over budget because no local firm has built one in decades, and its planners argue that later lines will come cheaper only if the city keeps building them**.

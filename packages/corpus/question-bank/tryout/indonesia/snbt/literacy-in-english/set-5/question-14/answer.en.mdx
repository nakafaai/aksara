# Given

The health office explains that Eastport reached **<InlineMath math="95\%" /> overall coverage** yet still suffered **<InlineMath math="38" /> cases** centered in one private school because <Highlight>a pocket of unvaccinated children can sustain an outbreak inside a well-vaccinated area</Highlight>.

# Asked

Which types of information would help the health office deploy a catch-up vaccination team where it prevents the most cases? Choose all that apply.

# Solution

Choose data that pinpoints **local clusters of unvaccinated children** rather than broad district or provincial averages that hide pockets like Eastport's school.

> Broad averages mask local clusters of unvaccinated children, so a catch-up team needs school-level counts of unvaccinated pupils and exact information on which schools and communities produced the cases.

## Evaluating all five data options

| Proposed information | Verdict | Why it helps or fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unvaccinated children in each school, not only each district | **Helpful** | Directly exposes hidden school-level pockets like Eastport's private school. |
| District coverage, skipping districts at <InlineMath math="95\%" /> | **Not helpful** | Eastport hit <InlineMath math="95\%" /> yet still had <InlineMath math="38" /> cases, so skipping <InlineMath math="95\%" /> districts misses major clusters. |
| Which communities and schools the outbreak's cases came from | **Helpful** | Pinpoints exactly where the virus already found vulnerable clusters. |
| Total children in each district | **Not helpful** | Riverside is the largest district (<InlineMath math="50{,}000" />) yet had only <InlineMath math="8" /> cases, so population size alone misdirects teams. |
| Provincial coverage over the past five years | **Not helpful** | Province-wide historical totals reveal zero local pockets. |

# Answer

- **The number of unvaccinated children in each school, not only in each district**.
- **Which communities and schools the outbreak's cases came from**.

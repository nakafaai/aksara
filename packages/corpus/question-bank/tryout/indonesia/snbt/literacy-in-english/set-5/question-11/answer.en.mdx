# Given

**The measles passage and** district table report child populations, two-dose vaccination coverage, and case counts across four districts.

# Asked

Which statements about unvaccinated children and case rates follow from the table.

# Solution

Most statements need two numbers worked out first: **unvaccinated children** (children times the share without both doses) and **cases for every <InlineMath math="100{,}000" /> children**.

> A small village with four sick children can actually be hit harder per family than a huge city with eight sick children.

## Computing unvaccinated counts and per population rates

| District | Unvaccinated children | Cases per <InlineMath math="100{,}000" /> |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Riverside | <InlineMath math="4\% \times 50{,}000 = 2{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="8 \div 50{,}000 \times 100{,}000 = 16" /> |
| Hillcrest | <InlineMath math="5\% \times 20{,}000 = 1{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="4 \div 20{,}000 \times 100{,}000 = 20" /> |
| Northgate | <InlineMath math="20\% \times 10{,}000 = 2{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="70 \div 10{,}000 \times 100{,}000 = 700" /> |
| Eastport | <InlineMath math="5\% \times 20{,}000 = 1{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="38 \div 20{,}000 \times 100{,}000 = 190" /> |

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. Both districts have <InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> **children without both doses**, and <InlineMath math="10{,}000" /> is a fifth of <InlineMath math="50{,}000" />. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />. The simple average of the four percentages is <InlineMath math="91.5\%" />, not <InlineMath math="94\%" />.

<BlockMath math="(96 + 95 + 80 + 95) \div 4 = 91.5\%" />

The provincial figure counts every child, so big Riverside weighs more.

<BlockMath math="(2{,}000 + 1{,}000 + 2{,}000 + 1{,}000) \div 100{,}000 = 6\%" />

That leaves <InlineMath math="6\%" /> unvaccinated, which is <InlineMath math="94\%" /> covered. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />. Add the cases across all four districts to find the total.

<BlockMath math="8 + 4 + 70 + 38 = 120" />

There are <InlineMath math="120" /> cases in total, and Northgate's <InlineMath math="70" /> is more than half. Northgate holds <InlineMath math="10{,}000" /> of <InlineMath math="100{,}000" /> children, a tenth. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />. Eastport and Hillcrest have the same number of children, and Eastport had <InlineMath math="38" /> cases against <InlineMath math="4" />. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="5" />. <InlineMath math="190 \div 20 = 9.5" />, which is more than nine. Yes.

# Answer

**Converting percentages into headcounts and dividing cases by district size settles all five statements cleanly**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Northgate had as many unvaccinated children as Riverside | Yes |
| <InlineMath math="94\%" /> is the simple average of the four percentages | No |
| Over half the cases came from a tenth of the children | Yes |
| Eastport had fewer cases per child than Hillcrest | No |
| Eastport's rate was over nine times Hillcrest's | Yes |

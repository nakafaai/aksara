The phrase follows the description of the **nocebo effect**: patients warned about side effects sometimes report them "even when they have been given a dummy pill". To "plant" something is to put a seed in place so that it grows. A warning can put the **expectation** of a symptom into a patient's mind, and that expectation can grow into a symptom the patient actually feels. Doctors must warn about risks without causing this.

- The problem is creating symptoms, not hiding real ones.
- Pleasant images are not mentioned.
- No drug is involved in the nocebo effect described. The pill is a dummy.
- Recording symptoms is a literal reading of the phrase.

So "planting symptoms in their minds" means making patients expect side effects so strongly that they begin to feel them.

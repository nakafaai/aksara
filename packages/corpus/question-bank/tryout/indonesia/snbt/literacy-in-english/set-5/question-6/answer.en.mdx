You're asked how Text 2 treats Text 1 as a whole. That means sorting what Text 2 accepts from what it fights.

**What Text 2 accepts.** It opens with "My colleague's arithmetic is sound as far as it goes" and "The overruns are real." In the last paragraph it agrees that this decade's money should go mainly to renewables "because early cuts do count most." Those are Text 1's two main facts.

**What Text 2 rejects.** The same paragraph says: "What I reject is the idea that the choice is one or the other." Text 1 had argued that "Every billion committed to a reactor is a billion not spent on turbines, panels, power lines and batteries." Text 2 wants both: renewables now, plus a short series of reactors started now. So it **grants the facts but denies the either-or**.

Why the others fail:

- Text 2 never says the overruns were exaggerated. It explains them with lost skills, which is a reason, not a denial.
- Waiting for storage is Text 1's plan. Text 2 warns, "If we wait until storage has failed us, we will have waited too long."
- Keeping existing reactors running is Text 1's own proposal, and Text 2 doesn't accept Text 1 "in full".
- Text 2 does concede the plant-by-plant cost, but its case rests on **grid costs and the 2040s**, not on emissions excusing delay.

So Text 2 accepts Text 1's facts on overruns and early cuts but rejects the idea that reactors and renewables are competing for the same money.

# Given

**Text 1** opposes funding new nuclear reactors because of cost overruns, delays, and the urgency of cutting emissions quickly with wind and solar. **Text 2** responds directly to this case.

# Asked

Which statement best describes how Text 2 responds to Text 1's argument against new reactors?

# Solution

Separate **what Text 2 concedes** (overruns and the priority of early emissions cuts) from **what Text 2 rejects** (the strict either-or trade-off between reactors and renewables).

> Text 2 explicitly grants Text 1's arithmetic on past overruns and this decade's renewable priority, yet rejects treating nuclear and renewables as mutually exclusive choices.

## Concessions and rebuttal in Text two

- **Facts granted**: Text 2 opens <Highlight>My colleague's arithmetic is sound as far as it goes. The overruns are real</Highlight> and later agrees <Highlight>this decade's money should go mainly to wind, solar and the grid, because early cuts do count most</Highlight>.
- **Premise disputed**: Text 2 immediately counters <Highlight>What I reject is the idea that the choice is one or the other</Highlight>, proposing renewables now alongside starting a short series of proven reactors for the <InlineMath math="2040" />s.

## Why the other options fail

- **Rejects overruns as exaggerated** contradicts Text 2 calling the overruns real and explaining them via retired skills.
- **Agrees reactors should wait for storage** reverses Text 2's warning: <Highlight>If we wait until storage has failed us, we will have waited too long</Highlight>.
- **Accepts Text 1 in full** ignores Text 2's explicit rebuttal on grid costs, gas phase-out, and building a short reactor series now.
- **Low emissions justify delays** misstates Text 2's core case, which rests on system-wide grid costs and replacing reserve gas plants in the <InlineMath math="2040" />s.

# Answer

**It grants Text 1's facts about overruns and the value of early cuts but disputes that money for reactors must come at the expense of wind and solar**.

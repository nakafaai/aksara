The author names three weaknesses of pilots: they are **small**, **short**, and **not universal**. Paragraph 5 asks for "larger and longer trials, ideally covering whole towns". Paying every adult in whole towns for many years addresses all three. Prices, wages, and long-term plans could respond as they would under a real scheme, at least locally.

- Six months and 500 people make the trial even shorter and smaller than existing pilots.
- A survey records what people say they would do, not what happens when money arrives.
- The Finnish design is exactly the kind of pilot the author calls far less informative than it appears.
- Payments that stop when someone finds work are conditional, unlike a universal basic income.

So the author would most likely consider payments to every adult in several whole towns for many years the most informative.

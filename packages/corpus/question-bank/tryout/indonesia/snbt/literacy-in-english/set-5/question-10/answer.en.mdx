# Given

Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> of **Text 2** contrasts **plant-by-plant electricity cost** with **whole-grid system cost** as weather-dependent generation rises toward <InlineMath math="100\%" />.

# Asked

Which statements are implied by Text 2's argument about cost? Choose all that apply.

# Solution

Test every option directly against paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> of Text 2, checking both the **grid-versus-plant distinction** and the **nonlinear steepening** of backup costs.

> Because a power grid needs extra storage, lines, and spare capacity that grow steeply as weather-dependent power approaches one hundred percent, the cheapest single plant need not yield the cheapest total grid.

## Checking all five implications

| Option | Verdict | Evidence from Text 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New nuclear is cheaper than wind and solar plant by plant | **False** | Text 2 grants the opposite: <Highlight>Measured plant by plant, new wind and solar now generate power more cheaply than new nuclear, and I do not dispute it</Highlight>. |
| Cheapest plant by plant is not necessarily cheapest for the whole grid | **True** | Follows directly from <Highlight>A grid, though, is not a pile of separate plants</Highlight> once storage, lines, and spare capacity are added. |
| Storage costs will fall fast enough to make steady sources unnecessary | **False** | Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> doubts storage improves <Highlight>faster than anyone can promise</Highlight>. |
| A grid of steady sources needs no spare capacity at all | **False** | Text 2 only says a steady source <Highlight>keeps it down</Highlight>, never that spare capacity drops to zero. |
| Extra cost of covering weather power rises faster than its share | **True** | Text 2 says backup cost <Highlight>is modest while wind and sun supply part of the power and climbs steeply as they approach all of it</Highlight>. |

# Answer

- **The source that is cheapest when plants are compared one by one is not necessarily the cheapest for the grid as a whole**.
- **The extra cost of covering weather-dependent power rises faster than the share of power that wind and sun supply**.

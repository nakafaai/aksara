In its second paragraph, Text 2 grants that "measured plant by plant, new wind and solar now generate power more cheaply than new nuclear", then adds: "A grid, though, is not a pile of separate plants." The more power that "rises and falls with the weather", the more the grid must spend on storage, spare capacity and lines, and this cost "is modest while wind and sun supply part of the power and climbs steeply as they approach all of it". Judge each option against those lines.

- **Not implied**. Text 2 says the opposite plant by plant: wind and solar are cheaper, "and I do not dispute it".
- **Implied.** That's the point of "a grid is not a pile of separate plants": the plant-by-plant winner can still cost the grid more once the cover is counted.
- **Not implied**. Text 2 warns that storage may not improve "faster than anyone can promise". It certainly doesn't predict that steady sources become unnecessary.
- **Not implied**. Text 2 says a steady source "keeps it down", not that it removes every need for spare capacity.
- **Implied.** "Modest" for part of the power and "climbs steeply" near all of it means **the cost grows faster than the share**.

Two statements follow from Text 2: the plant-by-plant cheapest source isn't necessarily the cheapest for the grid, and the extra cost of covering weather-dependent power rises faster than its share.

# Given

Paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> weighs **both sides of the historical dispute** over Alfred Wegener's continental drift against the seafloor-spreading evidence discovered in the <InlineMath math="1950" />s and <InlineMath math="1960" />s.

# Asked

Which statement best sums up the author's overall judgement of the dispute?

# Solution

Combine the **two verdicts** stated explicitly in paragraph <InlineMath math="5" />: what Wegener's critics got right and wrong, plus how seafloor spreading resolved the debate.

> Seafloor evidence vindicated continental movement while proving continents ride on plates rather than ploughing through ocean rock, showing critics were right to ask for a mechanism yet wrong to treat its absence as disproof.

## Evidence from the final paragraph

Paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> states the **author's balanced verdict**: <Highlight>Continents do not plough through the ocean floor. They ride on plates that include the seafloor itself</Highlight>. It closes with the exact judgement on the critics: <Highlight>His critics were right to demand a mechanism. Where they went wrong was treating the missing mechanism as proof that the movement itself could not be real</Highlight>.

## Why the other options fail

- **Seafloor supplied the driving force** contradicts paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> stating geologists <Highlight>still debate which forces drive the plates</Highlight>.
- **Critics were wrong to demand a mechanism** contradicts the explicit praise that they <Highlight>were right to demand a mechanism</Highlight>.
- **Seafloor confirmed ploughing** reverses the text: continents do not plough through the ocean floor.
- **Land bridges explained fossils just as well** contradicts paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> noting no bridge was ever found and sunken bridges could not explain ancient ice.

# Answer

**The critics were right to demand a mechanism but wrong to treat its absence as disproof, and the seafloor later confirmed drift while ruling out the ploughing Wegener had proposed**.

Paragraph 2 explains the problem with counting every improvement as a placebo effect. Many illnesses **improve on their own**, and patients tend to seek help when their symptoms are **at their worst**, so they are likely to feel better soon "whatever they are given". Some of the improvement Beecher counted would have happened **without any treatment**.

- Nothing suggests the pills contained real medicine.
- The passage says patients seek help when symptoms are worst, not that they exaggerate.
- The paragraph's examples (colds, back pain) are conditions that clear up, not incurable ones.
- The size of the 2001 review is never given, and size alone would not explain an inflated figure.

So Beecher's figure may have been too high because many patients would have improved even without any treatment.

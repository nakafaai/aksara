# Asked

Which pair of details from the passage best supports the claim that the popular story overlooks valid historical objections?

# Solution

Select **two genuinely sound scientific objections** raised against Wegener at the time, rejecting details that either bolster the outsider parable or highlight weaknesses in the critics' own case.

> Because physicists proved Wegener's spin and tidal forces far too weak and Harold Jeffreys rightly showed continents cannot plough through solid ocean rock, the critics had legitimate scientific grounds for doubt.

## Valid objections raised in paragraph two

Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> gives both **well-founded scientific criticisms**: <Highlight>Physicists showed that both forces were far too feeble to move a continent, and the British geophysicist Harold Jeffreys argued, correctly, that a continent could not plough through the ocean floor without breaking apart</Highlight>.

Both details directly undercut the myth that the establishment merely mocked an outsider out of closed-mindedness.

## Why the other options fail

- **Meteorologist outsider rejected in <InlineMath math="1926" />** reinforces the popular parable rather than challenging it.
- **Matching coastlines and *Glossopteris* fossils** are Wegener's own supporting evidence, not reasons his critics had sound doubts.
- **Wegener dying in <InlineMath math="1930" /> in the minority** belongs to the tragic-outsider narrative, not proof of valid scientific objections.
- **No one found a land bridge** exposes a flaw in the critics' alternative rather than validating their criticism of Wegener.

# Answer

**Physicists showed that Wegener's proposed forces were far too feeble, and Jeffreys argued, correctly, that a continent could not plough through the ocean floor**.

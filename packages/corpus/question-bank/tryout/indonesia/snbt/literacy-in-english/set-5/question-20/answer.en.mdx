# Given

**The story is told** in first person looking back from a later time, showing how the narrator's view of her grandfather's quiet habits deepens.

# Asked

Which statements about the narrator and her grandfather can be inferred from the story.

# Solution

Look at **who tells the story** and how her view of him shifts.

> Children often notice what grown-ups do first and only understand why they did it years later when they look back.

## Tracing voice perspective and character moments

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. "When I was small, I had thought he wrote on his hands because he forgot things. Later I noticed that he never looked at what he wrote." Her reading of the same habit changes with age. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />. When she asks about the water, he says only "That's the idea", and she "waited for more". He explains in pieces, during the night, and once he speaks to a branch rather than to her. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />. "I said it badly. He nodded as if I had said it well." He chooses to encourage her rather than correct her. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />. She tells it as "I", and she already knows what came later: the bare trees in May and the list she found "the next spring". Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="5" />. Watered trees bore fruit and unwatered ones didn't, and he had worked all night to save his. Calling the Hallers "unlucky" sounds like **kindness about the neighbours**, not a belief that the watering made no difference. No.

# Answer

Statements **<InlineMath math="1" />**, **<InlineMath math="3" />**, and **<InlineMath math="4" />** match the retrospective voice and gentle mentorship, while <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> misread his quiet style.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Her understanding of his habits changes with age | Yes |
| He explains the method in full beforehand | No |
| The nod shows encouragement | Yes |
| She tells it later, knowing more | Yes |
| He thought the frost alone decided it | No |

Look at **who tells the story** and how her view of him shifts.

Statement 1. "When I was small, I had thought he wrote on his hands because he forgot things. Later I noticed that he never looked at what he wrote." Her reading of the same habit changes with age. Yes.

Statement 2. When she asks about the water, he says only "That's the idea", and she "waited for more". He explains in pieces, during the night, and once he speaks to a branch rather than to her. No.

Statement 3. "I said it badly. He nodded as if I had said it well." He chooses to encourage her rather than correct her. Yes.

Statement 4. She tells it as "I", and she already knows what came later: the bare trees in May and the list she found "the next spring". Yes.

Statement 5. Watered trees bore fruit and unwatered ones didn't, and he had worked all night to save his. Calling the Hallers "unlucky" sounds like **kindness about the neighbours**, not a belief that the watering made no difference. No.

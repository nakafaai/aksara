# Given

**The passage traces Alfred** Wegener's continental drift evidence, early critics' objections, the <InlineMath math="1926" /> New York symposium, and <InlineMath math="1950" />s seafloor discoveries.

# Asked

Which statements linking facts about Wegener's theory are actually supported by the passage.

# Solution

**Each statement links two** facts. Ask whether the passage itself makes that link, not whether the link sounds reasonable.

> Two true facts sitting in the same paragraph do not automatically mean one caused the other unless the author connects them.

## Testing each link against the passage

**Greenland's speed and the feeble forces**. Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> gives two separate problems. Physicists showed the forces "were far too feeble to move a continent." Then, as an extra point, the Greenland estimate "did not help." Nothing says the speed estimate was a reason for the physicists' verdict. This one **joins two separate objections**, so it's No.

**Land bridges and the ice**. Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> says critics used land bridges "to explain why the same fossils appear on both sides of an ocean", and that a sunken bridge "could not explain the ancient ice in places that are hot today." Paragraph <InlineMath math="1" /> names those places: "India and southern Africa." Put the two paragraphs together and the statement holds. Yes.

**The <InlineMath math="1926" /> symposium**. Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> says only that "nearly every speaker rejected drift." The line "did not mainly dispute his maps" in paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> is about the critics in general, and "not mainly" isn't the same as "accepted". The word "only" goes even further. That's an **overreach**, so No.

**Jeffreys's objection**. Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> says Jeffreys argued "correctly" that a continent can't plough through the ocean floor. Paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> agrees: "Continents do not plough through the ocean floor." The seafloor evidence backed Jeffreys. No.

# Answer

Only the land-bridge statement holds, because the other three invent a link, stretch a general remark, or flip Jeffreys's result.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Greenland's speed was a reason physicists judged the forces too feeble | No |
| Land bridges explained the fossils but not the ancient ice | Yes |
| Most 1926 speakers accepted the evidence and objected only to the mechanism | No |
| The seafloor evidence showed Jeffreys was mistaken | No |

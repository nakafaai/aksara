# Given

**Eastport reached** <InlineMath math="95\%" /> district-wide vaccination coverage yet recorded <InlineMath math="38" /> measles cases, clustered mainly in one private school with many unvaccinated pupils.

# Asked

Which conclusions about Eastport's outbreak follow from the passage and table.

# Solution

**Eastport is the interesting** district: <InlineMath math="95\%" /> overall, yet <InlineMath math="38" /> cases, most of them "among pupils of a single private school, where many families had declined the vaccine".

> An umbrella that covers almost the whole playground still leaves a dry-looking average while one uncovered corner gets completely soaked.

## Checking each claim about Eastport cluster

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. That's exactly Eastport's story: the district reached the aim, and an outbreak still ran through one community. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />. Most cases were at a school where many families "had declined the vaccine", and nothing in the passage shows vaccinated children falling ill. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />. The district figure is <InlineMath math="95\%" />, right at the aim. Looking only at that number, nothing would stand out, yet the school was where most of the cases were. **A whole-district figure hides the pocket**. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />. The passage never says how many pupils the school has or how many of them are unvaccinated, so you **can't work out** how far the district figure would rise. "Well above" goes beyond the figures. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="5" />. The health office says most cases came from "a single private school", a pocket. That is the opposite of an even spread. No.

# Answer

**Eastport shows that a high district average can hide a vulnerable pocket, without implying that vaccinated children fell ill**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| A district can reach the aim and still have an outbreak | Yes |
| Two doses often fail | No |
| The district figure alone couldn't flag the school | Yes |
| Vaccinating the school would lift coverage well above the aim | No |
| The unvaccinated were spread evenly | No |

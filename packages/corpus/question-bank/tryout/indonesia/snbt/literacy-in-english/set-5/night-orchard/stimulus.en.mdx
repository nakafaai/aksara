The forecast said minus three by four in the morning, and Grandpa said nothing about it at supper except, "Wear the green coat."

At eleven we walked out between the rows. The apple blossom had opened that week, pale in the torchlight, and he shone the beam on one cluster after another as if counting them. At the end of the row he turned the valve on the old sprinkler line. Water hissed and then rose in thin arcs over the trees.

"You're watering them," I said. "It's going to freeze."

"That's the idea."

I waited for more, but he only checked the thermometer nailed to the post, wrote the reading on the back of his hand with a pen, and walked on. When I was small, I had thought he wrote on his hands because he forgot things. Later I noticed that he never looked at what he wrote.

By two the thermometer read minus one. Every twig was cased in ice, every blossom inside its own glass bead, and the trees creaked when the wind moved them. It looked like the end of the harvest. Grandpa put his palm against an iced branch and held it there the way he held a calf's neck to feel its breathing. "Still wet under the ice," he said. "As long as the water keeps coming, the freezing keeps them warm." He said it to the branch, not to me.

Around three he sat down on the upturned crate by the pump and did not get up for a long time. I asked if he was all right. He said the pump needed watching, which it did not, since it ran by itself. So I watched the pump, and he watched me watch it, and the water kept falling.

At first light the sun came up behind the hill, and the ice began to drip. He told me to shut the valve only when the last of it had run clear off the twigs, not before, and he made me say back to him why. I said it badly. He nodded as if I had said it well.

In May the trees on our side of the road set fruit, and the Hallers' trees across the road, which nobody had watered, stood bare. Grandpa said the Hallers had been unlucky. When I took the green coat down from its hook the next spring, there was a list in the pocket in his handwriting: the valve, the pump, the thermometer, and the order to do them in. I had never seen him write down anything he expected to remember.

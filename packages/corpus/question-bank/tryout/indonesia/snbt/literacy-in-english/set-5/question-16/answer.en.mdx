# Given

Around three in the morning during the frost watch, Grandpa sits on an upturned crate by the pump and **does not get up for a long time**. When the narrator asks if he is all right, he replies that **the pump needed watching**, even though the narrator notes <Highlight>which it did not, since it ran by itself</Highlight>.

# Asked

What does Grandpa's reply most likely show?

# Solution

Read Grandpa's pretext against **his silent exhaustion** and the **shared gaze** in the next sentence (<Highlight>So I watched the pump, and he watched me watch it</Highlight>).

> Claiming a self-running pump needed watching lets a proud, exhausted grandfather stay seated while giving his granddaughter a dignified role so neither of them has to mention his fatigue aloud.

## Reading the unspoken pretext

Because the pump runs entirely on its own, Grandpa's answer is clearly **a face-saving cover** for needing to rest after sitting down for a long time at three in the morning. By handing the narrator a task while sitting right beside her, he lets **both of them avoid saying aloud how tired he is**.

## Why the other options fail

- **Believes the pump is close to failing** conflicts with the narrator's certainty that the pump ran by itself without needing attention.
- **Sends her away so she will not see him rest** directly contradicts <Highlight>he watched me watch it</Highlight>: they stay right together.
- **Testing whether she can repair the pump** invents a breakdown that never happens.
- **Annoyed at her earlier question** has no support: he calmly explains his method and gently guides her all night.

# Answer

**He is more tired than he will admit, and he gives her a task so that neither of them has to say so**.

Paragraph 2 gives the reason. Many Victorians believed in **miasma**, the theory that diseases such as cholera spread through bad air from rotting matter. "If foul air caused disease, a stinking river threatened everyone who breathed near it." The smell was therefore seen as **a danger to health**, not just a nuisance, and Parliament passed a law "within weeks".

- Few people accepted Snow's conclusion at the time.
- No deaths among members of Parliament are mentioned.
- The water was low that summer. There was no flood.
- Bazalgette designed the sewers after the law, as the board's engineer.

So Parliament acted quickly because many believed foul air spread disease, which made the smell seem dangerous.

The last sentence decides most of these: "I had never seen him write down anything he expected to remember."

Statement 1. If he wrote down only what he didn't expect to remember, and he knew this job by heart, the list was most likely **for someone else**, and it was left in the coat he had told her to wear. Yes.

Statement 2. She found it "When I took the green coat down from its hook the next spring", not on the night. No.

Statement 3. A list of steps, in order, left for her to find the next spring suggests he was thinking of a time when she would do it alone. Yes.

Statement 4. He wrote down only what he didn't expect to remember, and on the night he knew every step without looking. The list shows **planning for someone else**, not doubt about his own memory. No.

Statement 5. The list holds "the valve, the pump, the thermometer, and the order to do them in": steps, not readings. And he "never looked at what he wrote" on his hand. No.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The list was most likely meant for someone else | Yes |
| She found the list on the night of the frost | No |
| He expected she might have to do it without him | Yes |
| He no longer trusted his own memory | No |
| The list repeats the night's readings | No |

# Given

**The next spring**, the narrator finds a neat list of orchard steps in the green coat pocket and realizes she had never seen her grandfather write down anything he expected to remember.

# Asked

Which inferences about the handwritten list at the end of the story are supported.

# Solution

**The last sentence decides** most of these: "I had never seen him write down anything he expected to remember."

> When an expert who knows every step by heart leaves an instruction card in your coat pocket, the card is a gift prepared for you, not a cheat sheet for himself.

## Reading the quiet clues in the final paragraph

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. If he wrote down only what he didn't expect to remember, and he knew this job by heart, the list was most likely **for someone else**, and it was left in the coat he had told her to wear. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />. She found it "When I took the green coat down from its hook the next spring", not on the night. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />. A list of steps, in order, left for her to find the next spring suggests he was thinking of a time when she would do it alone. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />. He wrote down only what he didn't expect to remember, and on the night he knew every step without looking. The list shows **planning for someone else**, not doubt about his own memory. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="5" />. The list holds "the valve, the pump, the thermometer, and the order to do them in": steps, not readings. And he "never looked at what he wrote" on his hand. No.

# Answer

**The list was quietly prepared for the narrator to run the orchard alone one day, not found that night or written out of forgetfulness**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The list was most likely meant for someone else | Yes |
| She found the list on the night of the frost | No |
| He expected she might have to do it without him | Yes |
| He no longer trusted his own memory | No |
| The list repeats the night's readings | No |

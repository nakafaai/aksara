The last sentence decides most of these: "I had never seen him write down anything he expected to remember."

Statement 1. If he wrote down only what he didn't expect to remember, and he knew this job by heart, the list was most likely **for someone else**, and it was left in the coat he had told her to wear. Yes.

Statement 2. This reads the old hand-writing habit the wrong way. The narrator says he "never looked at what he wrote", and on the night he knew every step. The list shows planning, not forgetting. No.

Statement 3. A list of steps, in order, left for her to find the next spring suggests he was thinking of a time when she would do it alone. Yes.

Statement 4. Grandpa says the Hallers "had been unlucky", and the narrator reports it without any blame of her own. No.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The list was most likely meant for someone else | Yes |
| He had begun to forget | No |
| He expected she might have to do it without him | Yes |
| She blames the Hallers | No |

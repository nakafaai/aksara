A claim that screens **themselves** reduce sleep predicts that cutting screens will **increase** sleep. A controlled trial compares students who cut evening screens with similar students who do not, so other differences are balanced out. If the students who cut screens slept no more than the others, the predicted effect is missing, which weakens the causal claim directly.

- Falling asleep later with more screen use fits the claim rather than weakening it.
- Owning a device says nothing about its effect on sleep.
- Lower marks concern the results of less sleep, not its cause.
- Feeling more rested after switching off screens supports the claim.

So the finding that most weakens the claim is that, in a controlled trial, students who cut evening screens slept no more than others.

Paragraph 3 argues that a temporary trial cannot show what a permanent payment would do. That only follows if **expectations about how long the money lasts change behaviour**. The paragraph contrasts a person who expects the money to end, who "may keep a job they dislike", with someone promised payments for life, who "might leave it, retrain, or start a business". Without the assumption that people may act differently in these two cases, a short trial would be enough.

- The paragraph says most pilots last two or three years.
- The paragraph says someone might leave a disliked job, so "never" contradicts it.
- The author does not rank starting a business above keeping a job.
- The paragraph says participants **know** when payments will stop.

So the argument depends on the assumption that people may act differently when they expect payments to last for life.

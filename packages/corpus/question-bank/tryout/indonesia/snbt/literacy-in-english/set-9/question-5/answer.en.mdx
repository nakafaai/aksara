# Given

A school's reading scores rise during a term when inspectors sit in on lessons, and the school's report dismisses the rise as **"just the Hawthorne effect"**. The final paragraph states that calling a result "just the Hawthorne effect" explains nothing until a specific mechanism is identified.

# Asked

Which response would the author of the passage most likely give to the school's report?

# Solution

> "Hawthorne" names a worry, not a mechanism, and a report that dismisses an awkward result as "just the Hawthorne effect" has explained nothing until it says which of these it means.

Apply the **author's closing standard** directly to the school's dismissal of its own score increase.

## Applying the closing rule to the school report

The author warns that an improvement under study could stem from <Highlight>attention, from feedback, from pay, from the calendar, or from a change nobody recorded</Highlight>. Simply stamping the reading gains as "just the Hawthorne effect" raises a worry without proving **which feature of the inspected term** actually drove the higher scores.

The other choices violate that rule. Dismissing the gain via factory calendar effects overgeneralises one study. Calling any studied improvement unreal treats the label itself as an explanation. Crediting inspector attention picks one cause without evidence, and the <InlineMath math="2014" /> review did not prove observation leaves behaviour untouched.

# Answer

The author would most likely reply that **the report has not yet explained the rise, and it still has to show which feature of the inspected term actually changed the scores**.

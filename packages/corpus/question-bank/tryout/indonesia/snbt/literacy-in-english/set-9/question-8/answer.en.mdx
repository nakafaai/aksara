First pin down the explanation you're attacking. Text 2 says "The likeliest explanation is that people protect their privacy by other means once the partitions are gone", with headphones, looking busy, and moving talk "to a screen".

A finding weakens a causal explanation best when it offers **another cause** that would produce the same result. Text 1 hands you one. Allen found that contact "fell steeply as the distance between their desks grew", and Text 2 agrees that "distance does reduce how often colleagues talk".

So suppose teammates ended up farther apart after the move, some even on different floors. Then the fall in face-to-face talk could come from **plain distance**, with no privacy habits needed. That makes the privacy explanation less necessary, which weakens it.

- The headphones finding fits the privacy story. Heavy headphone users talking least is what that story predicts, so it strengthens it.
- The email finding also fits it. People who sit close yet send more messages are moving talk to a screen.
- The finding about chatter is a true limit from Text 2, but it's about the value of the lost talk, not about why the talk fell.
- A similar fall at both companies makes the result look sturdier. It doesn't point to any other cause.

The strongest challenge is a finding that teammates sat farther apart after the move, because distance alone could then explain the drop in face-to-face talk.

Mercator's map keeps **angles** true and turns compass bearings into straight lines. It pays for this with sizes that grow away from the equator. Test each task against those two facts.

- **Not suited.** Areas swell with distance from the equator, four times at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, so countries at different latitudes can't be compared by size. The last paragraph calls it "the wrong map for that job".
- **Suited.** This is what the map was designed for: "rule a line between two ports, measure its angle, and hold that bearing".
- **Not suited.** A ruled line is a rhumb line, and paragraph 3 says the straight lines "are not the shortest routes".
- **Not suited.** Only "the shapes of small areas" stay true. A whole continent near a pole stretches more and more towards the pole, so its outline bends.
- **Suited.** Online street maps use a version of Mercator "because a map that can be zoomed into any city must keep street corners square at every scale".

Mercator's projection suits two of the tasks: plotting a course on one compass heading, and zooming into a city without bending its street corners.

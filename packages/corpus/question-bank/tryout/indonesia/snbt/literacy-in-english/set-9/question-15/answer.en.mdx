Actual recycling rises when **more bin material passes the sorting plant**. The passage says paper "is often rejected when it is wet or covered in food". Paper is the largest material in the bins, so keeping it dry and clean would let more of those 1,200 tonnes be sold and recycled.

- More mixed food trays would add material with few buyers, so most would be rejected.
- Reporting more often changes the report, not the recycling.
- A larger landfill changes where rejects go, not how much is recycled.
- Measuring glass differently changes a number on paper, not the amount recycled.

So the most effective change is persuading residents to keep paper dry and free of food before recycling it.

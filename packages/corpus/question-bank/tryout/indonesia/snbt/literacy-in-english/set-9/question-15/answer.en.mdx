# Given

**The head teacher argues** cooling frames are unnecessary because efficiency falls only in summer, when output is already highest, while the table shows <InlineMath math="3{,}800" /> kWh of sunlight in July versus <InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> kWh in March.

# Asked

Which statements evaluating the head teacher's summer argument can be concluded.

# Solution

**The head teacher treats** summer as the season when losses matter least. Check that idea against the numbers.

> Losing one penny from every dollar hurts almost twice as much on a giant pile of sunlight as it does on a small spring pile.

## Weighing what one percentage point is worth in July versus March

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. Above <InlineMath math="25" /> °C are May, June, July and August, with <InlineMath math="3{,}200" />, <InlineMath math="3{,}600" />, <InlineMath math="3{,}800" /> and <InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> kWh of sunlight. March and April had only <InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> and <InlineMath math="2{,}600" />. The hot months are the four sunniest. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" /> comes next, because statement <InlineMath math="2" /> depends on it. One percentage point of efficiency is <InlineMath math="1\%" /> of the sunlight that arrives:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{July: } 1\% \times 3800 &= 38 \text{ kWh} \\
\text{March: } 1\% \times 2000 &= 20 \text{ kWh}
\end{aligned}" />

A point is worth almost twice as much in July. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" /> turns that around. Because more sunlight arrives in summer, **the same fall in efficiency costs more electricity** in summer, not less. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" /> goes too far. The installer says the frames win back only "part of what the heat takes", and dust may also be lowering July's efficiency. No.

# Answer

The head teacher has it backwards: **summer efficiency losses cost the most electricity because summer brings the most sunlight**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The hot months were the four sunniest | Yes |
| A summer fall in efficiency costs less | No |
| A point of efficiency was worth more in July | Yes |
| Frames would bring July up to March efficiency | No |

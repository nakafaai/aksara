The phrase is figurative, so first find **what plays the part of the walls**. The sentence just before it lists the new habits: people "put on headphones against the noise, try to look busy, and move conversations to a screen, where nobody nearby can listen". These habits keep a person private, the job the partitions used to do. And they cut face-to-face talk.

Statement 1. That's the core of the image: privacy comes back, but through behaviour. Yes.

Statement 2. Nothing physical goes up. The walls are "inside people's habits". No.

Statement 3. Moving conversations "to a screen" is one of the habits, and Text 2 reports that "email and instant messaging rose". The claim explains that rise. Yes.

Statement 4. The habits **reduce** talk in person. They don't hide it from the badges. Messages on a screen are a different channel, not missed conversations. No.

Statement 5. The habits exist to protect privacy. A team that doesn't need privacy has less reason to build them, and Text 2 says that teams "whose work depends on overhearing one another, such as a newsroom, may thrive in the open". Yes.

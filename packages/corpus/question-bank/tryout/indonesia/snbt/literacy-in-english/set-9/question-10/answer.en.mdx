# Given

Text <InlineMath math="2" /> **observes that when physical** partitions disappear, workers put on headphones, try to look busy, and shift talks to screens, rebuilding walls inside their habits.

# Asked

Which statements follow from that figurative claim together with the rest of Text <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Solution

The phrase is figurative, so first find **what plays the part of the walls**. The sentence just before it lists the new habits: people "put on headphones against the noise, try to look busy, and move conversations to a screen, where nobody nearby can listen". These habits keep a person private, the job the partitions used to do. And they cut face-to-face talk.

> If someone takes away your bedroom door, you might put on big headphones and text instead of talking out loud, rebuilding privacy through habits instead of wood.

## Testing each statement against the metaphor

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. That's the core of the image: privacy comes back, but through behaviour. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />. Nothing physical goes up. The walls are "inside people's habits". No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />. Moving conversations "to a screen" is one of the habits, and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> reports that "email and instant messaging rose". The claim explains that rise. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />. The habits **reduce** talk in person. They don't hide it from the badges. Messages on a screen are a different channel, not missed conversations. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="5" />. The habits exist to protect privacy. A team that doesn't need privacy has less reason to build them, and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> says that teams "whose work depends on overhearing one another, such as a newsroom, may thrive in the open". Yes.

# Answer

**The metaphor points to behavioural privacy, rising screen messaging, and less disruption for teams that welcome overhearing**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Privacy is recreated by what employees do | Yes |
| Physical barriers were rebuilt | No |
| The rise in messaging fits the claim | Yes |
| The badges undercounted talk | No |
| A team that doesn't mind being overheard loses less talk | Yes |

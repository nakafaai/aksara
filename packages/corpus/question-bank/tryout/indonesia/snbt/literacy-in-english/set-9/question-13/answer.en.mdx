# Given

The school newsletter called July the panels' best month because it produced the most electricity (<InlineMath math="627" /> kWh) and concluded **hot weather does the panels no harm**. The table also lists arriving solar energy for every month from March to August, and the installer warns that dust also accumulated since March.

# Asked

Which statement accurately explains what is wrong with the newsletter's reasoning?

# Solution

> July produced the most electricity only because the most sunlight arrived.

Compare **monthly efficiency** (electricity divided by arriving sunlight) rather than raw electricity totals alone.

## Comparing efficiency across all six months

Divide electricity produced by arriving solar energy for each month to see how well the panels converted sunlight:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{March} &= \frac{380}{2{,}000} = 19\%, \quad \text{April} = \frac{494}{2{,}600} = 19\%, \quad \text{May} = \frac{576}{3{,}200} = 18\% \\ \text{June} &= \frac{612}{3{,}600} = 17\%, \quad \text{July} = \frac{627}{3{,}800} = 16.5\%, \quad \text{August} = \frac{525}{3{,}000} = 17.5\% \end{aligned}" />

| Month | Sunlight (kWh) | Electricity (kWh) | Panel temperature (°C) | Efficiency |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| March | <InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="380" /> | <InlineMath math="18" /> | <InlineMath math="19\%" /> |
| June | <InlineMath math="3{,}600" /> | <InlineMath math="612" /> | <InlineMath math="42" /> | <InlineMath math="17\%" /> |
| July | <InlineMath math="3{,}800" /> | <InlineMath math="627" /> | <InlineMath math="47" /> | <InlineMath math="16.5\%" /> |
| August | <InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="525" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> | <InlineMath math="17.5\%" /> |

July had the **lowest efficiency** (<InlineMath math="16.5\%" />) of all six months and topped raw output solely because <InlineMath math="3{,}800" /> kWh of sunlight arrived. Notice why the "heat alone" option fails: the installer explicitly cautions that unwashed dust may share the blame.

# Answer

The flaw in the newsletter's reasoning is that **July's efficiency was about <InlineMath math="16.5\%" />, the lowest of the six months, so its high output came from having the most sunlight**.

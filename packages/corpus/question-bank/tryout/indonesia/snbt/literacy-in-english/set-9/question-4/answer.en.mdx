For each statement, ask whether the passage **gives you enough to conclude it**. A claim that goes past the evidence gets "No", even if it sounds reasonable.

**Statement 1** is a "Yes". Paragraph 2 says Landsberger popularized the label "in 1958". The same paragraph says the lighting records were ones "nobody analysed formally for more than eighty years". The tests ran from 1924 to 1927, and eighty years later is past 2000, so in 1958 no formal analysis existed yet.

**Statement 2** is a "No". Paragraph 4 says "Most reported some effect". The reviewers couldn't say how large the effect is or when it appears, and that's very different from saying it rarely happens.

**Statement 3** is a "No". The studies came "all but one from the health sciences", but that tells you where researchers looked, not where the effect happens. This is a **scope trap**: the sample's field is read as the effect's home.

**Statement 4** is a "Yes". Paragraph 3 says output "tended to be higher on Mondays whether or not anything had been altered" and that part of every rise "was simply what any Monday would have produced". Comparing with an ordinary Monday removes that part, so the rises would probably look smaller.

# Given

**The Hawthorne**-effect passage contrasts the <InlineMath math="1927" /> lighting records, Landsberger's <InlineMath math="1958" /> label, Monday baseline effects, and a later review of mostly health-science studies.

# Asked

Which statements about the Hawthorne label and later research strictly follow from the passage.

# Solution

For each statement, ask whether the passage **gives you enough to conclude it**. A claim that goes past the evidence gets "No", even if it sounds reasonable.

> Looking for seashells only on one beach tells you where you searched, not that seashells only exist on that beach.

## Checking timelines scope traps and Monday baselines

**Statement <InlineMath math="1" />** is a "Yes". Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> says Landsberger popularized the label "in <InlineMath math="1958" />". The same paragraph says the lighting records were ones "nobody analysed formally for more than eighty years". The tests ended in <InlineMath math="1927" /> and the records waited more than eighty years for an analysis, while <InlineMath math="1958" /> was only <InlineMath math="31" /> years after <InlineMath math="1927" />, so no formal analysis existed yet.

**Statement <InlineMath math="2" />** is a "No". Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> says "Most reported some effect". The reviewers couldn't say how large the effect is or when it appears, and that's very different from saying it rarely happens.

**Statement <InlineMath math="3" />** is a "No". The studies came "all but one from the health sciences", but that tells you where researchers looked, not where the effect happens. This is a **scope trap**: the sample's field is read as the effect's home.

**Statement <InlineMath math="4" />** is a "Yes". Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> says output "tended to be higher on Mondays whether or not anything had been altered" and that part of every rise "was simply what any Monday would have produced". Comparing with an ordinary Monday removes that part, so the rises would probably look smaller.

# Answer

The timeline gap (<InlineMath math="1927" /> to <InlineMath math="1958" />) and Monday comparison hold, while the two middle statements distort the review's findings and scope.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| No formal analysis of the lighting records when Landsberger popularized the label | Yes |
| The review found that research participation rarely changes behaviour | No |
| Research participation effects arise mainly in health research | No |
| Rises judged against an ordinary Monday would probably look smaller | Yes |

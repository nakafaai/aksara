You're looking for evidence that helps Parsons **more than it helps the attention story**. Evidence that fits both stories can't do that.

Paragraph 2 gives the two stories. The parable says the workers "had responded to the attention". Parsons says the women got "regular information about how much they were producing" and their pay "depended on what their small group produced", so attention mattered "only because it arrived together with feedback and money".

In the real relay room, attention, feedback and group pay all came together. Both stories predict a climb there, so the climb alone can't pick a winner. To separate them, you need a group that keeps the attention but loses the feedback and the pay. If that group shows **no climb**, attention alone wasn't enough, which is exactly Parsons's claim.

- The group with the same reports, pay and watching repeats the original mix. Both stories predict its climb, so it decides nothing.
- The group that climbed without feedback or pay points the other way. Attention alone worked, which weakens Parsons.
- The Monday pattern comes from the lighting tests in paragraph 3. A weekly swing can't build a climb over several years, and it says nothing about attention versus feedback.
- What the women enjoyed is a feeling about the attention. If anything, it leans toward the attention story.

The strongest support for Parsons is a closely watched group on a flat wage, given no output figures, whose output did not climb.

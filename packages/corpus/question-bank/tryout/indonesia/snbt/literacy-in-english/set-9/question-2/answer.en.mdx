# Given

In the relay room, workers received **close attention**, **regular output feedback**, and **small-group pay** at the same time. The traditional story credits attention, while H. McIlvaine Parsons (<InlineMath math="1974" />) argued attention mattered only alongside feedback and money.

# Asked

Which archival finding would most strongly support Parsons's explanation over the attention explanation?

# Solution

> On his account, attention mattered only because it arrived together with feedback and money.

To separate two **co-occurring causes**, look for a test case that keeps **attention present** while removing **feedback and group pay**.

## Separating attention from feedback and pay

A second relay group that was <Highlight>watched just as closely</Highlight> but paid a flat wage and given no output reports isolates attention cleanly. If that group shows **no climb**, then attention alone could not drive the rise, which directly supports Parsons's claim that feedback and pay did the work.

Every other option fails to decide between the two stories. Repeating all three factors together fits both accounts equally. Climbing without feedback or pay directly weakens Parsons. A weekly Monday swing comes from the lighting tests and cannot explain a multi-year climb, while enjoying researcher interest leans slightly toward attention.

# Answer

The strongest support for Parsons is: **a second relay group, watched just as closely but paid a flat wage and told nothing about its output, showed no climb**.

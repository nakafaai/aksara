# Given

The installer proposes mounting the panels on **raised frames** so air flows underneath and carries heat away. Efficiency only drops above roughly <InlineMath math="25" /> °C, while March (<InlineMath math="18" /> °C) and April (<InlineMath math="24" /> °C) stayed below that threshold at <InlineMath math="19\%" />.

# Asked

According to the installer's account, which factors would the mounting frames be expected to change?

# Solution

> She proposed mounting the panels on frames that leave a gap underneath, so that air can flow behind them and carry heat away.

Trace the **single mechanism** of the frames: they cool the back of the panels and <Highlight>cannot add any sunlight</Highlight>.

## Testing each factor against the cooling mechanism

| Factor | Link to airflow cooling | Expected to change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Panel temperature on hot, sunny days | Airflow carries heat away from hot panels | **Yes** |
| Amount of sunlight reaching the panels | Frames <Highlight>cannot add any sunlight</Highlight> | **No** |
| Panels' efficiency in the hottest months | Cooler panels above <InlineMath math="25" /> °C lose less efficiency | **Yes** |
| Panels' efficiency in March and April | Averages were <InlineMath math="18" /> °C and <InlineMath math="24" /> °C (below <InlineMath math="25" /> °C) | **No** |
| Amount of dust settling on the glass | Gap underneath does not stop dust on top glass | **No** |

Notice the **threshold check** for March and April: because panel temperatures stayed below roughly <InlineMath math="25" /> °C and already achieved full <InlineMath math="19\%" /> efficiency, cooling frames have no heat loss to recover there.

# Answer

Mounting the panels on frames would be expected to change:

- The panel temperature on hot, sunny days.
- The panels' efficiency in the hottest months.

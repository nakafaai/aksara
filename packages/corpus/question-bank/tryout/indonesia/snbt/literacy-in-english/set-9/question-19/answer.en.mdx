# Given

On Mercator's map, at latitude **<InlineMath math="60^\circ" />** both east-west and north-south lengths double, making areas appear **four times too large**. Stretch keeps growing towards the poles, and Africa has roughly **fourteen times** Greenland's real area.

# Asked

Which statements fit the passage for a teacher using Mercator's map in a lesson on country sizes?

# Solution

> At latitude <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> both stretches double every length, so areas appear four times too large.

Separate **length scaling** from **area scaling**: doubling lengths in two perpendicular directions multiplies area by <InlineMath math="2 \times 2 = 4" />.

## Testing all five scaling statements

Check each classroom statement against the scaling rule and the Africa-Greenland ratio:

<BlockMath math="\text{Area scale at } 60^\circ = 2 \times 2 = 4 \implies \text{fair area} = \frac{1}{4} \text{ of map area}" />

| Statement | Geometric check | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Near <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, shrink by about a quarter for fair comparison | Area is <Highlight>four times too large</Highlight>, so divide by <InlineMath math="4" /> | **Yes** |
| Near <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, area appears twice as large | Length doubles in two directions, giving <InlineMath math="4" /> times area, not <InlineMath math="2" /> | **No** |
| Greenland is enlarged by more than four times | Stretch grows beyond <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> towards the poles | **Yes** |
| Small island near <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> shows true area because shape stays true | True local shape still scales area by <InlineMath math="4" /> | **No** |
| Equal-area map shows Greenland at about one fourteenth of Africa | Africa is <Highlight>roughly fourteen times</Highlight> Greenland's area | **Yes** |

Watch the fourth statement closely: preserving **local shape** means lengths stretch equally in both directions, which still enlarges area fourfold at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />.

# Answer

The statements that fit the passage are:

- A country near latitude <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> appears about four times its true area, so it would have to be shrunk to about a quarter for a fair comparison.
- Greenland, which lies even farther from the equator, is enlarged by more than four times.
- On an equal-area map, Greenland would cover about one fourteenth of the area that Africa covers.

Keep **lengths and areas** apart. At latitude <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> "both stretches double every length", and doubling the length in both directions multiplies an area by <InlineMath math="2 \times 2 = 4" />.

- **Four times, shrink to a quarter**. That is the passage's own figure, "four times too large". Fits.
- **Twice, since lengths double**. Doubling lengths in two directions gives four times the area, not twice. Doesn't fit.
- **Greenland enlarged by more than four times**. The stretch keeps growing towards the poles, and Greenland looks as large as Africa although Africa is about fourteen times larger. Fits.
- **True shape means true area**. Shapes of small areas stay true, but "areas appear four times too large" at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />. Doesn't fit.
- **One fourteenth on an equal-area map**. Areas are kept in proportion, and Africa has "roughly fourteen times" Greenland's area. Fits.

So the statements that fit are the fourfold enlargement at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, Greenland enlarged by more than four times, and Greenland at about one fourteenth of Africa on an equal-area map.

Paragraph 2 lists the raised-letter books' problems: they were **heavy, expensive, slow to read**, few in number, and pupils **could not write** with them. Each of these is a problem that Braille's compact, writable six-dot system would answer. The description sets up **the need** for his invention.

- The pupils used the books, and nothing suggests they did not want to read.
- The author mentions that some teachers preferred raised letters but does not argue for them.
- The injury is described in paragraph 1, separately.
- The raised-letter books were the school's. Barbier's system used dots.

So the author describes them to show the problems that Braille's system would later solve.

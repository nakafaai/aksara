# Given

Text <InlineMath math="2" /> **summarizes badge studies at** two companies where face-to-face talk dropped by about <InlineMath math="70\%" /> while email and instant messaging rose, without recording speech content or work quality.

# Asked

Which statements can be validly concluded from those badge studies.

# Solution

Each statement has to be checked against what the badge studies **actually measured**. Text <InlineMath math="2" /> tells you three things: the badges recorded "when they were talking face to face, though not what they said", face-to-face interaction fell by about <InlineMath math="70\%" />, and "email and instant messaging rose".

> A counter that only clicks when two people stand close can tell you they spoke, not whether they discussed homework or lunch.

## Checking what the badges actually measured

**Statement <InlineMath math="1" />** is a "Yes". The badges caught talking but not its content, and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> itself says "some of the lost talk may have been chatter". So the badges alone can't tell you whether the lost conversations were about work.

**Statement <InlineMath math="2" />** is a "No". One channel went down and two went up. Text <InlineMath math="2" /> gives no totals, so you **can't add them up** to say that communication fell overall.

**Statement <InlineMath math="3" />** is a "No". Text <InlineMath math="2" /> says the studies "did not measure whether the work itself got better or worse". Less talk is not the same as worse work.

**Statement <InlineMath math="4" />** is a "No". "The studies covered two companies." Stretching two cases to most companies is an overreach in scope.

# Answer

**Only the first statement stays inside the badge measurements. the remaining three guess totals, work quality, or industry-wide rules**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The badge records alone can't show whether the lost conversations were about work | Yes |
| Employees communicated less in total after the move | No |
| The move made the employees' work worse at both companies | No |
| Open plans reduce face-to-face interaction at most companies | No |

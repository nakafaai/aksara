A view gets "Yes" only when **both writers hold it**. If only one writer holds it, or the other writer denies it, it gets "No".

**Statement 1** is a "Yes". Text 1 builds on Allen: contact "fell steeply as the distance between their desks grew". Text 2 agrees: "distance does reduce how often colleagues talk".

**Statement 2** is a "No". Text 1 believes it ("A team that can see itself is a team that talks"). Text 2 reports that after the move "face-to-face interaction fell". This is the main point where the texts disagree.

**Statement 3** is a "Yes". Text 1 calls noise "the honest objection" and admits an "occasional distraction". Text 2 has people putting on "headphones against the noise" and says noise makes a quiet question "harder to ask".

**Statement 4** is a "No". Text 2 says "I also suspect that the savings explain more than advocates admit". Text 1 says the savings "are not why the firms I have advised took down their walls". This view is **one-sided**, held only by Text 2.

**Statement 5** is a "Yes". Text 1 values a question "answered across a desk in a minute". Text 2 says "the quick question at a colleague's desk is valuable". They agree it matters and disagree about which office protects it.

# Given

Text <InlineMath math="1" /> **champions open**-plan offices for quick conversations, whereas Text <InlineMath math="2" /> warns that removing walls pushes people into headphones and screen messages.

# Asked

Which views about offices are held by both Text <InlineMath math="1" /> and Text <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Solution

A view gets "Yes" only when **both writers hold it**. If only one writer holds it, or the other writer denies it, it gets "No".

> Both writers agree that quick hallway questions are treasure and noise is a real headache, even though they pick opposite room layouts to protect those chats.

## Comparing shared ground against points of disagreement

**Statement <InlineMath math="1" />** is a "Yes". Text <InlineMath math="1" /> builds on Allen: contact "fell steeply as the distance between their desks grew". Text <InlineMath math="2" /> agrees: "distance does reduce how often colleagues talk".

**Statement <InlineMath math="2" />** is a "No". Text <InlineMath math="1" /> believes it ("A team that can see itself is a team that talks"). Text <InlineMath math="2" /> reports that after the move "face-to-face interaction fell". This is the main point where the texts disagree.

**Statement <InlineMath math="3" />** is a "Yes". Text <InlineMath math="1" /> calls noise "the honest objection" and admits an "occasional distraction". Text <InlineMath math="2" /> has people putting on "headphones against the noise" and says noise and constant visibility make the quick question at a colleague's desk "harder to ask".

**Statement <InlineMath math="4" />** is a "No". Text <InlineMath math="2" /> says "I also suspect that the savings explain more than advocates admit". Text <InlineMath math="1" /> says the savings "are not why the firms I have advised took down their walls". This view is **one-sided**, held only by Text <InlineMath math="2" />.

**Statement <InlineMath math="5" />** is a "Yes". Text <InlineMath math="1" /> values a question "answered across a desk in a minute". Text <InlineMath math="2" /> says "the quick question at a colleague's desk is valuable". They agree it matters and disagree about which office protects it.

# Answer

Both writers agree on distance, noise cost, and valuable quick questions, while disagreeing on open-plan outcomes and rent motives.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Distance affects how often colleagues talk | Yes |
| Taking down partitions increases face-to-face talk | No |
| Noise is a real cost of an open floor | Yes |
| Lower rent per desk is a main reason for open plans | No |
| Quick, unplanned questions can be valuable for work | Yes |

Paragraph 3 claims that wide-ranging animals such as elephants **show stress in captivity**, and that even good enclosures cannot meet their needs. A finding that elephants in well-designed enclosures show **no more stress than wild elephants** would contradict that claim directly.

- Visitors' preferences say nothing about the animals' stress.
- Long daily walks in the wild support the author's point about distance.
- Spending on enclosures does not show whether the animals are stressed.
- Threats to wild polar bears concern their habitat, not stress in zoos.

So the finding that most weakens the claim is that elephants in well-designed enclosures show no more signs of stress than wild ones.

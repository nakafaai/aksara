Paragraph 5 gives the reason: "Some teachers preferred raised letters, which sighted people could also read." Raised letters let **sighted teachers read** what pupils used, while Braille's dots required **learning a new code**. This preference helped delay official adoption until 1854.

- The passage says 63 patterns were enough for every letter, number, and punctuation mark.
- No teacher is said to have preferred Barbier's system.
- Not knowing about the system is not given as a reason.
- The army's use is not mentioned as a condition.

So some teachers resisted because they preferred raised letters, which sighted people could also read.

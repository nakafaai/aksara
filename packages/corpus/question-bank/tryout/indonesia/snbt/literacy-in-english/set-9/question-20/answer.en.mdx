## Reading the Peters map as a trade off

Just as Mercator keeps angles at the expense of area, the Peters map <Highlight>kept the areas of countries in proportion, but it squeezed their shapes</Highlight>. Guided by the closing question of <Highlight>which property a task needs</Highlight>, the author treats Peters as **one more trade-off**: helpful when comparing areas, unsuitable when shapes or angles matter.

The other choices misread that balanced stance. James Gall's <InlineMath math="1855" /> map proves Peters was not the first. The author reports Peters's claim of fairness without adopting it, never flips the trade-off to shapes over areas, and reports cartographers' objections without rejecting equal-area maps outright.

# Answer

The author treats the Peters map **as one more trade-off, keeping areas true by giving up shapes, which suits some tasks but is not the right map for every job**.

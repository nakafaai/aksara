# Given

The Mercator-map passage states that straight lines show **constant-compass rhumb lines** rather than shortest routes, while equal east-west and north-south stretching doubles lengths at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> latitude.

# Asked

Which conclusions about area distortion, routes, and small shapes on Mercator's map follow from the text.

# Solution

The key facts are in paragraphs <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="3" />: straight lines are rhumb lines, the map stretches lengths **by the same factor in both directions**, and at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> that factor is <InlineMath math="2" />.

> Stretching a square photo equally sideways and upwards makes it four times bigger in area while keeping every corner a crisp right angle.

## Checking stretch factors bearings and small shapes

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. At <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> **both stretches double every length**, so areas appear four times too large, while the equator has no stretch. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />. Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> says the straight lines "are not the shortest routes". The shortest path follows a great circle, which looks curved on the map. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />. Mercator spaced the parallels so as "to turn every rhumb line into a straight line", and a rhumb line is the path of a single compass bearing. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />. Greenland looks big because of the stretch, which is built into the projection. Nothing says Mercator measured it wrongly. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="5" />. Near <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> the island is stretched <InlineMath math="2" /> times east to west and <InlineMath math="2" /> times north to south. Equal stretch both ways keeps it square, just four times larger. That's exactly what "keeps angles, and the shapes of small areas, true" means. Yes.

# Answer

Equal two-way stretching inflates high-latitude areas fourfold while preserving bearings and small local shapes.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Area four times too large near <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> | Yes |
| The straight line is the shortest route | No |
| A straight line keeps one bearing | Yes |
| Greenland was measured wrongly | No |
| A small square island stays square | Yes |

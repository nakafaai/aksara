The key facts are in paragraphs 1 to 3: straight lines are rhumb lines, the map stretches lengths **by the same factor in both directions**, and at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> that factor is <InlineMath math="2" />.

Statement 1. At <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> "both stretches double every length, so areas appear four times too large", while the equator has no stretch. Yes.

Statement 2. Paragraph 3 says the straight lines "are not the shortest routes". The shortest path follows a great circle, which looks curved on the map. No.

Statement 3. Mercator spaced the parallels so as "to turn every rhumb line into a straight line", and a rhumb line is the path of a single compass bearing. Yes.

Statement 4. Greenland looks big because of the stretch, which is built into the projection. Nothing says Mercator measured it wrongly. No.

Statement 5. Near <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> the island is stretched <InlineMath math="2" /> times east to west and <InlineMath math="2" /> times north to south. Equal stretch both ways keeps it square, just four times larger. That's exactly what "keeps angles, and the shapes of small areas, true" means. Yes.

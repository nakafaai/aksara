# Given

**The Westford solar**-roof table gives monthly sunlight (kWh), electricity produced (kWh), and panel temperature (°C) from March through August, alongside the installer's note on summer dust.

# Asked

Which conclusions about panel efficiency, temperature, and output follow from the passage and table.

# Solution

**Most statements need each** month's efficiency, so work them out once: electricity divided by sunlight.

> A bucket that catches more rain in a storm can still have a bigger leak than it did in a light drizzle.

## Computing monthly efficiency and checking every statement

| Month | Efficiency | Panel temperature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| March | <InlineMath math="380 \div 2000 = 19\%" /> | <InlineMath math="18" /> °C |
| April | <InlineMath math="494 \div 2600 = 19\%" /> | <InlineMath math="24" /> °C |
| May | <InlineMath math="576 \div 3200 = 18\%" /> | <InlineMath math="33" /> °C |
| June | <InlineMath math="612 \div 3600 = 17\%" /> | <InlineMath math="42" /> °C |
| July | <InlineMath math="627 \div 3800 = 16.5\%" /> | <InlineMath math="47" /> °C |
| August | <InlineMath math="525 \div 3000 = 17.5\%" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> °C |

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. The months above <InlineMath math="25" /> °C are May to August, and all four are below March's <InlineMath math="19\%" />. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />. July's <InlineMath math="16.5\%" /> is lower than June's <InlineMath math="17\%" />. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />. Sunlight went from <InlineMath math="2{,}000" /> to <InlineMath math="2{,}600" />, a rise of <InlineMath math="600" />, which is <InlineMath math="30\%" /> and so more than a quarter. Efficiency stayed at <InlineMath math="19\%" />. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />. The installer herself names a second possible cause: the glass hadn't been washed since March, and **dust builds up** in a dry summer. So the table can't pin July's drop on heat alone. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="5" />. July produced the most electricity but had the lowest efficiency. No.

# Answer

Efficiency drops in every month above **<InlineMath math="25" />** °C and stays flat from March to April, while heat alone cannot take all the blame because dust also accumulated.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Every month above <InlineMath math="25" /> °C was less efficient than March | Yes |
| July beat June on efficiency | No |
| Sunlight up more than a quarter, efficiency unchanged | Yes |
| Heat alone caused July's drop | No |
| Most electricity went with highest efficiency | No |

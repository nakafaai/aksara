Barbier's twelve-dot symbols were "too many to feel with a single touch of the fingertip". Braille's cell of six dots was "small enough to be felt under one fingertip". A reader could recognise **each symbol in a single touch** instead of tracing it, which made reading much faster, while 63 patterns were still enough for letters, numbers, and punctuation.

- Printing cost is not compared between six and twelve dots.
- Some teachers preferred raised letters because sighted people could read them, the opposite of this option.
- Barbier's system represented sounds. Braille's covered letters and spelling.
- Nothing says Barbier asked Braille to change the system.

So the six-dot cell mattered because a whole symbol could be felt at once under one fingertip.

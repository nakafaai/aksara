# Given

Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> describes Arno Peters's <InlineMath math="1973" /> **equal-area map** (shapes squeezed, areas kept in proportion, and James Gall had described almost the same map in <InlineMath math="1855" />), while paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> gives the real area ratio between Africa and Greenland (**roughly fourteen times**).

# Asked

Can each of the five statements about the Peters map and the cartographers' reaction be concluded (**Yes** or **No**)?

# Solution

> His map kept the areas of countries in proportion, but it squeezed their shapes, stretching Africa and South America into tall, narrow forms.

Combine **equal-area preservation** from paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> with the **true Africa-to-Greenland ratio** from paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />.

## Verifying all five statements about Peters

| Statement | Evidence in paragraphs <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> | Concluded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | <Highlight>kept the areas of countries in proportion, but it squeezed their shapes</Highlight> | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | James Gall <Highlight>had described almost the same projection in <InlineMath math="1855" /></Highlight> | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | Equal-size appearance of Greenland and Africa happens on Mercator, not Peters | **No** |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | Proportional areas plus Africa having <Highlight>roughly fourteen times</Highlight> Greenland's area | **Yes** |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | Cartographers objected to distorted shapes and lack of novelty, not wrong areas | **No** |

Notice how **Statements <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />** are opposites: because an equal-area map shows true area ratios, Africa covers roughly fourteen times Greenland's area on Peters's map rather than looking the same size.

# Answer

- Statement <InlineMath math="1" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="2" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="3" />: **No**
- Statement <InlineMath math="4" />: **Yes**
- Statement <InlineMath math="5" />: **No**

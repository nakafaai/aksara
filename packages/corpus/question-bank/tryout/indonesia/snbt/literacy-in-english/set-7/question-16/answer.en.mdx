# Given

After returning from three weeks in hospital, Mrs Okonjo finds nineteen returned umbrellas with notes, receives visitors at her door, and hands the umbrellas out again. The narrator concludes that her father **never understood what the umbrellas had been for**.

# Asked

Which pair of details best reveals what the umbrellas "had been for" in the narrator's view?

# Solution

> She read every note at the kitchen table, and then she gave the umbrellas out again, one by one, to the people who came to the door to ask how she was.

Because the narrator contrasts her view with her father's focus on cost and returns, the right pair must highlight **human connection rather than keeping inventory**.

## Matching both halves to the narrator view

When neighbours <Highlight>came to the door to ask how she was</Highlight>, we see the warmth and community the lending created. When Mrs Okonjo <Highlight>gave the umbrellas out again, one by one</Highlight> to those very visitors, we see that holding on to umbrellas was never her goal.

Every other option includes at least one detail tied to the **father's transactional view** or mere inventory: calling the lending a "leak", counting nineteen returned umbrellas, stressing cheap umbrellas that went missing, or pairing a late-return apology with stopping further purchases.

# Answer

The pair of details that best shows what the umbrellas had been for is that **people came to the door to ask how she was, and she gave the umbrellas out to them again, one by one**.

Check each claim against **what the 1993 study compared and measured**: Mozart against silence and relaxation instructions, on one spatial task, for about fifteen minutes.

- **Better than after relaxation instructions**. The study compared exactly this. Not beyond it.
- **Lasts long after the music stops**. The improvement "had faded within about fifteen minutes", and none of the researchers "had claimed a lasting effect". Beyond it.
- **Better than after silence**. Also one of the study's comparisons. Not beyond it.
- **Raises intelligence in general**. The study measured "a spatial reasoning task". The IQ-style figure describes the size of the change on that task, not a change in general intelligence. Beyond it.
- **Mozart in particular**. The study compared Mozart only with silence and relaxation instructions, so it couldn't show that Mozart mattered more than music in general. Later work found that lively music helped "whether or not the lively piece was by Mozart". Beyond it.

The claims that went beyond the study are that the benefit lasts long after the music, that it raises intelligence in general, and that it comes from Mozart in particular.

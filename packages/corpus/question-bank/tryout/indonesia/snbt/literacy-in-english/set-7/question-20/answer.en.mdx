# Given

Mrs Okonjo lent umbrellas freely to anyone on the street without tracking them or putting up a return sign, kept replacing them despite low returns, and valued the **notes and visits from neighbours** above getting the umbrellas back.

# Asked

Which analogous situation is most like Mrs Okonjo's lending as presented in the story?

# Solution

> She read every note at the kitchen table, and then she gave the umbrellas out again, one by one, to the people who came to the door to ask how she was.

Match **both core traits** of Mrs Okonjo's lending: giving freely without a tracking system or demand for returns, and valuing the human visits more than the objects themselves.

## Matching the analogy on both traits

A neighbour who lends tools <Highlight>without keeping a list</Highlight> mirrors Mrs Okonjo declining to put up a sign, and discovering that <Highlight>the visits from people bringing them back matter more to her than the tools</Highlight> captures why Mrs Okonjo felt she had all she needed once neighbours came to check on her.

Every other option breaks the **second half of the parallel**: putting up a return notice repeats the father's sign, and viewing a mass return as repayment misses that Mrs Okonjo gave the umbrellas right back out.

Hiding losses does not fit someone who openly kept buying replacements, and quitting when too few return contradicts her continuing even when <Highlight>hardly any came back</Highlight>.

# Answer

The situation most like Mrs Okonjo's lending is **a neighbour who lends tools without keeping a list, and finds that the visits from people bringing them back matter more to her than the tools**.

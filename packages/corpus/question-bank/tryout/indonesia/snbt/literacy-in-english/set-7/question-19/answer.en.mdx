The father **counts umbrellas**: he calls the lending a "leak", and even after the returns he says the sign "would have worked faster". Mrs Okonjo let the sign never go up, kept lending without asking, and said she "had all she needed" after the notes and visits. What she got from lending was her neighbours, not her umbrellas.

- The facts are right, but they still measure the lending by returns, the very view of the lending that the narrator says he never understood.
- The first half is right, but the umbrellas were "none of them hers", and she bought them for others, so saving money for herself was not her aim.
- The first half fits "leak", but keeping the street dry doesn't explain the notes, the visits, or why she gave the umbrellas out again.
- The first half fits "leak", but it is the father, not the narrator, who says the sign would have been faster.

So he never understood because he judged the lending by returns, while for her the point was the ties it built with her neighbours.

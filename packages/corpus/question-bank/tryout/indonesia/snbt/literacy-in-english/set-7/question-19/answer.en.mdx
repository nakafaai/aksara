The opening tells how Carnegie, as a poor young worker, borrowed books from Anderson's library and later said they "shaped his life". The next sentences describe how he gave much of his fortune to **public libraries**. Placing the two together suggests **a personal reason** for his gifts: he was offering others what a library had once given him.

- The passage does not compare private and public libraries.
- His fortune came from steel, which the opening does not explain.
- Nothing is said about other working boys becoming rich.
- Saturday opening was only Anderson's arrangement, not a recommendation.

So the author begins with Anderson's library to suggest a personal reason behind Carnegie's gifts to libraries.

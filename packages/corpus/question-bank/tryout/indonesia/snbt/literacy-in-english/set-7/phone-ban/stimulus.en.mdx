**Text A**

Last September our school banned phones from the moment pupils arrive until the end of the day. By June the share of pupils passing their end-of-year maths exam had risen from <InlineMath math="61\%" /> to <InlineMath math="70\%" />, and reports of online bullying during school hours had fallen by half. Teachers say lessons are calmer. I would urge every school to follow our lead.

**Text B**

I don't doubt that the school feels calmer, and keeping phones out of lessons seems sensible to me. But the pass rate is a weak measure of the ban's effect. That same September the school also changed its maths textbook and hired two new maths teachers, so the rise could come from any of the three changes. And bullying reports during school hours were always likely to fall once pupils no longer had phones in school. What matters is whether the bullying moved to the evening, and nobody counted that. Studies that compared schools with and without bans in the same years have found small gains in results, mostly among pupils who were already struggling. I would support a ban, but I would not promise parents a nine-point rise.

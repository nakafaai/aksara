Paragraph 3 gives Carnegie's reasoning: "a community which paid for its own library would value it and keep it open", while a gift that cost nothing "might be neglected once the novelty wore off". The yearly payment was meant to give the town **a stake in its library**.

- The yearly payment was about a tenth of the cost and went to running the library, not to repaying Carnegie.
- The taxes paid for the library, not for his steel mills.
- Many small towns received their first library through the grants.
- Selling the buildings back is not mentioned.

So Carnegie required the payment because he thought a community that paid for its library would value it.

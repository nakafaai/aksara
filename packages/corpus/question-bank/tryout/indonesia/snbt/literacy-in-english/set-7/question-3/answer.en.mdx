# Given

A school plans to play a Mozart sonata every **morning** over its loudspeakers to boost scores on an **afternoon** maths test. In the passage, the boost faded quickly and depended on alertness and mood right before the task.

# Asked

Based on the passage, what outcome should the school expect from this plan?

# Solution

> The improvement was modest, the equivalent of eight or nine points on an IQ-style scale, and it had faded within about fifteen minutes.

Two facts from the passage settle this scenario, and the school's schedule breaks **both conditions**.

## Timing and mood at test time

First, the gain <Highlight>had faded within about fifteen minutes</Highlight>, so music played in the morning cannot carry over to an afternoon exam. Second, the mood explanation states that any gain depends on how <Highlight>alert and cheerful</Highlight> pupils feel right before <Highlight>a puzzle taken straight afterwards</Highlight>, not hours earlier.

This rules out the other options cleanly. A small pooled effect does not last for hours, pupils who enjoy morning music still lose the lift by the afternoon, the composer is **not what drives the effect**, and the failure comes from timing and mood rather than the thirty-six-student sample size of the first study.

# Answer

The school should expect **little or no benefit, because any effect fades within about fifteen minutes and depends on how alert and cheerful pupils feel when they take the test**.

# Given

Text A reports that the maths pass rate rose from <InlineMath math="61\%" /> to <InlineMath math="70\%" /> after the **September changes**. The question supposes that the new textbook and two new teachers together would have produced a <InlineMath math="6" />-percentage-point rise on their own.

# Asked

Reasoning like Text B, how much of Text A's rise could the phone ban then account for?

# Solution

> That same September the school also changed its maths textbook and hired two new maths teachers, so the rise could come from any of the three changes.

Find the total rise in percentage points, subtract the part explained by the **other two changes**, and express the remainder as a fraction of the total rise.

## Splitting the pass rate rise

First compute the total rise and the share left for the phone ban:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Total rise} &= 70\% - 61\% = 9 \text{ percentage points} \\ \text{Remainder for ban} &= 9 - 6 = 3 \text{ percentage points} \\ \text{Share of the rise} &= \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3} \end{aligned}" />

| Component | Percentage points | Share of total rise |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| New textbook and new teachers | <InlineMath math="6" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{3}" /> |
| Phone ban remainder | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}" /> |
| Total rise (<InlineMath math="61\%" /> to <InlineMath math="70\%" />) | <InlineMath math="9" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |

This eliminates the distractors cleanly. The remaining <InlineMath math="3" /> percentage points are a third of the **<InlineMath math="9" />-point rise**, not a third of the old <InlineMath math="61\%" /> rate (which would be about <InlineMath math="20" /> points). Likewise, <InlineMath math="6" /> points belong to the textbook and teachers, Text B never states a numerical size for other studies, and <InlineMath math="3" /> percentage points is not <InlineMath math="3\%" /> of the old rate.

# Answer

The ban could then account for **about <InlineMath math="3" /> percentage points, a third of the rise**.

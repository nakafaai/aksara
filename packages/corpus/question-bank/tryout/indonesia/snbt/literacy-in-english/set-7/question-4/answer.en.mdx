The author's standard has three parts, and paragraph 6 says a strong case needs **all three at once**:

1. "an action that is not part of the species' ordinary behaviour,"
2. "materials whose properties the bird has had no chance to learn by handling them,"
3. "the right choice made before the first attempt."

So check every option against all three. One miss is enough to fail.

The option with materials the crow "has never touched" meets condition 2. The crow picks the piece "before trying any," which meets condition 3. And the task is "unlike anything crows do in the wild," which meets condition 1. It's the only option that passes every test.

Why the other options fall short:

- The first-try option is the speed argument that paragraph 6 calls "weak evidence," and it uses twigs the crow handles daily, so it fails conditions 1 and 2.
- The plastic-strip option meets condition 2, but the crow succeeds only "after several failed attempts," so it fails condition 3.
- The filmed wild crow is doing ordinary hook making, which paragraph 3 says is part of the species' natural behaviour, so it fails condition 1.
- The weeks-of-handling option fails condition 2, because the crow has had plenty of chance to learn how those wire pieces behave.

So the observation closest to the author's standard is a crow that, without ever having handled the materials, chooses the right piece before any attempt in a task crows never meet in the wild.

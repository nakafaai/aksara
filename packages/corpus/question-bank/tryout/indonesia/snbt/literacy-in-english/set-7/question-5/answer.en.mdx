# Given

Under the **mood explanation**, anything that leaves a student briefly more alert and cheerful right before a short spatial puzzle can offer a modest lift, while the gain fades within about fifteen minutes.

# Asked

If the mood explanation is right, which pieces of advice for a student about to solve a short spatial puzzle are supported by the passage?

# Solution

> Anything that leaves a listener briefly more alert and in a better mood, such as a favourite song, an interesting story or perhaps a cup of coffee, should help a little with a puzzle taken straight afterwards.

Every piece of advice must satisfy **two requirements**: it must raise alertness and positive mood, and it must happen right before the puzzle.

## Testing each piece of advice

Match each suggestion against the passage's mood and timing criteria.

| Advice | Lifts mood and alertness | Right timing | Supported |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Favourite song just before the puzzle | Yes (<Highlight>a favourite song</Highlight>) | Yes (<Highlight>straight afterwards</Highlight>) | **Yes** |
| Favourite song an hour before | Yes | No (fades in about fifteen minutes) | **No** |
| Interesting story just before, if you prefer stories | Yes (<Highlight>preferred a story read aloud</Highlight>) | Yes | **Yes** |
| Slow, sad music just before | No (performed worse than lively music) | Yes | **No** |
| Mozart rather than any other composer | Wrong reason (<Highlight>not about Mozart</Highlight>) | Not stated | **No** |

Notice how each rejected option fails a **clear passage rule**: waiting an hour lets the fifteen-minute boost disappear, slow and sad music lowers performance compared with lively music, and insisting on Mozart contradicts the mood explanation directly.

# Answer

**The supported pieces of advice are**:

- Listen to a favourite song just before the puzzle.
- If you enjoy stories more than music, listen to an interesting story just before the puzzle.

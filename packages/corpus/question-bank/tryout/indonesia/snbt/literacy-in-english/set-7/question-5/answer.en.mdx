Each piece of advice has to pass **two tests**: it must lift alertness and mood, and it must come **just before** the puzzle, since the effect fades within about fifteen minutes.

- **A favourite song just before**. The passage names "a favourite song" as something that should help "a puzzle taken straight afterwards". Passes both. Supported.
- **A favourite song an hour before**. Right kind of lift, wrong timing. By then the effect has faded. Not supported.
- **A story, for someone who prefers stories**. Students "who preferred a story read aloud did as well after hearing the story" as others after Mozart. Passes both. Supported.
- **Slow, sad music**. Students who heard "slow, sad music" did worse than those who heard lively music. Wrong kind of mood. Not supported.
- **Mozart rather than anyone else**. The mood explanation says the result "was not about Mozart". Not supported.

The supported advice is to listen to a favourite song just before the puzzle, or, for someone who prefers stories, to hear an interesting story just before it.

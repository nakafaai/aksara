This asks for the author's own view, so watch how strong each claim is. The author often says "weak" or "may," and a statement that turns that into "nothing at all" goes too far.

**Speed tells us nothing.** Paragraph 6 says "Speed on its own is weak evidence." Weak evidence is still some evidence, so the author wouldn't say it tells us nothing at all. No.

**Payoff versus foresight.** Paragraph 4 says a payoff "tells us why a habit of hook making would persist" but "tells us nothing about what an individual bird pictures before it starts." Those are two different things. Yes.

**Quick success from learned rules.** Paragraph 6 says a bird "that has learned general rules, such as 'longer reaches farther', can succeed quickly without imagining anything." Yes.

**Betty tells us nothing.** Paragraph 3 says "None of this shows that she was not clever." Only **the part of the case that rested on novelty** got weaker. Her result still shows a clever bird using an unfamiliar material. No.

**Captive results and wild behaviour.** This one needs paragraphs 2 and 3 together. Betty's bending was read as invention because bending "was thought to be new to the species." Once the 2016 study showed that bending is natural behaviour, that reading looked weaker. Not knowing the wild behaviour led to the misjudgment. Yes.

Text B's objection is that the ban arrived together with a new textbook and new teachers. To strengthen Text A, you need evidence that **the other two changes don't produce the rise without the ban**.

The finding about the nearby school supplies that. A nearby school made the same textbook and teacher changes, **without** a ban, and its pass rate didn't move. That makes the ban the likelier cause of the rise at Text A's school.

- More homework would be a possible way the ban helped, but it doesn't rule out the textbook or the teachers.
- Rises at other schools with bans fit Text A, but those schools may have made other changes too. Unlike the nearby school with the same changes but no ban, this doesn't separate the ban from the textbook and teachers.
- A rise already under way for five years points to a trend, not the ban, so it weakens Text A.
- Award-winning new teachers make the teachers a stronger rival explanation, so this weakens Text A too.

The finding that would most strengthen Text A is that a nearby school with the same textbook and new teachers, but no ban, saw no change in its pass rate.

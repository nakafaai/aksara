# Given

Text A claims the **phone ban** raised the maths pass rate, while Text B points out two rival causes introduced at the exact same time: a new maths textbook and two new maths teachers.

# Asked

Which hypothetical finding would most strengthen Text A's claim that the ban caused the pass-rate rise?

# Solution

> That same September the school also changed its maths textbook and hired two new maths teachers, so the rise could come from any of the three changes.

To strengthen Text A against Text B's objection, we need evidence that **rules out the two rival changes** as the source of the rise.

## Ruling out the rival explanations

A nearby school that adopted the <Highlight>same textbook</Highlight> and hired new maths teachers **without banning phones**, yet saw no change in its pass rate, acts like a control group. It shows that the other two changes alone did not lift pass rates, making the phone ban a far stronger explanation at Text A's school.

The remaining options do not isolate the ban from its **confounding variables**. More homework still leaves the textbook and teachers unexamined. Rises at three other schools with bans fail to control for other changes those schools may have made. Meanwhile, a five-year upward trend or award-winning new teachers actively weaken Text A by pointing away from the ban.

# Answer

The finding that would most strengthen Text A is that **a nearby school that changed to the same textbook and hired new maths teachers that year, but did not ban phones, saw no change in its pass rate**.

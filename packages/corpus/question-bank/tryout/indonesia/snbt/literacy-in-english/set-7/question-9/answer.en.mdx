Paragraph 3 worries that a pupil "who reads to collect badges may stop reading once the badges stop". The concern is about **what happens after the rewards end**. A finding that pupils who read for badges **kept reading** after the badges stopped would show the feared effect did not happen, which weakens the concern directly.

- Finishing more exercises while points are given is what supporters already claim. It says nothing about after.
- Discouraged pupils near the bottom support the author's second concern.
- Points being easy to add matches the author's last paragraph and does not test the concern.
- A preference between badges and certificates does not show what happens when rewards stop.

So the finding that most weakens the concern is that pupils who once read for badges kept reading after the badges stopped.

# Given

Text A states that the maths pass rate climbed from <InlineMath math="61\%" /> to <InlineMath math="70\%" />. Text B notes that the school also changed its **maths textbook** and hired **two new maths teachers** in the same September.

# Asked

How large was the pass-rate increase as a proportion of the **old pass rate**, and how does Text B evaluate that rise?

# Solution

> By June the share of pupils passing their end-of-year maths exam had risen from <InlineMath math="61\%" /> to <InlineMath math="70\%" />, and reports of online bullying during school hours had fallen by half.

Solve the two parts in order: first measure the relative increase against the **starting base of <InlineMath math="61\%" />**, then match Text B's causal judgment.

## Relative increase and causal judgment

Divide the <InlineMath math="9" />-point rise by the old pass rate of <InlineMath math="61\%" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Absolute rise} &= 70\% - 61\% = 9 \text{ percentage points} \\ \text{Relative rise} &= \frac{9}{61} \approx 0.1475 \approx 15\% \end{aligned}" />

| Calculation error to avoid | Formula used | Value | Why wrong |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Treating points as relative percent | <InlineMath math="70 - 61" /> | <InlineMath math="9\%" /> | Ignores base of <InlineMath math="61\%" /> |
| Dividing by the new rate | <InlineMath math="9 \div 70" /> | <InlineMath math="13\%" /> | Uses end rate instead of old rate |
| Dividing by the old rate | <InlineMath math="9 \div 61" /> | <InlineMath math="15\%" /> | Correct relative increase |

For the second part, Text B does not call the rise too small or attribute it solely to the ban. Instead, Text B states that <Highlight>the rise could come from any of the three changes</Highlight> introduced that September.

# Answer

The pass rate rose **by about <InlineMath math="15\%" /> of the old rate, which Text B says could have come from any of three changes made that September**.

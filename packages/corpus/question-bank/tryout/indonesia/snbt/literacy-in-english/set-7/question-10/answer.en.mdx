# Given

The question asks you to check five statements describing **what Text A and Text B actually say** about the phone ban, bullying, other studies, and the new maths textbook.

# Asked

Which statements about the two texts are accurate?

# Solution

> Studies that compared schools with and without bans in the same years have found small gains in results, mostly among pupils who were already struggling. I would support a ban, but I would not promise parents a nine-point rise.

Check each statement against the **exact attribution and wording** of Text A and Text B.

## Verifying each statement against the texts

| Statement | Direct textual evidence | Accurate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text B supports a ban but doubts the size of Text A's rise | <Highlight>I would support a ban, but I would not promise parents a nine-point rise</Highlight> | **Yes** |
| Text B says bullying increased after the ban | Text B only says <Highlight>nobody counted that</Highlight> in the evening | **No** |
| Text B says a fall during school hours was expected | <Highlight>always likely to fall once pupils no longer had phones in school</Highlight> | **Yes** |
| Text B reports gains mostly among struggling pupils | <Highlight>mostly among pupils who were already struggling</Highlight> | **Yes** |
| Text A reports that the new maths textbook improved results | Text A never mentions the textbook | **No** |

Notice the two **attribution errors** that rule out the false choices: Text B raises evening bullying as an unmeasured question rather than claiming an increase, and only Text B mentions the new maths textbook.

# Answer

**The accurate statements are**:

- Text B supports a ban but doubts that it explains the size of the rise Text A reports.
- Text B's point about bullying is that a fall during school hours was expected once phones were gone.
- Text B reports that the gains found in other studies were mostly among pupils who were already struggling.

# Given

Snow's table shows **Lambeth** supplied <InlineMath math="26{,}107" /> houses and recorded **<InlineMath math="98" /> cholera deaths**, while **Southwark and Vauxhall** suffered a rate of **<InlineMath math="315" /> deaths per <InlineMath math="10{,}000" /> houses**.

# Asked

If Lambeth's houses had suffered Southwark and Vauxhall's death rate, about how many deaths would they have had, and how does that compare with their actual <InlineMath math="98" /> deaths?

# Solution

Multiply Lambeth's **<InlineMath math="26{,}107" /> houses** by the **rate per <InlineMath math="10{,}000" /> houses**, then subtract the <InlineMath math="98" /> actual deaths.

> Applying Southwark and Vauxhall's rate of three hundred and fifteen deaths per ten thousand houses to Lambeth's houses predicts about eight hundred and twenty deaths, roughly seven hundred and twenty more than the ninety-eight recorded.

## Applying the higher death rate to Lambeth houses

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Lambeth houses supplied | <InlineMath math="26{,}107" /> |
| Southwark and Vauxhall rate (per <InlineMath math="10{,}000" /> houses) | <InlineMath math="315" /> |
| Expected Lambeth deaths at higher rate | <InlineMath math="\approx 822 \approx 820" /> |
| Actual Lambeth deaths | <InlineMath math="98" /> |
| Excess deaths avoided | <InlineMath math="\approx 724 \approx 720" /> |

Computing **expected deaths** and the **difference above actual deaths**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Expected deaths} &= 26{,}107 \times \frac{315}{10{,}000} \approx 822 \approx 820 \\ \text{Difference above actual} &= 822 - 98 = 724 \approx 720 \text{ more} \end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="8{,}220" />** divides by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> instead of <InlineMath math="10{,}000" />, making the **place value ten times too large**.
- **<InlineMath math="1{,}263" />** copies Southwark and Vauxhall's raw count without adjusting for Lambeth supplying fewer houses.
- **<InlineMath math="820" />, Lambeth's water more dangerous** gets the calculation right but reverses the conclusion: Lambeth's actual <InlineMath math="37" /> per <InlineMath math="10{,}000" /> was far safer.
- **<InlineMath math="720" /> deaths, <InlineMath math="622" /> more** mistakes the difference (<InlineMath math="720" />) for the expected total and subtracts <InlineMath math="98" /> a second time.

# Answer

**About <InlineMath math="820" />, which is about <InlineMath math="720" /> more than the <InlineMath math="98" /> they actually had**.

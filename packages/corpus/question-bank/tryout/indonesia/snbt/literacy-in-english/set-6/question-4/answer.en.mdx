# Given

Five observations from the <InlineMath math="1854" /> London cholera outbreak must be sorted by whether **both the bad-air theory and Snow's water theory** can explain them equally well.

# Asked

Which observations fit the bad-air theory about as well as Snow's water theory? Select all that apply.

# Solution

Test each observation against **what both theories predict**: an observation fits both only when exposure to local air and local pump water move together.

> Clustering around the pump and falling deaths after residents fled both involve local air and pump water together, whereas the distant widow, mixed-supply neighbours, and brewery workers all decouple water from air.

## Comparing predictions of both theories

| Observation | Verdict | Air theory versus water theory |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deaths clustered around the Broad Street pump | **Fits both** | Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> says <Highlight>a cluster around the pump fitted the bad-air theory almost as well as Snow's</Highlight> because nearby residents shared the air. |
| Deaths fell after many residents left the area | **Fits both** | Fleeing residents escaped both Broad Street's air and its pump water, so either theory predicts a drop. |
| Widow miles away drank Broad Street water and died | **Water only** | She lived miles away from Broad Street's air, which foul local air cannot explain. |
| Neighbours on the same street died at different rates by water company | **Water only** | Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> stresses <Highlight>Neighbours breathed the same air and lived alike, yet drank different water</Highlight>. |
| Brewery workers beside the pump escaped almost entirely | **Water only** | They breathed the exact same air beside the pump yet avoided its water, contradicting bad air. |

# Answer

- **Deaths clustered around the Broad Street pump**.
- **Deaths fell after many residents left the area, which took them away from both the pump and the air around it**.

The two groups differ in **one key way**: ducks at the ends sit in "the most exposed positions", while ducks in the middle are shielded by others. Because the ducks at the ends spent far more time with one eye open, the comparison links the behaviour to **how exposed a duck is**. That is how the experiment shows that half-brain sleep "can be adjusted to danger".

- The experiment measured one-eyed sleep, not sleep quality.
- The ducks' ages are never mentioned.
- Counting ducks is not the purpose of a comparison between positions.
- Middle ducks did sleep. They simply kept one eye open less often.

So the comparison is useful because it shows that the behaviour changes with how exposed a duck is.

Paragraph 3 argues that a curved grade loses its **meaning**: it shows only a student's rank, not what the student can do. The strong-class example makes this concrete: "a student who has mastered the material may still receive a C". If a curve can give a low grade to someone who has **learned the material well**, the grade no longer tells an employer what that student can do.

- Separate classes are never proposed.
- Cheating is not mentioned.
- Under a curve only a fixed share receives an A, so not every student in a strong class can.
- The example concerns curved grades, not a teacher's own standards.

So the author describes the strong class to show that a curve can give low marks to students who have learned well.

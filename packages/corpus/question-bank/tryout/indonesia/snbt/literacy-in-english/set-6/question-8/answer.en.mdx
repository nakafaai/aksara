# Given

Text A reports that the share of admitted students from low-income families rose from **<InlineMath math="12\%" /> to <InlineMath math="19\%" />**.

# Asked

By how many percentage points did that share change, and what is that change as a percentage of the old share?

# Solution

Compute the **percentage-point difference** first (<InlineMath math="19\% - 12\%" />), then divide by the **original <InlineMath math="12\%" /> share** rather than the new <InlineMath math="19\%" /> share.

> Rising from twelve percent to nineteen percent is an increase of seven percentage points, which relative to the starting base of twelve percent equals seven twelfths, or about fifty-eight percent.

## Computing percentage points and relative growth

| Measure | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Absolute change | <InlineMath math="19\% - 12\%" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> percentage points |
| Relative change (of old share) | <InlineMath math="\frac{7}{12}" /> | <InlineMath math="\approx 58.3\% \approx 58\%" /> |

Evaluating **both measures** together:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Percentage-point change} &= 19\% - 12\% = 7 \text{ percentage points} \\ \text{Proportion of old share} &= \frac{7}{12} \approx 58.3\% \approx 58\% \end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="37\%" />** divides by the **new share** (<InlineMath math="\frac{7}{19}" />) instead of the starting <InlineMath math="12\%" /> base.
- **A rise of <InlineMath math="7\%" />** confuses percentage points with relative percentage growth.
- **<InlineMath math="19" /> percentage points** mistakes the final share for the amount of change.
- **<InlineMath math="40\%" />** copies the unrelated increase in total applications.

# Answer

**It rose by <InlineMath math="7" /> percentage points, about <InlineMath math="58\%" /> of the old share**.

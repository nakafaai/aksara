# Given

Starting from the **<InlineMath math="2024" /> figures** (<InlineMath math="162{,}000" /> print, <InlineMath math="27{,}000" /> e-books, <InlineMath math="13{,}200" /> audiobooks), suppose next year e-books **triple again**, print falls by **another <InlineMath math="10\%" />**, and audiobooks remain unchanged.

# Asked

Which statement would be true next year?

# Solution

Apply both changes to the **<InlineMath math="2024" /> baseline**, then compare the projected print and e-book counts directly.

> Next year print books drop to one hundred forty-five thousand eight hundred while e-books triple to eighty-one thousand, leaving print ahead by sixty-four thousand eight hundred loans.

## Projecting next year loans

| Format | <InlineMath math="2024" /> loans | Rule for next year | Next year loans |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Print books | <InlineMath math="162{,}000" /> | Fall <InlineMath math="10\%" /> | <InlineMath math="145{,}800" /> |
| E-books | <InlineMath math="27{,}000" /> | Triple (<InlineMath math="\times 3" />) | <InlineMath math="81{,}000" /> |
| Audiobooks | <InlineMath math="13{,}200" /> | Unchanged | <InlineMath math="13{,}200" /> |
| Total loans | <InlineMath math="202{,}200" /> | Sum | <InlineMath math="240{,}000" /> |

Computing **each projection** and the **remaining lead of print over e-books**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Next-year e-books} &= 3 \times 27{,}000 = 81{,}000 \\ \text{Next-year print} &= 162{,}000 - 10\% \times 162{,}000 = 145{,}800 \\ \text{Print lead over e-books} &= 145{,}800 - 81{,}000 = 64{,}800 \text{ loans} \end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options fail

- **E-books overtake print (<InlineMath math="600\%" />)** gets both the growth math (tripling twice multiplies by <InlineMath math="9" />, an <InlineMath math="800\%" /> rise) and the comparison (<InlineMath math="81{,}000 < 145{,}800" />) **wrong**.
- **Lent about equally** ignores that e-books gain <InlineMath math="54{,}000" /> while print drops only <InlineMath math="16{,}200" />, still leaving a wide gap.
- **Ahead by <InlineMath math="135{,}000" />** subtracts the unchanged <InlineMath math="2024" /> values (<InlineMath math="162{,}000 - 27{,}000" />).
- **Total loans would fall** contradicts the new total of <InlineMath math="240{,}000" />, up from <InlineMath math="202{,}200" />.

# Answer

**Print books would still be lent more often than e-books, by about <InlineMath math="64{,}800" /> loans**.

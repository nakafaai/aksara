# Given

Five details from the story describe Mrs Varga's behaviour in the spring and June, of which **four** act as clues to her declining hearing while **one** is her polite parting remark to the mother.

# Asked

Which details suggest that Mrs Varga could no longer hear well? Select all that apply.

# Solution

Keep every detail showing **missed sound, demand for loudness, or visual substitution**, while excluding her surface-level parting statement to the mother.

> Praising a halted performance, demanding ever-louder playing, tracking the metronome arm by eye, and missing the ringing hall telephone all point directly to failing hearing, whereas her polite farewell remark hides it.

## Evaluating all five details

| Detail | Verdict | Connection to hearing loss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Praised a performance so bad the narrator stopped | **True** | Calling a broken-off piece <Highlight>Good. Much better</Highlight> shows she could not hear the errors. |
| Asked for the slow movement louder and louder | **True** | Asking <Highlight>louder, then louder again, until it was not slow at all</Highlight> shows she needed extreme volume to hear it. |
| Told the mother she had taught her everything she could | **False** | This polite explanation conceals her hearing loss rather than evidencing it. |
| Watched the metronome's swinging arm instead of its tick | **True** | Placing it on the stand and watching <Highlight>the arm rather than me</Highlight> substitutes sight for sound. |
| Did not move when the telephone rang in the hall | **True** | Failing to react when <Highlight>the telephone rang in the hall</Highlight> directly indicates she did not hear it. |

# Answer

- **She praised a performance so bad that the narrator stopped in the middle**.
- **She asked for the slow movement to be played louder and louder**.
- **She watched the metronome's swinging arm rather than listening for its tick**.
- **She did not move when the telephone rang in the hall**.

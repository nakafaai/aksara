# Given

A local newspaper runs the headline <Highlight>A reading revolution</Highlight> because e-book loans tripled from <InlineMath math="9{,}000" /> to <InlineMath math="27{,}000" />, yet the library's director observes they lent <Highlight>about the same number of books, in different shapes</Highlight>.

# Asked

Which statement best explains why that headline is misleading?

# Solution

Join **both numerical facts** behind the director's remark: e-books started from a **tiny <InlineMath math="9{,}000" /> base**, and their entire **<InlineMath math="18{,}000" /> gain** simply replaced the **<InlineMath math="18{,}000" /> drop** in print loans.

> Tripling a small nine-thousand base adds eighteen thousand e-book loans that exactly cancel eighteen thousand lost print loans, leaving overall borrowing almost flat at just over one percent growth.

## Why tripling exaggerates the shift

- **Small starting base**: Going from <InlineMath math="9{,}000" /> to <InlineMath math="27{,}000" /> triples e-books, yet adds only <InlineMath math="18{,}000" /> loans inside a <InlineMath math="200{,}000" />-loan library.
- **Exact offset by print**: Print fell by the same <InlineMath math="18{,}000" /> (<InlineMath math="180{,}000" /> to <InlineMath math="162{,}000" />), leaving total loans barely higher (<InlineMath math="200{,}000" /> to <InlineMath math="202{,}200" />).

## Why the other options fail

- **Should have reported <InlineMath math="200\%" /> instead of tripling** misses the point: tripling and a <InlineMath math="200\%" /> increase mean the exact same thing.
- **Should have called it a reading slowdown** is false: a <InlineMath math="1\%" /> increase is still a slight rise, not a decline.
- **Came mostly from new readers** is unsupported by the table and contradicted by the matching print drop.
- **Director's print-collection remark shows figures are wrong** misreads a planning policy as a data error.

# Answer

**E-book loans started from a small base, and their whole rise matched the fall in print loans, so total loans barely changed**.

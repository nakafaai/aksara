# Given

During the first three years, Mrs Varga told the narrator that a repeated wrong note in bar twelve had become <Highlight>part of the furniture</Highlight>. In the final sentence, Mr Hollis <Highlight>stopped me at bar twelve</Highlight> in his very first week.

# Asked

What does Mrs Varga mean by "part of the furniture", and how does the ending return to it?

# Solution

Unpack **both halves**: the **idiom** describing an ingrained habit the player no longer notices, and the **closing payoff** confirming that the mistake survived Mrs Varga's silent spring until Mr Hollis heard it immediately.

> Calling a repeated mistake part of the furniture means it has blended into the background so completely the player stops noticing it, and Mr Hollis catching it in week one confirms Mrs Varga could no longer hear it.

## Idiom meaning and closing echo

- **The metaphor**: Just as you stop noticing familiar household furniture, the narrator had played the wrong note in bar twelve **so often she no longer noticed it**.
- **The ending echo**: Because Mrs Varga stopped correcting it during spring while her hearing failed, the old mistake stayed intact until **Mr Hollis, who can hear it, immediately stops her at bar twelve** in week one.

## Why the other options fail

- **Narrator fixed it before leaving** is disproved by Mr Hollis stopping her at bar twelve right away.
- **Mrs Varga had stopped hearing it when she coined the phrase** gets the timeline wrong: she used the phrase during the first three years when she still heard every flaw.
- **Conservatory piano is out of tune** invents an instrument fault instead of the narrator's habitual wrong note.
- **Note came from an old piano and was never wrong** contradicts paragraph <InlineMath math="1" /> calling it <Highlight>A wrong note in bar twelve that I had played</Highlight>.

# Answer

**That the note is a habit so old the narrator no longer notices it, and Mr Hollis, who can hear it, stops her there in his first week**.

Two halves: the image, and the echo at the end.

**The image**. The narrator had played the wrong note "so often it had become" part of the furniture. Furniture is what you stop seeing in a room you live in. The mistake has become **so familiar that the narrator no longer notices it**.

**The echo**. The story ends: "The first week, he stopped me at bar twelve." The note was still there all spring, and Mrs Varga, whose hearing the story suggests was failing, never mentioned it. Mr Hollis catches it at once, which suggests he can hear what she no longer could.

- The narrator never fixes it. Mr Hollis stops her on that bar in the first week.
- Mrs Varga said this back when she still corrected everything, so the image is about the narrator's habit, not her own hearing.
- The narrator's habit is right, but the conservatory piano is never mentioned. The wrong note travelled with the narrator.
- The note was a wrong note the narrator played, not a fault in an old piano.

She means the mistake is a habit so old that the narrator no longer notices it, and the ending returns to it when Mr Hollis, who can hear it, stops her at bar twelve in his first week.

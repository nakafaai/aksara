# Given

A street map clustering deaths around the Broad Street pump fitted **both** the bad-air theory and John Snow's water theory because nearby residents shared the same air. By contrast, a **nearby workhouse** and **nearby brewery** with their own water supplies escaped largely unharmed.

# Asked

Which further finding would be telling for the exact same reason as the workhouse and brewery?

# Solution

Match **both decisive features** that break the tie between air and water: **right beside the Broad Street pump** (sharing its air) yet **drinking a different water supply** with almost no cases.

> People living beside the Broad Street pump breathed the exact same air as victims, so escaping cholera while drinking spring water cleanly separates contaminated water from foul air.

## Separating air from water

Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> highlights these exceptions: <Highlight>A workhouse near the pump, with its own well, had few deaths among hundreds of inmates. Workers at a nearby brewery, who drank beer and water from the brewery's own supply, escaped almost entirely</Highlight>.

A **school beside the pump whose pupils drank spring water** and suffered almost no cases repeats the exact same control: same local air, different water, no outbreak.

## Why the other options fail

- **A street far from the pump** avoids both the pump's air and its water, so both theories predict few cases.
- **A family beside the pump drinking its water** shares both exposures, so both theories predict illness.
- **A district south of the river with more cases** does not separate air from water on its own.
- **A doctor near the pump falling ill** fits either theory because he shared the air and worked closely with sick patients.

# Answer

**A school beside the pump whose pupils drank only water carried from a distant spring had almost no cases**.

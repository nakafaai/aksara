Paragraph 2 claims that a curve damages **cooperation**: helping others becomes risky, so "study groups become rarer" in curved courses. If students in curved courses formed study groups **as often as** students elsewhere, the predicted effect would be missing, which weakens the claim directly.

- More stress before exams does not test whether students cooperate.
- Marking time concerns teachers' convenience, not cooperation.
- What employers look at relates to paragraph 3 on meaning, not paragraph 2.
- Some students liking curves says nothing about whether they still work together.

So the finding that most weakens paragraph 2 is that students in curved courses formed study groups as often as students elsewhere.

The author grants that supporters' concerns are "real" but argues that a strict curve "solves them at too high a price". Paragraph 4 gives the alternative: **set clear standards in advance and grade against them**, adjusting a standard openly when a test proves too hard. The author wants grades that measure students **against standards, not against classmates**.

- The author keeps grades and criticises only one way of assigning them.
- The author says curving solves the hard-test problem at too high a price, and offers a better way.
- The author warns that a curve can give A grades to students with serious gaps, not that more A grades are needed.
- Study groups are discussed as a casualty of curving, not as a replacement for grades.

So the author's position is that grades should measure students against clear standards, not against each other.

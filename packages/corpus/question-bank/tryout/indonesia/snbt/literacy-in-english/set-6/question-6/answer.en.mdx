# Given

**Text A** claims dropping entrance tests widened access <Highlight>without lowering the standard</Highlight> because no-score admits earned first-year grades "almost as high". **Text B** directly scrutinises that phrase.

# Asked

Which pair of points in Text B most directly challenges Text A's claim that the standard did not fall?

# Solution

Choose the **two facts in Text B** that specifically undermine "almost as high": the **numerical grade gap** (<InlineMath math="2.9" /> vs. <InlineMath math="3.1" />) and the **pre-selection context** showing the gap persisted even after full holistic review.

> Showing a zero-point-two GPA drop among students who had already passed every other admissions screen directly undercuts Text A's assurance that standards stayed intact.

## Direct evidence against Text A in Text B

Text B links both facts in one sharp sentence: <Highlight>Students admitted without scores averaged <InlineMath math="2.9" /> in their first year, against <InlineMath math="3.1" /> for those with scores, and that gap appeared among students the university had already chosen after weighing everything else it knew about them</Highlight>.

Together, those two facts prove the gap is both **measurable** and **unexplained by the rest of the application file**.

## Why the other options fail

- **More low-income admissions** is a welcome outcome in Text B, not a challenge to academic standards.
- **School-context research and coaching on essays** address future admissions design rather than proving current first-year standards slipped.
- **Keeping tests optional and tracking later years** are Text B's policy recommendations, not evidence refuting Text A's grade claim.
- **Grade gap paired with coaching on essays** mixes one relevant grade fact with an unrelated argument about alternative admissions criteria.

# Answer

**Students admitted without scores averaged <InlineMath math="2.9" /> against <InlineMath math="3.1" />, and both groups had already been chosen after the university weighed everything else it knew**.

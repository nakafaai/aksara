# Given

The **library table** lists loans across three formats in **<InlineMath math="2023" />** (<InlineMath math="180{,}000" /> print, <InlineMath math="9{,}000" /> e-books, <InlineMath math="11{,}000" /> audiobooks) and **<InlineMath math="2024" />** (<InlineMath math="162{,}000" /> print, <InlineMath math="27{,}000" /> e-books, <InlineMath math="13{,}200" /> audiobooks).

# Asked

By how much did total library loans change from <InlineMath math="2023" /> to <InlineMath math="2024" />, and what does this suggest about the headline "A reading revolution"?

# Solution

Sum **all three formats** for each year, compute the percentage rise in total loans, and compare the e-book gain (<InlineMath math="+18{,}000" />) against the print drop (<InlineMath math="-18{,}000" />).

> Total loans edged up by only one point one percent while e-books gained the exact eighteen thousand loans that print lost, showing borrowers mostly switched formats rather than reading more.

## Comparing annual totals across all formats

| Format | <InlineMath math="2023" /> | <InlineMath math="2024" /> | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Print books | <InlineMath math="180{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="162{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="-18{,}000" /> |
| E-books | <InlineMath math="9{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="27{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="+18{,}000" /> |
| Audiobooks | <InlineMath math="11{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="13{,}200" /> | <InlineMath math="+2{,}200" /> |
| Total loans | <InlineMath math="200{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="202{,}200" /> | <InlineMath math="+2{,}200" /> |

Computing the **percentage change in total loans**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Total change} &= 202{,}200 - 200{,}000 = +2{,}200 \text{ loans} \\ \text{Percentage change} &= \frac{2{,}200}{200{,}000} = 1.1\% \approx 1\% \end{aligned}" />

Because total borrowing barely grew while e-books offset print loan-for-loan, readers **mostly changed format** (<Highlight>about the same number of books, in different shapes</Highlight>).

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="200\%" />** measures e-books alone, not **total library loans**.
- **<InlineMath math="11\%" />** improperly compares the <InlineMath math="2024" /> total (<InlineMath math="202{,}200" />) against <InlineMath math="2023" /> print loans alone (<InlineMath math="180{,}000" />).
- **Fell by <InlineMath math="10\%" />** tracks print books alone, whereas total loans rose slightly.
- **<InlineMath math="1\%" />, drawing many new readers** misinterprets a tiny total rise paired with an exact <InlineMath math="18{,}000" /> shift from print to e-books.

# Answer

**They rose by about <InlineMath math="1\%" />, which suggests that readers mostly changed format rather than read more**.

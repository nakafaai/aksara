# Given

A modern town closes a suspect well during an outbreak and cases drop sharply the following week, yet **many residents had already left the area the week before the closure**.

# Asked

Based on the passage, which conclusion is best supported?

# Solution

Apply Snow's **exact Broad Street caution** from paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />: when residents flee beforehand, a drop after closing the water source cannot prove the closure caused the decline.

> Just as Broad Street deaths had already begun dropping while residents fled before the handle came off, a post-closure drop in the modern town cannot by itself prove the well caused the outbreak.

## Applying the Broad Street parallel

Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> explains why timing alone is inconclusive: <Highlight>Deaths had already fallen sharply before the handle came off, partly because many residents had fled</Highlight>.

Because residents in the modern town also left the week before the well closed, **cases may already have been falling due to people leaving**, so the drop alone does not prove the well was the cause.

## Why the other options fail

- **Closing the well ended the outbreak just like <InlineMath math="1854" />** repeats the exact popular myth the passage refutes.
- **Cases dropping within a week proves the well was the cause** confuses sequence with causation when an exodus happened first.
- **Only comparing two water companies could prove it** overstates the text: the workhouse, brewery, and widow were also telling evidence.
- **Because no case map was drawn** ignores that paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> calls Snow's map <Highlight>suggestive, too, but not decisive</Highlight>.

# Answer

**The fall alone does not show that the well was the cause, because cases may already have been falling as people left**.

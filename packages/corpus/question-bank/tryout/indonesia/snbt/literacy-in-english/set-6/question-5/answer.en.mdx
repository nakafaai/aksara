Paragraph 2 says fur seals sleep "mainly with one hemisphere at a time" **in the water**, but on land, "where they are safer", they usually sleep with both. The situation most like the first case is **resting in open water** away from the shore.

- A quiet beach is land, where they usually sleep with both hemispheres.
- Rocks beside the colony are on land as well.
- A protected enclosure is safe, the condition under which both hemispheres sleep.
- An empty beach is still land.

So a fur seal is most likely to sleep with one hemisphere at a time while resting in open water away from the shore.

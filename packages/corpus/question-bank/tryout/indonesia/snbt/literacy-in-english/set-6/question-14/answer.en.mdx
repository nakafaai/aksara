The report names a weakness in **how the areas were chosen**: Areas A and B applied, and their residents already cared about saving water. Households that want to save water may cut their use with or without meters. Choosing at random which similar areas receive meters spreads such differences evenly between the two groups, so a difference in results is more likely to come from **the meters themselves**.

- Adding meters halfway would mix two periods and blur the comparison.
- Whether residents liked the app does not measure water use.
- Reading ordinary meters less often changes nothing about how areas were chosen.
- Raising prices would add a second change, making the effect of the meters harder to judge.

So the most useful change is to choose at random which similar areas receive meters instead of using areas that applied.

The last paragraph sums up both stories: a new technology made local time impractical, **private companies agreed on a common standard**, and **governments followed years later**. In Britain, railways adopted London time from 1840 and the law came in 1880. In the United States, the railroads chose zones in 1883 and the law came in 1918.

- The order is reversed. Companies acted first, governments followed.
- Local time lasted for decades in some towns, as the Bristol clock shows.
- Britain adopted railway time in the 1840s, long before 1883.
- The passage describes what happened, not what companies always do.

So the pattern is that practical needs led companies to agree on a standard before the law did.

Two steps: read the clues, then compare them with what Mrs Varga **says**.

**The clues**. She praises a performance so bad the narrator stopped. She moves to where "she could see my hands". She wants the slow movement "louder, then louder again". She watches the metronome's "little arm" instead of listening to its tick. She doesn't move when "the telephone rang in the hall". Together they point to **hearing loss**.

**What she says**. She tells the narrator's mother "that she had taught me everything she could". Read beside the clues, there's a reason she leaves out: she can **no longer hear well enough to judge** the playing.

- Hearing loss is right, but nothing shows the narrator had outgrown her. The narrator still played the Schubert badly, and the old wrong note was still there.
- Fondness doesn't explain the chair, the volume, the metronome or the telephone.
- Losing interest doesn't fit either. She watches the narrator's hands closely and makes careful arrangements for her.
- Hearing loss is right, but this repeats her stated reason instead of adding to it.

The changes suggest she was losing her hearing, and that she sent the narrator on partly because she could no longer judge her playing, a reason she didn't give.

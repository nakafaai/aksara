# Given

After three years of sharp corrections, Mrs Varga suddenly **praises a botched Schubert piece**, moves her chair to **see the narrator's hands**, asks for the slow movement **louder and louder**, watches the **metronome's swinging arm**, and misses the **ringing hall telephone**, before sending the narrator to Mr Hollis.

# Asked

What do these spring changes suggest, and what do they add to the reason Mrs Varga gives for sending the narrator on?

# Solution

Connect the **five sensory clues** to diagnose Mrs Varga's **unspoken hearing loss**, then read her polite parting remark (<Highlight>she had taught me everything she could</Highlight>) in that new light.

> Moving to watch hands, demanding louder volume, tracking the metronome visually, and missing the telephone all point to failing hearing, revealing she sent the narrator to Mr Hollis because she could no longer judge her playing by ear.

## Combining the sensory clues with her parting reason

Every spring change replaces **hearing with sight or volume**: praising a halted piece, moving <Highlight>where she could see my hands</Highlight>, asking for the slow movement <Highlight>louder, then louder again</Highlight>, watching the metronome's arm, and ignoring the hall telephone.

Beside those clues, her claim of having taught everything she could quietly conceals the real reason: **she could no longer hear well enough to judge the narrator's playing**.

## Why the other options fail

- **Narrator had become too good for her** is contradicted by the botched Schubert and the lingering bar-twelve mistake.
- **Grown fond of the narrator** cannot explain watching the metronome arm or missing the telephone.
- **Losing interest in teaching** conflicts with her intense visual focus and careful conservatory referral.
- **Already told her everything she knew** merely repeats her stated excuse instead of adding what the clues reveal.

# Answer

**That she was losing her hearing, and that she sent the narrator on partly because she could no longer judge her playing, which she did not say**.

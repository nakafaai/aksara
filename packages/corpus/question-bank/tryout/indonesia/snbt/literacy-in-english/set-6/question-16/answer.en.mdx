Paragraph 2 explains the cause. Before railways, the ten-minute gap between towns did not matter because travel was slow. Trains made journeys **a few hours long** and needed **timetables**. If each station kept its own local time, "a single journey involved several different clocks", and passengers missed connections. A common time solved this.

- Daylight hours are not mentioned as a limit on trains.
- Local time already matched the sun. Railway time moved clocks away from it.
- The law came in 1880. In 1840 the Great Western Railway chose London time itself.
- No town is said to have refused trains. Some only kept local time for daily life.

So railways needed a common time because faster journeys meant that several local times in one timetable caused confusion.

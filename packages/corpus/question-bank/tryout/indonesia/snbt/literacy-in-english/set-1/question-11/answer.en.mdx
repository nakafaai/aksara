The question has two conditions: the group's **lateness rate must be lower** than the train group's 27 percent, and its **number of late students must be higher**. Rates alone cannot answer this, so convert each rate into a number of students.

The train group: <InlineMath math="0.27 \times 144 \approx 39" /> late students. Now check the groups whose rate is below 27 percent:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Walking: } 0.09 \times 264 &\approx 24 \\ \text{Bicycle: } 0.11 \times 216 &\approx 24 \\ \text{Car or motorbike: } 0.21 \times 204 &\approx 43 \end{aligned}" />

Only the car or motorbike group has more late students, about 43 against 39, while its rate of 21 percent is lower. It is a much larger group, which is why a lower rate still produces more late arrivals.

- Walking and cycling each give about 24 late students, fewer than 39.
- The bus group has more late students, but its rate of 34 percent is higher, so it fails the second condition.
- "None of the other groups" is wrong because the car or motorbike group meets both conditions.

So the answer is the car or motorbike group.

In September, the transport office of Northfield University asked 1,200 students how they usually travel to campus and whether they had arrived late for a first lecture at least once during the previous two weeks. The university must decide how to spend a limited transport budget: on a free shuttle bus from the main railway station or on secure bicycle parking. The table summarises the responses.

Bus users most often blamed unreliable timetables for their lateness, while train users mainly blamed the twenty-minute walk from the station to campus. When asked why more students do not cycle, respondents most often mentioned theft: 40 percent of students who do not currently cycle said they would consider it if they could lock their bicycles in a covered, monitored area.

The transport office notes two limits of the survey. Lateness was reported by the students themselves and may not match attendance records. The survey also did not ask how far students live from campus, so it cannot say how many non-cyclists could realistically ride.

Paragraph 2 answers this directly. It first rules out the obvious answer, "McLean's insight was not the box itself", because containers had been tried before. It then states the insight: "the box, rather than the goods inside it, should be the unit that ships, trucks, trains, and cranes were all designed to handle". The insight is about **making the box the shared unit** of the whole transport chain.

- Theft is mentioned in paragraph 1 as a problem of the old system, not as McLean's idea.
- The *Ideal X* was a converted tanker, but the passage does not compare its capacity with other ships.
- The ship sailed from Newark to Houston. Trucks were linked to ships, not used to replace them.
- Containerisation reduced dock work, the opposite of hiring more dockworkers.

So McLean's key insight was that the container should be the unit every part of the transport chain was designed to handle.

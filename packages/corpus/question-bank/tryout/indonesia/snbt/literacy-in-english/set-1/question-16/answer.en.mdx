# Given

Figures from the passage on the <InlineMath math="1714" /> Longitude Act:
- Earth turns **<InlineMath math="15" /> degrees every hour** (<InlineMath math="60" /> minutes).
- One degree of longitude spans about **<InlineMath math="111" /> kilometres** at the equator.
- The <InlineMath math="1714" /> Act required fixing longitude to **within half a degree**.

# Asked

About how large a clock error a timekeeper could carry while meeting the <InlineMath math="1714" /> Act's limit, and about how many kilometres east or west that corresponds to near the equator.

# Solution

Convert the **half-degree** limit into minutes using Earth's rotation rate of <InlineMath math="15" /> degrees per hour, then convert that same half-degree into kilometres at the equator.

> One degree of longitude equals four minutes of clock time and roughly one hundred eleven kilometres at the equator, so half a degree equals two minutes and about fifty-five kilometres.

## Converting half a degree into time and distance

The text states <Highlight>Earth turns through <InlineMath math="360" /> degrees in <InlineMath math="24" /> hours, or <InlineMath math="15" /> degrees every hour</Highlight>, <Highlight>spans about <InlineMath math="111" /> kilometres at the equator</Highlight>, and the prize limit <Highlight>within half a degree</Highlight>.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Time per degree: } \frac{60\text{ minutes}}{15} = 4\text{ minutes}, \qquad \text{Half a degree: } \frac{1}{2} \times 4 = 2\text{ minutes} \\
\text{Equatorial distance: } \frac{1}{2} \times 111\text{ km} = 55.5\text{ km} \approx 55\text{ km}
\end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options fail

- **About <InlineMath math="7.5" /> minutes, <InlineMath math="55" /> km** multiplies half a degree by <InlineMath math="15" /> instead of converting degrees into minutes.
- **About <InlineMath math="30" /> seconds, <InlineMath math="55" /> km** assumes Earth turns one degree per minute instead of every <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes.
- **About <InlineMath math="2" /> minutes, <InlineMath math="111" /> km** gets the time right but forgets to halve <InlineMath math="111" /> km.
- **About <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes, <InlineMath math="111" /> km** gives both figures for a full degree instead of half a degree.

# Answer

A timekeeper could carry an error of **About <InlineMath math="2" /> minutes, which is about <InlineMath math="55" /> km at the equator**.

# Given

**The longitude passage contrasts** the lunar-distance method with Harrison's H4 chronometer and states the Longitude Act's half-degree accuracy rule.

# Asked

Which statements about finding longitude at sea can be concluded from the passage.

# Solution

**Ask of each statement**: does the passage give you enough to be sure of it? Two of them need a small calculation.

> Imagine slices of an orange that are wide at the middle and skinny near the top: half a slice covers far more ground at the equator than near the poles.

## Checking each method and time to angle conversion

Statement <InlineMath math="1" /> misreads the lunar method. Measuring the moon's angle from a star and checking printed tables "could give the home-port time". The printed tables turn the moon's steady angle into home-port time, so this method didn't need a clock carried from port. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" /> runs against the passage. The Nautical Almanac "made lunar distances practical for ordinary navigators", and chronometers "stayed costly for decades". Nothing says navigators stopped using lunar distances. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" /> needs the Act's limit in time. Earth turns <InlineMath math="15" /> degrees an hour, so a degree takes <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes and half a degree takes <InlineMath math="2" /> minutes, or <InlineMath math="120" /> seconds. A twentieth of that is <InlineMath math="6" /> seconds, and H4 lost "only about <InlineMath math="5" /> seconds". Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" /> joins the limit to the size of a degree. The limit is the same angle everywhere, half a degree. But a degree spans about <InlineMath math="111" /> kilometres at the equator and **shrinks towards the poles**, so the same half degree covers more kilometres near the equator. Yes.

# Answer

Only the two statements grounded in H4's <InlineMath math="5" />-second drift and shrinking degree width toward the poles follow.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The lunar method still needed a clock kept from port | No |
| Most navigators stopped using lunar distances | No |
| H4's loss was under a twentieth of the allowed error | Yes |
| The limit allowed more kilometres near the equator | Yes |

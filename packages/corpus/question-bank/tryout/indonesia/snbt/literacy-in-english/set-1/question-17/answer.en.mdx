Paragraph 3 opens with the condition, "That idea worked only if the boxes were alike", and then describes the problem: companies used different lengths and corner fittings, so "a crane built for one firm's boxes could not lift another's". McLean's idea needs every crane, truck, and ship to handle every box. **Incompatible boxes** break that chain, and **common sizes and fittings** repair it.

- Painting and repair costs are never mentioned.
- The standards fixed "a small set of sizes", not one size. The twenty-foot box became widespread, but it was not the only size allowed.
- Theft belongs to the old system in paragraph 1 and is not linked to standards.
- Unions are named in paragraph 5 as one of the parties whose decisions mattered, not as controllers of port capacity.

So standards were necessary because, without common sizes and fittings, equipment built for one company's boxes could not handle another's.

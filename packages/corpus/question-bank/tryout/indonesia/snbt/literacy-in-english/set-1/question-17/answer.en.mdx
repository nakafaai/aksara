# Given

In paragraph <InlineMath math="5" />, the author defends the Board of Longitude's demand for a second trial, a full account, and copies by other craftsmen as **not unreasonable**, since a navy needed hundreds of reliable timekeepers.

# Asked

Which finding, if true, would **most weaken the author's argument** that the Board's demand was not unreasonable.

# Solution

The author's defence works only if the Board applied those strict hurdles as a **consistent, impartial standard**. Look for evidence of a **double standard** showing the Board excused a rival from those very requirements.

> Showing that the Board approved a rival's method after one voyage without explanations or copies exposes a double standard and undercuts the claim that Harrison's hurdles were simply reasonable.

## Testing each finding against the author defence

The author defends the Board on the ground that <Highlight>a method only one man could build was no method for a navy that needed hundreds</Highlight>.

| Finding | Effect on the author's argument |
| --- | --- |
| The Board earlier **accepted a rival's method after one voyage** with no account or copies | **Most weakens** by exposing an unfair double standard and lending weight to Harrison's charge of prejudice. |
| Kendall's copy took years and cost far more than lunar tables | **Supports** the author by proving scalability was a genuine concern for the navy. |
| H4 performed worse on its second trial than in <InlineMath math="1761" /> | **Supports** the Board's insistence on a second trial. |
| Cook's crew also took lunar sightings alongside Kendall's watch | Neutral, merely echoing paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> without touching fairness. |
| Harrison refused for years to explain how his clocks worked | **Supports** the reasonableness of demanding a full account. |

## Why the other options fail

- Three options actually **strengthen** the Board's caution, while Cook's lunar sightings say nothing about whether the Board treated Harrison fairly.

# Answer

The finding that most weakens the author's argument is **The Board had earlier accepted a rival's method after a single voyage, without asking for an account of how it worked or for copies**.

Look for the author's own statement of each point, and reject any statement that **adds a promise or a ban** the author never makes.

| Statement | Answer | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimums were meant to prevent congestion | Yes | Paragraph 1: planners feared drivers would circle the block, clogging streets. |
| No new parking would be built | No | Paragraph 4: developers "would still build spaces where buyers want them". |
| Formulas are borrowed from places with different habits | Yes | Paragraph 4: formulas are "often copied from suburbs with very different travel habits". |
| Every reforming city saw housing prices fall | No | Paragraph 5 reports fewer spaces and some projects going ahead, and warns that early results "do not prove" reform suits every place. Prices are not mentioned. |

The fourth statement is tempting because the author favours reform, but the passage reports only **early, limited results**.

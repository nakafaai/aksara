The evidence for more cycling is that 40 percent of non-cyclists **would consider** riding with secure parking. The office itself names the gap: the survey "did not ask how far students live from campus, so it cannot say how many non-cyclists could realistically ride". Interest only turns into riders if the distance is manageable, so the missing fact is **how many non-cyclists live within cycling distance**.

- Walking trip time describes a group that already lives close by and does not show whether non-cyclists can ride.
- The shuttle's cost concerns the other option, not whether bike parking would increase cycling.
- The number of bus routes does not tell us whether non-cyclists could ride.
- Train users' preference about a shuttle is again about the other option.

So the office would most need to know how many non-cycling students live close enough to ride to campus.

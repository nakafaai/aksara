# Given

**The passage compares mantis** shrimp colour receptors (<InlineMath math="12" /> types), human receptors (<InlineMath math="3" /> types), and honeybee discrimination results.

# Asked

Which statements about mantis shrimp vision can be concluded from the passage.

# Solution

**Check each statement on** its own. The question is whether the passage gives you enough to conclude it.

> Having more crayons in a box does not automatically mean you can tell two very close shades of blue apart.

## Checking each conclusion against the text

Statement <InlineMath math="1" /> **joins two paragraphs**. Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> says that under opponent coding "every extra channel should make the comparisons finer still". Paragraph <InlineMath math="1" /> gives the shrimp <InlineMath math="12" /> kinds of colour receptor against <InlineMath math="3" /> in people. Put together, that model predicts finer separation than a person manages. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" /> is the trap. Failing at gaps of <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="25" /> nanometres shows poor discrimination. Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> says such a score "does not show that the shrimp sees colour badly in every sense", and paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> says the number of colours an animal sees depends on what the brain does with the signals. Turning a discrimination limit into a count of colours goes too far. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" /> is a "what if". Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> says that because the colour receptors "sit in a narrow strip", the shrimp "would also need to sweep its eyes across a scene". Remove the narrow strip and that reason for scanning shrinks. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" /> uses paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />. Honeybees, "which have far fewer receptor types than the shrimp", did better. Fewer types beat more types, so the count alone doesn't decide how finely an animal separates colours. Yes.

# Answer

Separating close shades and seeing a wide colour range are two different things, so three statements hold and the second fails.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The opponent model predicted finer separation than a person's | Yes |
| A limit of <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="25" /> nanometres shows fewer colours | No |
| Receptors across the whole eye would make scanning matter less | Yes |
| Receptor count alone doesn't decide fine separation | Yes |

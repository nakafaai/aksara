A shuttle from the railway station helps only the people who arrive by train, so a good argument must connect **the train group's problem** to **what the shuttle changes**. Train users have the longest average trip (45 minutes) and a lateness rate of 27 percent, and they mainly blame the twenty-minute walk from the station. A shuttle replaces exactly that walk.

- Bus users are the largest group, but their problem is unreliable timetables. A shuttle from the railway station does nothing for city buses.
- The low lateness rate of walkers is a fact about a group the shuttle does not serve.
- The car and motorbike trip time does not involve the station.
- The 40 percent figure supports the bicycle option, the alternative to the shuttle.

So the best argument for the shuttle is that train users have long trips and high lateness caused by the walk the shuttle would replace.

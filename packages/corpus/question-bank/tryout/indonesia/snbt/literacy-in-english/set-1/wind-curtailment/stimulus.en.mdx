In 2025, the grid operator in the Northmoor region repeatedly ordered its <InlineMath math="4" /> wind farms to turn their turbines down even though the wind was blowing. This is called curtailment, and it happens for one of two reasons. Either the line that carries a farm's power towards the cities is already full, or demand across the whole region is so low, typically on windy nights, that the grid has no use for the extra power. The operator estimates the energy each farm could have produced from the wind speeds measured at its turbines. The curtailed energy is the part of that estimate that the farm was told not to generate, and the rest was delivered to the grid.

After the figures were published, a local campaign group pointed out that Brackenmoor had curtailed more energy than any other farm. It argued that the region should stop approving large wind farms, because "the bigger the farm, the more it wastes".

The operator rejected that reading. In its view, what matters is where a farm connects, and the North line, built decades ago to serve a coal plant, is often full on windy evenings. It plans to double the capacity of the North line by 2028. The operator added two cautions of its own. The North line was shut for repairs for <InlineMath math="3" /> weeks in February, normally one of the windiest months, so the North farms' figures for 2025 may be higher than in a typical year. And a bigger line will not help on nights when demand across the region is too low, because then there is nowhere for the power to go, whichever line carries it.

# Given

**The grid operator explains** curtailment by which transmission line each wind farm uses: the crowded North line or the freer South line, plus region-wide low demand.

# Asked

Which number in the wind-farm table is hardest to explain using the operator's line-based account alone.

# Solution

Start with what the operator's account predicts. The North line "is often full on windy evenings", so farms on the North line should lose a lot. Farms on the South line should lose little, unless demand is low, and low demand hits **every farm in the region**, whichever line it uses.

> If two cars drive on the exact same empty highway yet one gets stuck in heavy traffic just like cars on the jammed road, the road alone cannot explain it.

## Comparing each farm curtailed share by transmission line

**Now work out each** farm's share:

| Farm | Line | Share curtailed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fellside | North | <InlineMath math="50 \div 200 = 25\%" /> |
| Brackenmoor | North | <InlineMath math="160 \div 800 = 20\%" /> |
| Saltmarsh | South | <InlineMath math="40 \div 800 = 5\%" /> |
| Gullrock | South | <InlineMath math="50 \div 200 = 25\%" /> |

Gullrock is on the South line, like Saltmarsh, yet it lost <InlineMath math="25\%" />, exactly as much as Fellside on the crowded North line. The line can't explain that, and low demand can't either, because Saltmarsh faced the same nights and lost only <InlineMath math="5\%" />.

The other figures fit the account:

- Fellside losing a quarter fits a farm on the full North line.
- Brackenmoor's large total fits a big farm on the North line.
- North losing a larger share than South is just what the account predicts.
- Saltmarsh's small loss fits a farm on the freer South line.

# Answer

**Gullrock losing the same share as Fellside** (<InlineMath math="25\%" /> on the South line) is the figure the operator's account cannot explain alone.

The figure that the operator's account can't explain on its own is Gullrock losing the same share as Fellside.

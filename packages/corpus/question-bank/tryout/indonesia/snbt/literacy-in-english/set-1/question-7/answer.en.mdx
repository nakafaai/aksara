# Given

Text <InlineMath math="1" /> **argues for dropping homework** across all ages after Westbrook School's pilot, while Text <InlineMath math="2" /> limits that approach to the youngest pupils.

# Asked

Which views are shared by both writers.

# Solution

**Check each view against** both writers. A view gets Yes only if Text <InlineMath math="1" /> and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> both hold it.

> Two teachers can care deeply about the exact same struggling child yet still disagree on whether extra practice helps or hurts.

## Comparing what both writers accept

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />. Text <InlineMath math="1" /> praises a pilot in which Years <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="6" /> "received no homework except reading" and scored "no lower". Text <InlineMath math="2" /> says "for the youngest pupils I would go as far as the pilot did" and calls them the group "in which little effect was expected". Both hold it. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />. Only Text <InlineMath math="2" /> describes "a weak link" in primary school and "a clearly stronger one" for older pupils. Text <InlineMath math="1" /> treats every age alike and wants homework dropped "at every age". That view is one-sided. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />. Text <InlineMath math="1" /> says homework "falls hardest on those least able to pay", such as children without a quiet room. Text <InlineMath math="2" /> says scrapping practice "could hurt most those who have the least help at home". Both writers put **the same group of pupils** at the centre. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />. This is where they split. Text <InlineMath math="1" /> thinks dropping homework helps those pupils. Text <InlineMath math="2" /> thinks that, for older pupils, dropping it could hurt them most. Same concern, **opposite conclusions**. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="5" />. Text <InlineMath math="1" /> calls the scores the most telling result and says they "should settle the debate". Text <InlineMath math="2" /> says they "tell us less than they seem to", because **a small loss could hide** in a comparison with different children on tests that count for nothing. Text <InlineMath math="2" /> backs the change for the youngest on other grounds, not on these scores. No.

# Answer

**Both writers agree on young pupils and on caring about children with less help at home, while splitting on older ages and remedies**.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Pilot-age pupils lose little without set homework | Yes |
| Homework's link to achievement differs by age | No |
| Pupils with little support should weigh heavily | Yes |
| Removing homework helps pupils with little support most | No |
| The scores are good evidence of no harm | No |

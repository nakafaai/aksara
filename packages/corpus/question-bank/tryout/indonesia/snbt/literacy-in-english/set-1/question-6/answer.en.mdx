# Given

Both texts discuss the end-of-term reading and mathematics scores from Westbrook School's **single-term no-homework pilot** for Years 3 to 6, which were no lower than the previous year's scores.

# Asked

Which statement accurately describes how each writer uses the pilot's end-of-term scores.

# Solution

Check both halves of each option: Text <InlineMath math="1" /> uses the scores to **settle the debate for every age**, whereas Text <InlineMath math="2" /> warns the scores **tell less than they seem** while still backing the primary-age change on separate research grounds.

> Both writers support dropping homework for the youngest pupils, yet Text <InlineMath math="2" /> rests that support on broader primary-school studies rather than on the single-term pilot scores.

## Comparing how both writers use the scores

| Writer | Use of pilot scores | Textual evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text <InlineMath math="1" /> | Treats them as decisive for every age | Declares <Highlight>That last result should settle the debate</Highlight> and urges schools to <Highlight>drop homework at every age</Highlight>. |
| Text <InlineMath math="2" /> | Says they tell less than they seem, yet backs primary change on other grounds | Warns the scores <Highlight>tell us less than they seem to</Highlight>, while backing young pupils because studies find <Highlight>only a weak link with achievement in primary school</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Proof that homework harms attainment** overstates Text <InlineMath math="1" />, which says removing homework costs nothing in attainment.
- **Decisive only for Years 3 to 6** misses Text <InlineMath math="1" />'s call to abolish homework at every age, and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> never discusses bedtime figures.
- **Equal weight with bedtime figures** ignores that Text <InlineMath math="1" /> elevates the scores with *most tellingly*, and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> questions the scores rather than bedtimes.
- **Rejects them outright as showing nothing** exaggerates Text <InlineMath math="2" />'s measured claim that they show less than they seem.

# Answer

The correct statement is **Text 1 treats them as settling the debate for every age, while Text 2 says they tell less than they seem, though it still backs the change for the youngest on other grounds**.

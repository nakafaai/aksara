# Given

In paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />, the Queensland team proposes that mantis shrimp process colour like a row of **tripwires**, reading which channel fired **without comparing one receptor signal against another**.

# Asked

Which finding, if true, would most directly **challenge the team's proposed colour-processing mechanism** rather than merely questioning how the shrimp were tested.

# Solution

Set aside options that support the team or critique the tank conditions, and find direct evidence that the shrimp's brain **actively compares signals across neighbouring receptor types**.

> Brain cells firing strongest to differences between neighbouring receptors are performing opponent coding, directly contradicting the claim that the shrimp skips signal comparisons.

## Testing each finding against the proposed mechanism

The team's core hypothesis is that the shrimp <Highlight>largely skips the comparing</Highlight> and recognises hues <Highlight>without weighing one signal against another</Highlight>.

| Finding | Effect on the team's proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Poor separation on mixed real-reef colours too | **Supports** the proposal by removing a test-setup criticism from paragraph <InlineMath math="5" />. |
| Tank lights were dimmer than shallow-reef daylight | Only **casts doubt on test conditions**, which the question explicitly excludes. |
| A second species failed at the same small gaps | **Supports** the proposal by generalizing the result. |
| Brain cells respond most strongly to **differences between neighbouring receptor types** | **Directly challenges the mechanism** by showing opponent comparison inside the brain. |
| Faster colour target identification than reef fish | **Supports** the speed-versus-precision trade-off proposed in paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />. |

## Why the other options fail

- Three options **strengthen** the team's findings or speed trade-off rather than challenging them.
- **Dimmer tank lights** questions experimental setup rather than the proposed neural mechanism.

# Answer

The finding that most directly challenges the proposed mechanism is **Cells in the shrimp's brain respond most strongly to the difference between signals from neighbouring receptor types**.

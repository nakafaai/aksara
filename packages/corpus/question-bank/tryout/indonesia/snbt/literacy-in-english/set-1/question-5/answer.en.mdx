The engineers argue that flexible machines "may work best when control is spread across the body rather than concentrated in a single processor". A finding that strengthens this must compare **distributed control with central control** in flexible machines and show the distributed version doing better.

The robot comparison does exactly that: same robot, two control designs, and the spread-out design is faster and needs less computing power.

- Jar-opening speed concerns octopus learning, which the passage links to the central brain, not to spread-out control in machines.
- Prosthetic arms with more joints are rigid human devices, and the finding says nothing about where control is located.
- How long severed arms keep responding does not test whether distributed control works better in robots.
- A cheaper central design would, if anything, weaken the engineers' case.

So the finding that most strengthens the argument is the soft robot with distributed controllers outperforming its centrally controlled twin.

# Given

Two competing explanations account for the mantis shrimp's poor discrimination scores:
- **The duller explanation** (paragraph <InlineMath math="5" />): an animal punching a glowing fibre for a snack may simply **stop trying** when the task gets hard.
- **The team's proposal** (paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />): the shrimp's visual system genuinely **skips comparing signals** and cannot separate narrow wavelength gaps.

# Asked

Which follow-up study would best decide between the motivation explanation and the team's visual-processing proposal.

# Solution

Pick a design where the two hypotheses predict **opposite outcomes**: boost natural hunting motivation with moving, prey-like targets, then check whether the shrimp suddenly separates much narrower colour gaps.

> Replacing dull glowing fibres with moving prey-like targets tests effort directly: sharper separation blames low motivation, whereas continued failure backs the tripwire mechanism.

## Comparing the five follow up studies

Paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> notes the shrimp <Highlight>may simply stop trying once the task gets hard</Highlight>, while paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> notes it hunts as <Highlight>a predator on a crowded reef</Highlight>.

| Follow-up study | Separates the two explanations? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Count receptor types in other mantis shrimp species | No | Paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> stresses that receptor counts cannot reveal how the brain processes signals. |
| Test the same glowing fibres across more target colours | No | Keeps the exact same artificial snack task, leaving the low-effort problem untouched. |
| Run the same fibre task on honeybees and butterflies | No | Tells nothing about whether mantis shrimp gave up trying. |
| Use **moving, prey-like targets** and test much smaller colour gaps | **Yes** | Engages real predatory drive so the two explanations make opposite predictions on fine gaps. |
| Time strikes when the two colours are very different | No | Tests reaction speed on easy contrasts, never testing fine discrimination limits. |

## Why the other options fail

- None of the other four designs removes the **artificial snack-task boredom** while re-testing fine colour separation.

# Answer

The best follow-up study is to **Use moving, prey-like targets in the two colours and see whether much smaller gaps are then separated**.

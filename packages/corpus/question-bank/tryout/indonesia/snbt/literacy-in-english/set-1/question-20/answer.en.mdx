# Given

**The passage recounts how** the Board of Longitude tested Harrison's chronometer, why sea clocks failed, and how ships paired chronometers with lunar sightings.

# Asked

Which claims about the longitude prize and navigation tools strictly follow from the passage.

# Solution

**Check each statement against** the exact words of the passage, and watch for words like "mainly" and "proves".

> Watch out for loud words like mainly or proves because a passage that lists two causes never picks a winner unless it says so directly.

## Checking every claim against exact wording

Statement <InlineMath math="1" />: the Board wanted copies because "a method only one man could build was no method for a navy that needed hundreds". The request is tied to numbers. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="2" />: the passage gives **two** troubles for clocks at sea, the "rolling deck" and temperature changes in "the metal parts and the oil". It never says which one mattered more, so "mainly" can't be concluded. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="3" />: the Act "did not say how this should be done". It offered the reward for "a method" that met the half-degree limit, and lunar distances were exactly such a method being worked on by astronomers. Yes.

Statement <InlineMath math="4" />: Harrison "pointed out" that Maskelyne sat on the Board, but that's Harrison's charge. The author calls the Board's demand "not unreasonable". A seat on the Board doesn't prove bias. No.

Statement <InlineMath math="5" />: many ships took "lunar sightings to check whether their chronometers had drifted". So a lunar sighting still had a use on a ship with a chronometer. Yes.

# Answer

Statements <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="3" />, and <InlineMath math="5" /> match the text, while <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> stretch beyond the evidence.

| Statement | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Copies were tied to how many timekeepers a navy needed | Yes |
| Pendulum clocks failed mainly because of temperature | No |
| A moon-based method could have won the prize | Yes |
| Maskelyne's seat proves bias | No |
| Lunar sightings still helped ships with chronometers | Yes |

In the passage, standardisation means **agreeing on shared dimensions and fittings** so that equipment from different firms works together: any crane can lift any box. The matching situation must show separate producers adopting **one common interface** that makes their products interchangeable. A single charging connector does exactly that: chargers and phones from different makers fit each other.

- A larger factory increases one firm's capacity and involves no shared standard.
- Hiring more workers is simply adding labour, closer to the old dock system than to containers.
- Extra buses add capacity on one route without any common interface between producers.
- Translating a novel adapts content for new readers. Nothing becomes interchangeable across different makers.

So the most similar situation is phone makers agreeing on one charging connector.

An assumption is a missing link the argument needs. A quick test: **deny it**. If the argument falls apart once the assumption is false, the step depends on it.

Text 1's chain: the pilot pupils had no homework for "a single term", their scores were "no lower than those of the same year groups the year before", so removing homework "costs nothing", so schools should drop it "at every age".

Statement 1. Deny it: older pupils lose more than the pilot pupils did. Then the pilot can't support "every age". Yes.

Statement 2. Parents finishing worksheets is only a picture of stressful evenings. Deny it, and the chain from the scores to the conclusion still stands. No.

Statement 3. Deny it: one term is too short for a cost to show. Then "no lower" after one term doesn't mean "costs nothing". Yes.

Statement 4. Text 1 wants homework gone whatever the current amount, so the step doesn't need this. No.

Statement 5. Deny it: last year's groups differ in some way that matters. Then "no lower than last year" stops showing that nothing was lost. Yes.

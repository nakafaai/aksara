An assumption is a missing link that the argument needs. Look for the gap between Text 1's evidence and its conclusion.

The evidence comes from one group only: the pilot "in which Years 3 to 6 received no homework except reading". The conclusion covers everyone: schools should "drop homework at every age". To get from the first to the second, Text 1 has to take for granted that **older pupils would respond the same way** as the pilot pupils did. Test it by denying it. If homework mattered more for older pupils, as Text 2 suggests, the call to drop it at every age would collapse. An assumption the argument can't survive without is the one it depends on.

- Text 1 mentions parents finishing worksheets as a picture of stressful evenings, but the argument never needs a claim about how much children learn from that help.
- How long older pupils spend now doesn't matter to the step, since Text 1 wants homework gone whatever the current amount.
- Text 1 also counts "giving children back their evenings", so it doesn't treat scores as the only outcome that matters.
- Text 1 says homework falls hardest on children without a quiet room, but its step needs no pilot scores for that group.

The step depends most on assuming that what happened to pupils in Years 3 to 6 would also happen to older pupils.

# Given

The board devises a bus-order swap (sending the middle-school round first) that moves high schools from 8:00 to 8:30 **without buying extra buses** and **without moving primary schools**.

# Asked

Under this new plan, which of Text B's concerns would still stand?

# Solution

Cross off the **logistics and budget concerns solved by the new plan**, then check what **remains unchanged** in Text B's critique.

> Solving bus costs and primary-school start times removes Text B's operational objections, yet high schools still shift by only thirty minutes so the sleep gain may still fall well short of thirty-four minutes.

## What remains after removing bus and primary school costs

Text B raises two separate worries: operational cost (primary schools at 9:30 or <InlineMath math="2.4" /> million dollars in extra buses) and **modest benefit** because <Highlight>Our high schools already start at 8:00, so moving to 8:30 would delay them by half an hour, not by almost an hour</Highlight>.

Because the swap still yields only a **half-hour delay**, the warning not to expect the other city's <InlineMath math="34" /> minutes still holds completely.

## Why the other options fail

- **Catching the bus at the old time** invents an obstacle: sending middle schools first simply moves the high-school bus round later.
- **Middle schools starting at 9:30** contradicts the prompt (middle schools run **first**) and invents a concern Text B never raised.
- **Delayed by almost an hour** reverses Text B's explicit point that 8:00 to 8:30 is only half an hour.
- **Extra buses costing <InlineMath math="2.4" /> million dollars** is eliminated because the prompt specifies no buses are bought.

# Answer

**That the gain in sleep may be smaller than <InlineMath math="34" /> minutes, because the high schools would start only half an hour later**.

**Text A**

Around puberty, the body clock shifts later. Most teenagers do not feel sleepy until about 11 p.m., and telling them to go to bed earlier changes little. A school that starts at 7:30 a.m. therefore leaves them short of sleep night after night, and a tired student learns less, drives less safely and falls ill more often. The evidence that later starts help is now strong. When one city moved its high schools from 7:50 to 8:45, students wore wrist monitors before and after the change. Their sleep rose by a median of <InlineMath math="34" /> minutes a night, and fewer of them were late or absent for the first lesson. Every high school should start at 8:30 or later.

**Text B**

I don't dispute the biology, and I would like our high schools to start later. But our district is not that city. Our high schools already start at 8:00, so moving to 8:30 would delay them by half an hour, not by almost an hour. Our buses also run in three rounds: high schools first, then middle schools, then primary schools. If the high schools move later, either the primary schools start at 9:30, which many working parents cannot manage, or we buy more buses, which our transport office puts at <InlineMath math="2.4" /> million dollars a year. I support a later start, but the board should ask what each option would cost and what it would buy, and it should not simply promise our students another city's <InlineMath math="34" /> minutes.

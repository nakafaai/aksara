A rate per 1,000 cyclists divides the injuries **by the matching number of cyclists**, then multiplies by 1,000. After the lanes opened, Main Street had 14 injuries and 1,600 daily cyclists.

<BlockMath math="\frac{14}{1{,}600} \times 1{,}000 = 8.75" />

- 2 is the rise in injuries from 12 to 14, not a rate.
- 14 is the raw number of injuries.
- 15 is the rate before the lanes: <InlineMath math="12 \div 800 \times 1{,}000" />.
- 17.5 divides the new injuries by the old number of cyclists, <InlineMath math="14 \div 800 \times 1{,}000" />, mixing two periods.

So Main Street recorded 8.75 injuries per 1,000 daily cyclists after the lanes opened.

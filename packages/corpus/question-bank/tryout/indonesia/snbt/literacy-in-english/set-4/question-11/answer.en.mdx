# Given

In the liver-screening table, **<InlineMath math="585" /> adults tested positive** last year: <InlineMath math="90" /> actually had the condition and <InlineMath math="495" /> did not.

# Asked

Among adults who tested positive, about what share truly had the condition, and what does that mean?

# Solution

Use the **positive row total (<InlineMath math="585" />)** as the denominator rather than the <InlineMath math="100" /> adults with the condition.

> Because only ninety of the five hundred and eighty-five positive tests came from people with the condition, roughly fifteen percent were true cases and about eighty-five percent were false alarms.

## Computing the true positive share

| Positive-test breakdown | Count | Share of positives |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Has the condition (true positive) | <InlineMath math="90" /> | <InlineMath math="\approx 15\%" /> |
| Does not have it (false alarm) | <InlineMath math="495" /> | <InlineMath math="\approx 85\%" /> |
| Total positive results | <InlineMath math="585" /> | <InlineMath math="100\%" /> |

Dividing **true positives** by **all positive results**:

<BlockMath math="\frac{\text{True positives}}{\text{All positives}} = \frac{90}{585} \approx 15.4\% \approx 15\%" />

With only about <InlineMath math="15\%" /> actually having the condition, **most positive results were false alarms**.

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="90\%" />** (<InlineMath math="\frac{90}{100}" />) is the share of ill adults who test positive, **flipping the denominator**.
- **<InlineMath math="95\%" />** is the maker's overall accuracy across all <InlineMath math="10{,}000" /> adults, not among positive tests.
- **<InlineMath math="85\%" />** is the false-alarm share (<InlineMath math="\frac{495}{585}" />), inverted into the wrong conclusion.
- **<InlineMath math="1\%" />** is baseline prevalence before testing: testing positive jumps risk fifteen-fold to <InlineMath math="15\%" />.

# Answer

**About <InlineMath math="15\%" />, so most positive results were false alarms**.

Each statement needs its own sentence of the passage, and two of them tempt you to read more into the commission than it found.

Statement 1. The commission "found no trickery", which means no deliberate signals. That is not the same as concluding that Hans could do sums, and the passage never says it did. No.

Statement 2. Hans "answered even when strangers asked the questions", and Pfungst found he was "nearly always right" whenever the questioner knew the answer and stood in view. Yes.

Statement 3. "None of the questioners knew they were doing it", and the commission found that von Osten gave "no signal they could detect". The cue was **unintended**. No.

Statement 4. When blinkers "hid the questioner from view", Hans's success "fell to the level of guessing". A knower out of sight gives Hans no cue to read. Yes.

Statement 5. Pfungst "made the same movements himself even when he tried not to". No.

So statements 2 and 4 are Yes, and statements 1, 3 and 5 are No.

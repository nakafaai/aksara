If cycling grew **across the whole city**, including streets that received no lanes, then something other than the lanes was also pushing numbers up. Part of the rise on the three streets would probably have happened anyway. The report's caution that cycling rose about 25 percent citywide "including on streets where no lanes were built" is exactly that evidence.

- The barriers describe how the lanes were built, not why cycling rose.
- The council member's claim concerns danger, not the cause of the rise.
- Comparing Station Avenue with Main Street says nothing about whether lanes caused growth.
- The analyst's point concerns how to measure risk, not the cause of more cycling.

So the citywide rise of about 25 percent, even on streets without lanes, suggests the lanes were not the only cause.

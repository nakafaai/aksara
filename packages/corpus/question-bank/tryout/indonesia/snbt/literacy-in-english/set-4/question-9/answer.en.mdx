# Given

Five statements describe **claims, time intervals, bus trade-offs, and statistical wording** across Text A and Text B.

# Asked

Which statements about the two texts are accurate? Select all that apply.

# Solution

Check each claim down to **its exact statistic and condition**, especially catching the difference between **median** and **average** in option <InlineMath math="5" />.

> Text B accepts sleep biology while questioning local fit, Text A's city shifted by fifty-five minutes, keeping primary schedules forces buying extra buses, and thirty-four minutes is a median rather than an average.

## Verifying all five statements

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence from Text A and Text B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text B accepts teen sleep biology but questions local fit | **True** | Text B states <Highlight>I don't dispute the biology</Highlight> followed by <Highlight>our district is not that city</Highlight>. |
| Text B argues a later start does not improve sleep | **False** | Text B supports a later start and only questions the size of the gain here. |
| Text A's evidence comes from a <InlineMath math="55" />-minute delay | **True** | Shifting from 7:50 to 8:45 is exactly <InlineMath math="55" /> minutes. |
| Keeping primary times while delaying high schools requires buying buses | **True** | Text B presents an either-or choice: <Highlight>either the primary schools start at 9:30 ... or we buy more buses</Highlight>. |
| <InlineMath math="34" /> minutes is the average gain across all students | **False** | Text A reports <Highlight>a median of <InlineMath math="34" /> minutes a night</Highlight>, not the average (mean). |

# Answer

- **Text B accepts Text A's account of teenagers' sleep but questions whether Text A's evidence fits the district**.
- **Text A's evidence comes from a change that made school start <InlineMath math="55" /> minutes later**.
- **If the district moved its high schools later but kept its primary schools at their present time, Text B says it would have to buy more buses**.

# Given

Between arriving at Gran's house, driving home on the motorway, and visiting again on Sunday, the narrator undergoes a **sharp shift in how she interprets the phone call**.

# Asked

Which statement best describes how the narrator's understanding of the phone call changes?

# Solution

Follow the **three-stage arc** from paragraphs <InlineMath math="7" /> to <InlineMath math="9" />: initial anger at feeling tricked, sudden motorway insight, and quiet unspoken help on Sunday.

> The narrator moves from resentment over an apparent loyalty test to recognising a proud cover for memory difficulty, answering it with practical help kept completely unspoken.

## Tracing the three stages of understanding

- **Stage one (feeling tricked)**: Hearing Gran <Highlight>had simply wanted to see whether I would come</Highlight>, the narrator admits <Highlight>I was angry for most of the drive home</Highlight>.
- **Stage two (seeing the cover)**: On the motorway she realises the five-flowerpot clue proves Gran had genuinely struggled to find the key.
- **Stage three (helping silently)**: On Sunday she moves the key to the kitchen drawer, tells Gran plainly, and honours her pride (<Highlight>neither of us mentioned it again</Highlight>).

## Why the other options fail

- **Locked herself out on purpose** misstates both the cause (an accidental lockout) and the narrator's insight (about failing to find the key).
- **Deciding Gran cannot live alone** invents a drastic decision never hinted at in the story.
- **Worrying about memory then realising she remembered** reverses the narrative arc completely.
- **Seeing Gran's memory is perfect and the test was a joke** contradicts the five-flowerpot realisation and the pencil note inside the cupboard door.

# Answer

**From feeling tricked by Gran's test to seeing that the test covered Gran's difficulty, which the narrator then helps with without saying so**.

# Given

A local radio host claims that anyone who tests positive <Highlight>almost certainly</Highlight> has the liver condition, even though the table shows only <InlineMath math="90" /> of <InlineMath math="585" /> positives (<InlineMath math="\approx 15\%" />) actually have it.

# Asked

Which statement best explains why the radio host's claim is wrong?

# Solution

Combine the **two base-rate forces** driving the positive row: a **rare condition** (<InlineMath math="100" /> affected) and a **huge healthy pool** (<InlineMath math="9{,}900" /> unaffected) exposed to a <InlineMath math="5\%" /> false-alarm rate.

> Even a modest five percent false-alarm rate applied to ninety-nine hundred healthy adults generates four hundred and ninety-five false positives, overwhelming the ninety true positives by more than five to one.

## Why false alarms overwhelm true positives

Because only <InlineMath math="1" /> in <InlineMath math="100" /> adults has the condition, the healthy group (<InlineMath math="9{,}900" />) is <InlineMath math="99" /> times larger than the affected group (<InlineMath math="100" />). Flagging <InlineMath math="5\%" /> of <InlineMath math="9{,}900" /> creates **<InlineMath math="495" /> false alarms**, far exceeding the **<InlineMath math="90" /> true positives** (<InlineMath math="90\%" /> of <InlineMath math="100" />).

## Why the other options fail

- **Missing <InlineMath math="10" /> of <InlineMath math="100" /> ill people** concerns false negatives, not why positive tests are mostly false alarms.
- **The <InlineMath math="95\%" /> figure is false** is incorrect: overall accuracy is <InlineMath math="9{,}495" /> of <InlineMath math="10{,}000" /> (<InlineMath math="\approx 95\%" />).
- **Most adults testing negative still have it** contradicts the table showing only <InlineMath math="10" /> of <InlineMath math="9{,}415" /> negatives have it.
- **Only <InlineMath math="5\%" /> of positives are false** swaps denominators: <InlineMath math="5\%" /> is the error rate among healthy adults, whereas <InlineMath math="\approx 85\%" /> of positive tests are false.

# Answer

**The condition is rare, so even a small false-alarm rate among the many healthy adults produces more positives than the few adults with the condition do**.

# Given

Oskar Pfungst wants to separate **two distinct explanations**: whether Clever Hans needs to **see the questioner** or only needs **the questioner to know the answer**.

# Asked

Which pair of test conditions best separates these two possibilities?

# Solution

To isolate two variables, build two trials that each **keep one factor present while removing the other**, matching Pfungst's actual design in paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />.

> Testing a knowledgeable questioner hidden from view alongside an uninformed questioner standing in full view keeps each factor alone and cleanly proves Hans needed both.

## Isolating sight and knowledge

Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> reports Pfungst's exact split: <Highlight>When the questioner did not know the answer, or when Hans wore blinkers that hid the questioner from view, his success fell to the level of guessing</Highlight>.

- **Knows the answer, hidden from view** tests whether **knowledge alone** works without sight.
- **Does not know the answer, in full view** tests whether **sight alone** works without knowledge.

## Why the other options fail

- **Asking harder sums in full view** keeps both sight and knowledge present, changing only item difficulty.
- **Uninformed questioner in view and hidden** removes knowledge from both trials, so knowledge is never tested.
- **Von Osten versus a knowledgeable stranger in view** keeps both sight and knowledge active and only varies who asks.
- **Same knowledgeable questioner on another day** changes only the calendar date.

# Answer

**A questioner who knows the answer stands hidden from Hans, and a questioner who does not know it stands in full view**.

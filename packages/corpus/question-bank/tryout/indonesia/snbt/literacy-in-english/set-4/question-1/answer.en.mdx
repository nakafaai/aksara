The textbook account in paragraph 1 makes two claims: take the otters away and the forest becomes a barren, then "bring the otters back, and the forest returns." You need the author's verdict on each claim, so you have to read the whole passage, not just the first paragraph.

Paragraph 1 only warns you that "the two kinds" of evidence "are not equally strong." It doesn't say which half is stronger, so you have to work that out from the later paragraphs.

- The loss of otters is supported. In paragraph 3, otters vanished from the same reefs and kelp thinned within a few years.
- The return of otters is doubtful. In paragraph 4, Monterey Bay's otters "did little to clear the barrens", and paragraph 5 says returning otters to a barren coast "may achieve little" without extra help such as removing urchins by hand.

So the author accepts the loss claim but doubts the return claim **once barrens are established**.

Here's why the other options fail:

- "Both of its claims hold" gets the early evidence right, since paragraph 2 calls the island survey only "suggestive", but it misses the author's doubt about the second claim.
- "It should be set aside" mixes up two questions. The debate is about what removed the otters, and paragraph 3 says the cascade "does not depend on who removed them."
- The option that calls the return claim well supported flips the two halves around.
- The option about heatwaves and disease takes one bay as the whole story. Paragraph 4 says those events let urchins multiply in Monterey Bay, not that they cause most kelp loss everywhere.

The author's judgement is that the account of losing otters holds up, while the promise that returning otters restore the forest is doubtful once barrens have formed.

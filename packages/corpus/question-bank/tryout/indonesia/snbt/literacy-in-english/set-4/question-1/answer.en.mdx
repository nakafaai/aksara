To separate two possible causes, each condition has to **keep one and remove the other**.

- "Knows but hidden" keeps the knowledge and removes the sight. If knowledge were enough, Hans would still be right.
- "Doesn't know but in view" keeps the sight and removes the knowledge. If sight were enough, Hans would still be right.

The passage reports that Hans failed in both cases: "When the questioner did not know the answer, or when Hans wore blinkers that hid the questioner from view, his success fell to the level of guessing." That pair is exactly what showed he needed **both**.

- Harder sums change the question, not who knows or who can be seen. Knowledge and sight stay together.
- Both conditions remove the knowledge. With no knower in either, the pair can't test whether knowledge alone is enough.
- Swapping von Osten for a stranger who knows tests **who** asks, which the commission already did. Sight and knowledge stay together.
- Testing on another day changes the date and nothing else.

The pair that separates the two possibilities is a knowing questioner hidden from Hans and an ignorant questioner in full view.

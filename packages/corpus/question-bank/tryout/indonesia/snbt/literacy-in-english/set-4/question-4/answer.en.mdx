# Given

Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" /> defines the **Clever Hans effect** as whenever <Highlight>an animal, a child or a machine appears to show an ability but is in fact responding to unintended cues from the people or the setting around it</Highlight>.

# Asked

Which of the five study designs is most vulnerable to a Clever Hans effect?

# Solution

Look for the single design that combines **both necessary conditions**: an examiner who **knows the expected group** and **direct live interaction** where subtle cues can steer the child or score.

> Only a knowledgeable examiner conducting a face-to-face spoken test with on-the-spot judgement exposes the child to unintended bodily cues and expectation-driven scoring.

## Why the live informed examiner fits

The examiner who knows which children received the new method, administers a **spoken test face to face**, and decides on the spot whether answers suffice mirrors Hans's knowledgeable questioners standing in plain sight. Small nods, pauses, or relaxed posture can leak unconsciously.

## Why the other options fail

- **Children taking a computer-marked written test** removes any knowledgeable observer giving live cues (children's own expectations are a separate placebo-style effect).
- **Computer test with researcher behind a one-way mirror** hides the knower like blinkers, blocking all cues.
- **Uninformed stranger giving a spoken test** applies Pfungst's double-blind remedy because the questioner holds no group expectation.
- **Knowledgeable teacher who leaves the room** prevents cues during the test, just like removing the knower from view.

# Answer

**An examiner who knows which children were taught with a new method gives each child a spoken test and decides on the spot whether each answer is good enough**.

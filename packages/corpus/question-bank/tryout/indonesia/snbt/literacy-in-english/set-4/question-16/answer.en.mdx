Put the narrator's two clues together. "She had not asked me where the key was. She had asked me which flowerpot." And "she had walked past all five on her way round the house before she rang." Gran had already looked through the flowerpots and **couldn't find the key**. She needed "The geranium. The dead one." So her claim that "She had found it at once" and only "wanted to see whether I would come" was **a cover** for that.

- This is Gran's own story, the one the narrator sees through on the motorway.
- She couldn't find it, but nothing suggests she pretended to be locked out. She was on the bench with the front door shut, and the narrator works out a memory problem, not a plan to get a visit.
- The narrator hid it in the flowerpot she chose, under the geranium. Nothing was wrong with the hiding place.
- She had the key in her lap and was never said to have trouble with doors. The clues are about **finding**, not using.

The narrator works out that Gran had not been able to find the key and needed the narrator's directions, and that her story of a test covered this.

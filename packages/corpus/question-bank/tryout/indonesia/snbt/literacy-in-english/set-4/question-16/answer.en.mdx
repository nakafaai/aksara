Paragraph 1 says the lump "attracted little notice at first". In 1902, **after it cracked apart**, an archaeologist noticed "a toothed gear wheel", something no one expected from the ancient world. That gear was the first sign the object was unusual.

- Its weight is never mentioned.
- The letters were tiny and many were unreadable until high-resolution scanning in the early 2000s.
- X-ray images came in the second half of the twentieth century, long after the discovery.
- The "manual" was the engraved text on the device itself, read much later, not a separate find.

So what first suggested the lump was unusual was a toothed gear wheel seen after it cracked apart.

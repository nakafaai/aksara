# Given

On Sunday, the narrator moves the spare key into the kitchen tea-towel drawer and tells Gran plainly where it is. Gran writes it down <Highlight>on the inside of the cupboard door in pencil, very small</Highlight>, and <Highlight>neither of us mentioned it again</Highlight>.

# Asked

Read together, what do the tiny hidden note and the shared silence suggest?

# Solution

Read **each detail as half of a quiet pact**: the hidden note shows Gran's willingness to accept **discreet memory help**, and the silence shows the narrator **respecting her dignity**.

> Writing the location in tiny pencil inside a cupboard door accepts practical memory support without public embarrassment, and never mentioning it again shows the narrator honours that boundary.

## What both closing details reveal

- **Accepting private help**: Gran writes the exact drawer location down instead of refusing help, yet keeps it **hidden inside the cupboard door in tiny pencil** so nobody else sees it.
- **Narrator going along**: By moving the key without lecturing and keeping silent (<Highlight>neither of us mentioned it again</Highlight>), the narrator cooperates tactfully with Gran's need for privacy.

## Why the other options fail

- **Narrator resents keeping quiet** invents bitterness absent from the gentle final paragraph.
- **Gran refuses any help** is contradicted by Gran accepting the simpler location and writing it down.
- **Gran will never forget again** misreads a memory aid as a cure for forgetting.
- **Writing it down proves sound memory** reverses the evidence: needing a written reminder acknowledges memory slips rather than disproving them.

# Answer

**That Gran accepts help with her memory as long as it stays private, and the narrator goes along with that**.

Each detail gives half of the answer.

**The note**. Gran writes the new place down, which is accepting help: she now has a reminder. But she writes it "on the inside of the cupboard door in pencil, very small", where no visitor would see it. The help stays **private**.

**The silence**. "Neither of us mentioned it again." The narrator doesn't push, praise or bring it up. She **goes along** with Gran's way of handling it, just as she moved the key and "told her exactly where it was" without a word about memory.

- The narrator's feelings at the end aren't described, and she has just worked out why Gran needed help. Nothing shows resentment.
- Gran does accept help. She writes down the place the narrator tells her.
- A note is help for forgetting, not proof that she won't forget.
- Proving her memory is sound would mean **not** needing a note. Writing it down shows the opposite.

Together, they suggest that Gran accepts help with her memory as long as it stays private, and that the narrator goes along with that.

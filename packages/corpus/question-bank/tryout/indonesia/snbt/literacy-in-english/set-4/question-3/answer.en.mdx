# Given

Pfungst secretly hands von Osten a card showing the **wrong sum (<InlineMath math="9" /> instead of <InlineMath math="8" />)** for <InlineMath math="5 + 3" />. Von Osten stands **in full view** believing the answer is <InlineMath math="9" />, while Pfungst stands **out of sight**.

# Asked

Based on the passage, what would Clever Hans most likely do?

# Solution

Track **whose cue Hans can see** and **when that cue happens**: Hans watches von Osten's body, and von Osten's posture shifts at the number he expects.

> Because Hans watches for the questioner's involuntary relaxation rather than calculating, he stops tapping at nine, the exact count where von Osten expects the answer.

## Applying the cue mechanism

Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> explains how Hans worked: <Highlight>Hans was not counting. He was watching. As the count neared the right number, questioners leaned forward slightly as they waited, then relaxed or lifted their heads when he reached it, and Hans stopped tapping at that cue</Highlight>.

Here, von Osten stands in view and **expects nine**. His posture will relax at the ninth tap rather than the eighth, causing Hans to **tap nine times**.

## Why the other options fail

- **Tap eight times by adding** contradicts the passage stating <Highlight>Hans was not counting</Highlight>.
- **Tap nine times by reading the card** invents reading ability: Hans read human head movements, not written numerals.
- **Tap eight times on the true sum** misunderstands the cue: involuntary tension and release follow the questioner's belief, not arithmetic truth.
- **Tap a random number** confuses an unheld belief with a false belief: von Osten actively expects nine and will signal at nine.

# Answer

**Tap nine times, because Hans stops at the questioner's cue, and the cue follows what the questioner expects**.

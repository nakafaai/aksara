When Gran asked me to hide her spare key, she made me promise not to tell her where.

"Somewhere I'd never think of," she said. "That's the point."

I chose the cracked flowerpot by the back step, under the geranium that had died in March and that she refused to throw away because, she said, it was only resting. I told no one. For three weeks the arrangement worked so well that I forgot about it.

Then she rang me at work. She had locked herself out, she said, and she was sitting on the bench by the front door, and would I come. I said the spare was in the flowerpot at the back. There was a pause long enough for me to hear a bus go by at her end.

"Which flowerpot?" she said.

"The geranium. The dead one."

"It is not dead," she said, and hung up.

When I got there forty minutes later, she was still on the bench, with the key in her lap and the front door shut. She had found it at once, she told me. She had simply wanted to see whether I would come.

I was angry for most of the drive home, and then, on the motorway, I worked out what she had not said. She had not asked me where the key was. She had asked me which flowerpot. There were five of them along the back wall, and she had walked past all five on her way round the house before she rang.

On Sunday I moved the key to the kitchen drawer, the one with the tea towels, and told her exactly where it was. She wrote it on the inside of the cupboard door in pencil, very small, and neither of us mentioned it again. The geranium flowered in June.

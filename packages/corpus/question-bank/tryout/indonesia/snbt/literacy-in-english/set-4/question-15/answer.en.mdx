# Given

The town restricts testing to adults with a family history, where **<InlineMath math="1" /> in <InlineMath math="10" />** (<InlineMath math="10\%" />) have the condition. Test rates remain unchanged: **<InlineMath math="90\%" />** true-positive rate and **<InlineMath math="5\%" />** false-alarm rate.

# Asked

In this higher-risk group, about what share of positive results would be correct?

# Solution

Rebuild the positive row for a clean sample of **<InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> high-risk adults** (<InlineMath math="100" /> affected, <InlineMath math="900" /> unaffected), then divide true positives by all positives.

> Raising prevalence from one in a hundred to one in ten produces ninety true positives and forty-five false alarms per thousand adults, making two thirds or about sixty-seven percent of positives correct.

## Rebuilding the positive row per thousand adults

| Subgroup (per <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> tested) | Adults | Positive rate | Positive results |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Has the condition (<InlineMath math="10\%" />) | <InlineMath math="100" /> | <InlineMath math="90\%" /> | <InlineMath math="90" /> |
| Does not have it (<InlineMath math="90\%" />) | <InlineMath math="900" /> | <InlineMath math="5\%" /> | <InlineMath math="45" /> |
| Total positive results | <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> | | <InlineMath math="135" /> |

Computing the **new positive predictive share**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{True positives} &= 90\% \times 100 = 90 \\ \text{False alarms} &= 5\% \times 900 = 45 \\ \text{Share of positives correct} &= \frac{90}{90 + 45} = \frac{90}{135} = \frac{2}{3} \approx 67\% \end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options fail

- **<InlineMath math="15\%" />** reflects the old **<InlineMath math="1" /> in <InlineMath math="100" />** general-population rate.
- **<InlineMath math="90\%" />** is the detection rate among affected people, not the share of positives that are right.
- **<InlineMath math="95\%" />** is the maker's overall accuracy across all tested adults.
- **<InlineMath math="50\%" />** would require equal true and false positives, whereas <InlineMath math="90" /> is twice <InlineMath math="45" />.

# Answer

**About <InlineMath math="67\%" />**

The review of the oral history archive began with a simple baseline. The team recorded the interviews with complete context each day for a week and compared those figures with brief user conversations. Every observed difficulty could therefore be traced to evidence.

The baseline led the team to test one explanation: clearer guidance might help users. It chose sample open questions for interviewers as the intervention and alternated its use with the earlier process to create a reasonable comparison.

The records gave 22 as the baseline value. Trial days then reached 30, while comparison days stood at 24. Conversation notes agreed with the figures but were still treated as limited supporting evidence.

The early findings justified continuing the work, not claiming that one method suited every setting. The test was short and the visitors changed daily. The next study will collect data for longer under the same rules.

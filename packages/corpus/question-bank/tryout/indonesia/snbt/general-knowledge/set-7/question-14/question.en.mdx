A community theatre group adapted a folktale about a traveller who breaks a promise. The written script they found was published only in the 1970s, while oral recordings from three villages differed in characters, sequence, and ending. No single version could be established as the first form.

The director rejected two easy routes: blending every version without explanation or selecting one and calling it the original. Instead, the group built a framing scene. Storytellers interrupt one another on stage, showing why different communities emphasise different consequences of the broken promise.

During audience testing, some viewers understood the diversity, but others thought the differences were performers' mistakes. The group added a short introduction and revised transitions without explaining the meaning of every scene. They wanted to orient viewers while retaining room for interpretation.

The programme notes identify the source of each section and explain dramatic changes. For the group, faithfulness is not measured by word-for-word copying. An adaptation is responsible when changes are traceable, source diversity remains visible, and the work still forms a coherent theatrical experience.

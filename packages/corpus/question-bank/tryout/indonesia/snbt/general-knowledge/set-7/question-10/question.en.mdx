A proposed improvement emerged in the electronics repair clinic, but the team requested data before applying it widely. During the first week, it recorded the devices fully inspected every day and asked users which steps helped or hindered them.

As a working hypothesis, the team proposed that more visible guidance could improve the process. It introduced an initial inspection desk before the technician queue on a limited basis. Comparison days continued as usual, and opening hours and staffing did not change.

The averages differed: 21 before the test, 28 when the change was used, and 23 on comparison days. Some users identified the next step more easily. The team considered those remarks useful support, not proof of a single cause.

The team neither dismissed the positive result nor exaggerated it. Two weeks and a changing visitor mix restricted the conclusion. It will repeat the trial, preserve the measurement rules, and look for unseen disadvantages.

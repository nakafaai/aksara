Instead of redesigning the entire classroom seed trial, the team first mapped the problem. Each day for one week, it recorded the number of pots with complete records. Brief user conversations added context so that the figures would not be interpreted on their own.

The hypothesis was deliberately narrow: one clearer prompt might make the process easier. That prompt took the form of cards recording each watering time. Trial and comparison days used the same opening hours and staffing levels.

The numerical result had a clear order. The trial value, 30, was higher than the baseline of 20 and the comparison value of 22. Users described an easier process, but the team kept those experiences separate from a firm causal claim.

The change appeared useful, but a higher value did not establish universal success. The study ran for only two weeks and involved a changing group of users. The team therefore chose to extend the trial and monitor overlooked effects.

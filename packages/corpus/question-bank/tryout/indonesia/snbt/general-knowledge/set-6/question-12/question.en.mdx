A food festival tested a deposit for reusable containers. Visitors paid a small extra amount and received it back when returning the container. On the first evening, returns were high and single-use container waste fell.

The figures were not treated as complete success. The return desk stood near the main entrance, so vendors at the far end received more complaints. Some visitors also left before the desk reopened after rain. The same incentive created different time costs.

The organisers moved one desk, extended its hours, and added clearer signs. On the next evening, the return-rate gap across the site narrowed. Vendors still requested calculations for washing costs and lost containers before the system became permanent.

The organisers concluded that a deposit can change behaviour, but service design determines who can recover the money easily. Final evaluation will include waste reduction, desk access, operating costs, and vendor complaints.

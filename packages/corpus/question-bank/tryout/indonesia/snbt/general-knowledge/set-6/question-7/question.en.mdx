Three questions guided the review of the school bus queue: what confused users, how often the difficulty appeared, and which small change was worth testing. The team counted the passengers boarding without changing queues each day for a week and recorded comments about unclear steps.

The team suspected that clearer guidance, rather than additional staff, might affect the result. It used a marked lane for each destination on trial days and retained the earlier process on comparison days. Schedules and staff numbers stayed stable.

The baseline daily average was 19. Trial days reached 28, whereas comparison days reached 21. Brief conversations showed a similar pattern, although user comments remained supporting observations rather than decisive proof.

The preliminary conclusion was limited to this trial. Its short duration and changing visitors meant that the pattern might not transfer elsewhere. The team will repeat the test over a longer period using the same procedure.

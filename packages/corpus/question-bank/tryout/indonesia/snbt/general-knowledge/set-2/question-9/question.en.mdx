<InlineMath math="(1)" /> Milk and dairy foods provide useful nutrients, but people do not all digest lactose equally well. <InlineMath math="(2)" /> Lactose intolerance means having digestive symptoms after consuming lactose because the body does not absorb it completely, a process called malabsorption. <InlineMath math="(3)" /> Normally, the small intestine produces lactase, an enzyme that splits lactose into simpler sugars that the body can absorb. <InlineMath math="(4)" /> When lactase levels are low, some undigested lactose passes into the colon.

<InlineMath math="(5)" /> Bacteria in the colon then ferment the lactose and produce gas and fluid, which may cause bloating, abdominal pain, or diarrhea. <InlineMath math="(6)" /> The amount that triggers symptoms differs from person to person, and many people with lactose intolerance can tolerate some lactose.

<InlineMath math="(7)" /> Symptoms may be managed by adjusting portion sizes, choosing lactose-reduced or lactose-free products, or using lactase products when appropriate. <InlineMath math="(8)" /> Some people digest yoghurt with live cultures better than ordinary milk because bacterial lactase can help break down its lactose. <InlineMath math="(9)" /> However, a fermented dairy product is not necessarily lactose-free or equally suitable for every person.

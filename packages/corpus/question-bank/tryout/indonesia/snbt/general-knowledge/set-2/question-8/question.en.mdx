(1) Milk and dairy foods provide useful nutrients, but people do not all digest lactose equally well. (2) Lactose intolerance means having digestive symptoms after consuming lactose because the body does not absorb it completely, a process called malabsorption. (3) Normally, the small intestine produces lactase, an enzyme that splits lactose into simpler sugars that the body can absorb. (4) When lactase levels are low, some undigested lactose passes into the colon.

(5) Bacteria in the colon then ferment the lactose and produce gas and fluid, which may cause bloating, abdominal pain, or diarrhea. (6) The amount that triggers symptoms differs from person to person, and many people with lactose intolerance can tolerate some lactose.

(7) Symptoms may be managed by adjusting portion sizes, choosing lactose-reduced or lactose-free products, or using lactase products when appropriate. (8) Some people digest yoghurt with live cultures better than ordinary milk because bacterial lactase can help break down its lactose. (9) However, a fermented dairy product is not necessarily lactose-free or equally suitable for every person.

*(Adapted from [NIDDK guidance on lactose intolerance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/lactose-intolerance) and [a review of lactose digestion from yoghurt](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24695892/).)*

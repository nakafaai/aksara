(1) Many bats leave their roosts at night to search for food. (2) During the day, they generally rest in sheltered places. (3) Many species roost upside down in caves, beneath bridges, inside hollow trees, or in other protected sites. (4) This posture offers bats several advantages.

(5) To take flight from a hanging roost, a bat can release its grip and drop into open air. (6) Starting already airborne makes takeoff easy, especially for species that are not well suited to running. (7) It would nevertheless be inaccurate to say that no bat can take off from the ground. (8) Experiments show that several species use their forelimbs and wrist joints to jump into flight, while some bats are also capable ground movers. (9) Hanging itself requires little muscular effort. (10) Tendons and ligaments in a bat's feet help the claws remain closed around a perch. (11) A high, hard-to-reach roost can also reduce exposure to danger. (12) Predators, including some birds of prey, may have more difficulty reaching a bat there. (13) The precise roosting and takeoff behavior varies among species. (14) Overall, upside-down roosting can combine efficient departure, a low-effort grip, and protection.

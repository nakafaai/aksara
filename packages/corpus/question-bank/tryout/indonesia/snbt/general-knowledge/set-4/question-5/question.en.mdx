A market manager tested one digital-payment lane over four weekends. Cashier records showed that transactions in that lane finished faster on average than cash transactions. Yet the digital lane was used mostly by younger shoppers carrying few items, while customers with larger purchases continued to choose cash.

Some traders proposed moving every transaction to digital payment immediately. Others objected because the signal was unstable on the market's eastern side and some shoppers lacked suitable devices. They argued that the average time was not representative of every user's needs.

The manager then separated the data by item count, user age, and stall location. After this separation, the time advantage remained for some groups but nearly disappeared for transactions with many items. Signal complaints were also concentrated in one aisle.

The market decided to add digital lanes without removing cash. The next test will improve the network and compare transactions with similar item counts. This decision recognises the observed benefit while limiting the claim to the conditions actually measured.


Which statement functions as evidence rather than an evaluation or a plan?

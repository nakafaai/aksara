A proposed improvement emerged in the campus bicycle parking, but the team requested data before applying it widely. During the first week, it recorded the bicycles parked in the correct rack every day and asked users which steps helped or hindered them.

As a working hypothesis, the team proposed that more visible guidance could improve the process. It introduced rack numbers visible from the entrance on a limited basis. Comparison days continued as usual, and opening hours and staffing did not change.

The averages differed: 23 before the test, 33 when the change was used, and 25 on comparison days. Some users identified the next step more easily. The team considered those remarks useful support, not proof of a single cause.

The team neither dismissed the positive result nor exaggerated it. Two weeks and a changing visitor mix restricted the conclusion. It will repeat the trial, preserve the measurement rules, and look for unseen disadvantages.

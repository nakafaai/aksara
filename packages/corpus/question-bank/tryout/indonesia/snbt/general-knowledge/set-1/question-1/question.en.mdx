(1) People have long wondered how and why dreams arise. (2) Scientists have several evidence-based hypotheses, although a complete answer is still out of reach. (3) Dreams are subjective experiences that the sleeping brain builds from several sources. (4) Their images and stories often combine fragments of memories, concerns, and recent experiences. (5) The brain and body remain active during sleep. (6) It consists of recurring non-REM and REM stages with different patterns of brain activity. (7) Essential processes continue in the brain and body throughout those stages. (8) Brain activity continues during sleep, however it changes across stages and differs from wakefulness. (9) During vivid REM dreams, some brain regions involved in deliberate control can be less active than when we are awake. (10) This may help explain why dream narratives can contain unusual associations without seeming strange at the time.

(11) Studies show that recent waking experiences can appear in dreams, usually as fragments with details changed from the original event. (12) Animals also show sleep-related patterns of neural activity, yet researchers cannot directly know what an animal subjectively experiences. (13) It is therefore reasonable to investigate dream-like processing in some animals, although current evidence cannot reveal the content of an animal's dreams. (14) Sleep supports memory consolidation, and memory processing can influence dream content. (15) However, an association between dreaming about a task and later performance does not prove that every dream strengthens memory. (16) The well-supported conclusion is that sufficient sleep helps learning and memory, while the exact function of dreams remains under study.

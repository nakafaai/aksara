A city museum received two descriptions of an old market photograph. An inventory book from 1968 called the location an evening market. However, four former traders remembered the main activity beginning in the morning. The curator did not immediately choose one account because the two kinds of source were produced through different processes.

The team then cross-checked the evidence. They examined the date on the back of the photograph, compared the shadows, read the market permit, and recorded each interviewee's age and position at the time. This work confirmed the location but did not settle the disagreement about when the activity occurred.

The exhibition label separated documented facts, witness recollections, and what remained uncertain. A visitor asked why the museum did not simply use the most vivid story. The curator replied that an engaging account is not automatically the most authentic. Hiding disagreement could lead future readers to mistake one memory for a settled record.

The museum will digitize the sources, display the label's revision history, and invite corrections. A change will be accepted only when it includes a source trail that can be checked.

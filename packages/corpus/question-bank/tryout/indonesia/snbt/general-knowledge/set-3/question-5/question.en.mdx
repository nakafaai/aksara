The organisers of the mobile library service wanted evidence about a recurring difficulty, not a quick guess. During the first week, the team counted the completed loans each day and spoke briefly with users. These records formed a baseline before any change.

The first week's notes produced a working hypothesis: users might need guidance that was easier to recognise. The team introduced a reminder sent one day before each visit and alternated the change with the previous process without changing hours or staffing.

Three figures summarised the result: a baseline of 20, a trial-day value of 27, and a comparison-day value of 22. Users also found the process easier to follow, but their responses did not prove cause and effect.

The team considered the result promising rather than final. Two weeks was too short for a broad claim, especially because the visitors were not the same each day. The next stage will repeat the study for longer and record possible harms.

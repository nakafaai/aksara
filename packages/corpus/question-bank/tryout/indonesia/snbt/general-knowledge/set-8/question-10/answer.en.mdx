A weakening finding must damage the link from evidence to decision. The passage relies on **A traceable catalogue must preserve multiple names and their sources so search convenience does not erase attribution**, supported by the finding that **A photograph without the maker's name circulated widely, while the curator's label was cited more often than the family's account**.

**Archive research proves that every local name appeared after the curator's label and deliberately copied it** weakens that basis by removing a necessary condition or providing a competing explanation for the pattern. A restated plan, additional support, or unrelated fact leaves the original reasoning intact.

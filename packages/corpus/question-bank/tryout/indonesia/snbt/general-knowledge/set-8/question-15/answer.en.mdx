Two observations limit what you can infer from the first map:

| Evidence in the passage | Consequence for interpreting reports |
| --- | --- |
| Roadside sites appear to recover fastest and are easiest to visit. | Frequent access could produce more reports at those sites. |
| Visits are uneven, so missing reports are hard to distinguish from missing observations. | Few reports elsewhere need not mean few seedlings. |

Together, these support the inference that the map **may reflect observer behaviour**, as well as mangrove change. *May* matters: the passage has not measured how much of the pattern comes from observation opportunities. It does not establish that every report is wrong or that all community data are worthless.

Lower agreement for high-tide photographs is a stated detail. Separating the map layers is a management action. Neither alone expresses the inference about how the spatial pattern may arise.

A weakening finding must damage the link from evidence to decision. The passage relies on **Historical dates must be tied to the events they denote because one object can have several beginnings and major changes**, supported by the finding that **Different records connect 1912 with construction, 1914 with use, and 1916 with inauguration**.

**New technical records prove that construction, opening, inauguration, and structural replacement all actually occurred in 1912** weakens that basis by removing a necessary condition or providing a competing explanation for the pattern. A restated plan, additional support, or unrelated fact leaves the original reasoning intact.

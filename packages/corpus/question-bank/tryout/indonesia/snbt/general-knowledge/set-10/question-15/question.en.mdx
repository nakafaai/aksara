Before discussing a solution for the museum audio guide, the team checked whether the difficulty appeared consistently. It counted the recordings played to completion each day for seven days and recorded short responses from users. Together, the two sources produced an inspectable baseline.

The team treated its hypothesis as a question, not a final answer. It predicted that short codes printed at eye level could make the next step easier to recognise. Trial days alternated with the previous process while schedules and staffing remained stable.

Compared with the baseline of 21, the trial-day value rose to 31. Days using the previous process reached 23. Users reported a similar improvement, although their comments could not establish every causal link.

The team kept its conclusion cautious. The change might be useful, but the evidence was not universal because the study lasted two weeks and its participants changed. A follow-up will use the same measures and a longer period.

A regional library reviewed its late-return policy. Six months of data showed that most books were one to three days late, but a small number remained overdue for more than a month and caused the greatest disruption to popular-title availability.

The first proposal raised the daily fine for every delay. The second sent reminders before the due date, allowed a short grace period, and then restricted new borrowing for long delays. Supporters called the second approach more proportional because it distinguished brief mistakes from delays that genuinely harmed other users.

In a limited trial, reminders reduced short delays. Borrowing restrictions also brought back several books held for a long time. Interviews revealed, however, that some users never received a message because their phone numbers had changed. The library therefore did not regard the system as complete.

The library chose to expand the trial with two reminder channels and an appeal process for emergencies. Success would be judged by book availability, not by the amount of money collected.


Which statement functions as evidence rather than an evaluation or a plan?

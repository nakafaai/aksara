A school archive recorded interviews about a campus move in the early 1990s. A former teacher remembered a single move because the old building was damaged. A former student remembered classes moving in stages while the new building was completed. Both spoke confidently, but neither observed the entire process from the same position.

The archive team refused to build a reconstruction from confidence alone. It compared timetables, photographs, room-use letters, and three additional interviews. The documents showed that some classes moved first and that an opening ceremony occurred months later. The memory of one large move appears to have combined two nearby stages.

The exhibition retained quotations from both witnesses. Curatorial notes explained each viewpoint, interview date, and the documents that supported or limited each memory. The goal was not to embarrass a witness but to show how history is built from sources that do not always align.

The archive also preserved the original recordings so future researchers could reassess the interpretation. The reconstruction was treated as the best current explanation, not a perfect copy of the past.


Which paraphrase preserves the limits of the writer's statement?

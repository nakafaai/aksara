A biology class noticed that newly germinated bean roots did not always take the shortest route to the bottom of a clear container. The students wondered whether the seed's starting position mattered, particularly when its opening faced the side rather than the top or bottom. To isolate that difference, they used seeds from one packet and kept the moisture, container size, and observation time constant.

| Condition | Mean result |
| --- | ---: |
| Baseline | 9 |
| With the change | 16 |
| Comparison | 10 |

After six days, a mean of 16 roots in the side-facing condition had reached the lower half of their containers. The corresponding means were 9 at baseline and 10 in the comparison condition. The gap was large enough to justify another test, but not a universal claim. The containers were small, the observation period was short, and the report did not establish how the pattern would change in soil.

The report used *gravitropism* for a directional growth response to gravity. That definition clarified what the class was trying to observe; it did not explain the numerical difference by itself. The students therefore proposed a longer repetition with more seeds and the same measurement rules. A repeated pattern would strengthen the evidence, while a different result would force them to revise the first interpretation.


Which interpretation combines an explicitly reported result with the passage's appropriate evidential limit?

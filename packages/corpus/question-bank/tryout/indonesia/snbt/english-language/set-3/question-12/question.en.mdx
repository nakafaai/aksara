Leah had spent three evenings redesigning the opening of her family-history podcast. She had selected music, changed the title twice, and drawn a detailed episode plan, yet she had not contacted a single relative. In the school media room, an empty recording track made the problem difficult to ignore. The polished outline was protecting her from the risk of asking an imperfect first question.

Her teacher did not rescue the project with a new script. Instead, he asked Leah to name the smallest piece of evidence the episode required. She reduced the plan to one interview with her aunt and listed the dates, names, and family claims that still needed confirmation. The shorter plan left gaps on the page, but those gaps were now questions another person could answer.

During the interview, her aunt corrected the year of a move that everyone else had placed a decade earlier. She also remembered a neighbour whom Leah had not planned to include. One recording therefore changed both the evidence and the shape of the next episode. Leah remained responsible for checking the account, but accepting help no longer felt like surrendering control of the work.

In her reflection, Leah used *self-efficacy* for confidence that grows through successfully managing specific tasks. Her confidence did not appear before the difficult step. It grew after she completed one reviewable interview and learned what to do next. The account therefore links confidence to action and feedback, not to a general claim that smaller tasks are always better.


Which interpretation best connects the character's choice with the change that follows?

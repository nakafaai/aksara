Before sunrise, Nora watched three passengers enter the station, study the route board, and walk confidently toward the wrong platform. The board reproduced every line and stop correctly. Its top edge, however, pointed in the direction seen from the staff booth. A passenger arriving from the street faced the map from the opposite side, so the first turn on the board appeared to lead left when the real route began to the right.

Nora unfolded a paper copy from the information desk and stood where arriving passengers normally paused. She rotated the sheet until its top matched the direction they were facing, then traced the path from the entrance to the correct platform. Because she could not alter the mounted board, she placed the rotated copy beside it as a temporary guide.

A child used the paper map and found the platform before either accompanying adult. When Nora folded the sheet again, she left a new crease along the passenger-facing edge. The crease did not correct any route. It marked the change in perspective that had made the same information usable. The map therefore comes to represent a distinction between possessing accurate information and presenting it from the reader's point of view.

The episode also uses *irony*, a contrast between what is expected and what actually occurs. The permanent, technically accurate board misdirects passengers, while a folded copy becomes the more reliable guide. This contrast supports an interpretation grounded in the board's orientation, Nora's adjustment, and the child's response, rather than in a general impression that official signs are unhelpful.

Leah entered the school media room intending to finish a family-history audio project. The plan had seemed manageable from a distance, but one problem appeared immediately: she kept redesigning the introduction instead of contacting her first speaker. For a moment, abandoning the task felt easier than exposing what was not working.

Instead of hiding the difficulty, Leah reduced the plan to one interview and wrote down what evidence was still missing. This step did not solve the entire project. It made the next decision visible and allowed another person to respond to it.

As a result, the first recording revealed a question she had not considered. The experience changed how Leah understood responsibility and support. The reflection was simple: a smaller completed task can create more direction than an elaborate unfinished plan.

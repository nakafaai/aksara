Mina entered an after-school laboratory intending to lead a science-fair team without deciding everything alone. The plan had seemed manageable from a distance, but one problem appeared immediately: two members stopped contributing after their early ideas were dismissed. For a moment, abandoning the task felt easier than exposing what was not working.

Instead of hiding the difficulty, Mina asked each member to state one concern before choosing the next step. This step did not solve the entire project. It made the next decision visible and allowed another person to respond to it.

As a result, the group replaced one weak measurement and divided the remaining work openly. The experience changed how Mina understood responsibility and support. The reflection was simple: speed can hide disagreement rather than resolve it.

In a final note, Mina used the term *psychological safety* to mean a condition in which people can raise questions or mistakes without fear of humiliation. The meaning is shown through the choices in the account rather than added as an isolated definition.

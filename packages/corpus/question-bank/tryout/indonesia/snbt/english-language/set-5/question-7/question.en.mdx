At a community food pantry, volunteers sometimes opened a newer delivery while an older suitable package remained on the shelf. Households, donors, volunteers, and food-safety advisers agreed that the pantry needed a clearer system, but they did not agree to make the first proposal permanent. During an initial week, a mean of 32 packages per observation period were selected in the intended older-before-newer order.

The team then alternated labelled and unlabelled periods. During labelled periods, every shelf displayed the date on which each package had entered the pantry. The mean rose to 47. During comparison periods with the previous shelf system, it was 33. Staffing and volunteer schedules were kept stable across both conditions.

| Stage | Mean value |
| --- | ---: |
| Baseline | 32 |
| Trial | 47 |
| Comparison | 33 |

The trial results were discussed with the groups affected by the service. They supported a limited extension, not immediate permanent adoption. Package demand changed during school holidays and local events, so a longer trial would need to include both busy and ordinary weeks.

In the report, *stock rotation* meant using older stock before newer stock when both remain suitable. The date labels were intended to support that practice; the definition itself did not prove that the labels caused the higher count.


Which interpretation combines an explicitly reported result with the passage's appropriate evidential limit?

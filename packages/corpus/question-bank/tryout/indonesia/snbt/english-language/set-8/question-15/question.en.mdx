Jonas entered a youth translation club intending to translate a welcome sign for several community events. The plan had seemed manageable from a distance, but one problem appeared immediately: one word sounded warm at home but too informal in a public notice. For a moment, abandoning the task felt easier than exposing what was not working.

Instead of hiding the difficulty, Jonas kept two translations and added a note describing where each was appropriate. This step did not solve the entire project. It made the next decision visible and allowed another person to respond to it.

As a result, organisers selected a version for the formal sign and another for spoken greetings. The experience changed how Jonas understood responsibility and support. The reflection was simple: faithful translation may require more than replacing one word with another.

In a final note, Jonas used the term *connotation* to mean the social meaning and level of formality carried by a word or phrase. The meaning is shown through the choices in the account rather than added as an isolated definition.


Which title best captures both the central issue and the passage's cautious scope?

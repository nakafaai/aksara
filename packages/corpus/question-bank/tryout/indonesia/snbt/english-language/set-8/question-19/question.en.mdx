A multilingual welcome desk grew quieter when Mei noticed a two-sided card. The object seemed ordinary, yet it returned whenever an unfinished task came to mind. This time, one phrase belonged to her grandmother's kitchen, while the other belonged to public meetings.

No one offered Mei a ready answer. Instead, Mei wrote both versions of the greeting on opposite sides of the same card. The movement was small enough to escape most people passing by, but it changed how the space felt.

By the end, she used the card as a doorway, choosing a side according to the person arriving. A two-sided card can then be read as language as movement between relationships. The story never announces this meaning directly. It develops through the repeated object, the character's choice, and the altered atmosphere.

This pattern relates to *metaphor*, meaning a comparison that treats one thing as another to create meaning. Any interpretation must remain connected to details in the story rather than personal impression alone.

Priya entered a residents' workshop intending to prepare a flood-safety message. The plan had seemed manageable from a distance, but one problem appeared immediately: the first draft contained every detail from the planning meeting. For a moment, abandoning the task felt easier than exposing what was not working.

Instead of hiding the difficulty, Priya wrote the warning in two sentences and tested it with a neighbour unfamiliar with the route. This step did not solve the entire project. It made the next decision visible and allowed another person to respond to it.

As a result, the neighbour identified the safe route without asking for clarification. The experience changed how Priya understood responsibility and support. The reflection was simple: important information becomes safer when the next action is easy to locate.

In a final note, Priya used the term *cognitive load* to mean the mental effort required to hold and process information at one time. The meaning is shown through the choices in the account rather than added as an isolated definition.

A class investigated whether blue-filtered light was associated with a change in the area of new leaves. The students used plants of the same variety and kept watering, light duration, and distance from the lamp constant. They measured new leaf area in square centimetres after the same growth period.

| Condition | Mean result |
| --- | ---: |
| Baseline | 12 |
| With the change | 19 |
| Comparison | 13 |

Plants under the blue filter produced a mean new leaf area of 19 cm², compared with 12 cm² at baseline and 13 cm² in the comparison condition. The difference is consistent with an effect under the tested setup, but the filter changed more than colour. A light meter showed that it also reduced total light intensity slightly.

Because colour and intensity changed together, the class could not tell which one produced the growth difference. The report called intensity a *confounding variable*: a factor that changes with the factor of interest and can confuse the interpretation.

For the follow-up, the students proposed more plants and light sources adjusted to deliver equal intensity at every leaf surface. They would retain the same plant variety, watering schedule, exposure time, and lamp distance. That design could test colour more directly instead of merely repeating the original uncertainty.

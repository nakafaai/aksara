The practice siren had already begun when a rain-spotted message card reached Asha. It named the river level, the blocked drains, and the streets likely to flood. The description was accurate, but the card gave no route to safety. A runner waited beside her while the remaining drill time ticked away.

Asha read the alert aloud and heard how long it took to reach the missing instruction. She crossed out every sentence that did not change the next action, then added the safe street and the meeting point at the top. The card still explained the danger, but the route could now be found before the explanation was finished.

The runner carried the shorter version down the block. It reached the final house just before the practice siren ended. The card first embodies the problem: the group knows a great deal about the flood but has not told residents what to do. Asha's cuts turn that knowledge into guidance before time runs out.

That struggle is the passage's *narrative conflict*, the central tension that drives a story forward. Here, accurate description competes with the urgent need for action. The deadline, Asha's decision, and the card's arrival at the final house all develop the conflict and its partial resolution.

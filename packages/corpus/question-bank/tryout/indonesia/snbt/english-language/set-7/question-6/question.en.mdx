A neighbourhood flood-warning group wanted residents to do more than recognise that a flood was possible. In an initial exercise, its usual alert led a mean of 41 households per round to identify the correct safe route. Residents, emergency volunteers, schools, and local authorities then reviewed the message together.

The revised alert named the affected street, the expected water depth, and the safe route to higher ground. During alternating trial and comparison rounds, the revised alert produced a mean of 62 correct route identifications, compared with 43 for the existing alert. Staffing, delivery time, and the households invited to participate were kept as similar as possible.

| Stage | Mean value |
| --- | ---: |
| Baseline | 41 |
| Trial | 62 |
| Comparison | 43 |

The group agreed to extend the revised alert for further testing, not to treat one drill as final proof. Participants knew that the exercise was simulated, so it could not reproduce the time pressure, fear, noise, or disrupted communication of an actual flood.

In the report, *actionable information* meant a message that states what action to take and why it matters. Naming the safe route makes the action explicit, while the street and expected depth explain why that route is relevant. A real emergency test would still require evidence beyond the drill.


Which option best integrates the passage's focus, evidence, and level of certainty?

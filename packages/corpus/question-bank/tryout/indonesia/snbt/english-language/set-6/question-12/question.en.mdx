Theo entered a small public archive intending to catalogue a box of community photographs. The plan had seemed manageable from a distance, but one problem appeared immediately: several photographs had names on the back but no reliable year. For a moment, abandoning the task felt easier than exposing what was not working.

Instead of hiding the difficulty, Theo kept the uncertain date visible and added a note explaining why it remained uncertain. This step did not solve the entire project. It made the next decision visible and allowed another person to respond to it.

As a result, a visitor later recognised one location and supplied a verifiable clue. The experience changed how Theo understood responsibility and support. The reflection was simple: honest uncertainty preserves more value than a confident guess.

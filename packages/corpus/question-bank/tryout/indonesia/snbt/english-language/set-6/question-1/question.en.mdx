A class designed a simulated investigation of enzyme activity in a classroom model. The question was whether holding the mixture at 37 degrees Celsius was associated with a change in colour-change units after five minutes. Before collecting data, the students agreed to keep sample volume, pH, and reaction time the same in every condition.

| Condition | Mean result |
| --- | ---: |
| Baseline | 18 |
| With the change | 31 |
| Comparison | 19 |

The condition with the change differed from both other conditions. The class did not treat this pattern as proof that the same result would always occur. Its report identified an important limitation: a colour scale estimated by eye cannot provide a highly precise concentration.

The report used the term *enzyme* to mean a biological catalyst that speeds a reaction without being consumed. The students proposed a longer repetition with more units and the same measurement rules. This next step would test the pattern without hiding the uncertainty in the first investigation.


Which option best integrates the passage's focus, evidence, and level of certainty?

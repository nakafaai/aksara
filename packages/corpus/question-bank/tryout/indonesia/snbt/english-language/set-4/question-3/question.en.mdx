A chemistry class was comparing the temperatures at which visible ice first appeared in salted and unsalted water. Each sample contained 200 millilitres of water, and each salted sample received 10 grams of table salt. The students used matching containers, fixed every thermometer at the same depth, and followed one observation procedure so that a change in temperature would not be confused with a change in measurement.

An initial run with unsalted water produced a mean of -1°C. In the formal run, visible ice first appeared at a mean of -4°C in the salted samples and 0°C in the unsalted comparison samples.

| Condition | Mean temperature when visible ice first formed |
| --- | ---: |
| Initial unsalted baseline | -1°C |
| Salted samples | -4°C |
| Unsalted comparison | 0°C |

The difference was consistent with salt lowering the temperature at which ice formed in this trial, but the freezer introduced an unresolved alternative explanation. Its shelves did not cool at exactly the same rate, and the containers remained in their original positions for most of the run. A colder shelf could therefore account for part of the gap.

The report defined a *solute* as a substance dissolved in a solvent, identifying salt's role without turning the definition into evidence. For the repetition, the class proposed more samples and regular rotation of container positions across the shelves. Keeping the same volumes, thermometer depth, and observation rule would preserve the useful controls while addressing the main source of uncertainty.


Which conclusion follows by combining at least two details without going beyond the evidence?

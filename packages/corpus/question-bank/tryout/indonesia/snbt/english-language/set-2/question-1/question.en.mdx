Swimming and other forms of water-based exercise can support both physical and mental health. According to the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, people can often exercise in water for longer without increasing joint or muscle pain. This can be especially helpful for some people with arthritis or osteoarthritis. Water-based exercise may also improve older adults' quality of life and reduce disability.

The benefits are not limited to physical movement. Swimming may improve mood, and warm-water exercise has been associated with reduced anxiety or depression in some groups. These findings describe possible benefits for particular people and conditions; they do not mean that swimming prevents every illness or is suitable for everyone in the same way.

Swimming also involves risks. Contaminated recreational water can spread illnesses, and people can be injured in or around the water. A balanced decision therefore considers a person's health, swimming ability, supervision, water conditions, and appropriate safety measures alongside the possible benefits.

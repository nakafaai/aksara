The immune system is a network of cells, tissues, and organs. It needs many nutrients to function normally, including vitamins A, C, D, and E and minerals such as iron, selenium, and zinc. Eating a varied, nutritious diet helps supply these nutrients. No single food or nutrient can make the immune system invulnerable to infection.

Vitamin C is essential for healthy immune function and collagen production. Citrus fruits and many other fruits and vegetables provide it. A deficiency can impair normal body functions, but more is not always better. For most people who are not deficient, increasing vitamin and mineral intake through supplements does not prevent infections or make recovery faster. Regular vitamin C supplementation may slightly reduce the duration or symptoms of a cold, but taking it only after a cold begins may not help.

A healthy eating pattern is broader than one vitamin. The World Health Organization emphasizes adequacy, balance, moderation, and diversity. Fruits and vegetables belong in that pattern, along with legumes, whole grains, nuts, and other foods suited to a person's needs. Limiting free sugars can also support a balanced diet, but sugar does not literally push vitamin C out of white blood cells.

Practical change can be gradual: add a piece of fruit, choose water when you would usually have a sugary drink, or include another vegetable with a meal. The goal is to build a varied and nutritious diet that can be maintained over time. One ingredient cannot "boost" immunity or eliminate illness.

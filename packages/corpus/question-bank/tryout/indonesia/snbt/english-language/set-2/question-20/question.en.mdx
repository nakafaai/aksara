A job's exposure to generative artificial intelligence, or GenAI, is not the same as its disappearance. Exposure means that the technology could perform or assist with some of the tasks in an occupation. Actual effects depend on which tasks are adopted, how work is reorganized, what infrastructure and skills are available, and whether people continue to perform or oversee other parts of the job.

In <InlineMath math="2025" />, the International Labour Organization and Poland's National Research Institute refined a global index of occupational exposure. The method combined task-level information from nearly <InlineMath math="30{,}000" /> occupational tasks with worker input, expert review, and AI-assisted scoring. The estimates were then linked to labour-force data from more than <InlineMath math="140" /> countries.

The index estimated that about one in four workers worldwide had an occupation with some GenAI exposure, while <InlineMath math="3.3\%" /> of global employment was in the highest-exposure category. Exposure was uneven. Clerical occupations remained the most exposed, and the share of employment in any exposure category was about <InlineMath math="34\%" /> in high-income countries compared with <InlineMath math="11\%" /> in low-income countries.

These figures estimate how much work could be exposed to the technology. They do not measure jobs that have already been lost. Barriers such as limited electricity, broadband, digital skills, or the cost of technology can reduce adoption. Because most occupations contain tasks that still require human input, the report judged job transformation more likely than complete replacement.

The report therefore treats policy and workplace choices as important. Training, worker participation, social dialogue, and attention to job quality can influence whether the technology complements workers, changes their duties, or removes particular tasks.

*(Adapted from the ILO working paper [Generative AI and Jobs: A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure).)*

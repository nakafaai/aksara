Technology and interactive media can support early childhood learning when educators use them intentionally and appropriately. Successful integration does not mean adding a screen to every activity. It occurs when technology becomes a transparent tool and the child's attention stays on the exploration itself. Integration is successful when it supports learning goals, gives children useful ways to create or communicate, and improves their learning experiences.

Within a classroom plan, digital tools should extend rather than distort or replace developmentally appropriate experiences. Drawing on a touch screen can complement drawing on paper. Photographing a block structure can help children revisit and discuss their design. Recording a child's story can support reflection and communication.

Treating technology as a tool helps educators avoid passive, non-interactive screen use. Before choosing it, an educator should ask whether traditional materials would meet the goal more simply or whether the digital tool offers a meaningful opportunity that would otherwise be difficult to provide. Intentionality is central to developmentally appropriate use.

New resources can create valuable opportunities, but novelty alone is not a reason to adopt them. Careful selection, adult guidance, equitable access, and attention to children's development must shape every decision.

*(Adapted from the [NAEYC and Fred Rogers Center position statement](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/topics/technology-and-media/resources) and its [full text](https://www.naeyc.org/sites/default/files/globally-shared/downloads/PDFs/resources/position-statements/ps_technology.pdf).)*

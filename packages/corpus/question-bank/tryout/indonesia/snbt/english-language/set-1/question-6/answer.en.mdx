The passage assumes that **successful integration depends on educators making informed judgments about when and how to use a tool**.

Technology is not presented as automatically beneficial. Educators must compare a digital tool with traditional materials, decide whether it serves a learning goal, and provide appropriate guidance.

That reasoning only works if educators' judgment affects whether technology supports or distracts from learning. The second choice states this necessary assumption; the other choices contradict the passage's insistence that digital tools should complement, not replace, existing practice.

Digital technology has expanded access to educational materials, communication, and practice. Yet access and learning gains remain uneven. UNESCO's 2023 Global Education Monitoring Report notes that some technologies improve some kinds of learning in some settings, but strong evidence is still limited. Distributing devices alone does not guarantee better learning.

Appropriate use begins with the local context and a clear learning objective. Teachers need enough training to decide when a digital tool adds value and when a non-digital activity works better. Technology should support teachers and face-to-face interaction, not replace the human relationships on which learning depends.

Equity matters as much as effectiveness. Accessible open resources can broaden what learners read, watch, and practise. However, unreliable connectivity, unaffordable devices, inaccessible design, or weak support can create new forms of exclusion. Technology becomes equitable only when different learners can use it meaningfully.

The central question is therefore not whether technology belongs in education. It is whether a particular use is appropriate, equitable, evidence-based, and sustainable for the learners it is meant to serve.

*(Adapted from the [UNESCO 2023 Global Education Monitoring Report](https://gem-report-2023.unesco.org/) and its [recommendations](https://gem-report-2023.unesco.org/recommendations/).)*

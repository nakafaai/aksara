A class designed a simulated investigation of a model filter for floating plastic fragments. The question was whether adding a removable mesh with smaller openings was associated with a change in the percentage of fragments collected. Before collecting data, the students agreed to keep water volume, fragment size, flow rate, and collection time the same in every condition.

| Condition | Mean result |
| --- | ---: |
| Baseline | 38 |
| With the change | 71 |
| Comparison | 40 |

The condition with the change differed from both other conditions. The class did not treat this pattern as proof that the same result would always occur. Its report identified an important limitation: the model used clean fragments of one material rather than mixed environmental debris.

The report used the term *selectivity* to mean the ability to choose one group of particles while allowing others to pass. The students proposed a longer repetition with more units and the same measurement rules. This next step would test the pattern without hiding the uncertainty in the first investigation.

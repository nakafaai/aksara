Hana entered a night market intending to understand why waste was entering the wrong stream. The plan had seemed manageable from a distance, but one problem appeared immediately: the bin colours seemed correct, yet collectors still found mixed bags. For a moment, abandoning the task felt easier than exposing what was not working.

Instead of hiding the difficulty, Hana followed one bag from a stall to collection instead of judging the whole market at a glance. This step did not solve the entire project. It made the next decision visible and allowed another person to respond to it.

As a result, she found that stall permits used different symbols from the bins. The experience changed how Hana understood responsibility and support. The reflection was simple: a visible mistake can begin earlier than the place where it is discovered.

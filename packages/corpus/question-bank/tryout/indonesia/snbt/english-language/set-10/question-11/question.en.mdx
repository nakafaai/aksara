Caleb was preparing the budget for a youth concert when two assumptions failed at once. The venue explained that security costs would rise if attendance exceeded 400, and the lighting supplier's quote would expire before ticket sales began. The existing spreadsheet still displayed both figures as final, so its balanced total gave the team more confidence than the evidence allowed.

Caleb relabelled every amount as confirmed, variable, or awaiting a new quote. He then prepared scenarios for 300 and 450 attendees and added a contingency line linked specifically to security and lighting. Beside each unresolved amount, he wrote who would confirm it and by what date.

The revised sheet did not produce one final total. It showed that keeping an optional projection rental could leave the venue deposit exposed in the 450-person scenario. The team removed that rental, protected the deposit, and kept the lighting decision open until the supplier replied.

In his notes, Caleb defined a *contingency* as a planned response to an identified uncertainty, rather than money added without a reason. The change made the budget less tidy on the page but more useful for making the next decision.


Which option best integrates the passage's focus, evidence, and level of certainty?

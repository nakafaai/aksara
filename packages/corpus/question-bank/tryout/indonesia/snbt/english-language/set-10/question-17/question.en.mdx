After a youth-concert planning meeting, Iris remained in the empty hall with a pencil-worn ledger that had passed between several student treasurers. Earlier pages held totals that had been crossed out as ticket forecasts changed. On the newest page, someone had underlined one balanced total and written “FINAL”, although the security cost and lighting quote were still unresolved.

Iris erased the word “FINAL” but kept the figures. Beneath them, she wrote three questions: Which amounts are confirmed? Which change with attendance? Which still need a new quote? She left the ledger open on the meeting table.

The next morning, two teammates had added their initials and deadlines beside the unanswered questions. The last line, “decision after ticket forecast”, remained blank. The story ends before the supplier replies or the team approves a final budget. By then, the ledger has changed from a record of apparent certainty into a shared place for examining uncertainty.

This pattern is an *open ending*, an ending that deliberately leaves part of the outcome unresolved. The story withholds the final total, yet it already shows a change in how the characters make decisions. The decision remains pending, but they now examine uncertainty together.


Which interpretation best connects the character's action, the recurring object, and the ending?

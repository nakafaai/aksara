Caleb entered an event-planning meeting intending to build a budget for a youth concert. The plan had seemed manageable from a distance, but one problem appeared immediately: the group had treated every quoted price as final. For a moment, abandoning the task felt easier than exposing what was not working.

Instead of hiding the difficulty, Caleb labelled each estimate and created a separate line for costs that could change. This step did not solve the entire project. It made the next decision visible and allowed another person to respond to it.

As a result, the team reduced one optional cost and protected the venue deposit. The experience changed how Caleb understood responsibility and support. The reflection was simple: a responsible budget shows uncertainty rather than hiding it.

In a final note, Caleb used the term *contingency* to mean a plan for responding if an uncertain event changes the original assumptions. The meaning is shown through the choices in the account rather than added as an isolated definition.

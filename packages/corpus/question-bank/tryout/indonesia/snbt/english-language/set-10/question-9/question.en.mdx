Mentors in a youth event-planning programme noticed that some teams counted the same sound-system rental once for each workshop. They tested a new budget sheet with separate columns for event-level fixed costs, per-participant flexible costs, and shared costs recorded once.

Three comparable rounds each involved 40 teams. Every round used the same event scenario, price list, time limit, and mentor instructions. The outcome was the number of budgets that classified all costs without double-counting a shared item.

| Round | Budgets classified correctly |
| --- | ---: |
| Baseline with the old sheet | 21 of 40 |
| Trial with the separated sheet | 35 of 40 |
| Comparison with the old sheet | 22 of 40 |

The separated sheet was associated with fewer classification errors, so the organisers proposed a limited trial in the next real event. They did not claim that the sheet guaranteed an accurate total budget because the workshop used estimated prices rather than final supplier quotes. Their report defined a *fixed cost* as a cost that stays unchanged within a stated range of activity, not a cost that can never change.


What role does the definition of *fixed cost* play in the passage's reasoning?

An empty hall after a planning meeting grew quieter when Iris noticed a pencil-worn ledger. The object seemed ordinary, yet it returned whenever an unfinished task came to mind. This time, the numbers balanced, but only because the group had treated uncertain prices as promises.

No one offered Iris a ready answer. Instead, Iris erased the total and rewrote the budget as three questions. The movement was small enough to escape most people passing by, but it changed how the space felt.

By the end, the final blank line was labelled 'what we still do not know'. A pencil-worn ledger can then be read as honesty about uncertainty within an apparently exact plan. The story never announces this meaning directly. It develops through the repeated object, the character's choice, and the altered atmosphere.

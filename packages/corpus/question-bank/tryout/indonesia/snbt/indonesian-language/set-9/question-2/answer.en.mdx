Compare **the differences at each frequency**, not just the readings with panels. Subtract the panel reading from each no-panel reading.

| Frequency | Difference from initial condition | Difference after panel removal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 250 Hz | <InlineMath math="72-68=4\,\mathrm{dB}" /> | <InlineMath math="71-68=3\,\mathrm{dB}" /> |
| 1,000 Hz | <InlineMath math="74-61=13\,\mathrm{dB}" /> | <InlineMath math="73-61=12\,\mathrm{dB}" /> |
| 4,000 Hz | <InlineMath math="73-62=11\,\mathrm{dB}" /> | <InlineMath math="72-62=10\,\mathrm{dB}" /> |

The largest difference in both comparisons occurs at 1,000 Hz. Decibels use a logarithmic scale, so dividing 13 by 74 does not give a percentage reduction in energy. Nor does this result determine the reduction in another auditorium.

Sentences (4) to (8) give the central reasoning. War is highly destructive, so both sides usually have a strong incentive to avoid unnecessary losses and bargain instead.

That supports the choice linking the costs of war to a strong **incentive for both sides to bargain**. The passage does not claim that opponents share goals, possess complete information, or always receive outside help. It also does not say that war never changes politics.

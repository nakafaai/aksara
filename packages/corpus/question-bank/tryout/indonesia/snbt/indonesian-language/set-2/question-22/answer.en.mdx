A single-disaster hypothesis would require all the remains to come from the same period. The study instead found two independent kinds of difference.

First, sentences <InlineMath math="(7)" /> through <InlineMath math="(11)" /> describe three genetic ancestry groups. Second, sentences <InlineMath math="(12)" /> and <InlineMath math="(13)" /> date the South Asian-related group to the <InlineMath math="7" />th through <InlineMath math="10" />th centuries, while the other two groups date to the <InlineMath math="17" />th through <InlineMath math="20" />th centuries.

A gap of about <InlineMath math="1{,}000 \text{ years}" /> rules out one death event for everyone. The options about an army, an epidemic, or the analysis of only one skeleton are not supported by the passage.

Therefore, the finding that most strongly contradicts the single-disaster idea is **Jasad berasal dari beberapa masa dan kelompok leluhur genetik**, meaning that the remains come from different periods and genetic ancestry groups.

# Given

Sentences (<InlineMath math="3" />) and (<InlineMath math="4" />) of the **rabies** passage describe how the virus progresses:

- Sentence (<InlineMath math="3" />): the virus travels slowly along nerves to the brain, so symptoms may appear only weeks after the bite.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="4" />): once symptoms appear, the disease almost always ends in death.

# Asked

Why a bite victim needs vaccination immediately even before feeling any symptoms.

# Solution

Combining sentences (<InlineMath math="3" />) and (<InlineMath math="4" />) shows why waiting is fatal: **symptoms may not show until weeks later, by which time the victim is almost beyond saving**.

> <Highlight>Begitu gejala muncul, penyakit ini hampir selalu berakhir dengan kematian</Highlight>.

## Checking the Other Options

Each distractor **contradicts the text** or invents unsupported claims.

- The passage never says vaccines are available only on the day of the bite.
- Symptoms can appear weeks later rather than always within one day.
- Washing the wound is only the first step, and victims still need vaccination (sentence <InlineMath math="5" />).
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="8" />) implies not all dogs are guaranteed to be vaccinated.

# Answer

**Symptoms can appear weeks later, when the victim is already almost beyond help**.

(*Gejala bisa muncul berminggu-minggu kemudian, saat korban sudah hampir tidak tertolong.*)

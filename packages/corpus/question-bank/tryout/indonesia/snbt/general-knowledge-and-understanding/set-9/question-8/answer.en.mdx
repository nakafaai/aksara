# Given

The **rabies** passage provides four relevant details:

- Sentences (<InlineMath math="1" />) and (<InlineMath math="2" />): rabies spreads via bites from infected *animals*, and in Indonesia *most* human cases come from dog bites.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="3" />): symptoms may not appear until several weeks after a bite.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="5" />): bite wounds must be washed immediately with soap and running water for about fifteen minutes before getting vaccinated.
- Sentences (<InlineMath math="7" />) and (<InlineMath math="8" />): experts advise vaccinating at least seventy percent of dogs to halt transmission.

# Asked

Whether each statement can be inferred (**Yes** / *Ya*) or not (**No** / *Tidak*) from the passage.

# Solution

Check the **seventy-percent threshold** and watch out for restrictive words like *only* and *enough*.

> Distinguish *most cases come from dogs* from the overstated claim that *only* dogs can transmit rabies.

## Evaluating All Four Statements

Matching each claim to **textual evidence** gives these verdicts.

| Statement | Textual Evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Washing the wound with soap and running water is a recommended first step | Matches sentence (<InlineMath math="5" />) | **Yes** (*Ya*) |
| Rabies can be transmitted only by dogs | Contradicts sentences (<InlineMath math="1" />) and (<InlineMath math="2" />): infected animals, mostly dogs | **No** (*Tidak*) |
| Vaccinating thirty percent of dogs suffices to stop spread | Contradicts sentences (<InlineMath math="7" />) and (<InlineMath math="8" />): at least seventy percent needed | **No** (*Tidak*) |
| Rabies symptoms do not always appear immediately after a bite | Matches sentence (<InlineMath math="3" />): can appear weeks later | **Yes** (*Ya*) |

# Answer

Statements <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> can be inferred (**Yes** / *Ya*), while statements <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> cannot (**No** / *Tidak*).

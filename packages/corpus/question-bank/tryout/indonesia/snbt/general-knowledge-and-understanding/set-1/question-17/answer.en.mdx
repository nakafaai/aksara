# Given

Sentences (<InlineMath math="11" />) to (<InlineMath math="14" />) explain fast charging: it shortens wait time, yet if **ions are pushed in faster than graphite can accommodate them** (especially in cold weather), lithium plates as metal, risking a short circuit, which is why phones reduce current near full charge.

# Asked

Which statement does **NOT** match the author's reasoning in sentences (<InlineMath math="11" />) to (<InlineMath math="14" />)?

# Solution

> Namun, jika ion didorong masuk lebih cepat daripada kemampuan grafit menampungnya, terutama saat udara dingin, sebagian litium dapat mengendap sebagai logam di permukaan anode.

Check the **direction of the rate comparison** in sentence (<InlineMath math="12" />) against every option.

## Spotting the reversed rate comparison

Sentence (<InlineMath math="12" />) says metallic lithium deposits when <Highlight>ion didorong masuk lebih cepat daripada kemampuan grafit menampungnya</Highlight> ("ions are pushed in faster than graphite can take them"). Claiming graphite absorbs ions faster than they are pushed in **flips the inequality**: if graphite easily kept up, ions would intercalate smoothly without plating on the anode.

The other four choices faithfully follow sentences (<InlineMath math="11" />) to (<InlineMath math="14" />): conceding shorter wait times, higher risk in cold air, sharp deposits piercing the separator, and lower current near full charge.

# Answer

The statement that does **NOT** match the author's reasoning is that **lithium deposits form when graphite absorbs ions faster than the rate at which ions are pushed in** (*Endapan litium terbentuk ketika grafit menampung ion lebih cepat daripada laju ion didorong masuk.*).

The question asks what *musim paceklik* means in sentence (6), not the meaning you hear most often. The KBBI (the official Indonesian dictionary) lists three meanings of *paceklik*: (1) "a season when food is short", (2) "a slow period (in trade, activity and the like)", and (3) "hard times". All three sound like they could fit a text about prices, so you need evidence from the sentences around it.

Step one is reading the purpose in sentence (6): fresh chili can't be stored long **to be ready for** (*untuk berjaga-jaga menghadapi*) the *musim paceklik*. People store chili in advance so there's chili when chili is hard to get. So *musim paceklik* here is a time when the chili supply is low. That's the KBBI's first meaning, a season when food is short, with chili as the food.

Step two is checking that meaning against other sentences. Sentence (7) explains why supply drops, too much rain, and sentence (12) mentions turning chili into products that keep. Both fit: the problem is having too little chili at certain times.

Here's why the other options are wrong.

- "The chili market is short of buyers" uses the KBBI's second meaning. Storing chili doesn't help you face a time with few buyers, and sentence (8) actually says demand for chili doesn't fall much.
- "The dry season, when chili plants lack water" is a common association, not the meaning of the word. *Paceklik* is defined by a shortage of food, not by the weather, and the only cause of falling harvests the reading names is too much rain.
- "All food prices rise together" widens the scope. Sentence (6) is only about the chili supply.
- "Hard times when chili prices crash" uses the KBBI's third meaning in the wrong direction. A price crash means chili is plentiful, and storing chili ahead of time doesn't help you face that.

So *musim paceklik* in sentence (6) means a time when the chili supply at the market is low.

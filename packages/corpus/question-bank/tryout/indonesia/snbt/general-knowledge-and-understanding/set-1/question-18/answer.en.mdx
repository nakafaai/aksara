You need the synonym of *jalan* as it's used in sentence (4). The KBBI lists many senses of *jalan* ("road, way, path"), so first look at what the electrons travel through.

Sentence (4) names two things that move while the battery is in use:

| What moves | From | To | Through |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lithium ions | anode | cathode | the electrolyte |
| electrons | anode | cathode | *jalan lain* ("another *jalan*"), namely the wires and the device's circuit |

The table shows two things. First, both start and end at the same places: from the anode to the cathode. Second, the only difference is **what they pass through along the way**. Sentence (3) explains why: electrons can't pass through the electrolyte, so they have to go through the wires.

So *jalan* here means the route something takes as it moves from one place to another. The KBBI includes this sense, and its synonym is *lintasan* ("path, track"), meaning a route or way that something passes along. Plug it in: the electrons head to the cathode through another *lintasan*, namely the wires and the circuit inside the device. The meaning stays the same.

The other options don't fit:

- *Cara* ("method") and *upaya* ("effort, means") are synonyms of the figurative sense of *jalan*, as in "there's no other *jalan* than negotiating". In sentence (4), *jalan lain* is spelled out right away as wires and a circuit, things the electrons travel through, not a way of acting.
- *Peluang* ("opportunity") is another figurative sense of *jalan*, as in "the *jalan* is still open". Sentence (4) isn't about opportunities.
- *Arah* ("direction") is the biggest trap. Direction means where something is heading. But the ions and the electrons both head to the cathode, so their direction is the same. What differs is their path.

So the synonym of *jalan* in sentence (4) is *lintasan*.

This question has two steps. First, find the antonym of *menipis* in the sense it has in sentence (6). Second, test that antonym on five new sentences and find the one where it doesn't fit.

Step one is the meaning of *menipis* in sentence (6). The KBBI (the official Indonesian dictionary) lists two meanings. The first is "to become thin", like a car tire that keeps getting thinner. The second is "to decrease (become little, nearly used up)", like reserves that keep running low. In sentence (6), the thing that *menipis* is the phosphorus supply, an amount, so the second meaning applies. The opposite of "to decrease" is *bertambah*, which the KBBI defines as "to become more".

Step two is testing *bertambah* on each sentence. Ask one thing: is it the amount or the thickness that shrinks?

| Sentence | What shrinks | Meaning of *menipis* | Its opposite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ice cubes at the stall | the amount in stock | to decrease | *bertambah* |
| the family's savings | the amount of money | to decrease | *bertambah* |
| paper erased too often | the paper's thickness | to become thin | *menebal* |
| spectators in the stands | the number of people | to decrease | *bertambah* |
| the team's hope | how much hope | to decrease | *bertambah* |

Only in the sentence about paper does *menipis* mean **to become thin**. The phrase *hingga hampir sobek* ("until it almost tears") shows that what changes is the thickness of the sheet, not how many sheets there are. Its opposite is *menebal* ("to thicken"), not *bertambah*, because "the paper *bertambah*" would mean there's more paper.

Here's why the other sentences aren't the answer.

- Ice cubes are a solid thing, but what *menipis* is the stock of them, an amount. This sentence is a trap because the object feels physical.
- Savings and hope both shrink in amount or degree. The sentence about hope even mirrors the KBBI's example for the second meaning.
- Spectators who *menipis* means there are fewer people, not that their bodies get thinner.

So the sentence where the antonym *bertambah* doesn't fit is the one about paper erased too often, because there *menipis* means to become thin.

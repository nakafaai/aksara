This question tests what *menggariskan* means inside sentence (2). The right replacement has to keep the meaning of sentence (2), not just look similar.

The KBBI lists three senses of *menggariskan*. The first two are literal: to scratch a mark and to draw a line on a surface. The third is figurative: **to set a guideline or rule as a reference**.

Now look at what the guideline *menggariskan* in sentence (2). The object is the order for searching for equivalents, and everything after the colon is a rule: related languages are consulted only if Indonesian has no precise word, and foreign languages are used only if neither has one. You can't scratch an order onto paper. So the sentence uses the figurative sense, which is *menetapkan* ("to set").

Plug it into the sentence: "The 1988 General Guidelines for Term Formation *menetapkan* (set) the search order for equivalents of new terms: ...". The sentence still works, and the guideline is still the one making the rule.

The other options don't fit:

- *Menggoreskan* ("to scratch a line") is the literal sense. A search order isn't something you can scratch.
- *Menggarisbawahi* ("to underline, to emphasize") looks the most similar, but it means stressing or drawing attention to something that already exists. Sentence (2) says the guideline is what sets the order, not that it merely stresses an order that was already there.
- *Mencoret* ("to cross out") means striking something through or cancelling it. That would flip the meaning, as if the guideline deleted the order.
- *Merintis* ("to clear a path, to pioneer") means being the first to start something. Sentence (2) never says the guideline was the first to start such an order.

So the word that can replace *menggariskan* in sentence (2) without changing its meaning is *menetapkan*.

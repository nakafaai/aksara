# Given

Sentence (<InlineMath math="7" />) states that fungal hyphae can *menjulur* ("extend outward") past the phosphorus-depleted soil layer. The question replaces *menjulur* with ***menjulurkan*** without altering any other word.

# Asked

What happens to sentence (<InlineMath math="7" />) if *menjulur* is replaced by *menjulurkan* without changing other words?

# Solution

> Hifa jamur, yaitu benang-benang halus penyusun tubuh jamur, jauh lebih ramping daripada akar sehingga dapat menjulur melewati lapisan tanah yang sudah miskin fosfor itu dan menyusup ke pori tanah yang terlalu sempit bagi akar.

Compare the **transitivity** of the Indonesian prefix *meN-* in *menjulur* with the circumfix *meN-kan* in *menjulurkan*.

## Contrasting intransitive and transitive forms

In KBBI, <Highlight>menjulur</Highlight> is intransitive ("to stretch out"), needing no direct object because the subject itself extends. Adding *-kan* turns *menjulurkan* into a transitive verb ("to stick something out") that **requires a direct object**. Because the next word is another verb (*melewati*, "passing through"), no object exists and the sentence becomes grammatically incomplete.

The distractors misread Indonesian morphology. Meaning does not stay the same, benefactive *-kan* still needs a recipient, *menjulurkan* remains active (passive would be *dijulurkan*), and *lapisan tanah* is the object of *melewati*, not of *menjulurkan*.

# Answer

The result is that **the verb requires a direct object (something extended by the hyphae), which is absent, leaving the sentence incomplete** (*kata itu menuntut objek, yaitu benda yang dijulurkan oleh hifa, padahal objek itu tidak ada sehingga kalimat menjadi tidak lengkap*).

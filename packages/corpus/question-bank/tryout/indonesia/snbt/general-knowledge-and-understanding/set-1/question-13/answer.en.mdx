Choose *Sesuai* ("consistent with the text") or *Tidak sesuai* ("not consistent") for each statement. Each one has to be matched to the right sentence, and two of them can only be judged once you combine two sentences.

| Statement | Verdict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A regional word that's rarely used can become common again after it's made an equivalent for a term. | Sesuai | Sentence (4): *gawai* was already rarely used when it was listed as the equivalent of *device*. Sentence (7): *gawai* came back to life and is now common. One example is enough for *dapat* ("can"). |
| The Javanese sense of *mengunduh* is no longer recorded in the KBBI. | Tidak sesuai | Sentence (6): that Javanese sense is still recorded, alongside the new one. What changed is only how often it's heard. |
| A regional word that often appears in national media no longer needs italics. | Tidak sesuai | Sentence (8): appearing often in national media doesn't make a regional word established. Sentence (9): as long as it's still treated as a regional word, it's written in italics. |
| Indonesian speakers can still show respect even though Indonesian has no speech levels. | Sesuai | Sentence (13): Indonesian has no speech levels, but it marks respect through forms of address such as *Bapak* or *Ibu*. |

The third statement is the biggest trap. Lots of people assume that a word that's all over the media automatically becomes an Indonesian word and stops needing italics. Here's the chain. Sentence (8) says media alone doesn't make the word established. That means a word that shows up often in the media can still be treated as a regional word. According to sentence (9), such a word is **still written in italics**. So frequent media use doesn't automatically remove the italics.

The fourth statement is also often misjudged, because sentence (13) says the nuance is hard to carry over. What's hard to carry over is the speech-level distinction, not the ability to show respect. The same sentence actually names how Indonesian marks respect.

So the first and fourth statements are consistent with the text, and the second and third are not.

The main idea is what ties **all the sentences** of a paragraph together. Here every option contains something that really is in the reading, so the deciding question is which option covers the whole paragraph without changing a single relationship. Paragraph two runs from sentence (6) to sentence (9), so first map what each sentence does.

- Sentence (6): fresh chili rots quickly, so it can't be stored as a reserve. That's the supply side.
- Sentence (7): too much rain can make harvests in many production areas drop at almost the same time. That's the supply side too.
- Sentence (8): demand for chili tends not to fall much when its price rises. That's the demand side.
- Sentence (9): as a result, even a small drop in supply can raise the price by a percentage much larger than the percentage drop in supply.

Sentence (9) opens with *Akibatnya* ("As a result"), so it draws the conclusion from sentences (6) to (8). The main idea has to include both sides **and the size of the result**: a supply that drops easily meets a demand that barely falls, so the change in price, in percentage terms, is much larger than the change in supply.

Here's why the other options are wrong.

- "Supply drops easily because of rotting and rain" only sums up sentences (6) and (7). The demand side and the result are missing.
- "The price rises in proportion to the fall in supply" includes both sides but gets the size of the result wrong. Sentence (9) says the price rise is much larger than the drop in supply, not proportional to it.
- "Bad weather is the main cause" overstates sentence (7). The paragraph also names chili's quick rotting and the demand that barely falls, and it never calls the weather the main cause.
- "Households keep buying chili" is only sentence (8).

So the main idea of paragraph two is that a chili supply that drops easily meets a demand that barely falls, so the change in the chili price, in percentage terms, is much larger than the change in supply.

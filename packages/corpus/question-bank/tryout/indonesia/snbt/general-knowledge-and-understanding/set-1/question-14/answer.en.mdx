The word *jati* has two well-known meanings: the teak tree with its hard wood, and **original** or genuine, as in *jati diri* (one's true self). The phrase *penutur jati* is about a person who speaks, so the tree meaning cannot apply.

In sentence (8), *penutur jati* teach the regional language classes for children. They are chosen because the language is their own from childhood, so the children hear natural speech rather than memorized phrases.

The other options do not fit:

- Hard wood is the meaning of *jati* as a tree, while the phrase is about people.
- Old age is not part of the meaning of *jati*. A native speaker can be young.
- Classroom teaching skill is not the meaning of *jati*, and the passage stresses that the classes avoid rote learning.
- Fame has nothing to do with the meaning of *jati*.

So *jati* in *penutur jati* means original or genuine. A *penutur jati* is a native speaker who has used the language since childhood.

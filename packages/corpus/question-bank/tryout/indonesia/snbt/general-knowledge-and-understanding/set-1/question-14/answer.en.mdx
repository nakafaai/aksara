This question asks what *halus* means in sentence (11). The KBBI lists several senses of *halus*, including smooth or soft, very fine in grain, polite and well-mannered, and of high quality. Your job is to pick the sense that fits sentences (11) to (13), not the sense you hear most often.

Follow three steps.

1. Sentence (11) says Javanese has speech levels: for the same meaning there's often a *halus* word and an ordinary word. So *halus* here names one of the word levels.
2. Sentence (12) gives the basis for choosing between them: **the speaker's relationship with the listener**, such as differences in age and status. The same sentence stresses that the choice isn't decided by the topic being discussed.
3. Sentence (13) says Indonesian marks respect through forms of address because it has no speech levels. That means in Javanese, the split between *halus* words and ordinary words is what helps mark respect.

Put the three steps together and a *halus* word is a polite word used to show respect to the listener, for example to someone older or of higher status. That matches the KBBI sense of *halus*: polite and well-mannered.

The other options don't fit:

- Soft and pleasant to the ear uses the most common sense of *halus*, smooth or not rough. But sentence (12) says the choice depends on the listener, not on how the word sounds.
- Used to soften a topic that's crude or taboo sounds close, but sentence (12) says outright that the choice isn't decided by the topic.
- High in quality and so common in literature uses a different KBBI sense, high quality. The text says nothing about quality or literature.
- Standard and grammatically correct mixes up polite with correct. The text never calls the ordinary word wrong, since both words are simply chosen according to the listener.

So *halus* in sentence (11) means polite and used to show respect to the listener.

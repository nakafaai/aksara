The word *pewarisan* (inheritance) is often linked to property, but its meaning can extend to anything passed down. So look at **the explanation that sentence (4) itself gives**.

After the word *yaitu* (namely), sentence (4) defines the break: it happens "when parents no longer speak to their children in that language". What is handed down is the language, and the way it is handed down is **speaking to children in that language every day**. The word *antargenerasi* (between generations) shows the direction: from the parents' generation to the children's.

The other options do not fit:

- Dividing family property is the legal meaning of *warisan*, not the meaning in this passage.
- Migration from villages to cities is not mentioned.
- The passage does not mention formal schools. Sentence (4) stresses parents talking with children at home.
- Recording vocabulary is closer to compiling a dictionary in sentences (6) and (7), and the passage says a dictionary does not get people used to conversation.

So *pewarisan antargenerasi* means passing a language from the older generation to the younger one through everyday use.

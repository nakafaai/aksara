You need the sentence that uses *menebal* ("to thicken") with the same meaning as in sentence (10). Start by pinning down what thickens in sentence (10).

Sentence (10) says *lapisan itu terus menebal* ("that layer keeps thickening"). *Lapisan itu* points back to sentence (8): the thin layer that forms on the surface of the anode. So what grows is **the distance between the bottom and the top surface of that layer**, like a thin sheet of paper turning into thick paper.

*Menebal* means becoming *tebal*, and the KBBI separates several senses of *tebal*:

| Sense of *tebal* in the KBBI | Example |
| --- | --- |
| wide between two surfaces, for flat or layered things | thick paper, thick cloth |
| clearly visible, for lines or letters | a bold line |
| dense and abundant, for clouds or smoke | thick clouds |
| lush, for hair or plants | thick grass |
| firm and strong, for faith or belief | a firm belief |

Sentence (10) uses the first sense. Now find the sentence that's also about a layered thing getting thicker. The limescale at the bottom of a kettle is a layer stuck to the metal surface. Each time hard water is boiled, more lime sticks to it, so the layer gets thicker. That's exactly the same meaning as the layer on the anode.

The other options use different senses:

- The fishermen's belief that thickens uses the firm and strong sense. A belief isn't a layer.
- The smoke that thickens uses the dense and abundant sense. The smoke gets denser, but it doesn't form a layer with two surfaces.
- The grass that thickens uses the lush sense: it grows more closely together.
- The letters that thicken use the clearly visible sense: their strokes look bolder.

So *menebal* in sentence (10) has the same meaning as *menebal* in the sentence about the limescale at the bottom of the kettle.

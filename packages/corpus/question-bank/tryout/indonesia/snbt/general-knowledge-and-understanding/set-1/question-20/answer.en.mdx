Choose *Sesuai* ("consistent with the text") or *Tidak sesuai* ("not consistent") for each statement. Two of the statements use words that sound absolute or shift the timing, so read the conditions in the text carefully.

| Statement | Verdict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The lithium that reacts during the first charge is wasted with no benefit at all. | Tidak sesuai | Sentence (9): the layer that forms is actually needed, because it stops the electrolyte from breaking down further. That lithium can't move anymore, but it isn't wasted with no benefit. |
| The separator keeps the two electrodes from connecting directly, yet still lets ions move between them. | Sesuai | Sentence (2): a porous film soaked in electrolyte separates the electrodes. Sentence (3): ions can pass through the electrolyte. Sentence (13): the electrodes only get connected if that film is pierced. |
| In a battery that rarely gets hot, the thin layer can still thicken. | Sesuai | Sentence (10): the layer thickens with use, especially when the battery is often hot. *Terutama* ("especially") isn't the same as *hanya* ("only"). |
| The charging system lowers the current from the very start of charging. | Tidak sesuai | Sentence (14): the current is lowered when the battery is almost full, so the first percentages actually fill faster. |

The third statement is the one people get wrong most often. Sentence (10) says two things: the layer thickens **with use**, and that thickening happens especially when the battery is often hot. Heat speeds it up, but it isn't the only condition. So even a battery that rarely gets hot still sees the layer thicken, just not as fast as one that's often hot.

The fourth statement shifts the timing. Sentence (14) says the current is lowered near full charge, not from the start. The same sentence adds the proof: the last percentages fill more slowly than the first ones.

So the second and third statements are consistent with the text, and the first and fourth are not.

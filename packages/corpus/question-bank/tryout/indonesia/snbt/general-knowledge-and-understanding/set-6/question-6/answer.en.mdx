The word *terisap* comes from *isap*, **to draw in** air or liquid, as when sucking water through a straw. In sentence (5), a jet engine draws in air, and the ash in that air is pulled into the engine with it. That ash then melts inside the engine.

The other options do not fit:

- *Terlempar keluar* (thrown out) is the opposite direction.
- *Terbakar habis* (burned up) contradicts the ash melting and hardening again.
- *Tersaring keluar* (filtered out) means kept outside, but the ash enters the engine.
- *Tertiup menjauh* (blown away) also means not entering the engine.

So *terisap* in sentence (5) means drawn in (*tertarik masuk*).

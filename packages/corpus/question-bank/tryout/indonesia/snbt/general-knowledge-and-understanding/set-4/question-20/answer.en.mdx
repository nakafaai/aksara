A conclusion that's certainly true has to be guaranteed by the passage. Such conclusions often use a careful word like _dapat_ ("can"), while wrong ones often use absolute words. But don't pick on wording alone. Prove it by **combining sentences**.

Sentence (15) says a wetland's role for the climate is judged from its balance, the comparison between the carbon it stores and the greenhouse gases it releases, _bukan dari besar simpanannya saja_ ("not from the size of its store alone"). So two sides are counted.

The second side can differ from one wetland to another. Sentence (12) says waterlogged soil can produce methane, while sentence (14) says that mostly doesn't happen much in mangrove forests. Put both together with sentence (15): two wetlands may well hold the same carbon store, but if their gas release differs, their balance differs. So their role for the climate **can differ**.

The passage contradicts the other four conclusions.

- The biggest store isn't automatically the most helpful. Sentence (15) rejects judging by the size of the store alone.
- Sentence (14) says methane formation in mangrove forests _umumnya tidak banyak_ ("mostly not much") happens. Not much isn't the same as none.
- Sentence (16) says rewetting can raise methane release. So gas release doesn't stop. The step pays off because the carbon dioxide from dry peat is far larger.
- Sentences (6) and (7) say the store builds up very slowly, taking thousands of years for a few metres. Carbon that has escaped doesn't come back within a few years.

So what's certainly true is that two wetlands with equally large carbon stores can play different roles for the climate.

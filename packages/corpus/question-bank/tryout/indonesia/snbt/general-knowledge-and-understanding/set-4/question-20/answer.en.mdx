# Given

Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" /> of the **wetlands** passage explains how climate impact is judged:

- Sentences (<InlineMath math="12" />) and (<InlineMath math="14" />): flooded soil can emit methane, whereas mangroves generally emit little methane.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="15" />): researchers evaluate a wetland's climate role by its **balance** between stored carbon and emitted greenhouse gases, *not by storage size alone*.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="16" />): rewetting drained peat can increase methane release yet still benefit the climate long term.

# Asked

The conclusion that is **CERTAINLY** true based on the passage.

# Solution

Combining sentences (<InlineMath math="12" />), (<InlineMath math="14" />), and (<InlineMath math="15" />) proves that storage alone does not settle climate impact. Two wetlands with **equal carbon stocks** can differ in gas emissions and therefore **differ in their climate role**.


> Karena itu, para peneliti menilai peran sebuah lahan basah bagi iklim dari <Highlight>neracanya, yaitu perbandingan antara karbon yang ditimbunnya dan gas rumah kaca yang dilepaskannya</Highlight>, bukan dari besar simpanannya saja.
## Why the Other Four Conclusions Fail

Each distractor contradicts **specific evidence** in the passage.

- Largest carbon storage is not automatically best because sentence (<InlineMath math="15" />) rejects judging by storage alone.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="14" />) says mangroves generally produce *little* methane, not zero greenhouse gases.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="16" />) says rewetting can *raise* methane release rather than stopping emissions.
- Sentences (<InlineMath math="6" />) and (<InlineMath math="7" />) state peat takes thousands of years to build up, not a few years.

# Answer

**Two wetlands with the same amount of stored carbon can play different roles for the climate**.

(*Dua lahan basah yang simpanan karbonnya sama besar dapat berbeda perannya bagi iklim.*)

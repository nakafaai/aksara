_Hal itu_ ("that") points to something already mentioned. Usually the referent sits in the nearest sentence, but here the nearest sentence is the trap. The safe way is to **put each candidate into sentence (14)** and see whether the reason given there still makes sense.

Sentence (14) says: in mangrove forests, that mostly doesn't happen much _karena sulfat dalam air laut membuat mikroba lain lebih unggul daripada mikroba penghasil metana_ ("because sulfate in seawater gives other microbes the edge over methane-producing microbes"). That reason is about methane-producing microbes losing the competition. If those microbes lose, what drops is **the making of methane**.

The making of methane is in sentence (12): without oxygen, part of what's left from decomposing plant remains is turned into methane by certain microbes. That's what _hal itu_ refers to.

The other candidates don't fit the reason in sentence (14).

- Methane trapping heat is in sentence (13), the nearest sentence. But sulfate doesn't change how methane traps heat. Sulfate only affects which microbes win.
- Methane's short stay in the atmosphere also comes from sentence (13), and it has nothing to do with sulfate either.
- Decomposition of plant remains without oxygen is the closest trap. That decomposition still happens in mangrove forests. Sentence (14) even says other microbes have the edge, meaning other microbes do the work. Only the step that turns things into methane is rare.
- Carbon storage in sentence (11) does happen in mangrove forests, because sentence (1) counts mangrove forests as wetlands.

So _hal itu_ refers to microbes turning part of the products of decomposing plant remains into methane.

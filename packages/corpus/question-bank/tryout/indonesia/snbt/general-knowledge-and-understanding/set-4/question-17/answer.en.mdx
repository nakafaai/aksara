# Given

Sentences (<InlineMath math="12" />) through (<InlineMath math="14" />) of the **wetlands** passage run in sequence:

- Sentence (<InlineMath math="12" />): without oxygen, certain microbes convert part of the breakdown products of plant remains into methane.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="13" />): methane's heat-trapping strength and shorter atmospheric lifetime.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="14" />): in mangrove forests, *hal itu* (that) generally does not happen much because seawater sulfate lets other microbes outcompete methane-producing microbes.

# Asked

The referent of *hal itu* in sentence (<InlineMath math="14" />).

# Solution

The reason in sentence (<InlineMath math="14" />) states that **methane-producing microbes lose out** because of seawater sulfate. What happens less when those microbes lose is **microbial conversion of part of decomposed plant remains into methane** from sentence (<InlineMath math="12" />).


> Dalam keadaan tanpa oksigen, <Highlight>sebagian hasil penguraian sisa tumbuhan diubah oleh mikroba tertentu menjadi metana</Highlight>.
## Checking the Other Candidate Referents

Plugging any other option into sentence (<InlineMath math="14" />) breaks the **causal link with seawater sulfate**.

- Methane's heat trapping and short atmospheric lifetime (sentence <InlineMath math="13" />) are atmospheric properties unaffected by sulfate.
- Anaerobic decomposition itself still occurs in mangroves, carried out by the *other microbes* that outcompete methane producers.
- Carbon storage still happens in mangroves, which sentence (<InlineMath math="1" />) classifies as wetlands.

# Answer

The phrase *hal itu* refers to **the conversion of part of decomposed plant remains into methane by microbes**.

(*pengubahan sebagian hasil penguraian sisa tumbuhan menjadi metana oleh mikroba*)

A good paraphrase may change the order and the words, but not the content. So first break sentence (9) into **its pieces of content**, then check whether each piece is still whole in every option.

Sentence (9) holds four things.

1. What it's about: high ancestral property, with rice fields, farmland and the _rumah gadang_ (the big clan house) as examples.
2. The line of inheritance: from a mother to her daughters.
3. The owner: the lineage as a whole.
4. The limit: it can't be sold _sesuka hati_ ("at will").

The words _memang_ ("it's true that") and _tetapi_ ("but") show that the second point is granted first, then the third and fourth are stressed. The sentence that opens with _walaupun_ ("although") and uses _tetap_ ("still") keeps that relation. It also keeps all four points, and _semaunya_ means the same as _sesuka hati_.

Each of the other sentences changes exactly one thing.

- The sentence saying the property _menjadi milik anak perempuan yang menerimanya_ ("becomes the property of the daughter who receives it") changes the owner. In sentence (9), the owner is the lineage.
- The sentence saying _tidak boleh dijual sama sekali_ ("can't be sold at all") changes the limit. Not being sold at will means not being sold on one's own wish. That isn't a total ban.
- The sentence saying the property _boleh dijual oleh anak perempuan yang mewarisinya_ ("may be sold by the daughter who inherits it") flips that limit.
- The sentence opening with _semua harta keluarga Minangkabau_ ("all the property of Minangkabau families") widens what's being talked about. Sentence (15) names another kind of property, earned wealth, which doesn't follow this rule.

So the right paraphrase is the sentence saying that although it passes from a mother to her daughters, high ancestral property remains the shared property of the lineage and can't be sold at will.

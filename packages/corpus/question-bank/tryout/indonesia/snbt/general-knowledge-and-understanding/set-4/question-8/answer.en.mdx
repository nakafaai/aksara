The affix pair _ke-an_ has several meanings, so pin down what it means in sentence (8) first. It says the low moon _tampak kemerahan_ ("looks reddish"). The moon doesn't turn fully red. It's only **somewhat red**. So here _ke-an_ attaches to a colour adjective (_merah_, "red"), produces an adjective, and means "somewhat" or "having a touch of".

Now look for the sentence where _ke-an_ also means "somewhat". In the sentence about the wilting leaf, _kekuningan_ means yellowish, from _kuning_ ("yellow"). It's also a colour adjective describing the leaf. The meaning is the same.

The other four sentences use _ke-an_ with a different meaning.

- _Kebesaran_ for the shirt means too big, from _besar_ ("big"). The meaning is "too", not "somewhat".
- _Kemalaman_ means caught out by nightfall, from _malam_ ("night"). The meaning is going through something by accident.
- _Kemerahan_ in the sentence about skin is the closest trap, because the word is exactly the same. But there _kemerahan_ is a **noun**, the patch of skin that went red, and it's the subject that disappears. Same form, different meaning.
- _Kehilangan_ means suffering a loss, from _hilang_ ("lost"). Again the meaning is going through something unwanted.

A quick check: swap the word for _agak_ ("somewhat") plus its base. _Tampak agak merah_ and _tampak agak kuning_ still read naturally, while _agak besar untuk adikku_ ("somewhat big for my younger sibling") already changes what the sentence says.

So the same meaning is in the word _kekuningan_ in the sentence about the wilting leaf.

Literally, _terkunci_ ("locked") is used for a door or a chest that can't be opened. Carbon has no lock, so the word is figurative. What you need to find is **how strong the lock is according to the passage**: is the carbon held forever, or held for as long as a certain condition lasts?

Sentence (4) says plant remains pile up layer on layer, and the carbon is locked in with them. The reason is in sentences (2) and (3): standing water keeps oxygen out, so decomposition runs very slowly. So the lock is the standing water.

Sentence (9) shows what happens when that lock is opened. As soon as the water is drained, oxygen gets in, the decomposing microbes become active, and the carbon escapes into the air. That means the carbon is **held for as long as the pile stays waterlogged**, not held forever.

The other meanings don't fit the passage.

- "Stored forever and unable to escape again" is the closest trap. It fits sentence (4) alone, but sentences (8) to (10) contradict it by saying the store can shrink fast.
- "Shut tight so water can't get in" is too literal and the wrong way round. The pile is saturated with water, and it's the water that holds the carbon.
- "Bound to the water, so it flows away with it" swaps the water's role. Water keeps oxygen out. It doesn't carry the carbon off.
- "Its decomposition has stopped because the decomposing microbes are dead" goes beyond sentence (3), which only says decomposition is very slow. Sentence (9) also shows those microbes turn active again once there's oxygen.

So _terkunci_ in sentence (4) means held inside the pile of plant remains for as long as that pile stays waterlogged.

The word *perantara* comes from *antara* (between), something in the middle. In trade, a *perantara* is **a party in the middle of the chain**, buying from one side and selling to another.

Sentence (3) describes that chain: spices passed through many ports and traders, and the price rose each time they changed hands. Sentence (6) says the Europeans wanted to **cut out** these middlemen, buying straight from the source so they would not pay each trader's profit along the chain.

The other options do not fit:

- The first seller on the home island is the source, not the middle of the chain.
- The final buyer in Europe is at the end of the chain.
- Farmers grow the spices rather than standing between buyer and seller.
- Sailors run the ships, but *perantara* in sentence (6) refers to traders who profit from buying and selling.

So *perantara* refers to the party that connects seller and buyer and takes a profit.

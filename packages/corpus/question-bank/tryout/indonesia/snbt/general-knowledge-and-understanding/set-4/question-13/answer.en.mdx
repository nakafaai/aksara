# Given

Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" /> of the **Minangkabau** passage follows a clear three-part structure:

- Sentences (<InlineMath math="7" />) and (<InlineMath math="8" />): reject the misconception that matriliny equals matriarchy.
- Sentences (<InlineMath math="9" />) to (<InlineMath math="12" />): give proof that clan property is overseen by the *mamak*, leadership titles go to nephews, and husbands do not lead their wives' clans.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="13" />): summarizes that descent follows women, while clan leadership is held by men from that lineage.

# Asked

The main idea of paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Solution

The main idea combines the **rejection of matriarchy with sentence (<InlineMath math="13" />)**: descent follows the female line, yet authority to lead the clan belongs to men of that line, so the system is not a matriarchy.


> Dengan kata lain, <Highlight>garis keturunan mengikuti perempuan, tetapi wewenang memimpin kaum dipegang laki-laki dari garis keturunan itu sendiri</Highlight>.
## Ruling Out the Other Options

Each distractor narrows the paragraph to **one minor detail** or contradicts a sentence.

- Pointing only to communal property ignores male clan leadership in sentences (<InlineMath math="10" />) and (<InlineMath math="13" />).
- Claiming husbands lead their wives' clans contradicts sentence (<InlineMath math="12" />).
- Claiming power is split equally invents female personal control over communal property.
- Mentioning only the misconception captures sentence (<InlineMath math="7" />) without the paragraph's actual argument.

# Answer

**In the Minangkabau matrilineal system, descent follows women, yet authority to lead the clan is held by men from that line, so it is not a matriarchy**.

(*Dalam sistem matrilineal Minangkabau, garis keturunan mengikuti perempuan, tetapi wewenang memimpin kaum dipegang laki-laki dari garis itu, sehingga sistem ini bukan matriarkat.*)

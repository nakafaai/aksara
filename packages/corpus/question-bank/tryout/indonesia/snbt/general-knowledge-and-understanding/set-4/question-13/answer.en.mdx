A main idea is one statement that **every** sentence in the paragraph explains. A statement that covers only part of the paragraph, or adds something that isn't there, isn't the main idea.

Look at how the second paragraph is built.

1. Sentences (7) and (8) open with a misconception and reject it: this system isn't a matriarchy.
2. Sentences (9) to (12) give the evidence. High ancestral property does pass through women, but the _mamak_ (the mother's brother) oversees it. The title of lineage head passes from a _mamak_ to his sister's son. A husband doesn't lead his wife's lineage.
3. Sentence (13) sums up with _dengan kata lain_ ("in other words"): descent follows the women, but the authority to lead the lineage is held by men from that same line of descent.

The statement that holds all three parts is the main idea: descent follows the women, authority to lead is held by men of that line, and so the system isn't a matriarchy.

The other four statements each miss at one point.

- The statement about shared property gives **the wrong reason**. The system isn't a matriarchy because the lineage's leaders are men, as in sentences (10) and (13). Shared ownership is just one detail in sentence (9).
- The statement that includes husbands clashes with sentence (12). A husband doesn't lead his wife's lineage. The leaders are men from the line of descent itself, the _mamak_.
- The statement about power being split evenly adds something that isn't there. Women don't control the property, because it belongs to the lineage and the _mamak_ oversees it.
- The statement about the misconception only covers sentence (7). That's the paragraph's opening, not what the paragraph proves.

So the main idea is that descent follows the women, but the authority to lead the lineage is held by men of that line, which is why the system isn't a matriarchy.

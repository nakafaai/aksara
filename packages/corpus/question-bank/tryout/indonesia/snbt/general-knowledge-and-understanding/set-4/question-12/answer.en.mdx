The word _luwes_ is mostly used for people: someone moves gracefully, or gets along with others easily. Sentence (14) uses it for a **system**, so those everyday meanings don't fit. The intended meaning only becomes clear once you read the sentences after it, because sentences (15) to (18) explain sentence (14).

Follow the explanation.

1. Sentence (16): in the older customary view, the wealth a man gathered was often seen as going back to his mother's lineage.
2. Sentence (17): today earned wealth is generally divided among the children under Islamic inheritance law. So something has changed.
3. Sentence (18): that change _tidak menggeser dasar sistemnya_ ("doesn't shift the base of the system"). A child still joins the mother's clan, and high ancestral property still doesn't pass to a father's children.

Two things hold at once: the system **takes in change**, yet **its base stays**. That's what _luwes_ means here: able to adapt without leaving its base behind.

Two other options look close, but each holds only one of those things.

- "Easily changed right down to its base" catches the change, but clashes with sentence (18), which stresses that the base doesn't shift.
- "Loose, so its rules may be broken" takes flexible to mean not firm. The passage mentions no rule-breaking. What changed is the rule for earned wealth, and that change itself follows another rule, Islamic inheritance law.

The remaining two options are meanings of _luwes_ for people: graceful movement and being easy to get along with. A kinship system doesn't move and doesn't socialise.

So _luwes_ in sentence (14) means able to adapt without leaving its base behind.

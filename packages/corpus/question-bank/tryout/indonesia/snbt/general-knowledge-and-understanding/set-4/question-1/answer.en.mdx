The word _pasti_ ("certainly") asks for a statement the passage guarantees. A statement that's only possibly true, or that goes further than the passage, has to go. Here the guarantee only appears when you **put two sentences together**.

Read sentences (14) and (15) side by side.

1. Sentence (14): in any fish, salt quickly makes the outer layer of the flesh taste salty.
2. Sentence (15): salt moves towards the middle slowly, so the middle of a thick fish can start to rot before the salt gets there.

Together they say this: a salty outer layer only tells you about the outside. In a thick fish, the middle may not have been reached by the salt yet and may already be rotting. So a salty taste on the outside doesn't guarantee a safe middle. The words _belum tentu_ ("not necessarily") keep the statement from overreaching.

The other four statements fall to sentences that sit far from the ones that tempt you.

- Sentence (18) does mention drying, but sentences (9) and (10) say many bacteria stay alive and can multiply fast again if the salted fish takes up water again.
- Sentence (17) only reports that salt rich in calcium and magnesium slows the salt's way in. The passage doesn't say salt without those compounds _always_ keeps fish longer.
- According to sentences (11) and (12), the reddish spots come from microbes that actually tolerate high salt, on fish stored too long in a warm place. Too little salt isn't the cause.
- Sentence (8) says salt dampens the bacteria's activity, and sentence (9) says some bacteria still grow. Less free water isn't the same as dead bacteria.

So what's certainly true is that a thick fish whose outer layer already tastes salty isn't necessarily safe from rotting in the middle.

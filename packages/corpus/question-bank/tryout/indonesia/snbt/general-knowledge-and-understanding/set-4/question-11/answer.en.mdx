# Given

The **Minangkabau matrilineal system** passage lays out linked kinship rules:

- Sentences (<InlineMath math="5" />) and (<InlineMath math="6" />): a child joins the mother's clan at birth, and custom forbids marriage between two people of the same clan.
- Sentences (<InlineMath math="9" />) and (<InlineMath math="10" />): ancestral property (*harta pusaka tinggi*) belongs to the clan and is overseen by the *mamak* (mother's brother).

# Asked

# Solution

Each claim requires **combining two sentences** so the kinship chain is verified without skipping a rule.


> Combine two linked customary rules before judging any statement about clan membership, titles, or property types.
## Evaluating All Four Statements

Combining the linked rules across the passage gives these results.

| Statement | Combined Textual Evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In a customary marriage, a child never shares the father's clan | Sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />) bars same-clan marriage and sentence (<InlineMath math="5" />) places the child in the mother's clan | **Consistent** (*Sesuai*) |
| A man can now pass his *penghulu* title to his own son | Sentence (<InlineMath math="11" />) passes titles to nephews, and sentence (<InlineMath math="17" />) changes only earned property | **Inconsistent** (*Tidak sesuai*) |

Statements <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> are **Consistent** (*Sesuai*), while statements <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> are **Inconsistent** (*Tidak sesuai*).

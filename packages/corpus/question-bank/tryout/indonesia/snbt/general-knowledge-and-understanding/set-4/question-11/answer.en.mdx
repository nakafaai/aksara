Here you judge four statements one at a time. None of them can be settled from a single sentence. Each one needs **two sentences of the passage put together**, and then you check whether that combination supports the statement or rules it out. A statement is Sesuai ("consistent") when the passage supports it, and Tidak sesuai ("not consistent") when the passage contradicts it or doesn't support it.

**Statement 1**: Sesuai. The words *tidak pernah* ("never") make it sound too absolute, but follow the chain.

1. Sentence (6): custom forbids marriage between two people of the same *suku* (clan). So in a marriage that follows custom, the father and the mother are always from different clans.
2. Sentence (5): a child becomes a member of the mother's clan at birth, not the father's.
3. Put them together: the child's clan is the mother's clan, and the mother's clan differs from the father's. So the child's clan is always different from the father's.

The condition *mengikuti adat* ("following custom") at the start of the statement is what makes *tidak pernah* correct.

**Statement 2**: Tidak sesuai. Sentence (11) says the *penghulu* title (the title of the lineage leader) passes from a *mamak* (mother's brother) to his *kemenakan*, his sister's son, not to his own son. The word *kini* ("now") pulls you toward the third paragraph, because sentence (17) does describe a change. But that change only concerns *harta pencaharian* (property a couple earns themselves), and sentence (18) says the base of the system hasn't moved: a child still joins the mother's clan. A *penghulu*'s son belongs to his mother's clan, so he isn't the next leader of his father's lineage. The passage never says the way the title passes has changed.

**Statement 3**: Tidak sesuai. This one is a trap because sentence (9) names the *rumah gadang* (the great family house) as an example of *harta pusaka tinggi* (ancestral lineage property). But sentence (15) defines *harta pencaharian* as property a married couple gets from their own work. A house a couple builds with their own earnings fits that definition, so it's *harta pencaharian*, not *harta pusaka tinggi*. What decides the category is **where the property came from**, not what kind of object it is.

**Statement 4**: Sesuai. It sounds odd, yet two sentences support it.

1. Sentence (10): the lineage property is overseen by the *mamak*, the mother's brother. So a man helps oversee the property of his mother's lineage.
2. Sentence (18): the *harta pusaka tinggi* of a father's lineage still doesn't pass to his children.

The man who acts as *mamak* can also be a father. The property he oversees belongs to his own lineage, and it doesn't pass to his children, because his children join his wife's clan.

So statements 1 and 4 are consistent with the passage, while statements 2 and 3 are not.

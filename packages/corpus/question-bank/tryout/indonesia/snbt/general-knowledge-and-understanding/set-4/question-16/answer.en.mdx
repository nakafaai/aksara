In sentence (3), the melting ice *menyerap* heat from its surroundings, including from the tube holding the mixture. So heat **moves from the tube into the ice**. The ice takes that heat, and the tube and mixture get colder. The best meaning is *mengambil* (to take).

Sentence (4) confirms this with *Akibatnya* (as a result): the ice and salt become very cold because they keep taking heat from around them.

The other options do not fit:

- *Memancarkan* (to emit) means giving off, the opposite direction of heat flow in sentence (3).
- *Menolak* (to reject) is also the opposite, since heat goes into the ice.
- *Menghasilkan* (to produce) means making something new, but the ice does not make heat. The heat is already in the tube and moves into the ice.
- *Menaikkan* (to raise) does not fit the object "heat from its surroundings", and the tube's temperature actually falls.

So *menyerap* in sentence (3) means to take in.

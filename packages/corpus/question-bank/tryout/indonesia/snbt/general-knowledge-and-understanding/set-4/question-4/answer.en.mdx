# Given

The reasoning in sentences (<InlineMath math="13" />) through (<InlineMath math="16" />) of the **fish salting** passage flows as follows:

- Sentence (<InlineMath math="13" />): salting takes time.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="14" />): on any fish, salt quickly makes the outer flesh taste salty.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="15" />): salt moves slowly inward, so the center of thick fish can start spoiling before salt arrives.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="16" />): large fish are split or scored to shorten the *distance* salt must travel.

# Asked

The one statement that does **NOT** align (**EXCEPT**) with the reasoning in sentences (<InlineMath math="13" />) to (<InlineMath math="16" />).

# Solution

The obstacle in thick fish is **longer travel distance**, not a slower speed of salt inside large fish compared to small fish. Splitting a fish shortens distance rather than altering diffusion speed.


> Oleh sebab itu, ikan besar biasanya dibelah atau disayat lebih dahulu agar <Highlight>jarak yang harus ditempuh garam lebih pendek</Highlight>.
## Confirming the Four Aligned Statements

Each remaining option follows directly from the **distance-and-time logic** of sentences (<InlineMath math="13" />) to (<InlineMath math="16" />).

- A salty outer surface does not guarantee salt has reached the core (sentences <InlineMath math="14" /> and <InlineMath math="15" />).
- The farther the center lies from the surface, the longer salt takes to reach it (sentences <InlineMath math="15" /> and <InlineMath math="16" />).
- Splitting large fish lets salt reach all flesh faster by shortening distance (sentence <InlineMath math="16" />).
- Proper salting can still fail if insufficient time is allowed (sentences <InlineMath math="13" /> and <InlineMath math="15" />).

# Answer

**The center of a thick fish can spoil because salt moves more slowly inside large fish flesh than inside small fish flesh**.

The word _nyata_ has more than one meaning. It can mean "clearly visible", and it can mean "really there, not just an impression". Its opposite depends on which meaning is in play, so settle what _nyata_ means in sentence (17) first.

Sentence (17) says the moon's size can also change _secara nyata_ ("for real"), but for a different reason. The words _juga_ ("also") and _sebenarnya_ ("actually") set it against the earlier part, the illusion. In the illusion, the moon only **feels** bigger, while photos show its size doesn't grow. In sentence (18), the change caused by the orbit really happens, and a camera records it. So _nyata_ here means "really happening".

Sentence (19) shuts the door on the other meaning. That difference is _tidak mudah disadari_ ("not easy to notice"). Something that's hard to notice can't be called clearly visible. So _nyata_ in sentence (17) doesn't mean "clear".

The opposite of "really happening" is _semu_ ("apparent", "illusory"): it looks as if it's there, but it isn't. That's exactly what the moon illusion is.

- _Samar_ ("faint") and _kabur_ ("blurred") are opposites of _nyata_ in the sense "clearly visible". Sentence (19) has already ruled that sense out.
- _Gaib_ ("supernatural", "unseen") is about things beyond the ordinary world. The passage isn't about that.
- _Kecil_ ("small") would fit if _nyata_ were read as "large" or "significant". The contrast in the passage is between really happening and only felt, not between large and small.

So the opposite of _nyata_ that fits sentence (17) is _semu_.

# Given

**The word *nyata*** in sentence (17) means genuinely occurring rather than merely apparent, while sentence (19) notes the shift is hard to notice.

# Asked

The sentence whose italicized word is the antonym of *nyata* in sentence (17).

# Solution

> Lock in the sense of *nyata* first: **because the change is hard to see yet genuinely happening, it means *real*, so its true opposite is *semu* (apparent/illusory), not *samar* (faint)**.

The word *nyata* has two close meanings with different opposites. Meaning "clearly visible", its opposite is *samar* ("faint"). Meaning "really happening, not just an impression", its opposite is *semu* ("apparent"). So first settle which meaning sentence (17) uses.

| Meaning of *nyata* | Its opposite | Fits sentences (17) to (19)? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| clearly visible | *samar* | no, sentence (19) says the change is hard to notice |
| really happening, not an impression | *semu* | yes, it is set against the moon illusion |

Sentence (17) says the moon's size *sebenarnya* ("actually") can *juga* ("also") change *secara nyata*, but for another reason. Those two words set it against the illusion described earlier, where the moon only feels bigger while photos show its size unchanged. Sentence (19) adds that this orbital change is hard to notice, so *nyata* here cannot mean clearly visible. **It means really happening**, and its opposite is something that only seems to happen.

The sun's daily path from east to west is exactly that. The sun looks as if it moves, but it is the Earth that turns, so the motion is called *semu*. That is the opposite of *nyata* in sentence (17).

Fog that makes the coast look *samar* does oppose *nyata*, but in the "clearly visible" sense that sentence (19) has ruled out. A *gaib* creature is the opposite of something visible and natural, which the passage never discusses. A price rise that feels *nyata* uses the same word in the sense "plainly felt", so it is no opposite at all. Water that is *jernih* ("clear") even agrees with the "clearly visible" sense.

# Answer

*Nyata* in sentence (17) is opposite in meaning to *semu*.

# Given

Sentences (<InlineMath math="3" />) and (<InlineMath math="4" />) of the **urban heat island** passage describe heat retention:

- Sentence (<InlineMath math="3" />): during the day, asphalt, concrete, and roofs absorb large amounts of solar heat.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="4" />): at night, that stored heat is released slowly into the air so the city cools slowly.

# Asked

Why cities cool down slowly at night according to sentences (<InlineMath math="3" />) and (<InlineMath math="4" />).

# Solution

The cause-and-effect chain in sentences (<InlineMath math="3" />) and (<InlineMath math="4" />) directly attributes slow nighttime cooling to **the gradual release of daytime heat absorbed by asphalt and concrete**.

> Pada malam hari, <Highlight>panas yang tersimpan itu dilepaskan perlahan ke udara</Highlight> sehingga kota lambat menjadi sejuk.

## Checking the Other Options

Each distractor **contradicts the mechanism** stated in the passage.

- Sunshine duration is never compared between city centers and outskirts.
- Asphalt and concrete absorb heat rather than reflecting all of it.
- Leaf evaporation cools the air (sentence <InlineMath math="5" />) rather than warming it.
- Outskirts are cooler, and no warm nighttime breeze from the outskirts is mentioned.

# Answer

Cities cool slowly at night because **heat absorbed by asphalt and concrete during the day is released slowly**.

(*panas yang diserap aspal dan beton pada siang hari dilepaskan perlahan*)

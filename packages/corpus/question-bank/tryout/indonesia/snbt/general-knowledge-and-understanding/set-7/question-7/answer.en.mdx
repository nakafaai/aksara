Sentence (7) describes two directions of travel on the same river. Young eels go up to the *hulu*, while adult eels go down to the sea. So **hulu here is the stretch of river near its source**, and the opposite stretch, toward the estuary and the sea, is called *hilir* ("downstream"). Settlements in the *hilir* of a river flooded by water sent down from above use the word in the same river sense.

| Eel | Direction on the river | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| young | up, against the current | the *hulu*, near the source |
| adult | down, with the current | the sea, through the *hilir* and estuary |

The slipperiest trap is the *hulu* of a keris. The word is the same, but there it means the hilt of the dagger, and its opposite is the tip of the blade, not a part of the river. A *mata air* ("spring") on a mountain slope is part of the upstream itself, where the river begins. The *puncak* ("summit") of a hill is the highest point of land, whose opposite is its foot. The *tepian* ("bank") of a river is its edge, which is opposite to no direction of flow.

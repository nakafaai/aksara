The passage itself tells you what *tradisional* means in sentence (1). Sentence (4) explains that a pinisi is built by craftsmen led by a panrita lopi, without written plans, from knowledge handed down. So **traditional here means made by inherited methods**, and its opposite is the use of present-day technology, *modern*. The steel ships built with *modern* technology in a Surabaya shipyard are the right opposite.

The other choices tempt because they sit close to the passage's mood. A *kuno* ("ancient") method comes from the past, so it points the same way as traditional, not against it. An *adat* ceremony ("customary") and a *lokal* product ("local") are only neighbours of traditional: custom is a practice kept alive, local means from the area, and their opposites are different, foreign or imported. A *sederhana* ("simple") tool can be perfectly modern, so simplicity is not the opposite of an inherited method.

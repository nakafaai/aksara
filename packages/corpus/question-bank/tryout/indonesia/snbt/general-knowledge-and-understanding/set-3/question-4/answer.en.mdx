This question isn't just asking what one word means. You have to compare the meanings of *perumpamaan* ("comparison") and *pengandaian* ("supposition"), then judge what happens to the whole of sentence (12) when one replaces the other.

Step one: pin down the sense of *perumpamaan* in sentence (12). KBBI, the official Indonesian dictionary, lists two senses: "perbandingan" or "ibarat" (a comparison, a likeness), and "peribahasa yang berupa perbandingan" (a proverb built on a comparison). Sentence (12) says the term "efek rumah kaca" (greenhouse effect) for the atmosphere is only a *perumpamaan*, then explains the difference right away: the atmosphere warms because gases absorb and re-emit infrared, while its air stays free to move. So the atmosphere is **being compared with a greenhouse**, and the comparison isn't perfect. The atmosphere's warming itself is still real.

Step two: pin down the sense of *pengandaian*. KBBI defines it as the act of supposing, a supposition, something only imagined. Put it in the sentence and you get "istilah 'efek rumah kaca' ... sebenarnya hanya sebuah pengandaian." The sentence no longer compares two things. It now says the greenhouse effect in the atmosphere is only supposed, not real. Check this against the part after the colon: the passage describes gases that really do absorb infrared, so the new meaning clearly differs from what the writer meant.

The other options are wrong for these reasons.

- A fixed saying handed down through generations is what a *peribahasa* (proverb) is, the second sense of *perumpamaan*. That effect would come from replacing the word with *peribahasa*, not *pengandaian*.
- The reason "both describe something not meant literally" mixes up two things. A comparison doesn't use the literal sense, but the things compared are still real. A supposition describes something that truly isn't real. That's why the meaning changes.
- The root-word reason is a trap too. *Umpama* can mean "suppose," but the derived word used in sentence (12), *perumpamaan*, means a comparison. Sharing a root doesn't guarantee that derived words share a meaning.
- Treating the atmosphere as an example of a greenhouse is the effect of the word *contoh* ("example"), not *pengandaian*.

So if *perumpamaan* is replaced with *pengandaian*, sentence (12) no longer compares the atmosphere with a greenhouse. Instead it says the greenhouse effect in the atmosphere is only supposed and not real.

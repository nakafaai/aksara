This question asks for the one sentence that uses *membalas* with a **different** meaning from sentence (5). The other four use the same meaning even though their settings vary, so test the meaning, not the shape of the sentence.

Step one: pin down the meaning of *membalas* in sentence (5). The bacteria take nitrogen gas and turn it into compounds the plant can use. That's a service the bacteria do for the plant. The plant then *membalasnya* (pays them back) with food made by photosynthesis. So the plant gives something in return for a service it has already received. KBBI, the official Indonesian dictionary, records this sense as giving or paying something in return for a service already received.

Step two: for each sentence, ask what is being answered or repaid. Is it a service or kindness already received, or something else?

| Sentence | What is repaid or answered | Sense of *membalas* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| repays a teacher's service by caring for them | the teacher's service | repaying a service |
| the forest repays the villagers with springs | the villagers' care | repaying a service |
| repays a delivery of seedlings with harvest | the seedlings received | repaying a service |
| repays the neighbors' help with part of the harvest | the neighbors' help | repaying a service |
| answers a friend's message with a short thank-you | the friend's message | replying |

The last sentence is a trap because it contains a kindness, being treated to lunch, and a thank-you. But what gets *membalas* in that sentence is the friend's **message**, not the lunch. Answering a message means replying to it, and KBBI lists that as a separate sense, "menjawab (surat dan sebagainya)" (to answer a letter and the like). The thank-you is simply what the reply says.

Three other sentences can also make you hesitate.

- The forest sentence is figurative, since a forest doesn't really give anything. Sentence (5) is figurative too, since a plant doesn't really intend to repay. The meaning is still repaying a service: the villagers' care is repaid with springs.
- The seedling sentence sounds like answering a delivery. But the seedlings are a useful gift, and the harvest is given in return.
- The teacher and neighbor sentences are the clearest, because *jasa* ("service") and *bantuan* ("help") name the kindness received directly.

So the sentence that uses *membalas* with a different meaning is the one about the person who answers a friend's message with a thank-you after being treated to lunch.

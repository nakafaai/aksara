You need to judge four statements about how a parked car's cabin heats up. None of them can be decided from a single sentence. Each one needs two or more sentences combined.

Start by laying out the path the energy takes in the first paragraph, then add the experiment's finding from the third paragraph.

| Stage | What happens | Sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Visible light and short-wave infrared pass through the glass. | (2) |
| 2 | The dashboard, seats, and steering wheel absorb that light. | (2) |
| 3 | The warmed objects give off long-wave infrared. | (4) |
| 4 | The glass absorbs most of the long-wave infrared and sends part of it back into the cabin. | (5) |

Here's the experiment's finding. The box with a rock-salt cover, which lets infrared through, still climbed far above the outside air temperature and ended up almost as hot as the glass-covered box (sentences 9 and 10). That's why many physicists judge that blocked air exchange adds more heating than the glass's property does, in greenhouses and in cars alike (sentence 11).

Statement 1 is *sesuai* (consistent with the text), even though it sounds wrong. The popular explanation makes you expect that a cabin wouldn't get hot without glass that traps infrared. But the rock-salt box already showed that even a cover that lets infrared through still leaves the box **far hotter than the outside air**. Sentence (11) applies that finding to cars, and sentence (8) gives the reason: a closed cabin cuts off the air exchange. So a cabin that stays closed can still get far hotter even if its glass is replaced.

Statement 2 is *tidak sesuai* (not consistent with the text), even though it sounds right. By stage 1, the short-wave infrared that arrives with sunlight goes straight through the glass. The glass only holds back the long-wave infrared from objects inside the cabin, which is stage 4. The "both ... and" wording stretches the glass's property to two kinds of infrared, while sentences (2) and (5) give it to only one.

Statement 3 is *tidak sesuai*. Its first half is right, because sentence (14) says opening the window a little helps only a little. The reason, though, is backwards. Sentence (14) suggests the gap is too narrow for meaningful air exchange, and sentence (11) rates air exchange as the bigger factor. So air exchange matters a lot. A narrow gap just can't bring back enough of it.

Statement 4 is *sesuai*. Sentence (15) says the windshield shade reflects part of the light back out before it can be absorbed. In the table, long-wave infrared only appears at stage 3, after the light has been absorbed (sentence 4). So the shade acts after the light has come through the glass but before the dashboard absorbs it, which is **before the energy is re-emitted as long-wave infrared**. A quick check: the reflected light is still light, and sentence (2) says light passes through glass, so it really can get back out.

So statements 1 and 4 match the passage, and statements 2 and 3 don't.

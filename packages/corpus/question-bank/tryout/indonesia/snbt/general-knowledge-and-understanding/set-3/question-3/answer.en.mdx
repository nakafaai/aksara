# Given

Sentence (<InlineMath math="9" />) compares a glass-covered box (blocking infrared) with a rock-salt-covered box (transparent to infrared). Sentence (<InlineMath math="10" />) reports both heated far above outside air to **nearly the same temperature**, and sentence (<InlineMath math="11" />) starts with ***Hal itu*** ("that").

# Asked

What does the phrase *hal itu* in sentence (<InlineMath math="11" />) refer to?

# Solution

> Pada awal abad ke-<InlineMath math="20" />, sebuah percobaan membandingkan dua kotak yang dijemur bersama: satu bertutup kaca, satu lagi bertutup lempeng garam batu, bahan yang tembus inframerah. Suhu kedua kotak dilaporkan naik jauh di atas suhu udara luar dan hampir sama satu sama lain.

Combine the **experimental contrast** in sentence (<InlineMath math="9" />) with the **temperature outcome** in sentence (<InlineMath math="10" />).

## Combining setup and result

One lid blocked infrared while the rock-salt lid was <Highlight>tembus inframerah</Highlight> ("transparent to infrared"), yet both sealed boxes reached <Highlight>hampir sama satu sama lain</Highlight> ("nearly the same temperature"). Thus *hal itu* refers to the **finding that trapping or passing infrared barely changed the sealed boxes' temperature**, prompting physicists to credit blocked air exchange.

The distractors contradict the experiment: glass did not get far hotter, rock salt truly transmits infrared, "no effect at all" overstates *hampir sama* ("nearly the same"), and both boxes grew far hotter than outside air.

# Answer

*Hal itu* refers to **the finding that blocking or transmitting infrared barely changes the temperature of a closed box** (*hasil bahwa menahan atau meloloskan inframerah hampir tidak mengubah suhu kotak yang tertutup*).

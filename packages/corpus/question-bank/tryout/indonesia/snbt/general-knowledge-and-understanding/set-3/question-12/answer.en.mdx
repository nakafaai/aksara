This **KECUALI** ("except") question asks for the one statement that doesn't fit the figure of speech in sentence (14): *Gambar menjadi jangkar, sedangkan tuturan menjadi tali yang dapat diulur dan ditarik* ("The picture becomes the anchor, while the narration becomes the rope that can be let out and pulled in"). There are two steps. First, understand the figure. Second, test each statement against the figure and against the sentence of the text it rests on.

Step 1: understand the figure. An anchor is a weight dropped from a ship so the ship stays near one point and doesn't drift away. The anchor rope can be let out or pulled in, so the ship's room to move can be adjusted, but the point itself doesn't shift. Sentence (14) comes after sentences (11) to (13), which describe the limits on the *dalang*, the storyteller. So the mapping looks like this.

| Part of the figure | What it stands for | Basis in the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anchor (doesn't shift) | The order of scenes and the look of the painted figures and events | Sentences (12) and (13) |
| Rope (let out, pulled in) | The length of the narration and what isn't painted, such as events before and after the painted moment and dialogue | Sentences (7), (8), and (9) |

Step 2: test each statement.

- A battle scene narrated much longer than a journey scene. That's about narration length, the rope. Sentence (8) lets the *dalang* narrate at length or briefly according to the scene's weight. It fits.
- A meeting scene can't come first if the farewell painting comes before it on the scroll. That's about order, the anchor. Sentence (12) says the order of scenes is locked by the order of paintings. It fits, even though it sounds like a restriction.
- Dialogue that isn't painted may be narrated. The figures' words aren't painted, so nothing on the anchor changes. Sentence (7) even says dialogue between figures is part of what the *dalang* narrates. It fits.
- The same scene can feel slow in one show and fast in another. That's narration length again, by sentences (8) and (9). It fits.
- An event moved to another scene as long as the order of sections stays the same. This keeps the order of sections, as sentence (12) requires, but **moving an event changes how the painted events go**. Sentence (13) says the *dalang* isn't free to do that. So this statement shifts the anchor while pretending only to let out the rope.

The main trap is that last statement: the condition "as long as the order stays the same" makes it sound safe, yet the anchor locks two things, the order and the content of the pictures. The statement about the meeting and the farewell sounds like an odd restriction, but it's exactly what sentence (12) says.

So the statement that doesn't fit the anchor and rope is that the *dalang* may move an event to another scene as long as the order of sections isn't changed.

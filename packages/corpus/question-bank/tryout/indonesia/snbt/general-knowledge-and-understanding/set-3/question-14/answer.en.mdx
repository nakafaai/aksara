# Given

Sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />) says painted figures ***membeku dalam satu sikap*** ("freeze in a single pose"), leaving the narrator's voice to bring the story to life. Five sentences offer italicised candidate antonyms.

# Asked

Which sentence contains an italicised word opposite in meaning to *membeku* in sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />)?

# Solution

> Tokoh-tokoh di dalam lukisan membeku dalam satu sikap, sehingga tugas menghidupkan cerita jatuh ke suara dalang.

Pin down the **figurative sense of *membeku*** here (static, motionless in one physical pose) before testing each option's exact sense.

## Testing the semantic domain of each antonym

| Option sentence | Meaning of italicised word | Opposite of static pose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ice begins *mencair* ("to melt") | Solid turning to liquid | **No** (physical state) |
| Dancer finally *bergerak* ("moves") after drumbeat | <Highlight>Changing physical posture</Highlight>, no longer still | **Yes** |
| Residents *bergerak* ("mobilise") to clean drains | Starting collective action | **No** (civic action) |
| Village head's stance *melunak* ("softens") | Attitude becoming less rigid | **No** (opinion) |
| Frozen bank funds *dicairkan* ("unfrozen") | Money released for withdrawal | **No** (finance) |

Notice the twin trap with *bergerak*: only the **dancer** sentence uses it for physical movement out of stillness, directly opposing painted figures <Highlight>membeku dalam satu sikap</Highlight>.

# Answer

The matching antonym appears in: **The dancer only *moved* (*bergerak*) after the drum was struck for the third time** (*Penari itu baru bergerak setelah kendang ditabuh untuk ketiga kalinya.*).

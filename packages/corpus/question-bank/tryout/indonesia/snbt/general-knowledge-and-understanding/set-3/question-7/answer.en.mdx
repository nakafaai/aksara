# Given

Sentence (<InlineMath math="11" />) says soybeans' ***sumbangan bersih*** ("net contribution") to soil is often smaller than expected. Sentences (<InlineMath math="12" />) to (<InlineMath math="14" />) explain that nitrogen hauled away in harvested seeds can exceed nitrogen fixed from air, depleting soil nitrogen.

# Asked

What does the word *bersih* signify in *sumbangan bersih* in sentence (<InlineMath math="11" />)?

# Solution

> Jika nitrogen yang terangkut bersama biji lebih banyak daripada nitrogen yang ditambat bakteri dari udara, penanaman kedelai justru dapat mengurangi persediaan nitrogen tanah.

Use the **inflow-minus-outflow calculation** in sentence (<InlineMath math="14" />) to identify KBBI's accounting sense of *bersih* ("net").

## Identifying the net accounting sense

Because soybeans can actually <Highlight>mengurangi persediaan nitrogen tanah</Highlight> ("reduce soil nitrogen") when seed export exceeds biological fixation, the contribution is measured **after subtracting what leaves in the harvest**. That is the *net* sense of *bersih*.

The distractors use senses ruled out by sentence (<InlineMath math="14" />): pure airborne nitrogen alone could never be negative, urea is a separate factor in sentence (<InlineMath math="15" />), pollution is not discussed, and plants trade food to bacteria rather than acting out of selfless generosity.

# Answer

The word *bersih* signifies that the contribution is **calculated after subtracting the nitrogen carried off during harvest** (*dihitung setelah nitrogen yang ikut terangkut keluar saat panen dikurangkan*).

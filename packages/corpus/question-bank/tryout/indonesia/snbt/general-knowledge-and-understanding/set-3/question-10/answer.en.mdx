Choose Ya (yes) or Tidak (no). Separate **what triggers a tsunami** from **what signals that one is coming**. A trigger is an event that pushes the water column so a wave forms.

| Event | Triggers a tsunami? | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An earthquake that suddenly lifts the seabed | Yes | Sentence (2): a tsunami forms when the seabed rises or drops suddenly. |
| A large undersea landslide | Yes | Sentence (5) names it as another trigger besides earthquakes. |
| The sea receding | No | Sentences (8) and (9): the water recedes **before the wave arrives** and is a natural sign, not a cause. |
| A very deep earthquake that barely changes the seabed | No | Sentence (4): such earthquakes usually do not change the seabed enough, and that change is what triggers a tsunami. |

Receding water is a trap because it appears together with a tsunami. But it is part of the wave already on its way, so the passage treats it as a sign, not a trigger.

So an earthquake that lifts the seabed and an undersea landslide can trigger a tsunami, while receding water and a very deep earthquake that barely changes the seabed cannot.

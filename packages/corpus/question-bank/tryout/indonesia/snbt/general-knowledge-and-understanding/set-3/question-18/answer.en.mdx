Every statement here needs **two sentences or one calculation** to decide. Two statements sound odd but are consistent, and two sound reasonable but aren't. A statement the text doesn't support counts as *Tidak sesuai* ("not consistent").

Statement 1 is *Sesuai* ("consistent"). Sentence (5) says part of the SHU, the residual operating surplus, is shared in proportion to each member's contribution, such as the size of their savings **and** loans. So savings count too, not only loans. Sentence (2) says every member pays principal savings and mandatory savings. That means a member who never borrows still has savings that count, and can still get a share of the SHU.

Statement 2 is *Sesuai*, even though many people assume savings interest always compounds. Sentence (8) says only interest *that isn't withdrawn* is added to the balance. If the interest is taken out every month and nothing new is deposited, the balance stays at the first deposit, so each month's interest is calculated on the same amount. Sentence (9) says simple interest is always calculated on the first deposit. It's the same situation.

Statement 3 is *Tidak sesuai*, even though it sounds reasonable because the gap is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100" /> after two months. Work out month three. Sentence (10) gives the compound balance after two months, <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}020{,}100" />, and by sentence (8) the third month's interest is calculated on that balance:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{compound balance} &= 1{,}020{,}100 + 1\% \times 1{,}020{,}100 = 1{,}020{,}100 + 10{,}201 = 1{,}030{,}301 \\ \text{simple balance} &= 1{,}000{,}000 + 3 \times 10{,}000 = 1{,}030{,}000 \\ \text{difference} &= 1{,}030{,}301 - 1{,}030{,}000 = 301 \end{aligned}" />

The difference is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}301" />, not exactly <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}300" />. The trap is assuming the gap grows by the same amount every month, while sentence (11) says the gap keeps widening.

Statement 4 is *Tidak sesuai*. Sentence (11) says the balance doubles in about <InlineMath math="70" /> months "at the same rate", meaning the <InlineMath math="1\%" /> per month from sentence (10). The figure of <InlineMath math="70" /> months holds for that rate only. At a lower rate, the balance takes longer to double, so "whatever the rate" isn't supported by the text.

So statements 1 and 2 are consistent with the text, while statements 3 and 4 aren't.

The main idea is the point the writer wants to make across the whole paragraph. In the second paragraph, the first sentence isn't the writer's own view, so you need to see where the paragraph turns. The safest method is to give each sentence a role.

| Sentence | Its role |
| --- | --- |
| (6) | A common view: monthly interest on voluntary savings looks too small to matter. |
| (7) | Where that view comes from: picturing the interest as the same amount every month. |
| (8) and (9) | The correction, marked by *padahal* ("in fact, however"): interest that isn't withdrawn goes into the balance, so the next interest grows. That's compound interest (*bunga majemuk*). |
| (10) | An example: after two months the gap is only <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100" />. |
| (11) | The point, marked by *namun* ("but"): the gap keeps widening, until the doubling time is about <InlineMath math="70" /> months against <InlineMath math="100" />. |

The paragraph builds a correction. Sentences (6) and (7) state a view, sentences (8) and (9) show why it's wrong, and sentences (10) and (11) back that up with numbers. So the main idea has to hold **the view being corrected together with the reason**. That's the option *Bunga simpanan yang tampak kecil tidak berarti hasilnya kecil, sebab bunga yang dibiarkan masuk ke saldo membuat selisihnya dengan bunga tunggal terus melebar* ("Savings interest that looks small doesn't mean small results, because interest left in the balance makes the gap with simple interest keep widening").

The other options are partly right but miss what the writer stresses:

- The option saying monthly interest really is small, so results only show with a large first deposit, takes the view from sentence (6) and adds a large-deposit condition that's never stated. The writer actually rejects that view with *padahal*.
- The option on how compound and simple interest are calculated is accurate, but it's only sentences (8) and (9), the tool of the correction. It names neither the view being corrected nor the result in sentence (11).
- The option about the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100" /> gap is the example in sentence (10). Read on its own, it even supports the view that the interest is small. Sentence (11) opens with *namun* precisely to show the gap doesn't stop there.
- The option saying compound interest pays more from the first month is wrong about the first month. In month one, compound and simple interest are both calculated on the first deposit, because no interest has entered the balance yet. The gap only appears in month two.

So the main idea of the second paragraph is that savings interest that looks small doesn't mean small results, because interest left in the balance makes the gap with simple interest keep widening.

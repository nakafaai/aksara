Sentence (5) alone isn't enough to fix the meaning of *tumpangan*. A newcomer can *mendapat tumpangan* ("get a *tumpangan*") in several ways, and in everyday talk the first meaning that comes to mind is getting a ride. **The next sentence is what decides it**.

KBBI records *tumpangan* as "something carried along (a load, a consignment, a parcel)" and "a place to stay for a while, lodging". Its verb, *menumpang*, means "to ride (a train, a ship and so on)" and "to stay or spend the night at someone's home". So the dictionary alone offers several candidates.

Now read sentence (6): *Selama berbulan-bulan, mereka tidur di ruang depan rumah kerabat itu sambil mencari kerja lewat jaringan yang dimiliki kerabatnya* ("For months they slept in the front room of that relative's house while looking for work through the relative's network"). It gives three clues:

1. They *sleep at the relative's house*. So what they got was a place to stay, not a vehicle or money.
2. It lasts *for months*, and sentence (7) says they only rent a room of their own *after they have a steady income*. So the stay is temporary and not rented.
3. Work is found *through the relative's network*, as a separate matter from the place to sleep. So *tumpangan* isn't a job.

The meaning is a temporary place to stay at a relative's house, matching the KBBI sense "a place to stay for a while, lodging".

Each other option fails one of the clues:

- *A vehicle from the home region to the city* is the most common meaning, and it fits *baru tiba* ("just arrived") in sentence (4). But sentence (6) is about sleeping at a relative's house for months, not about a journey.
- *A room rented from the relative after getting a job* blends sentence (5) with sentence (7). The rented room comes later, and it's *tidak jauh dari* ("not far from") the relative, not in the relative's house.
- *A chance to work in the relative's home business* borrows from sentence (8). Sentence (6) says work is found through the network, not handed over as *tumpangan*.
- *Help with living costs* sounds reasonable for a newcomer without work. But the reading only mentions a place to sleep, not money.

So *tumpangan* in sentence (5) means a temporary place to stay at a relative's house.

To judge what a word swap does, you need three things: the old word's meaning in that sentence, the new word's meaning, and the part of the meaning that **gets added or lost** once the words are swapped.

The old word first. KBBI gives *menghidupi* two senses: "to maintain, to provide a living for" and "to let live (not kill)". Sentence (8) says the camphor and benzoin trade *menghidupi* many families in the interior, from the collectors in the forest to the porters. The sense is the first one: the trade was their livelihood. The sentence doesn't say how the money reached them.

The new word. KBBI defines *menggaji* as "to pay wages" or "to employ people by paying wages". *Gaji* itself is "pay for work, paid at fixed intervals". So *menggaji* brings in something *menghidupi* doesn't have: a relationship between an employer and workers who are paid at regular intervals.

Put the new word in:

> Selama berabad-abad, perdagangan ini menggaji banyak keluarga di pedalaman, dari pengumpul di hutan sampai para pemikul.

Now the trade appears as an employer, and the collectors and porters appear as salaried staff. The original sentence only said they earned a living from the trade. That's the change in meaning.

The other options point to changes that don't happen:

- *Hidup makmur* ("living in prosperity") is the meaning of *menyejahterakan* ("to make prosperous"). Wages don't mean prosperity, and *menggaji* says nothing about how well off anyone is.
- *The families paying for the trade* reverses who pays whom. With *menggaji*, the payer is the one named first, the trade.
- *Membiarkan tetap hidup* ("letting them stay alive") is the second sense of *menghidupi*. It describes the old word, not the change *menggaji* brings.
- *Higher income than before* adds a comparison that isn't there. *Menggaji* doesn't compare income before and after the trade.

So the sentence's meaning changes because the collectors and porters are now portrayed as workers paid regular wages by an employer, when originally they were only said to earn a living from the trade.

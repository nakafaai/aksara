# Given

Sentence (<InlineMath math="8" />) states that the camphor and benzoin trade ***menghidupi*** ("sustained / provided a livelihood for") many inland families, from forest gatherers to porters. The question replaces *menghidupi* with ***menggaji*** ("to pay a salary to").

# Asked

Why does the meaning of sentence (<InlineMath math="8" />) change if *menghidupi* is replaced by *menggaji*?

# Solution

> Selama berabad-abad, perdagangan ini menghidupi banyak keluarga di pedalaman, dari pengumpul di hutan sampai para pemikul.

Compare KBBI's meaning of ***menghidupi*** ("to provide a livelihood") against ***menggaji*** ("to pay regular wages as an employer").

## Comparing livelihood with salaried employment

In the original sentence, <Highlight>menghidupi banyak keluarga</Highlight> simply means the trade was their source of income without specifying how money changed hands. Substituting *menggaji* injects an **employer-employee wage relationship**, portraying independent gatherers and porters as regularly salaried workers.

The other options invent non-existent shifts: a salary does not imply prosperity, *menggaji* has the trade paying families rather than the reverse, "letting them live" is another sense of the old verb, and *menggaji* adds no income comparison against earlier eras.

# Answer

The meaning changes because **the sentence now portrays gatherers and porters as workers paid regular wages by an employer, whereas originally it only said they earned a livelihood from the trade** (*kalimat menjadi menggambarkan pengumpul dan pemikul sebagai pekerja yang diupah secara berkala oleh pemberi kerja, padahal semula mereka hanya disebut memperoleh nafkah dari perdagangan*).

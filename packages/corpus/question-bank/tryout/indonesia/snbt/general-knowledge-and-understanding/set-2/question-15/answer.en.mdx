At first glance the five options look the same. The right paraphrase has to **keep every part of the meaning** of sentence (6): nothing added, dropped or reversed. So break sentence (6) down first, then check each option part by part.

Sentence (6) has four parts:

| Part | What sentence (6) says |
| --- | --- |
| Cause | from the outside, a tree holding crystals can't be told apart from an empty one |
| How often | *biasanya* ("usually") |
| Effect | they fell *banyak* ("many") trees |
| Where the search stops | once they find one whose contents are *cukup untuk membayar jerih payah* ("enough to repay their toil") |

The last part matters. "Enough to repay their toil" means the earlier trees didn't hold enough. They might have been empty, or they might have held a few crystals.

The option with *biasanya baru mendapat ... setelah menebang banyak pohon* ("usually only get ... after felling many trees") keeps all four parts. *Tampak sama dari luar* ("look the same from the outside") equals can't be told apart from the outside. *Biasanya* is kept as it is. *Baru mendapat satu ... setelah menebang banyak pohon* has the same order as *menebang banyak pohon sebelum menemukan satu* ("fell many trees before finding one"). *Hasilnya sepadan dengan jerih payah* ("its yield is worth their toil") equals "enough to repay their toil".

Each other option changes just one part, and one change is enough to break the paraphrase:

- *Selalu* ("always") replaces *biasanya*. Sentence (6) leaves room for a lucky first tree now and then, and *selalu* rules that out.
- *Mendapat satu yang berisi kristal* ("get one that holds crystals") replaces where the search stops. The bar drops from "holds enough" to "holds anything", so it sounds as if all the earlier trees were completely empty.
- *Karena menebang banyak pohon, pohon menjadi tampak sama* ("because they fell many trees, the trees come to look the same") reverses cause and effect. In sentence (6), the trees looking alike is the cause and the heavy felling is the effect.
- *Lebih banyak daripada pohon-pohon lainnya* ("more than the other trees") swaps the comparison. Sentence (6) compares a tree's contents with the collectors' toil, not with other trees.

So the paraphrase that keeps the meaning of sentence (6) is that, because empty trees and trees holding crystals look the same from the outside, the collectors usually only get one tree whose yield is worth their toil after felling many trees.

The expression *jerih payah* is made of two words that both mean tired. As an expression, its meaning widens to **hard effort made with great difficulty**, and often the fruit of that effort.

In sentence (8), residents feel their *jerih payah* is not worth the return. What is weighed against the return is **the effort of sorting waste**. So the fitting meaning is the hard effort that was made.

The other options do not fit:

- *Tiredness after a full day of work* takes only the literal meaning of *jerih* and *payah*. Sentence (8) weighs effort against return, not feelings of tiredness.
- *A fixed monthly wage* does not fit, since the savings vary and are not a wage.
- *Residents' complaints* are not the meaning of this expression.
- *Light work* is the opposite of *jerih payah*.

So *jerih payah* in sentence (8) means the hard effort that was made.

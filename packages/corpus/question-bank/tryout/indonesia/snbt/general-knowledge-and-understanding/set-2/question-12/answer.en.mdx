A statement matches the reading (*Sesuai*) only when **every part of it is backed by a sentence in the reading**. If even one part contradicts the text or has no support, it doesn't match (*Tidak sesuai*). Each statement here needs two or more sentences. Be careful with first impressions too: one statement sounds overstated but is true, and one sounds convincing but is false.

**Statement 1**: *Sesuai*. Sentence (4) says benzoin is obtained by cutting the bark and waiting for the sap to harden. The trunk isn't split. Sentence (5) says camphor crystals can only be taken after the tree is felled and its trunk is split into pieces. So only benzoin can be had without splitting the trunk. Don't mix up *melukai kulit* ("cutting the bark") with *membelah batang* ("splitting the trunk").

**Statement 2**: *Tidak sesuai*, even though it sounds convincing. Sentence (11) does mention ceramics from China and an inscription from the late 11th century. But Chinese ceramics show goods from China that arrived in Barus, not a shipping route from Barus. The inscription is in Tamil, so it says nothing about voyages to China either. Sentence (10) only says goods from Barus *akhirnya sampai* ("eventually reached") the Chinese market, without any direct voyage.

**Statement 3**: *Tidak sesuai*. Put two dates together. By sentence (14), Aceh's influence began around the mid-16th century and gave way to the VOC in the second half of the 17th century. By sentence (15), competition with factory-made camphor only began in the early 20th century. Aceh's period was long over before factory camphor appeared.

**Statement 4**: *Sesuai*, even though *hanya* ("only") and *mengorbankan* ("sacrificing") sound overstated. Sentence (2) says the camphor from Fansur, that is Barus, was praised in Arab sources. Sentence (4) explains that this camphor is made of crystals inside cracks in the trunk. Sentence (5) says those crystals can only be taken after the tree is felled and its trunk split. A tree that's felled and split is clearly sacrificed, and *baru dapat* ("can only once") means the reading names no other way. So *hanya* is fully supported here.

So statements 1 and 4 match the reading, while statements 2 and 3 don't.

A main idea has to **cover the whole paragraph** and catch where it's heading. An option that's true but stands for only one sentence is too narrow, and an option that adds *terutama* ("mainly") or "main source" without support is too broad.

Map the second paragraph, sentences (5) to (11).

1. Sentences (5) and (6) explain what resistance is and that it belongs to the bacteria.
2. Sentences (7) and (8) explain the mechanism: a genetic change appears at random in a small share of cells, and when the drug is given, those cells escape death and seed the next generation.
3. Sentences (9) and (10) widen it: the drug also reaches gut bacteria, so every use, *even as prescribed*, helps select bacteria.
4. Sentence (11) adds that the gene for resistance can be inherited or moved to other bacteria.

The common thread: resistance grows through selection, that selection happens with every use, prescribed use included, and the resistant trait can then be inherited and spread. That's the main idea.

Here's why the other options are wrong.

- "It arises mainly because antibiotics are bought without a prescription" is the strongest trap. Its content comes from the first paragraph, but nothing in the text supports *terutama* ("mainly"). No sentence compares buying without a prescription with other uses, and sentence (10) stresses that prescribed use selects bacteria too.
- "Antibiotics turn bacteria resistant" reverses the mechanism. Sentence (7) says the change appears at random, and sentence (8) says the cells that aren't resistant die. The drug filters bacteria, it doesn't change them.
- "Resistance belongs to the bacteria, not to the body" is true, but it's only sentence (6). It's too narrow.
- "Gut bacteria are the main source of resistance" overstates sentence (9). The passage only says the drug also reaches gut bacteria. It never calls them the main source.

So the main idea of the second paragraph is that resistance grows through selection that happens with every antibiotic use, prescribed use included, and the resistant trait can then be inherited and spread.

The question asks for the one statement that **misreads the writer's position**. The other four read the passage correctly, even though some of them sound odd.

Start with sentences (9) and (10). Sentence (9) uses *memang* ("admittedly"): the urban kampung is admittedly crowded, its alleys narrow, and some houses lack permits. *Memang* marks a concession: the writer accepts that these drawbacks are real. Sentence (10) opens with *Namun* ("however") and says this very crowding keeps rent low and work close by. The pattern "admittedly this, however that" concedes first and then makes the point. The point made is the benefit.

This pattern is often misread as "the drawbacks don't matter". To test that, you need the third paragraph. Sentence (13) describes a program that fixed alleys, drains and clean-water facilities inside the kampung. Sentence (14) says kampungs can be improved without evicting anyone. Sentence (15) discusses how such improvements can last. So the writer actually supports fixing things. What the writer rejects is eviction, not improvement.

That's why the statement that the writer considers the drawbacks *tidak perlu dibenahi* ("not in need of fixing") doesn't fit. It turns "admits the drawbacks but still defends the kampung" into "ignores the drawbacks".

The other four statements fit:

- *Admits the drawbacks but doesn't see them as reason enough to evict*: sentence (9) is the admission, and sentence (12) uses *padahal* ("even though") to question eviction because residents can lose customers and work networks.
- *Crowding is both the source of the drawbacks and the source of the benefits*: it sounds odd, but it's true. In sentence (9), *padat* ("crowded") is listed as a drawback. In sentence (10), *kepadatan itulah* ("this very crowding") keeps rent low and work close. The same thing plays both roles.
- *If it weren't so crowded, rent wouldn't necessarily be this cheap*: if crowding causes the low rent, then without the crowding the low rent is no longer guaranteed. *Belum tentu* ("not necessarily") keeps the claim careful.
- *Some drawbacks can be fixed without moving residents out*: the narrow alleys in sentence (9) are a drawback, and by sentences (13) and (14) alleys were among the things fixed inside the kampung without evicting anyone.

So the statement that doesn't fit the writer's position is the one claiming the writer sees no need to fix the urban kampung's drawbacks.

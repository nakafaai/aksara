The word you want must pass **two tests at once**: it has to fit in place of *dicap* in sentence (11), but not fit in place of *dinilai* in sentence (3). So pin down both words' meanings first.

*Dicap* in sentence (11). KBBI lists *mengecap* with the senses "to stamp", "to print", "to brand", and the figurative sense "to regard as, to declare (bad, good and so on)". Sentence (11) says a kampung is easily *dicap kumuh* ("labeled a slum") *oleh orang yang hanya melihatnya dari luar* ("by people who only see it from the outside"), *bahkan sebelum ada yang menghitung* ("even before anyone has counted"). There's no stamp or physical mark, and no inspection. The sense is the figurative one: judging something bad without checking.

*Dinilai* in sentence (3). That sentence says a village's change of status doesn't depend on newcomers arriving, *sebab yang dinilai adalah ciri wilayahnya* ("because what is assessed is the area's characteristics"), such as density and facilities. Here *menilai* means "to estimate or determine its value", that is, to examine those characteristics.

Now test all five options in both sentences:

| Option | Fits sentence (11)? | Fits sentence (3)? | Passes? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *dinilai* | yes | yes, it's the original word | no |
| *distempel* | no | no | no |
| *diukur* | no | yes | no |
| *ditetapkan* | no | doesn't matter | no |
| *dianggap* | yes | no | yes |

The reasons one by one:

- *Dianggap* ("regarded as") fits sentence (11): *mudah dianggap kumuh oleh orang yang hanya melihatnya dari luar*. It's still a negative judgment with no inspection. In sentence (3), *yang dianggap adalah ciri wilayahnya* makes no sense, because *menganggap* needs a complement, as in *dianggap penting* ("considered important") or *dianggap sebagai* ("regarded as"). So *dianggap* passes both tests.
- *Dinilai* ("judged, assessed") is the most tempting because it does fit sentence (11). KBBI even gives *menilai* the sense "to regard". But *dinilai* is the original word in sentence (3), so it certainly fits there. It fails the second test.
- *Distempel* ("stamped") is a synonym of the literal sense of *cap*. A kampung can't be stamped, and neither can an area's characteristics.
- *Diukur* ("measured") fits sentence (3), since density and facilities can be measured. But *mudah diukur kumuh* ("easily measured slum") makes no sense in sentence (11).
- *Ditetapkan* ("officially designated") means decided officially. Sentence (11) instead names people who only see the kampung from the outside, before anyone has counted. An official decision without inspection isn't what this sentence means, so it already fails the first test.

So the word that can replace *dicap* in sentence (11) but can't replace *dinilai* in sentence (3) is *dianggap*.

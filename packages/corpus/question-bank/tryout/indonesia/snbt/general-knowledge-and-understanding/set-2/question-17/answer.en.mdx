The **second paragraph** answers this directly. Sentence (6) says not every full moon produces an eclipse, and sentence (7) gives the reason with the word *sehingga* (so that, as a result).

The Moon's orbital plane is tilted about five degrees. As a result, at most full moons the Moon passes slightly above or below Earth's shadow and **does not enter it**. An eclipse happens only when a full moon falls while the Moon is right on the line with the Sun and Earth.

The other options are not supported:

- The passage does not say the Moon stops reflecting light. A full moon looks bright precisely because it reflects sunlight.
- Sentence (5) says the atmosphere scatters blue light during an eclipse, not that it scatters all light at every full moon.
- The Moon's distance is not discussed as a cause.
- Earth always has a shadow on the side facing away from the Sun. The passage does not say the umbra forms only at certain times.

So a lunar eclipse does not happen at every full moon because the Moon's orbital plane is tilted, so the Moon often passes above or below Earth's shadow.

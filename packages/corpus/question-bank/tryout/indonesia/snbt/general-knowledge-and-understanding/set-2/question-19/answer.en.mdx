The question doesn't ask for a summary. It asks for the **role** the third paragraph plays relative to the second. Several options describe the third paragraph's content partly right, so what you need to test is the role: adding examples, limiting, explaining an effect, changing the topic, or using the earlier paragraph as grounds for a judgment.

First sum up the second paragraph, sentences (4) to (10). It lays out what the urban kampung does for newcomers: a first place to get by, a roof and a work network from relatives, homes that double as businesses, cheap rent and work close by. Its drawbacks are admitted too, in sentence (9).

Then trace how the third paragraph, sentences (11) to (15), uses that content:

1. Sentence (11) opens with *Meski begitu* ("even so"): despite those functions, the kampung is easily labeled a slum without being checked.
2. Sentence (12) says the label is used as a reason for eviction, *padahal* ("even though") residents can lose **customers and work networks**. The customers come from the home businesses in sentence (8), and the work networks come from the relatives in sentence (6). So the functions from the second paragraph are used as grounds to question eviction.
3. Sentences (13) to (15) offer another way: improving the kampung where it stands, without eviction, with residents involved.

So the third paragraph uses the second paragraph's functions as grounds for judging policy toward the kampung.

The other options assign the wrong role:

- *Adding examples of the kampung's functions* looks right because the third paragraph does mention customers and work networks. But they aren't new examples. Both already appear in the second paragraph, and the third paragraph uses them as reasons against eviction.
- *Limiting it by admitting the kampung really is a slum* misreads sentence (11). That sentence says outsiders *label* the kampung a slum *before anyone has counted*, so the writer actually doubts the label.
- *Explaining an effect of the crowding* links two things the reading doesn't link. By sentence (12), the reason for eviction is the slum label, not the crowding. This option also skips sentences (13) to (15).
- *Moving on to city housing policy in general* is triggered by the words *rumah susun* ("public housing blocks") and *program*. Yet every sentence of the third paragraph is still about the kampung: the label on the kampung, the eviction of its residents, and improvements inside it.

So the third paragraph's role is to use the urban kampung's functions from the second paragraph as grounds for judging policy: eviction is questioned, and improvement in place is put forward.

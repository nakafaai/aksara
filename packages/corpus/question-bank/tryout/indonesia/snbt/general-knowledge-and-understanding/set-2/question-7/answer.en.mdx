This question asks which statement is **certainly** true (*PASTI*). That means the passage has to guarantee it. A statement that might be true but isn't guaranteed, or that another sentence contradicts, doesn't qualify.

Test the statement about a patient taking antibiotics as prescribed for a urinary tract infection. Two sentences work together.

1. Sentence (9): a swallowed drug also reaches the bacteria living in the gut, not only the bacteria causing disease. So a drug for a urinary tract infection reaches gut bacteria too.
2. Sentence (10): every use of antibiotics, *even as prescribed*, helps select bacteria.

Put together: the use is exactly as prescribed, yet the patient's gut bacteria are still selected. Both sentences guarantee it, so the statement is certainly true. It sounds wrong because many people assume correct use has no side effect on bacteria, but the passage says otherwise.

The other statements aren't guaranteed or are contradicted.

- "The gut bacteria of someone who takes antibiotics without a prescription will turn resistant" isn't guaranteed. Sentence (7) says the genetic change *can* appear in a small share of cells. If no such cell exists, selection leaves no resistant cells behind. The word "turn" is wrong too, because the drug filters bacteria rather than changing them (sentence 8).
- "Antibiotics that hit only a few kinds of bacteria let gut bacteria escape selection" sounds supported by sentence (16). But sentence (16) only names that choice as a step to keep drugs effective, while sentence (9) says a swallowed drug also reaches gut bacteria. The passage doesn't guarantee that gut bacteria escape.
- "Resistance genes from gut bacteria can only pass to the next generation of gut bacteria" is contradicted by sentence (11): the gene can also be moved to other bacteria, including other species.
- "Unnecessary use selects more strongly than prescribed use" changes what's being compared. Sentence (10) only says unnecessary use *adds* selection without curing anything. The passage never compares how strong the selection is in a single use.

So the statement that's certainly true is that a patient taking antibiotics as prescribed for a urinary tract infection still selects the bacteria in their gut.

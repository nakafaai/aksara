This question asks for the **function** of the third paragraph, not a summary of it. Every option gets part of the third paragraph's content right, so what decides it is the role the paragraph plays toward the first two.

First, map the three paragraphs.

1. The first paragraph explains how a delta grows: the current slows, the load settles, and new land grows out to sea (sentence 6).
2. The second paragraph sets a condition: a delta's shape depends on a tug-of-war between the river's sediment supply and the strength of the sea (sentence 8).
3. The third paragraph opens with "Keseimbangan itu kini mudah terganggu" (sentence 12), meaning "that balance is now easily disturbed". The word *itu* ("that") points back to the tug-of-war in the second paragraph. Then the supply shrinks because of dams (sentence 13), the ground sinks through compaction and groundwater pumping (sentences 14 and 15), and when new sediment loses out, the shoreline moves inland (sentence 16).

So the third paragraph takes the balance condition from the second paragraph and uses it to show that the land movement from the first paragraph can reverse: from growing out to sea to retreating inland.

Here's why the other options are wrong.

- "It adds compaction as the last stage of building land" flips what compaction does. Sentence (14) says compaction makes the delta surface sink, so it works against growth instead of completing it.

So the third paragraph uses the balance condition from the second paragraph to show that the land growth in the first paragraph can turn into shrinking.

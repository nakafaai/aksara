This analogy wants a pair that relates the same way as *sungai* ("river") and *delta* **on two features at once**. A pair that matches only one feature doesn't count.

First, spell out the relationship from the first paragraph. The river carries sand, mud, and clay (sentence 2), that load settles at the mouth (sentences 3 to 5), and the growing deposits then form new land (sentence 6). That gives two features.

1. The first word is a natural force that forms the second word, and the second word is a landform.
2. The landform forms because material carried by that force settles and piles up, not because rock is worn away.

Now test *angin : gumuk* ("wind : coastal dune"). KBBI defines *gumuk* as a sand hill on the coast. It forms when wind blows beach sand along and heaps it up. Wind is the natural force that forms the dune (feature 1), and the dune is made of sand the wind dropped (feature 2). Both features fit.

The other pairs match only one feature.

| Pair | Feature 1: natural force and landform | Feature 2: formed by deposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *sungai : sedimentasi* ("river : sedimentation") | no, sedimentation is the process of settling, not a landform | yes |
| *pasir : gumuk* ("sand : dune") | no, sand is the material, not the force that shapes it | yes |
| *sungai : ngarai* ("river : canyon") | yes | no, KBBI says a canyon forms through erosion |
| *gunung api : kawah* ("volcano : crater") | yes | no, a crater is a hollow at the summit that erupted material passes through |

The strongest traps are the two pairs starting with *sungai*. Their first word matches the question, but *sedimentasi* fails the first feature and *ngarai* fails the second.

So *sungai* and *delta* relate the same way as *angin* and *gumuk*: a natural force and a landform built from the material it deposits.

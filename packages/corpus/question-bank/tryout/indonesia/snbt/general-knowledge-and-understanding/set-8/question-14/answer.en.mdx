# Given

Sentences (<InlineMath math="6" />) and (<InlineMath math="7" />) of the **seaweed** passage stand in contrast:

- Sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />): this cultivation needs no fertilizer or feed, keeping capital costs relatively low.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="7" />): *However* (*Namun*), yields depend heavily on sea conditions.

# Asked

The relationship between sentence (<InlineMath math="7" />) and sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />).

# Solution

The contrast marker *Namun* (however) pivots from the **advantage of low input cost** in sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />) to the **weakness of heavy dependence on ocean conditions** in sentence (<InlineMath math="7" />).

> <Highlight>Namun, hasilnya sangat bergantung pada keadaan laut</Highlight>.

## Checking the Other Four Options

Each distractor misses the **advantage-versus-weakness contrast**.

- Sentence (<InlineMath math="7" />) contrasts with rather than exemplifies sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />).
- The reason for low capital is already stated inside sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />).
- It introduces a new vulnerability rather than restating sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />).
- It opens the discussion of risks continued in sentences (<InlineMath math="8" />) and (<InlineMath math="9" />), not a full-passage summary.

# Answer

**Sentence (<InlineMath math="7" />) states a weakness of the cultivation after sentence (<InlineMath math="6" />) notes its advantage**.

(*Kalimat (7) menyatakan kelemahan budi daya setelah kalimat (6) menyebut kelebihannya.*)

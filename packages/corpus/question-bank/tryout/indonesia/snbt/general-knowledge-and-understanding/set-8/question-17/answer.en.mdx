# Given

Sentences (<InlineMath math="3" />) and (<InlineMath math="4" />) of the **peatland** passage trace how canals trigger fire risk:

- Sentence (<InlineMath math="3" />): when canals are dug to drain peatland, water flows out and the peat layer dries.
- Sentence (<InlineMath math="4" />): dry peat catches fire very easily.

# Asked

Why digging canals can lead to peat fires according to the passage.

# Solution

Sentences (<InlineMath math="3" />) and (<InlineMath math="4" />) give a direct chain: **canals drain water away so the peat dries out and becomes highly flammable**.

> Ketika kanal-kanal digali untuk mengeringkan lahan gambut, <Highlight>air di dalamnya mengalir keluar dan lapisan gambut menjadi kering</Highlight>.

## Checking the Other Options

Each distractor invents a cause **absent from the text**.

- Canals are not described as bridges for fire.
- Canal water is not flammable, and water keeps peat wet.
- Canal diggers are never said to burn plant remains.
- Faster decomposition of plant remains is not discussed.

# Answer

**Canals drain water out so the peat becomes dry and catches fire easily**.

(*Kanal mengalirkan air keluar sehingga gambut kering dan mudah terbakar.*)

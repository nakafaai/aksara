The second paragraph names two risks: **disease** from sudden changes in temperature or salinity, and **prices** that rise and fall with factory demand. A sudden change in water temperature just before harvest can trigger the disease that turns seaweed white and makes it drop off, so the crop fails right before it can be sold.

The other options would not cause a loss:

- Seaweed farming uses no fertilizer or feed, so their prices do not affect the farmers.
- Higher factory demand tends to raise prices, which helps.
- Clear, calm water is the condition the farming needs.
- Collectors arriving early is not named as a risk.

So the situation most likely to cause a loss is a sudden change in sea temperature just before harvest.

The word *rapuh* means **brittle, easily broken or crumbled**. In sentence (8), brittle manuscripts are already weak and damage easily when touched. Its opposite is *kuat* (strong), not easily broken or damaged.

The other options do not fit:

- *Lapuk* (decayed) means damaged by age, close in meaning to *rapuh*.
- *Tipis* (thin) describes thickness, not the opposite of brittle.
- *Usang* (worn out, outdated) is also close to the state of a brittle manuscript.
- *Kering* (dry) is not the opposite of *rapuh*. Dry things often become brittle.

So the opposite of *rapuh* is *kuat*.

# Given

Sentences (<InlineMath math="7" />) and (<InlineMath math="8" />) of the **lontar** passage describe physical decay of the manuscripts:

> Banyak naskah yang tersimpan di rumah warga <Highlight>sudah rapuh dan sulit dibaca</Highlight>.

# Asked

The sentence whose italicized word is opposite in meaning to *rapuh* (fragile/brittle) in sentence (<InlineMath math="8" />).

# Solution

Applied to deteriorating palm leaves, *rapuh* denotes **physical fragility** (easily broken or crumbling). Its exact physical antonym is **kuat** (**strong**) in *Tali ijuk itu masih kuat meskipun sudah bertahun-tahun dipakai menimba air*.

## Separating Physical Antonyms from Emotional Traps

Keeping the **physical material domain** avoids the distractor *tegar* (resilient in spirit), which opposes emotional fragility rather than brittle objects.

| Italicized Word | Domain and Relation to Physical *rapuh* | Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *kuat* (palm-fiber rope) | Physically durable under use | Correct, direct physical antonym |
| *lapuk* (rotted timber) | Decayed by termites | Wrong, similar direction to *rapuh* |
| *tipis* (thin leaf) | Describes thickness | Wrong, not an antonym |
| *tegar* (steadfast after defeat) | Emotional resilience | Wrong, emotional domain |
| *kering* (dry grass) | Lack of moisture | Wrong, not an antonym |

# Answer

The matching antonym appears in **That palm-fiber rope is still *strong* even after years of drawing water**.

(*Tali ijuk itu masih kuat meskipun sudah bertahun-tahun dipakai menimba air.*)

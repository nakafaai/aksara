# Given

The **Student Eye Screening** passage highlights two key points: students struggling with the board often **do not notice their vision problem**, and some avoid wearing glasses because they feel **embarrassed by teasing**.

# Asked

The action best aligned with the passage if a classmate keeps squinting at the chalkboard.

# Solution

Imagine a friend squinting every time the teacher writes on the board. Two practical steps match the passage directly: **suggest an eye checkup** so a doctor can test their vision, and **never tease them** if they start wearing glasses.

> Applying an informational text to daily life means acting directly on both the causes and the solutions the passage identifies.

## Linking the screening findings to classroom action

Paragraph one stresses hidden vision trouble: <Highlight>tidak menyadari bahwa penglihatannya bermasalah</Highlight> (unaware their eyesight has a problem). Paragraphs three and four address social barriers: <Highlight>malu diejek teman</Highlight> (ashamed of being teased) and schools stepping in against mockery.

Therefore, the best response is **suggesting they get their eyes checked and not teasing them if they later wear glasses**.

## Why the other four actions clash with the text

- **Scolding them for laziness** repeats the exact unfair judgment paragraph one criticizes.
- **Lending your own glasses** ignores that prescriptions require a doctor's examination.
- **Moving to the back row** reverses paragraph four's rule to seat newly bespectacled students up front.
- **Laughing at them** worsens the teasing that keeps students from wearing needed glasses.

# Answer

The most suitable action is **advising your classmate to have their eyes examined and not teasing them if they later wear glasses**.

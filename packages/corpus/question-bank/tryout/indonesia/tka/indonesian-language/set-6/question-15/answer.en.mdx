# Given

Paragraph two records sequential counts: **<InlineMath math="900" /> middle schoolers** were screened, <InlineMath math="162" /> needed follow-up, and after a doctor's exam **<InlineMath math="120" /> needed glasses**, yet only **<InlineMath math="45" /> already owned them**.

# Asked

The sentence that reports the eye-screening results most accurately.

# Solution

Imagine reporting a school survey: you must keep the **total tested (<InlineMath math="900" />)**, the **final doctor diagnosis (<InlineMath math="120" />)**, and **those already wearing glasses (<InlineMath math="45" />)** straight without inflating numbers or confusing preliminary screening with the final count.

> Accurate data reporting preserves each exact number, separates preliminary referrals from final diagnoses, and avoids exaggeration.

## Checking all figures against paragraph two

The passage separates each step clearly: <Highlight>memeriksa mata <InlineMath math="900" /> siswa SMP</Highlight> (<InlineMath math="900" /> examined), <Highlight><InlineMath math="162" /> siswa perlu diperiksa lebih lanjut</Highlight> (<InlineMath math="162" /> referred), and <Highlight>Setelah diperiksa dokter, <InlineMath math="120" /> di antaranya membutuhkan kacamata. Namun, baru <InlineMath math="45" /> siswa yang sudah memilikinya</Highlight> (<InlineMath math="120" /> need glasses, only <InlineMath math="45" /> own them).

Thus, the accurate report states: **Out of <InlineMath math="900" /> students examined, <InlineMath math="120" /> need glasses, yet only <InlineMath math="45" /> already have them**.

## Why the other four report sentences fail

- **Almost all students have poor vision** wildly exaggerates <InlineMath math="120" /> out of <InlineMath math="900" />.
- **<InlineMath math="162" /> students need glasses** mistakes the referral count for the doctor's final <InlineMath math="120" />.
- **Proven lazy** reverses the passage's point that vision issues explain copying mistakes.
- **<InlineMath math="75" /> already own glasses** swaps owners (<InlineMath math="45" />) with those still lacking glasses (<InlineMath math="120 - 45 = 75" />).

# Answer

The best reporting sentence is: **Out of <InlineMath math="900" /> students examined, <InlineMath math="120" /> need glasses, but only <InlineMath math="45" /> already own them**.

# Given

Tika looks down expecting Bu Lastri to blame her slanted handwriting. Instead, Bu Lastri **gives a small laugh while tapping her own forehead**, says **"It's my eyes that need new glasses"**, and immediately pins Tika's trousers to fit.

# Asked

What Bu Lastri's remark "It's my eyes that need new glasses" means in context.

# Solution

Imagine a kind teacher misreading your slanted digit, then smiling and joking, **"Looks like my eyes need an upgrade!"** instead of scolding you. That **lighthearted self-joke** spares Tika embarrassment and shoulders the mix-up warmly.

> A playful remark in fiction is decoded through the speaker's expression, gesture, and immediate follow-up action.

## Reading the gesture and action in paragraph three

Tika braces for blame: <Highlight>Ia mengira penjahit itu akan menyalahkan tulisannya</Highlight> (she thought the tailor would blame her writing). Instead, Bu Lastri laughs gently: <Highlight>tertawa kecil sambil menepuk dahinya sendiri</Highlight> (chuckled while tapping her forehead), jokes <Highlight>Mataku yang perlu kacamata baru</Highlight>, and starts fixing the hem right away.

Her remark means **Bu Lastri does not blame Tika and shoulders the mistake lightly**.

## Why the other four readings fail

- **Asking Tika to buy glasses** reads a warm joke literally.
- **Anger at Tika's handwriting** clashes with her chuckle and gentle tone.
- **Refusing to alter the trousers** contradicts her pinning the hem immediately.
- **Too sick to sew** is disproven when the trousers are ready the very next day.

# Answer

Bu Lastri's remark means that **she does not blame Tika and accepts the mistake with lighthearted grace**.

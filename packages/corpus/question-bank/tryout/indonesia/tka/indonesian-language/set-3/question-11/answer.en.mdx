# Given

Three weeks of **Refill Station** data show dispensed water rising from <InlineMath math="310\,\mathrm{L}" /> to <InlineMath math="438\,\mathrm{L}" /> and visible bottles dropping from <InlineMath math="184" /> to <InlineMath math="119" />, while the cafeteria stopped selling one bottled brand, temperatures climbed, and the team frames early data as a guide for service upgrades.

# Asked

Which conclusion best fits the three weeks of refill-station data.

# Solution

Imagine a school juice bar seeing **refills climb while trash-bin bottles fall** over three weeks. That pattern clearly guides smarter tank scheduling and bottle loans, yet hotter weather and a dropped bottled brand mean outside factors still need tracking.

> A careful field conclusion uses early trends to improve service operations while acknowledging outside factors still requiring measurement.

## Reading the final summary of the team

The closing paragraph states directly: <Highlight>Mereka menyebut data awal sebagai dasar perbaikan layanan, bukan bukti tunggal tentang jumlah sampah seluruh sekolah</Highlight> (they treat initial data as a basis for service improvements, not sole proof of school-wide waste). Earlier paragraphs also flag the dropped cafeteria brand and rising temperatures.

Thus **a pattern supports service improvements, yet other factors still need to be measured**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Translating every liter straight into saved bottles** is explicitly rejected in paragraph three.
- **Using one fixed count hour as a daily estimate** misreads why they standardize timing (to avoid trash-pickup shifts).
- **Claiming temperature cannot affect drinking** and **shutting down the station** contradict the passage directly.

# Answer

The most accurate conclusion is that a pattern supports service improvements, yet other factors still need to be measured.

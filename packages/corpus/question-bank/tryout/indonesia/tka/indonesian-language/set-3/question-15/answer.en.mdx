# Given

In the **Refill Station** report, three weeks of figures record only dispensed water and bottles visible in four main bins without opening trash bags or isolating cafeteria changes and temperature shifts.

# Asked

Which objective report phrasing best replaces the overstated claim "this program is a total success".

# Solution

Imagine noticing **fewer plastic bottles on top of four school trash bins** for three weeks without checking bags taken home. Instead of shouting "total victory", an honest report simply states the two exact trends actually observed.

> Objective reporting replaces sweeping praise with plain descriptive wording limited to what the data directly show.

## Matching wording to the observed pattern

Paragraph three states the verified trend concisely: <Highlight>penggunaan stasiun meningkat dan jumlah botol yang terlihat menurun</Highlight> (station use rose and the count of visible bottles fell).

Saying **initial data show usage increasing and visible bottles declining** reports the facts accurately without leaping beyond the evidence.

## Why the other four options overreach

- **Judging total packaging reduction from visible bottles** ignores closed bags and bottles taken home.
- **Ruling out alternative explanations** contradicts the text flagging cafeteria changes and hotter weather.
- **Claiming most students use the station routinely from volume alone** goes beyond the meter reading.
- **Predicting impact at other schools** overgeneralizes from three weeks at one campus.

# Answer

The best replacement phrasing is that **initial data show usage increasing and the number of visible bottles declining**.

# Given

Early in **The Empty Measure**, Rafi feels silence equals doing nothing and scribbles extra clarinet notes onto the page. At the concert in paragraph five, a listener praises Ayu's cello solo without mentioning Rafi's clarinet at all.

# Asked

Which detail best proves that Rafi no longer measures contribution by how much sound he makes.

# Solution

Imagine running **the stage lights for a school play**. Afterward, audience members praise only the lead actor and never mention your name, yet you smile peacefully because you know your quiet role made her performance glow.

> The surest proof of a shifted mindset appears in how a character reacts when someone else receives the spotlight.

## Reading the concert reaction in paragraph five

Paragraph five states his new outlook directly: <Highlight>Rafi tidak kecewa karena klarinetnya tidak disebut pada bagian tersebut</Highlight> (Rafi was not disappointed that his clarinet went unmentioned in that section), since he now knows giving others space is real contribution.

That **he feels no disappointment when his clarinet is not mentioned** directly proves he stopped equating volume with worth.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Keeping beats so his clarinet stands out** reverses his willingness to step back for the cello.
- **Ayu arriving late**, **the conductor halting rehearsal**, and **how the score was printed** are external facts rather than Rafi's inner shift.

# Answer

The strongest proof is that Rafi is not disappointed when his clarinet is not mentioned.

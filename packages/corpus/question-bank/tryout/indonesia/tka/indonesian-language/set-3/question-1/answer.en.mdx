# Given

In the **City Museum audio-label trial**, visitors finishing three or more clips rose from <InlineMath math="18" /> in week one to <InlineMath math="26" /> in week two after staff added earphones, renamed clips as questions, added transcripts, and lowered starting volume all at once.

# Asked

Which statement most accurately interprets the rise from <InlineMath math="18" /> to <InlineMath math="26" /> participants.

# Solution

Imagine baking **a tastier cake** after changing sugar, butter, and oven temperature all in one batch. You clearly see a better result, yet you cannot tell which single tweak drove the gain.

> When multiple changes roll out together on a different participant group, an improved score cannot isolate one single cause.

## Reading the explicit limit in paragraph four

Paragraph four states directly that <Highlight>Kenaikan itu bertepatan dengan beberapa perubahan sekaligus, sehingga tim tidak dapat menentukan perubahan mana yang paling berpengaruh</Highlight> (the rise coincided with several simultaneous changes, so the team cannot identify which mattered most). Week two also involved different visitors.

Thus **the increase followed a bundle of changes, yet does not pinpoint one specific cause**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Earphones as the sole proven cause** clashes directly with four changes occurring together.
- **Dismissing transcripts** ignores staff observing visitors reading transcripts before choosing clips.
- **Identical groups** is factually wrong (<InlineMath math="40" /> versus <InlineMath math="38" /> different visitors), and **predicting identical results at other museums** overgeneralizes.

# Answer

The most accurate statement is that **the increase occurred after a bundle of changes, yet does not identify a single specific cause**.

# Given

In **Mapmaker's Ink**, spilled red ink blots out the northern route Laras has never walked. Annoyed at first and wanting to reprint, she checks the path at her mother's request, discovers real hazards, and later leaves the faint stain visible as a reminder of care.

# Asked

How the ink stain connects to the shift in Laras's attitude.

# Solution

Imagine spilling water on **your homework page** in frustration, only to spot a major math error while wiping it dry before turning it in. An annoying accident turns into the spark that teaches careful checking.

> A mishap acts as a turning point in fiction when it triggers an action that transforms the character's outlook.

## Tracing Laras from frustration to carefulness

In paragraph two Laras leaves carrying <Highlight>rasa kesal yang belum reda</Highlight> (lingering annoyance). After finding the broken bridge and fence, she tells the village in paragraph five: <Highlight>Noda ini membuat saya memeriksa jalan yang hanya saya kenal dari garis</Highlight> (this stain made me inspect a road I only knew as lines).

Thus **the stain triggers an inspection that turns frustration into carefulness**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Calling the initial map useless** ignores that she patches and keeps it.
- **Assuming the old route stayed usable** ignores the broken bridge and fence.
- **Closing the new lane** and **distrusting field notes** invert the entire ending.

# Answer

The ink stain triggers an inspection that transforms Laras's frustration into carefulness.

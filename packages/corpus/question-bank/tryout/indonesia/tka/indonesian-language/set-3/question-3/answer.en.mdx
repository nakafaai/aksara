# Given

**Paragraph four of Audio Labels** reports that <InlineMath math="30" /> of <InlineMath math="38" /> week-two visitors tried audio and <InlineMath math="26" /> finished at least three clips, then notes that multiple changes occurred at once on a different visitor group.

# Asked

Which statement best summarizes paragraph four.

# Solution

Think of a **lab update** that opens with good news ("week two scored higher") and immediately adds a caution ("several changes happened at once, so we cannot isolate which worked"). A complete summary holds both halves together.

> An accurate summary of a research paragraph pairs the observed gain with the methodological limit placed right beside it.

## Pairing the gain and limit in paragraph four

Paragraph four reports completions climbing to <InlineMath math="26" />, then explains <Highlight>tim tidak dapat menentukan perubahan mana yang paling berpengaruh</Highlight> (the team cannot tell which change mattered most) because adjustments were bundled and participants differed.

The sharpest summary is that **week-two results improved, yet the causes of change cannot be separated yet**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Avoiding non-question labels** belongs to week one's problem, not week two.
- **Keeping old filenames** clashes with adopting question-style names.
- **Transcripts lowering turnout** is invented, week two simply had a different cohort.
- **Using the same visitors** contradicts <Highlight>Kelompok peserta juga berbeda dari minggu pertama</Highlight> (the participant group also differed from week one).

# Answer

The best summary of paragraph four is that week-two results improved, yet the causes of improvement cannot be separated yet.

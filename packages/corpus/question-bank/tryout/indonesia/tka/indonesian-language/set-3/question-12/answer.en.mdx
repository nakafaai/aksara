# Given

A short **Refill Station** survey of <InlineMath math="96" /> students revealed <InlineMath math="20" /> often forgot bottles and <InlineMath math="15" /> lacked a tightly sealing bottle. Based on those answers, student council launched drying racks and a **bottle-lending program** using student IDs.

# Asked

How the student survey connects to the bottle-lending program.

# Solution

Imagine asking **classmates before recess** why some skip games, learning that several forgot or lack a ball, and then opening a ball-lending bin. The survey pinpoints the exact barriers, and the new service answers them directly.

> The link between survey and program is problem identification followed by a targeted service response.

## Tracing cause and response in paragraph four

Paragraph four places them back to back: <Highlight>Dua puluh orang mengatakan sering lupa, sedangkan <InlineMath math="15" /> lainnya tidak memiliki botol yang dapat ditutup rapat</Highlight> (twenty said they often forget, while <InlineMath math="15" /> lacked leakproof bottles), followed by <Highlight>Berdasarkan jawaban itu, OSIS menyediakan rak pengering dan program peminjaman botol</Highlight> (based on those answers, student council provided drying racks and a bottle-loan program).

Thus **the survey identifies barriers that are then addressed with a service**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Running the loan program before knowing barriers** reverses "based on those answers".
- **Claiming non-users mainly simply did not bring bottles** blurs the distinct barriers (forgetting versus lacking leakproof bottles) and misses the link to the service response.
- **Unrelated to the station** and **reducing drinking water volume** contradict the passage directly.

# Answer

**The survey identifies specific barriers that are subsequently addressed by launching the bottle-lending service**.

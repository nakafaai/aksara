# Given

During **Mangrove Nursery** monitoring, a log entry says "Dead Seedling" even though **neither the seedling nor its marker has been found** after searching nearby, leaving survival unverified.

# Asked

Which revised wording most accurately replaces "Dead Seedling" when proof is lacking.

# Solution

Imagine logging a check on **a neighbor's cat that is not on the porch**. You would not write "cat gone forever" just because you did not spot it. Clear field notes report only what was observed and admit what remains unknown.

> Precise field language separates an observational fact ("not found") from an unproven verdict ("dead").

## Matching the revision to the rule in paragraph five

Paragraph five defines the exact standard: after checking the area, staff <Highlight>mencatat status "tidak ditemukan" jika bukti tidak cukup</Highlight> (log "not found" when evidence is insufficient) so mortality counts stay faithful to observation.

Writing **Bibit tidak ditemukan. Status hidup bibit belum dapat dipastikan** (Seedling not found. Survival status cannot yet be confirmed) states the observation cleanly without overclaiming death.

## Why the other four rewrites fail

- **Most likely lost** and **assumed dead** still jump to unproven conclusions.
- **Calling monitoring unusable** overreacts, since verified entries still guide improvements.
- **Blaming currents for seedling death** invents an unproven causal link.

# Answer

The most accurate language revision is **Bibit tidak ditemukan. Status hidup bibit belum dapat dipastikan (Seedling not found. Survival status cannot yet be confirmed)**.

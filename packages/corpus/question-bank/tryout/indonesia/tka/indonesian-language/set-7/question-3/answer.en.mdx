# Given

Paragraph two records a trial involving **six members of the local blind association**. Average time to locate a departure lane **dropped from eleven minutes to about four minutes** after relocating several signs, while paragraph four notes remaining obstacles with vendors and staff.

# Asked

The most accurate sentence for reporting the terminal's trial results.

# Solution

Imagine writing a brief science report: an accurate summary states the **sample size (six participants)**, the **exact direction of change (eleven down to four minutes)**, and the **triggering change**, without sweeping claims that every problem vanished.

> Faithful reporting respects the exact sample size, keeps start and end figures in the right order, and avoids unsupported superlatives.

## Checking every figure against paragraph two

All key facts sit together: <Highlight>enam anggota persatuan diminta menemukan jalur keberangkatan tertentu</Highlight> (six members tested), <Highlight>membutuhkan waktu rata-rata sebelas menit</Highlight> (eleven minutes initially), and <Highlight>Setelah beberapa papan dipindahkan ke ujung ubin pemandu, waktunya turun menjadi sekitar empat menit</Highlight> (down to about four minutes after moving signs).

Thus, the best sentence is: **In a trial with six participants, average time to find a lane dropped from eleven to about four minutes after some signs were moved**.

## Why the other four sentences fail

- **All blind travelers without difficulty** overgeneralizes six testers at one terminal.
- **Eliminated every problem** contradicts paragraph four's warning that issues remain.
- **Dropped seven minutes to eleven minutes** reverses the math, since <InlineMath math="11 - 4 = 7" /> ended at four minutes.
- **Most accessible terminal in Indonesia** invents a national ranking absent from the text.

# Answer

The best reporting sentence is: **In a trial with six participants, average time to locate a lane dropped from eleven to about four minutes after some signs were relocated**.

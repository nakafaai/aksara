# Given

A community-hall poster promises **"bulk refill shops will eliminate plastic waste in Sukasari within a year"**. Waste-bank organizers call this claim too extreme because bulk sales have only been tried at **four out of more than sixty shops**, and paragraph three notes **not all shoppers are interested**.

# Asked

The fairest evaluation of the poster at the community hall.

# Solution

Imagine only **<InlineMath math="4" /> out of over <InlineMath math="60" /> shops** testing refill jars while many customers remain hesitant, yet a poster vows **"zero plastic waste within one year"**. Making an absolute promise from such a tiny pilot is clearly **exaggerated**.

> Grand promises to eliminate a community-wide problem must be tested against actual trial scale and public adoption.

## Testing the poster against two passage limits

Paragraph four gives the small trial scale: <Highlight>penjualan curah baru dicoba di empat warung, sedangkan di kelurahan itu ada lebih dari enam puluh warung</Highlight> (tried in only four of more than sixty shops). Paragraph three adds customer resistance: <Highlight>tidak semua pembeli tertarik</Highlight> (not every shopper is interested).

Thus, **the poster is exaggerated because bulk selling has only been tried at a small fraction of shops and has not won over all buyers**.

## Why the other four evaluations fail

- **Accurate because bulk coffee is cheaper** ignores that small savings have not converted all buyers or sixty-plus shops.
- **True because sachets are <InlineMath math="8" /> percent by weight** misses that sachets exceed <InlineMath math="40" /> percent by item count and does not back wiping out all plastic waste.
- **Bulk shops add plastic waste** reverses customers bringing reusable containers.
- **Unrelated to sachet waste** ignores that the poster directly targets plastic waste.

# Answer

The best evaluation is that **the poster is exaggerated because bulk sales have only been tested in a small fraction of shops and have not attracted all buyers**.

# Given

Paragraph four reports Bu Anisa's small survey across two classes: **<InlineMath math="25" /> of <InlineMath math="64" /> students** say they read at home at least twice a week. Yet the closing sentence notes **that figure was never recorded before the program**, so it cannot yet be compared.

# Asked

The main idea summarizing paragraph four as a whole.

# Solution

Imagine measuring a plant at <InlineMath math="25" /> centimeters today after forgetting to measure it last month. You now have a **starting snapshot**, yet **cannot tell how much it grew**. Both halves together form paragraph four's **main idea**.

> When a survey paragraph includes a baseline limitation, the main idea must combine the initial finding with the inability to compare before and after.

## Uniting the survey snapshot with its missing baseline

The opening lines give the snapshot: <Highlight>Dari <InlineMath math="64" /> siswa, <InlineMath math="25" /> orang mengaku membaca di rumah setidaknya dua kali seminggu</Highlight> (<InlineMath math="25" /> of <InlineMath math="64" /> read at home twice a week). The closing line states the limit: <Highlight>Sebelum program dimulai, angka itu tidak pernah dicatat sehingga belum dapat dibandingkan</Highlight> (never recorded beforehand, so not yet comparable).

Thus, **the initial survey shows some students read at home, yet the result cannot yet be compared with pre-program habits**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **All students reading at home** exaggerates <InlineMath math="25" /> out of <InlineMath math="64" /> (under half).
- **Proving program success** is impossible without a before-program comparison.
- **Surveying the whole school** contradicts the text specifying *dua kelas* (two classes).
- **Borrowing dropping after the survey** never appears in the passage.

# Answer

The main idea of paragraph four is that **an initial survey shows some students read at home, but results cannot yet be compared with conditions before the program**.

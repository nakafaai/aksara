# Given

Paragraph one of **Sachet Waste** contrasts two sides linked by **Namun** (however): to shoppers, sachets feel **cheap and handy for single use**, whereas to waste managers, fused layers of plastic and aluminum make them **almost impossible to recycle**.

# Asked

The main idea capturing the entire first paragraph.

# Solution

Think of a tiny single-serve pouch: it looks **affordable and convenient in the buyer's hand**, yet turns into a **headache for waste workers** because bonded layers defy recycling. Joining both sides produces paragraph one's **main idea**.

> When a paragraph pivots on *namun* (however), its main idea unites the benefit introduced first with the problem revealed second.

## Uniting shopper view and waste manager view

First comes shopper convenience: <Highlight>Bagi pembeli, saset terasa hemat karena harganya murah dan isinya pas untuk sekali pakai</Highlight> (cheap and sized for one use). Then *Namun* introduces the waste burden: <Highlight>sulit dipisahkan dan hampir tidak bisa didaur ulang</Highlight> (hard to separate and almost unrecyclable).

Hence, the main idea is that **sachets feel economical to buyers yet complicate waste management because they can barely be recycled**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Made of biodegradable paper** contradicts layered plastic and aluminum.
- **Sachet coffee cheaper than bulk** reverses paragraph three, where bulk coffee costs slightly less per gram.
- **Shops selling only sachet coffee** ignores shampoo, seasonings, and refill trials.
- **Waste managers banning sachets** never appears in the text.

# Answer

The main idea of paragraph one is that **sachets feel economical for buyers, yet complicate waste handling because they are nearly impossible to recycle**.

# Given

In the **Mango Tree** story, neighborhood kids call Pak Darmo stern because he is quiet and once scolded children climbing his fence. Yet when Ayu returns a bucket of fallen mangoes, Pak Darmo **brings out a basket-tipped picking pole** and invites her to **pick mangoes and share them with the alley children**.

# Asked

The strongest evidence that Pak Darmo is not as harsh as the neighborhood children believe.

# Solution

Imagine a neighbor rumored to be scary suddenly **lending you his picking pole and asking you to share fruit with every kid on the street**. Generous action directed at those very children is the strongest **counter-evidence** against a harsh label.

> Evidence disproving a character's harsh reputation must be a warm deed that directly contradicts the negative label.

## Reading the generosity in paragraph five

His kindness shines directly through his deed and words: <Highlight>keluar membawa galah berujung keranjang</Highlight> (came out carrying a basket-tipped pole) and <Highlight>Kalau mau, petiklah yang masih di pohon. Bagikan juga kepada anak-anak di gang. Bilang saja dari Bu Darmo</Highlight> (pick from the tree and share with the alley kids from Bu Darmo).

Therefore, the strongest evidence is that **Pak Darmo lends his picking pole and asks Ayu to share mangoes with the neighborhood children**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Rarely returning greetings** and **scolding fence-climbers** are the reasons kids called him stern in the first place.
- **Staring silently at the bucket** does not yet reveal warmth.
- **The leaning mango tree** is simply the setting, not Pak Darmo's character.

# Answer

The strongest evidence is that **Pak Darmo lends his picking pole and invites Ayu to share the mangoes with the neighborhood children**.

# Given

Bu Anisa recommends tracking **books actually finished** and **reading habits outside program hours**. Paragraph four also exposes a baseline gap because no reading habit data was gathered **before the program began**.

# Asked

The evaluation practice for another school that best applies Bu Anisa's advice.

# Solution

Imagine testing whether morning jogging improves fitness: you record stamina **before starting** and **afterward** rather than merely counting running shoes bought. Applying Bu Anisa's advice means combining **before-and-after habit tracking** with **finished-book counts**.

> Sound real-world application of an evaluation critique combines pre-and-post baseline measurement with authentic behavior metrics.

## Combining paragraph three advice with paragraph four lesson

Paragraph three asks schools to measure genuine reading: <Highlight>berapa banyak buku yang benar-benar selesai dibaca</Highlight> (books actually finished) and <Highlight>membaca di luar jam program</Highlight> (reading outside program hours). Paragraph four adds the baseline lesson: <Highlight>Sebelum program dimulai, angka itu tidak pernah dicatat</Highlight> (never logged before launch).

Therefore, another school should **record students' reading habits before and after the program, including books actually finished**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Counting monthly loans alone** repeats the exact flaw Bu Anisa critiqued.
- **Requiring book summaries** conflicts with the program's no-summary approach.
- **Declaring success on day one** violates evidence-based evaluation.
- **Stopping the program once loans rise** makes no sense and contradicts the text.

# Answer

The best application is **recording students' reading habits before and after the program, including books completed**.

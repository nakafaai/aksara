# Given

The principal concludes students love reading from library loan counts alone. Bu Anisa says that conclusion **needs closer detail**, proposing to track **books actually finished** and ask whether students **read outside program hours**.

# Asked

The relationship between Bu Anisa's proposal and the principal's statement.

# Solution

Imagine someone claiming students love vegetables just because trays leave the counter, and you suggest checking how much gets **actually eaten** and whether kids eat veggies at home. You are not opposing healthy meals, you are **seeking sharper data to test the claim**.

> Proposing more precise indicators tests whether an optimistic claim holds up in real behavior without rejecting the underlying program.

## Connecting the proposal in paragraph three to the claim

Bu Anisa judges the claim <Highlight>perlu dirinci</Highlight> (needs finer breakdown), then offers two better measures: <Highlight>berapa banyak buku yang benar-benar selesai dibaca</Highlight> (books actually finished) and <Highlight>apakah mereka membaca di luar jam program</Highlight> (reading outside program hours).

Thus, **her proposal aims to gather more accurate data to test whether students genuinely enjoy reading**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Rejecting the reading program** contradicts the school continuing and expanding it.
- **Proving the principal lied** overstates a constructive request for sharper data.
- **Testing students on book content** clashes with the no-test design and was never proposed.
- **Unrelated to loan counts** misses that her proposal directly addresses the weakness of loan numbers.

# Answer

The link is that **Bu Anisa's proposal seeks better data to test whether students truly enjoy reading**.

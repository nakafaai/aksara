# Given

Right after Bu Ratih signals *continue, slowly* on the **wet, slick stage**, Laksmi immediately alters her stance and movement in paragraph three, setting the example the rest of the troupe follows.

# Asked

The strongest evidence that Laksmi quickly adapts to the slippery stage.

# Solution

Imagine walking across a freshly mopped floor without slipping: you instinctively **lower your center of gravity, widen your stance, and slow your turns**. Laksmi's immediate physical adjustment to prevent falls is the clearest **proof of adaptability**.

> Evidence of quick adaptation must be an active move that directly solves the hazard rather than passive effects or waiting for orders.

## Reading the exact adjustment in paragraph three

On cue, Laksmi reshapes her choreography instantly: <Highlight>Laksmi merendahkan tubuhnya, memperlebar langkah agar kakinya tidak tergelincir, dan memperlambat putarannya</Highlight> (she lowered her body, widened her steps so she would not slip, and slowed her turns).

Thus, the strongest evidence is that **Laksmi lowers her body, widens her steps, and slows her turns**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Looking at Bu Ratih** and **waiting for a cue to step off** happen before she adapts, while expecting cancellation.
- **Her bun nearly coming undone** and **soaked costumes** are passive effects of rain, not Laksmi's adaptive choices.

# Answer

The strongest evidence is that **Laksmi lowers her body, widens her steps, and slows her turns**.

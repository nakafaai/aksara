# Given

During the braille-sign trial at the **Kota Arunika Bus Terminal**, six visually impaired participants initially needed an **average of eleven minutes**. After several signs were moved to the **ends of the tactile guide tiles**, search time dropped to **about four minutes**.

# Asked

The relationship between moving signs to the ends of the tactile tiles and the time participants needed in paragraph two.

# Solution

Picture following a **handrail in a dim hallway**: if the room sign sits off to the side, your hand searches for ages. Moving that sign right to the tip of the rail puts it under your fingertips instantly. That clear **cause and effect** explains the time drop.

> When relocating a sign immediately cuts search time sharply, the new placement acts as the cause that makes discovery easier.

## Tracing the sequence in paragraph two

Paragraph two gives the starting time: <Highlight>mereka membutuhkan waktu rata-rata sebelas menit</Highlight> (they needed an average of eleven minutes). Right after relocation, search time plunges: <Highlight>Setelah beberapa papan dipindahkan ke ujung ubin pemandu, waktunya turun menjadi sekitar empat menit</Highlight> (after signs moved to the tile ends, time fell to around four minutes).

Thus, **placing signs at the tile ends made them easier to find, so search time dropped**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Walking farther** clashes with time shrinking from eleven to four minutes.
- **Search time rising** reverses the seven-minute decrease.
- **Moved because of street vendors** mixes in a later issue from paragraph four.
- **No connection to placement** is disproven by the immediate drop after moving the signs.

# Answer

The link is that **signs at the ends of the tactile tiles are easier for participants to find, reducing search time**.

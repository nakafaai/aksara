# Given

After Grandfather advises Bima to watch the whole board and replays move twenty aloud, **Bima writes a pencil reminder** inside the library book, erases it before returning the book, and still recalls the lesson every time he sets up the pieces.

# Asked

Which detail most strongly proves that Grandfather's lesson truly stuck with Bima.

# Solution

Imagine erasing a reminder off a whiteboard, yet **still carrying the exact advice in your head every time you step up to play**. Remembering a lesson long after the written note is gone proves it genuinely sank in.

> The clearest proof that a lesson has stuck is its lasting presence in the character's memory and recurring habits afterward.

## Reading the direct proof in the final paragraph

The closing line states the proof verbatim: <Highlight>Ia menghapus catatan itu sebelum mengembalikan buku ke perpustakaan, tetapi kalimatnya tetap ia ingat setiap kali menyusun bidak</Highlight> (he erased that note before returning the book, yet remembered the sentence every time he set up the pieces).

Therefore, **Bima still remembering his written sentence whenever he sets up the pieces even after erasing it** is the strongest proof.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Memorizing an opening**, **holding back a smile**, and **forking rook and queen** all happened *before* Grandfather taught the lesson.
- **Staring disappointed at the board** was merely his brief reaction before Grandfather explained why.

# Answer

**The strongest proof is that Bima keeps remembering his note every time he sets up the pieces even though the pencil writing was erased**.

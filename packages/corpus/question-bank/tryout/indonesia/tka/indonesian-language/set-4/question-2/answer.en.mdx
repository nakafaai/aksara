# Given

In **paragraph three of Solar Streetlamps**, residents clean panels every Friday and relocate two poles in May as nightly outages drop to one, yet May also happens to have the **lowest rainfall** of the entire three-month observation period.

# Asked

How the remark about May rainfall connects to the drop in lamp outages in paragraph three.

# Solution

Imagine **washing your bedroom window** to brighten the room during the sunniest week of the year. You cannot yet tell whether the room grew brighter strictly from clean glass or because clouds stayed away.

> When weather shifts at the same time as a maintenance fix, weather becomes a rival explanation that keeps causality uncertain.

## Reading the caveat in paragraph three

Paragraph three connects both points directly: May had the <Highlight>curah hujan paling rendah</Highlight> (lowest rainfall, giving panels more sunlight), so <Highlight>teknisi belum dapat memastikan bahwa penurunan itu hanya disebabkan oleh pembersihan panel dan pemindahan tiang</Highlight> (the technician cannot yet confirm that cleaning and relocating alone caused the drop).

Therefore, **low rainfall acts as a possible alternative explanation, leaving the effect of panel cleaning uncertain**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Proving panel cleaning had zero effect** goes too far, both factors could help together.
- **Being caused by moving two poles** is physically impossible.
- **Explaining why residents felt safer** misses the topic, and **making leaves cover panels more** never appears in the text.

# Answer

Low rainfall serves as a possible alternative explanation, so the effect of panel cleaning cannot yet be confirmed.

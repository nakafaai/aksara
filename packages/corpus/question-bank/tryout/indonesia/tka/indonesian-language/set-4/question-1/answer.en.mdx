The key is in paragraph four. The figure of 47 out of 60 shows that most residents say they **feel safer**. But the text adds two details right away: the survey was spoken, run by the same officers who installed the lamps, and some residents felt uneasy voicing complaints face to face. That setup can push answers to sound more positive than people's real views. So the fair judgment is that the data shows a positive impression, but it **may be shaped by how it was collected**.

- The text never mentions crime, so saying the lamps reduced crime goes too far.
- Sixty respondents still give useful information. The problem is how they were asked, not that the data is worthless.
- Thirteen residents simply did not say they felt safer. The text doesn't say they felt less safe.
- The number of lamps going out comes from the technicians' records in paragraphs two and three, not from the survey.

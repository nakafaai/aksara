# Given

In the **Classroom Noise** trial, students attach corkboards, flannel, and heavy curtains, and the physics teacher records clap decay in an **empty classroom** dropping from about <InlineMath math="1.4" /> seconds to <InlineMath math="0.8" /> seconds on a **phone app**.

# Asked

Which report sentence accurately presents the middle school's experiment result.

# Solution

Imagine reporting **a practice sprint** timed on a phone stopwatch along an empty track. An honest report never promises you will win every race, it states the measured improvement together with the exact conditions and tool used.

> An accurate experiment sentence names the intervention, observed direction of change, testing condition, and measuring tool without arithmetic errors or absolutes.

## Checking all four factual constraints from the text

Paragraphs three and four specify every detail: damping material went on the back wall and windows, testing ran <Highlight>di kelas kosong</Highlight> (in an empty room) using <Highlight>aplikasi pengukur bunyi di ponsel</Highlight> (a phone sound-meter app), and claps faded faster (a <InlineMath math="1.4 - 0.8 = 0.6" /> second drop).

Thus **after the back wall and windows received damping materials, clap sounds in the empty classroom faded faster according to the phone app** hits every fact accurately.

## Why the other four sentences fail

- **Eliminating all noise** and **never being noisy again** are sweeping exaggerations.
- **Dropping by <InlineMath math="0.8" /> seconds** gets the math wrong: <InlineMath math="0.8" /> seconds is the *final* time, while the drop is <InlineMath math="0.6" /> seconds.
- **Crowded rooms echoing more** contradicts bodies and clothing absorbing extra sound.

# Answer

The best report sentence is that after the back wall and windows received damping materials, clap sounds in the empty classroom faded faster according to the phone app.

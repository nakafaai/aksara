# Given

In paragraph one of **The Chess Draw**, Grandfather always plays the **white pieces** and Bima suspects he never truly teaches. In paragraph six, Grandfather turns the board so white sits before Bima and **for the first time** replays from move twenty while voicing every possibility aloud.

# Asked

What Grandfather's gesture of turning the board to give Bima the white pieces signifies.

# Solution

Imagine a master baker who always stood at the main counter, then one day **swaps spots with you** and talks through every step aloud. Handing over his usual side signals warmly that he is opening up his craft to teach you.

> A physical role reversal in fiction symbolizes moving from silent opponent to open, active mentor.

## Connecting paragraphs one and six

Early on, Grandfather always held white while Bima wondered if he cared to teach. In paragraph six, right after turning the board, <Highlight>Untuk pertama kalinya, Kakek mengajak Bima mengulang permainan dari langkah kedua puluh, kali ini sambil menyebutkan setiap kemungkinan dengan suara keras</Highlight> (for the first time, Grandfather invited Bima to replay from move twenty, naming every possibility aloud).

Giving Bima his usual white side signals that **Grandfather begins teaching Bima openly**.

## Why the other four readings fail

- **Ending the match out of irritation** clashes with his smile and invitation to replay.
- **Accusing Bima of cheating**, **admitting defeat**, and **demanding the library book back** never appear in the story.

# Answer

**Turning the board gives Bima the side Grandfather usually played, symbolizing that Grandfather is beginning to teach Bima openly**.

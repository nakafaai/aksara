# Given

At the end of **The Chess Draw**, Bima jots down "Move twenty: do not look at just one threat", and even after erasing the pencil note before returning the library book, **he remembers that sentence every time he sets up the pieces**.

# Asked

Which continuation best fits the ending of the story.

# Solution

Imagine learning from a **soccer mistake** to "scan the whole field before shooting". In the very next match, the most natural follow-up is actually glancing across every wing before attacking.

> The best story continuation puts the protagonist's newly internalized lesson directly into action next time.

## Extending the closing line to future games

The final sentence confirms Bima's lasting habit: he recalls <Highlight>jangan cuma lihat satu ancaman</Highlight> (do not look at just one threat) <Highlight>setiap kali menyusun bidak</Highlight> (every time he sets up the pieces).

Therefore, **in future games, Bima checks his opponent's pieces across the whole board before attacking**.

## Why the other four continuations fail

- **Quitting chess** clashes with his continuing to set up the pieces for future games.
- **Asking Grandfather to take white back** and **forbidding chess books** have zero textual support.
- **Keeping the borrowed book** contradicts <Highlight>sebelum mengembalikan buku ke perpustakaan</Highlight> (before returning the book to the library).

# Answer

**In the next game, Bima checks his opponent's moves across the entire board before launching an attack**.

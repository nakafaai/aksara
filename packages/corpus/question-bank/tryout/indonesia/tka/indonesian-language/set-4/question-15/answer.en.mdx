# Given

The closing paragraph of **Classroom Noise** concludes that schools do not need costly renovations to cut echo, placing **simple sound-absorbing materials on major reflecting surfaces** already makes a clear difference.

# Asked

Which practical application best transfers the passage's idea to reducing echo in a school auditorium.

# Solution

Imagine a slick-walled school hall where **announcements bounce and blur together**. Following the passage's physics, you do not silence everyone or crank up the speakers, you cover the main bouncing walls with soft absorbing fabric or cork.

> Applying an acoustic principle to a new room transfers the exact physical mechanism, placing soft absorbers on primary reflecting surfaces.

## Transferring the principle to an auditorium

Paragraph five states the takeaway plainly: <Highlight>Bahan sederhana yang ditempatkan pada bidang pantul utama sudah dapat memberi perbedaan</Highlight> (simple materials placed on main reflecting surfaces already make a difference), echoing corkboard, flannel, and heavy curtains from paragraph three.

Therefore, **placing soft materials like fabric or cork on the walls that reflect the most sound** directly applies the text.

## Why the other four options fail or backfire

- **Forbidding students from talking** ignores the hard room surfaces causing echo.
- **Louder speakers** and **painting walls smoother** increase reflections and worsen echo.
- **Measuring only when packed** changes the reading without fixing the auditorium's acoustics.

# Answer

The best application is **placing soft materials such as fabric or cork on the walls that reflect the most sound**.

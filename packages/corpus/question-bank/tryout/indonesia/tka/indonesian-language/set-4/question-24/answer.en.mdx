# Given

Seeing monthly shortfalls shrink from <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}180{.}000" /> (July) to <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}105{.}000" /> (August) and <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}56{.}000" /> (September), **Dimas declares** "friends are getting more honest," while Bu Ratna warns of possible stock-counting errors and growing familiarity with new payment rules.

# Asked

Which evaluation best judges Dimas's statement that students are becoming more honest.

# Solution

Imagine **fewer pencils going missing** after you start counting better and classmates learn where the pencil box sits. Jumping straight to "everyone became more honest" without separating counting mistakes is too hasty.

> Drawing a single moral conclusion from financial shortfalls is premature whenever clerical errors and learning curves remain unseparated.

## Testing the claim against the advisor cautions

Paragraph four names two non-honesty factors: <Highlight>sebagian selisih bisa saja terjadi karena salah hitung saat stok dicatat</Highlight> (some shortfall could stem from stock-counting errors) and <Highlight>turunnya selisih bisa juga terjadi karena siswa makin terbiasa</Highlight> (students growing used to the new system).

Therefore, **the statement is too hasty because shortfalls do not come solely from unpaid items**.

## Why the other four evaluations fail

- **Proven true by September's <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}56{.}000" />** ignores that causes have not been separated.
- **August's gap exceeding July's** misreads the table (<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}105{.}000 < \text{Rp}180{.}000" />).
- **Irrelevant** misses that Dimas directly addresses the current data.
- **Money received rising every month** also misreads the table (August <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{.}395{.}000" /> dipped to September <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{.}344{.}000" />).

# Answer

The best evaluation is that **Dimas's statement is too hasty because shortfalls do not arise solely from unpaid items**.

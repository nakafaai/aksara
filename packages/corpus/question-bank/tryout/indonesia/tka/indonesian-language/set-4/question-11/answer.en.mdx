# Given

**Paragraph two of Classroom Noise** explains how echo blurs soft consonants like *s*, *f*, and *t* for back-row students, causing classmates to speak louder and teachers to raise their voices until the whole room turns noisy unintentionally.

# Asked

The main idea of paragraph two.

# Solution

Picture **a rising loop of voices** in a tiled hallway: echoes blur words, so one person speaks louder, forcing the next person to raise their voice even more just to be heard. Soon the whole room roars without anyone trying to be disruptive.

> The main idea of a causal-chain paragraph captures the starting trigger (echo) and the escalating cycle it produces.

## Tracing the escalating chain in paragraph two

Paragraph two maps each link: <Highlight>Gaung membuat siswa di bangku belakang lebih sulit menangkap kata-kata</Highlight> (echo makes words harder to catch in the back), then <Highlight>Siswa kemudian berbicara lebih keras kepada teman sebangku agar terdengar, dan guru ikut menaikkan suara</Highlight> (students talk louder and teachers raise their voice), making the room <Highlight>makin gaduh</Highlight> (noisier).

Hence **echo can trigger escalating noise as people raise their voices to be heard**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Intentionally speaking loudly** contradicts <Highlight>tidak ada yang berniat membuat keributan</Highlight> (no one intends to make noise).
- **Saying *s*, *f*, *t* are louder** reverses the text calling them weak consonants.
- **Scolding students harder** and **blaming only brick walls** miss ceramic floors, concrete ceilings, and acoustic causes.

# Answer

The main idea of paragraph two is that **echo can trigger escalating noise because people raise their voices to be heard**.

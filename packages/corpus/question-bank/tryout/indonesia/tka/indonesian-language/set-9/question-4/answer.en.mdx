# Given

In paragraph three of the **waste oil soap** passage, the environmental officer says the leader's claim must be tested because **there has been no measurement of river water quality before and after the activity**.

# Asked

The meaning of the loanword *kualitas* in the phrase *kualitas air sungai* (river water quality).

# Solution

When you buy apples, two questions are completely separate: **how many apples are in the bag** versus **how good or fresh they are**. The word *kualitas* asks **how good or bad the condition is**, never how many liters exist.

> A loanword's exact meaning in a passage matches what the surrounding action is actually measuring or checking.

## Reading the river measurement context

The officer points out a specific missing check: <Highlight>belum ada pengukuran kualitas air sungai sebelum dan sesudah kegiatan</Highlight> (no measurement of river water quality before and after the activity). That test checks **whether the river is still polluted with oil or has improved**.

Therefore, *kualitas* means **the standard or level of how good or bad the river water is**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Number of liters flowing daily** describes *kuantitas* (quantity), not quality.
- **Color seen from a bridge** is at most one surface clue, whereas water quality testing evaluates **pollutant levels**.
- **How fast the river flows in a flood** refers to current speed, not cleanliness.
- **The name of the river** is a place label, not something measured before and after an activity.

# Answer

In the phrase *kualitas air sungai*, the loanword *kualitas* means **the standard or degree of how good or bad the river water is**.

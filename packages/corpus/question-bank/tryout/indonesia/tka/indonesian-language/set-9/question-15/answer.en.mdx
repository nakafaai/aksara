# Given

In paragraph two of the **pencak silat club** passage, Pak Rahmat explains that every session opens with a **salute of respect to both coach and opponent**, and students learn to **control their strength** during paired drills. He emphasizes that **respect and self-control are the core of pencak silat**.

# Asked

The pencak silat value from the passage that best applies to everyday life.

# Solution

Think about playing a close game or debating a classmate: even when competing hard, you still **treat others politely and keep your temper in check**. That everyday balance matches **respect and self-control** directly.

> A transferable everyday value should grow straight out of the core principle endorsed by the passage itself.

## Drawing the core value from the coach

Paragraph two highlights two concrete habits: <Highlight>salam penghormatan kepada pelatih dan lawan</Highlight> (a bow of respect to both coach and opponent) and **holding back excess force so partners stay safe**. Pak Rahmat sums both up as **respect and self-control**.

Outside the training field, that translates directly into **respecting others and practicing self-control, even while competing**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Always striving to win every rivalry** conflicts with the text calling matches only a **small part** of the club.
- **Using maximum force against training partners** is the opposite of **controlling strength** to avoid injury.
- **Avoiding activities needing regular practice** clashes with the passage's positive view of weekly training.
- **Practicing only before competitions** contradicts the steady Tuesday and Thursday practice schedule.

# Answer

The value most applicable to everyday life is **respecting others and controlling oneself, even when competing**.

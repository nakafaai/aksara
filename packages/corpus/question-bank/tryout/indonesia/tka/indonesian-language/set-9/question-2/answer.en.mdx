# Given

In paragraph two of the **waste oil soap** passage, filtered oil is mixed with a caustic soda solution. The text states that **this solution is hot and can injure the skin**, so makers **must wear gloves, protective goggles, and a mask**.

# Asked

The relationship between wearing gloves, protective goggles, and a mask and the caustic soda solution.

# Solution

When you lift a boiling pot off the stove, you put on **oven mitts so you do not burn your hands**. Here the logic is identical: the caustic soda mixture can **burn and harm skin**, so protective gear is mandatory for safety.

> The conjunction *sehingga* (so that / therefore) links a cause in the first clause directly to its practical consequence in the second clause.

## Reading the cause and effect in paragraph two

Paragraph two lays out the link explicitly: <Highlight>Larutan ini panas dan dapat melukai kulit sehingga pembuatnya wajib memakai sarung tangan, kacamata pelindung, dan masker</Highlight> (the solution is hot and can injure skin, so makers must wear gloves, goggles, and a mask). The hazard of **heat and skin injury** is the cause.

The clause after *sehingga* states the direct response, namely **wearing protective equipment** to prevent burns and splashes.

## Why the other four options fail

- **To make the soap smell better** fails because protective gear is never linked to **soap fragrance**.
- **Made from old gloves** fails because gloves protect hands rather than serving as a chemical ingredient.
- **Because waste oil smells bad** fails because odor is reduced by **soaking the oil with charcoal**, not by wearing goggles or gloves.
- **No relationship** fails because *sehingga* directly connects the dangerous solution to the safety rule.

# Answer

The relationship is that **protective gear is worn because the caustic soda solution is hot and can injure the skin**.

# Given

In the closing sentence of the **lost cat** story, Nayla writes in her diary that **she wants to adopt one of the kittens, if Bu Wening allows it**.

# Asked

The event most likely to happen after the story ends.

# Solution

If someone writes in their diary that they want to borrow a neighbor's guitar **if the neighbor agrees**, the very next thing they will do is **ask for permission**. Nayla's closing wish points straight to that exact step.

> A conditional closing phrase such as *if Bu Wening allows it* acts as a direct signpost toward the protagonist's next action.

## Reading the closing condition in the final line

The ending spells out Nayla's next step clearly: <Highlight>ia ingin memelihara salah satu anak kucing itu, kalau Bu Wening mengizinkan</Highlight> (she wants to raise one of the kittens, if Bu Wening allows it). Since everything hinges on **Bu Wening's permission**, the natural sequel is that **Nayla asks Bu Wening for permission to adopt one of the kittens**.

## Why the other four events fail

- **Giving Puspa to the vegetable vendor** clashes with **Bu Wening's deep attachment** to her cat.
- **Nayla stopping her visits** contradicts her warmth toward Bu Wening and her newfound fondness for the kittens.
- **Returning Puspa to the rice warehouse** makes no sense right after Nayla and her mother **carefully brought the cats home**.
- **Making new lost-cat flyers** is pointless because **Puspa has already been found**.

# Answer

The event most likely to happen next is that **Nayla asks Bu Wening for permission to adopt one of the kittens**.

# Given

In the passage on turning **used cooking oil into laundry soap**, a neighborhood group collected <InlineMath math="240" /> liters of waste oil in three months and produced about <InlineMath math="1{,}500" /> bars sold at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" /> each. The group leader claimed the project **"stopped oil pollution in the neighborhood river"**, while an environmental officer noted **oil came from only some residents** and **river water quality had not been measured**.

# Asked

The most accurate evaluation of the leader's claim that the project stopped oil pollution in the neighborhood river.

# Solution

Imagine picking up ten plastic bottles along one corner of a beach and declaring the entire coastline **completely trash-free** without inspecting the rest. A bold claim about river water needs **direct water measurements** before and after the activity.

> An environmental claim must be tested against direct measurements of the affected water, not inferred from how much waste was collected.

## Checking the two evidence gaps in paragraph three

Paragraph three quotes the officer's critique directly: <Highlight>jelantah yang terkumpul baru berasal dari sebagian warga, dan belum ada pengukuran kualitas air sungai sebelum dan sesudah kegiatan</Highlight> (the collected oil comes only from some residents, and river water quality has not been measured before and after). Neither fact proves **the river itself is clean**.

Paragraph four reinforces this gap because the group still only **plans to ask officers to sample river water every three months**.

## Why the other four evaluations fail

- **Proven by <InlineMath math="240" /> liters collected** confuses collection volume with actual river cleanliness.
- **False because the soap did not sell** contradicts the text, which never reports poor sales and notes a price of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" /> per bar.
- **Accurate because the soap is cheap** links **retail price** to river pollution with no logical bridge.
- **Irrelevant because caustic soda is used** mistakes a soap ingredient for evidence about water quality.

# Answer

The best evaluation is that **the claim is not yet backed by data because oil has been collected from only some residents and river water quality has not been measured**.

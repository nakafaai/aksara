# Given

In paragraph two of the **lost cat** story, the narrator notes that **Nayla did not really like cats**, yet helped out of sympathy for Bu Wening. That evening at the end of the story, **Nayla writes in her diary that she wants to adopt one of the kittens**.

# Asked

The strongest evidence that Nayla's feelings toward cats have begun to change.

# Solution

Helping look for a neighbor's lost pet only proves you **care about the neighbor**, not that you like the animal. The real shift shows up when Nayla privately admits she wants to **raise a kitten herself**.

> Proof that a character's feeling toward something has changed must show a new personal desire toward that thing itself, not merely kindness to its owner.

## Contrasting her starting attitude with her diary note

Paragraph two states her starting point plainly: <Highlight>Nayla sebenarnya tidak terlalu suka kucing</Highlight> (Nayla did not really like cats). Her search efforts flow from compassion for Bu Wening.

The genuine shift appears in the final sentence: <Highlight>ia ingin memelihara salah satu anak kucing itu</Highlight> (she wants to adopt one of the kittens). Wanting a cat of her own directly reverses her earlier indifference and proves **her feelings toward cats have warmed**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Borrowing her mother's phone for flyers** shows **empathy for Bu Wening**, not fondness for cats.
- **Cycling to the rice warehouse** likewise reflects **helping her elderly neighbor**.
- **Calling her mother because she did not know what to do** shows **uncertainty around cats**, not a change of heart.
- **Posting flyers at the guard post** is simply part of her effort to assist Bu Wening.

# Answer

The strongest evidence is that **Nayla wrote in her diary that she wanted to adopt one of the kittens.**

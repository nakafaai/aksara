# Given

Paragraph two of the **pencak silat club** passage opens with Pak Rahmat explaining that **pencak silat does not only teach self-defense techniques**. Every practice opens with a **bow of respect**, students learn to **control their strength** so they do not hurt partners, and the closing line declares **respect and self-control to be the core of pencak silat**.

# Asked

The main idea of paragraph two.

# Solution

If a coach says swimming is not just about kicking fast, then stresses safety and teamwork, the real message is **the values behind the skill**. Here Pak Rahmat highlights **respect and self-control** rather than fighting moves alone.

> When a paragraph's opening claim and closing summary state the same central message, they lock down its main idea directly.

## Checking the opening and closing lines

Both ends of paragraph two frame the same point: <Highlight>pencak silat tidak hanya mengajarkan teknik membela diri</Highlight> (it does not only teach self-defense) and <Highlight>nilai hormat dan kendali diri itulah inti pencak silat</Highlight> (respect and self-control are the heart of pencak silat).

The two middle examples, **greeting coach and opponent with respect** and **controlling force during partner drills**, exist to illustrate those exact **core values**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Only teaches winning matches** directly contradicts the paragraph's focus on values beyond fighting.
- **Every session opens with a match** is false because practice starts with a **salute of respect**.
- **Students are banned from paired drills** is false since they **do train in pairs** while restraining their power.
- **UNESCO intangible heritage recognition** belongs to **paragraph one**, not paragraph two.

# Answer

The main idea of paragraph two is that **pencak silat teaches respect and self-control, not only self-defense techniques**.

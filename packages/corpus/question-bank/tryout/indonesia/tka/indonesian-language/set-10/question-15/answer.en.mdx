# Given

The mini-bank committee launches the **"save one thousand a day"** program with certificates for at least <InlineMath math="20" /> deposits per semester to fix the habit of saving only at the start of the term and then stopping.

# Asked

The everyday practice that best applies the idea behind "save one thousand a day".

# Solution

Good saving works like **steady drops filling a jar**: setting aside a small amount regularly builds a lasting habit, whereas putting in a lump sum once and quitting never sticks.

> Applying a habit-building program to daily life means adopting its core routine rather than chasing a temporary reward.

## Extracting the core habit behind the program

The <Highlight>menabung seribu sehari</Highlight> (save one thousand a day) initiative combines a modest daily sum with a <InlineMath math="20" />-deposit milestone to cultivate **small, repeatable discipline**.

In everyday life, that translates directly to **setting aside a little money regularly rather than saving a large sum once and stopping**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Saving only when receiving a large amount** repeats the uneven habit the program aims to fix.
- **Spending all allowance before month's end** opposes the purpose of saving.
- **Borrowing from friends just to deposit** creates debt rather than healthy money habits.
- **Stopping right after earning the certificate** treats the reward as the goal instead of building a lasting routine.

# Answer

The best everyday application is **setting aside a small amount of money regularly rather than saving a lot all at once and then stopping**.

# Given

In the **grandmother's recipe book** story, Sinta fails three times on her own. Just as she almost gives up, **her mother squeezes the dough** and shares Grandmother's rule that proper dough neither sticks nor cracks. Right afterward, **Sinta follows her mother's technique and succeeds on the fourth try**.

# Asked

The strongest evidence that her mother's help was the key to Sinta's success.

# Solution

To prove someone's help was the **turning point**, look right at the **before-and-after shift**: three failed tries alone, followed immediately by success the moment Sinta **copies her mother's dough-kneading check**.

> The strongest evidence for a decisive turning point links applying a specific piece of guidance directly to the breakthrough result.

## Tracing the direct breakthrough in paragraph four

Paragraph four places cause and result back to back: <Highlight>Sinta menambahkan tepung sedikit demi sedikit sambil meremas adonan seperti yang ditunjukkan Ibu. Pada percobaan keempat, nastar keluar dari oven dengan warna keemasan</Highlight> (Sinta added flour bit by bit while kneading as Mother showed her, and on the fourth try the tarts came out golden brown).

This immediate sequence from **following Mother's demonstration** to **succeeding on attempt four** proves her mother's guidance was decisive.

## Why the other four options fail

- **The first batch spread flat on the tray** occurred **before Mother stepped in**.
- **Keeping the book in a kitchen drawer** is merely opening background.
- **Her aunt's question about baking *nastar*** only starts the plot.
- **Slanted handwriting** describes the recipe book rather than Mother's help.

# Answer

The strongest evidence is that **Sinta added flour bit by bit while kneading the dough as her mother showed her, and then succeeded on the fourth attempt**.

# Given

In paragraph two of the **school butterfly garden** passage, students initially **planted only colorful flowers**, so butterflies **stopped by briefly and left**. The science teacher then explained that **butterflies need host plants to reproduce**, and without them the garden is only a brief stopover.

# Asked

The main idea of paragraph two.

# Solution

Think of a roadside rest stop with snacks but no beds: travelers **stop briefly and move on**. Colorful nectar flowers invite butterflies to feed, yet **host plants** are essential if they are to lay eggs and stay.

> A paragraph's main idea unites the opening observation with the core explanation that ties every sentence together.

## Connecting the opening problem and teacher explanation

Paragraph two starts with students planting **only colorful flowers**, leaving butterflies to **visit briefly and leave**. The science teacher then states the core cause: <Highlight>kupu-kupu membutuhkan tumbuhan inang untuk berkembang biak. Tanpa tumbuhan itu, taman hanya menjadi tempat singgah</Highlight> (butterflies need host plants to reproduce, and without them the garden is merely a stopover).

Together, these lines point to one main idea: **a garden with only flowers is not enough because butterflies need host plants to breed**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Butterflies dislike colorful flowers** clashes with the text, which notes they **did visit** the flowers.
- **The teacher forbade planting flowers** invents a ban when the teacher simply explained adding host plants.
- **Adult butterflies eat citrus leaves** confuses stages, since **caterpillars** eat leaves while adults drink **nectar**.
- **The garden covers two volleyball courts** is stated in **paragraph one**, not paragraph two.

# Answer

The main idea of paragraph two is that **a garden containing only flowers is not enough because butterflies need host plants to reproduce**.

# Given

The goal of the **butterfly garden** is for butterflies not just to stop by, but to **reproduce** there. When some parents suggest spraying leaf-eating caterpillars with pesticide, **students refuse, explaining that caterpillars are the very stage of a butterfly's life they have been waiting for**.

# Asked

The relationship between the students' refusal to spray pesticide and the purpose of building the garden.

# Solution

If you plant a mango tree to harvest fruit and someone suggests cutting off every blossom because petals look messy, you refuse because **without blossoms there will never be fruit**. Likewise, **without caterpillars there will never be new butterflies**.

> Protecting an essential middle stage in a life cycle directly safeguards the final goal of the entire project.

## Connecting caterpillars to the breeding goal

Paragraph two explains that host plants were added so **butterflies can breed**. Paragraph three then states the link directly: <Highlight>Siswa menolak dengan menjelaskan bahwa ulat adalah tahap hidup kupu-kupu yang justru mereka tunggu</Highlight> (students refused, explaining that caterpillars are the butterfly life stage they were waiting for).

Killing caterpillars with pesticide would break the cycle and **defeat the garden's very purpose**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Pesticide is too expensive** is never mentioned anywhere in the passage.
- **Caterpillars do not damage leaves at all** contradicts the text, which notes they **eat leaves until full of holes**.
- **The refusal drove butterflies away** reverses the outcome, since sightings **rose sharply by month four**.
- **Students wanted caterpillars moved to another school** adds an idea absent from the passage.

# Answer

The relationship is that **caterpillars are a stage in a butterfly's life, so killing them would defeat the purpose of the garden**.

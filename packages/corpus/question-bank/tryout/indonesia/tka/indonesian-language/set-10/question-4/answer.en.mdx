# Given

In the **butterfly garden** weekly log, average sightings climbed from **<InlineMath math="6" /> butterflies (<InlineMath math="3" /> species)** in month one to **<InlineMath math="17" /> butterflies (<InlineMath math="7" /> species)** in month four. However, the science teacher cautions that **month four coincided with blooming season around the school**, so part of the increase may not come solely from the garden.

# Asked

The most accurate evaluation of the rise in butterflies from month one to month four.

# Solution

Suppose iced-lemonade sales jump during the **hottest week of summer**. The numbers look **promising**, yet an honest review admits the hot weather likely helped too. Here the **local blooming season** is that outside factor.

> A balanced evaluation of evidence acknowledges encouraging gains while factoring in outside influences happening at the same time.

## Reading the increase alongside the teacher caution

Jumping from <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="17" /> butterflies and from <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="7" /> species is clearly **encouraging**. Still, the final sentence cautions: <Highlight>bulan keempat bertepatan dengan musim berbunga di sekitar sekolah sehingga sebagian kenaikan mungkin bukan hanya karena taman</Highlight> (month four coincided with blooming season nearby, so part of the rise may not stem solely from the garden).

Thus the fairest assessment is that **the increase is promising, yet part of it may stem from the blooming season around the school**.

## Why the other four evaluations fail

- **Proves the garden is the sole cause** ignores the teacher's warning about an **outside seasonal factor**.
- **Meaningless because checks last <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes** overlooks that consistent weekly intervals still reveal real trends.
- **Wrong because species dropped** contradicts the data, which rose from **<InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="7" /> species**.
- **Caused by parents spraying pesticide** is false because students **rejected pesticide use**.

# Answer

The best evaluation is that **the increase is promising, though part of it may be caused by the blooming season around the school**.

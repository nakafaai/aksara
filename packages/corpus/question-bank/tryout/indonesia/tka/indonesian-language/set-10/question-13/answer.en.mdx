# Given

In the **school mini-bank** passage, by year two only **<InlineMath math="360" /> out of <InlineMath math="600" /> students** had opened accounts, and near the end of each semester **deposits fell by almost half**. A teacher writes in the school magazine that **"the mini-bank has already made all students diligent savers"**.

# Asked

The most accurate evaluation of the teacher's magazine statement.

# Solution

The statement makes two sweeping claims at once: **all students** and **diligent**. Since <InlineMath math="240" /> students still have no account and deposits plunge late in the semester, the claim plainly **overstates the facts**.

> Absolute words like *all* and consistency claims like *diligent* must be checked directly against both participation numbers and steady performance.

## Testing both parts of the claim against paragraph three

Paragraph three gives the exact limits: <Highlight>Pada tahun pertama, 210 dari 600 siswa membuka tabungan. Pada tahun kedua, jumlahnya naik menjadi 360 siswa</Highlight> (rising to <InlineMath math="360" /> out of <InlineMath math="600" /> students in year two), while **deposits drop nearly by half at semester's end**.

Neither **all students** nor **steady diligence** holds true, making the teacher's claim **exaggerated**.

## Why the other four evaluations fail

- **Accurate because savers rose in year two** ignores that <InlineMath math="360" /> of <InlineMath math="600" /> is still not **every student**, nor are deposits steady.
- **Accurate because student tellers rotate monthly** describes staffing rather than proving universal saving habits.
- **False because no students save** contradicts the **<InlineMath math="360" /> active savers**.
- **Irrelevant because a regional bank assists** does not bear on whether the teacher overstated the numbers.

# Answer

The best evaluation is that **the statement is exaggerated because only <InlineMath math="360" /> out of <InlineMath math="600" /> students save and deposits fall at the end of the semester**.

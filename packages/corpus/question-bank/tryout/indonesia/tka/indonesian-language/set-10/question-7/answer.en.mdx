# Given

After succeeding on her fourth try, Sinta takes a pencil and **writes in the margin in small letters so she does not cover her grandmother's handwriting**: about <InlineMath math="450" /> grams of flour, dough neither sticky nor cracking, <InlineMath math="150" /> degrees for <InlineMath math="25" /> minutes.

# Asked

The meaning behind Sinta writing small margin notes so they do not cover her grandmother's handwriting.

# Solution

Imagine inheriting an heirloom map and jotting a modern landmark lightly in pencil **along the edge without marking over the original ink**. You are **making the guide easier for the next person** while **treating the original with deep respect**.

> Looking at both *what* a character adds and *how* carefully they place it reveals two complementary motives at once.

## Reading both sides of Sinta action in the closing paragraph

In content, Sinta records **clear measurements**: <InlineMath math="450" /> grams of flour, dough texture, <InlineMath math="150" /> degrees, and <InlineMath math="25" /> minutes so future bakers can succeed more easily.

In manner, the text stresses: <Highlight>menulis di pinggir halaman resep, dengan huruf kecil agar tidak menutupi tulisan Nenek</Highlight> (writing in the margin in small letters so as not to cover Grandmother's handwriting). That care shows **deep respect for her grandmother's original notes**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Replacing Grandmother's recipe** fails because Sinta preserves every original word untouched.
- **Ashamed of failing three times** fails because she writes practical guidance rather than hiding embarrassment.
- **Wanting to erase the strange measures** fails because she **adds margin notes** without erasing anything.
- **Avoiding her mother's anger** fails because her mother was never angry and **smiled through tears**.

# Answer

Sinta's action means that **she wants to complete the recipe for future bakers while still respecting her grandmother's handwriting**.

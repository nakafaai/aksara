# Given

The **Rain Garden** table records gate-puddle durations of <InlineMath math="46" /> and <InlineMath math="51" /> minutes before construction, versus <InlineMath math="24" /> and <InlineMath math="28" /> minutes afterward, while rainfall intensity, drain cleaning, and prior soil moisture were not measured separately.

# Asked

Which report sentence accurately matches the evidence without overstating it.

# Solution

Imagine getting new running shoes and running faster on **your next two practice laps** than on the prior two. An honest report states that those two runs were quicker without claiming the shoes alone caused the improvement.

> A sound report sentence states only the observed pattern and stays clear of unproven single-cause claims or broad generalizations.

## Checking the sentence against table and text

Paragraph three explicitly limits the claim: <Highlight>Data itu menunjukkan genangan surut lebih cepat pada dua kejadian setelah pembangunan</Highlight> (the data show puddles drained faster in the two post-construction events), while <Highlight>tim belum menyebut taman sebagai satu-satunya penyebab</Highlight> (the team does not yet name the garden as the sole cause).

Stating that **in the two rainstorms observed after construction, puddles receded faster than in the two earlier observations** is completely accurate and disciplined.

## Why the other four sentences overreach

- **Naming the garden the primary cause** ignores drain cleaning and unmeasured rainfall intensity.
- **Generalizing to other schools** stretches beyond two storms at one gate.
- **Claiming rainfall intensity was lower** invents data the text says were never measured.
- **Saying no further data are needed** contradicts the team's new rain-gauge monitoring plan.

# Answer

The most accurate report sentence is that in the two rain events observed after construction, puddles receded faster than in the two prior observations.

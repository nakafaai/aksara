# Given

The **Study Breaks** text compares two <InlineMath math="30" />-item practice sessions (one without a break, one with a short break) among twelve Grade XII students in a fixed order, stressing that neither causation nor general effectiveness is proven yet.

# Asked

Which title accurately describes the passage without overstating its findings.

# Solution

Imagine titling **notes from testing two cookie recipes in your kitchen**. Calling it "Proven Best Cookie Formula for Every Baker" clearly exaggerates. A fair title simply names the exact two recipes you compared.

> An honest scientific title describes the actual comparison conducted and avoids causal or sweeping words like *effect* or *effectiveness* when confounders remain.

## Matching the title to what was truly tested

The group compared two practice packets (Tuesday without breaks and Thursday with a break) while stressing <Highlight>Kelompok tidak langsung menyimpulkan bahwa jeda menyebabkan peningkatan</Highlight> (the group did not conclude that the break caused the gain).

The title **Perbandingan Hasil Dua Paket Latihan dengan dan tanpa Jeda** (Comparison of Results on Two Practice Sets With and Without a Break) is purely descriptive and faithful to the evidence.

## Why the other four titles fail

- **Effect of a Five-Minute Break** and **Effectiveness Test on High School Students** overclaim causation and broad generalization across all high schoolers.
- **With Different Orders** is factually wrong, as counterbalanced orders are only planned for the next trial.
- **After Forty Minutes of Practice** drops the central contrast between break and no-break sessions.

# Answer

The most accurate title without overstatement is Perbandingan Hasil Dua Paket Latihan dengan dan tanpa Jeda (Comparison of Results on Two Practice Sets With and Without a Break).

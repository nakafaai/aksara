# Given

The **Study Breaks** simulation reports measured scores in paragraph three (late-section averages rising from <InlineMath math="10.8" /> without a break to <InlineMath math="12.7" /> with a break) and lists design and sampling constraints in paragraph four.

# Asked

How to classify three items as either a result or a limitation of the simulation.

# Solution

Think of a taste-test report: **which cake scored higher** is the result, whereas **every judge tasting the same cake first while hungriest** is a limitation affecting how strongly you can trust the score.

> A result states what participants actually scored or achieved, while a limitation identifies design or sample conditions that restrict causal claims.

## Sorting the three items using paragraphs three and four

Paragraph three reports the outcome: <Highlight>Pada bagian akhir, rata-ratanya <InlineMath math="10{,}8" /> tanpa jeda dan <InlineMath math="12{,}7" /> dengan jeda</Highlight> (in the second half, averages were <InlineMath math="10.8" /> without a break and <InlineMath math="12.7" /> with a break). Paragraph four lists constraints: <Highlight>Semua peserta menjalani kondisi tanpa jeda terlebih dahulu</Highlight> (all participants ran no-break first) and <Highlight>dua belas peserta berasal dari satu kelas</Highlight> (twelve participants came from one class).

| Element | Category | Textual evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Higher late-section average with a break | Result | Observed performance finding in paragraph three. |
| All participants followed the same order | Limitation | Creates a possible practice effect from Tuesday to Thursday. |
| Participants came from one class | Limitation | Narrow sample restricting generalization in paragraph four. |

## Checking the boundary between categories

The score difference is an empirical **result**, while fixed session order and single-class sampling are both methodological **limitations**.

# Answer

**A higher late-section average on the break session is a result, whereas having all participants follow the same order and drawing participants from one class are limitations**.

# Given

After splicing the bamboo frame and reattaching paper at Grandfather Jati's workbench, Nara complains that the kite looks imperfect. Grandfather hands her the string and takes her to the **open field in steady afternoon wind**, where the kite rises cleanly without spinning.

# Asked

What narrative function the open field setting serves in the story.

# Solution

Imagine tightening **your bicycle brakes inside the garage**. You cannot truly trust them until you roll outside and test them on a real road. The windy field plays that exact proving role for the repaired kite.

> A testing setting puts workshop craftsmanship against real-world forces so both character and reader see whether the repair actually holds.

## Seeing the field test in paragraph four

Paragraph four states directly: <Highlight>Mereka menguji layangan di lapangan ketika angin sore mulai tetap</Highlight> (they tested the kite on the field when the afternoon wind turned steady). There, <Highlight>Burung kertas itu naik tanpa berputar</Highlight> (the paper bird climbed without spinning) as the splice held the left wing steady.

The field setting serves **to test whether the repair works in real wind**, proving performance matters more than flawless looks.

## Why the other options fail

- **Selling the kite to judges** and **replacing the contest with music** never happen.
- **Hiding the bamboo slats** reverses the story, since the splice stays visible.
- **Claiming new paper is the main factor** directly contradicts the story's core lesson about the frame.

# Answer

The open field setting serves to test whether the repair works in real wind.

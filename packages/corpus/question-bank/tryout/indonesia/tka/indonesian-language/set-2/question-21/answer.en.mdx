# Given

The Grade XII **Study Breaks** two-session simulation found higher late-section scores with a short break, yet the group notes order effects, different days, and small class size, concluding the pattern is worth retesting rather than enforcing as one rule for everyone.

# Asked

Which practical application makes the most sense for a study group.

# Solution

Imagine a classmate finding that **a five-minute stretch** kept him sharp during one homework session. The smartest move is not forcing the whole school into one rigid rule, but trying a planned break yourself and tracking how your own focus responds.

> Practical takeaways from a small preliminary trial stay experimental and self-monitored rather than turning into rigid mandates or medical claims.

## Matching application to the closing conclusion

The final sentence states clearly that <Highlight>pola yang terlihat layak diuji lagi, bukan dijadikan aturan bahwa setiap orang harus beristirahat dengan cara yang sama</Highlight> (the pattern is worth testing again, not turning into a rule that everyone must rest the same way).

Therefore, **trying planned breaks while tracking personal results** is the most sensible takeaway.

## Why the other options fail

- **Enforcing a fixed five-minute rule** and **ignoring individual differences** directly violate the closing caveat.
- **Keeping the identical session order** repeats the exact design flaw they plan to fix.
- **Treating the results as medical advice** contradicts paragraph one stating it is <Highlight>bukan penelitian medis</Highlight> (not a medical study).

# Answer

The most reasonable application for a study group is **trying planned breaks while monitoring personal results**.

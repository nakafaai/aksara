# Given

Early in **The Kite Frame**, Nara wants shiny new paper and complains that visible patches spoil perfection. On contest day, a judge asks why the seam was left uncovered, and Nara explains what that repair line means to her.

# Asked

Which detail most strongly proves that Nara learned from repairing the kite with Grandfather Jati.

# Solution

Imagine learning to build **a sturdy paper boat**. The strongest proof you truly learned is not merely sitting beside your teacher, but explaining in your own words to a stranger why the folded brace matters more than shiny stickers.

> The clearest proof of character learning is when the protagonist puts the lesson into her own words and choices independently.

## Reading the answer to the judge at the end

In the final paragraph, when a judge asks why she left the repair uncovered, <Highlight>Nara menjawab bahwa garis itu menunjukkan tempat ia belajar mendengar rangka sebelum mempercantik permukaan</Highlight> (Nara replied that the line marked where she learned to listen to the frame before decorating the surface).

Her **explaining the meaning of the repair line to the judge** proves she made Grandfather's lesson truly her own.

## Why the other details are weaker proof

- **Bringing the frame to Grandfather** and **closing her eyes when asked** happen at the beginning, before her view shifts.
- **Not winning best decoration** and **steady afternoon wind** are outside circumstances rather than proof of personal growth.

# Answer

The strongest proof that Nara learned from the repair process is that she explains the meaning of the repair line to the judge.

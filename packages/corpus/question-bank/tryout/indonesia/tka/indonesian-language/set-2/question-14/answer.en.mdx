# Given

The school keeps the **rain garden** because the basin works well, plants grow, and the gate clears sooner in two storms after construction, while the team's report notes that stronger causal proof needs more rain events and measurements of other factors.

# Asked

Which conclusion best matches the data in the Rain Garden passage.

# Solution

Imagine putting **a new doormat by your front door** and noticing the hallway stays drier on the next two rainy days. That is a solid practical reason to keep the mat, even before proving it was the sole cause.

> A well-balanced conclusion recognizes visible practical value while staying honest about what single-cause proof still requires.

## Combining both parts of the closing paragraph

The final paragraph separates both claims cleanly. Keeping the garden because <Highlight>area gerbang lebih cepat dapat dilalui pada dua pengamatan setelah pembangunan</Highlight> (the gate cleared faster in two observations) is practical.

Meanwhile, <Highlight>klaim sebab-akibat yang lebih kuat membutuhkan lebih banyak kejadian hujan dan pengukuran faktor lain</Highlight> (stronger causal proof needs more rain events and other measurements).

Thus **the two post-construction observations support practical benefits, yet do not prove a single cause**.

## Why the other conclusions fail

- **Eliminating puddles altogether** is false, since puddles still lasted <InlineMath math="24" /> and <InlineMath math="28" /> minutes.
- **Claiming drain cleaning could not matter** clashes directly with paragraph three.
- **Four storms representing a full season** and **tearing down the garden** contradict the school's decision to keep it and collect more data.

# Answer

The conclusion that best fits the data is that the two post-construction observations support practical benefits, yet do not prove a single cause.

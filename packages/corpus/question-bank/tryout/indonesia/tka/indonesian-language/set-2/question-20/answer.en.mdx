# Given

In **The Kite Frame**, Grandfather Jati never lectures Nara. He invites her to close her eyes and listen to the bamboo, repairs the frame alongside her, hands her the string for the field test, and lets her discover on her own why to leave the seam uncovered.

# Asked

Which emotional response is most reasonable toward Grandfather Jati's attitude.

# Solution

Picture a cooking mentor who **never snatches the spoon out of your hand**, but lets you smell the spices, stir beside her, and taste the soup until the lesson clicks on its own. That calm guidance naturally inspires genuine **appreciation**.

> A grounded emotional response toward a mentor figure mirrors the exact teaching style the character practices across the scenes.

## Observing patient guidance across the scenes

Grandfather guides through experience: he <Highlight>meminta Nara menutup mata</Highlight> (asks Nara to close her eyes) to hear the crack, then <Highlight>menyerahkan benang kepadanya</Highlight> (hands her the kite string) on the field so she feels the repair hold firsthand, and ends with four quiet, proud taps instead of boasting.

The most justified response is **appreciating Grandfather's patience in guiding Nara until she discovers the meaning of repair herself**.

## Why the other responses misread Grandfather

- **Ignoring the frame for shiny looks** reverses Grandfather's exact priority.
- **Covering up every repair mark** is false, since he leaves the lighter splice visible.
- **Ignoring flight testing** contradicts his taking Nara straight to the windy field.
- **Denying Nara a chance to learn** clashes with his involving her at every single step.

# Answer

The most reasonable emotional response is appreciating Grandfather's patience in guiding Nara until she discovers the meaning of repair on her own.

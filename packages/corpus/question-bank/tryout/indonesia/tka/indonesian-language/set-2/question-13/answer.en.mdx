# Given

In the **Rain Garden** follow-up plan, besides logging puddle duration, rainfall amount, drain cleaning, and soil moisture, the team also **marks the highest water line** on the gate wall.

# Asked

Why the team marks the highest water level on the gate wall.

# Solution

Imagine two yard puddles both draining in **twenty minutes**, yet one barely wets your sneakers while the other reaches your knees. Timing alone makes them look identical, even though one flood is far more severe.

> Duration alone leaves out flood depth, marking peak water height captures how severe each puddle actually gets.

## Reading the direct purpose in paragraph four

Paragraph four explains that the team <Highlight>menandai batas air tertinggi pada dinding gerbang</Highlight> (marks the highest water line on the gate wall) in order to <Highlight>menilai durasi dan tinggi genangan</Highlight> (assess both the duration and height of standing water).

Marking the peak level lets them **compare the severity of pooling alongside its duration**.

## Why the other options fail

- **Keeping water out of the garden** misses the whole purpose of a rain garden, which is built to receive runoff.
- **Skipping drain checks** or **ignoring rainfall intensity** contradicts the very same paragraph, where the team adds both drain logs and a rain gauge.
- **Moving plants to the gate** never appears in the passage.

# Answer

The highest water mark is added so the severity of puddles can be compared in addition to their duration.

# Given

The student-council chair writes that the drive **"proves everyone at our school is healthy"**. In fact, pre-donation screening checks only **whether a person can safely donate that day**, not overall health, and **<InlineMath math="54" /> of <InlineMath math="186" /> registrants failed screening**.

# Asked

The fairest evaluation of the sentence "proves everyone at our school is healthy".

# Solution

Imagine passing a temperature check at a door and claiming every person inside has zero illnesses, even while <InlineMath math="54" /> people were turned away. Such a sweeping claim is plainly **unsupported by the data**.

> Universal health claims fail whenever the screening test is narrow in scope and dozens of applicants fail to pass.

## Testing the sentence against both facts in paragraph three

The officer cites two clear limits: <Highlight>pemeriksaan sebelum donor hanya memastikan seseorang aman menyumbangkan darah pada hari itu, bukan pemeriksaan kesehatan menyeluruh</Highlight> (only confirms safe same-day donation, not a full checkup) and <Highlight><InlineMath math="54" /> pendaftar justru tidak lolos pemeriksaan</Highlight> (<InlineMath math="54" /> registrants failed screening).

Therefore, **the sentence is unsupported by data because screening only checks same-day donation safety and <InlineMath math="54" /> registrants did not pass**.

## Why the other four evaluations miss

- **Accurate because <InlineMath math="132" /> bags were collected** measures turnout, not school-wide health.
- **Accurate because everyone was screened** ignores the narrow scope of pre-donation checks.
- **Nobody passed screening** contradicts <InlineMath math="132" /> people donating blood.
- **Irrelevant because teachers and parents joined** misses the core evidence gap.

# Answer

The best evaluation is that **the sentence is unsupported by the data because screening only verifies same-day donation safety and <InlineMath math="54" /> registrants did not pass**.

# Given

In paragraph four, the analyst warns that **charging takes about four hours**, meaning extra backup buses are needed if schedules expand. In paragraph five, the city plans to **build two fast-charging points in the industrial zone so buses can top up between trips**.

# Asked

The connection between the analyst's charging-time warning and the city government's plan.

# Solution

Imagine a friend warning your tablet takes four hours to charge overnight, so you add a fast charger at your desk to top up during short breaks. The city's fast-charging plan directly **answers the slow-charging bottleneck**.

> When a closing infrastructure plan directly eases the timing constraint raised one paragraph earlier, the link is a targeted operational response.

## Linking the charging warning to the quick charging plan

Paragraph four states the constraint: <Highlight>pengisian daya membutuhkan waktu sekitar empat jam</Highlight> (charging takes about four hours). Paragraph five answers it directly: <Highlight>membangun dua titik pengisian cepat di kawasan industri agar bus dapat mengisi daya di sela perjalanan</Highlight> (building two fast-charging points so buses can recharge between trips).

Thus, **fast-charging stations respond to long charging times so buses can recharge between trips**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Cannot charge at the terminal** contradicts paragraph one stating they charge nightly at the terminal.
- **Cutting bus prices in half** has no connection to charging stations.
- **Eliminating battery replacement** is never claimed.
- **Built for diesel buses** makes no sense because diesel buses do not charge electricity.

# Answer

The link is that **the fast-charging points address the long charging time by letting buses recharge between trips**.

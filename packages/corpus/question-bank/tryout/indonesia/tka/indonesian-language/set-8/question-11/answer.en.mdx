# Given

In paragraph three of **School Blood Drive**, the student-council chair writes that the drive **"proves everyone at our school is healthy"**. A transfusion officer immediately **asks to revise that line** because pre-donation screening is not a full medical checkup and **<InlineMath math="54" /> registrants did not pass**.

# Asked

The main idea capturing the entire third paragraph.

# Solution

Imagine an organizer claiming everyone is healthy just because people lined up to donate, and a nurse stepping in: pre-donation checks only verify same-day donation safety, plus <InlineMath math="54" /> people were turned away. The **main idea** is that the chair's conclusion must be corrected.

> When every supporting line explains why an opening claim overreaches, the main idea centers on the need to revise that claim.

## Tracing the claim and the officer correction

The student chair claims <Highlight>membuktikan warga sekolah kita sehat semua</Highlight> (proves our whole school is healthy). The officer responds directly: <Highlight>Petugas unit transfusi meminta kalimat itu diperbaiki</Highlight> (asked for the sentence to be corrected) because screening is not a comprehensive checkup and <InlineMath math="54" /> failed.

Hence, **the student chair's conclusion needs revision because pre-donation screening is not a comprehensive health exam**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Everyone proven healthy** repeats the exact overstatement being corrected.
- **Opposing school blood drives** misreads constructive feedback as rejection.
- **<InlineMath math="54" /> donors giving twice** reverses the fact that <InlineMath math="54" /> registrants were deferred.
- **Student chair checking blood pressure** never appears in the passage.

# Answer

The main idea of paragraph three is that **the student chair's conclusion must be corrected because pre-donation screening is not a full medical exam**.

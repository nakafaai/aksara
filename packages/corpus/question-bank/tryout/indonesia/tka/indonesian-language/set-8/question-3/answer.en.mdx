# Given

The **sasi** tradition uses a **community-agreed temporary pause** on harvesting so natural resources can **grow and reproduce**, allowing a richer shared harvest once *buka sasi* opens again.

# Asked

The core principle of *sasi* most applicable to everyday life.

# Solution

Think of a worn-out school soccer field: resting the grass for a few weeks so it grows back thick lets everyone enjoy it far longer. That **rest-and-recovery principle** is the heart of *sasi*.

> Applying a traditional conservation practice to daily life captures its core sustainability goal: letting resources recover before harvesting again.

## Distilling the core principle from paragraphs one and three

Paragraph one defines the shared pause: <Highlight>dilarang mengambil hasil alam tertentu ... dalam jangka waktu yang disepakati bersama</Highlight> (banning harvest for a mutually agreed time). Paragraph three explains its purpose: <Highlight>memberi kesempatan siput tumbuh dan berkembang biak</Highlight> (giving snails time to grow and breed).

Thus, the applicable principle is **giving a resource time to recover before using it again**.

## Why the other four principles clash with sasi

- **Taking as much as possible first** destroys the resource and reverses *sasi*.
- **Making rules without involving residents** violates *disepakati bersama* (mutually agreed).
- **Eliminating sanctions** undermines rule enforcement.
- **Selling everything to outsiders** misstates how proceeds fund local divers and community facilities.

# Answer

The principle best applied to daily life is **giving a resource time to recover before using it again**.

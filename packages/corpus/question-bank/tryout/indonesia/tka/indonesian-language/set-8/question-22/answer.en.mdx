# Given

The mayor declares electric buses are **"definitely cheaper and more practical in every way"**. A transport analyst counters that full costs must include **nearly double the purchase price**, **costly battery replacement**, and **four-hour charging times** requiring backup buses.

# Asked

The fairest evaluation of the mayor's sweeping claim.

# Solution

Imagine calling a scooter **"cheaper and handier in every single way"** solely for low electricity bills while ignoring a double sticker price, costly battery swaps, and four-hour recharges. Claiming *in every way* is clearly **exaggerated**.

> Absolute claims like *definitely* and *in every way* are exaggerated whenever major lifecycle costs and operational trade-offs are left out.

## Testing the claim against the analyst objections

The analyst pinpoints every missing piece: <Highlight>perhitungan biaya harus mencakup harga pembelian dan penggantian baterai, bukan hanya biaya energi</Highlight> (calculations must include purchase price and battery replacement, not just energy) and <Highlight>pengisian daya membutuhkan waktu sekitar empat jam</Highlight> (charging takes about four hours).

Therefore, **the statement is exaggerated because it overlooks purchase price, battery replacement, and long charging times**.

## Why the other four evaluations miss

- **Accurate due to lower energy costs** looks only at one operating line item.
- **Wrong because electric buses waste more energy** flips <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{.}200" /> versus diesel's <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}4{.}000" /> per km.
- **Accurate because riders like quiet rides** does not prove cheaper or more practical in every way.
- **Unrelated to the trial** misses that the mayor is reacting directly to trial outcomes.

# Answer

The best evaluation is that **the statement is exaggerated because it omits purchase price, battery replacement, and long charging times**.

# Given

Blood-drive records show **<InlineMath math="186" /> people registered**, **<InlineMath math="54" /> failed screening**, and **<InlineMath math="132" /> bags of blood were collected**. The officer asked to replace the chair's sweeping health claim with an accurate line.

# Asked

The best replacement sentence for the student chair's report.

# Solution

Write a clean factual report line: state the exact **registrant count (<InlineMath math="186" />)** and the exact **qualified donors that day (<InlineMath math="132" />)** without sweeping claims of total disease-free health or citywide superiority.

> A strong factual replacement preserves exact numbers, checks arithmetic consistency, and confines claims strictly to what happened that day.

## Verifying the numbers against paragraph two

Paragraph two keeps every count exact (<InlineMath math="186 - 54 = 132" />): <Highlight><InlineMath math="186" /> orang mendaftar</Highlight> (<InlineMath math="186" /> registered), <Highlight><InlineMath math="54" /> orang tidak lolos pemeriksaan</Highlight> (<InlineMath math="54" /> did not pass), and <Highlight><InlineMath math="132" /> kantong darah terkumpul</Highlight> (<InlineMath math="132" /> bags collected).

Thus, the best line is: **Out of <InlineMath math="186" /> registrants, <InlineMath math="132" /> passed screening and donated blood that day**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **All students free of disease** repeats the exact overclaim rejected by the officer.
- **<InlineMath math="186" /> bags collected** confuses total registrants (<InlineMath math="186" />) with bags (<InlineMath math="132" />).
- **Only <InlineMath math="54" /> passed** swaps deferred applicants (<InlineMath math="54" />) with qualified donors (<InlineMath math="132" />).
- **Healthiest in the city** adds an unsupported superlative.

# Answer

The best replacement sentence is: **Out of <InlineMath math="186" /> registrants, <InlineMath math="132" /> passed screening and donated blood on that day**.

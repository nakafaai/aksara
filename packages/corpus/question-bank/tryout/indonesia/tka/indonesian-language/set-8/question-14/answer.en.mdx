# Given

Paragraph two reports that **<InlineMath math="54" /> registrants failed screening**, mainly due to **low hemoglobin** or **insufficient sleep**. Paragraph four gives two officer suggestions: **reminders to sleep enough and eat breakfast** plus advising **those with low hemoglobin to visit the health clinic**.

# Asked

The connection between why registrants failed screening and the officer's recommendations.

# Solution

Imagine runners dropping out from skipped warm-ups and dehydration, and organizers replying with warm-up reminders and water stations next year. Every single suggestion directly **answers a specific failure cause** found during screening.

> When closing recommendations pair one-to-one with earlier root causes, the link is a direct solution tailored to observed problems.

## Pairing paragraph two causes with paragraph four advice

Paragraph two lists the two main hurdles: <Highlight>kadar hemoglobinnya terlalu rendah atau karena kurang tidur pada malam sebelumnya</Highlight> (low hemoglobin or poor sleep). Paragraph four addresses both: <Highlight>tidur cukup dan sarapan sebelum donor</Highlight> (sleep well and eat breakfast) and <Highlight>hemoglobin rendah dianjurkan memeriksakan diri ke puskesmas</Highlight> (check low hemoglobin at the clinic).

Thus, **the suggestions respond directly to the observed causes of failing screening, such as poor sleep and low hemoglobin**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Reducing sign-ups next year** reverses the goal of helping more registrants qualify safely.
- **Unrelated to screening results** ignores the exact one-to-one match.
- **Banning deferred students from returning** contradicts recommending a clinic visit.
- **Raising the minimum donor age** never appears in the suggestions.

# Answer

The link is that **the suggestions directly address the causes of failing screening, namely insufficient sleep and low hemoglobin**.

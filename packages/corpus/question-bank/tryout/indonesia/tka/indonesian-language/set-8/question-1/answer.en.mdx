# Given

In the passage on **Sea Sasi in Negeri Waimahu**, residents may not harvest lola sea snails for two years. The text notes **"Warga yang melanggar dikenai sanksi adat, misalnya membayar denda kepada negeri atau membersihkan jalan kampung"** (violators face customary penalties such as paying a fine or cleaning village roads).

# Asked

The contextual meaning of the loanword *sanksi* in "Warga yang melanggar dikenai sanksi adat".

# Solution

Think of a classroom rule where breaking it means **paying a fine or sweeping the courtyard**. Because it applies directly to **rule-breakers**, *sanksi* clearly means **a penalty for someone who breaks a rule**, not approval or a ceremony.

> When a loanword carries opposite senses in other languages, the concrete examples listed right after it lock down the exact intended meaning.

## Reading the penalty examples in paragraph two

Paragraph two defines the word by example: <Highlight>Warga yang melanggar dikenai sanksi adat, misalnya membayar denda kepada negeri atau membersihkan jalan kampung</Highlight> (violators face customary sanctions like paying fines or cleaning village roads). Both examples are penalties.

Therefore, *sanksi* means **a punishment for people who violate the rules**.

## Why the other four meanings fail

- **Official approval or permission** clashes with the phrase *warga yang melanggar* (people who break the rule).
- **Ceremony lifting the ban** refers to *buka sasi*.
- **Guard group watching the reef** defines *kewang*.
- **Proceeds from lola sales** belongs to paragraph three rather than this term.

# Answer

In context, *sanksi* means **a penalty or punishment for anyone who breaks the rule**.

# Given

Arif is actually a **fifth-grade neighbor boy** who often buys crackers. Yet for an entire week he patiently drew phone icons and coached Nenek Sumi to master video calls, inspiring her to write **"For Teacher Arif"** on his gift bag of crackers.

# Asked

What the label "Untuk Pak Guru Arif" (For Teacher Arif) on the cracker bag means.

# Solution

Imagine a grandmother playfully calling the fifth-grader who patiently taught her smartphone buttons **"Mr. Teacher"**. That affectionate title does not mean he works at a school, it **honors him warmly as her teacher despite his young age**.

> An honorific given affectionately to a child in fiction expresses genuine gratitude for what they taught rather than a literal profession.

## Reading the affectionate title across paragraphs three and six

The story states plainly who he is: <Highlight>Arif, anak tetangga kelas lima SD</Highlight> (Arif, a fifth-grade neighbor boy). Because <Highlight>Arif menggambar tombol-tombol di secarik kertas</Highlight> (he drew button diagrams and taught her), writing <Highlight>Untuk Pak Guru Arif</Highlight> honors his patient teaching.

Hence, **Nenek Sumi appreciates Arif as the teacher who taught her, even though he is still a child**.

## Why the other four readings fail

- **Arif is actually a schoolteacher** contradicts him being a fifth grader.
- **Misspelling a customer's name** ignores her deliberate thank-you gesture.
- **Wishing he becomes a schoolteacher later** is never mentioned.
- **Selling crackers to Arif's teacher** misreads a free thank-you gift meant for Arif himself.

# Answer

The note means that **Nenek Sumi honors Arif as the teacher who patiently taught her, even though he is still a child**.

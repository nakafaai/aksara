# Given

**Low-Cost Air Sensors** walks through affordable school sensors (paragraph one), humidity and local-smoke errors (paragraph two), three researcher calibration steps (paragraph three), and how corrected sensors **complement official stations** (paragraph four).

# Asked

The main idea of the entire passage.

# Solution

Imagine describing **affordable mini cameras for a wildlife project**: cheap enough to place everywhere, imperfect on their own, yet able to widen coverage once calibrated and read properly alongside main gear. The passage's main idea holds both the promise and the conditions.

> The central idea of a complete expository text combines the benefit of a tool with the calibration and reading discipline needed to overcome its limits.

## Synthesizing all four paragraphs

After outlining calibration, daily averages, and location logging in paragraph three, paragraph four concludes: <Highlight>Jaringan sensor yang sudah dikoreksi dapat menunjukkan perbedaan kualitas udara antarlingkungan yang tidak terlihat dari satu stasiun saja</Highlight> (a corrected network reveals neighborhood differences a single station misses).

Thus **low-cost air sensors can widen monitoring when their limits are managed through calibration and proper reading**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **More accurate than official stations** flips the text, since cheap sensors must be calibrated against official stations.
- **Humid air creating more dust** confuses real dust with fog droplets being miscounted.
- **Schools buying official stations** ignores their huge cost, and **the one-million-rupiah price** is only one minor detail.

# Answer

The main idea is that low-cost air sensors can expand monitoring if their limitations are addressed through calibration and proper reading.

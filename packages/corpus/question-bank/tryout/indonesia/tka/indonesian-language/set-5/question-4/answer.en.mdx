# Given

Sukamaju Clinic calculates wait time solely as **the gap between pulling a ticket number and being called**. Home bookers log waiting time before arriving, while elderly visitors queuing at the desk before receiving a number go uncounted, prompting plans to **log arrival time at the entrance**.

# Asked

Which evaluation best judges the clinic's original way of measuring wait time.

# Solution

Imagine timing a **bakery queue** from the moment a receipt prints rather than when customers walk in. Anyone standing in line before getting a ticket is missed, while online pre-orders look like long waits. The measure fails because it skips real arrival time.

> Service wait times are accurate only when the clock starts at actual physical arrival rather than ticket issuance.

## Reading the root flaw in paragraphs two and four

Paragraph two identifies the method: <Highlight>selisih jam pengambilan nomor dan jam pasien dipanggil</Highlight> (time between taking a number and being called). Paragraph four confirms the exact fix needed: <Highlight>mencatat jam kedatangan setiap pasien di pintu masuk, bukan hanya jam pengambilan nomor</Highlight> (logging each patient's arrival time at the door, not just ticket pickup).

Thus **the method is inaccurate because it fails to record when patients actually arrive at the clinic**.

## Why the other four evaluations fail

- **Already accurate because it comes from an app** ignores both distortions in paragraph two.
- **Making elderly wait times look longer** gets it backward because omitting pre-ticket waits makes their recorded wait look *shorter*.
- **Needing no change** contradicts paragraph four's redesign, and **proving waits actually rose** goes beyond the text's data.

# Answer

The best evaluation is that **the method is inaccurate because it does not record when patients actually arrive at the clinic**.

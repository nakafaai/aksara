# Given

**Paragraph one of Low-Cost Air Sensors** introduces the affordability and convenience of lunchbox-sized sensors sending readings to phones every few minutes. **Paragraph two** opens straight with "That convenience comes with weaknesses" and details humidity bias and local smoke spikes.

# Asked

How paragraph two relates to paragraph one.

# Solution

Imagine praising **how handy an inexpensive new watch is**, then immediately adding "yet that convenience comes with a catch in heavy rain." The second paragraph directly balances the advantages introduced in the first.

> A transition sentence like "That convenience comes with weaknesses" signals a direct balance between strengths and limitations.

## Reading the direct pivot at paragraph two

Paragraph one highlights low cost and phone-ready readings every few minutes. Paragraph two pivots cleanly with <Highlight>Kemudahan itu disertai kelemahan</Highlight> (that convenience comes with weaknesses), explaining mist droplets and nearby kitchen or parking smoke.

Therefore, **paragraph two presents the weaknesses of low-cost sensors after paragraph one highlights their convenience**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Giving example schools** does not occur in paragraph two.
- **Denying it can count fine particles** misreads the flaw because it counts particles yet mistakes fog droplets for dust too.
- **Explaining why official stations cost more** and **urging a ban** clash with paragraph four treating cheap sensors as useful complements.

# Answer

Paragraph two presents the weaknesses of low-cost sensors after paragraph one explains their convenience.

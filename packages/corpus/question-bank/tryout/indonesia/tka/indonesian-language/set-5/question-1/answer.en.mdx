# Given

**Paragraph two of Sukamaju Clinic Queue** reports average wait time dropping from <InlineMath math="95" /> to <InlineMath math="40" /> minutes, then points out two flaws: patients booking from home wait less on site than logged, while elderly patients' counter queue before receiving a ticket is omitted altogether.

# Asked

The main idea of paragraph two.

# Solution

Imagine timing a school lunch only from **picking up a fork to finishing**, skipping the long line before getting a tray while still counting walk time from class. That logged average does not truly reflect how long people actually waited on site.

> The main idea of an evaluative paragraph joins the reported figure with the measurement gaps that qualify it.

## Pairing the reported drop with both measurement gaps

After quoting the drop from <InlineMath math="95" /> to <InlineMath math="40" /> minutes, paragraph two explains <Highlight>waktu yang benar-benar mereka habiskan di ruang tunggu lebih singkat daripada yang tercatat</Highlight> (actual waiting-room time is shorter for home bookers) while <Highlight>catatan itu tidak memuat lamanya pasien lansia mengantre di loket sebelum mendapat nomor</Highlight> (it omits elderly patients queuing before getting a number).

Thus **the reported wait-time drop does not fully reflect how long patients actually wait at the clinic**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Every patient waiting <InlineMath math="40" /> minutes at the clinic** accepts the flawed figure uncritically.
- **Elderly patients always waiting longer** overgeneralizes beyond the text.
- **Intentionally hiding data** invents bad intent absent from the passage.
- **Delayed text messages in weak-signal villages** appears in paragraph three, not two.

# Answer

The main idea of paragraph two is that **the reported drop in waiting time does not fully reflect how long patients actually wait at the clinic**.

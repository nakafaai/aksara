# Given

In **paragraph three of Clinic Queue**, Pak Harun (<InlineMath math="67" />) arrives at seven yet waits until nine for a number because manual registration is merged with the forgotten-card line. In **paragraph four**, responding to those complaints, the clinic opens a **dedicated manual registration counter** starting in June.

# Asked

How Pak Harun's experience connects to opening the dedicated counter in paragraph four.

# Solution

Imagine a student describing a two-hour library bottleneck where borrowing and late-fee lines got lumped together, prompting staff to open a **separate borrowing desk**. The personal story illustrates the exact problem that triggered the service fix.

> Linking a concrete user story to a subsequent policy forms a clear problem-and-response relationship.

## Connecting paragraphs three and four

Paragraph three presents Pak Harun's bottleneck: <Highlight>antrean pendaftaran manual disatukan dengan antrean pasien yang lupa membawa kartu berobat</Highlight> (manual sign-in was merged with patients who forgot clinic cards). Paragraph four immediately answers: <Highlight>Menanggapi keluhan itu, puskesmas membuka satu loket khusus untuk pendaftaran manual mulai Juni</Highlight> (responding to that complaint, the clinic opens one dedicated manual counter from June).

Thus **Pak Harun's experience illustrates the problem later addressed by opening a separate manual registration counter**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **The special counter causing his two-hour wait** reverses cause and timeline.
- **Proving the app useless for everyone** ignores home-booking patients who benefited.
- **Opening the counter only for forgotten cards** swaps the two lines because the new desk serves *manual registration*.
- **No connection** clashes directly with "Responding to those complaints".

# Answer

**Pak Harun's experience illustrates the problem that the clinic subsequently addresses by opening a dedicated manual registration counter**.

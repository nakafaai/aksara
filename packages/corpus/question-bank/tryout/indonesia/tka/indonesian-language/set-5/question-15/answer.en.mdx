# Given

In paragraphs two and three of **Low-Cost Air Sensors**, researchers warn against placing sensors beside local smoke sources and advise **three steps**: calibrate beside an official station, read daily averages, and log exact mounting details.

# Asked

Which action best follows the passage's advice if student council installs a low-cost sensor at school.

# Solution

Imagine following a **science-kit setup checklist** cleanly: hang the sensor well clear of kitchen chimneys and parking exhaust, write down its exact spot and height, and read the full-day average rather than one noisy spike.

> Sound real-world application combines the passage's positive guidelines while steering clear of every pitfall it warns against.

## Matching the action to paragraphs two and three

Paragraph two warns that sensors placed <Highlight>dekat dapur atau tempat parkir</Highlight> (near kitchens or parking lots) cause unrepresentative spikes. Paragraph three advises <Highlight>hasil dibaca sebagai rata-rata harian, bukan angka sesaat</Highlight> (read daily averages, not snapshots) and <Highlight>lokasi pemasangan dicatat dengan jelas</Highlight> (log the installation site clearly).

Therefore, **installing the sensor away from smoke sources, logging its location, and reading daily averages** follows every rule.

## Why the other four actions fail

- **Mounting beside the canteen kitchen** and **announcing each morning's peak** directly violate the warnings against local spikes and foggy morning bias.
- **Replacing official stations** clashes with paragraph four calling cheap sensors a *complement*.
- **Turning off sensors during rain** is never suggested and breaks daily averaging.

# Answer

The best action is **placing the sensor away from smoke sources, recording its location, and reading its daily averages**.

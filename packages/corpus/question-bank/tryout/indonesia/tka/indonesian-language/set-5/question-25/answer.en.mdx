# Given

In **paragraph three of Sea Turtle Nests**, warmer sand yields more female hatchlings while cooler sand yields more males, so volunteers install thermometers and **shade part of the nests with paranet netting** to keep sand temperatures from getting too high.

# Asked

How the sand-temperature fact relates to installing paranet shade netting.

# Solution

Imagine putting **a sunshade over half a seedling tray** so midday heat does not skew how the plants develop. The biological temperature effect is the exact reason volunteers add partial shading.

> A cause-and-action link in science writing connects a biological mechanism directly to the practical control used to keep it balanced.

## Reading the direct link in paragraph three

Paragraph three connects both sentences explicitly: <Highlight>Suhu pasir ternyata ikut menentukan jenis kelamin tukik</Highlight> (sand temperature helps determine hatchling sex), so volunteers <Highlight>menaungi sebagian sarang dengan jaring paranet agar suhu pasir tidak terlalu tinggi</Highlight> (shade some nests with paranet netting so sand does not overheat).

Thus **paranet netting is installed to prevent sand temperatures from rising too high because temperature affects hatchling sex**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **Protecting against stray dogs** is the role of the *fenced hatchery*, not paranet shade netting.
- **Making every hatchling male** ignores that only *some* nests are shaded for balance.
- **Trapping heat to raise sand temperature** reverses what shade netting does, and **blocking flash photography** misstates its purpose.

# Answer

**Paranet netting is installed to keep sand temperature from becoming too high because temperature influences hatchling sex**.

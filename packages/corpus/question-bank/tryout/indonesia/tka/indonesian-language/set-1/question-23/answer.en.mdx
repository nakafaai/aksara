# Given

In paragraph four of the Cafeteria Sorting passage, the managers state clear rules on what may enter the **biopore hole** and what must stay out to prevent bad odors and pests.

# Asked

Whether chopped vegetables, oily broth, and dry leaves are allowed or not allowed in the biopore hole according to the passage.

# Solution

Think of a biopore hole as **a gentle soil stomach** that digests light plant matter cleanly. Vegetable scraps and dry leaves break down smoothly, while oily broth smells sour and attracts unwanted animals.

> In procedural texts, allowed versus disallowed classifications must follow the exact inclusions and exclusions stated in the rules.

## Checking each material against paragraph four

Paragraph four states <Highlight>Sisa nabati yang sudah terpilah dimasukkan ke lubang biopori dalam porsi kecil dan ditutup daun kering</Highlight> (sorted plant scraps go into the biopore hole in small portions topped with dry leaves), while managers <Highlight>tidak memasukkan minyak, kuah, atau sisa hewani</Highlight> (exclude oil, broth, or animal scraps).

| Material | Category | Evidence from the rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chopped vegetables | Allowed | Sorted plant scraps placed in small portions. |
| Oily broth | Not allowed | Both oil and broth are explicitly banned to avoid odor and animals. |
| Dry leaves | Allowed | Used to cover plant scraps and balance excess moisture. |

## Confirming the role of dry leaves

Dry leaves are not rejected waste, because they actively **cover scraps and absorb extra moisture**, making them explicitly permitted inside the biopore hole.

# Answer

Chopped vegetables and dry leaves are allowed in the biopore hole, while oily broth is not allowed.

# Given

At the end of SMA Arunika's cafeteria sorting trial, the team concludes that **labels alone are not enough**, because clear instructions need real examples, brief reasons, and ongoing feedback.

# Asked

Which supporting idea most strongly backs the conclusion that labels alone are not enough.

# Solution

Imagine putting a sign saying **"Wooden Toys" on a bin**, yet a little sibling keeps tossing in plastic blocks that look similar. Mistakes drop only after you tape a real wooden block on the lid and explain the difference. That drop right after the fix is the strongest proof.

> The strongest evidence for a cause-and-effect conclusion is the measurable improvement that happens right after the missing ingredient is added.

## Tracing the direct proof from weeks two and three

Paragraph three reports that even with labeled bins, paper straws kept ending up in plant scraps. Once the team <Highlight>memasang contoh nyata pada tutup wadah dan menambahkan penjelasan</Highlight> (attached real examples to the lids and added an explanation about coatings), <Highlight>Jumlah kesalahan menurun pada minggu berikutnya</Highlight> (the number of mistakes dropped the next week).

That **drop in mistakes after real examples and explanations were added** directly proves labels alone fell short.

## Why the other facts are weaker support

- **Daily cafeteria waste** and **the four-week trial length** are only background context.
- **Checking the biopore hole regularly** relates to composting moisture, not bin labeling.
- **Volunteers standing near the bins** does not show the before-and-after drop in sorting errors.

# Answer

**The strongest supporting idea is that sorting mistakes decreased after real examples and explanations were added**.

# Given

At the end of the **Harbor Lamp** story, Raka no longer sits alone waiting for his father's sold boat. Village teenagers join him to set up pier emergency markers, while the old lamp stays safely kept to honor how his father watched the sea.

# Asked

Which emotional response best fits the ending of the story.

# Solution

Imagine keeping **an older sibling's jersey** after they move away. At first it makes you ache, until you start coaching younger kids using everything your sibling taught you. The heavy waiting softens into genuine **relief**.

> A fitting emotional response grows directly out of the main character's emotional resolution at the close of the story.

## Reading the emotional turn at the end

The final paragraph reveals Raka's breakthrough: <Highlight>Untuk pertama kalinya, ia tidak merasa telah meninggalkan ayahnya</Highlight> (for the first time, he did not feel he had left his father behind) and <Highlight>Raka dapat melepaskan penantiannya kepada satu perahu</Highlight> (Raka could let go of waiting for one single boat).

His memory of his father stays intact while blooming into a shared safety network for all boats, creating **relief** because his memory gains **new meaning**.

## Why the other emotions miss the mark

- **Anger over the new lamp** fails because Raka gladly accepts it while keeping the old lamp on the shelf.
- **Flatness from mere practicality** misses the deep personal release Raka experiences.
- **Anxiety that neighbors erase his memory** directly contradicts his peaceful realization that he has not abandoned his father.
- **Disappointment that his father's boat returned** invents an event that never happens in the text.

# Answer

The most fitting emotional response is relief because Raka's memory gains new meaning.

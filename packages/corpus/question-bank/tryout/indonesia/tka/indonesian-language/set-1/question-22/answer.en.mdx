# Given

In week two, many students mistakenly dropped **paper straws** into the plant-scrap bin because they thought anything plant-based could be composted, even though glue and protective coatings slow breakdown.

# Asked

Which bin label is clearest for keeping paper straws out of the plant-scrap bin.

# Solution

Imagine labeling a **ready-to-eat fruit basket**. A sign saying "Nice Green Basket" leaves people guessing whether candy wrappers belong inside. A crisp label states both what goes in and what stays out.

> An effective procedural label states the exact accepted material while directly blocking the most common mistake.

## Covering both rules inside one clear label

Paragraph three pinpoints why students got confused: paper straws come from plants, yet their coatings and glue make them non-food packaging rather than clean <Highlight>sisa nabati</Highlight> (plant food scraps).

The label **Sisa Nabati Bersih Tanpa Kemasan** (Clean Plant Scraps Without Packaging) states both rules at once: only clean plant scraps belong, and packaging stays out.

## Why the other labels fail

- **Put Anything In** and **Place for Unwanted Items** recreate the exact mixed-trash problem the trial fixed.
- **Maybe Compostable** invites guesswork, keeping paper straws inside the wrong bin.
- **Nice Green Bin** describes appearance rather than sorting rules.

# Answer

**The clearest label for the plant-scrap bin is Sisa Nabati Bersih Tanpa Kemasan (Clean Plant Scraps Without Packaging)**.

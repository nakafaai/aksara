# Given

SMA Arunika's sorting method separates clean plant scraps, animal scraps, and non-food items using clear examples and periodic checks, while explicitly noting that the trial **did not eliminate all cafeteria waste**.

# Asked

Which benefit is most relevant if the same procedure is applied at home.

# Solution

Imagine setting up **three neat bins in your kitchen** with clear picture examples on the lids. Household waste does not vanish overnight, yet everyone in the family immediately knows which scraps are clean enough to compost.

> Applying a procedure to daily life keeps its realistic strengths while respecting every limit named in the original text.

## Translating school results to a home kitchen

The closing paragraph records two verifiable outcomes: <Highlight>sisa nabati menjadi lebih bersih untuk diolah</Highlight> (plant scraps became cleaner to process) and <Highlight>siswa semakin jarang salah memilih wadah</Highlight> (students made fewer sorting mistakes).

Brought into a household, the most direct and realistic benefit is that **family members can more easily separate materials that can be processed**.

## Why the other claimed benefits contradict the procedure

- **Cutting most household waste away** clashes with <Highlight>Uji coba tidak menghapus seluruh sampah kantin</Highlight> (the trial did not eliminate all cafeteria waste).
- **Adding unlimited animal scraps** breaks paragraph four's explicit ban.
- **Delaying bin cleaning** contradicts daily disposal right after end-of-day photos.
- **Needing no monitoring** ignores the required regular moisture checks on the biopore hole.

# Answer

The most relevant benefit at home is that family members can more easily separate materials that can be processed.

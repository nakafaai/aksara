# Given

In the initial OSIS SMA Nusa survey, the back garden (<InlineMath math="30.4\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}" />) combined **dense trees and shade** over open soil. In the follow-up plan, the team adds one new measuring spot **under a canopy without trees**.

# Asked

Why the team adds a measuring point under a treeless canopy.

# Solution

Imagine sipping **iced sweet tea** and feeling refreshed. Was it the ice, the tea, or the sugar that cooled you down. To test the ice alone, you taste **plain iced water with no tea**.

> When two factors always appear together, a careful experiment adds a comparison spot that isolates just one of those factors.

## Separating shade from trees

In the back garden, shade and trees occur together, so the initial data cannot tell which one lowers temperature. Paragraph three explains that <Highlight>menambah satu titik di bawah kanopi tanpa pohon</Highlight> (adding one spot under a treeless canopy) helps <Highlight>mengurangi penjelasan alternatif</Highlight> (reduce competing explanations).

A canopy provides shade without foliage or tree cover, letting the team **compare the effect of shade without directly involving trees**.

## Why the other options fail

- **Representing** or **replacing the back garden** misses the goal: the canopy is an added comparison site, not a substitute.
- **Testing the effect of a fan** is why they switch off the reading-room fan, not why they measure under a canopy.
- **Reducing the number of locations** contradicts the passage explicitly saying they add a site.

# Answer

**The team adds a measuring point under a treeless canopy to compare the effect of shade without directly involving trees**.

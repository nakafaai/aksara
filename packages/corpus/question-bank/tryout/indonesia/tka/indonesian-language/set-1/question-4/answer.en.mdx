# Given

Paragraph two describes the **first-month snag** when envelopes returned without notes and staff added history cards, while paragraph three shows what changed **three months later** once residents and staff used those cards.

# Asked

How paragraph two and paragraph three are related in the Seed Cabinet passage.

# Solution

Think of **a shoelace snapping**, replacing it with a sturdy lace, and running comfortably the next day. The first scene states the problem and fix, while the second scene shows how well that fix worked.

> Paragraph relationships follow the logical role each paragraph plays in the sequence of events rather than surface topic words.

## Comparing the roles of both paragraphs

Paragraph two reports <Highlight>banyak amplop kembali tanpa keterangan</Highlight> (many envelopes returned without details), followed by <Highlight>Tim perpustakaan lalu menambahkan kartu riwayat</Highlight> (the library team then added history cards). Paragraph three opens with <Highlight>Tiga bulan kemudian, kartu tersebut mengubah cara warga memilih benih</Highlight> (three months later, the cards changed how residents chose seeds).

| Paragraph | Core content | Role in flow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paragraph two | Unlabeled envelopes hindered sorting, so staff added history cards. | Problem and fix introduced. |
| Paragraph three | Residents pick better seeds and staff tag untested batches. | Positive impact of the fix. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Comparing two laboratories** is absent, as no laboratory testing happens.
- **Conclusion followed by definition** mislabels a narrative problem and its results.
- **Reasons for shutting down** contradicts the thriving program in later paragraphs.
- **Making the solution less relevant** reverses the clear improvements reported in paragraph three.

# Answer

**Paragraph two states the problem and initial fix, while paragraph three shows the impact of that improvement**.

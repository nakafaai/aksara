# Given

The third paragraph of the Seed Cabinet passage gives **three changes** seen three months after adding history cards: narrow-yard residents picking pot-friendly plants, school gardens comparing harvest times, and staff tagging untested seeds for small trial plantings.

# Asked

Which statement best summarizes the entire third paragraph.

# Solution

Imagine putting **three different toys** into one clearly labeled box. A good summary label covers every example inside the paragraph without shrinking to just one toy or inventing a fourth one.

> A strong paragraph summary captures every major example together instead of focusing on one detail or adding claims absent from the text.

## Combining the three pieces of evidence

The third paragraph shows two linked benefits of the history cards. First, they guide **seed selection**, as residents <Highlight>mencari tanaman yang tahan ditanam dalam pot</Highlight> (look for plants suited to pots) and schools <Highlight>membandingkan waktu panen</Highlight> (compare harvest times).

Second, they guide **seed testing**, as staff <Highlight>memberi label perlu uji dan menanam sampel kecil terlebih dahulu</Highlight> (label untested seeds and plant a small sample first).

## Why the other options fail

- **Preferring the consultation desk over cards** brings in the fourth paragraph and invents a preference.
- **Making school gardens the main target** exaggerates one example into the whole goal.
- **Claiming one sample represents an entire variety** stretches beyond what the text says.
- **Excluding narrow-yard homes** reverses the passage, since those residents actively pick pot-friendly plants.

# Answer

The best summary of the third paragraph is that **history cards help with selecting and testing seeds**.

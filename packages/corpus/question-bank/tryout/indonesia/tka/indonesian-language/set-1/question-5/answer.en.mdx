# Given

History cards at the Seed Cabinet record resident observations that help growers choose seeds, yet managers remind everyone that the notes come from neighborhood gardens, **have not been lab-tested**, and growth can still differ from garden to garden.

# Asked

Which evaluation best judges the usefulness and limits of the information on the history cards.

# Solution

Imagine a friend telling you that **her mango tree grew sweet fruit** with evening watering. Her tip gives you a helpful starting clue, yet it cannot guarantee the exact same harvest in your yard where soil and sunlight differ.

> A balanced evaluation of information recognizes its practical usefulness while clearly naming the limits of what it can guarantee.

## Weighing usefulness alongside limits

The final paragraph states <Highlight>Kartu tersebut menjadi bahan pertimbangan, sedangkan hasil pertumbuhan tetap dapat berbeda di setiap kebun</Highlight> (the cards serve as a reference point, while growth results can still vary in each garden). Both halves matter equally.

The cards are **useful as a guide** for pot gardening and harvest schedules, yet because conditions vary and no lab test was run, they **do not guarantee identical results**.

## Why the other options fall short

- **Claiming full accuracy** or **estimating germination without trial samples** ignores both the managers' warning and the staff's small sample trials.
- **Requiring a sample test before any use** dismisses how residents already rely on the cards.
- **Limiting usefulness to staff only** contradicts residents and school gardens using them directly.

# Answer

The most accurate evaluation is that the history card information is useful as a guide, yet it does not guarantee the same results.

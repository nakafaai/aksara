# Given

In the Seat Seven story, every Friday Mira sits in the **station waiting room** hoping the afternoon train brings her brother Seno home. As soon as that train pulls out, she leaves the very same station for town hall to rehearse her violin.

# Asked

Why the train station setting is important to the story.

# Solution

Think of **a school gate** where you wait for a parent to arrive and also step out to run your track race. A great story setting does more than paint scenery, because it mirrors the tug between waiting and moving forward.

> A story setting carries weight when its daily rhythm directly embodies the character's inner tension and forward movement.

## Seeing both movements meet at the station

Paragraph three traces Mira's weekly boundary at the station: <Highlight>setiap Jumat Mira menunggu hanya sampai kereta sore berangkat</Highlight> (every Friday Mira waited only until the afternoon train departed), and <Highlight>Setelahnya, ia pergi ke balai kota untuk berlatih sendiri</Highlight> (afterward, she went to town hall to practice alone).

The station physically brings together **waiting** for Seno and **continuing her own journey** toward rehearsal and performance.

## Why the other options miss the text

- **Viewing the station as a workplace instead of a waiting place** contradicts her Friday vigil.
- **Explaining how violins are made**, **holding the festival inside a train**, and **Pak Damar quitting his job** never appear anywhere in the story.

# Answer

The station setting is important because it serves as the meeting point between waiting and continuing the journey.

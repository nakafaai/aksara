# The shape that is left

Picture the cylinder cut straight down through its axis. At the top there's a hollow shaped like half a ball, and at the bottom there's a hollow shaped like a cone. Both have the same radius as the cylinder, so **the top and the bottom faces are removed completely**. From the outside, only the curved side of the cylinder is left.

The sizes you need: <InlineMath math="r=6" />, cylinder height <InlineMath math="18" />, cone height <InlineMath math="6" />. The slant height of the cone is <InlineMath math="s=\sqrt{6^2+6^2}=6\sqrt2" />.

# First statement

Work out the surface area of the original cylinder and of the solid that's left.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{cylinder}}&=2\pi r^2+2\pi rt=72\pi+216\pi=288\pi \\
L_{\text{left}}&=2\pi rt+2\pi r^2+\pi rs=216\pi+72\pi+36\sqrt2\,\pi=(288+36\sqrt2)\pi
\end{aligned}" />

The inside of the half ball has area <InlineMath math="2\pi r^2=72\pi" />, exactly the area of the top and bottom faces that disappeared. Then the curved surface of the cone adds <InlineMath math="36\sqrt2\,\pi" />. So **cutting pieces away actually increases the surface area**. This statement is **False**.

# Second statement

The volume removed is the volume of the half ball plus the volume of the cone.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{cylinder}}&=\pi\cdot6^2\cdot18=648\pi \\
V_{\text{removed}}&=\tfrac{2}{3}\pi\cdot6^3+\tfrac{1}{3}\pi\cdot6^2\cdot6=144\pi+72\pi=216\pi \\
V_{\text{left}}&=648\pi-216\pi=432\pi=\tfrac{2}{3}\cdot648\pi
\end{aligned}" />

The removed part is exactly <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> of the cylinder, so **what's left is <InlineMath math="\tfrac23" /> of the cylinder**. This statement is **True**.

# Third statement

Measure heights from the bottom of the cylinder. The tip of the cone is at height <InlineMath math="6" />. The centre of the half ball is the centre of the top face, at height <InlineMath math="18" />, and the deepest point of that hollow is at height <InlineMath math="18-6=12" />. The two hollows **don't touch**, because <InlineMath math="12>6" />.

Every point on the curved surface of the half ball is <InlineMath math="6" /> away from its centre. The tip of the cone is <InlineMath math="18-6=12" /> away from that centre. The point of the ball's surface closest to the tip lies on the line from the tip to the centre, which is the deepest point of the hollow.

<BlockMath math="\text{shortest distance}=12-6=6\text{ cm}" />

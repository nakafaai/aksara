# The slope from the midpoint

The line isn't given, so find its slope first and then the length of <InlineMath math="AB" />. The midpoint of <InlineMath math="AB" /> lies on the line <InlineMath math="AB" />, so the line passes through <InlineMath math="P(2,-3)" />. The vertical line <InlineMath math="x=2" /> meets the parabola at only one point, so the line has a slope <InlineMath math="m" />.

<BlockMath math="y=m(x-2)-3" />

The <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinates of the intersection points are the roots of the equation you get by setting the two formulas for <InlineMath math="y" /> equal.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-3x-4&=m(x-2)-3 \\
x^2-(3+m)x+(2m-1)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Call the roots <InlineMath math="x_1" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2" />. By Vieta, <InlineMath math="x_1+x_2=3+m" />. **The midpoint's <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate is the average of the two roots**, and it has to be <InlineMath math="2" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}=\frac{3+m}{2}=2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad m=1" />

So the line is <InlineMath math="y=x-5" />, and the intersection equation becomes <InlineMath math="x^2-4x+1=0" />.

# The length of the chord

From <InlineMath math="x^2-4x+1=0" />, Vieta gives <InlineMath math="x_1+x_2=4" /> and <InlineMath math="x_1x_2=1" />. Both points lie on a line with slope <InlineMath math="1" />, so **the difference in <InlineMath math="y" /> is <InlineMath math="m" /> times the difference in <InlineMath math="x" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x_1-x_2)^2&=(x_1+x_2)^2-4x_1x_2=16-4=12 \\
AB^2&=(x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2=(1+m^2)(x_1-x_2)^2=2\cdot12=24 \\
AB&=\sqrt{24}=2\sqrt{6}
\end{aligned}" />

A direct check: the roots are <InlineMath math="x=2\pm\sqrt{3}" />, so <InlineMath math="A(2-\sqrt{3},\,-3-\sqrt{3})" /> and <InlineMath math="B(2+\sqrt{3},\,-3+\sqrt{3})" />. Their midpoint really is <InlineMath math="(2,-3)" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{3}" /> is only the horizontal difference <InlineMath math="|x_1-x_2|" />. The line is slanted, so you still have to multiply by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{1+m^2}=\sqrt{2}" />.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> is the distance between the points where the parabola crosses the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=4" />. **<InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> are where the parabola meets the line, not the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis**.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{7}" /> uses the wrong identity <InlineMath math="(x_1-x_2)^2=(x_1+x_2)^2-2x_1x_2=14" />. The right one subtracts <InlineMath math="4x_1x_2" />.
- <InlineMath math="4\sqrt{2}" /> uses <InlineMath math="x_1+x_2=2" />, forgetting to halve for the midpoint. That gives <InlineMath math="m=-1" />, and the line <InlineMath math="y=-x-1" /> meets the parabola at <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" />, whose midpoint is <InlineMath math="(1,-2)" />, not <InlineMath math="P" />.

# Two possible centres

This question has two parts. First, find the centre of rotation <InlineMath math="P" />. Second, turn the point <InlineMath math="B" /> about the same centre in the same direction. The catch is that **the direction isn't given**, so you need to try both directions and throw one away.

A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> rotation turns the vector that starts at the centre. Anticlockwise, the vector <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> becomes <InlineMath math="(-b,a)" />. Clockwise, it becomes <InlineMath math="(b,-a)" />. Write <InlineMath math="P(p,q)" />, so <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PA}=(5-p,\ 1-q)" />.

For an anticlockwise rotation:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A'&=P+(q-1,\ 5-p)=(p+q-1,\ q-p+5)=(3,7) \\
p+q&=4,\quad q-p=2 \\
P&=(1,3)
\end{aligned}" />

For a clockwise rotation:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A'&=P+(1-q,\ p-5)=(p-q+1,\ p+q-5)=(3,7) \\
p-q&=2,\quad p+q=12 \\
P&=(7,5)
\end{aligned}" />

Both centres lie on the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AA'" />, the line <InlineMath math="x-3y+8=0" />. So the bisector alone can't tell you which centre it is.

# Choosing the right centre

Now use the condition on the distance from the origin.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
|OP_1|&=\sqrt{1^2+3^2}=\sqrt{10}<5 \\
|OP_2|&=\sqrt{7^2+5^2}=\sqrt{74}>5
\end{aligned}" />

So the centre is <InlineMath math="P(1,3)" /> and **the rotation is anticlockwise**. Quick check: <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PA}=(4,-2)" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PA'}=(2,4)" /> have the same length, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{20}" />, and their dot product is <InlineMath math="8-8=0" />, so they really are perpendicular.

# The image of B

Turn <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PB}" /> anticlockwise, then **add the result to the centre**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{PB}&=(4-1,\ 6-3)=(3,3) \\
(3,3)&\longmapsto(-3,3) \\
B'&=(1-3,\ 3+3)=(-2,6)
\end{aligned}" />

So the image of <InlineMath math="B" /> is <InlineMath math="(-2,6)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(8,8)" /> is the image for the centre <InlineMath math="(7,5)" /> with a clockwise turn. That centre passes the first calculation, but it's more than <InlineMath math="5" /> units from <InlineMath math="O" />.
- <InlineMath math="(4,0)" /> uses the right centre but turns clockwise.
- <InlineMath math="(2,4)" /> takes the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AA'" />, <InlineMath math="(4,4)" />, as the centre. That only works for a <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation.
- <InlineMath math="(-6,4)" /> turns <InlineMath math="B" /> about the origin, as if the centre were <InlineMath math="O" />.

# The real sample space

The last roll always shows two different faces, because any double is rolled again. So **re-rolling is the same as removing the doubles from the sample space**. The new sample space holds every ordered pair with different faces:

<BlockMath math="n(S)=6\cdot6-6=30" />

These <InlineMath math="30" /> outcomes are equally likely. On every roll each of them has probability <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{36}" />, and repeating the roll doesn't favor any of them. You can also check it with a series: the rolling stops on roll <InlineMath math="k" /> if the first <InlineMath math="k-1" /> rolls are doubles (each with probability <InlineMath math="\frac16" />).

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{14}{36}\left(1+\frac16+\frac1{36}+\cdots\right)=\frac{14}{36}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\frac16}=\frac{14}{30}" />

The <InlineMath math="14" /> in that series is the number of different-face outcomes with a prime sum. The next part counts them.

# Outcomes with a prime sum

The sum of two dice runs from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="12" />, and the primes in that range are <InlineMath math="2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 11" />. A sum of <InlineMath math="2" /> only comes from <InlineMath math="(1,1)" />, which is a double, so **a sum of <InlineMath math="2" /> can never be the last roll**. Count the ordered pairs (first die, second die):

| Sum | Pairs | Count |
| :---: | :--- | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="(1,2),\ (2,1)" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="(1,4),\ (4,1),\ (2,3),\ (3,2)" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> |
| <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="(1,6),\ (6,1),\ (2,5),\ (5,2),\ (3,4),\ (4,3)" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> |
| <InlineMath math="11" /> | <InlineMath math="(5,6),\ (6,5)" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{2+4+6+2}{30}=\frac{14}{30}=\frac{7}{15}" />

So the probability that the sum on the last roll is prime is <InlineMath math="\frac{7}{15}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{12}" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{15}{36}" />, the chance of a prime sum on one ordinary roll. It ignores the re-roll rule and still counts <InlineMath math="(1,1)" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{7}{18}" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{14}{36}" />. The doubles are gone from the top, but **the bottom is still <InlineMath math="36" />**, even though a double is never the last roll.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{7}{30}" /> comes from counting pairs without order, so <InlineMath math="\{1,2\}" /> counts once and there are <InlineMath math="7" /> pairs, while the bottom uses the <InlineMath math="30" /> ordered outcomes.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{7}{36}" /> mixes two slips: unordered pairs on top and <InlineMath math="36" /> outcomes on the bottom.

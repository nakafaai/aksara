# When the hands are perpendicular

The question asks for the arc length covered by the tip of the minute hand, but the end time isn't given. So first find when the two hands make a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> angle for the second time after 04.00.

Measure angles clockwise from the 12. The minute hand turns <InlineMath math="360^\circ" /> in <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes, so <InlineMath math="6^\circ" /> per minute. The hour hand turns <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> in <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes, so <InlineMath math="0.5^\circ" /> per minute. At 04.00 the minute hand is at <InlineMath math="0^\circ" /> and the hour hand at <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />. After <InlineMath math="t" /> minutes:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{position of the minute hand}&=6t \\
\text{position of the hour hand}&=120+0.5t
\end{aligned}" />

Remember that **the hour hand keeps moving too**, so the minute hand closes in by only <InlineMath math="6-0.5=5.5^\circ" /> per minute. A right angle can happen in two situations:

| Situation | Equation | Time |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Minute hand <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> behind the hour hand | <InlineMath math="120+0.5t-6t=90" /> | <InlineMath math="t=\frac{60}{11}\approx5.45" /> minutes |
| Minute hand <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> ahead of the hour hand | <InlineMath math="6t-(120+0.5t)=90" /> | <InlineMath math="t=\frac{420}{11}\approx38.18" /> minutes |

The first right angle comes at about 04.05, before the minute hand has caught up with the hour hand. The second comes at <InlineMath math="t=\frac{420}{11}" /> minutes, at about 04.38.

# The arc length

In <InlineMath math="\frac{420}{11}" /> minutes the minute hand turns through:

<BlockMath math="\theta=6\cdot\frac{420}{11}=\frac{2{,}520}{11}\text{ degrees}" />

The tip moves along a circle of radius <InlineMath math="11\text{ cm}" />, so **the arc length is the fraction of a full turn times the circumference**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{arc length}&=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot2\pi r \\
&=\frac{2{,}520}{11\cdot360}\cdot2\pi\cdot11 \\
&=7\cdot2\pi=14\pi\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="\frac{2{,}520}{11}\approx229^\circ" />, a bit more than half a turn, which fits about <InlineMath math="38" /> minutes.

So the tip of the minute hand travels <InlineMath math="14\pi" /> cm.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2\pi" /> cm is the arc up to the **first** right angle, at <InlineMath math="t=\frac{60}{11}" /> minutes. The question asks for the second one.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{77}{6}\pi" /> cm comes from treating the hour hand as frozen on the 4. Then the minute hand would only need to turn <InlineMath math="120^\circ+90^\circ=210^\circ" />, but the hour hand has moved about <InlineMath math="19^\circ" /> by then.
- <InlineMath math="26\pi" /> cm comes from missing the right angle at 04.05, so the one at about 05.11 gets counted as the second.
- <InlineMath math="28\pi" /> cm comes from using the diameter, <InlineMath math="22\text{ cm}" />, where the radius belongs in the circumference formula.

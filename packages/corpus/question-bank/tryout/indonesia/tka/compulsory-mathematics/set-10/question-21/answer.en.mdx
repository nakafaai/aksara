# The median study time

This question has two steps: first find the median study time, then average the scores of the students who study more than that median. The median is the middle value **after the data are sorted**, so don't just take the middle rows of the table.

Sort the eight study times from smallest to largest. There are <InlineMath math="8" /> values, an even number, so the median is the mean of the <InlineMath math="4" />th and <InlineMath math="5" />th values.

Sorted, the data are <InlineMath math="2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 6,\ 6,\ 7,\ 11,\ 17" />:

<BlockMath math="\text{median}=\frac{6+6}{2}=6\text{ hours}" />

# The students who count

The condition is study time **more than** <InlineMath math="6" />, so Asri and Indra, at exactly <InlineMath math="6" /> hours, are out. Only Hana (<InlineMath math="7" /> hours), Bima (<InlineMath math="11" /> hours), and Fajar (<InlineMath math="17" /> hours) qualify:

<BlockMath math="\text{mean}=\frac{70+88+82}{3}=\frac{240}{3}=80" />

So the mean score of the students who study more than the median is <InlineMath math="80" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="85" /> comes from using the mean study time, <InlineMath math="\frac{56}{8}=7" />, as the cutoff instead of the median. Only Bima and Fajar study more than <InlineMath math="7" /> hours, and <InlineMath math="\frac{88+82}{2}=85" />.
- <InlineMath math="76" /> comes from counting Asri and Indra too, as if the condition were **at least** <InlineMath math="6" /> hours: <InlineMath math="\frac{72+68+70+88+82}{5}=76" />.
- <InlineMath math="72" /> comes from taking the median from rows <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> of the unsorted table, Dani and Eka, <InlineMath math="\frac{2+3}{2}=2.5" />. Then every student except Dani counts: <InlineMath math="\frac{504}{7}=72" />.
- <InlineMath math="70" /> is the mean score of all the students, <InlineMath math="\frac{560}{8}" />, with no study-time condition at all.

# The image through the foot point

You're looking for a point <InlineMath math="B" /> on line <InlineMath math="g" /> that makes angle <InlineMath math="ABA'" /> a right angle, with <InlineMath math="B" /> in quadrant I. So there are two jobs: find the image <InlineMath math="A'" /> first, then find the points of <InlineMath math="g" /> that see segment <InlineMath math="AA'" /> at a right angle.

A reflection in a slanted line is safest through the **foot of the perpendicular**, the point <InlineMath math="M" /> on <InlineMath math="g" /> closest to <InlineMath math="A" />. Line <InlineMath math="g" /> has normal vector <InlineMath math="(2,-1)" />, so every point on the perpendicular from <InlineMath math="A" /> looks like <InlineMath math="(6+2t,\ 1-t)" />. The foot is the one that also satisfies the equation of <InlineMath math="g" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2(6+2t)-(1-t)&=1 \\
11+5t&=1 \\
t&=-2 \\
M&=(6-4,\ 1+2)=(2,3)
\end{aligned}" />

Point <InlineMath math="M" /> is the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AA'" />, so <InlineMath math="A'=2M-A" />:

<BlockMath math="A'=(2\cdot2-6,\ 2\cdot3-1)=(-2,5)" />

# The right angle on the mirror line

The mirror line <InlineMath math="g" /> is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="AA'" /> and passes through its midpoint <InlineMath math="M" />. A right angle at <InlineMath math="B" /> means **<InlineMath math="B" /> lies on the circle with diameter <InlineMath math="AA'" />** (Thales' theorem). That circle has center <InlineMath math="M" /> and radius <InlineMath math="MA" />:

<BlockMath math="MA=\sqrt{(6-2)^2+(1-3)^2}=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5" />

Point <InlineMath math="B" /> is on <InlineMath math="g" />, so write <InlineMath math="B=(t,\ 2t-1)" />. Now use the condition <InlineMath math="MB=2\sqrt5" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(t-2)^2+(2t-1-3)^2&=20 \\
(t-2)^2+4(t-2)^2&=20 \\
5(t-2)^2&=20 \\
(t-2)^2&=4
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="t-2=2" /> or <InlineMath math="t-2=-2" />. That gives two candidates: <InlineMath math="t=4" /> gives <InlineMath math="B=(4,7)" />, and <InlineMath math="t=0" /> gives <InlineMath math="B=(0,-1)" />.

# The quadrant condition

The candidate <InlineMath math="(0,-1)" /> sits on the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis, so it's **not in quadrant I** and has to go. That leaves <InlineMath math="B=(4,7)" />.

Check the angle with gradients. Two lines are perpendicular when the product of their gradients is <InlineMath math="-1" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m_{BA}&=\frac{1-7}{6-4}=-3 \\
m_{BA'}&=\frac{5-7}{-2-4}=\frac13 \\
m_{BA}\cdot m_{BA'}&=-3\cdot\frac13=-1
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="(6,11)" /> comes from moving away from <InlineMath math="M" /> by the whole length <InlineMath math="AA'=4\sqrt5" />. The radius of the Thales circle is only **half the diameter**, which is <InlineMath math="2\sqrt5" />.
- <InlineMath math="(3,5)" /> comes from taking <InlineMath math="AA'" /> equal to the distance from <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="g" />, which is <InlineMath math="2\sqrt5" />, and then halving it. The image is twice as far from <InlineMath math="A" /> as the mirror is, so <InlineMath math="AA'=4\sqrt5" />.
- <InlineMath math="(2,3)" /> is the foot <InlineMath math="M" />. There <InlineMath math="A" />, <InlineMath math="M" />, and <InlineMath math="A'" /> lie on one line, so the angle is <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, not <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="(0,-1)" /> does make a right angle, but it isn't in quadrant I.

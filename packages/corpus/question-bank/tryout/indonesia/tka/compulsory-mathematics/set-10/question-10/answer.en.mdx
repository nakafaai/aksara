# The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral

The question asks for the angle between the diagonal <InlineMath math="CA" /> and the extension <InlineMath math="CE" />. You need three circle facts used one after another, and the first step moves the <InlineMath math="64^\circ" /> angle over to the point <InlineMath math="A" />.

In a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles add up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />. The points <InlineMath math="B" />, <InlineMath math="C" />, and <InlineMath math="E" /> lie on one line, so <InlineMath math="\angle BCD" /> and <InlineMath math="\angle DCE" /> also add up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\angle BAD&=180^\circ-\angle BCD \\
\angle DCE&=180^\circ-\angle BCD \\
\angle BAD&=\angle DCE=64^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

So **the exterior angle at <InlineMath math="C" /> equals the opposite interior angle at <InlineMath math="A" />**, not its supplement.

# An isosceles triangle and inscribed angles

Since <InlineMath math="AB=AD" />, triangle <InlineMath math="ABD" /> is isosceles, so its two base angles are equal.

<BlockMath math="\angle ABD=\angle ADB=\frac{180^\circ-64^\circ}{2}=58^\circ" />

The inscribed angles <InlineMath math="\angle ACB" /> and <InlineMath math="\angle ADB" /> **stand on the same arc**, arc <InlineMath math="AB" />, so <InlineMath math="\angle ACB=\angle ADB=58^\circ" />. In the same way, <InlineMath math="\angle ACD=\angle ABD=58^\circ" />, because both stand on arc <InlineMath math="AD" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\angle ACE&=180^\circ-\angle ACB=180^\circ-58^\circ=122^\circ \\
\text{check: }\angle ACD+\angle DCE&=58^\circ+64^\circ=122^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="58^\circ" /> stops at <InlineMath math="\angle ACB" /> or <InlineMath math="\angle ACD" />. That's an inscribed angle, not the angle between <InlineMath math="CA" /> and the extension <InlineMath math="CE" />.
- <InlineMath math="116^\circ" /> treats <InlineMath math="\angle ACE" /> and <InlineMath math="\angle BAD" /> as opposite angles that add up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />. The value <InlineMath math="116^\circ" /> is really <InlineMath math="\angle BCD" />.
- <InlineMath math="128^\circ" /> assumes <InlineMath math="CD" /> cuts <InlineMath math="\angle ACE" /> in half, so <InlineMath math="\angle ACD=\angle DCE=64^\circ" />. But <InlineMath math="\angle ACD=58^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="148^\circ" /> uses <InlineMath math="\angle BAD=180^\circ-64^\circ=116^\circ" />. **The exterior angle equals the opposite interior angle, not its supplement**. With that mistake the base angles become <InlineMath math="32^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="\angle ACE" /> becomes <InlineMath math="148^\circ" />.

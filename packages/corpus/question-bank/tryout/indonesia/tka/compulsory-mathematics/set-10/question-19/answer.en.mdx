# Starting water and the tank's capacity

The question asks for the water level after two pourings, a possible spill, and then the concrete block being lifted out. The key is this: **while the block is still in the tank, the water can only use the tank's base minus the block's base**. The block is taller than the tank, so it always sticks out through the water surface.

Work out the tank's base, the block's base, and the area left for the water:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{tank base}&=70\cdot44=3{,}080\text{ cm}^2 \\
\text{block base}&=35\cdot22=770\text{ cm}^2 \\
\text{base for the water}&=3{,}080-770=2{,}310\text{ cm}^2
\end{aligned}" />

That gives the starting volume of water and the most water that fits while the block is inside:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{starting water}&=2{,}310\cdot32=73{,}920\text{ cm}^3 \\
\text{water capacity up to the rim}&=2{,}310\cdot40=92{,}400\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

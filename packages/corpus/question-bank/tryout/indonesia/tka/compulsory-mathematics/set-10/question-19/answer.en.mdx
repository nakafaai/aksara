# Starting water and the tank's capacity

The question asks for the water level after two pourings, a possible spill, and then the concrete block being lifted out. The key is this: **while the block is still in the tank, the water can only use the tank's base minus the block's base**. The block is taller than the tank, so it always sticks out through the water surface.

Work out the tank's base, the block's base, and the area left for the water:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{tank base}&=70\cdot44=3{,}080\text{ cm}^2 \\
\text{block base}&=35\cdot22=770\text{ cm}^2 \\
\text{base for the water}&=3{,}080-770=2{,}310\text{ cm}^2
\end{aligned}" />

That gives the starting volume of water and the most water that fits while the block is inside:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{starting water}&=2{,}310\cdot32=73{,}920\text{ cm}^3 \\
\text{water capacity up to the rim}&=2{,}310\cdot40=92{,}400\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

# Water poured in and water spilled

The bucket is a cylinder, and the bottle holds <InlineMath math="1.54" /> liters, which is <InlineMath math="1{,}540\text{ cm}^3" /> because <InlineMath math="1" /> liter <InlineMath math="=1{,}000\text{ cm}^3" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{bucket}&=\frac{22}{7}\cdot14^2\cdot35=21{,}560\text{ cm}^3 \\
\text{total poured}&=21{,}560+1{,}540=23{,}100\text{ cm}^3 \\
\text{starting water}+\text{poured water}&=73{,}920+23{,}100=97{,}020\text{ cm}^3

In fact the tank already overflows while the bucket is being poured, because <InlineMath math="73{,}920+21{,}560=95{,}480" /> is already past <InlineMath math="92{,}400" />. Exactly <InlineMath math="92{,}400\text{ cm}^3" /> of water stays in the tank.

- <InlineMath math="40" /> cm comes from assuming the tank stays full after the block is lifted. But **the submerged part of the block leaves too**, so the surface drops.
- <InlineMath math="39.5" /> cm comes from forgetting the block completely. The starting water becomes <InlineMath math="3{,}080\cdot32=98{,}560" />, plus <InlineMath math="23{,}100" /> gives <InlineMath math="121{,}660" />, still below <InlineMath math="3{,}080\cdot40=123{,}200" />, so it looks as if nothing spills.

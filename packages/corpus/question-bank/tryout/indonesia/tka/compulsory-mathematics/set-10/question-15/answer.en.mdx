Write each transformation as **a rule for a general point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" />**, then compose the rules in order.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x,y)&\xrightarrow{\ [O,2]\ }(2x,2y)\xrightarrow{\ [O,-3]\ }(-6x,-6y) \\
(x,y)&\xrightarrow{\ [O,3]\ }(3x,3y)\xrightarrow{\ x\text{-axis}\ }(3x,-3y) \\
(x,y)&\xrightarrow{\ x\text{-axis}\ }(x,-y)\xrightarrow{\ [O,3]\ }(3x,-3y) \\
(x,y)&\xrightarrow{\ [(1,0),2]\ }(2x-1,\,2y)\xrightarrow{\ [O,\frac12]\ }\left(x-\tfrac12,\,y\right)
\end{aligned}" />

- The scale factors multiply, <InlineMath math="2\cdot(-3)=-6" />, and the centre stays <InlineMath math="O" />. This statement is true.
- The combined factor is <InlineMath math="6" />, so areas are multiplied by <InlineMath math="6^2=36" />, not <InlineMath math="6" />. This statement is false.
- Both orders give <InlineMath math="(3x,-3y)" />. This statement is true.
- The final rule is <InlineMath math="\left(x-\tfrac12,\,y\right)" />, a translation of <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> to the left. This statement is true.
- The previous statement is an example of two dilations that make a translation, not a dilation. This statement is false.

So the true statements are the ones about the factor <InlineMath math="-6" />, the dilation and reflection that can swap order, and the translation by <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix}-\frac12\\0\end{pmatrix}" />.

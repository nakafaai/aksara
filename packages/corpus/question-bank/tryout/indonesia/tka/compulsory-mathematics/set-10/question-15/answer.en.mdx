# The scale factor and the image triangle

All five statements are about the dilation <InlineMath math="D" />, so start by finding its scale factor. A dilation with center <InlineMath math="P" /> and factor <InlineMath math="k" /> sends a point <InlineMath math="X" /> to <InlineMath math="X'" /> by the rule <InlineMath math="X'-P=k\,(X-P)" />. Use the pair <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="A'" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A'-P&=(0-2,\ -3-1)=(-2,-4) \\
A-P&=(3-2,\ 3-1)=(1,2) \\
(-2,-4)&=k\,(1,2)\ \Rightarrow\ k=-2
\end{aligned}" />

**The scale factor is negative**: <InlineMath math="k=-2" />. With the rule <InlineMath math="X'=P-2\,(X-P)" />, the other two vertices go to:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
B'&=(2,1)-2\,(3,0)=(-4,1) \\
C'&=(2,1)-2\,(2,-2)=(-2,5)
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking each statement

**Statement 1**. From the work above, <InlineMath math="C'-P=(-4,4)=-2\,(2,-2)=-2\,(C-P)" />. The vector <InlineMath math="C'-P" /> is a negative multiple of <InlineMath math="C-P" />, so the three points lie on one line and <InlineMath math="C" /> and <InlineMath math="C'" /> sit on opposite sides of <InlineMath math="P" />. That happens for every point because <InlineMath math="k<0" />. **True**.

**Statement 2**. Find the area of <InlineMath math="ABC" /> with the coordinate formula (the shoelace formula):

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{ABC}&=\tfrac12\left|3(1-(-1))+5(-1-3)+4(3-1)\right| \\
&=\tfrac12\left|6-20+8\right|=3
\end{aligned}" />

The dilation multiplies every length by <InlineMath math="|k|=2" />, so **the area is multiplied by <InlineMath math="k^2=4" />**, not by <InlineMath math="2" />, and never by a negative number. The area of <InlineMath math="A'B'C'" /> is <InlineMath math="4\cdot3=12" /> square units. **True**.

**Statement 3**. A dilation sends a line to a parallel line, so the image has the form <InlineMath math="x-2y=c" />. Take one point of the original line, say <InlineMath math="(4,0)" />, find its image, and put it into that form:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(4,0)'&=(2,1)-2\,(2,-1)=(-2,3) \\
c&=-2-2\cdot3=-8
\end{aligned}" />

The image is the line <InlineMath math="x-2y=-8" />. **True**.

**Statement 4**. The sign inside the shoelace formula tells you the direction of the vertex order: negative means clockwise. For <InlineMath math="ABC" /> it's <InlineMath math="-6" />, and for <InlineMath math="A'B'C'" />:

<BlockMath math="0(1-5)+(-4)(5-(-3))+(-2)(-3-1)=-32+8=-24" />

Both are negative, so **both triangles run clockwise**. Here's why: a dilation with factor <InlineMath math="-2" /> is the same as a <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation about <InlineMath math="P" /> followed by a dilation with factor <InlineMath math="2" />. A rotation doesn't flip the vertex order the way a reflection does. **False**.

**Statement 5**. Line <InlineMath math="BC" /> has gradient <InlineMath math="\frac{-1-1}{4-5}=2" />, and its image is parallel and passes through <InlineMath math="B'(-4,1)" /> and <InlineMath math="C'(-2,5)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
BC&:\ 2x-y=9 \\
B'C'&:\ 2x-y=-9 \\
\text{distance from } P \text{ to } BC&=\frac{|2\cdot2-1-9|}{\sqrt{2^2+1^2}}=\frac{6}{\sqrt5} \\
\text{distance from } BC \text{ to } B'C'&=\frac{|9-(-9)|}{\sqrt5}=\frac{18}{\sqrt5}
\end{aligned}" />

The ratio is <InlineMath math="18:6=3" />. The image line is on the far side of <InlineMath math="P" /> at twice the distance, so the total gap is <InlineMath math="1+2=3" /> times as large. **True**.

So the true statements are statements 1, 2, 3, and 5.

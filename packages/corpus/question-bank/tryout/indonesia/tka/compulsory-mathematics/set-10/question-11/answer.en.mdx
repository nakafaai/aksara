# A perpendicular from the centre

The part of the park outside the pond is a ring, and its area is <InlineMath math="\pi R^2-\pi r^2=\pi(R^2-r^2)" />, where <InlineMath math="R" /> is the park's radius and <InlineMath math="r" /> is the pond's radius. You **don't need <InlineMath math="R" /> and <InlineMath math="r" /> separately**, only the difference of their squares.

Draw the line from the centre <InlineMath math="O" /> perpendicular to the path, and call its foot <InlineMath math="M" />. A perpendicular from the centre to a chord always cuts that chord in half. <InlineMath math="AB" /> is a chord of the park and <InlineMath math="CD" /> is a chord of the pond, and both lie on the same line, so <InlineMath math="M" /> is the midpoint of both.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
CM&=MD=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot6=3 \\
AM&=MB \;\Rightarrow\; DB=AC=5 \\
AM&=AC+CM=5+3=8
\end{aligned}" />

# The difference of the squared radii

Triangles <InlineMath math="OMA" /> and <InlineMath math="OMC" /> both have a right angle at <InlineMath math="M" /> and share the side <InlineMath math="OM" />. Write Pythagoras for both and subtract. **The unknown side <InlineMath math="OM" /> drops out**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
R^2&=OM^2+AM^2=OM^2+64 \\
r^2&=OM^2+CM^2=OM^2+9 \\
R^2-r^2&=64-9=55 \\
\text{ring area}&=\pi(R^2-r^2)=55\pi\text{ m}^2
\end{aligned}" />

Check it another way: <InlineMath math="AC\cdot AD=5\cdot11=55" />, the same as <InlineMath math="R^2-r^2" />. The result doesn't depend on how far the path is from the centre.

So the area of the park outside the pond is <InlineMath math="55\pi\text{ m}^2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="110\pi\text{ m}^2" /> computes <InlineMath math="\frac{AB^2-CD^2}{2}=\frac{256-36}{2}" />. Squaring half a length divides by <InlineMath math="4" />, not by <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="80\pi\text{ m}^2" /> uses <InlineMath math="AC\cdot AB=5\cdot16" />. The right product is <InlineMath math="AC\cdot AD" />, the two distances from <InlineMath math="A" /> to the points where the path meets the pond.
- <InlineMath math="73\pi\text{ m}^2" /> adds <InlineMath math="AM^2+CM^2=64+9" />. The two Pythagoras equations have to be subtracted so that <InlineMath math="OM^2" /> drops out.
- <InlineMath math="64\pi\text{ m}^2" /> uses <InlineMath math="\pi\cdot AM^2" />, the formula for a chord that only **touches** the pond. This path cuts through the pond, so <InlineMath math="CM^2" /> still has to be subtracted.

**At least once** is easiest through the opposite event, no <InlineMath math="6" /> in either throw. The two throws are independent.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{no }6)&=\frac56\cdot\frac56=\frac{25}{36} \\
P(\text{at least one }6)&=1-\frac{25}{36}=\frac{11}{36}
\end{aligned}" />

Check by counting: exactly one <InlineMath math="6" /> gives <InlineMath math="5+5=10" /> outcomes, and two <InlineMath math="6" />s give <InlineMath math="1" /> outcome, so <InlineMath math="\frac{11}{36}" />.

So the probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{11}{36}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{36}" /> is the probability of a <InlineMath math="6" /> on both throws.
- <InlineMath math="\frac16" /> is the probability of a <InlineMath math="6" /> on a single throw.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{18}" /> is the probability of exactly one <InlineMath math="6" />, leaving out <InlineMath math="(6,6)" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> adds <InlineMath math="\frac16+\frac16" />, so the outcome <InlineMath math="(6,6)" /> is counted twice.

# The chance the lamp is on

The question gives you extra information: the lamp is **already known to be on**. So it asks for a conditional probability, the chance that <InlineMath math="C" /> isn't working among all the situations in which the lamp is on:

<BlockMath math="P(C\text{ broken}\mid\text{on})=\frac{P(C\text{ broken and on})}{P(\text{on})}" />

Start with the bottom. The parallel part <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="C" /> fails only when both are broken, so use the complement. Then multiply by the chance that <InlineMath math="A" /> works, because <InlineMath math="A" /> is in series:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(B\text{ or }C\text{ works})&=1-(1-0.6)(1-0.5)=1-0.2=0.8 \\
P(\text{on})&=0.9\cdot0.8=0.72
\end{aligned}" />

# Lamp on and a switch broken

If <InlineMath math="C" /> is broken, the only path for the current goes through <InlineMath math="B" />. So the lamp being on with <InlineMath math="C" /> broken means **<InlineMath math="A" /> works, <InlineMath math="B" /> works, and <InlineMath math="C" /> is broken**:

<BlockMath math="P(C\text{ broken and on})=0.9\cdot0.6\cdot0.5=0.27" />

# The conditional probability

Divide the two:

<BlockMath math="P(C\text{ broken}\mid\text{on})=\frac{0.27}{0.72}=\frac{3}{8}" />

Notice that <InlineMath math="A" />'s <InlineMath math="0.9" /> sits on the top and on the bottom, so it cancels. A lit lamp already guarantees that <InlineMath math="A" /> works, which leaves only three situations for <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="C" />. **Those three situations aren't equally likely**:

| <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="C" /> | Probability |
| :--- | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="B" /> works, <InlineMath math="C" /> works | <InlineMath math="0.6\cdot0.5=0.3" /> |
| <InlineMath math="B" /> works, <InlineMath math="C" /> broken | <InlineMath math="0.6\cdot0.5=0.3" /> |
| <InlineMath math="B" /> broken, <InlineMath math="C" /> works | <InlineMath math="0.4\cdot0.5=0.2" /> |

The share with <InlineMath math="C" /> broken is <InlineMath math="\frac{0.3}{0.3+0.3+0.2}=\frac{0.3}{0.8}=\frac38" />, the same as before.

So the probability that switch <InlineMath math="C" /> isn't working while the lamp is on is <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{8}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{27}{100}" /> is the chance that the lamp is on **and** <InlineMath math="C" /> is broken. It hasn't been divided by the chance that the lamp is on, even though you already know it's on.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}" /> comes from treating the three situations for <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="C" /> as equally likely. The table shows their chances are <InlineMath math="0.3" />, <InlineMath math="0.3" />, and <InlineMath math="0.2" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{12}" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{0.3}{0.72}" />. The chance for <InlineMath math="A" /> was left out of the top but kept in the bottom.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}" /> is the starting chance that <InlineMath math="C" /> is broken. It ignores the fact that the lamp is on.

# Keeping one variable

The question asks how many arrangements there are, not for one arrangement. There are three unknowns but only two equations, so **write everything in terms of one variable and then filter with the conditions**.

Let <InlineMath math="x" />, <InlineMath math="y" />, and <InlineMath math="z" /> be the numbers of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}000" />, <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}000" />, and <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" /> notes. Count the money in thousands of rupiah.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x+y+z&=50 \\
x+2y+5z&=110
\end{aligned}" />

Subtract the first equation from the second, then put the result back into the first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y+4z&=60 \quad\Rightarrow\quad y=60-4z \\
x&=50-y-z=50-(60-4z)-z=3z-10
\end{aligned}" />

# Filtering with the conditions

Now <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> depend on <InlineMath math="z" />, and <InlineMath math="z" /> must be a whole number. Each kind of note appears at least once, and <InlineMath math="y" /> must be more than <InlineMath math="2z" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x\ge1&:\quad 3z-10\ge1 \;\Rightarrow\; z\ge\tfrac{11}{3} \;\Rightarrow\; z\ge4 \\
y>2z&:\quad 60-4z>2z \;\Rightarrow\; z<10 \;\Rightarrow\; z\le9 \\
y\ge1&:\quad 60-4z\ge1 \;\Rightarrow\; z\le14
\end{aligned}" />

The condition <InlineMath math="y\ge1" /> is already covered by <InlineMath math="z\le9" />. So **<InlineMath math="z" /> can only be <InlineMath math="4,5,6,7,8,9" />**, six values.

| <InlineMath math="z" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="8" /> | <InlineMath math="9" /> |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="y=60-4z" /> | <InlineMath math="44" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> | <InlineMath math="36" /> | <InlineMath math="32" /> | <InlineMath math="28" /> | <InlineMath math="24" /> |
| <InlineMath math="x=3z-10" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="8" /> | <InlineMath math="11" /> | <InlineMath math="14" /> | <InlineMath math="17" /> |

Check one column, say <InlineMath math="(2,44,4)" />: that's <InlineMath math="2+44+4=50" /> notes worth <InlineMath math="2+88+20=110" /> thousand. The value <InlineMath math="z=10" /> gives <InlineMath math="y=20" />, exactly <InlineMath math="2z" />, so it fails "lebih dari" (more than).

So there are <InlineMath math="6" /> possible arrangements.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="14" /> counts <InlineMath math="z=1" /> to <InlineMath math="14" />. It forgets both <InlineMath math="x\ge1" /> and the "more than twice" condition.
- <InlineMath math="11" /> counts <InlineMath math="z=4" /> to <InlineMath math="14" />, which forgets the condition <InlineMath math="y>2z" />.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> counts <InlineMath math="z=1" /> to <InlineMath math="9" />. **For <InlineMath math="z=1,2,3" /> the number of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}000" /> notes comes out negative**: <InlineMath math="-7" />, <InlineMath math="-4" />, and <InlineMath math="-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="7" /> also counts <InlineMath math="z=10" />, but there <InlineMath math="y=2z" />, which isn't more than <InlineMath math="2z" />.

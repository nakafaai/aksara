Variance is the **average squared deviation** from the mean. Adding a constant does not change those deviations. Multiplying the data by <InlineMath math="a" /> multiplies deviations by <InlineMath math="a" /> and variance by <InlineMath math="a^2" />.

A deviation is each value minus the data mean. For <InlineMath math="Y=aX+b" />, the mean becomes <InlineMath math="\bar Y=a\bar X+b" />, so the constant <InlineMath math="b" /> cancels when the mean is subtracted.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}Y-\bar Y&=(aX+b)-(a\bar X+b)=a(X-\bar X) \\ (Y-\bar Y)^2&=a^2(X-\bar X)^2\end{aligned}" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(Y)&=1.5^2\operatorname{Var}(X) \\
&=2.25(16)=36
\end{aligned}" />

In the second transformation, <InlineMath math="\bar Y" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> are constants for the entire data set. The factor <InlineMath math="2" /> multiplies variance by <InlineMath math="4" />.

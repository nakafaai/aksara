# What one day imports and exports

The question asks for the October bill plus the November bill. Two rules decide it: imports and exports are counted **day by day**, and the credit for exports is capped **in each month on its own**.

Start with a single day. Panel power during the day first covers the daytime use of <InlineMath math="4\text{ kWh}" />. Whatever is left is exported. If the panel falls short, the gap is imported. The night use of <InlineMath math="4\text{ kWh}" /> is always imported.

| Type of day | Exported | Imported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sunny | <InlineMath math="14-4=10\text{ kWh}" /> | <InlineMath math="4\text{ kWh}" /> (night) |
| Cloudy | <InlineMath math="0\text{ kWh}" /> | <InlineMath math="(4-2)+4=6\text{ kWh}" /> |

On a cloudy day the panel makes only <InlineMath math="2\text{ kWh}" />, so the daytime use is still <InlineMath math="2\text{ kWh}" /> short. That gap is imported too.

# The October bill

October has <InlineMath math="29" /> sunny days and <InlineMath math="2" /> cloudy days. Find the month's energy, then the cost of imports and the credit.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{imported}&=29\cdot4+2\cdot6=128\text{ kWh} \\
\text{exported}&=29\cdot10=290\text{ kWh} \\
\text{cost of imports}&=128\cdot1{,}500=192{,}000 \\
\text{credit}&=290\cdot800=232{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The credit is bigger than the cost of imports. By the rule, **the October bill is just the fixed charge**, <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}30{,}000" />. The extra credit of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}40{,}000" /> is lost and can't be used in November.

# The November bill

November has <InlineMath math="15" /> sunny days and <InlineMath math="15" /> cloudy days.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{imported}&=15\cdot4+15\cdot6=150\text{ kWh} \\
\text{exported}&=15\cdot10=150\text{ kWh} \\
\text{bill}&=30{,}000+150\cdot1{,}500-150\cdot800 \\
&=30{,}000+225{,}000-120{,}000=135{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

This time the credit is smaller than the cost of imports, so **the whole credit is used** and the bill is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}135{,}000" />.

The total is

<BlockMath math="30{,}000+135{,}000=165{,}000" />

So the total bill for October and November is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}165{,}000" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}144{,}000" /> comes from adding up the month's production and use first. That treats the surplus of sunny days as if it could cover the shortfall of cloudy days, but **imports and exports are recorded each day**.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}135{,}000" /> makes the October bill <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}0" />. The credit only reduces the cost of imports, never the fixed charge.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}125{,}000" /> carries October's extra credit of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}40{,}000" /> into November. The rule says that extra credit is lost.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}120{,}000" /> forgets that a cloudy day also imports <InlineMath math="2\text{ kWh}" /> for daytime use, so each cloudy day is counted with only <InlineMath math="4\text{ kWh}" /> imported.

You need the **length of the time interval** in which <InlineMath math="h(t)>34" />. Set up the inequality and bring it to standard form.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
-2t^2+16t+10&>34 \\
-2t^2+16t-24&>0 \\
t^2-8t+12&<0 \\
(t-2)(t-6)&<0
\end{aligned}" />

Dividing by <InlineMath math="-2" /> reverses the inequality sign. The product is negative when the factors have opposite signs, that is, for <InlineMath math="2<t<6" />. The interval lasts <InlineMath math="6-2=4" /> seconds.

As a check, <InlineMath math="h(2)=-8+32+10=34" /> and <InlineMath math="h(4)=-32+64+10=42>34" />.

So the drone is higher than <InlineMath math="34" /> metres for <InlineMath math="4" /> seconds.

- <InlineMath math="2" /> seconds is the moment the drone reaches <InlineMath math="34" /> metres, not the duration.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> seconds is the moment it drops below <InlineMath math="34" /> metres again.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> seconds adds the two moments instead of subtracting them.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> seconds is the product of the two moments, the constant term of <InlineMath math="t^2-8t+12" />.

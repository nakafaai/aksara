First calculate the **input to the inverse function**. Inverse notation means reversing the function mapping, not taking the reciprocal of its value.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f(4)&=\frac{3(4)-2}{5}=2 \\
f(4)+2&=4
\end{aligned}" />

To obtain the inverse, solve <InlineMath math="y=f(x)" /> for <InlineMath math="x" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
5y&=3x-2 \\
3x&=5y+2 \\
x&=\frac{5y+2}{3} \\
f^{-1}(y)&=\frac{5y+2}{3}
\end{aligned}" />

Insert the input <InlineMath math="4" /> into the inverse.

<BlockMath math="f^{-1}(4)=\frac{5(4)+2}{3}=\frac{22}{3}" />

Check the result with the original function. The input we found does produce 4.

<BlockMath math="f\!\left(\frac{22}{3}\right)=\frac{3(22/3)-2}{5}=\frac{22-2}{5}=4" />

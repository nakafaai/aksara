# September's energy

The question asks for the smallest price for exported energy that brings the September bill down to the fixed charge alone. That means **the credit has to cover the whole cost of imports**. The credit can't reduce the fixed charge, so the fixed charge isn't part of what it covers.

Work out the energy per day first, because imports and exports are recorded each day. A sunny day exports <InlineMath math="14-4=10\text{ kWh}" /> and imports <InlineMath math="4\text{ kWh}" /> for the night. A cloudy day exports nothing and imports <InlineMath math="(4-2)+4=6\text{ kWh}" />, because its daytime use is still <InlineMath math="2\text{ kWh}" /> short.

September has <InlineMath math="20" /> sunny days and <InlineMath math="10" /> cloudy days:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{exported}&=20\cdot10=200\text{ kWh} \\
\text{imported}&=20\cdot4+10\cdot6=140\text{ kWh} \\
\text{cost of imports}&=140\cdot1{,}500=210{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

# When the bill equals the fixed charge

At a price of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}b" /> per exported kWh, the credit is <InlineMath math="200b" /> rupiah. The bill drops to the fixed charge exactly when the credit is **at least as large as** the cost of imports. If the credit is bigger, the extra is lost and the bill doesn't drop any further.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
200b&\ge210{,}000 \\
b&\ge1{,}050
\end{aligned}" />

Check <InlineMath math="b=1{,}050" />: the credit is <InlineMath math="200\cdot1{,}050=210{,}000" />, exactly the cost of imports, so the bill is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}30{,}000" />. At today's price of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}800" /> the bill is still <InlineMath math="30{,}000+210{,}000-160{,}000=80{,}000" />.

So the smallest value of <InlineMath math="b" /> is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}050" /> per kWh.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}600" /> only checks one sunny day: <InlineMath math="10b\ge4\cdot1{,}500" />. **Cloudy days have imports too**, and the credit must cover them.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}900" /> forgets that a cloudy day imports its <InlineMath math="2\text{ kWh}" /> daytime gap, so the imports are counted as <InlineMath math="30\cdot4=120\text{ kWh}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}000" /> adds up the month's production first (<InlineMath math="300\text{ kWh}" />) and treats <InlineMath math="300-120=180\text{ kWh}" /> as exported. But the surplus of sunny days can't cover the gap on cloudy days.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}200" /> makes the credit cover the fixed charge as well: <InlineMath math="200b\ge240{,}000" />. The rule says the credit only reduces the cost of imports.

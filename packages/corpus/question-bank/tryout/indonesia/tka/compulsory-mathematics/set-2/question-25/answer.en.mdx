This is a conditional probability. You already know "both students are from the same club", so **the sample space shrinks** to the same-club pairs only. The grade X column isn't used, because only one grade XI student and one grade XII student are chosen.

# All possible pairs

One grade XI student is chosen from <InlineMath math="4+6+2=12" /> students and one grade XII student from <InlineMath math="5+2+3=10" /> students. The two choices are independent, so by the multiplication rule **every pair is equally likely**.

<BlockMath math="12\cdot10=120\text{ pairs}" />

# Same-club pairs

Count same-club pairs club by club, multiplying the number of grade XI and grade XII students in that club. The three clubs don't overlap, so **add the results**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Robotik}&:\ 4\cdot5=20 \\
\text{Jurnalistik}&:\ 6\cdot2=12 \\
\text{Paduan Suara}&:\ 2\cdot3=6 \\
\text{same club}&:\ 20+12+6=38
\end{aligned}" />

# The conditional probability

Of the <InlineMath math="38" /> pairs that meet the condition, <InlineMath math="20" /> are Robotik pairs. **The denominator is <InlineMath math="38" />**, not <InlineMath math="120" />.

<BlockMath math="P(\text{Robotik}\mid\text{same club})=\frac{20/120}{38/120}=\frac{20}{38}=\frac{10}{19}" />

That makes sense: Robotik has the most same-club pairs, so its probability is above one half.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac16" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{20}{120}" />. It divides by all pairs, even though the "same club" condition is already known.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{19}{60}" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{38}{120}" />, the probability of the condition itself, not the probability asked for.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{9}{22}" /> adds student counts, <InlineMath math="\frac{4+5}{9+8+5}" />, as if only one person were chosen. Pairs are counted by **multiplying**, not adding.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{18}{37}" /> picks any two grade XI or XII students in one club, <InlineMath math="\frac{\binom92}{\binom92+\binom82+\binom52}=\frac{36}{74}" />. The question requires exactly one grade XI student and one grade XII student.

So the probability that both are Robotik members is <InlineMath math="\frac{10}{19}" />.

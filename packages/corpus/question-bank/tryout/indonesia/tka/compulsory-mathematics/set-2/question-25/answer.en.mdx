There are <InlineMath math="\binom82=28" /> equally likely unordered pairs. An odd sum requires one odd card and one even card. Each group contains four cards.

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{4\cdot4}{28}=\frac47" />

The available primes are <InlineMath math="2,3,5,7" />. For the larger number to be <InlineMath math="8" />, the other card can be any of the seven smaller cards.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(\text{prime, prime})&=\frac{\binom42}{28}=\frac3{14} \\
P(\max=8)&=\frac7{28}=\frac14
\end{aligned}" />

Given an even first card, four odd cards remain among seven. The probability of an odd second card is <InlineMath math="\frac47" />. This differs from the unconditional probability <InlineMath math="\frac48=\frac12" />, so the events are not independent.

The four numerical probability statements are true. The independence statement is false.

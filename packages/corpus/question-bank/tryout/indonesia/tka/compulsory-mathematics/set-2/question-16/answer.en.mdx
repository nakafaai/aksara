The volume of a prism is **base area times height**. Here the base is the trapezium in the lengthwise cross-section of the pool, and the height of the prism is the width <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{\text{trapezium}}&=\frac{1+2}{2}\cdot25=37.5\text{ m}^2 \\
V&=37.5\cdot10=375\text{ m}^3=375{,}000\text{ litres}
\end{aligned}" />

One cubic metre holds <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> litres.

As a check, the pool has an average depth of <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" />, and <InlineMath math="25\cdot10\cdot1.5=375" />.

So filling it takes <InlineMath math="375{,}000" /> litres.

- <InlineMath math="250{,}000" /> litres uses the shallow depth <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> everywhere.
- <InlineMath math="500{,}000" /> litres uses the deep end <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> everywhere.
- <InlineMath math="750{,}000" /> litres forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> in the trapezium.
- <InlineMath math="3{,}750{,}000" /> litres uses <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}^3=10{,}000" /> litres.

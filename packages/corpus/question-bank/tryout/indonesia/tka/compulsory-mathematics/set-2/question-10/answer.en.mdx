Both hands move steadily, and you measure both angles clockwise from twelve. The key point is that **the hour hand also keeps moving between full hours**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{minute hand}&:\ 20\cdot6^\circ=120^\circ \\
\text{hour hand}&:\ 4\cdot30^\circ+20\cdot0.5^\circ=120^\circ+10^\circ=130^\circ \\
\text{difference}&:\ 130^\circ-120^\circ=10^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

The minute hand covers <InlineMath math="360^\circ" /> in <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes, so <InlineMath math="6^\circ" /> per minute. The hour hand covers only <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> in <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes, so <InlineMath math="0.5^\circ" /> per minute.

As a check, at <InlineMath math="4" /> o'clock the hands are <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> apart. In <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes the minute hand gains <InlineMath math="20\cdot5.5^\circ=110^\circ" />, which leaves <InlineMath math="10^\circ" />.

So the smaller angle is <InlineMath math="10^\circ" />.

- <InlineMath math="0^\circ" /> keeps the hour hand exactly on the four.
- <InlineMath math="20^\circ" /> moves the hour hand <InlineMath math="1^\circ" /> per minute instead of <InlineMath math="0.5^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> is the angle between two neighbouring numbers on the dial.
- <InlineMath math="350^\circ" /> is the larger of the two angles between the hands.

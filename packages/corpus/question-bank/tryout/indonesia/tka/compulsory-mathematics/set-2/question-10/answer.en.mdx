# The ratio along the diagonal

The diagonals split the trapezium into four triangles. Triangles <InlineMath math="AOB" /> and <InlineMath math="BOC" /> share the vertex <InlineMath math="B" />, and their bases <InlineMath math="AO" /> and <InlineMath math="OC" /> lie on the same line. So they have the same height, and **their areas are in the ratio of their bases**.

<BlockMath math="AO:OC=36:24=3:2" />

# The other two triangles

Triangles <InlineMath math="ABD" /> and <InlineMath math="ABC" /> have the same base <InlineMath math="AB" /> and the same height, because <InlineMath math="DC" /> is parallel to <InlineMath math="AB" />. Both contain triangle <InlineMath math="AOB" />, so what's left is equal too: **the area of <InlineMath math="AOD" /> equals the area of <InlineMath math="BOC" />**, which is <InlineMath math="24" />.

Triangles <InlineMath math="AOD" /> and <InlineMath math="COD" /> share the vertex <InlineMath math="D" /> with bases <InlineMath math="AO" /> and <InlineMath math="OC" />, so their areas are also in the ratio <InlineMath math="3:2" />.

<BlockMath math="A_{COD}=\tfrac23\cdot24=16" />

# The area of the trapezium

**Add up all four triangles**:

<BlockMath math="36+24+24+16=100" />

So the area of trapezium <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> is <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option **forgets one triangle or guesses its area**.

- <InlineMath math="76\text{ cm}^2" /> forgets triangle <InlineMath math="AOD" />.
- <InlineMath math="84\text{ cm}^2" /> forgets triangle <InlineMath math="COD" />.
- <InlineMath math="108\text{ cm}^2" /> assumes <InlineMath math="COD" /> has the same area as <InlineMath math="BOC" />. Only <InlineMath math="AOD" /> and <InlineMath math="BOC" /> are equal.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^2" /> assumes <InlineMath math="COD" /> has the same area as <InlineMath math="AOB" />. But <InlineMath math="DC" /> is shorter than <InlineMath math="AB" />, so that triangle is smaller.

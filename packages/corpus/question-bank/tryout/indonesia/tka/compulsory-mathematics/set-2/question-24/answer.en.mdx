The question counts committees of <InlineMath math="4" /> people. All members have the same role, so the order of choosing doesn't matter and you use **combinations**. Rani can't join, which leaves <InlineMath math="10" /> candidates: <InlineMath math="5" /> boys and <InlineMath math="5" /> girls.

# The boys and girls condition

The quickest route is the complement: count every committee from the <InlineMath math="10" /> people, then throw out the ones that are **all boys** or **all girls**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\binom{10}{4}&=210 \\
\text{all boys}&=\binom54=5,\qquad\text{all girls}=\binom54=5 \\
210-5-5&=200
\end{aligned}" />

# The Andi and Budi condition

From those <InlineMath math="200" /> committees, remove the ones with both Andi and Budi. If both are in, the other two seats are filled from the remaining <InlineMath math="8" /> people: <InlineMath math="3" /> boys and <InlineMath math="5" /> girls.

| The other two members | Number of ways | Already thrown out? |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> girls | <InlineMath math="\binom52=10" /> | no |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> boy, <InlineMath math="1" /> girl | <InlineMath math="3\cdot5=15" /> | no |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> boys | <InlineMath math="\binom32=3" /> | yes, all boys |

Those last three committees were already thrown out as all-boy committees, so **don't remove them twice**. Only <InlineMath math="10+15=25" /> still need to go.

<BlockMath math="200-25=175" />

Check it another way, by how many of Andi and Budi are in.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{neither Andi nor Budi}&:\ \binom84-\binom54=70-5=65 \\
\text{exactly one of them}&:\ 2\cdot\left[\binom83-\binom33\right]=2\cdot(56-1)=110 \\
\text{total}&=65+110=175
\end{aligned}" />

In the first line the candidates are <InlineMath math="3" /> boys and <InlineMath math="5" /> girls, and the all-girl committees have to go. In the second line there's already one boy, so the other three just can't all be boys.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="172" /> removes all <InlineMath math="\binom82=28" /> committees with Andi and Budi. Three of them were already removed as all-boy committees, so they get **removed twice**.
- <InlineMath math="180" /> removes only one single-gender group, <InlineMath math="210-5-25" />. All-boy and all-girl committees are both banned.
- <InlineMath math="200" /> forgets the Andi and Budi condition.
- <InlineMath math="277" /> still includes Rani, so there are <InlineMath math="11" /> candidates.

So there are <InlineMath math="175" /> ways to form the committee.

Drop a perpendicular from <InlineMath math="B" /> to the line <InlineMath math="SA" />, meeting it at <InlineMath math="H" />. In right triangle <InlineMath math="SHB" />, <InlineMath math="SB" /> is the hypotenuse. First check that <InlineMath math="H" /> lies on the segment <InlineMath math="SA" />.

<BlockMath math="SH=10\cos60^\circ=10\cdot\frac12=5" />

Since <InlineMath math="0<5<8" />, <InlineMath math="H" /> lies between <InlineMath math="S" /> and <InlineMath math="A" />. The required shortest distance is the perpendicular length <InlineMath math="BH" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sin60^\circ&=\frac{BH}{10} \\
BH&=10\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=5\sqrt3
\end{aligned}" />

The shortest distance is <InlineMath math="5\sqrt3\,\mathrm{km}" />.

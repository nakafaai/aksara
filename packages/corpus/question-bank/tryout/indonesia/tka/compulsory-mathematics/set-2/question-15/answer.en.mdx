A <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation about <InlineMath math="O" /> sends every point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-x,-y)" />. So for each composition, **work out the image of a general point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" />** and compare it with <InlineMath math="(-x,-y)" />. Testing one or two points isn't enough, because two different transformations can happen to agree at a few points.

# The rules you need

For a centre <InlineMath math="P(a,b)" />, start from the vector <InlineMath math="(x-a,\ y-b)" />: turn the vector, then add <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> back.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
R_{P,90^\circ}:&\ (x,y)\mapsto\big(a-(y-b),\ b+(x-a)\big) \\
R_{P,180^\circ}:&\ (x,y)\mapsto(2a-x,\ 2b-y) \\
\text{reflection in } x=h:&\ (x,y)\mapsto(2h-x,\ y) \\
\text{reflection in } y=k:&\ (x,y)\mapsto(x,\ 2k-y)
\end{aligned}" />

One more fact helps. Two <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> rotations in a row always give **a <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation about some centre**, but that centre isn't necessarily <InlineMath math="O" />. So angles adding up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> aren't enough on their own. You also have to check the centre.

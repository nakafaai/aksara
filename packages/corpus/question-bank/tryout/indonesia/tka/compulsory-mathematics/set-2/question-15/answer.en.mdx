Reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis changes the second coordinate’s sign. The following rotation swaps the coordinates and changes the sign of the new first coordinate.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x,y)&\longmapsto(x,-y) \\
&\longmapsto(y,x) \\
T(x,y)&=(y,x) \\
T(2,-3)&=(-3,2) \\
T^2(x,y)&=T(y,x) \\
&=(x,y)
\end{aligned}" />

Thus <InlineMath math="T^2" /> is the identity. A fixed point must satisfy <InlineMath math="(y,x)=(x,y)" />, so the fixed points are exactly the line <InlineMath math="y=x" />. The map <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)" /> is reflection in that line and **reverses orientation**.

The four claims about the coordinate rule, point image, identity, and fixed points are true. Preservation of orientation is false.

Orientation here means the order around a figure’s vertices. Triangle ABC runs counterclockwise in the diagram, while its image A′B′C′ runs clockwise. Side lengths and area stay the same because this transformation is a reflection.

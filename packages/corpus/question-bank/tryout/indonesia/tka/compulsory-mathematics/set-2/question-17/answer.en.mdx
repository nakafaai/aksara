The question asks for the length of plastic bought off the roll, not the area of plastic. The roll is <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" /> wide, so **every piece uses up roll length**, including the extra bits for clamping and the overlaps. Work it out piece by piece: the length of one roof sheet, the number of sheets, then the pieces for the two ends.

# Length of one roof sheet

One sheet arches from one long wall to the other. That arch is **half the circumference of a circle** with radius <InlineMath math="3.5\text{ m}" />, plus <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}" /> at each end for clamping.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{arch}&=\tfrac12\cdot2\pi r=\tfrac{22}{7}\cdot3.5=11\text{ m} \\
\text{one sheet}&=11+0.5+0.5=12\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

# Number of roof sheets

The sheets sit side by side to cover the <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}" /> length of the roof. The first sheet covers <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" />. Every later sheet overlaps the one before by <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}" />, so it **only adds <InlineMath math="3.5\text{ m}" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3\text{ sheets}&:\ 4+2\cdot3.5=11\text{ m}<12\text{ m} \\
4\text{ sheets}&:\ 4+3\cdot3.5=14.5\text{ m}\ge12\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Three sheets still leave a gap, so you need <InlineMath math="4" /> sheets. The roll length for the roof is <InlineMath math="4\cdot12=48\text{ m}" />.

# The two end pieces

Each half-disc end is as tall as the radius, <InlineMath math="3.5\text{ m}" />, so it fits across the <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" /> roll. By the rule, each end uses a piece as long as the diameter, <InlineMath math="7\text{ m}" />. The part outside the half disc **still gets bought**, even though it's thrown away.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{two ends}&=2\cdot7=14\text{ m} \\
\text{total}&=48+14=62\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

- <InlineMath math="106\text{ m}" /> uses the full circumference, <InlineMath math="22\text{ m}" />, for one sheet. The roof is only a **half** cylinder, so the arch is half the circumference.
- <InlineMath math="58\text{ m}" /> leaves out the <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}" /> extra at both ends of a sheet, so one sheet is taken as <InlineMath math="11\text{ m}" />.
- <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" /> ignores the overlap, so <InlineMath math="12\div4=3" /> sheets look like enough. With overlaps, three sheets cover only <InlineMath math="11\text{ m}" />.
- <InlineMath math="42.625\text{ m}" /> comes from the plastic area <InlineMath math="132+38.5=170.5\text{ m}^2" /> divided by the roll width. That skips the overlaps, the clamped ends and the offcuts from the end pieces.

So the least length of plastic to buy is <InlineMath math="62\text{ m}" />.

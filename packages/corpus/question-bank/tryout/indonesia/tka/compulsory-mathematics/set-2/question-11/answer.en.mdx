# The height of the post from the first position

The light ray passes over the top of Dinda's head and lands at the tip of the shadow. So there are two right triangles that are **similar**: the small one made by Dinda and the shadow, and the big one from the post to the tip of the shadow. The base of the big triangle is the distance to the post **plus** the shadow length.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{H}{6+2}&=\frac{1.5}{2} \\
H&=6
\end{aligned}" />

So the post is <InlineMath math="6" /> m tall.

# The second position

Now the shadow is <InlineMath math="3" /> m. Let Dinda's distance from the post be <InlineMath math="d" />. **Use the same ratio** with the post height you now know.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{6}{d+3}&=\frac{1.5}{3} \\
d+3&=12 \\
d&=9
\end{aligned}" />

Dinda moves from <InlineMath math="6" /> m to <InlineMath math="9" /> m from the post, so the distance walked is <InlineMath math="9-6=3" /> m.

So Dinda walks <InlineMath math="3" /> m.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option is **another length from the same picture**.

- <InlineMath math="12" /> m is how far the tip of the second shadow is from the foot of the post.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> m is Dinda's distance from the post in the second position, before subtracting the starting position.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> m is the height of the post.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> m is how far the tip of the shadow moves, from <InlineMath math="8" /> m to <InlineMath math="12" /> m. The tip of the shadow moves farther than Dinda does.

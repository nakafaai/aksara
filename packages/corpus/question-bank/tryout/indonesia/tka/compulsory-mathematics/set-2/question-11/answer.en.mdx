The longest segment inside a cuboid joins two opposite corners: it is the **space diagonal**. You get it with the Pythagorean theorem in two steps, first the diagonal of the floor, then the space diagonal from the floor diagonal and the height.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
d_{\text{floor}}^2&=120^2+40^2=14{,}400+1{,}600=16{,}000 \\
d^2&=d_{\text{floor}}^2+60^2=16{,}000+3{,}600=19{,}600 \\
d&=\sqrt{19{,}600}=140\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, all three edges share the factor <InlineMath math="20" />, and <InlineMath math="6^2+2^2+3^2=49=7^2" />. So <InlineMath math="d=20\cdot7=140" />.

So the longest rod that fits is <InlineMath math="140\text{ cm}" /> long.

- About <InlineMath math="156.2\text{ cm}" /> adds width and height before squaring, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{120^2+(40+60)^2}=\sqrt{24{,}400}" />.
- About <InlineMath math="126.5\text{ cm}" /> is the floor diagonal, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{16{,}000}" />. Tilting the rod upward lets a longer rod fit.
- About <InlineMath math="134.2\text{ cm}" /> is the diagonal of a side face, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{120^2+60^2}" />.
- About <InlineMath math="186.5\text{ cm}" /> adds the floor diagonal and the height, <InlineMath math="126.5+60" />. The two segments do not lie on one line, so their lengths do not add.

# What a dilation about P means

A dilation about <InlineMath math="P" /> with factor <InlineMath math="k" /> **multiplies the step from <InlineMath math="P" /> to a point by <InlineMath math="k" />**. If <InlineMath math="k" /> is negative, the image lands on the other side of <InlineMath math="P" />.

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{PQ'}=k\cdot\overrightarrow{PQ}" />

# Finding the scale factor

Work out the step from <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="A" /> and from <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="A'" />, then **compare the two steps**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{PA}&=(4-2,\ 2-1)=(2,1) \\
\overrightarrow{PA'}&=(-2-2,\ -1-1)=(-4,-2) \\
k&=-2
\end{aligned}" />

Both coordinates give <InlineMath math="k=-2" />, so the factor checks out.

# The image of C

The step from <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="C" /> is <InlineMath math="(3,0)" />. Multiply it by <InlineMath math="-2" />, then **add it back onto <InlineMath math="P" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{PC'}&=-2\cdot(3,0)=(-6,0) \\
C'&=(2,1)+(-6,0)=(-4,1)
\end{aligned}" />

So the image of <InlineMath math="C" /> is <InlineMath math="(-4,1)" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option **mishandles the centre or the factor**.

- <InlineMath math="(-10,-2)" /> uses factor <InlineMath math="-2" /> about <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> instead of <InlineMath math="P" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-6,0)" /> is the step <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PC'}" />, never added back onto <InlineMath math="P" />.
- <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac12,1\right)" /> uses <InlineMath math="k=-\tfrac12" />, the ratio upside down.
- <InlineMath math="(8,1)" /> uses <InlineMath math="k=2" />, so the minus sign got lost.

# The type of the largest angle

The largest angle always faces the longest side, which is <InlineMath math="8" />. **Compare the square of the longest side with the sum of the squares of the other two**:

<BlockMath math="3^2+7^2=58<64=8^2" />

Since it's smaller, the angle facing side <InlineMath math="8" /> is obtuse.

# The angle facing side seven

Use the cosine rule for the angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> facing side <InlineMath math="7" />. **Side <InlineMath math="7" /> goes on the left**, while <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="8" /> are the sides that enclose the angle.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
7^2&=3^2+8^2-2\cdot3\cdot8\cos\theta \\
49&=73-48\cos\theta \\
\cos\theta&=\tfrac12 \\
\theta&=60^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

# The height to side eight

The <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> angle sits between sides <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="8" />, so you get the area from two sides and the angle between them. Then **use the same area again** with base <InlineMath math="8" /> to find the height.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A&=\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot8\cdot\sin60^\circ=6\sqrt3 \\
h&=\frac{2A}{8}=\frac{12\sqrt3}{8}=\tfrac32\sqrt3
\end{aligned}" />

# Judging each statement

The first statement is **False**, because the angle facing side <InlineMath math="8" /> is obtuse. The second is **True**, because the angle facing side <InlineMath math="7" /> is <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />. The third is **True**, because the height is <InlineMath math="\tfrac32\sqrt3" /> cm.

So the answers are False, True, True.

# The cost of each plan

The question asks how many whole weights <InlineMath math="u" /> from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="40" /> make Paket Kuota **strictly cheaper**, not equally priced. Each plan has an allowance, so its cost formula changes at <InlineMath math="u=3" /> and at <InlineMath math="u=12" />. That's why you compare the plans piece by piece.

Write the costs in thousands of rupiah. Hemat charges <InlineMath math="6" /> thousand only for each kg above <InlineMath math="3" />, and Kuota charges <InlineMath math="10" /> thousand only for each kg above <InlineMath math="12" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
H(u)&=\begin{cases}30, & u\le3 \\ 30+6(u-3)=6u+12, & u>3\end{cases} \\
K(u)&=\begin{cases}60, & u\le12 \\ 60+10(u-12)=10u-60, & u>12\end{cases}
\end{aligned}" />

# Comparing piece by piece

**First piece**, <InlineMath math="1\le u\le3" />: Hemat is <InlineMath math="30" /> and Kuota is <InlineMath math="60" />, so Kuota costs more.

**Second piece**, <InlineMath math="3<u\le12" />: Hemat already pays for extra kilograms, while Kuota is still a flat <InlineMath math="60" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
60&<6u+12 \\
6u&>48 \\
u&>8
\end{aligned}" />

At <InlineMath math="u=8" /> both cost <InlineMath math="60" /> thousand. So Kuota is cheaper for <InlineMath math="u=9,10,11,12" />.

**Third piece**, <InlineMath math="12<u\le40" />: both plans are past their allowance.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
10u-60&<6u+12 \\
4u&<72 \\
u&<18
\end{aligned}" />

Draw both cost functions. **Kuota is cheaper exactly where its graph lies below the graph of Hemat**, between the two crossings at <InlineMath math="u=8" /> and <InlineMath math="u=18" />. The crossings themselves do not count because the costs are equal there.

- <InlineMath math="29" /> counts the weights where Hemat is cheaper, <InlineMath math="7+22" />, which is the opposite of what's asked.
- <InlineMath math="17" /> forgets Hemat's <InlineMath math="3\text{ kg}" /> allowance and charges Hemat <InlineMath math="30+6u" /> from the first kilogram.
- <InlineMath math="11" /> also counts <InlineMath math="u=8" /> and <InlineMath math="u=18" />. At those two weights the costs are equal, so Kuota isn't cheaper.

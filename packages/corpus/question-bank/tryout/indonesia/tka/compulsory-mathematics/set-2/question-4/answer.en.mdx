Use **elimination by addition**. The coefficients of <InlineMath math="y" /> are opposites, so the terms containing <InlineMath math="y" /> cancel.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}(3x+3y)+(x-3y)&=20+8 \\ 4x&=28 \\ x&=\frac{28}{4}=7\end{aligned}" />

Substitute <InlineMath math="x=7" /> into the second equation to find <InlineMath math="y" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}7-3y&=8 \\ -3y&=8-7=1 \\ y&=-\frac13\end{aligned}" />

Check the first equation, then calculate the requested product.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}3(7)+3\left(-\frac13\right)&=21-1=20 \\ xy&=7\left(-\frac13\right)=-\frac73\end{aligned}" />

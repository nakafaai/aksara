# The new sample space

The phrase **given that the product is even** changes the sample space. You no longer count from all <InlineMath math="216" /> outcomes, only from the outcomes whose product is even.

A product of three numbers is odd only when all three are odd. Each die has <InlineMath math="3" /> odd faces, so <InlineMath math="3^3=27" /> outcomes have an odd product.

<BlockMath math="\text{even product}=216-27=189" />

# Counting outcomes with a six

First count the outcomes **without** a <InlineMath math="6" />: each die has <InlineMath math="5" /> choices, so there are <InlineMath math="5^3=125" />. The rest, <InlineMath math="216-125=91" /> outcomes, have at least one <InlineMath math="6" />.

All <InlineMath math="91" /> of those automatically have an even product, because <InlineMath math="6" /> is even. So they're all inside the new sample space.

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{91}{189}=\frac{13}{27}" />

That makes sense: it's bigger than <InlineMath math="\tfrac{91}{216}" />, because knowing the product is even throws away outcomes that certainly have no <InlineMath math="6" />.

So the probability is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{13}{27}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{25}{63}" /> counts only outcomes with **exactly** one <InlineMath math="6" />, which is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{75}{189}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> adds <InlineMath math="\tfrac16+\tfrac16+\tfrac16" />, but the three events aren't mutually exclusive.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{19}{27}" /> thinks an even product means all three faces are even, so the sample space shrinks to <InlineMath math="27" /> outcomes.

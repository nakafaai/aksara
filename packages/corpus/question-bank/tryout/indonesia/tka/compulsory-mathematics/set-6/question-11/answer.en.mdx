The area of a trapezium needs **its vertical height**, not the length of the sloping wall. Drop vertical lines from both ends of the base to the top. The wider top splits into <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> in the middle and two equal pieces on the left and right.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{edge piece}&=\frac{2.2-1}{2}=0.6\text{ m} \\
t&=\sqrt{1^2-0.6^2}=\sqrt{0.64}=0.8\text{ m} \\
L&=\frac{1+2.2}{2}\cdot0.8=1.6\cdot0.8=1.28\text{ m}^2
\end{aligned}" />

So the cross-section of the channel is <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ m}^2" />.

- <InlineMath math="0.64\text{ m}^2" /> halves the correct result a second time.
- <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ m}^2" /> uses the <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> sloping wall as the height.
- <InlineMath math="2.56\text{ m}^2" /> forgets to halve the sum of the parallel sides.
- <InlineMath math="3.2\text{ m}^2" /> forgets to halve and uses the sloping wall as the height.

Write out both compositions first. **The order decides which function acts first**: <InlineMath math="(f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))" /> and <InlineMath math="(g\circ f)(x)=g(f(x))" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(f\circ g)(x)&=2x^2+1 \\
(g\circ f)(x)&=(2x+1)^2=4x^2+4x+1 \\
f^{-1}(x)&=\frac{x-1}{2}
\end{aligned}" />

- Since <InlineMath math="x^2\ge0" />, <InlineMath math="2x^2+1" /> is always at least <InlineMath math="1" />. This statement is true.
- <InlineMath math="(2x+1)^2" /> equals <InlineMath math="0" /> at <InlineMath math="x=-\frac12" />, so it is not always at least <InlineMath math="1" />. This statement is false.
- <InlineMath math="2x^2+1=4x^2+4x+1" /> becomes <InlineMath math="2x^2+4x=0" />, that is <InlineMath math="2x(x+2)=0" />. The solutions are exactly <InlineMath math="x=0" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-2" />. This statement is true.
- <InlineMath math="g(3)=9" /> and <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(9)=\frac{9-1}{2}=4" />. Check: <InlineMath math="f(4)=9" />. This statement is true.
- <InlineMath math="(f\circ g)(-1)=2+1=3" />, but <InlineMath math="(g\circ f)(-1)=(-1)^2=1" />. This statement is false.

So the true statements are <InlineMath math="(f\circ g)(x)\ge1" />, the solutions <InlineMath math="x=0" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-2" />, and <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(g(3))=4" />.

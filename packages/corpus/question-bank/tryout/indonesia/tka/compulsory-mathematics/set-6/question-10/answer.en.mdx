# The angle between a side face and the base

The question asks for the volume, but you only know the surface area and an angle. So **the angle has to be turned into a relation between lengths first**.

Let the base edge be <InlineMath math="s" />, let <InlineMath math="M" /> be the center of the base and <InlineMath math="N" /> the midpoint of edge <InlineMath math="BC" />. Segments <InlineMath math="TN" /> and <InlineMath math="MN" /> are both perpendicular to <InlineMath math="BC" />, so the angle between face <InlineMath math="TBC" /> and the base is angle <InlineMath math="TNM" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="TMN" /> has its right angle at <InlineMath math="M" />, with <InlineMath math="MN=\tfrac{s}{2}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\cos60^\circ=\frac{MN}{TN} \;&\Rightarrow\; TN=\frac{s/2}{1/2}=s \\
\tan60^\circ=\frac{TM}{MN} \;&\Rightarrow\; TM=\frac{s}{2}\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}" />

Segment <InlineMath math="TN" /> is the height of a triangular side face, while <InlineMath math="TM" /> is the height of the pyramid. They're different, and the volume uses <InlineMath math="TM" />.

# The base edge from the surface area

The surface is one square plus four triangles with base <InlineMath math="s" /> and height <InlineMath math="TN=s" />. **The height of each side face is already known in terms of <InlineMath math="s" />**, so the area depends on <InlineMath math="s" /> alone:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
s^{2}+4\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot s\cdot s&=144 \\
3s^{2}&=144 \\
s^{2}&=48 \quad\Rightarrow\quad s=4\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}" />

# Height and volume

Put <InlineMath math="s=4\sqrt{3}" /> into the formula for the pyramid's height, then work out the volume:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
TM&=\frac{4\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot\sqrt{3}=6\text{ cm} \\
V&=\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot s^{2}\cdot TM=\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot48\cdot6=96\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

Check with Pythagoras in triangle <InlineMath math="TMN" />: <InlineMath math="TM^{2}+MN^{2}=36+12=48=TN^{2}" />. **The three lengths fit together**.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option **uses the wrong length as the height or misreads the area**.

- <InlineMath math="288\text{ cm}^3" /> forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{3}" />: <InlineMath math="48\cdot6=288" />.
- <InlineMath math="72\sqrt{6}\text{ cm}^3" /> treats <InlineMath math="144\text{ cm}^2" /> as the area of the four side faces only: <InlineMath math="2s^{2}=144" /> gives <InlineMath math="s^{2}=72" /> and a height of <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{6}" />.
- <InlineMath math="32\sqrt{15}\text{ cm}^3" /> uses the slant edge <InlineMath math="TB=\sqrt{36+24}=2\sqrt{15}" /> as the height.
- <InlineMath math="64\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}^3" /> uses the side-face height <InlineMath math="TN=4\sqrt{3}" /> as the pyramid's height.

So the volume of the pyramid is <InlineMath math="96\text{ cm}^3" />.

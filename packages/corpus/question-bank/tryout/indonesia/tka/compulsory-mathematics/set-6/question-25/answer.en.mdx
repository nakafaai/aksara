# The sample space of triangles

The words **given that they form a triangle** mean the sample space isn't every set of three points, only the sets that aren't collinear.

There are <InlineMath math="\binom93=84" /> ways to choose three of the nine points. On this grid, three points in a line sit on <InlineMath math="3" /> rows, <InlineMath math="3" /> columns and <InlineMath math="2" /> diagonals, so <InlineMath math="8" /> sets are collinear.

<BlockMath math="\text{triangles}=84-8=76" />

# Triangles that use the centre

If <InlineMath math="(1,1)" /> is chosen, the other two points come from the remaining eight, in <InlineMath math="\binom82=28" /> ways. But some pairs are in line with <InlineMath math="(1,1)" />: one on the middle row, one on the middle column and one on each diagonal. So **<InlineMath math="4" /> pairs have to be thrown out**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{triangles with } (1,1)&=28-4=24 \\
P&=\frac{24}{76}=\frac{6}{19}
\end{aligned}" />

So the probability is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{6}{19}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> doesn't remove collinear sets at all, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{28}{84}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{7}{19}" /> removes the collinear sets from the denominator but forgets the <InlineMath math="4" /> collinear pairs in the numerator, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{28}{76}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{13}{19}" /> is the opposite event: <InlineMath math="(1,1)" /> is **not** a vertex.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{19}{21}" /> stops at the probability of getting a triangle, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{76}{84}" />.

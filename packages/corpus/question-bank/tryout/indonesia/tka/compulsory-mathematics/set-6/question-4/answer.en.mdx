# How fast each pump works

The question asks for the earliest time pump A can be switched off so the pond is still empty by 17:00 (5 p.m.) at the latest. What you can add up is **the fraction of the pond emptied per hour**, not the times themselves.

Say pump A empties <InlineMath math="u" /> of the pond per hour and pump B empties <InlineMath math="v" /> per hour. The two situations in the question give two equations:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
8u+8v&=1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad u+v=\tfrac{1}{8} \\
4u+16v&=1
\end{aligned}" />

Multiply the first equation by <InlineMath math="4" /> to get <InlineMath math="4u+4v=\tfrac{1}{2}" />, then subtract it from the second:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
12v&=1-\tfrac{1}{2}=\tfrac{1}{2} \\
v&=\tfrac{1}{24} \\
u&=\tfrac{1}{8}-\tfrac{1}{24}=\tfrac{1}{12}
\end{aligned}" />

So pump A alone needs <InlineMath math="12" /> hours and pump B alone needs <InlineMath math="24" /> hours.

# The condition for finishing on time

From 06:00 to 17:00 there are <InlineMath math="11" /> hours. Pump B runs for all <InlineMath math="11" /> hours, while pump A runs for <InlineMath math="k" /> hours and is then switched off. The pond is empty on time when **the fractions emptied add up to at least one whole pond**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{k}{12}+\frac{11}{24}&\ge1 \\
\frac{k}{12}&\ge\frac{13}{24} \\
k&\ge6.5
\end{aligned}" />

Pump A can only be switched off exactly on the hour, so <InlineMath math="k" /> has to be a whole number. The smallest whole number with <InlineMath math="k\ge6.5" /> is <InlineMath math="k=7" />, which is 13:00.

# Checking both sides of the limit

If A is switched off at 13:00, the two pumps have already emptied <InlineMath math="\tfrac{7}{8}" /> of the pond. B empties the remaining <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{8}" /> in <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{8}\div\tfrac{1}{24}=3" /> hours, so the pond is empty at 16:00, before 17:00. If A is switched off at 12:00, a quarter of the pond is left and B needs <InlineMath math="6" /> hours, so the pond is only empty at 18:00. **13:00 really is the earliest whole hour that works**.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option comes from **one swapped or skipped step**.

- 08:00 comes from swapping the speeds, so A takes <InlineMath math="24" /> hours and B takes <InlineMath math="12" />. Then <InlineMath math="\tfrac{k}{24}+\tfrac{11}{12}\ge1" /> gives <InlineMath math="k\ge2" />.
- 11:00 comes from letting B work another <InlineMath math="11" /> hours after the joint phase: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{k}{8}+\tfrac{11}{24}\ge1" /> gives <InlineMath math="k\ge4\tfrac{1}{3}" />. That counts B's work during the joint phase twice.
- 12:00 comes from rounding <InlineMath math="6.5" /> down. With <InlineMath math="k=6" />, the pond is only empty at 18:00.
- 12:30 is the answer without the whole-hour rule. Pump A can't be switched off at 12:30.

So the earliest time pump A can be switched off is 13:00.

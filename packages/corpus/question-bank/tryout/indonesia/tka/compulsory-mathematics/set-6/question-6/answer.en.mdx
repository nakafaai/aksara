# Reading points on the inverse graph

The question asks for <InlineMath math="f\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right)" />, but the points you're given lie on the graph of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" />, not on the graph of <InlineMath math="f" />. An inverse function swaps input and output, so **flip the coordinates of each point**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f^{-1}(6)=1 \;&\Rightarrow\; f(1)=6 \\
f^{-1}(54)=3 \;&\Rightarrow\; f(3)=54
\end{aligned}" />

# Finding a and b

Write both facts with the rule <InlineMath math="f(x)=a\cdot b^{x}" />, then divide the second equation by the first so that <InlineMath math="a" /> cancels:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a\cdot b=6, \qquad a\cdot b^{3}&=54 \\
\frac{a\cdot b^{3}}{a\cdot b}=b^{2}&=\frac{54}{6}=9
\end{aligned}" />

The equation <InlineMath math="b^{2}=9" /> has two solutions, <InlineMath math="b=3" /> and <InlineMath math="b=-3" />. **The condition <InlineMath math="b>0" /> rules out <InlineMath math="b=-3" />**, so <InlineMath math="b=3" /> and <InlineMath math="a=6\div3=2" />. The function is <InlineMath math="f(x)=2\cdot3^{x}" />.

# Working with a fractional exponent

Split the exponent <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5}{2}" /> into a whole part and a half, because **a power of one half is a square root**:

<BlockMath math="f\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right)=2\cdot3^{\frac{5}{2}}=2\cdot3^{2}\cdot3^{\frac{1}{2}}=2\cdot9\cdot\sqrt{3}=18\sqrt{3}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="f(1)=2\cdot3=6" /> and <InlineMath math="f(3)=2\cdot27=54" />, which match the two flipped points. The value <InlineMath math="18\sqrt{3}\approx31.2" /> also makes sense, since it lies between <InlineMath math="f(2)=18" /> and <InlineMath math="f(3)=54" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

An exponential function grows by **multiplying by the same factor**, not by adding the same amount, and most wrong options forget that.

- <InlineMath math="162" /> comes from reading <InlineMath math="b^{2}=9" /> as <InlineMath math="b=9" />. Then <InlineMath math="a=\tfrac{2}{3}" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2}{3}\cdot9^{\frac{5}{2}}=\tfrac{2}{3}\cdot243=162" />.
- <InlineMath math="54\sqrt{3}" /> comes from taking <InlineMath math="a" /> to be <InlineMath math="f(1)=6" />. In fact <InlineMath math="a=f(0)" />.
- <InlineMath math="42" /> comes from straight-line interpolation between <InlineMath math="(1,6)" /> and <InlineMath math="(3,54)" />: <InlineMath math="6+\tfrac{3}{2}\cdot24=42" />. An exponential graph isn't a straight line.
- <InlineMath math="36" /> comes from getting <InlineMath math="f(2)=18" /> right but treating the halfway point between <InlineMath math="x=2" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" /> as linear. Half a step in the exponent multiplies by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{3}" />, it doesn't add half the gap.

So <InlineMath math="f\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right)=18\sqrt{3}" />.

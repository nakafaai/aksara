# What each view demands

The question asks for the **fewest** cubes, so you're looking for an arrangement that matches all three views with as few cubes as possible. Each view sets its own condition:

- Top view: the seven squares marked ■ must hold at least one cube, and the two squares marked □ are empty.
- Front view: the left column must have a stack of height <InlineMath math="3" />, the middle column one of height <InlineMath math="2" />, and the right column one of height <InlineMath math="3" />.
- Side view: the front row must have a stack of height <InlineMath math="3" />, the middle row one of height <InlineMath math="3" />, and the back row one of height <InlineMath math="2" />.

Also, no stack can be taller than its column view or its row view allows.

# One stack for two conditions

The trick is that **one tall stack can meet a column condition and a row condition at the same time**. A stack of <InlineMath math="3" /> on the front-right square meets the right column and the front row. A stack of <InlineMath math="3" /> on the middle-left square meets the left column and the middle row. A stack of <InlineMath math="2" /> on the back-middle square meets the middle column and the back row. Every other ■ square only needs one cube.

You can't do it with fewer. Those three tall stacks have to be there, and no other square can cover two such conditions at once, because the front-middle and back-left squares are empty. The other four ■ squares each need at least one cube.

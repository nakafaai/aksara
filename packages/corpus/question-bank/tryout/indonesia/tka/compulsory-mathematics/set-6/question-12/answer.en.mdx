In a cyclic quadrilateral, **opposite angles add up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />**. The opposite pairs are <InlineMath math="\angle A" /> with <InlineMath math="\angle C" />, and <InlineMath math="\angle B" /> with <InlineMath math="\angle D" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\angle A+\angle C=2\angle C+\angle C&=180^\circ\ \Rightarrow\ \angle C=60^\circ,\ \angle A=120^\circ \\
\angle D&=180^\circ-85^\circ=95^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

So the statement that must be true is <InlineMath math="\angle D=95^\circ" />.

- <InlineMath math="\angle A=60^\circ" /> swaps <InlineMath math="\angle A" /> and <InlineMath math="\angle C" />. In fact <InlineMath math="\angle A=120^\circ" />.
- If <InlineMath math="AC" /> were a diameter, <InlineMath math="\angle B" />, the inscribed angle facing <InlineMath math="AC" />, would have to be <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. Since <InlineMath math="\angle B=85^\circ" />, <InlineMath math="AC" /> is definitely not a diameter.
- <InlineMath math="\angle B=85^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="\angle D=95^\circ" />, so they aren't equal.
- <InlineMath math="\angle A+\angle B=120^\circ+85^\circ=205^\circ" />. Angles <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> are neighbours, not opposite.

Eight equal small cubes are stacked <InlineMath math="2\times2\times2" />, so **each small cube's edge is half the original edge**. Let the original edge be <InlineMath math="s" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{original}}&=6s^2 \\
L_{\text{8 small cubes}}&=8\cdot6\left(\frac s2\right)^2=8\cdot\frac{6s^2}{4}=12s^2 \\
\frac{12s^2}{6s^2}&=2
\end{aligned}" />

You can also see why from the cuts. Each of the three cutting planes opens up two new faces of area <InlineMath math="s^2" />, so the area grows by <InlineMath math="3\cdot2s^2=6s^2" />, as much as the original area.

So the total surface area of the eight small cubes is <InlineMath math="2" /> times that of the original cube.

- <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> turns the ratio upside down.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> assumes the surface area stays the same because the volume does.
- <InlineMath math="1\frac12" /> assumes each cut opens only one new face.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> matches the ratio to the number of cubes, but each small cube has only a quarter of the original surface area.

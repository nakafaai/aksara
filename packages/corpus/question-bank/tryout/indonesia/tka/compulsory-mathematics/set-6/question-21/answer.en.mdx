This question runs in three steps. The months are chosen using **City P**'s data, but the mean you're asked for uses **City Q**'s data in those months. The words "lebih dari" (more than) matter too, because one month sits exactly on the mean.

# The mean of City P

The cut-off for choosing months is City P's six-month mean. Add City P's six values from the table, then **divide by six**, the number of months recorded:

<BlockMath math="\bar x_P=\frac{330+270+240+200+120+40}{6}=\frac{1{,}200}{6}=200\text{ mm}" />

# Choosing the months

Compare City P's value in each month with <InlineMath math="200\text{ mm}" />. **A month at exactly <InlineMath math="200\text{ mm}" /> is not more than the mean**, so it isn't chosen.

| Month | City P | More than <InlineMath math="200" />? | City Q |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| January | <InlineMath math="330" /> | yes | <InlineMath math="250" /> |
| February | <InlineMath math="270" /> | yes | <InlineMath math="230" /> |
| March | <InlineMath math="240" /> | yes | <InlineMath math="180" /> |
| April | <InlineMath math="200" /> | no, equal to the mean | <InlineMath math="320" /> |
| May | <InlineMath math="120" /> | no | <InlineMath math="300" /> |
| June | <InlineMath math="40" /> | no | <InlineMath math="280" /> |

The chosen months are January, February, and March.

# The mean of City Q in those months

Now take City Q's values in those three months:

<BlockMath math="\frac{250+230+180}{3}=\frac{660}{3}=220\text{ mm}" />

Sanity check: in those three months City Q really is drier than City P, so it makes sense that the result is **smaller than City Q's six-month mean** of <InlineMath math="260\text{ mm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="245\text{ mm}" /> also includes April, even though City P's April value is exactly the mean: <InlineMath math="\frac{660+320}{4}=245" />.
- <InlineMath math="260\text{ mm}" /> is City Q's mean over all six months. **The step of choosing months was skipped**.
- <InlineMath math="280\text{ mm}" /> is City P's mean in the chosen months, <InlineMath math="\frac{330+270+240}{3}" />. The cities got swapped.
- <InlineMath math="300\text{ mm}" /> comes from choosing the months where City Q is above its own mean, which are April, May, and June. The condition in the question uses City P.

So City Q's mean rainfall in those months is <InlineMath math="220\text{ mm}" />.

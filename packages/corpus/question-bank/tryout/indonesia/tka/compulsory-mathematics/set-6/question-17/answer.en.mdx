The fabric is made of **two slanted rectangles** and **two triangles** at the front and the back. The width of each slanted rectangle is the triangle's slanted side, which comes from Pythagoras with half the base, <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" />, and the height, <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
s&=\sqrt{1.5^2+2^2}=\sqrt{6.25}=2.5\text{ m} \\
L_{\text{slanted sides}}&=2\cdot(2.5\cdot4)=20\text{ m}^2 \\
L_{\text{triangles}}&=2\cdot\left(\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot2\right)=6\text{ m}^2 \\
L&=20+6=26\text{ m}^2
\end{aligned}" />

So the tent needs <InlineMath math="26\text{ m}^2" /> of fabric.

- <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}^2" /> counts only one slanted side and the two triangles.
- <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}^2" /> counts only the two slanted sides, without the front and the back.
- <InlineMath math="22\text{ m}^2" /> uses the triangle's <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> height as the width of the slanted sides.
- <InlineMath math="23\text{ m}^2" /> counts only one triangle.

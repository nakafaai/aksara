The functions <InlineMath math="f" /> and <InlineMath math="g" /> share the same formula, but **the domain of <InlineMath math="f" /> is cut down to <InlineMath math="x\le1" />**. That cut decides the sign in the inverse formula, the domain of the inverse, and when <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> undoes <InlineMath math="g" />.

# The range of f

Complete the square: <InlineMath math="x^2-4x+7=(x-2)^2+3" />. The vertex of the parabola is at <InlineMath math="x=2" />, so on <InlineMath math="x\le1" /> the graph keeps going down from left to right. A function that keeps going down never gives the same value twice, so <InlineMath math="f" /> has an inverse.

The smallest value of <InlineMath math="f" /> is at the right end of its domain, <InlineMath math="f(1)=1-4+7=4" />. Further left, the values get bigger. So the range of <InlineMath math="f" /> is <InlineMath math="y\ge4" />, and **the domain of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> is <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\ge4\}" />**.

# The inverse formula

Write <InlineMath math="y=f(x)" /> and solve for <InlineMath math="x" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y&=(x-2)^2+3 \\
(x-2)^2&=y-3 \\
x-2&=\pm\sqrt{y-3}
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="x\le1" />, <InlineMath math="x-2" /> is always negative, so **you take the minus sign**.

<BlockMath math="f^{-1}(x)=2-\sqrt{x-3},\qquad x\ge4" />

# Judging each statement

1. **False**. The inverse does exist, but the sign is wrong. The formula <InlineMath math="2+\sqrt{x-3}" /> always gives at least <InlineMath math="2" />, for example <InlineMath math="4" /> at <InlineMath math="x=7" />, while every value of the inverse has to lie in the domain of <InlineMath math="f" />, <InlineMath math="x\le1" />.
2. **False**. The formula <InlineMath math="2-\sqrt{x-3}" /> is defined for <InlineMath math="x\ge3" />, but the domain of the inverse is the range of <InlineMath math="f" />, <InlineMath math="x\ge4" />. Take <InlineMath math="3.25" />: the equation <InlineMath math="(x-2)^2=0.25" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=1.5" /> or <InlineMath math="x=2.5" />, and both are outside <InlineMath math="x\le1" />.
3. **True**. <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(12)=2-\sqrt{9}=-1" />. Check: <InlineMath math="-1\le1" /> and <InlineMath math="f(-1)=1+4+7=12" />.
4. **False**. It's true for <InlineMath math="a\le1" />, but not for every <InlineMath math="a\le2" />. Take <InlineMath math="a=1.5" />: <InlineMath math="g(1.5)=3.25" />, and <InlineMath math="3.25" /> isn't in the domain of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" />, so <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(g(1.5))" /> isn't even defined.
5. **True**. For <InlineMath math="a\ge3" />, <InlineMath math="g(a)=(a-2)^2+3\ge4" />, so it lies in the domain of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f^{-1}(g(a))&=2-\sqrt{(a-2)^2} \\
&=2-|a-2| \\
&=2-(a-2)=4-a
\end{aligned}" />

The last line uses <InlineMath math="|a-2|=a-2" /> because <InlineMath math="a-2>0" />. Example: <InlineMath math="g(5)=12" /> and <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(12)=-1=4-5" />. So **<InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> only undoes <InlineMath math="g" /> on the domain of <InlineMath math="f" /> itself**.

So the true statements are statements 3 and 5.

Calibration applies **the same linear rule to every reading**. When you add all 40 calibrated readings, the factor 1.5 stays outside the raw total, while the subtraction of 8 occurs 40 times. Dividing by 40 gives this relationship between the means.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\bar y&=\frac{1}{40}\sum_{i=1}^{40}(1.5x_i-8) \\
&=1.5\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{40}x_i}{40}-\frac{40\cdot8}{40} \\
&=1.5\bar x-8
\end{aligned}" />

Insert the calibrated mean of 70 to find the raw mean.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
70&=1.5\bar x-8 \\
1.5\bar x&=78 \\
\bar x&=\frac{78}{1.5}=52
\end{aligned}" />

The original raw total is <InlineMath math="40(52)=2{,}080" />. The correction reduces it by <InlineMath math="64-56=8" /> while leaving the count unchanged.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S'&=2{,}080-8=2{,}072 \\
\bar x'&=\frac{2{,}072}{40}=51.8 \\
\bar y'&=1.5(51.8)-8=69.7
\end{aligned}" />

The corrected calibrated mean is <InlineMath math="69.7" />.

| Record | Raw reading | Calibrated reading |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Original | 64 | 88 |
| Corrected | 56 | 76 |

You can check the result by correcting the calibrated total directly. One reading falls from 88 to 76, so **the calibrated total decreases by 12**, while the number of readings stays at 40.

<BlockMath math="70-\frac{12}{40}=70-0.3=69.7" />

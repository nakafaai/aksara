The question asks for the concrete volume of the whole staircase. The staircase is solid, so every step and the landing is a block standing on the garage floor. That means **the volume of the staircase is the sum of the volumes of all those blocks**. For that, you first need the height of one riser.

# The volume of all the blocks

Each step is a block <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> long, <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}" /> wide, and as tall as its own height. Add up the heights of the nine steps first, then add the landing block, which is <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}" /> long:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{sum of step heights}&=(20+40+60+80+100)+(140+160+180+200) \\
&=300+680=980\text{ cm} \\
V_{\text{steps}}&=30\cdot100\cdot980=2{,}940{,}000\text{ cm}^3 \\
V_{\text{landing}}&=100\cdot100\cdot120=1{,}200{,}000\text{ cm}^3 \\
V&=2{,}940{,}000+1{,}200{,}000=4{,}140{,}000\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}^3=1{,}000{,}000\text{ cm}^3" />, the volume is <InlineMath math="4.14\text{ m}^3" />. **Check it stage by stage**: stage I holds <InlineMath math="0.9+1.2=2.1\text{ m}^3" /> and stage II holds <InlineMath math="2.04\text{ m}^3" />, which again adds up to <InlineMath math="4.14\text{ m}^3" />.

So the concrete volume of the whole staircase, without the extra mix, is <InlineMath math="4.14\text{ m}^3" />.

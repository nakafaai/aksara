The question asks for the mass of the real statue. There are three steps: find the model's volume from the rise in the water, scale that volume up correctly, then multiply by the density of the stone. **You can't just scale up the model's mass**, because the model is made of resin, not stone.

# The volume of the model

When the model goes fully under water, the water rises because the model takes up space. So **the volume of the risen water equals the volume of the model**. The risen water is a cylinder with radius <InlineMath math="7\text{ cm}" /> and height <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ cm}" />:

<BlockMath math="V_{\text{model}}=\pi r^2h=\frac{22}{7}\cdot7^2\cdot2.5=154\cdot2.5=385\text{ cm}^3" />

# The scale factor and the real volume

Put both heights in the same unit first: <InlineMath math="3.6\text{ m}=360\text{ cm}" />. The length scale factor is

<BlockMath math="k=\frac{360}{18}=20" />

For two similar solids, lengths are multiplied by <InlineMath math="k" />, areas by <InlineMath math="k^2" />, and **volumes by <InlineMath math="k^3" />**:

<BlockMath math="V_{\text{real}}=385\cdot20^3=385\cdot8{,}000=3{,}080{,}000\text{ cm}^3" />

# The mass of the real statue

Mass is density times volume:

<BlockMath math="m=2.5\cdot3{,}080{,}000=7{,}700{,}000\text{ g}=7{,}700\text{ kg}" />

Here's why the model's mass doesn't help. The resin's density is <InlineMath math="\frac{462}{385}=1.2\text{ g/cm}^3" />, much less than the stone's <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ g/cm}^3" />. With different materials, **you have to go through the volume**.

So the mass of the real statue is <InlineMath math="7{,}700\text{ kg}" />.

- <InlineMath math="19.25\text{ kg}" /> scales the volume by <InlineMath math="k=20" /> only: <InlineMath math="385\cdot20\cdot2.5=19{,}250\text{ g}" />. **Volume grows with the cube of the scale**, not in step with length.
- <InlineMath math="385\text{ kg}" /> uses <InlineMath math="k^2=400" />, which is the area scale, not the volume scale.
- <InlineMath math="3{,}080\text{ kg}" /> forgets to multiply by the stone's density. It treats <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}^3" /> as weighing <InlineMath math="1\text{ g}" />, like water.

Call the two legs <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" />, and the hypotenuse <InlineMath math="c" />. You need three facts about these three numbers, so you'll use **two properties of the incircle** plus the area.

# The perimeter from the area

Join the centre of the incircle to the three corners. The triangle splits into three small triangles with bases <InlineMath math="p" />, <InlineMath math="q" />, <InlineMath math="c" />, and each has height equal to the radius <InlineMath math="r=5" />. Add their areas to get the whole area <InlineMath math="L" />, and write <InlineMath math="s" /> for half the perimeter.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L&=\tfrac12 r p+\tfrac12 r q+\tfrac12 r c=r\cdot s \\
210&=5s\ \Rightarrow\ s=42 \\
p+q+c&=84
\end{aligned}" />

So **the area equals the radius times half the perimeter**, and the perimeter is <InlineMath math="84\text{ cm}" />.

# The hypotenuse from the radius

At the right angle, the two radii to the touching points form a square with side <InlineMath math="r" />. So from the right-angle corner, the tangent length to the circle is <InlineMath math="r" />. From the other two corners, the tangent lengths are <InlineMath math="p-r" /> and <InlineMath math="q-r" />, and **those two pieces make up the hypotenuse exactly**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
c&=(p-r)+(q-r)\ \Rightarrow\ p+q-c=2r=10 \\
c&=\frac{84-10}{2}=37,\qquad p+q=47
\end{aligned}" />

# The two legs

From the area, <InlineMath math="\tfrac12pq=210" />, so <InlineMath math="pq=420" />. Two numbers with sum <InlineMath math="47" /> and product <InlineMath math="420" /> are the roots of this quadratic equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t^2-47t+420&=0 \\
t&=\frac{47\pm\sqrt{2209-1680}}{2}=\frac{47\pm23}{2} \\
t&=35\ \text{ or }\ t=12
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="12^2+35^2=144+1225=1369=37^2" />, and <InlineMath math="\frac{12+35-37}{2}=5" />. **Every given fact holds**, so the longer leg is <InlineMath math="35\text{ cm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" /> is the shorter leg, the other root of the quadratic.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> is the piece of the hypotenuse from the touching point to the bottom right corner. **The leg still needs <InlineMath math="r=5" /> added**.
- <InlineMath math="37\text{ cm}" /> is the hypotenuse, not a leg.
- <InlineMath math="84\text{ cm}" /> comes from writing the area as <InlineMath math="\tfrac12 r s" />. Then <InlineMath math="s=84" />, <InlineMath math="c=79" />, <InlineMath math="p+q=89" />, and the roots are <InlineMath math="84" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />. But the half is already inside <InlineMath math="s" />.

So the longer leg is <InlineMath math="35\text{ cm}" /> long.

Then the translation adds <InlineMath math="a" /> to the x-coordinate and <InlineMath math="b" /> to the y-coordinate. So **the dilation comes first**, then the translation:

<BlockMath math="T(x,y)=(kx+a,\ ky+b)" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="(5,-5)" /> comes from using <InlineMath math="k=2" /> straight away. **The quadrant III condition rules out <InlineMath math="k=2" />**, because with <InlineMath math="k=2" /> you get <InlineMath math="B'=(9,9)" /> in quadrant I.
- <InlineMath math="(-14,-8)" /> comes from translating <InlineMath math="C" /> first and dilating afterwards: <InlineMath math="-2\,(2+5,\ -1+5)=(-14,-8)" />. The question says the dilation comes first.
- <InlineMath math="(-4,2)" /> is only the dilation <InlineMath math="-2\cdot(2,-1)" />. The translation <InlineMath math="(5,5)" /> was never added.
- <InlineMath math="(-9,-3)" /> uses the translation vector with the wrong sign. From <InlineMath math="A'=kA+(a,b)" />, the translation is <InlineMath math="A'-kA=(5,5)" />, not <InlineMath math="kA-A'=(-5,-5)" />.

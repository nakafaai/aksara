The function <InlineMath math="f(x)=2+\frac7{x-1}" /> has asymptotes <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="y=2" />, intersecting at <InlineMath math="(1,2)" />. For <InlineMath math="x>1" />, the fraction is positive, so <InlineMath math="f(x)>2" />.

On each interval <InlineMath math="(-\infty,1)" /> and <InlineMath math="(1,\infty)" />, the reciprocal <InlineMath math="\frac1{x-1}" /> decreases as <InlineMath math="x" /> increases. Multiplication by positive <InlineMath math="7" /> and addition of <InlineMath math="2" /> preserve this behavior. To find fixed points, solve <InlineMath math="f(x)=x" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x&=2+\frac7{x-1} \\
x(x-1)&=2(x-1)+7 \\
x^2-3x-5&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The discriminant <InlineMath math="9+20=29>0" /> gives two real roots. Since <InlineMath math="x=1" /> does not satisfy this quadratic, both roots are in the domain. For the inverse, solve for the original variable.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y-2&=\frac7{x-1} \\
x&=1+\frac7{y-2} \\
f^{-1}(y)&=\frac{y+5}{y-2}
\end{aligned}" />

The function and its inverse have different domains, excluding <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="2" /> respectively. Thus <InlineMath math="f^{-1}=f" /> is false. The other four statements are true.

First statement: the trapezium <InlineMath math="DBCE" /> is the big triangle minus the small one, so it has <InlineMath math="25-4=21" /> parts. The ratio is <InlineMath math="4:21" />, not <InlineMath math="4:9" />. You get <InlineMath math="4:9" /> if you **square <InlineMath math="AD:DB" />**. This statement is **False**.

Second statement: the perimeter of <InlineMath math="ADE" /> is always <InlineMath math="\tfrac25" /> of the perimeter of <InlineMath math="ABC" />. But the trapezium's perimeter contains the fixed <InlineMath math="BC=20" /> and <InlineMath math="DE=8" />, plus <InlineMath math="\tfrac35" /> of <InlineMath math="AB+AC" />, which changes. For example, with <InlineMath math="AB=AC=20" /> the ratio is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{6}{13}" />, but with <InlineMath math="AB=15" /> and <InlineMath math="AC=13" /> it's <InlineMath math="\tfrac37" />. Third statement: let the parallel segment have length <InlineMath math="x" />. It forms a triangle similar to <InlineMath math="ABC" />, and the areas of similar triangles go with the square of the base. So the part of the trapezium above the segment is proportional to <InlineMath math="x^2-8^2" />, and the part below it to <InlineMath math="20^2-x^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-64&=400-x^2 \\
2x^2&=464 \\
x&=\sqrt{232}=2\sqrt{58}
\end{aligned}" />

Reflection in <InlineMath math="y=x" /> swaps the coordinates. Then add the translation vector.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
M(x,y)&=(y,x)+(2,-1) \\
&=(y+2,x-1) \\
M(3,-2)&=(-2+2,3-1)=(0,2)
\end{aligned}" />

Both reflection and translation preserve distances. Reflection **reverses orientation** and translation preserves it, so their composition reverses orientation. Applying the transformation twice gives:

Write the coordinates after the second application as <InlineMath math="x_2" /> and <InlineMath math="y_2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
M^2(x,y)=M(y+2,x-1) \\
x_2=(x-1)+2=x+1 \\
y_2=(y+2)-1=y+1 \\
M^2(x,y)=(x+1,y+1)
\end{gathered}" />

A fixed point must satisfy <InlineMath math="x=y+2" /> and <InlineMath math="y=x-1" />. Substitution gives the impossible equation <InlineMath math="x=x+1" />. There are no fixed points. The four statements about the image, distances, orientation, and <InlineMath math="M^2" /> are true.

Orientation means the order around the vertices. In the diagram, A to B to C is counterclockwise, while A′ to B′ to C′ is clockwise. The two triangles remain congruent.

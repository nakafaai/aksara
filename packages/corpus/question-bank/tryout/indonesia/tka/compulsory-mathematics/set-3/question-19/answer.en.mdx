Base circumference is proportional to radius, base area to radius squared, and volume to radius squared times height.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
k_U&=3 \\
k_A&=3^2=9 \\
k_V&=3^2\cdot\frac12=\frac92
\end{aligned}" />

Slant height satisfies <InlineMath math="s^2=r^2+h^2" />. To test the claimed fixed factor, take a cone with <InlineMath math="r=h=1" />. After scaling, its radius is <InlineMath math="3" /> and height <InlineMath math="\frac12" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
s^2&=2 \\
(s')^2&=9+\frac14=\frac{37}{4} \\
\left(\frac{s'}{s}\right)^2&=\frac{37}{8}\ne\left(\frac32\right)^2
\end{aligned}" />

The slant-height factor is not always <InlineMath math="\frac32" />. Since lateral area is <InlineMath math="\pi rs" />, its scale factor is <InlineMath math="3\frac{s'}{s}" />. It equals <InlineMath math="\frac92" /> only when the slant-height factor is <InlineMath math="\frac32" />, which the counterexample disproves.

The circumference, base-area, and volume statements are true. The final two statements are false.

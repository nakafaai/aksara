In two similar solids every length has the same ratio, here <InlineMath math="12:8=3:2" />. **Volumes are in the ratio of the cube of the length ratio**, because a cylinder's volume contains two radii and one height.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{V_{\text{large}}}{V_{\text{small}}}&=\left(\frac32\right)^3=\frac{27}{8} \\
V_{\text{large}}&=320\cdot\frac{27}{8}=40\cdot27=1{,}080\text{ mL}
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, the radius and the height are both multiplied by <InlineMath math="\frac32" />, so <InlineMath math="\pi r^2h" /> is multiplied by <InlineMath math="\frac32\cdot\frac32\cdot\frac32=\frac{27}{8}" />.

So the large glass holds <InlineMath math="1{,}080\text{ mL}" />.

# The new sample space

Your friend's remark throws out every result that has no <InlineMath math="5" />. So **you only count the results that are still possible**. Write each result as a pair (first die, second die):

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{first die }5&:\ (5,1),(5,2),(5,3),(5,4),(5,5),(5,6) \\
\text{only second die }5&:\ (1,5),(2,5),(3,5),(4,5),(6,5)
\end{aligned}" />

The result <InlineMath math="(5,5)" /> is counted only once, so **there are <InlineMath math="11" /> equally likely results**, not <InlineMath math="12" />.

# The results you want

One die showing <InlineMath math="2" /> and the other showing <InlineMath math="5" /> can happen in **two orders**: <InlineMath math="(2,5)" /> and <InlineMath math="(5,2)" />.

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{2}{11}" />

So the probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{11}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

Most traps come from **treating the other die as untouched by your friend's remark**.

- <InlineMath math="\frac15" /> assumes the other die can't be <InlineMath math="5" /> and picks from the <InlineMath math="5" /> remaining faces. But <InlineMath math="(5,5)" /> can happen.
- <InlineMath math="\frac16" /> assumes one specific die is surely <InlineMath math="5" /> and the other is a normal roll. Your friend didn't say which die.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{11}" /> counts only the order <InlineMath math="(2,5)" /> and forgets <InlineMath math="(5,2)" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{18}" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{36}" />, the probability without your friend's remark.

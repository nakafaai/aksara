To compare powers with different bases, rewrite them with **the same exponent**. Then the new base alone decides.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
p&=2^{48}=\left(2^{3}\right)^{16}=8^{16}, & q&=3^{32}=\left(3^{2}\right)^{16}=9^{16} \\
p&=2^{48}=\left(2^{12}\right)^{4}=4{,}096^{4}, & r&=5^{20}=\left(5^{5}\right)^{4}=3{,}125^{4}
\end{aligned}" />

This gives <InlineMath math="r<p<q" />.

- Since <InlineMath math="9^{16}>8^{16}" />, <InlineMath math="q>p" />. The statement is true.
- Since <InlineMath math="4{,}096^{4}>3{,}125^{4}" />, <InlineMath math="p>r" />. The statement is true.
- From <InlineMath math="r<p<q" /> it follows that <InlineMath math="r<q" />. The statement is false.
- Pair each factor <InlineMath math="2" /> with a factor <InlineMath math="5" /> to make <InlineMath math="10" />: <InlineMath math="p\cdot r=2^{28}\cdot10^{20}" />. With <InlineMath math="2^{28}=2^{20}\cdot2^{8}=1{,}048{,}576\cdot256=268{,}435{,}456" />, the number has <InlineMath math="9+20=29" /> digits. The statement is true.
- <InlineMath math="q=9^{16}=81^{8}>80^{8}=8^{8}\cdot10^{8}=16{,}777{,}216\cdot10^{8}" />, which is greater than <InlineMath math="10^{15}" />. The statement is false.

So the true statements are <InlineMath math="q>p" />, <InlineMath math="p>r" /> and the statement about the <InlineMath math="29" /> digits.

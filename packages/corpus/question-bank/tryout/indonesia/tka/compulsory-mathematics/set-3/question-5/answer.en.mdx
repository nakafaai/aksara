The figure only shows two lines and a shaded region. So the first step is **building the system of inequalities from the figure**. After that, you check every statement against that system, not by eye.

# The system from the figure

A line that cuts the axes at <InlineMath math="(a,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,b)" /> has the equation <InlineMath math="\frac{x}{a}+\frac{y}{b}=1" />. Multiply through by the denominators to get whole numbers.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{6}+\frac{y}{9}=1\ &\Rightarrow\ 3x+2y=18 \\
\frac{x}{12}+\frac{y}{4}=1\ &\Rightarrow\ x+3y=12
\end{aligned}" />

The point <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> is in the shaded region, and <InlineMath math="0\le18" /> and <InlineMath math="0\le12" />. So the region is on the "less than or equal to" side of both lines.

<BlockMath math="3x+2y\le18,\qquad x+3y\le12,\qquad x\ge0,\qquad y\ge0" />

You get the corner where the two lines cross by substituting <InlineMath math="x=12-3y" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3(12-3y)+2y&=18 \\
36-7y&=18\ \Rightarrow\ y=\tfrac{18}{7},\quad x=12-\tfrac{54}{7}=\tfrac{30}{7}
\end{aligned}" />

The crossing point is <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac{30}{7},\tfrac{18}{7}\right)\approx(4.29,\ 2.57)" />. **To the left of the crossing point, the line <InlineMath math="x+3y=12" /> is the lower one**, and to the right of it, the line <InlineMath math="3x+2y=18" /> is lower. The top edge of the region is always the lower line.

# Judging each statement

1. **True**. For <InlineMath math="(4,2)" />: <InlineMath math="3\cdot4+2\cdot2=16\le18" /> and <InlineMath math="4+3\cdot2=10\le12" />, so it's inside. For <InlineMath math="(4,3)" />: <InlineMath math="3\cdot4+2\cdot3=18\le18" />, but <InlineMath math="4+3\cdot3=13>12" />. The point <InlineMath math="(4,3)" /> sits exactly on the first line but above the second one, so it's outside.
2. **False**. For each <InlineMath math="x" />, the biggest <InlineMath math="y" /> is set by the lower line, the smaller of <InlineMath math="\frac{18-3x}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac{12-x}{3}" />.

| <InlineMath math="x" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{18-3x}{2}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{12-x}{3}" /> | Allowed whole <InlineMath math="y" /> | Points |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="4.5" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="0,1,2,3" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="2\tfrac{2}{3}" /> | <InlineMath math="0,1,2" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="1.5" /> | <InlineMath math="2\tfrac{1}{3}" /> | <InlineMath math="0,1" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |

The total is <InlineMath math="4+3+2=9" />, not <InlineMath math="10" />. You get <InlineMath math="10" /> if you use the line <InlineMath math="x+3y=12" /> for the whole strip, but **at <InlineMath math="x=5" /> the limit has already switched to the line <InlineMath math="3x+2y=18" />**.

3. **True**. At the crossing point the value is <InlineMath math="2\cdot\tfrac{30}{7}+3\cdot\tfrac{18}{7}=\tfrac{114}{7}\approx16.3" />, but that point isn't a whole-number point. So whole-number points give at most <InlineMath math="16" />. Check whether <InlineMath math="16" /> can be reached: <InlineMath math="2x+3y=16" /> with whole <InlineMath math="x,y\ge0" /> only has <InlineMath math="(8,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(5,2)" /> and <InlineMath math="(2,4)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(8,0)&:\ 3\cdot8+2\cdot0=24>18 \\
(5,2)&:\ 3\cdot5+2\cdot2=19>18 \\
(2,4)&:\ 2+3\cdot4=14>12
\end{aligned}" />

All three are outside the region. The value <InlineMath math="15" /> is reached at <InlineMath math="(3,3)" />, because <InlineMath math="9+6=15\le18" /> and <InlineMath math="3+9=12\le12" />. Rounding the crossing point to <InlineMath math="(4,3)" /> leaves the region, and <InlineMath math="(4,2)" /> only gives <InlineMath math="14" />.

So statement 1 is true, statement 2 is false, and statement 3 is true.

# Counting all arrangements

At a round table, all that matters is who sits next to whom. So **fix one person as the reference point** and arrange the rest around them. For <InlineMath math="7" /> people there are <InlineMath math="(7-1)!=720" /> arrangements.

# Ana and Budi side by side

To make sure Ana and Budi sit together, **tie them into one block**. Now there are <InlineMath math="6" /> units at the round table, and inside the block Ana and Budi can still swap seats.

<BlockMath math="(6-1)!\cdot2=120\cdot2=240" />

# Removing Citra and Dimas side by side

Some of those <InlineMath math="240" /> arrangements also put Citra and Dimas together. **Count the ones you don't want and subtract them**. Tie Citra and Dimas into a second block, so there are <InlineMath math="5" /> units, and each block can swap its order.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Citra and Dimas also together}&=(5-1)!\cdot2\cdot2=96 \\
\text{wanted arrangements}&=240-96=144
\end{aligned}" />

# The probability

**Divide by the number of all arrangements**:

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{144}{720}=\frac15" />

So the probability is <InlineMath math="\frac15" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option **skips one part of the count**.

- <InlineMath math="\frac29" /> multiplies <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> by <InlineMath math="\frac23" />, as if the two conditions didn't affect each other. But once Ana and Budi sit side by side, the chance that Citra and Dimas sit together changes to <InlineMath math="\frac{96}{240}=\frac25" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{15}" /> forgets that Citra and Dimas can swap seats inside their block, so it removes only <InlineMath math="48" /> arrangements.
- <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> uses only the Ana and Budi condition, which is <InlineMath math="\frac{240}{720}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac35" /> divides by <InlineMath math="240" /> instead of <InlineMath math="720" />. That's the chance Citra and Dimas aren't together when Ana and Budi are already known to be together.

# The formula of the function

The graph of a quadratic function is symmetric about its axis. Since <InlineMath math="f(-2)=f(8)" />, the axis of symmetry is exactly halfway between <InlineMath math="-2" /> and <InlineMath math="8" />, at <InlineMath math="x=\frac{-2+8}{2}=3" />. The range <InlineMath math="y\ge-6" /> means the graph opens upward and **its minimum value <InlineMath math="-6" /> is at the vertex**. So <InlineMath math="f(x)=a(x-3)^2-6" /> with <InlineMath math="a>0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f(1)&=a(1-3)^2-6=-4 \\
4a&=2\ \Rightarrow\ a=\tfrac12 \\
f(x)&=\tfrac12(x-3)^2-6
\end{aligned}" />

# The conditions as a distance from the axis

Let <InlineMath math="d=|x-3|" />, the distance from <InlineMath math="x" /> to the axis of symmetry. Since <InlineMath math="x" /> is an integer, so is <InlineMath math="d" />. Turn both inequalities into conditions on <InlineMath math="d" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
-4<\tfrac12d^2-6\ &\Rightarrow\ d^2>4\ \Rightarrow\ d>2 \\
\tfrac12d^2-6<12\ &\Rightarrow\ d^2<36\ \Rightarrow\ d<6
\end{aligned}" />

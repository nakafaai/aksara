The question counts accounts, not subscriptions. So first find how many subscriptions of each plan there are with a **system of equations**, then convert each subscription into its accounts. Let <InlineMath math="d" /> be the number of Duo subscriptions and <InlineMath math="k" /> the number of Keluarga subscriptions. Amounts in thousands of rupiah:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
d+k&=1{,}000 \\
90d+108k&=98{,}640 \\
98{,}640-90\cdot1{,}000=18k\ &\Rightarrow\ k=480,\quad d=520 \\
\text{accounts}&=2\cdot520+6\cdot480=1{,}040+2{,}880=3{,}920
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, <InlineMath math="90\cdot520+108\cdot480=46{,}800+51{,}840=98{,}640" />.

So there are <InlineMath math="3{,}920" /> accounts.

- <InlineMath math="2{,}880" /> counts only the accounts of the Keluarga subscriptions.
- <InlineMath math="4{,}000" /> skips the system and assumes an average of <InlineMath math="4" /> accounts per subscription, the mean of <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="6" />.
- <InlineMath math="4{,}080" /> swaps the two solutions, taking <InlineMath math="520" /> Keluarga and <InlineMath math="480" /> Duo subscriptions.
- <InlineMath math="6{,}000" /> treats all <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> subscriptions as Keluarga plans.

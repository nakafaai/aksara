The volume of wax does not change, so **the number of small candles is the volume of six large candles divided by the volume of one small candle**. A small candle is a cone, <InlineMath math="V=\frac13\pi r^2h" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{large}}&=\frac{22}{7}\cdot12.25\cdot12=462\text{ cm}^3 \\
V_{\text{small}}&=\frac13\cdot\frac{22}{7}\cdot12.25\cdot6=77\text{ cm}^3 \\
n&=\frac{6\cdot462}{77}=\frac{2{,}772}{77}=36
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, both candles have the same base, so one large candle of height <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" /> equals <InlineMath math="3\cdot2=6" /> cones of height <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />, and <InlineMath math="6\cdot6=36" />.

So <InlineMath math="36" /> small candles can be made.

- <InlineMath math="12" /> treats the small candle as a cylinder, without the factor <InlineMath math="\frac13" />.
- <InlineMath math="108" /> applies the factor <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> twice to the cone.
- <InlineMath math="144" /> uses the diameter <InlineMath math="7\text{ cm}" /> as the radius of the large candle but not of the small one.
- <InlineMath math="216" /> multiplies the correct result by <InlineMath math="6" /> once more.

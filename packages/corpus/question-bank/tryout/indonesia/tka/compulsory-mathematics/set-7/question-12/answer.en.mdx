# Recognising the prism from its net

The two congruent pentagons are the base and the top of the prism. The five rectangles are the side faces: their widths <InlineMath math="6" />, <InlineMath math="4" />, <InlineMath math="4" />, <InlineMath math="5" />, and <InlineMath math="5" /> match the five sides of the pentagon, and the length <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> is the height of the prism. So this is **a pentagonal prism of height <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" />**.

The pentagon is shaped like a house. Because <InlineMath math="\angle A=\angle B=90^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="BC=EA=4" />, its lower part is the rectangle <InlineMath math="ABCE" />, <InlineMath math="6\times4" />. On top sits the isosceles triangle <InlineMath math="CDE" /> with base <InlineMath math="CE=6" /> and legs <InlineMath math="5" />. Find the triangle's height with Pythagoras on half the base:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t&=\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=\sqrt{16}=4\text{ cm} \\
A_{\text{base}}&=6\cdot4+\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot4=24+12=36\text{ cm}^2
\end{aligned}" />

# The first statement

**True**. A pentagonal prism has <InlineMath math="5" /> edges on the base, <InlineMath math="5" /> on the top, and <InlineMath math="5" /> side edges, so <InlineMath math="15" /> edges. The base edges and the top edges each add up to the pentagon's perimeter, and every side edge is <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> long.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{perimeter of the pentagon}&=6+4+4+5+5=24\text{ cm} \\
\text{total edge length}&=2\cdot24+5\cdot10=98\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Be careful if you add up the perimeters of all the faces in the net. You get <InlineMath math="196\text{ cm}" />, twice the answer, because **every edge is shared by two faces**.

# The second statement

**False**. The volume of a prism is the base area times the height.

<BlockMath math="V=36\cdot10=360\text{ cm}^3" />

That's less than <InlineMath math="375\text{ cm}^3" />. The statement looks true if you use the triangle's leg <InlineMath math="5" /> as its height, because then the base area becomes <InlineMath math="24+15=39" /> and the volume <InlineMath math="390\text{ cm}^3" />. **A triangle's height must be perpendicular to its base**, and here it's <InlineMath math="4" />, not <InlineMath math="5" />.

# The third statement

**True**. The <InlineMath math="6\times10" /> face comes from edge <InlineMath math="AB" />. With that face on the floor, both pentagons stand upright with <InlineMath math="AB" /> at the bottom. The highest points lie on the edge through <InlineMath math="D" />. Their height is the rectangle's height plus the triangle's height:

<BlockMath math="BC+t=4+4=8\text{ cm}" />

So the first statement is true, the second is false, and the third is true.

The question asks for the mean salary of the design department after one person leaves. That person's salary isn't given, so you first have to find it from the April mean of all employees. One tool does all the work: **mean times number of values equals the total**. Every amount below is in millions of rupiah.

# Salary totals in March

Turn each mean in the table into a total. The two departments have different head counts, so **the overall mean isn't the mean of the two means**. What you add up are the totals.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{sewing}&=15\cdot4.8=72 \\
\text{design}&=5\cdot7.2=36 \\
\text{all}&=72+36=108
\end{aligned}" />

# Salary of the person who left

Call the salary of the design employee who left <InlineMath math="x" />. In April, **the number of employees becomes <InlineMath math="20-1+2=21" />**. You can write the April total in two ways, from the new mean and from the changes that happened.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
21\cdot5.2&=108-x+2\cdot3.9 \\
109.2&=115.8-x \\
x&=6.6
\end{aligned}" />

# Design mean in April

The design department now has <InlineMath math="5-1=4" /> people, and its total drops by the salary of the person who left.

<BlockMath math="\frac{36-6.6}{4}=\frac{29.4}{4}=7.35" />

Check the direction: the person who left earned <InlineMath math="6.6" />, **less than the design mean of <InlineMath math="7.2" />**. Removing a value below the mean pushes the mean up, so <InlineMath math="7.35" /> makes sense.

# The length of each tangent segment

The band has three parts: segment <InlineMath math="PA" />, segment <InlineMath math="PB" />, and the arc that sticks to the can. You need the tangent length and the central angle of that arc. The key fact is this: **a radius drawn to the point of tangency is always perpendicular to the tangent**.

The shortest distance from the nail to the can is measured along the line <InlineMath math="OP" />, so <InlineMath math="OP=12+12=24\text{ cm}" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="OAP" /> has its right angle at <InlineMath math="A" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
PA&=\sqrt{OP^2-OA^2}=\sqrt{24^2-12^2}=\sqrt{432}=12\sqrt3\text{ cm} \\
\sin\angle OPA&=\frac{OA}{OP}=\frac{12}{24}=\frac12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \angle OPA=30^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

By symmetry, <InlineMath math="PB=PA=12\sqrt3\text{ cm}" />, and the angle between the two tangents is <InlineMath math="\angle APB=60^\circ" />.

# The arc the band touches

Look at the quadrilateral <InlineMath math="OAPB" />. The angles at <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> are each <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />, and the angle at <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />. The angles of a quadrilateral add up to <InlineMath math="360^\circ" />, so the central angle of the minor arc is:

<BlockMath math="\angle AOB=360^\circ-90^\circ-90^\circ-60^\circ=120^\circ" />

The stretched band **wraps around the side of the can away from the nail**, which is the major arc <InlineMath math="AB" />. Its central angle is <InlineMath math="360^\circ-120^\circ=240^\circ" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{major arc }AB&=\frac{240^\circ}{360^\circ}\cdot2\pi\cdot12=16\pi\text{ cm} \\
\text{band length}&=PA+PB+\text{major arc }AB \\
&=12\sqrt3+12\sqrt3+16\pi=(24\sqrt3+16\pi)\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="(24\sqrt3+8\pi)\text{ cm}" /> uses the minor arc of <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />. The minor arc is on the side facing the nail, and the band never touches it.
- <InlineMath math="(24\sqrt3+20\pi)\text{ cm}" /> treats the <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> angle at <InlineMath math="P" /> as the central angle of the minor arc, so the major arc becomes <InlineMath math="300^\circ" />. **The central angle and the angle between the tangents are supplementary**: they add up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="(24+16\pi)\text{ cm}" /> takes the tangent length to be the shortest distance <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" />. That distance points towards the centre, not along the tangent.
- <InlineMath math="(48+16\pi)\text{ cm}" /> takes the tangent length to be <InlineMath math="OP=24\text{ cm}" />. But <InlineMath math="OP" /> is the hypotenuse of the right triangle, so the tangent must be shorter.

So the stretched rubber band is <InlineMath math="(24\sqrt3+16\pi)\text{ cm}" /> long.

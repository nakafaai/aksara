The question asks how far the top of the ladder drops. The ladder's length isn't given, but **it's the same in both positions**. So use the slide of the foot to find the length first, and only then work out the drop of the top.

# Length of the ladder

Call the ladder's length <InlineMath math="L" />. The ladder, the wall, and the floor make a right triangle with angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> at the foot. The distance from the foot to the wall is the side next to the angle, <InlineMath math="L\cos\theta" />, and the height of the top is the side opposite the angle, <InlineMath math="L\sin\theta" />.

The foot slides <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" />, so the two distances from the foot to the wall differ by <InlineMath math="1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L\cos45^\circ-L\cos60^\circ&=1 \\
L\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)&=1 \\
L&=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}-1}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}+1}{\sqrt{2}+1}=\frac{2\left(\sqrt{2}+1\right)}{2-1}=2\sqrt{2}+2
\end{aligned}" />

In the last line, **the denominator <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2}-1" /> is rationalised with its conjugate** <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2}+1" />, because <InlineMath math="\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)\left(\sqrt{2}+1\right)=2-1=1" />.

# Drop of the top

The top goes down from <InlineMath math="L\sin60^\circ" /> to <InlineMath math="L\sin45^\circ" />. **The length <InlineMath math="L" /> from the last step gets used again here**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L\sin60^\circ-L\sin45^\circ&=L\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}}{2} \\
&=\left(\sqrt{2}+1\right)\left(\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}\right) \\
&=\sqrt{6}-2+\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2} \\
&=\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}-2
\end{aligned}" />

Check with rough numbers: <InlineMath math="2.449+1.732-1.414-2\approx0.77\text{ m}" />. The height changes from about <InlineMath math="4.18\text{ m}" /> to about <InlineMath math="3.41\text{ m}" />, and the difference really is about <InlineMath math="0.77\text{ m}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\left(\sqrt{6}-\sqrt{3}+\sqrt{2}-2\right)\text{ m}" /> comes from <InlineMath math="L=2\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)" />. That happens when you multiply by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2}-1" /> where you should divide.
- <InlineMath math="\left(\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}\right)\text{ m}" /> comes from reading <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> as the starting distance of the foot from the wall, which gives <InlineMath math="L=2" />. The <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> is **how far the foot slides**, not where it started.
- <InlineMath math="\left(\sqrt{6}-\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}+2\right)\text{ m}" /> comes from swapping sine and cosine, so the foot distance is taken as <InlineMath math="L\sin\theta" /> and the height as <InlineMath math="L\cos\theta" />.
- <InlineMath math="\left(2\sqrt{2}+2\right)\text{ m}" /> is the length of the ladder. The working stopped one step before the drop.

So the top of the ladder drops <InlineMath math="\left(\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}-2\right)\text{ m}" />.

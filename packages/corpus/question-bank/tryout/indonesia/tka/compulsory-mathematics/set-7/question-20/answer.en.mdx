Each wire, the pole, and the ground form a right triangle with **the wire as the hypotenuse** and the pole as the side opposite <InlineMath math="\alpha" />. So <InlineMath math="\sin\alpha" /> is the pole's height divided by the wire's length.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sin\alpha=\frac{9}{s}=0.6\ &\Rightarrow\ s=\frac{9}{0.6}=15\text{ m} \\
3s&=45\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Check: the foot of each wire is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{15^2-9^2}=12" /> m from the pole, so the sides are <InlineMath math="9" />, <InlineMath math="12" />, and <InlineMath math="15" />.

So the three wires are <InlineMath math="45" /> m long in total.

- <InlineMath math="15" /> m is the length of one wire.
- <InlineMath math="16.2" /> m multiplies the height by <InlineMath math="\sin\alpha" />, but the height has to be divided by <InlineMath math="\sin\alpha" />.
- <InlineMath math="27" /> m treats each wire as being as long as the pole.
- <InlineMath math="36" /> m adds up the <InlineMath math="12" /> m horizontal distance for three wires, not the wire lengths.

# What you really know

The sketch is just one example. A statement that's **certainly true** has to hold for every parabola with <InlineMath math="a<0" />, <InlineMath math="-1<p<0" />, and <InlineMath math="1<q<2" />. So turn each statement into a fact about the graph, then check whether that fact always happens.

You have two tools. First, because <InlineMath math="a<0" />, the value <InlineMath math="g(x)" /> is positive between the two intercepts and negative outside them. Second, the x-coordinate of the vertex, call it <InlineMath math="h" />, is the midpoint of the two intercepts:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
h&=-\frac{b}{2a}=\frac{p+q}{2} \\
-1+1<p+q&<0+2 \\
0<h&<1
\end{aligned}" />

Also, <InlineMath math="g(0)=c" />, <InlineMath math="g(1)=a+b+c" />, and <InlineMath math="g(-1)=a-b+c" />.

# Judging each statement

1. **False**. The statement <InlineMath math="a+b>0" /> isn't certain. Notice that <InlineMath math="a+b=g(1)-g(0)" />. Its sign depends on where the vertex is: if <InlineMath math="h>\tfrac12" />, the point <InlineMath math="x=1" /> is closer to the vertex, so <InlineMath math="g(1)>g(0)" />. If <InlineMath math="h<\tfrac12" />, it's the other way round. Here are two examples that both meet the conditions:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
p=-0.4,\ q=1.6:&\quad g(x)=-x^2+1.2x+0.64,\quad a+b=0.2>0 \\
p=-0.9,\ q=1.2:&\quad g(x)=-x^2+0.3x+1.08,\quad a+b=-0.7<0
\end{aligned}" />

2. **True**. From <InlineMath math="h=-\frac{b}{2a}" /> you get <InlineMath math="b=-2a\,h" />. Since <InlineMath math="a<0" />, the number <InlineMath math="-2a" /> is positive, and <InlineMath math="h>0" />. So <InlineMath math="b>0" /> for every parabola that meets the conditions.
3. **True**. The value <InlineMath math="a-b+c" /> is <InlineMath math="g(-1)" />. Because <InlineMath math="-1<p" />, the point <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> lies to the left of both intercepts, where the graph is below the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis. So <InlineMath math="a-b+c<0" />.
4. **True**. Substitute <InlineMath math="b=-2a\,h" /> to get <InlineMath math="2a+b=2a\,(1-h)" />. The factor <InlineMath math="2a" /> is negative, and <InlineMath math="1-h" /> is positive because <InlineMath math="h<1" />. **A negative number times a positive number is always negative**, so <InlineMath math="2a+b<0" />.
5. **True**. The points <InlineMath math="x=0" /> and <InlineMath math="x=1" /> both lie between <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" />, so <InlineMath math="c=g(0)>0" /> and <InlineMath math="a+b+c=g(1)>0" />. The product of two positive numbers is positive.

So the statements that are certainly true are <InlineMath math="b>0" />, <InlineMath math="a-b+c<0" />, <InlineMath math="2a+b<0" />, and <InlineMath math="c\,(a+b+c)>0" />.

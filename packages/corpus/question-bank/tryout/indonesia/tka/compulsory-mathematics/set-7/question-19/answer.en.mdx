The question asks for a ratio of **areas**, not a ratio of sides. Square <InlineMath math="PQRS" /> has only two vertices on the circle, so it isn't the usual square drawn inside a circle. You need the radius of the circle first, then the side of <InlineMath math="PQRS" /> from Pythagoras' theorem.

# Radius of the circle

Circle <InlineMath math="L" /> touches all four sides of square <InlineMath math="ABCD" />, so **its diameter equals the side of the square**, <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" />. The two touching points on opposite sides sit exactly at the two ends of one diameter. The radius is <InlineMath math="r=10\text{ cm}" />, and line <InlineMath math="g" /> is one of the circle's diameters.

# Side of square PQRS

Call the side of <InlineMath math="PQRS" /> <InlineMath math="s" />. Points <InlineMath math="R" /> and <InlineMath math="S" /> lie on the circle, and <InlineMath math="SR" /> is parallel to <InlineMath math="g" />. The perpendicular from the centre <InlineMath math="O" /> to the chord <InlineMath math="SR" /> always cuts that chord in half. The same perpendicular also cuts <InlineMath math="PQ" /> in half, because <InlineMath math="PS" /> and <InlineMath math="QR" /> are both perpendicular to <InlineMath math="g" />. So **<InlineMath math="O" /> is exactly the midpoint of <InlineMath math="PQ" />**, and <InlineMath math="OQ=\tfrac{s}{2}" />.

# Ratio of the areas

The area of <InlineMath math="PQRS" /> is <InlineMath math="s^2=80\text{ cm}^2" />, and the area of <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> is <InlineMath math="20^2=400\text{ cm}^2" />. Divide the small area by the big one to get the ratio.

<BlockMath math="\frac{A_{PQRS}}{A_{ABCD}}=\frac{80}{400}=\frac{1}{5}" />

The sides themselves are in the ratio <InlineMath math="\sqrt{80}:20=1:\sqrt{5}" />. **The ratio of areas is the square of the ratio of sides**, so <InlineMath math="\left(1:\sqrt{5}\right)^2=1:5" />, which matches the calculation above.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1:8" /> comes from putting <InlineMath math="O" /> at the point <InlineMath math="P" />. Then <InlineMath math="OR" /> would be a diagonal, <InlineMath math="s\sqrt{2}=10" />, and <InlineMath math="s^2=50" />.
- <InlineMath math="1:4" /> comes from assuming, with no reason, that the side of the square equals the radius, <InlineMath math="s=10" />.
- <InlineMath math="1:\sqrt{5}" /> is the ratio of the sides. **The question asks for the ratio of areas**, so the side ratio still has to be squared.
- <InlineMath math="1:2" /> belongs to a square with all four vertices on the circle, whose diagonal is <InlineMath math="20" /> and area <InlineMath math="200" />. Here <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> lie on a diameter, not on the circle.

So the area of square <InlineMath math="PQRS" /> to the area of square <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> is <InlineMath math="1:5" />.

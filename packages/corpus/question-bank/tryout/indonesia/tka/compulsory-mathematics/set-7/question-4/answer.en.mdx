# The boat's speed and the current

The question asks how long a round trip takes when the engine dies on the way back. First you need two speeds: the boat's speed in still water <InlineMath math="v" /> and the speed of the current <InlineMath math="a" />. **Downstream the speed is <InlineMath math="v+a" />. Upstream it's <InlineMath math="v-a" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v+a&=\frac{40}{2.5}=16 \\
v-a&=\frac{20}{2.5}=8 \\
v&=12\text{ km/h},\quad a=4\text{ km/h}
\end{aligned}" />

# The trip upstream

From P to Q the boat goes **against the current**, so its speed is <InlineMath math="8\text{ km/h}" />.

<BlockMath math="t_{\text{out}}=\frac{24}{8}=3\text{ h}" />

# The trip back

The way back goes with the current, so the speed is <InlineMath math="16\text{ km/h}" /> while the engine runs. This part splits into three pieces.

1. From Q to the midpoint, <InlineMath math="12\text{ km}" />, with the engine on.
2. The engine is off for <InlineMath math="1" /> hour. The boat doesn't stand still. **It drifts at the speed of the current, <InlineMath math="4\text{ km/h}" /> towards P**, so it moves <InlineMath math="4\text{ km}" />.
3. The rest of the way to P is <InlineMath math="12-4=8\text{ km}" />, with the engine on again.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t_{\text{back}}&=\frac{12}{16}+1+\frac{8}{16} \\
&=\tfrac34+1+\tfrac12=2\tfrac14\text{ h}=2\text{ h }15\text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

The total time is <InlineMath math="3\text{ h}+2\text{ h }15\text{ min}=5\text{ h }15\text{ min}" />.

As a check, without the breakdown the way back takes <InlineMath math="\tfrac{24}{16}=1.5" /> hours. The breakdown adds <InlineMath math="1" /> hour, but the boat covers <InlineMath math="4\text{ km}" /> by drifting, which saves <InlineMath math="\tfrac4{16}" /> hour, or <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes. That gives <InlineMath math="1.5" /> hours plus <InlineMath math="45" /> minutes, which is <InlineMath math="2" /> hours <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4" /> hours <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes uses the <InlineMath math="4\text{ km}" /> of drifting but forgets to add the hour the engine was off.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> hours <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes ignores the breakdown completely.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> hours uses the average speed <InlineMath math="12\text{ km/h}" /> both ways and then adds the hour. **The speeds out and back are different, so you can't average them**. The boat spends more time at the slow speed.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> hours <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes treats the boat as standing still while the engine is off. The current still carries it <InlineMath math="4\text{ km}" /> closer to P.

So the whole trip takes <InlineMath math="5" /> hours <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes.

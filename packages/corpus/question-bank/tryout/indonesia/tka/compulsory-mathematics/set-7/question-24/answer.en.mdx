The courier may pick up the parcel at <InlineMath math="C" />, at <InlineMath math="D" />, or at both, and must not pass <InlineMath math="X" />. **The routes through <InlineMath math="C" /> and the routes through <InlineMath math="D" /> overlap**, so count each set on its own and combine them by inclusion and exclusion: a route through both may be counted only once.

# How to count routes on a grid

A route that only goes east and north is a sequence of steps. For example, going from <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> to <InlineMath math="(4,2)" /> takes <InlineMath math="4" /> steps east and <InlineMath math="2" /> steps north, <InlineMath math="6" /> steps in all. **A route is fixed by choosing which of the six steps go north**, so there are <InlineMath math="\binom{6}{2}" /> routes.

Every route from <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="B" /> through <InlineMath math="C" /> is a part <InlineMath math="A\to C" /> followed by a part <InlineMath math="C\to B" />. Any first part can be paired with any second part, so the counts multiply.

# From A to C without passing X

There are <InlineMath math="\binom{6}{2}" /> routes from <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="C" />. Take away the ones through <InlineMath math="X" />, which are the routes <InlineMath math="A\to X" /> times the routes <InlineMath math="X\to C" />. The junction <InlineMath math="X" /> only lies in this part, so **you subtract in the first part only**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A\to C&:\ \binom{6}{2}=15 \\
A\to X\to C&:\ \binom{3}{1}\cdot\binom{3}{1}=3\cdot3=9 \\
A\to C \text{ avoiding } X&:\ 15-9=6
\end{aligned}" />

Check the first part by writing the number of routes at every junction. **Each number is the sum of the numbers to its left and below it, and junction <InlineMath math="X" /> gets <InlineMath math="0" /> because it is closed**. The number at <InlineMath math="C" /> comes out as <InlineMath math="6" />, matching the subtraction.

Check another way. A route that avoids <InlineMath math="X" /> crosses the line <InlineMath math="x=2" /> only at <InlineMath math="y=0" /> or only at <InlineMath math="y=2" />. That's <InlineMath math="3+3=6" />, which matches.

# Routes through C

From <InlineMath math="C(4,2)" /> to <InlineMath math="B(6,4)" /> takes <InlineMath math="2" /> steps east and <InlineMath math="2" /> steps north. The junction <InlineMath math="X" /> is outside this part of the grid, so nothing gets subtracted. **Every first part joins with every second part**, so you multiply.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
C\to B&:\ \binom{4}{2}=6 \\
\text{routes through }C&=6\cdot6=36
\end{aligned}" />

# Routes through D

The point <InlineMath math="D(1,2)" /> is already above the row <InlineMath math="y=1" />, so **a route from <InlineMath math="D" /> can never drop back to <InlineMath math="X(2,1)" />**. The part <InlineMath math="A\to D" /> doesn't pass <InlineMath math="X" /> either, because <InlineMath math="X" /> lies to the right of <InlineMath math="D" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A\to D&:\ \binom{3}{1}=3 \\
D\to B&:\ \binom{7}{2}=21 \\
\text{routes through }D&=3\cdot21=63
\end{aligned}" />

# Routes through both

A route through <InlineMath math="D" /> and <InlineMath math="C" /> runs <InlineMath math="A\to D\to C\to B" />. From <InlineMath math="D(1,2)" /> to <InlineMath math="C(4,2)" /> there is **only one route**, straight east along <InlineMath math="y=2" />.

<BlockMath math="3\cdot1\cdot6=18" />

# The total number of routes

**The routes through both are already counted in <InlineMath math="36" /> and again in <InlineMath math="63" />**, so subtract them once.

<BlockMath math="36+63-18=81" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="105" /> counts every route that avoids <InlineMath math="X" /> without the pickup condition, including the <InlineMath math="24" /> routes that pass neither <InlineMath math="C" /> nor <InlineMath math="D" />.
- <InlineMath math="99" /> adds <InlineMath math="36+63" /> and forgets that **<InlineMath math="18" /> routes are counted twice**.
- <InlineMath math="135" /> forgets the closed junction, so the routes through <InlineMath math="C" /> come out as <InlineMath math="15\cdot6=90" />, then <InlineMath math="90+63-18" />.
- <InlineMath math="63" /> only counts the routes through <InlineMath math="D" /> and loses the case of routes that stop at <InlineMath math="C" /> only.

So the courier has <InlineMath math="81" /> different routes.

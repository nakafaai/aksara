This question has two conditions at once: the courier **has to pass <InlineMath math="C" />** and **must not pass <InlineMath math="X" />**. The tidy way is to cut every route at <InlineMath math="C" />, count each part, and multiply.

# How to count routes on a grid

A route that only goes east and north is a sequence of steps. For example, going from <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> to <InlineMath math="(4,2)" /> takes <InlineMath math="4" /> steps east and <InlineMath math="2" /> steps north, <InlineMath math="6" /> steps in all. **A route is fixed by choosing which of the six steps go north**, so there are <InlineMath math="\binom{6}{2}" /> routes.

Every route from <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="B" /> through <InlineMath math="C" /> is a part <InlineMath math="A\to C" /> followed by a part <InlineMath math="C\to B" />. Any first part can be paired with any second part, so the counts multiply.

# From A to C without passing X

There are <InlineMath math="\binom{6}{2}" /> routes from <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="C" />. Take away the ones through <InlineMath math="X" />, which are the routes <InlineMath math="A\to X" /> times the routes <InlineMath math="X\to C" />. The junction <InlineMath math="X" /> only lies in this part, so **you subtract in the first part only**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A\to C&:\ \binom{6}{2}=15 \\
A\to X\to C&:\ \binom{3}{1}\cdot\binom{3}{1}=3\cdot3=9 \\
A\to C \text{ avoiding } X&:\ 15-9=6
\end{aligned}" />

Check another way. A route that avoids <InlineMath math="X" /> crosses the line <InlineMath math="x=2" /> only at <InlineMath math="y=0" /> or only at <InlineMath math="y=2" />. Along the bottom, it goes to <InlineMath math="(3,0)" /> and then to <InlineMath math="C" />, giving <InlineMath math="\binom{3}{1}=3" /> routes. Along the top, it first goes to <InlineMath math="(1,2)" />, giving <InlineMath math="\binom{3}{1}=3" /> routes, and then straight across to <InlineMath math="C" />. That's <InlineMath math="3+3=6" />, which matches.

# From C to B and the total

From <InlineMath math="C(4,2)" /> to <InlineMath math="B(6,4)" /> takes <InlineMath math="2" /> steps east and <InlineMath math="2" /> steps north. The junction <InlineMath math="X" /> is outside this part of the grid, so nothing gets subtracted. **Every first part joins with every second part**, so you multiply.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
C\to B&:\ \binom{4}{2}=6 \\
\text{number of routes}&=6\cdot6=36
\end{aligned}" />

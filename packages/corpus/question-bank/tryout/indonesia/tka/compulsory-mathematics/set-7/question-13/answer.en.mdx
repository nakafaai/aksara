# Two mirrors make one rotation

The question asks for the image of <InlineMath math="P" /> after applying <InlineMath math="T" /> three times. You don't need to reflect six times one by one, because **two reflections in two intersecting lines make one rotation**.

Both lines pass through the origin <InlineMath math="O" />. Line <InlineMath math="g" /> makes an angle of <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> with the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, since its gradient is <InlineMath math="1" />. Line <InlineMath math="h" /> makes an angle of <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, since its gradient is <InlineMath math="\sqrt3=\tan60^\circ" />.

The rule: the first mirror followed by the second mirror is a rotation about the point where they meet, through **twice** the angle from the first line to the second, in the direction from the first line towards the second.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{angle from } g \text{ to } h&=60^\circ-45^\circ=15^\circ \\
T&=\text{rotation through } 2\cdot15^\circ=30^\circ \text{ anticlockwise}
\end{aligned}" />

# Three rotations

Applying <InlineMath math="T" /> three times means turning <InlineMath math="3\cdot30^\circ=90^\circ" /> anticlockwise about <InlineMath math="O" />. A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> rotation turns <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> into <InlineMath math="(-y,x)" />.

<BlockMath math="P(4,2)\ \longmapsto\ (-2,4)" />

As a check, **the distance to the centre doesn't change**, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{16+4}=\sqrt{4+16}" />, and the dot product <InlineMath math="(4,2)\cdot(-2,4)=-8+8=0" />, so the turn really is a right angle.

So the final image of <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="(-2,4)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(2,-4)" /> comes from swapping the order, reflecting in <InlineMath math="h" /> first and then <InlineMath math="g" />. The rotation then goes clockwise.
- <InlineMath math="(\sqrt2,\ 3\sqrt2)" /> forgets to double the angle, so each <InlineMath math="T" /> counts as <InlineMath math="15^\circ" /> and the total is only <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-4,-2)" /> treats each single reflection as a <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> rotation, so six reflections give <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="(2\sqrt3-1,\ \sqrt3+2)" /> applies <InlineMath math="T" /> only once, a <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> rotation.

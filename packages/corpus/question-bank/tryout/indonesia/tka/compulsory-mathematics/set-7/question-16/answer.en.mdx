The question asks for the diameter of the solid cylinder made by melting one pipe. The key idea is that **the volume of iron doesn't change**: the volume of the pipe wall equals the volume of the new solid cylinder. So first find the volume of iron in the pipe, then find the radius of a cylinder with that volume.

# Volume of iron in the pipe

The iron sits only in the wall, the ring between the outer circle and the hollow. The outer radius is <InlineMath math="R=\frac{10}{2}=5\text{ cm}" />. **The <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> wall is on both sides of the hollow**, so the hollow has diameter <InlineMath math="10-2\cdot1=8\text{ cm}" /> and the inner radius is <InlineMath math="r=4\text{ cm}" />. The length has to be in centimetres too, <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}=500\text{ cm}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{iron}}&=\left(\pi R^2-\pi r^2\right)\cdot L \\
&=\pi\,(5^2-4^2)\cdot500 \\
&=\pi\cdot9\cdot500=4500\pi\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

# Radius of the solid cylinder

Call the radius of the solid cylinder <InlineMath math="\rho" />. Its height is <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" />, and its volume has to match the iron volume. The factor <InlineMath math="\pi" /> appears on both sides, so **you don't need to put in <InlineMath math="\tfrac{22}{7}" /> at all**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\pi\rho^2\cdot20&=4500\pi \\
\rho^2&=\frac{4500}{20}=225 \\
\rho&=15\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

The question wants the diameter, so <InlineMath math="d=2\rho=30\text{ cm}" />. Quick sense check: the <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}" /> pipe becomes a cylinder <InlineMath math="25" /> times shorter, so its cross section has to be <InlineMath math="25" /> times the ring area, <InlineMath math="25\cdot9\pi=225\pi" />. That's exactly the area of a circle with radius <InlineMath math="15" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}" /> comes from leaving the length as <InlineMath math="5" /> instead of <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}" />. **Every measurement has to be in the same unit**.
- <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> comes from matching only the cross sections, as if the height were still <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}" />. The new cylinder is much shorter, so its cross section must be much bigger.
- <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> comes from taking the ring area as <InlineMath math="\pi\,(R-r)^2=\pi" />. A ring's area is the difference of two circle areas, not the area of a circle whose radius is the wall thickness.
- <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" /> is the radius. The last step, doubling it to get the diameter, got skipped.

So the solid cylinder has a diameter of <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" />.

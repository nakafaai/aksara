# Volume of iron in the old pipe

An outer diameter of <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> gives an outer radius of <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}" />. **The wall thickness comes off the radius, not the diameter**, so the inner radius is <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" />.

<BlockMath math="V_{\text{old}}=\pi\left(5^2-4^2\right)\cdot500=4{,}500\pi\text{ cm}^3" />

# Iron for the new pipe

Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of the iron is left, so the new pipe uses <InlineMath math="0.8\cdot4{,}500\pi=3{,}600\pi\text{ cm}^3" />. The new pipe has outer radius <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> and length <InlineMath math="180\text{ cm}" />, while **its inner radius <InlineMath math="r" /> is still unknown**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\pi\left(6^2-r^2\right)\cdot180&=3{,}600\pi \\
36-r^2&=20 \\
r&=4
\end{aligned}" />

# The wall thickness

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> is the inner radius of the new pipe, or the difference of diameters <InlineMath math="12-8" /> without halving it.
- <InlineMath math="\left(6-\sqrt{11}\right)\text{ cm}" /> forgets that <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of the iron is lost, so <InlineMath math="36-r^2=25" />.
- <InlineMath math="\left(6-\sqrt6\right)\text{ cm}" /> adds <InlineMath math="20\%" /> instead of taking it away, so <InlineMath math="36-r^2=30" />.
- <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> assumes the wall of the new pipe is as thick as the old one. **The size of the pipe changes, so the thickness has to be worked out again**.

So the wall of the new pipe is <InlineMath math="2\text{ cm}" /> thick.

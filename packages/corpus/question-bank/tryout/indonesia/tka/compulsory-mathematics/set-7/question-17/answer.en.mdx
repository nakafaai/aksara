<BlockMath math="3+4+5+4+3=19\text{ pipes}" />

You can also count ring by ring. The centre pipe is <InlineMath math="1" />, the first ring is <InlineMath math="6" />, and the outer ring is <InlineMath math="6\cdot3-6=12" />. **Each corner pipe belongs to two sides at once**, so the six corner pipes are taken off once. You get the same total, <InlineMath math="1+6+12=19" />.

# Mass of one pipe

The outer radius is <InlineMath math="R=5\text{ cm}" />. The <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> wall is on both sides of the hollow, so the inner radius is <InlineMath math="r=4\text{ cm}" />. The length is <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}=500\text{ cm}" />. The iron volume is the ring area times the length, and then **mass equals density times volume**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V&=\frac{22}{7}\,(5^2-4^2)\cdot500=\frac{22}{7}\cdot9\cdot500=\frac{99{,}000}{7}\text{ cm}^3 \\
m&=7\cdot\frac{99{,}000}{7}=99{,}000\text{ g}=99\text{ kg}
\end{aligned}" />

# Mass of one bundle

One bundle holds <InlineMath math="19" /> identical pipes, and the strapping is ignored. Since every pipe is the same size, **the bundle's mass is the number of pipes times the mass of one pipe**.

<BlockMath math="19\cdot99\text{ kg}=1{,}881\text{ kg}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2{,}475\text{ kg}" /> is <InlineMath math="25\cdot99" />. The outer ring was counted as <InlineMath math="6\cdot3=18" /> without removing the corner pipes that were counted twice.
- <InlineMath math="3{,}663\text{ kg}" /> is <InlineMath math="37\cdot99" />. The phrase about <InlineMath math="3" /> pipes on each side was read as three rings around the centre pipe, <InlineMath math="1+6+12+18=37" />.
- <InlineMath math="5{,}225\text{ kg}" /> is <InlineMath math="19\cdot275" />. Here the pipes were treated as solid, but **the hollow inside a pipe holds no iron**.
- <InlineMath math="7{,}524\text{ kg}" /> is <InlineMath math="19\cdot396" />. The diameters <InlineMath math="10" /> and <InlineMath math="8" /> were used as radii, which makes the ring area four times too big.

So one bundle of pipes has a mass of <InlineMath math="1{,}881\text{ kg}" />.

One complete cone needs **the ice cream in the cone plus the hemisphere on top**. Both have radius <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}" />, and <InlineMath math="1" /> litre is <InlineMath math="1{,}000\text{ cm}^3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{cone}}&=\tfrac13\cdot3.14\cdot3^2\cdot12=113.04 \\
V_{\text{hemisphere}}&=\tfrac23\cdot3.14\cdot3^3=56.52 \\
V_{\text{one serving}}&=113.04+56.52=169.56\text{ cm}^3 \\
\frac{1{,}000}{169.56}&\approx5.9
\end{aligned}" />

A sixth complete cone can't be made, because <InlineMath math="6\cdot169.56=1{,}017.36\text{ cm}^3" />, more than <InlineMath math="1{,}000\text{ cm}^3" />. So the result is **rounded down**.

So at most <InlineMath math="5" /> complete cones can be made.

- <InlineMath math="4" /> puts a full ball on top of the cone instead of a hemisphere.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> rounds <InlineMath math="5.9" /> up, but there isn't enough ice cream for a sixth cone.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> counts only the ice cream inside the cone.
- <InlineMath math="17" /> counts only the hemisphere.

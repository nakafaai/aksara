# The bill before the discount

This question runs backwards: from the money paid, you find the water used. The first step is to remove the discount. The <InlineMath math="25\%" /> discount is taken from the full bill, so **the amount paid is <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of the original bill**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.75\cdot T&=120{,}000 \\
T&=\frac{120{,}000}{0.75}=160{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The bill before the discount is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}160{,}000" />.

# Finding the usage block

The price per cubic metre changes from block to block, so first find out which block the usage falls in. Work out the bill exactly at the block limits.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T(10)&=40{,}000+10\cdot3{,}000=70{,}000 \\
T(30)&=70{,}000+20\cdot5{,}000=170{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

Because <InlineMath math="70{,}000<160{,}000<170{,}000" />, **the usage is in the second block**: above <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}^3" /> and no more than <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}^3" />.

# Working out the usage

In the second block, the bill for usage <InlineMath math="u" /> is <InlineMath math="70{,}000" /> plus <InlineMath math="5{,}000" /> for every <InlineMath math="\text{m}^3" /> above <InlineMath math="10" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
70{,}000+5{,}000\,(u-10)&=160{,}000 \\
5{,}000\,(u-10)&=90{,}000 \\
u-10&=18 \\
u&=28
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, the bill for <InlineMath math="28\text{ m}^3" /> is <InlineMath math="40{,}000+30{,}000+90{,}000=160{,}000" />, and after the discount it's <InlineMath math="0.75\cdot160{,}000=120{,}000" />. **That's exactly the amount paid**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="18\text{ m}^3" /> is only the part above <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}^3" />. The first <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}^3" /> were never added back.
- <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}^3" /> treats <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}120{,}000" /> as the bill, which means the discount was never removed.
- <InlineMath math="24\text{ m}^3" /> uses the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" /> rate for all the water, <InlineMath math="(160{,}000-40{,}000)\div5{,}000" />. But the first <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}^3" /> are cheaper.
- <InlineMath math="26\text{ m}^3" /> adds <InlineMath math="25\%" /> of the amount paid, giving a bill of <InlineMath math="150{,}000" />. **The discount is a share of the original bill, not of the amount paid**, so the right move is dividing by <InlineMath math="0.75" />.

So the customer used <InlineMath math="28\text{ m}^3" /> of water.

# May's bill before the discount

This question has two stages. From May's payment you find May's usage, then you carry that usage into June. The <InlineMath math="25\%" /> discount is taken from the full bill, so **the amount paid in May is <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of the original bill**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.75\cdot T&=120{,}000 \\
T&=\frac{120{,}000}{0.75}=160{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

# May's usage

The price per cubic metre changes from block to block, so first find the block that holds a bill of <InlineMath math="160{,}000" />. Work out the bill exactly at the block limits.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T(10)&=40{,}000+10\cdot3{,}000=70{,}000 \\
T(30)&=70{,}000+20\cdot5{,}000=170{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="70{,}000<160{,}000<170{,}000" />, **May's usage lies in the second block**.

<BlockMath math="70{,}000+5{,}000\,(u-10)=160{,}000\ \Rightarrow\ u-10=18\ \Rightarrow\ u=28" />

# June's bill

June's usage is <InlineMath math="50\%" /> higher, so <InlineMath math="1.5\cdot28=42\text{ m}^3" />. **This usage goes past the <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}^3" /> limit**, so the last <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}^3" /> are charged at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}8{,}000" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T(42)&=40{,}000+10\cdot3{,}000+20\cdot5{,}000+12\cdot8{,}000 \\
&=40{,}000+30{,}000+100{,}000+96{,}000=266{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The customer pays after the <InlineMath math="10" />th, so there's no discount. Draw the bill as a function of usage. **May's point is in the second block, while June's point is already in the steeper third block**, so June's bill isn't <InlineMath math="1.5" /> times May's bill.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}170{,}000" /> treats <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}120{,}000" /> as May's bill without removing the discount. May's usage then reads as <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}^3" /> and June's as <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}199{,}500" /> still gives the <InlineMath math="25\%" /> discount in June, even though the payment comes after the <InlineMath math="10" />th.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}230{,}000" /> uses the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" /> rate for everything above <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}^3" /> and skips the third block.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}240{,}000" /> multiplies May's bill by <InlineMath math="1.5" />. **The fixed charge doesn't grow and the rate changes from block to block**, so the bill isn't proportional to the usage.

So the amount paid for June's bill is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}266{,}000" />.

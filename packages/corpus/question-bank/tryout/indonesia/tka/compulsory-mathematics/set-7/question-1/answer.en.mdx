# Intensity from each stage

The question asks for the sound level at the guest tent while two groups of drums play together. By the first rule in the reading, **you add intensities, not decibels**. So turn decibels into intensity first, work out each stage, and only then add.

One big drum at <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> has a level of <InlineMath math="100\text{ dB}" />. The formula in the reading gives its intensity:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
10\log\frac{I}{10^{-12}}&=100 \\
\frac{I}{10^{-12}}&=10^{10} \\
I_{\text{big}}&=10^{-2}\ \text{W/m}^2 \\
I_{\text{small}}&=\tfrac14\cdot10^{-2}=2.5\cdot10^{-3}\ \text{W/m}^2
\end{aligned}" />

Those are intensities at <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" />. By the second rule, at distance <InlineMath math="r" /> you divide by <InlineMath math="r^2" />. The tent is <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> from stage A, so divide by <InlineMath math="400" />. It's <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> from stage B, so divide by <InlineMath math="25" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
I_A&=8\cdot\frac{10^{-2}}{400}=2\cdot10^{-4}\ \text{W/m}^2 \\
I_B&=4\cdot\frac{2.5\cdot10^{-3}}{25}=4\cdot10^{-4}\ \text{W/m}^2
\end{aligned}" />

Notice that stage B delivers twice as much as stage A. Its drums are smaller and fewer, but they're much closer to the tent.

# The total level

Add the two intensities, then convert back to decibels with <InlineMath math="\log6=\log2+\log3=0.78" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
I&=2\cdot10^{-4}+4\cdot10^{-4}=6\cdot10^{-4}\ \text{W/m}^2 \\
\mathrm{TI}&=10\log\frac{6\cdot10^{-4}}{10^{-12}}=10\log\left(6\cdot10^{8}\right) \\
&=10\,(0.78+8)=87.8\text{ dB}
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, stage B alone gives <InlineMath math="10\log(4\cdot10^8)=86\text{ dB}" />. Stage A adds only half of B's intensity, so **the result must be above <InlineMath math="86\text{ dB}" /> but below <InlineMath math="89\text{ dB}" />**. The value <InlineMath math="87.8\text{ dB}" /> fits.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="83\text{ dB}" /> is stage A alone, <InlineMath math="10\log(2\cdot10^8)" />. You get it if you assume the big drums must dominate and forget stage B.
- <InlineMath math="84.5\text{ dB}" /> is the average of <InlineMath math="83" /> and <InlineMath math="86" />. **You can't average or add decibels directly**, because the scale is logarithmic.
- <InlineMath math="86\text{ dB}" /> is stage B alone. Stage A still adds some intensity, however small.
- <InlineMath math="89\text{ dB}" /> adds <InlineMath math="3\text{ dB}" /> as if both stages were equally strong. Adding <InlineMath math="3\text{ dB}" /> only works when the intensity doubles, but here it only grows to <InlineMath math="1.5" /> times.

So the sound level at the guest tent is <InlineMath math="87.8\text{ dB}" />.

# Intensity of one drum

By the first rule, **you add intensities, not decibels**. So turn decibels into intensity first. One big drum at <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> has a level of <InlineMath math="100\text{ dB}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
10\log\frac{I}{10^{-12}}&=100\ \Rightarrow\ I_{\text{big}}=10^{-2}\ \text{W/m}^2 \\
I_{\text{small}}&=\tfrac14\cdot10^{-2}\ \text{W/m}^2
\end{aligned}" />

# Where the point T is

Let <InlineMath math="T" /> be <InlineMath math="x" /> metres from stage A, so <InlineMath math="20-x" /> metres from stage B. Intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance, and the condition is that both stages contribute the same.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{9\cdot10^{-2}}{x^2}&=\frac{4\cdot\tfrac14\cdot10^{-2}}{(20-x)^2} \\
\frac{9}{x^2}&=\frac{1}{(20-x)^2}\ \Rightarrow\ x=3\,(20-x)
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="T" /> lies between the stages, both distances are positive and you take the positive root. That gives <InlineMath math="x=15" />, so **<InlineMath math="T" /> is <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" /> from A and <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> from B**, closer to the stage with the weaker sound.

Draw each stage's contribution along the segment <InlineMath math="AB" />, in units of <InlineMath math="10^{-4}\ \text{W/m}^2" />. Stage A's curve falls as you move away from A, stage B's curve rises as you approach B, and **the two meet at <InlineMath math="x=15" /> with value <InlineMath math="4" />**.

# The level at T

Each stage contributes <InlineMath math="\frac{9\cdot10^{-2}{225}=4\cdot10^{-4}" />, so **the total intensity is twice that**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
I&=2\cdot4\cdot10^{-4}=8\cdot10^{-4}\ \text{W/m}^2 \\
\mathrm{TI}&=10\log\left(8\cdot10^{8}\right)=10\,(3\cdot0.30+8)=89\text{ dB}
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="86\text{ dB}" /> counts only one stage's share, <InlineMath math="4\cdot10^{-4}" />, and forgets to add the other.
- <InlineMath math="90\text{ dB}" /> puts <InlineMath math="T" /> at the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AB" />. **At the midpoint the two stages don't contribute equally**: they give <InlineMath math="9\cdot10^{-4}" /> and <InlineMath math="10^{-4}" />.
- <InlineMath math="91\text{ dB}" /> forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> for the small drums, which puts <InlineMath math="T" /> <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}" /> from A with <InlineMath math="6.25\cdot10^{-4}" /> from each stage.
- <InlineMath math="92\text{ dB}" /> adds <InlineMath math="6\text{ dB}" /> for two equally strong sources. Doubling the intensity only adds <InlineMath math="10\log2=3\text{ dB}" />.

So the sound level at the point <InlineMath math="T" /> is <InlineMath math="89\text{ dB}" />.

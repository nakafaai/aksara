Use the cosine ratio in that right triangle.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\cos\angle PSM&=\frac{SM}{SP}=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2} \\
\angle PSM&=60^\circ \\
\angle PSQ&=2\cdot60^\circ=120^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

Triangle <InlineMath math="SMQ" /> is an exact mirror image of triangle <InlineMath math="SMP" /> in the line <InlineMath math="SM" />. That's why **the central angle of the segment is twice <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />**.

# Area of the segment

A sector is the region bounded by two radii and the arc between them. The segment is the <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> sector minus triangle <InlineMath math="SPQ" />. The triangle sits on the side of <InlineMath math="S" />, so **the triangle still gets wet**, and only the segment is lost.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{\text{sector}}&=\frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ}\cdot\pi\cdot6^2=12\pi \\
A_{\triangle SPQ}&=\frac{1}{2}\cdot6\cdot6\cdot\sin120^\circ=18\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}=9\sqrt{3} \\
A_{\text{segment}}&=12\pi-9\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}" />

# Area that gets wet

Take the segment away from the full circle, <InlineMath math="\pi\cdot6^2=36\pi" />. Watch the brackets, because **subtracting a difference adds the triangle back**.

<BlockMath math="36\pi-\left(12\pi-9\sqrt{3}\right)=24\pi+9\sqrt{3}\approx75.4+15.6=91.0\text{ m}^2" />

Sense check: the wall is beyond the centre <InlineMath math="S" />, so the wet area must be more than half the circle, <InlineMath math="18\pi\approx56.5" />, and less than the full circle, <InlineMath math="36\pi\approx113.1" />. The result <InlineMath math="91.0" /> sits right in between.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\left(30\pi+9\sqrt{3}\right)\text{ m}^2" /> uses <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> as the central angle of the segment. That <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> is only half of it, the angle <InlineMath math="PSM" />.
- <InlineMath math="24\pi\text{ m}^2" /> throws away the whole <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> sector. **Triangle <InlineMath math="SPQ" /> is in front of the wall**, so it gets wet too.
- <InlineMath math="\left(24\pi-9\sqrt{3}\right)\text{ m}^2" /> is a sign slip when subtracting the segment, so both the sector and the triangle get thrown away.
- <InlineMath math="\left(12\pi-9\sqrt{3}\right)\text{ m}^2" /> is the area of the segment behind the wall. That's the dry part, not the one the question asks for.

So the area of lawn that gets wet is <InlineMath math="\left(24\pi+9\sqrt{3}\right)\text{ m}^2" />.

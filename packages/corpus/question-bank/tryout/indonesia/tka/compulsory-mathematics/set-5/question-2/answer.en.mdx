# The cost of one module

The question asks for the largest number of modules whose total cost doesn't go over <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}275{,}000" />. The key is the rule that **blank sheets may be added**: ordering more sheets can lower the price per sheet.

A <InlineMath math="30" />-page module is printed on both sides, so one module needs <InlineMath math="\frac{30}{2}=15" /> sheets. With <InlineMath math="n" /> modules you really need <InlineMath math="15n" /> sheets, and binding costs <InlineMath math="4{,}000n" />.

# Without extra sheets

For <InlineMath math="14\le n\le33" /> the order has <InlineMath math="210" /> to <InlineMath math="495" /> sheets, so the price is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}400" /> per sheet. One module then costs <InlineMath math="15\cdot400+4{,}000=10{,}000" /> rupiah:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
10{,}000n&\le275{,}000 \\
n&\le27.5
\end{aligned}" />

This way **gives at most <InlineMath math="27" /> modules**. Without extra sheets, the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}300" /> price only starts when <InlineMath math="15n\ge500" />, that is <InlineMath math="n\ge34" />, and <InlineMath math="34" /> modules already cost <InlineMath math="34\cdot(15\cdot300+4{,}000)=289{,}000" /> rupiah. That's over the budget.

# With extra sheets

Now order exactly <InlineMath math="500" /> sheets: the module sheets plus blank ones. As long as <InlineMath math="15n\le500" />, printing stays at <InlineMath math="500\cdot300=150{,}000" /> rupiah, whatever <InlineMath math="n" /> is. **Only the binding still grows**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
150{,}000+4{,}000n&\le275{,}000 \\
4{,}000n&\le125{,}000 \\
n&\le31.25
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="n=31" />. Check: <InlineMath math="31" /> modules need <InlineMath math="465" /> sheets, plus <InlineMath math="35" /> blank sheets makes <InlineMath math="500" />, and the total is <InlineMath math="150{,}000+124{,}000=274{,}000" /> rupiah. For <InlineMath math="32" /> modules the cheapest way is also <InlineMath math="500" /> sheets, with a total of <InlineMath math="150{,}000+128{,}000=278{,}000" /> rupiah, which is over the budget.

So the largest number of modules that can be ordered is <InlineMath math="31" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="27" /> stops at the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}400" /> price and **forgets that blank sheets may be added** to reach <InlineMath math="500" /> sheets.
- <InlineMath math="25" /> treats the prices like steps: the first <InlineMath math="199" /> sheets at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}500" />, the rest at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}400" />. That way <InlineMath math="25" /> modules cost <InlineMath math="269{,}900" /> and <InlineMath math="26" /> modules cost <InlineMath math="279{,}900" />. But one price applies to all sheets.
- <InlineMath math="23" /> uses the tier of a single module. One module is only <InlineMath math="15" /> sheets, so it gets <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}500" /> per sheet and <InlineMath math="11{,}500" /> rupiah per module. The tier depends on the sheets of the whole order.
- <InlineMath math="21" /> reads <InlineMath math="30" /> pages as <InlineMath math="30" /> sheets, so one module costs <InlineMath math="30\cdot300+4{,}000=13{,}000" /> rupiah. The modules are printed on both sides, so it's only <InlineMath math="15" /> sheets.

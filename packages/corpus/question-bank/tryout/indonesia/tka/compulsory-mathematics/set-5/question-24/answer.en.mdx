# Three possible balls to move

What's in box B when the two balls are drawn depends on the colour of the ball moved from box A, and that colour is unknown. So **split into cases by the colour of the moved ball**, find the chance of a matching pair in each case, then combine them.

Box A holds <InlineMath math="4" /> balls, so the moved ball is red, blue, or green with probabilities <InlineMath math="\tfrac24=\tfrac12" />, <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" />, and <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" />. They aren't equal because there are two red balls.

# The chance of a matching pair in each case

After the move, box B holds <InlineMath math="6" /> balls. Two balls drawn one at a time without replacement form one pair, and the order doesn't affect whether the colours match. There are <InlineMath math="\binom62=15" /> possible pairs. **A matching pair can only come from a colour with at least two balls**.

| Ball moved | Probability | Box B (red, blue, green) | Matching pairs | Chance of a match |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| red | <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> | <InlineMath math="3,\ 2,\ 1" /> | <InlineMath math="3+1+0=4" /> | <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4}{15}" /> |
| blue | <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> | <InlineMath math="2,\ 3,\ 1" /> | <InlineMath math="1+3+0=4" /> | <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4}{15}" /> |
| green | <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> | <InlineMath math="2,\ 2,\ 2" /> | <InlineMath math="1+1+1=3" /> | <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{15}" /> |

For example, in the red case, three red balls give <InlineMath math="\binom32=3" /> pairs, two blue balls give <InlineMath math="1" /> pair, and a single green ball can't form a pair.

# Combining the three cases

Multiply the probability of each case by the chance of a match in that case, then add:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P&=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{4}{15}+\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac{4}{15}+\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac{3}{15} \\
&=\tfrac{8}{60}+\tfrac{4}{60}+\tfrac{3}{60}=\tfrac{15}{60}=\tfrac14
\end{aligned}" />

Check the red case by counting in order: <InlineMath math="\frac{3\cdot2+2\cdot1}{6\cdot5}=\frac{8}{30}=\frac{4}{15}" />. **It matches the pair count**, so the table is right.

So the probability that the two balls drawn from box B have the same colour is <InlineMath math="\frac14" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac15" /> ignores the move and uses the original box B, <InlineMath math="\frac{1+1}{10}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{11}{45}" /> treats the three colours of the moved ball as **equally likely**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> each, but box A has two red balls.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{15}" /> assumes the moved ball must be red because red is the most likely. The blue and green cases get lost.
- <InlineMath math="\frac38" /> draws with replacement, for example <InlineMath math="\frac{9+4+1}{36}" /> in the red case, but the balls are drawn without replacement.

# A handy unit

The question asks how much water still has to go in, from a water surface <InlineMath math="0.3\text{ m}" /> above the tip of the cone up to the safety line. That water fills two parts: **the rest of the cone above the water already there**, and then the cylinder up to the safety line.

Work in decimetres, because <InlineMath math="1\text{ dm}^3=1" /> litre. The radius is <InlineMath math="r=7\text{ dm}" />, the cone is <InlineMath math="6\text{ dm}" /> tall, the cylinder is <InlineMath math="15\text{ dm}" /> tall, and the safety line sits <InlineMath math="3\text{ dm}" /> below the top rim. The cylinder and the cone share the same base area:

<BlockMath math="\pi r^2=\frac{22}{7}\cdot7^2=154\text{ dm}^2" />

# The empty part of the cone

The full cone holds <InlineMath math="\tfrac13\cdot154\cdot6=308" /> litres. The water already there forms a small cone <InlineMath math="3\text{ dm}" /> tall, half the height of the big cone. The small cone is similar to the big one, so its radius is also half, <InlineMath math="3.5\text{ dm}" />. Because the height and the radius are both multiplied by <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />, **the volume is multiplied by <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac12\right)^3=\tfrac18" />**, not by <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{water at start}}&=\tfrac18\cdot308=38.5\text{ litres} \\
\text{check}&:\ \tfrac13\cdot\tfrac{22}{7}\cdot3.5^2\cdot3=38.5\text{ litres} \\
V_{\text{rest of cone}}&=308-38.5=269.5\text{ litres}
\end{aligned}" />

# The cylinder up to the safety line

Inside the cylinder, the water rises from the bottom of the cylinder to <InlineMath math="3\text{ dm}" /> below the top rim. **The water in the cylinder is only <InlineMath math="15-3=12\text{ dm}" /> deep**, not the full height of the cylinder.

<BlockMath math="V_{\text{cylinder}}=154\cdot12=1{,}848\text{ litres}" />

# The total to pump in

<BlockMath math="269.5+1{,}848=2{,}117.5\text{ litres}" />

Check that it makes sense. A tank filled up to the safety line holds <InlineMath math="308+1{,}848=2{,}156" /> litres, and it started with <InlineMath math="38.5" /> litres. The difference is <InlineMath math="2{,}156-38.5=2{,}117.5" /> litres, **the same result**.

So the amount of water that has to be pumped in is <InlineMath math="2{,}117.5" /> litres.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2{,}002" /> litres assumes that water up to half the cone's height fills half the cone's volume, <InlineMath math="154" /> litres. For similar solids, **the volume changes with the cube of the height ratio**.
- <InlineMath math="2{,}156" /> litres forgets to subtract the <InlineMath math="38.5" /> litres already in the cone.
- <InlineMath math="2{,}579.5" /> litres fills the cylinder to the top rim, <InlineMath math="154\cdot15=2{,}310" /> litres, but the water may only reach the safety line.
- <InlineMath math="2{,}656.5" /> litres forgets the <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> in the cone volume, so the cone counts as <InlineMath math="924" /> litres and the empty part as <InlineMath math="924-115.5=808.5" /> litres.

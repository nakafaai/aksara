# The first deposit and the monthly increase

Deposits that grow by the same amount every month form an **arithmetic sequence**. The question doesn't give the first deposit, so find it from the two deposits you know. To keep the numbers light, every value below is in thousands of rupiah.

Let the first deposit be <InlineMath math="a" /> and the monthly increase <InlineMath math="b" />. The deposit in month <InlineMath math="n" /> is <InlineMath math="a+(n-1)b" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+3b&=160 \\
a+8b&=260 \\
5b&=100\ \Rightarrow\ b=20,\quad a=160-60=100
\end{aligned}" />

So Rina starts with <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000" /> and her deposit goes up by <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}20{,}000" /> each month.

# The total after some months

The total after <InlineMath math="n" /> months is **the sum of the first <InlineMath math="n" /> terms** of that sequence:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S_n&=\frac n2\left(2a+(n-1)b\right) \\
&=\frac n2\left(200+20(n-1)\right) \\
&=\frac n2\left(180+20n\right)=10n^2+90n
\end{aligned}" />

# When the total goes past the target

The target is <InlineMath math="5{,}800" /> thousand, and the total has to be **more than** the target, not equal to it:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
10n^2+90n&>5{,}800 \\
n^2+9n-580&>0 \\
(n-20)(n+29)&>0
\end{aligned}" />

The number of months <InlineMath math="n" /> is positive, so <InlineMath math="n+29" /> is always positive and the condition becomes <InlineMath math="n>20" />. Check around the boundary:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S_{20}&=10\cdot400+90\cdot20=5{,}800 \\
S_{21}&=10\cdot441+90\cdot21=6{,}300
\end{aligned}" />

After month <InlineMath math="20" />, the total is **exactly** <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}800{,}000" />, not more. Only after month <InlineMath math="21" /> does it pass the target.

So Rina's total is more than <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}800{,}000" /> for the first time after the deposit in month <InlineMath math="21" />.

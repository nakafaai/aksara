# Percentages and numbers of students

The table gives percentages, but some statements talk about numbers of students. **A bigger percentage doesn't have to mean more students**, because the grades are different sizes. So first work out the number of students in each cell: the percentage times the size of that grade.

| Grade | Robotik | Futsal | Paduan Suara | Jurnalistik |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| X (<InlineMath math="160" />) | <InlineMath math="40" /> | <InlineMath math="56" /> | <InlineMath math="48" /> | <InlineMath math="16" /> |
| XI (<InlineMath math="120" />) | <InlineMath math="36" /> | <InlineMath math="36" /> | <InlineMath math="24" /> | <InlineMath math="24" /> |
| XII (<InlineMath math="80" />) | <InlineMath math="28" /> | <InlineMath math="20" /> | <InlineMath math="12" /> | <InlineMath math="20" /> |

Now judge the five statements in order.

# Judging each statement

## Robotik in grade X

Statement 1. **True**. Robotik has <InlineMath math="40" />, <InlineMath math="36" />, and <InlineMath math="28" /> members from grades X, XI, and XII, so grade X contributes the most. Its percentages are <InlineMath math="25\%" />, <InlineMath math="30\%" />, and <InlineMath math="35\%" />, so grade X really has the smallest percentage. Both hold at once because grade X is much bigger.

## Paduan Suara in grades X and XII

Statement 2. **False**. Paduan Suara has <InlineMath math="48" /> members from grade X and <InlineMath math="12" /> from grade XII. The ratio is <InlineMath math="48:12=4" />, so it's **four times**, not twice. The "twice" comes from the percentages <InlineMath math="30\%" /> and <InlineMath math="15\%" />, but those two percentages are taken from grades of different sizes.

## Eight students switch clubs

Statement 3. **True**. The students who switch are still in grade XII, so grade XII still has <InlineMath math="80" /> students. Only the two clubs change:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Robotik}&: \frac{28-8}{80}\cdot100\%=\frac{20}{80}\cdot100\%=25\% \\
\text{Futsal}&: \frac{20+8}{80}\cdot100\%=\frac{28}{80}\cdot100\%=35\%
\end{aligned}" />

Both match the grade X percentages, <InlineMath math="25\%" /> and <InlineMath math="35\%" />. Careful: <InlineMath math="8" /> students aren't <InlineMath math="8" /> percentage points. If you subtract directly, you get <InlineMath math="35\%-8\%=27\%" /> and wrongly call this statement false.

## Robotik and Jurnalistik together

Statement 4. **False**. The two clubs together get <InlineMath math="40+16=56" /> students from grade X, <InlineMath math="36+24=60" /> from grade XI, and <InlineMath math="28+20=48" /> from grade XII. Grade XI contributes the most. The trap is that grade XII does have the biggest combined percentage, <InlineMath math="35\%+25\%=60\%" />, but **grade XII is the smallest grade**.

## New students in grade XII

Statement 5. **False**. The new students also increase the size of grade XII, so the denominator changes too, to <InlineMath math="80+40=120" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{28+40}{80+40}\cdot100\%=\frac{68}{120}\cdot100\%\approx56.7\%<60\%" />

If you leave the denominator at <InlineMath math="80" />, you get <InlineMath math="\frac{68}{80}=85\%" /> and wrongly call this statement true.

So the true statements are statements 1 and 3.

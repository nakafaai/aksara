The safest method is to **follow one general point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" />** through each composition and compare the final rules.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x,y)&\xrightarrow{\ x=1\ }(2-x,\,y)\xrightarrow{\ x=4\ }(8-(2-x),\,y)=(x+6,\,y) \\
(x,y)&\xrightarrow{\ y=x\ }(y,\,x)\xrightarrow{\ x\text{-axis}\ }(y,\,-x) \\
(x,y)&\xrightarrow{\ [O,\,-2]\ }(-2x,\,-2y) \\
(x,y)&\xrightarrow{\ R(O,\,180^\circ)\ }(-x,\,-y)
\end{aligned}" />

- Two reflections in parallel lines give <InlineMath math="(x+6,y)" />, a translation by twice the distance between the lines, <InlineMath math="2\cdot3=6" />, to the right. This statement is true.
- The composition gives <InlineMath math="(y,-x)" />. A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> counterclockwise rotation gives <InlineMath math="(-y,x)" />, so this composition is a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> **clockwise** rotation. This statement is false.
- A scale factor of <InlineMath math="-2" /> stretches every length by <InlineMath math="|-2|=2" />, so areas become <InlineMath math="2^2=4" /> times as large. The minus sign only flips the figure's position. This statement is true.
- A <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation and a dilation with factor <InlineMath math="-1" /> both map <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-x,-y)" />. This statement is true.
- The two translations cancel, because <InlineMath math="3+(-3)=0" /> and <InlineMath math="-1+1=0" />, so every point returns to where it started. This statement is false.

So the true statements are the ones about the two parallel reflections, the area under the factor <InlineMath math="-2" /> dilation, and the <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation.

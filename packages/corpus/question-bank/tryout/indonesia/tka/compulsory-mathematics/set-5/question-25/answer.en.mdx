# Use the complement

The sample space is every pair of two cards, <InlineMath math="\binom{20}{2}=190" /> pairs, all equally likely. The event "even product or sum a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" />" covers many pairs, so it's easier to count the opposite: **both conditions fail**.

The product of two numbers is odd only when both numbers are odd. So the complement is the pairs where both numbers are odd and the sum isn't a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" />. There are <InlineMath math="10" /> odd numbers, <InlineMath math="1,3,5,\dots,19" />, which give <InlineMath math="\binom{10}{2}=45" /> odd pairs.

# Odd pairs whose sum is a multiple of three

Whether a sum is a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" /> depends on the remainders when you divide by <InlineMath math="3" />. Group the ten odd numbers by remainder:

| Remainder after dividing by <InlineMath math="3" /> | Odd numbers | Count |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="3,\ 9,\ 15" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="1,\ 7,\ 13,\ 19" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="5,\ 11,\ 17" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |

The sum is a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" /> when both remainders are <InlineMath math="0" />, or when **one has remainder <InlineMath math="1" /> and the other remainder <InlineMath math="2" />**, like <InlineMath math="1+5=6" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{odd pairs, sum a multiple of }3&=\binom32+4\cdot3=3+12=15 \\
\text{odd pairs, sum not a multiple of }3&=45-15=30
\end{aligned}" />

# The probability of the union

<BlockMath math="P=1-\frac{30}{190}=\frac{160}{190}=\frac{16}{19}" />

Check it with the addition rule. Pairs with an even product number <InlineMath math="190-45=145" />. From <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="20" />, the numbers with remainder <InlineMath math="0" />, <InlineMath math="1" />, and <InlineMath math="2" /> number <InlineMath math="6" />, <InlineMath math="7" />, and <InlineMath math="7" />, so the pairs whose sum is a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" /> number <InlineMath math="\binom62+7\cdot7=64" />. **The overlap has to be subtracted once**: <InlineMath math="64-15=49" /> pairs satisfy both. That gives <InlineMath math="145+64-49=160" />, the same as the complement method.

So the probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{16}{19}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{29}{38}" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{145}{190}" />, the probability of an even product only. The condition on the sum hasn't been used.
- <InlineMath math="\frac56" /> treats the two events as independent with the rough probabilities <InlineMath math="\tfrac34" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" />, giving <InlineMath math="\tfrac34+\tfrac13-\tfrac14" />. But the cards are drawn without replacement, and the remainder groups aren't the same size.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{169}{190}" /> **treats a sum like a product**, as if a sum could only be a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" /> when one of the numbers is.

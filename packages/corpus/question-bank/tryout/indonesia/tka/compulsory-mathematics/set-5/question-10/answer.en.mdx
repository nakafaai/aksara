The point <InlineMath math="H" /> is **the vertex opposite <InlineMath math="B" />**, since <InlineMath math="BH" /> is a space diagonal:

# The space diagonal is perpendicular to the plane

The line <InlineMath math="AC" /> is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="BD" /> because both are diagonals of the square <InlineMath math="ABCD" />, and <InlineMath math="AC" /> is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="DH" /> because <InlineMath math="DH" /> stands straight up from the base. In the same way, <InlineMath math="AF" /> is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="BE" /> and <InlineMath math="EH" />, so <InlineMath math="AF" /> is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="BH" />.

The line <InlineMath math="BH" /> is perpendicular to two intersecting lines of the plane <InlineMath math="ACF" />, so **<InlineMath math="BH" /> is perpendicular to the plane <InlineMath math="ACF" />**. That means the distances from <InlineMath math="B" /> and from <InlineMath math="H" /> to the plane are both measured along the diagonal <InlineMath math="BH" />.

# The distance from the corner

Work out the volume of the pyramid <InlineMath math="B.ACF" /> in two ways. With base <InlineMath math="ABC" /> and height <InlineMath math="BF" />, the volume is easy. With base <InlineMath math="ACF" />, the height is the distance <InlineMath math="d_B" /> you want. The triangle <InlineMath math="ACF" /> is equilateral, and its sides are face diagonals of length <InlineMath math="6\sqrt2" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V&=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot6\cdot6=36 \\
\text{area }ACF&=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\left(6\sqrt2\right)^2=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\cdot72=18\sqrt3 \\
d_B&=\frac{3V}{\text{area }ACF}=\frac{108}{18\sqrt3}=\frac{6}{\sqrt3}=2\sqrt3
\end{aligned}" />

So the plane <InlineMath math="ACF" /> crosses the diagonal <InlineMath math="BH" /> at <InlineMath math="2\sqrt3" /> from <InlineMath math="B" />, which is **one third of the diagonal**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt3\text{ cm}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="B" />, the corner that gets cut off. The one-third rule only works for that corner, **not for <InlineMath math="H" /> on the far side**.
- <InlineMath math="3\sqrt3\text{ cm}" /> assumes the plane <InlineMath math="ACF" /> goes through the centre of the cube, cutting the diagonal in half. It cuts it at one third.
- <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" /> takes the area of <InlineMath math="ACF" /> as if it were a right triangle, <InlineMath math="\frac12\left(6\sqrt2\right)^2=36" />, and then gets <InlineMath math="\frac{3\cdot72}{36}=6" />. The triangle <InlineMath math="ACF" /> is equilateral.
- <InlineMath math="\frac43\sqrt3\text{ cm}" /> uses <InlineMath math="\frac{V}{\text{area}}" /> without the <InlineMath math="3" />. From <InlineMath math="V=\frac13\cdot\text{area}\cdot d" />, the height is <InlineMath math="d=\frac{3V}{\text{area}}" />.

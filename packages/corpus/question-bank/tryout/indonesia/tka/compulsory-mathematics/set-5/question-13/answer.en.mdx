# The result of the dilation

The question asks for the area of the part of the final triangle that the original triangle doesn't cover. So you need two things: **the area of the final triangle**, and **where the original triangle sits** relative to it.

A dilation with centre <InlineMath math="A" /> and factor <InlineMath math="-3" /> multiplies every vector from <InlineMath math="A" /> by <InlineMath math="-3" />. The point <InlineMath math="A" /> itself doesn't move:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\vec{AB}=(2,0)&\ \Rightarrow\ -3(2,0)=(-6,0),\quad B'=(-5,1) \\
\vec{AC}=(0,3)&\ \Rightarrow\ -3(0,3)=(0,-9),\quad C'=(1,-8)
\end{aligned}" />

The minus sign puts triangle <InlineMath math="AB'C'" /> on the opposite side of <InlineMath math="A" />. At this stage it only touches <InlineMath math="ABC" /> at the point <InlineMath math="A" />.

# The result of the rotation

A <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation about <InlineMath math="A" /> reverses every vector from <InlineMath math="A" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(-6,0)&\ \Rightarrow\ (6,0),\quad B''=(7,1) \\
(0,-9)&\ \Rightarrow\ (0,9),\quad C''=(1,10)
\end{aligned}" />

This rotation **reverses again the direction that the negative factor reversed**. The end result is the same as a dilation about <InlineMath math="A" /> with factor <InlineMath math="+3" />, so the final triangle is back on the same side as <InlineMath math="ABC" />.

# The area you want

Triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> has a right angle at <InlineMath math="A" /> with legs <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />. Every side is multiplied by <InlineMath math="3" />, so **the area is multiplied by <InlineMath math="3^2=9" />**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{area }ABC&=\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot3=3 \\
\text{area }A''B''C''&=\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot9=27=9\cdot3
\end{aligned}" />

Both triangles share the right angle at <InlineMath math="A" /> with legs pointing the same way, and <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is smaller. Check with the hypotenuse of the final triangle, the line <InlineMath math="3x+2y=23" />: the points <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="C" /> both give <InlineMath math="11\le23" />, so both are inside. The whole of <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is covered by the final triangle:

<BlockMath math="27-3=24" />

So the area inside triangle <InlineMath math="A''B''C''" /> not covered by triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is <InlineMath math="24" /> square units.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="27" /> is the whole area of the final triangle. You get it if you think the factor <InlineMath math="-3" /> leaves the final triangle **on the opposite side** of <InlineMath math="ABC" />, but the <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation brings it back, so <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is covered.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> multiplies the area by <InlineMath math="3" />, giving <InlineMath math="9-3" />. Area changes by the square of the scale factor.
- <InlineMath math="48" /> forgets the <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> in the triangle area: <InlineMath math="6\cdot9-2\cdot3" />.
- <InlineMath math="78" /> multiplies the area by <InlineMath math="3^3=27" />, giving <InlineMath math="81-3" />. The cube is for volume, not area.

# Two angles around the altitude

The altitude <InlineMath math="CD" /> splits triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> into two right triangles, <InlineMath math="ACD" /> and <InlineMath math="BCD" />, both with the right angle at <InlineMath math="D" />. You'll write <InlineMath math="AD" /> and <InlineMath math="DB" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="CD" />, then use the midpoint <InlineMath math="M" /> to find the actual lengths.

In triangle <InlineMath math="ACD" />, the side opposite angle <InlineMath math="ACD" /> is <InlineMath math="AD" />, and the side next to that angle is <InlineMath math="CD" />. So <InlineMath math="\tan\angle ACD=\frac{AD}{CD}=2" />, which means <InlineMath math="AD=2\,CD" />.

Angles <InlineMath math="ACD" /> and <InlineMath math="BCD" /> together make the right angle at <InlineMath math="C" />, so **<InlineMath math="\angle BCD=90^\circ-\angle ACD" /> and its tangent is the reciprocal of <InlineMath math="2" />**. In triangle <InlineMath math="BCD" />, the side opposite angle <InlineMath math="BCD" /> is <InlineMath math="DB" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tan\angle BCD&=\frac{DB}{CD}=\frac12\quad\Rightarrow\quad CD=2\,DB \\
AD&=2\,CD=4\,DB
\end{aligned}" />

# Using the midpoint

Let <InlineMath math="DB=k" />. Then <InlineMath math="CD=2k" />, <InlineMath math="AD=4k" />, and <InlineMath math="AB=5k" />. The midpoint <InlineMath math="M" /> is <InlineMath math="AM=2.5k" /> from <InlineMath math="A" />. Because <InlineMath math="AD=4k" /> is longer than <InlineMath math="AM" />, **the point <InlineMath math="D" /> lies between <InlineMath math="M" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
DM&=AD-AM=4k-2.5k=1.5k \\
1.5k&=6\quad\Rightarrow\quad k=4
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking the lengths

With <InlineMath math="k=4" /> you get <InlineMath math="DB=4" />, <InlineMath math="CD=8" />, <InlineMath math="AD=16" />, and <InlineMath math="AB=20" />. Check this in two ways. First, <InlineMath math="MB=10" /> and <InlineMath math="DM=MB-DB=10-4=6" />, as given. Second, **the triangle really has its right angle at <InlineMath math="C" />**:

<BlockMath math="AC^2+BC^2=(16^2+8^2)+(4^2+8^2)=320+80=400=20^2=AB^2" />

So the length of <InlineMath math="DB" /> is <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="8\text{ cm}" /> is the altitude <InlineMath math="CD" />, not <InlineMath math="DB" />.
- <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> is <InlineMath math="MB" />, half of <InlineMath math="AB" />. The point <InlineMath math="D" /> isn't the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AB" />.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" /> uses the ratio <InlineMath math="AD:DB=2:1" />, as if the tangent applied only once. In fact **the ratio <InlineMath math="2" /> shows up twice**, in <InlineMath math="AD:CD" /> and in <InlineMath math="CD:DB" />, so <InlineMath math="AD:DB=4:1" />.
- <InlineMath math="16\text{ cm}" /> swaps the opposite and adjacent sides of angle <InlineMath math="ACD" />, giving <InlineMath math="CD=2\,AD" />. The long piece then moves next to <InlineMath math="B" />, and the length you get is really <InlineMath math="AD" />.

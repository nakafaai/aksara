# The fraction inequality

The question asks how many integers satisfy two conditions at once. So solve each inequality on its own, then take the overlap.

Multiply both sides of the first inequality by <InlineMath math="12" />, the least common multiple of <InlineMath math="3" />, <InlineMath math="4" />, and <InlineMath math="6" />. Since <InlineMath math="12" /> is positive, the sign stays the same:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
4(2x+5)-3(2x-3)&>2(4x-5) \\
8x+20-6x+9&>8x-10 \\
2x+29&>8x-10 \\
-6x&>-39
\end{aligned}" />

Now divide both sides by <InlineMath math="-6" />. **Dividing by a negative number flips the sign** from <InlineMath math=">" /> to <InlineMath math="<" />:

<BlockMath math="x<\frac{-39}{-6}=\frac{13}{2}=6.5" />

# The absolute value inequality

The form <InlineMath math="|u|\le v" /> means <InlineMath math="-v\le u\le v" />. Here <InlineMath math="u=3x-4" /> and <InlineMath math="v=x+12" />, so there are **two conditions that must hold together**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
-(x+12)\le3x-4\ &\Rightarrow\ -x-12\le3x-4\ \Rightarrow\ -8\le4x\ \Rightarrow\ x\ge-2 \\
3x-4\le x+12\ &\Rightarrow\ 2x\le16\ \Rightarrow\ x\le8
\end{aligned}" />

That gives <InlineMath math="-2\le x\le8" />. If you prefer cases, the case <InlineMath math="x\ge\frac43" /> gives the bound <InlineMath math="x\le8" />, and the case <InlineMath math="x<\frac43" /> gives <InlineMath math="4-3x\le x+12" />, that is <InlineMath math="x\ge-2" />. You need both cases. Check the left end: for <InlineMath math="x=-2" />, <InlineMath math="|{-10}|=10" /> and <InlineMath math="x+12=10" />, so <InlineMath math="x=-2" /> counts because the sign is <InlineMath math="\le" />.

# The overlap and its integers

Put both results together:

<BlockMath math="-2\le x<6.5" />

The integers are <InlineMath math="-2,-1,0,1,2,3,4,5,6" />, which is **nine numbers**. Check the right end in the first inequality: for <InlineMath math="x=6" /> the left side is <InlineMath math="\frac{17}{3}-\frac94=\frac{41}{12}" /> and the right side is <InlineMath math="\frac{19}{6}=\frac{38}{12}" />, so it works. For <InlineMath math="x=7" /> the left side is <InlineMath math="\frac{43}{12}" /> and the right side is <InlineMath math="\frac{46}{12}" />, so it fails.

So <InlineMath math="9" /> integers satisfy both inequalities.

- <InlineMath math="5" /> only uses the case <InlineMath math="3x-4\ge0" />, so it starts at <InlineMath math="x\ge\frac43" /> and loses <InlineMath math="-2,-1,0,1" />.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> writes <InlineMath math="-(x+12)" /> as <InlineMath math="-x+12" />, which turns the left bound into <InlineMath math="x\ge4" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> forgets to flip the sign when dividing by <InlineMath math="-6" />, gets <InlineMath math="x>6.5" />, and keeps only <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="8" />.

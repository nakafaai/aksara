# The central angle from the chord's distance

All three statements depend on one fact: the size of the central angle <InlineMath math="AOB" />. So find that angle first from the radius and the chord's distance to the centre.

Draw the line from <InlineMath math="O" /> perpendicular to <InlineMath math="AB" />, meeting it at <InlineMath math="M" />. This line **cuts the chord into two equal halves**, so triangle <InlineMath math="OMA" /> has a right angle at <InlineMath math="M" />, with <InlineMath math="OA=10" /> and <InlineMath math="OM=5" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AM&=\sqrt{10^2-5^2}=5\sqrt3,\qquad AB=10\sqrt3 \\
\cos\angle AOM&=\frac{OM}{OA}=\frac{5}{10}=\frac12\ \Rightarrow\ \angle AOM=60^\circ \\
\angle AOB&=2\cdot60^\circ=120^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

# The first statement

A tangent is always perpendicular to the radius at its point of tangency, so triangle <InlineMath math="OAP" /> has a right angle at <InlineMath math="A" />. The picture is symmetric, so <InlineMath math="P" /> lies on the extension of <InlineMath math="OM" />, and <InlineMath math="\angle AOP=\angle AOM=60^\circ" />:

<BlockMath math="OP=\frac{OA}{\cos60^\circ}=\frac{10}{\frac12}=20\text{ cm}" />

If you wrongly took the central angle as <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, you'd get <InlineMath math="\frac{10}{\cos30^\circ}\approx11.5" />. With the right angle, **<InlineMath math="OP" /> is exactly <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" />**. The statement is **True**.

# The second statement

The minor arc <InlineMath math="AB" /> belongs to the central angle <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />, which is a third of the circle:

<BlockMath math="\text{minor arc }AB=\frac{120}{360}\cdot2\pi\cdot10=\frac{20\pi}{3}" />

Since <InlineMath math="\pi>3" />, you get <InlineMath math="\frac{20\pi}{3}>\frac{20\cdot3}{3}=20" />. The arc is about <InlineMath math="20.9\text{ cm}" />, **more than** <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" />, even though the chord is only <InlineMath math="10\sqrt3\approx17.3\text{ cm}" />. The statement is **False**.

# The third statement

A point <InlineMath math="C" /> with <InlineMath math="CA=CB" /> lies on the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AB" />, which is the line <InlineMath math="OM" />. That line meets the circle at **two points**: <InlineMath math="C_1" /> on the far side of the chord and <InlineMath math="C_2" /> on the near side. The height of the triangle over the base <InlineMath math="AB" /> is different for each:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
C_1&:\ \text{height}=10+5=15,\quad \text{area}=\tfrac12\cdot10\sqrt3\cdot15=75\sqrt3 \\
C_2&:\ \text{height}=10-5=5,\quad \text{area}=\tfrac12\cdot10\sqrt3\cdot5=25\sqrt3
\end{aligned}" />

The area can be <InlineMath math="75\sqrt3" /> or <InlineMath math="25\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2" />, so the word *pasti* (certainly) in the statement doesn't fit. The statement is **False**.

So the answers are True, False, False.

To find the image of a line, write the original point in terms of the image coordinates, then **substitute into the original equation**.

The translation by <InlineMath math="T" /> maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(x+1,y-2)" />. So if the image is <InlineMath math="(x,y)" />, the original point is <InlineMath math="(x-1,y+2)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2(x-1)-(y+2)+3&=0 \\
2x-y-1&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Reflecting in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(x,-y)" />, so replace <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="-y" />.

<BlockMath math="2x-(-y)-1=0\ \Rightarrow\ 2x+y-1=0" />

Check with the point <InlineMath math="(0,3)" /> on <InlineMath math="g" />: the translation gives <InlineMath math="(1,1)" />, and the reflection gives <InlineMath math="(1,-1)" />. That point satisfies <InlineMath math="2(1)+(-1)-1=0" />.

So the final image has the equation <InlineMath math="2x+y-1=0" />.

- <InlineMath math="2x-y-1=0" /> is only the translated line, the reflection hasn't been done.
- <InlineMath math="2x+y+1=0" /> gets the sign of the constant wrong when replacing <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="-y" />.
- <InlineMath math="2x+y+3=0" /> reflects first and translates afterwards.
- <InlineMath math="2x+y+7=0" /> translates the opposite way, replacing <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="x+1" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="y-2" />.

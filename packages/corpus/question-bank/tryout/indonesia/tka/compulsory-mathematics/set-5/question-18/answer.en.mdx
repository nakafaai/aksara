# The upright side from the area

The perimeter needs the length <InlineMath math="AD" />, but you're only given <InlineMath math="AB" /> and the area of the plate. So find <InlineMath math="AD" /> from the area first, then build the perimeter.

The area of the plate is the area of the rectangle, **plus the two small semicircles attached on top, minus the big semicircle cut out**. The small semicircles have radius <InlineMath math="7" />, and the big one has radius <InlineMath math="14" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{area}&=28\cdot AD+2\cdot\tfrac12\pi\cdot7^2-\tfrac12\pi\cdot14^2 \\
&=28\cdot AD+49\pi-98\pi=28\cdot AD-49\pi \\
28\cdot AD-49\pi&=1{,}120-49\pi\quad\Rightarrow\quad AD=40\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Check the shape. The cut-out semicircle reaches <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" /> into the plate, well below <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}" />, so the cut doesn't break through the top side.

# Which parts form the edge

The perimeter is the length of the plate's outer edge only. The side <InlineMath math="DC" /> is covered by the two attached semicircles, so it lies inside the plate. The side <InlineMath math="AB" /> was thrown away with the cut-out piece. **Neither of them is part of the edge**, so they don't count.

Half the circumference of a circle is <InlineMath math="\pi r" />. Add up every piece of the edge:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AD+BC&=40+40=80 \\
\text{two small arcs}&=2\cdot\pi\cdot7=14\pi \\
\text{big arc}&=\pi\cdot14=14\pi \\
\text{perimeter}&=80+14\pi+14\pi=80+28\pi
\end{aligned}" />

So the perimeter of the plate is <InlineMath math="(80+28\pi)\text{ cm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="136\text{ cm}" /> assumes that adding and cutting semicircles doesn't change the perimeter, so it equals the rectangle's perimeter <InlineMath math="2(28+40)" />. In fact **the arcs replace straight segments**, and the arcs are <InlineMath math="28\pi\approx88" /> long, more than <InlineMath math="56" />.
- <InlineMath math="(80+21\pi)\text{ cm}" /> flips the signs in the area: it adds the cut-out semicircle and subtracts the attached ones. That gives <InlineMath math="28\cdot AD=1{,}120-98\pi" />, so <InlineMath math="AD=40-3.5\pi" />.
- <InlineMath math="\left(80+\tfrac{49}{2}\pi\right)\text{ cm}" /> treats the plate's area as just the rectangle, <InlineMath math="28\cdot AD=1{,}120-49\pi" />, so <InlineMath math="AD=40-1.75\pi" />.
- <InlineMath math="(136+28\pi)\text{ cm}" /> also counts the sides <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="DC" />, but one lies inside the plate and the other outside it, not on its edge.

The question asks for the curved area on the inside of the wall. That is the lateral surface of a cylinder with the inner radius and a height equal to the length of the culvert. The outer surface and the two end circles are not included.

The inner radius is the outer radius minus the **wall thickness**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
r&=35-7=28\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

All units must match, so convert the length to centimetres, <InlineMath math="\ell=1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}" />. The lateral area of a cylinder is <InlineMath math="2\pi r\ell" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L&=2\cdot\frac{22}{7}\cdot28\cdot100=17{,}600\text{ cm}^2
\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}^2=10{,}000\text{ cm}^2" />, <InlineMath math="17{,}600\text{ cm}^2=1.76\text{ m}^2" />.

- <InlineMath math="0.44\text{ m}^2" /> uses the wall thickness, 7 cm, as the radius.
- <InlineMath math="0.88\text{ m}^2" /> forgets the factor 2 in the lateral area formula.
- <InlineMath math="2.2\text{ m}^2" /> uses the outer radius, 35 cm, so it measures the outside surface.
- <InlineMath math="3.52\text{ m}^2" /> uses the diameter, 56 cm, as the radius.

So the curved inner surface of the culvert is 1.76 m².

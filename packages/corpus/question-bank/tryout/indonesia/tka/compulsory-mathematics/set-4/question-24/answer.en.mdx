A five-number summary (*statistik lima serangkai*) gives only five values for each group: the minimum, the lower quartile, the median, the upper quartile, and the maximum. A statement that depends only on these five values can be judged directly. A statement with the word **certainly** (*pasti*) is already false if one data set matches the summaries but breaks the statement.

#### Spread and position of the data

The interquartile range is **the upper quartile minus the lower quartile**, the width of the box in the plot. Let <InlineMath math="J_A" /> and <InlineMath math="J_B" /> be the interquartile ranges of medium A and medium B.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
J_A&=Q_3-Q_1=15-12=3\text{ cm} \\
J_B&=Q_3-Q_1=15-13=2\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

The median of medium B is <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" />. With <InlineMath math="40" /> ordered values <InlineMath math="x_1\le x_2\le\dots\le x_{40}" />, the median is the mean of the twentieth and twenty-first values. The twenty-first value is not smaller than the twentieth, so it is at least the mean of the two.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{x_{20}+x_{21}}{2}&=14 \\
x_{21}&\ge\frac{x_{20}+x_{21}}{2}=14
\end{aligned}" />

So the values in positions <InlineMath math="21" /> through <InlineMath math="40" />, which are <InlineMath math="20" /> seedlings or half of the group, are at least <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" /> tall.

#### A mean that is not determined

The table shows one data set that matches both summaries. It shows that **a five-number summary does not determine the mean**.

| Height <InlineMath math="(\text{cm})" /> | Seedlings in medium A | Seedlings in medium B |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| <InlineMath math="10" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| <InlineMath math="11" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| <InlineMath math="12" /> | <InlineMath math="10" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| <InlineMath math="13" /> | <InlineMath math="10" /> | <InlineMath math="19" /> |
| <InlineMath math="15" /> | <InlineMath math="10" /> | <InlineMath math="19" /> |
| <InlineMath math="17" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| <InlineMath math="19" /> | <InlineMath math="9" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| Total | <InlineMath math="40" /> | <InlineMath math="40" /> |

Check the summaries. In medium A, value <InlineMath math="1" /> is <InlineMath math="10" />, values <InlineMath math="2" /> through <InlineMath math="11" /> are <InlineMath math="12" />, values <InlineMath math="12" /> through <InlineMath math="21" /> are <InlineMath math="13" />, values <InlineMath math="22" /> through <InlineMath math="31" /> are <InlineMath math="15" />, and the rest are <InlineMath math="19" />. So <InlineMath math="Q_1=\frac{x_{10}+x_{11}}{2}=12" />, the median is <InlineMath math="\frac{x_{20}+x_{21}}{2}=13" />, and <InlineMath math="Q_3=\frac{x_{30}+x_{31}}{2}=15" />. In medium B, value <InlineMath math="1" /> is <InlineMath math="11" />, values <InlineMath math="2" /> through <InlineMath math="20" /> are <InlineMath math="13" />, values <InlineMath math="21" /> through <InlineMath math="39" /> are <InlineMath math="15" />, and value <InlineMath math="40" /> is <InlineMath math="17" />. So <InlineMath math="Q_1=13" />, the median is <InlineMath math="\frac{13+15}{2}=14" />, and <InlineMath math="Q_3=15" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\bar x_A&=\frac{10+10\cdot12+10\cdot13+10\cdot15+9\cdot19}{40}=\frac{581}{40}=14.525 \\
\bar x_B&=\frac{11+19\cdot13+19\cdot15+17}{40}=\frac{560}{40}=14
\end{aligned}" />

In this example the mean of medium A is actually larger than the mean of medium B, even though both five-number summaries are exactly the ones in the problem.

#### Judging each statement

1. The statement that the interquartile range of medium A is larger is true, because <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}>2\text{ cm}" />.
2. The statement that the mean of medium A is certainly smaller is false. The data set above matches the summaries, but **the mean of medium A is larger**, <InlineMath math="14.525\text{ cm}" /> against <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" />.
3. The statement that at least half of the seedlings in medium B are <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" /> or taller is true, because the values in positions <InlineMath math="21" /> through <InlineMath math="40" /> in medium B are at least <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" />.
4. The statement that the median of medium A is larger is false, because the median of medium A is <InlineMath math="13\text{ cm}" />, smaller than the median of medium B, <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" />.
5. The statement that the shortest seedling grows in medium A is true. The shortest seedling in medium A is <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> tall, while every seedling in medium B is at least <InlineMath math="11\text{ cm}" /> tall.

The true statements are the ones about the interquartile range, about at least half of the seedlings in medium B reaching <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" />, and about the shortest seedling growing in medium A.

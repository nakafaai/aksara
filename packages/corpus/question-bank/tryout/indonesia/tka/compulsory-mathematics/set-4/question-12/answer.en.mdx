The top entry of the vector <InlineMath math="T" /> is the horizontal shift and the bottom entry is the vertical shift. So this translation **moves every point <InlineMath math="2" /> units to the left and <InlineMath math="3" /> units up**, <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(x-2,\,y+3)" />. A translation does not change the shape of the parabola, so it is enough to follow the vertex. First rewrite the equation in vertex form by completing the square.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y&=x^2-4x+1 \\
&=\left(x^2-4x+4\right)-4+1 \\
&=(x-2)^2-3
\end{aligned}" />

The vertex of the original parabola is <InlineMath math="(2,-3)" />. After the translation, the vertex is <InlineMath math="(2-2,\,-3+3)=(0,0)" />. The coefficient of <InlineMath math="x^2" /> stays <InlineMath math="1" />, so the image is the parabola <InlineMath math="y=x^2" />.

Check by substitution. A point <InlineMath math="(x',y')" /> on the image comes from the point <InlineMath math="(x'+2,\,y'-3)" /> on the original graph. That original point satisfies the original equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y'-3&=(x'+2)^2-4(x'+2)+1 \\
&=x'^2+4x'+4-4x'-8+1 \\
&=x'^2-3 \\
y'&=x'^2
\end{aligned}" />

The equation of the image is therefore <InlineMath math="y=x^2" />.

- <InlineMath math="y=x^2-6" /> comes from applying the vertical part in the wrong direction, as if the graph moved <InlineMath math="3" /> units down, which puts the vertex at <InlineMath math="(0,-6)" />.
- <InlineMath math="y=x^2+8" /> comes from substituting <InlineMath math="x+2" /> only into the <InlineMath math="x^2" /> term and leaving the <InlineMath math="-4x" /> term unchanged.
- <InlineMath math="y=x^2-8x+10" /> comes from reversing both parts, <InlineMath math="2" /> units to the right and <InlineMath math="3" /> units down, which puts the vertex at <InlineMath math="(4,-6)" />.
- <InlineMath math="y=x^2-8x+16" /> comes from reversing only the horizontal part, <InlineMath math="2" /> units to the right, which puts the vertex at <InlineMath math="(4,0)" />.

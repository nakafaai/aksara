Look at a panel from the side. The tilted side of the panel, its projection on the roof, and the vertical segment from the top edge to the roof form a right triangle with hypotenuse <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ m}" /> and angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> at the bottom edge. **The horizontal projection uses the cosine**, and **the height uses the sine**. The angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> is acute, so <InlineMath math="\sin\theta" /> is positive.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta&=\sqrt{1-\cos^2\theta}=\sqrt{1-0.9216}=\sqrt{0.0784}=0.28 \\
p&=2.5\cos\theta=2.5\cdot0.96=2.4\text{ m} \\
t&=2.5\sin\theta=2.5\cdot0.28=0.7\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Here <InlineMath math="p" /> is the horizontal projection of the panel and <InlineMath math="t" /> is the height of its top edge above the roof. The smallest horizontal gap from the top edge of one panel to the bottom edge of the next is <InlineMath math="c=3t" />. The distance between the bottom edges of two consecutive rows is the projection of the panel plus that gap.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
c&=3t=3\cdot0.7=2.1\text{ m} \\
d&=p+c=2.4+2.1=4.5\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Check the side triangle with the Pythagorean theorem: <InlineMath math="2.4^2+0.7^2=5.76+0.49=6.25=2.5^2" />. It is the <InlineMath math="7" />, <InlineMath math="24" />, <InlineMath math="25" /> triangle scaled down by a factor of ten.

The smallest horizontal distance between the bottom edges of two consecutive rows is therefore <InlineMath math="4.5\text{ m}" />.

- <InlineMath math="2.1\text{ m}" /> is only the gap between the top edge of the front panel and the bottom edge of the panel behind it, without the projection of the panel itself.
- <InlineMath math="3.1\text{ m}" /> is the projection plus the height, <InlineMath math="2.4+0.7" />, which forgets the factor three in the gap rule.
- <InlineMath math="4.6\text{ m}" /> appears when the panel length <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ m}" /> is used directly as the horizontal projection, although the panel is tilted and its projection is <InlineMath math="2.5\cos\theta" />.
- <InlineMath math="7.9\text{ m}" /> appears when the sine and cosine are swapped, so the height is taken as <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ m}" /> and the projection as <InlineMath math="0.7\text{ m}" />.

# The first statement

The key words are **pasti untung**, "certainly makes a profit". One customer who doesn't profit is enough to make the statement false. The May buyback price is higher than the February buy price, by <InlineMath math="1{,}300{,}000-1{,}200{,}000=100{,}000" /> per gram. But the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}25{,}000" /> fee per transaction doesn't shrink when the amount of gold is small.

Take a customer who buys <InlineMath math="0.2" /> gram in February, which costs <InlineMath math="0.2\cdot1{,}200{,}000=240{,}000" />. This weight is a multiple of <InlineMath math="0.1" /> gram, so it can all be sold.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{money received in May}&=0.2\cdot1{,}300{,}000-25{,}000=235{,}000 \\
235{,}000&<240{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

This customer loses <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" />. In general there's a profit only when <InlineMath math="100{,}000\,m>25{,}000" />, that is <InlineMath math="m>0.25" /> gram. At <InlineMath math="0.1" /> and <InlineMath math="0.2" /> gram the customer loses money. The statement is **False**.

# The second statement

First, find Sari's gold and the money she paid.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{2{,}500{,}000}{1{,}250{,}000}+\frac{2{,}400{,}000}{1{,}200{,}000}&=2+2=4\text{ grams} \\
\text{money paid}&=2{,}500{,}000+2{,}400{,}000=4{,}900{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

Now work out what Sari would receive if she sold all <InlineMath math="4" /> grams at once in each month. **The <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}25{,}000" /> fee has to come off**.

| Month of sale | Money received | Compared with <InlineMath math="4{,}900{,}000" /> |
| --- | --- | --- |
| March | <InlineMath math="4\cdot1{,}180{,}000-25{,}000=4{,}695{,}000" /> | loss |
| April | <InlineMath math="4\cdot1{,}230{,}000-25{,}000=4{,}895{,}000" /> | loss of <InlineMath math="5{,}000" /> |
| May | <InlineMath math="4\cdot1{,}300{,}000-25{,}000=5{,}175{,}000" /> | profit |

April almost breaks even. Without the fee, April would bring <InlineMath math="4{,}920{,}000" /> and look like a profit, but the fee turns it into a loss. The first month without a loss really is May. The statement is **True**.

# The third statement

Let the weight sold be <InlineMath math="m" /> grams, with <InlineMath math="m" /> at least <InlineMath math="0.1" />. Compare the two percent increases.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{money in April}&=1{,}230{,}000\,m-25{,}000 \\
\text{money in May}&=1{,}300{,}000\,m-25{,}000 \\
\text{rise in money}&=\frac{70{,}000\,m}{1{,}230{,}000\,m-25{,}000} \\
\text{rise in buyback price}&=\frac{70{,}000}{1{,}230{,}000}=\frac{70{,}000\,m}{1{,}230{,}000\,m}
\end{aligned}" />

Both fractions have the same numerator. The denominator for the money is smaller because <InlineMath math="25{,}000" /> is subtracted, and it's still positive, since for <InlineMath math="m\ge0.1" /> it's at least <InlineMath math="123{,}000-25{,}000=98{,}000" />. **A smaller positive denominator makes a bigger fraction**. The statement is **True**.

The question asks how many days have an income close to the mean, at a distance of **less than** one standard deviation. Plan two moves: find the mean and standard deviation of the new income, then translate that condition back into cups in the table.

# Mean and standard deviation of the income

The price rises by <InlineMath math="20\%" />, so the new price is <InlineMath math="1.2\cdot\text{Rp}5{,}000=\text{Rp}6{,}000" />. On a day with <InlineMath math="x" /> cups sold, the income is <InlineMath math="P=6{,}000x+30{,}000" />.

Every value is multiplied by <InlineMath math="6{,}000" /> and then increased by <InlineMath math="30{,}000" />. The mean gets both the multiplication and the addition. The standard deviation only gets the multiplication, because **adding the same number to every value doesn't change how spread out the data are**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\bar{P}&=6{,}000\cdot40+30{,}000=270{,}000 \\
S_P&=6{,}000\cdot6=36{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

# Back to cups

The condition is that the income differs from the mean by less than <InlineMath math="36{,}000" />. Rewrite it in terms of <InlineMath math="x" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\left|6{,}000x+30{,}000-270{,}000\right|&<36{,}000 \\
\left|6{,}000x-240{,}000\right|&<36{,}000 \\
|x-40|&<6 \\
34<x&<46
\end{aligned}" />

That's the same as **less than one standard deviation in the cup data**, with mean <InlineMath math="40" /> and standard deviation <InlineMath math="6" />. Multiplying by a positive number and adding a fixed amount doesn't move any day's position when you measure it in standard deviations.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="5" /> days comes from taking the mean income as <InlineMath math="6{,}000\cdot40=240{,}000" /> without the fixed amount, even though each day's income includes it. The condition shifts to <InlineMath math="29<x<41" />.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> days also counts the two days with <InlineMath math="46" /> cups. Their gap is exactly one standard deviation, and the question asks for **less than**.
- <InlineMath math="7" /> days uses a standard deviation of <InlineMath math="1.2^2\cdot30{,}000=43{,}200" />, as if the factor <InlineMath math="1.2" /> were applied twice. The condition widens to <InlineMath math="|x-40|<7.2" />.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> days adds <InlineMath math="30{,}000" /> to the standard deviation, so <InlineMath math="S_P=66{,}000" /> and the condition becomes <InlineMath math="|x-40|<11" />. The same extra amount every day doesn't widen the spread.

# The new standard deviation

Find **how far each new value is from the new mean**, <InlineMath math="42" />, square it, and add.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{differences}&:\ -1,-4,4,1,-6,3,-2,6,-5,4 \\
\textstyle\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2&=1+16+16+1+36+9+4+36+25+16=160 \\
S&=\sqrt{\tfrac{160}{10}}=4
\end{aligned}" />

Compare the old and new data day by day. **Only four bars rise by <InlineMath math="5" />, and they are the four lowest days**, so the new data cluster more tightly around the mean <InlineMath math="42" /> and the standard deviation drops.

# Judging each statement

1. **False**. The median rises from <InlineMath math="40.5" /> to <InlineMath math="42" />, because the two middle values change.
2. **True**. The mean becomes <InlineMath math="42" />.
3. **True**. The range drops from <InlineMath math="17" /> to <InlineMath math="12" />, because the smallest value rises from <InlineMath math="31" /> to <InlineMath math="36" /> while the largest stays <InlineMath math="48" />.
4. **True**. The new standard deviation is <InlineMath math="4" />, no longer <InlineMath math="6" />.
5. **True**. The lowest sales are now <InlineMath math="36" />, which is <InlineMath math="42-36=6" /> from the mean, and <InlineMath math="6>4" />. If you used the old standard deviation of <InlineMath math="6" />, the gap wouldn't be more than one standard deviation. That's the trap.

So the true statements are the second, third, fourth and fifth.

The shortest distance from a point to a line is **the length of the segment perpendicular to that line**. Any other segment from <InlineMath math="O" /> to the highway is longer, because it is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose leg is that perpendicular segment. So the new road is the altitude of triangle <InlineMath math="OAB" /> from <InlineMath math="O" /> to side <InlineMath math="AB" />.

The angle at <InlineMath math="O" /> is a right angle because <InlineMath math="A" /> lies on the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis and <InlineMath math="B" /> on the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis. Let <InlineMath math="d" /> be the length of the new road. Find <InlineMath math="AB" /> with the Pythagorean theorem, then write **the area of triangle <InlineMath math="OAB" /> with two pairs of base and height**: base <InlineMath math="OA" /> with height <InlineMath math="OB" />, and base <InlineMath math="AB" /> with height <InlineMath math="d" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AB&=\sqrt{12^2+9^2}=\sqrt{225}=15 \\
\frac12\cdot OA\cdot OB&=\frac12\cdot AB\cdot d \\
\frac12\cdot12\cdot9&=\frac12\cdot15\cdot d \\
54&=\frac{15}{2}\,d \\
d&=\frac{108}{15}=7.2
\end{aligned}" />

Check with a second method. The highway satisfies <InlineMath math="\frac{x}{12}+\frac{y}{9}=1" />, or <InlineMath math="3x+4y=36" />. The slope of <InlineMath math="AB" /> is <InlineMath math="-\frac34" />, so a segment perpendicular to <InlineMath math="AB" /> has slope <InlineMath math="\frac43" />. Its foot can be written as <InlineMath math="F(3k,4k)" />. Substitute it into the equation of the highway.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3(3k)+4(4k)&=36 \\
25k&=36 \\
k&=\frac{36}{25} \\
OF&=\sqrt{(3k)^2+(4k)^2}=5k=\frac{36}{5}=7.2
\end{aligned}" />

The new road is therefore <InlineMath math="7.2\text{ km}" /> long.

- <InlineMath math="3.6\text{ km}" /> comes from dividing the area <InlineMath math="54" /> directly by <InlineMath math="AB=15" />, although the area is half of base times height, so <InlineMath math="d=\frac{2\cdot54}{15}" />.
- <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ km}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="O" /> to the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AB" />, <InlineMath math="M\left(6,\frac92\right)" />. The midpoint is not the nearest point, because <InlineMath math="OM" /> is not perpendicular to the highway.
- <InlineMath math="9\text{ km}" /> is the length of a road along the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis to <InlineMath math="B" />. That road meets the highway at a slant, so it is longer than <InlineMath math="OF" />.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ km}" /> is the length of a road along the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis to <InlineMath math="A" />, which is also longer than the perpendicular road.

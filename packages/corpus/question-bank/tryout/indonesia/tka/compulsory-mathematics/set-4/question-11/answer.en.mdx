# Unfolding the box

The shortest path on the surface of a box is found by **unfolding the faces it crosses into one flat sheet** and drawing a straight line. On a flat sheet, a straight line is the shortest way.

Point <InlineMath math="G" /> is on the top, but the top is wet. So the ant has to climb using the side faces only. The best route crosses two neighbouring side faces, for example <InlineMath math="ABFE" /> and then <InlineMath math="BCGF" />. Unfolded, they make a rectangle <InlineMath math="15+9=24" /> wide and <InlineMath math="7" /> tall, with <InlineMath math="A" /> in a bottom corner and <InlineMath math="G" /> in the opposite top corner.

<BlockMath math="AG=\sqrt{(15+9)^2+7^2}=\sqrt{576+49}=\sqrt{625}=25" />

The route over <InlineMath math="ADHE" /> and then <InlineMath math="DCGH" /> gives a rectangle of the same size, <InlineMath math="9+15" /> by <InlineMath math="7" />, so it's also <InlineMath math="25" /> long.

# The forbidden routes

Without the wet top, the ant could save distance by cutting across the top, for example <InlineMath math="\sqrt{(9+7)^2+15^2}=\sqrt{481}" />, which is shorter than <InlineMath math="25" />. But **that route crosses the wet top**, so it isn't allowed.

So the shortest path for the ant is <InlineMath math="25\text{ cm}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{481}\text{ cm}" /> is the shortest route if the top could be used.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{565}\text{ cm}" /> also crosses the top, over a different side.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{355}\text{ cm}" /> is the space diagonal through the inside of the box, not a path on the surface.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{274}\text{ cm}" /> uses only the face <InlineMath math="ABFE" />, so it ends at <InlineMath math="F" />, not at <InlineMath math="G" />.

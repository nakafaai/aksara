The shortest new road from the post <InlineMath math="P" /> to the highway is the segment that is **perpendicular to the highway**. This segment meets the highway at one point. Any other point on the highway forms a right triangle with <InlineMath math="P" /> and that meeting point, so it is always farther from <InlineMath math="P" />. You only need the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to the line <InlineMath math="AB" />.

First find the slope of <InlineMath math="AB" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m&=\frac{1-9}{11-5}=\frac{-8}{6}=-\frac43
\end{aligned}" />

With the point <InlineMath math="A(5,9)" />, the line is <InlineMath math="y-9=-\frac43(x-5)" />. Multiply both sides by 3 and rearrange:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3y-27&=-4x+20 \\
4x+3y&=47
\end{aligned}" />

The distance from <InlineMath math="P(2,3)" /> to this line follows from the point-to-line distance formula:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{|4\cdot2+3\cdot3-47|}{\sqrt{4^2+3^2}}=\frac{|-30|}{\sqrt{25}}=\frac{30}{5}=6\text{ km}
\end{aligned}" />

Check the result with the foot of the perpendicular, <InlineMath math="F" />. It lies at <InlineMath math="P+t(4,3)" /> with <InlineMath math="t=\frac65" />, so <InlineMath math="F(6.8,\,6.6)" />. The vector <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PF}=(4.8,\,3.6)" /> has length <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4.8^2+3.6^2}=\sqrt{36}=6\text{ km}" />, which matches. The x-coordinate <InlineMath math="6.8" /> lies between <InlineMath math="5" /> and <InlineMath math="11" />, so <InlineMath math="F" /> is on the segment <InlineMath math="AB" />, and the new road really meets the highway there.

- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ km}" /> comes from dividing by 25, the square of 5, and forgetting the square root.
- <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ km}" /> comes from dividing by 4 instead of 5, which is a wrong value for the square root in the denominator.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{45}\text{ km}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="A" />. That segment is not perpendicular to the highway.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{85}\text{ km}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="B" />. That segment is not perpendicular to the highway either.

So the shortest new road from the post to the highway is 6 km long.

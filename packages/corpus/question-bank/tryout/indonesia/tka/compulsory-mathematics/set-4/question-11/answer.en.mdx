The shortest new road from the office <InlineMath math="O" /> to the highway is the segment that is **perpendicular to the highway**. Let that segment meet the highway at <InlineMath math="F" />. For any other point <InlineMath math="P" /> on the highway, triangle <InlineMath math="OFP" /> has a right angle at <InlineMath math="F" />, so <InlineMath math="OP^2=OF^2+FP^2" />. Therefore <InlineMath math="OP" /> is never shorter than <InlineMath math="OF" />. So you only need the length of <InlineMath math="OF" />.

Triangle <InlineMath math="OAB" /> has a right angle at <InlineMath math="O" />, because the two axes are perpendicular. By the Pythagorean theorem, the length of <InlineMath math="AB" /> is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AB&=\sqrt{OA^2+OB^2}=\sqrt{12^2+9^2}=\sqrt{225}=15\text{ km}
\end{aligned}" />

The area of triangle <InlineMath math="OAB" /> can be found from its legs, and also from the base <InlineMath math="AB" /> and the height <InlineMath math="OF" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac12\cdot OA\cdot OB&=\frac12\cdot AB\cdot OF \\
\frac12\cdot12\cdot9&=\frac12\cdot15\cdot OF \\
OF&=\frac{108}{15}=7.2\text{ km}
\end{aligned}" />

Before you use this result, check that <InlineMath math="F" /> lies on segment <InlineMath math="AB" />, not on its extension. Triangle <InlineMath math="AFO" /> is similar to triangle <InlineMath math="AOB" />, so <InlineMath math="AF=\frac{OA^2}{AB}=\frac{144}{15}=9.6\text{ km}" />. In the same way, <InlineMath math="FB=\frac{OB^2}{AB}=\frac{81}{15}=5.4\text{ km}" />. Both lengths are positive, so <InlineMath math="F" /> lies between <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />. As a check, <InlineMath math="OF^2=AF\cdot FB=9.6\cdot5.4=51.84=7.2^2" />.

- <InlineMath math="9\text{ km}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="O" /> to <InlineMath math="B" />. Segment <InlineMath math="OB" /> meets the highway at <InlineMath math="B" /> and is not perpendicular to it.

- <InlineMath math="15\text{ km}" /> is the length of the highway from <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="B" />. It is not a distance from <InlineMath math="O" />.
- <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ km}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="O" /> to the midpoint <InlineMath math="M" /> of <InlineMath math="AB" />. Because the angle at <InlineMath math="O" /> is a right angle, <InlineMath math="OM=\frac12AB=7.5\text{ km}" />.

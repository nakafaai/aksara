The natural domain holds every <InlineMath math="x" /> for which the formula makes sense. There are **two conditions**: the number under the root is not negative, and the denominator is not zero.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
12-3x\ge0\ &\Rightarrow\ x\le4 \\
x+1\ne0\ &\Rightarrow\ x\ne-1
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, <InlineMath math="f(4)=\frac{0}{5}=0" /> is defined, while <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> makes the denominator zero and <InlineMath math="x=5" /> makes the number under the root negative.

So the natural domain is <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\le4,\ x\ne-1\}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\le4\}" /> forgets that the denominator must not be zero.
- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x<4,\ x\ne-1\}" /> drops <InlineMath math="x=4" />, although the root of zero is defined.
- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\ge4\}" /> does not reverse the inequality when dividing by <InlineMath math="-3" />.
- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\ge-4,\ x\ne-1\}" /> gets a sign wrong when moving the <InlineMath math="12" />, which gives <InlineMath math="3x\ge-12" />.

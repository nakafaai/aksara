All four statements are judged from the graph. On each slanted piece, **the speed of the lift is the size of the height change divided by the duration of the piece**, and on each flat piece the lift stands still.

| Time interval | Motion of the lift | Speed |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| <InlineMath math="0\le t\le10" /> | rises from <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{20}{10}=2\text{ m/s}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="10\le t\le16" /> | stays at <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="0\text{ m/s}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="16\le t\le24" /> | falls from <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="8\text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{12}{8}=1.5\text{ m/s}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="24\le t\le28" /> | stays at <InlineMath math="8\text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="0\text{ m/s}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="28\le t\le40" /> | rises from <InlineMath math="8" /> to <InlineMath math="26\text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{18}{12}=1.5\text{ m/s}" /> |

For the height of <InlineMath math="14\text{ m}" />, write the height formula on each slanted piece and set it equal to <InlineMath math="14" />. On the first piece <InlineMath math="h=2t" />, on the third piece <InlineMath math="h=20-1.5(t-16)" />, and on the fifth piece <InlineMath math="h=8+1.5(t-28)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2t=14&\iff t=7 \\
20-1.5(t-16)=14&\iff t=20 \\
8+1.5(t-28)=14&\iff t=32
\end{aligned}" />

Each of these times lies inside the interval of its piece. On the two flat pieces the height stays at <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="8\text{ m}" />, so there is no other moment when the lift is at <InlineMath math="14\text{ m}" />.

For the last statement, let <InlineMath math="v_1" /> be the average speed on <InlineMath math="0\le t\le24" /> and <InlineMath math="v_2" /> the average speed on <InlineMath math="24\le t\le40" />. The distance in each interval is the sum of the distances on the pieces inside it.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v_1&=\frac{20+0+12}{24}=\frac{32}{24}=\frac43\text{ m/s} \\
v_2&=\frac{0+18}{16}=\frac{18}{16}=\frac98\text{ m/s}
\end{aligned}" />

1. The statement that the lift moves fastest during the first <InlineMath math="10" /> seconds is true. Its speed there is <InlineMath math="2\text{ m/s}" />, while on every other piece the speed is at most <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m/s}" />.
2. The statement that the lift is at a height of <InlineMath math="14\text{ m}" /> exactly three times is true, at <InlineMath math="t=7" />, <InlineMath math="t=20" />, and <InlineMath math="t=32" />.
3. The statement about the interval <InlineMath math="16\le t\le24" /> is false. On that interval the lift **goes down from <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> to <InlineMath math="8\text{ m}" />**, so its height decreases by <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> every second instead of increasing.
4. The statement that the two average speeds are equal is false, because <InlineMath math="\frac43\text{ m/s}\ne\frac98\text{ m/s}" />. The speed over the first <InlineMath math="24" /> seconds is larger, since <InlineMath math="\frac43=\frac{32}{24}" /> while <InlineMath math="\frac98=\frac{27}{24}" />.

So the first two statements are true, and the statements about the interval <InlineMath math="16\le t\le24" /> and about equal average speeds are false.

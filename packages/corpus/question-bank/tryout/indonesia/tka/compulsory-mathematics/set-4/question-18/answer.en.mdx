Mass equals density times volume, so you need **the volume of concrete in the wall only**, not the volume of the whole cylinder. The cross section of the concrete culvert (*gorong-gorong*) is a ring between an outer circle of radius <InlineMath math="R=35\text{ cm}" /> and an inner circle of radius <InlineMath math="r=35-7=28\text{ cm}" />. The density is in <InlineMath math="\text{g/cm}^3" />, so the length must also be in centimeters, <InlineMath math="\ell=1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}" />.

The area of the ring is the area of the outer circle minus the area of the inner circle. The difference of squares is easiest to find by factoring, <InlineMath math="35^2-28^2=(35-28)(35+28)=7\cdot63=441" />. The density of concrete is written <InlineMath math="\rho=2.5\text{ g/cm}^3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V&=\pi\left(R^2-r^2\right)\ell \\
&=\frac{22}{7}\cdot441\cdot100 \\
&=138{,}600\text{ cm}^3 \\
m&=\rho V=2.5\cdot138{,}600=346{,}500\text{ g}=346.5\text{ kg}
\end{aligned}" />

Check with a second method. If you unroll the wall into a slab, its length is the circumference of the middle circle of radius <InlineMath math="\frac{35+28}{2}=31.5\text{ cm}" />, its width is <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}" />, and its thickness is <InlineMath math="7\text{ cm}" />. This is exact because <InlineMath math="R^2-r^2=(R-r)(R+r)" />.

<BlockMath math="2\cdot\frac{22}{7}\cdot31.5\cdot7\cdot100=138{,}600\text{ cm}^3" />

One culvert therefore has a mass of <InlineMath math="346.5\text{ kg}" />.

- <InlineMath math="3.465\text{ kg}" /> appears when the length <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> is not converted to <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}" />, which leaves a volume of only <InlineMath math="1{,}386\text{ cm}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="38.5\text{ kg}" /> appears when the ring area is computed as <InlineMath math="\pi\cdot7^2" />, squaring the wall thickness, although the ring area is <InlineMath math="\pi\left(R^2-r^2\right)" />.
- <InlineMath math="138.6\text{ kg}" /> appears when the volume <InlineMath math="138{,}600\text{ cm}^3=138.6" /> liters is read directly as <InlineMath math="138.6\text{ kg}" />, as if concrete had the density of water, <InlineMath math="1\text{ g/cm}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="962.5\text{ kg}" /> is the mass of a solid cylinder of radius <InlineMath math="35\text{ cm}" /> with the hollow inside not subtracted.

This question uses two different volumes. The rise of the water tells you the **outer volume of the ball**, because the sealed hollow pushes water aside too. The mass of the weight comes only from the **volume of the metal**, which is the outer volume minus the hollow.

# Outer radius from the water rise

The displaced water forms a cylinder with radius <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" /> and height <InlineMath math="2\text{ cm}" />. Its volume **equals the volume of the outer ball** with radius <InlineMath math="R" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac43\pi R^3&=\pi\cdot12^2\cdot2=288\pi \\
R^3&=288\cdot\tfrac34=216 \\
R&=6\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

# Volume of the metal

The wall thickness of <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> runs from the outer surface to the inner surface, so the **radius of the hollow** is <InlineMath math="r=6-1=5\text{ cm}" />. The metal's volume is the difference of two ball volumes:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{metal}}&=\tfrac43\pi\left(R^3-r^3\right) \\
&=\tfrac43\pi\,(216-125) \\
&=\tfrac43\pi\cdot91=\tfrac{364}{3}\pi\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

# Mass of the weight

Mass is density times the volume of the metal:

<BlockMath math="m=6\cdot\tfrac{364}{3}\pi=728\pi\text{ grams}" />

Quick sense check: the average mass per unit of outer volume is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{728\pi}{288\pi}\approx2.5\text{ g/cm}^3" />. That's more than the density of water, <InlineMath math="1\text{ g/cm}^3" />, so the weight **really does sink**, just as the question says.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="397\pi\text{ grams}" /> subtracts the <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> thickness from the diameter <InlineMath math="12" /> only once, which makes the hollow's radius <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ cm}" />. The wall sits at both ends of a diameter, so the hollow's diameter is <InlineMath math="12-2\cdot1=10\text{ cm}" />.
- <InlineMath math="864\pi\text{ grams}" /> uses surface area times thickness, <InlineMath math="4\pi\cdot6^2\cdot1" />. That's only an estimate for a very thin wall.
- <InlineMath math="1{,}216\pi\text{ grams}" /> subtracts the thickness twice from the radius, giving <InlineMath math="r=4\text{ cm}" />. Twice the thickness only comes off the diameter.
- <InlineMath math="1{,}728\pi\text{ grams}" /> uses the outer volume <InlineMath math="288\pi" /> as the metal's volume, as if the displaced water were all metal. The hollow pushes water aside too, but **the hollow has no mass**.

So the mass of the weight is <InlineMath math="728\pi" /> grams.

One turn of the pedals turns the chainring (*gir depan*) once, so the chain passes <InlineMath math="48" /> teeth. The same <InlineMath math="48" /> teeth must pass the sprocket (*gir belakang*), which has only <InlineMath math="16" /> teeth per turn. So **the sprocket turns <InlineMath math="\frac{48}{16}=3" /> times** for each pedal turn. The rear wheel turns with it <InlineMath math="3" /> times, and each turn of the wheel moves the bicycle forward by the circumference of the wheel.

Let <InlineMath math="K" /> be the circumference of the wheel and <InlineMath math="s" /> the distance the bicycle travels for one pedal turn.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
K&=\pi d=\frac{22}{7}\cdot70=220\text{ cm} \\
s&=3\cdot220=660\text{ cm}=6.6\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

| Part | Motion for one pedal turn |
| --- | --- |
| Pedals and chainring | <InlineMath math="1" /> turn, the chain passes <InlineMath math="48" /> teeth |
| Sprocket | <InlineMath math="\frac{48}{16}=3" /> turns |
| Rear wheel | <InlineMath math="3" /> turns, together with the sprocket |
| Bicycle | <InlineMath math="3\cdot220\text{ cm}=660\text{ cm}" /> |

1. The statement that one pedal turn moves the bicycle <InlineMath math="6.6\text{ m}" /> is true, as the computation <InlineMath math="s=660\text{ cm}" /> shows.
2. The statement about <InlineMath math="600" /> pedal turns is false. The distance <InlineMath math="1.32\text{ km}=1{,}320\text{ m}" /> needs only <InlineMath math="\frac{1{,}320}{6.6}=200" /> pedal turns. The number <InlineMath math="600" /> is the number of wheel turns, <InlineMath math="200\cdot3" />, not pedal turns.
3. The statement about a <InlineMath math="24" />-tooth sprocket is false. With that sprocket the wheel turns <InlineMath math="\frac{48}{24}=2" /> times, so the bicycle moves <InlineMath math="2\cdot220=440\text{ cm}=4.4\text{ m}" />. So **a larger sprocket shortens the distance** per pedal turn. The value <InlineMath math="9.9\text{ m}" /> appears when <InlineMath math="6.6" /> is multiplied by <InlineMath math="\frac{24}{16}" />, which turns the ratio upside down.
4. The statement about a speed of <InlineMath math="23.76\text{ km/h}" /> is true. In one minute the bicycle travels <InlineMath math="60\cdot6.6=396\text{ m}" />, and one hour has <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v&=60\cdot6.6\text{ m/min}=396\text{ m/min} \\
&=396\cdot60\text{ m/h}=23{,}760\text{ m/h}=23.76\text{ km/h}
\end{aligned}" />

So the statements about the distance of <InlineMath math="6.6\text{ m}" /> and about the speed of <InlineMath math="23.76\text{ km/h}" /> are true, while the statements about <InlineMath math="600" /> turns and about a distance of <InlineMath math="9.9\text{ m}" /> are false.

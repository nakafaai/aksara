The cube from each log is limited by two things: **the circular cross-section and the length of the log**. The largest edge is the smaller of those two limits. Work it out for each log, then compare the volumes.

# The limit from the cross-section

One edge of the cube is parallel to the axis, so one face of the cube is a square perpendicular to the axis, and it has to fit inside the circular cross-section. The largest square inside a circle has all four corners on the circle, so **its diagonal equals the diameter**. For edge <InlineMath math="s" /> and diameter <InlineMath math="d" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
s\sqrt2&\le d\;\Rightarrow\;s\le\frac{d}{\sqrt2} \\
\text{log }P:\;s&\le\frac{50}{\sqrt2}=25\sqrt2\approx35.4\text{ cm} \\
\text{log }Q:\;s&\le\frac{40}{\sqrt2}=20\sqrt2\approx28.3\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

# The limit from the length

The edge that runs along the axis can't be longer than the log. So <InlineMath math="s\le30" /> for log <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="s\le80" /> for log <InlineMath math="Q" />. Take the smaller limit for each log:

| Log | Cross-section limit | Length limit | Largest edge |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="P" /> | <InlineMath math="25\sqrt2\approx35.4" /> | <InlineMath math="30" /> | <InlineMath math="30" /> |
| <InlineMath math="Q" /> | <InlineMath math="20\sqrt2\approx28.3" /> | <InlineMath math="80" /> | <InlineMath math="20\sqrt2" /> |

For log <InlineMath math="P" />, **the length is what limits the cube**, not the circle. A square with side <InlineMath math="30" /> still fits, because its diagonal is <InlineMath math="30\sqrt2\approx42.4" />, which is less than the diameter <InlineMath math="50" />.

# Comparing the two cubes

Now find the volume of the largest cube from each log, using the edges in the table.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_P&=30^3=27{,}000\text{ cm}^3 \\
V_Q&=\left(20\sqrt2\right)^3=8{,}000\cdot2\sqrt2=16{,}000\sqrt2\approx22{,}627\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

Log <InlineMath math="Q" /> is longer, but its cross-section is smaller, so its cube comes out smaller. **The largest cube comes from log <InlineMath math="P" />**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="15{,}625\text{ cm}^3" /> uses an edge equal to the radius, <InlineMath math="25" />. A square with the radius as its side is much smaller than the largest square inside the circle.
- <InlineMath math="16{,}000\sqrt2\text{ cm}^3" /> is the cube from log <InlineMath math="Q" />. It shows up if you cross out log <InlineMath math="P" /> because a cube with edge <InlineMath math="25\sqrt2" /> doesn't fit, when you only need to shrink that cube to edge <InlineMath math="30" />.
- <InlineMath math="31{,}250\sqrt2\text{ cm}^3" /> uses the edge <InlineMath math="25\sqrt2\approx35.4" /> on log <InlineMath math="P" /> without **checking the log's length** of <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" />.
- <InlineMath math="64{,}000\text{ cm}^3" /> uses an edge equal to the diameter of log <InlineMath math="Q" />, <InlineMath math="40" />. A square with the diameter as its side doesn't fit, because its diagonal <InlineMath math="40\sqrt2" /> is longer than the diameter.

So the largest cube has a volume of <InlineMath math="27{,}000\text{ cm}^3" />, and it comes from log <InlineMath math="P" />.

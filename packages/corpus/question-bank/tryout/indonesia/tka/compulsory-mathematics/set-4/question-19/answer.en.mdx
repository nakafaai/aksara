A sphere's volume is proportional to the cube of its radius, <InlineMath math="V=\frac43\pi r^3" />. If the diameter, and so the radius, is multiplied by <InlineMath math="1.5" />, then **the volume is multiplied by <InlineMath math="1.5^3" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1.5^3&=3.375 \\
\text{increase}&=3.375-1=2.375=237.5\,\%
\end{aligned}" />

As a check with numbers, a sphere of radius <InlineMath math="2" /> becomes one of radius <InlineMath math="3" />, and <InlineMath math="\frac{3^3}{2^3}=\frac{27}{8}=3.375" />.

So the volume of air grows by <InlineMath math="237.5\,\%" />.

- <InlineMath math="50\,\%" /> assumes the volume grows by the same percentage as the diameter.
- <InlineMath math="100\,\%" /> doubles the growth of the diameter.
- <InlineMath math="125\,\%" /> uses <InlineMath math="1.5^2-1" />, the growth of the surface area.
- <InlineMath math="150\,\%" /> multiplies the <InlineMath math="50\,\%" /> growth by <InlineMath math="3" /> instead of cubing <InlineMath math="1.5" />.

# Every way to get exactly one hundred twenty pieces

Say the committee buys <InlineMath math="x" /> boxes of <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> boxes of <InlineMath math="5" />. There are three conditions: exactly <InlineMath math="120" /> pieces, at least one box of each kind, and some budget **still left over**, which means the cost must be less than <InlineMath math="760{,}000" />, not equal to it.

Start with the number of pieces.

<BlockMath math="3x+5y=120" />

Both <InlineMath math="3x" /> and <InlineMath math="120" /> are multiples of <InlineMath math="3" />, so <InlineMath math="5y" /> must be a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" /> too. That means **<InlineMath math="y" /> must be a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" />**. Write <InlineMath math="y=3k" /> for a whole number <InlineMath math="k" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3x+15k&=120 \\
x&=40-5k
\end{aligned}" />

At least one box of each kind gives <InlineMath math="y=3k\ge1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=40-5k\ge1" />, so <InlineMath math="k=1,2,\ldots,7" />.

# The budget condition

The cost in thousands of rupiah is <InlineMath math="20x+30y" />. Substitute <InlineMath math="x=40-5k" /> and <InlineMath math="y=3k" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
20(40-5k)+30(3k)&<760 \\
800-10k&<760 \\
k&>4
\end{aligned}" />

The sign is **greater than**, so <InlineMath math="k=4" /> is out. Together with <InlineMath math="k\le7" />, that leaves <InlineMath math="k=5,6,7" />.

# The tempting options

Each of the other options **loosens one condition**.

- <InlineMath math="4" /> comes from counting <InlineMath math="(20,12)" />, whose cost is exactly <InlineMath math="760{,}000" />, so nothing is left over. You get the same number by counting <InlineMath math="(0,24)" />, which has no box of <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> comes from counting both of those plans.
- <InlineMath math="7" /> is the number of plans for <InlineMath math="k=1" /> to <InlineMath math="7" /> if you forget the budget.
- <InlineMath math="11" /> comes from solving the inequality for <InlineMath math="y" /> only, <InlineMath math="12<y\le23" />, and counting every whole number <InlineMath math="y=13,\ldots,23" />. But when <InlineMath math="y" /> isn't a multiple of <InlineMath math="3" />, the number of boxes <InlineMath math="x" /> isn't a whole number.

So the number of purchase plans that meet every condition is <InlineMath math="3" />.

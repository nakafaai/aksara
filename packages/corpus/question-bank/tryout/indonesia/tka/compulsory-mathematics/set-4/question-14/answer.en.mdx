A reflection does not change size, while a dilation with factor <InlineMath math="k" /> **multiplies area by <InlineMath math="k^2" />**. So work out the original area and multiply it by <InlineMath math="3^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{ABC}&=\frac12\cdot AB\cdot AC=\frac12\cdot4\cdot3=6 \\
A'&=3^2\cdot6=54
\end{aligned}" />

As a check with coordinates, the final image is <InlineMath math="(3,3)" />, <InlineMath math="(3,15)" /> and <InlineMath math="(12,3)" />, a right triangle with legs <InlineMath math="12" /> and <InlineMath math="9" />, and <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot12\cdot9=54" />.

So the final triangle has area <InlineMath math="54" /> square units.

- <InlineMath math="2" /> square units divides the area by <InlineMath math="3" />, as if the dilation shrank it.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> square units forgets the dilation and keeps only the reflection.
- <InlineMath math="18" /> square units multiplies the area by <InlineMath math="3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="3^2" />.
- <InlineMath math="162" /> square units multiplies the area by <InlineMath math="3^3" />, as for a volume.

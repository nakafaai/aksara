A reflection does not change size, while a dilation with factor <InlineMath math="k" /> **multiplies area by <InlineMath math="k^2" />**. So work out the original area and multiply it by <InlineMath math="3^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{ABC}&=\frac12\cdot AB\cdot AC=\frac12\cdot6\cdot3=9 \\
A'&=3^2\cdot9=81
\end{aligned}" />

As a check with coordinates, the final image is <InlineMath math="(3,6)" />, <InlineMath math="(3,24)" /> and <InlineMath math="(12,6)" />, a right triangle with legs <InlineMath math="18" /> and <InlineMath math="9" />, and <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot18\cdot9=81" />.

So the final triangle has area <InlineMath math="81" /> square units.

- <InlineMath math="3" /> square units divides the area by <InlineMath math="3" />, as if the dilation shrank it.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> square units forgets the dilation and keeps only the reflection.
- <InlineMath math="27" /> square units multiplies the area by <InlineMath math="3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="3^2" />.
- <InlineMath math="243" /> square units multiplies the area by <InlineMath math="3^3" />, as for a volume.

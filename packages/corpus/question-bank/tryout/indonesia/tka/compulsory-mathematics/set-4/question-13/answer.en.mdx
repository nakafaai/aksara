Let the center of rotation be <InlineMath math="P(a,b)" />. To rotate a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> counterclockwise about <InlineMath math="P" />, **move <InlineMath math="P" /> to the origin, rotate, then move back**. The vector <InlineMath math="(x-a,\,y-b)" /> turns into <InlineMath math="\bigl(-(y-b),\,x-a\bigr)" />, which gives the following map.

<BlockMath math="(x,y)\mapsto\bigl(a-(y-b),\ b+(x-a)\bigr)" />

Apply the map to <InlineMath math="A(2,2)" />, which goes to <InlineMath math="A'(-1,-1)" />. Each coordinate gives one equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a-(2-b)&=-1 \\
b+(2-a)&=-1
\end{aligned}" />

Rewrite both equations, add them to find <InlineMath math="a" />, then substitute <InlineMath math="a" /> into <InlineMath math="a+b=1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+b&=1 \\
a-b&=3 \\
2a&=4 \\
a&=2 \\
b&=1-a=-1
\end{aligned}" />

One center has to work for every point, so **check with <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="C" />** using <InlineMath math="P(2,-1)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
B(6,3)&\mapsto\bigl(2-(3+1),\ -1+(6-2)\bigr)=(-2,3)=B' \\
C(1,5)&\mapsto\bigl(2-(5+1),\ -1+(1-2)\bigr)=(-4,-2)=C'
\end{aligned}" />

The coordinates of <InlineMath math="P" /> are therefore <InlineMath math="(2,-1)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(-1,2)" /> sends <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="A'" /> if the rotation is clockwise. This center fails for <InlineMath math="B" />, which would go to <InlineMath math="(0,-5)" /> instead of <InlineMath math="B'" />.
- <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> comes from assuming that every rotation is about the origin, although a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> rotation about the origin sends <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="(-2,2)" />, not to <InlineMath math="A'" />.
- <InlineMath math="\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)" /> is the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AA'" />. The center lies at the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AA'" /> only for a <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation, while for a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> rotation it only lies on the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AA'" />.
- <InlineMath math="(1,-3)" /> is the shift vector in <InlineMath math="A'=R(A)+(1,-3)" />, where <InlineMath math="R" /> is the <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> rotation about the origin. That vector is not the center, because the center satisfies <InlineMath math="P-R(P)=(1,-3)" />.

# Gold bought

The question asks how much gold Rina has left after two purchases, one sale and one more purchase. For a purchase, the weight is the amount of money divided by that month's **buy price**, and the result isn't rounded.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{January: } \frac{2{,}950{,}000}{1{,}250{,}000}&=2.36\text{ grams} \\
\text{February: } \frac{2{,}220{,}000}{1{,}200{,}000}&=1.85\text{ grams} \\
\text{balance after February}&=2.36+1.85=4.21\text{ grams}
\end{aligned}" />

# The sale in March

When Rina sells, she's paid the **buyback price**, not the buy price. Then the fee of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}25{,}000" /> comes off. So if she sells <InlineMath math="m" /> grams, the money she gets has to be at least <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}3{,}540{,}000" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1{,}180{,}000\,m-25{,}000&\ge 3{,}540{,}000 \\
m&\ge \frac{3{,}565{,}000}{1{,}180{,}000}\approx 3.021
\end{aligned}" />

The weight sold must be a multiple of <InlineMath math="0.1" /> gram. The smallest multiple of <InlineMath math="0.1" /> that isn't below <InlineMath math="3.021" /> is <InlineMath math="3.1" />. Check the two nearest choices:

| Weight sold | Money received | Enough? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="3.0" /> grams | <InlineMath math="3{,}540{,}000-25{,}000=3{,}515{,}000" /> | no |
| <InlineMath math="3.1" /> grams | <InlineMath math="3{,}658{,}000-25{,}000=3{,}633{,}000" /> | yes |

Selling <InlineMath math="3.0" /> grams falls short by <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}25{,}000" />, exactly the fee. So Rina **has to sell <InlineMath math="3.1" /> grams**, and she keeps <InlineMath math="4.21-3.1=1.11" /> grams.

# The purchase in April

The April purchase uses the **April buy price**. Add the result to what was left after the March sale.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{2{,}227{,}500}{1{,}350{,}000}&=1.65\text{ grams} \\
\text{balance after April}&=1.11+1.65=2.76\text{ grams}
\end{aligned}" />

# The tempting options

Each of the other options comes from **one wrong step**.

- <InlineMath math="1.11" /> grams is the balance before the April purchase. The last step is missing.
- <InlineMath math="2.60" /> grams comes from rounding every purchase down to <InlineMath math="2.3" />, <InlineMath math="1.8" /> and <InlineMath math="1.6" />. The multiple of <InlineMath math="0.1" /> gram rule is only for sales, and purchases aren't rounded.
- <InlineMath math="2.86" /> grams comes from forgetting the fee, because <InlineMath math="\frac{3{,}540{,}000}{1{,}180{,}000}=3" /> exactly. But <InlineMath math="3" /> grams only brings in <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}3{,}515{,}000" />. You also get this value if you round <InlineMath math="3.021" /> down.
- <InlineMath math="3.06" /> grams comes from using the March buy price for the sale, <InlineMath math="\frac{3{,}565{,}000}{1{,}280{,}000}\approx2.79" />, so only <InlineMath math="2.8" /> grams would be sold.

So Rina's gold balance at the end of April is <InlineMath math="2.76" /> grams.

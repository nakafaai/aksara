# Which receipts got the discount

Call the normal price of one pen (pulpen) <InlineMath math="p" />, of one sketchbook (buku gambar) <InlineMath math="b" /> and of one marker (spidol) <InlineMath math="s" />. The question asks for <InlineMath math="p+b+s" />. The table only shows what each person paid, so you first have to **turn every receipt back into its normal price**.

The rule: the <InlineMath math="10\%" /> discount is given only when the normal price is more than <InlineMath math="60{,}000" />. With a discount, the amount paid is <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of the normal price, so the normal price is the amount paid divided by <InlineMath math="0.9" />. Test each receipt:

| Buyer | Paid | If no discount | If discounted | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ani | <InlineMath math="48{,}000" /> | normal <InlineMath math="48{,}000\le60{,}000" />, fits | normal <InlineMath math="48{,}000\div0.9\approx53{,}333" />, not more than <InlineMath math="60{,}000" /> | no discount |
| Budi | <InlineMath math="62{,}100" /> | normal <InlineMath math="62{,}100>60{,}000" />, should have had a discount | normal <InlineMath math="69{,}000>60{,}000" />, fits | discounted |
| Citra | <InlineMath math="54{,}000" /> | normal <InlineMath math="54{,}000\le60{,}000" />, fits | normal exactly <InlineMath math="60{,}000" />, not more than <InlineMath math="60{,}000" /> | no discount |

Citra's receipt is the boundary case. A normal price of <InlineMath math="60{,}000" /> is **not more than** <InlineMath math="60{,}000" />, so there's no discount. Citra's normal price is <InlineMath math="54{,}000" />.

# Adding the three equations

Write the three normal prices as equations.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2p+b+3s&=48{,}000 \\
p+3b+2s&=69{,}000 \\
3p+2b+s&=54{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

You don't need each item's price. When you add the three equations, each item appears <InlineMath math="2+1+3=6" /> times. So **just add everything**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
6p+6b+6s&=48{,}000+69{,}000+54{,}000=171{,}000 \\
p+b+s&=\frac{171{,}000}{6}=28{,}500
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, the system gives <InlineMath math="p=4{,}500" />, <InlineMath math="b=16{,}500" /> and <InlineMath math="s=7{,}500" />, which add up to <InlineMath math="28{,}500" />.

# The tempting options

Each of the other options **goes wrong at one step**.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}9{,}500" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{171{,}000}{18}" />, the average price of one item. The question asks for three items together, so you'd still need to multiply by <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}27{,}350" /> comes from not undoing the discount on Budi's receipt, <InlineMath math="\frac{48{,}000+62{,}100+54{,}000}{6}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}28{,}385" /> comes from undoing the discount by adding <InlineMath math="10\%" /> of the amount paid, <InlineMath math="62{,}100\cdot1.1=68{,}310" />. The <InlineMath math="10\%" /> was taken from the normal price, so the right move is dividing by <InlineMath math="0.9" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}29{,}500" /> comes from treating Citra's receipt as discounted too, with a normal price of <InlineMath math="60{,}000" />. The discount is only for normal prices above <InlineMath math="60{,}000" />.

So the total normal price of one pen, one sketchbook and one marker is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}28{,}500" />.

The shaded region is bounded by the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis and two slanted lines, so **each boundary line gives one inequality**. For each slanted line, first write its equation from the points where it meets the axes. Then decide the direction of the inequality with a **test point** that does not lie on that line.

A line that meets the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at <InlineMath math="(a,0)" /> and the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis at <InlineMath math="(0,b)" /> has the equation <InlineMath math="\frac{x}{a}+\frac{y}{b}=1" />. The first line meets the axes at <InlineMath math="(6,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,6)" />, and the second at <InlineMath math="(9,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,3)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{6}+\frac{y}{6}=1&\iff x+y=6 \\
\frac{x}{9}+\frac{y}{3}=1&\iff x+3y=9
\end{aligned}" />

Use the origin <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> as the test point. For the line <InlineMath math="x+y=6" />, the origin gives <InlineMath math="0\le6" />, and the shaded region lies on the same side as the origin. So the region satisfies <InlineMath math="x+y\le6" />. For the line <InlineMath math="x+3y=9" />, the origin gives <InlineMath math="0<9" />, but the shaded region lies on the opposite side from the origin. So the region satisfies <InlineMath math="x+3y\ge9" />. The left edge of the region is the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis, which gives <InlineMath math="x\ge0" />.

The condition <InlineMath math="y\ge0" /> that every answer option contains holds automatically. Subtracting <InlineMath math="x+y\le6" /> from <InlineMath math="x+3y\ge9" /> gives a lower bound for <InlineMath math="y" />.

<BlockMath math="(x+3y)-(x+y)\ge9-6\iff2y\ge3\iff y\ge\frac32" />

To check, test the point <InlineMath math="(1,3)" />, which lies inside the region. Both slanted inequalities must hold.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1+3&=4\le6 \\
1+3(3)&=10\ge9
\end{aligned}" />

The shaded region is therefore the solution set of the system <InlineMath math="x+y\le6,\ x+3y\ge9,\ x\ge0,\ y\ge0" />.

- <InlineMath math="x+y\le6,\ x+3y\le9" /> with the two sign conditions describes the region below both lines, which is the quadrilateral containing the origin.
- <InlineMath math="x+y\le6,\ 3x+y\ge9" /> with the two sign conditions uses a line through <InlineMath math="(3,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,9)" />, because the roles of the two intercepts were swapped when the second line was written.
- <InlineMath math="x+y\ge6,\ x+3y\ge9" /> with the two sign conditions describes the region above both lines, which keeps extending up and to the right.
- <InlineMath math="x+y\ge6,\ x+3y\le9" /> with the two sign conditions describes the small triangle with corners <InlineMath math="\left(\frac92,\frac32\right)" />, <InlineMath math="(6,0)" />, and <InlineMath math="(9,0)" />, not the shaded region.

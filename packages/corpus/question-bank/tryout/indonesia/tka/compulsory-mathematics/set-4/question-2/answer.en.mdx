# Money out and money in

The question asks for Dimas's loss as a percent. **The base is the money he paid when buying**, <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}250{,}000" />. So you need the total money he got back from the two sales.

First, find how much gold Dimas has. The January buy price is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}250{,}000" /> per gram, so

<BlockMath math="\frac{1{,}250{,}000}{1{,}250{,}000}=1\text{ gram}" />

Dimas sells <InlineMath math="0.8" /> gram in February, so the remaining <InlineMath math="0.2" /> gram is sold in May. Each sale is paid at **that month's buyback price**, then <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}25{,}000" /> comes off. There are two transactions, so the fee is taken twice.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{February: } 0.8\cdot1{,}110{,}000-25{,}000&=888{,}000-25{,}000=863{,}000 \\
\text{May: } 0.2\cdot1{,}300{,}000-25{,}000&=260{,}000-25{,}000=235{,}000 \\
\text{total received}&=863{,}000+235{,}000=1{,}098{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

# The loss as a percent

The loss is money out minus money in. Divide it by the money out to get the percent.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{loss}&=1{,}250{,}000-1{,}098{,}000=152{,}000 \\
\text{loss percent}&=\frac{152{,}000}{1{,}250{,}000}\cdot100\%=12.16\%
\end{aligned}" />

Notice that the May sale on its own doesn't make a profit either. That <InlineMath math="0.2" /> gram cost <InlineMath math="250{,}000" /> and brought in <InlineMath math="235{,}000" />. A fixed fee of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}25{,}000" /> **hurts a small sale a lot**.

# The tempting options

Each of the other options **skips one rule from the reading**.

- <InlineMath math="10.16\%" /> comes from taking the fee only once. There are two sales, so the fee is <InlineMath math="2\cdot25{,}000" />.
- <InlineMath math="9.85\%" /> is the plain average of the two sales' loss percents. The February sale loses <InlineMath math="\frac{1{,}000{,}000-863{,}000}{1{,}000{,}000}=13.7\%" /> and the May sale loses <InlineMath math="\frac{250{,}000-235{,}000}{250{,}000}=6\%" />. Their average <InlineMath math="\frac{13.7+6}{2}=9.85" /> is wrong, because the two parts cost very different amounts, so you can't just average their percents.
- <InlineMath math="8.16\%" /> comes from forgetting the fee completely.
- <InlineMath math="4.8\%" /> comes from selling at the February and May buy prices instead of the buyback prices.

So Dimas's loss is <InlineMath math="12.16\%" /> of the money he paid.

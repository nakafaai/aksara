The problem uses two exchange rates. The selling rate (*kurs jual*) of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}17{,}500" /> applies when the bank sells euros to the student, and the buying rate (*kurs beli*) of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}17{,}000" /> applies when the bank buys the leftover euros back. What the student really pays is the **net cost**: the rupiah paid out minus the rupiah received back. Divide that cost by the **euros actually spent**, which is <InlineMath math="600" /> euros, not <InlineMath math="750" /> euros.

| Step | Change in euros | Change in rupiah |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Buy euros at the selling rate | <InlineMath math="+750" /> | <InlineMath math="-13{,}125{,}000" /> |
| Spend euros in Germany | <InlineMath math="-600" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| Sell the leftover euros at the buying rate | <InlineMath math="-150" /> | <InlineMath math="+2{,}550{,}000" /> |
| Total | <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="-10{,}575{,}000" /> |

The table shows that all the euros are gone while the student's money dropped by <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}10{,}575{,}000" />. Let <InlineMath math="B" /> be the net cost in rupiah and <InlineMath math="r" /> the average cost per euro spent.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
B&=750\times17{,}500-150\times17{,}000 \\
&=13{,}125{,}000-2{,}550{,}000 \\
&=10{,}575{,}000 \\
r&=\frac{B}{600}=\frac{10{,}575{,}000}{600}=17{,}625
\end{aligned}" />

Check with a second method. Each euro sold back loses <InlineMath math="17{,}500-17{,}000=500" /> rupiah, so the <InlineMath math="150" /> leftover euros lose <InlineMath math="75{,}000" /> rupiah in total. That loss is shared by the <InlineMath math="600" /> euros that were spent.

<BlockMath math="17{,}500+\frac{150\times500}{600}=17{,}500+125=17{,}625" />

The average cost of each euro the student actually spent is therefore <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}17{,}625" />.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}14{,}100" /> comes from dividing the net cost by <InlineMath math="750" /> euros, although <InlineMath math="150" /> of them were never spent.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}16{,}875" /> appears when the selling and buying rates are swapped, as if the student bought euros at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}17{,}000" /> and sold them back at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}17{,}500" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}17{,}500" /> is only the selling rate, as if the leftover euros were bought back at the same price and the rate spread cost nothing.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}21{,}875" /> comes from dividing the whole purchase cost by <InlineMath math="600" /> euros without subtracting the money received for the leftover euros.

The interior angles of an <InlineMath math="n" />-gon add up to <InlineMath math="(n-2)\cdot180^\circ" />. In a regular polygon **that sum is shared equally by the <InlineMath math="n" /> angles**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(12-2)\cdot180^\circ&=1{,}800^\circ \\
\frac{1{,}800^\circ}{12}&=150^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

As a check with the exterior angle, each exterior angle is <InlineMath math="\frac{360^\circ}{12}=30^\circ" />, and <InlineMath math="180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ" />.

So each interior angle is <InlineMath math="150^\circ" />.

- <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> is the exterior angle, not the interior one.
- <InlineMath math="135^\circ" /> uses the <InlineMath math="(n-3)" /> from the formula for diagonals, <InlineMath math="\frac{9\cdot180^\circ}{12}" />.
- <InlineMath math="165^\circ" /> uses the exterior angle <InlineMath math="\frac{360^\circ}{24}" />, as if there were <InlineMath math="24" /> sides.
- <InlineMath math="1{,}800^\circ" /> is the sum of all interior angles, not yet divided by <InlineMath math="12" />.

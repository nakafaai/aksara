The altitude <InlineMath math="CD" /> from the right angle splits triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> into two smaller right triangles, <InlineMath math="ADC" /> and <InlineMath math="CDB" />. These two triangles are **similar**, so the ratios of their sides lead to <InlineMath math="CD" />.

They are similar because two pairs of angles are equal. Angles <InlineMath math="ADC" /> and <InlineMath math="CDB" /> are both <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. The angles of triangle <InlineMath math="ADC" /> and the angles of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> each add up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, so the following two angles are equal.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\angle ACD&=180^\circ-90^\circ-\angle A=90^\circ-\angle A \\
\angle CBD&=180^\circ-90^\circ-\angle A=90^\circ-\angle A
\end{aligned}" />

In this similarity, <InlineMath math="A" /> matches <InlineMath math="C" />, <InlineMath math="D" /> matches <InlineMath math="D" />, and <InlineMath math="C" /> matches <InlineMath math="B" />. So **the ratios of the legs of the two triangles are equal**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{AD}{CD}&=\frac{CD}{DB} \\
CD^2&=AD\cdot DB=4\cdot9=36 \\
CD&=6
\end{aligned}" />

A length is positive, so <InlineMath math="CD=6\text{ cm}" />.

Check the result with the Pythagorean theorem. Compute <InlineMath math="AC^2" /> and <InlineMath math="BC^2" /> from the two small triangles, then confirm that they satisfy the theorem in the large triangle with <InlineMath math="AB=4+9=13" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AC^2&=4^2+6^2=52 \\
BC^2&=9^2+6^2=117 \\
AC^2+BC^2&=169=13^2=AB^2
\end{aligned}" />

The length of <InlineMath math="CD" /> is therefore <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{13}{2}\text{ cm}" /> is half of <InlineMath math="AB" />, the length of the median from <InlineMath math="C" /> to the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AB" />. The median and the altitude have equal length only when <InlineMath math="AD=DB" />.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{13}\text{ cm}" /> is the length of <InlineMath math="AC" />, which follows from <InlineMath math="AC^2=AD\cdot AB=4\cdot13" />, not the length of <InlineMath math="CD" />.
- <InlineMath math="6\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}" /> appears when <InlineMath math="AB^2=AC^2+BC^2" /> is written as <InlineMath math="169=16+81+CD^2" />, although <InlineMath math="CD^2" /> occurs twice, once in <InlineMath math="AC^2" /> and once in <InlineMath math="BC^2" />.
- <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{13}\text{ cm}" /> is the length of <InlineMath math="BC" />, which follows from <InlineMath math="BC^2=DB\cdot AB=9\cdot13" />.

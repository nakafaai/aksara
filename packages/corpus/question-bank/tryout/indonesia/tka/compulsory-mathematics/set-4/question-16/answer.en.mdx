Each roof plane is a rectangle. So you need its two measurements: **the sloping length from the lower edge up to the ridge** and the length along the ridge. Both have to include the parts that stick out past the walls.

# Sloping length up to the wall

Look at the cross-section. From the top of a wall to the point under the ridge is half the house's width, <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot8=4\text{ m}" />, and the ridge is <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> higher. The piece of roof from the wall top to the ridge is the **hypotenuse of that right triangle**:

<BlockMath math="s_1=\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=\sqrt{25}=5\text{ m}" />

# The overhang at the lower edge

The <InlineMath math="80\text{ cm}" /> is measured **horizontally**, not along the roof. The slope stays the same, so the overhang forms a small triangle similar to the big one. In the big triangle, every <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" /> across matches <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> along the roof. The same ratio holds in the small triangle:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
s_2&=0.8\cdot\frac{5}{4}=1\text{ m} \\
\text{sloping length}&=s_1+s_2=5+1=6\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

# Length along the ridge and the area

Along the ridge, the roof sticks out <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}" /> at **both** ends, so its length is <InlineMath math="11+0.5+0.5=12\text{ m}" />. The roof is made of two equal rectangles:

<BlockMath math="L=2\cdot(6\cdot12)=144\text{ m}^2" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="115.2\text{ m}^2" /> uses the horizontal width <InlineMath math="4+0.8=4.8\text{ m}" /> as the width of a roof plane. The plane is tilted, so **its width is measured along the slope**.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ m}^2" /> forgets the overhang at the lower edge and uses only <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" />.
- <InlineMath math="132\text{ m}^2" /> forgets the extra <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}" /> at both ends of the ridge and uses a length of <InlineMath math="11\text{ m}" />.
- <InlineMath math="139.2\text{ m}^2" /> adds <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ m}" /> straight onto <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" />. The <InlineMath math="0.8" /> is a horizontal measurement, and its sloping length is <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" />.

So the whole roof surface has an area of <InlineMath math="144\text{ m}^2" />.

Let <InlineMath math="O" /> be the point where the two diagonals of the base meet. In a regular square pyramid the apex <InlineMath math="T" /> lies directly above <InlineMath math="O" />, so <InlineMath math="TO=3\text{ m}" /> is perpendicular to the base. Triangle <InlineMath math="TOA" /> therefore has a right angle at <InlineMath math="O" />, and **the lateral edge <InlineMath math="TA" /> is the hypotenuse of triangle <InlineMath math="TOA" />**. Its legs are <InlineMath math="TO" /> and <InlineMath math="OA" />, which is half of a diagonal of the base.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AC&=\sqrt{8^2+8^2}=8\sqrt2 \\
OA&=\frac12\,AC=4\sqrt2 \\
TA^2&=TO^2+OA^2=3^2+\left(4\sqrt2\right)^2=9+32=41 \\
TA&=\sqrt{41}
\end{aligned}" />

The result makes sense. The value <InlineMath math="\sqrt{41}\approx6.4" /> is larger than both legs, <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4\sqrt2\approx5.66" />, as a hypotenuse must be the longest side. It is also smaller than the sum of the legs, as the triangle inequality requires.

The lateral edge <InlineMath math="TA" /> is therefore <InlineMath math="\sqrt{41}\text{ m}" /> long.

- <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> is the slant height of a face, the distance from <InlineMath math="T" /> to the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AB" />. It comes from the leg <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" />, half of a base side, not from <InlineMath math="OA" />.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{73}\text{ m}" /> appears when the base side <InlineMath math="8\text{ m}" /> is used as a leg, although the distance from <InlineMath math="O" /> to <InlineMath math="A" /> is half of a diagonal.
- <InlineMath math="\left(3+4\sqrt2\right)\text{ m}" /> is the sum of the two legs. The hypotenuse comes from the Pythagorean theorem, not from adding the legs.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{137}\text{ m}" /> appears when the whole diagonal <InlineMath math="8\sqrt2\text{ m}" /> is used as a leg, although <InlineMath math="O" /> sits at the middle of the diagonal.

Stage 0 is a right isosceles triangle with legs of 8 cm, so its shaded area is <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot8\cdot8=32\text{ cm}^2" />. At each stage, every shaded triangle is split into four congruent triangles, and only the middle one loses its shading. So each shaded triangle leaves **three shaded triangles**, and each new triangle has legs half as long as the triangle it came from.

Let <InlineMath math="c_n" /> be the number of shaded triangles and <InlineMath math="A_n" /> the shaded area at stage <InlineMath math="n" />. Each shaded triangle becomes three shaded triangles, so the count is **multiplied by 3** at each stage. Each new triangle has one quarter of the area of its parent, so the shaded area is multiplied by <InlineMath math="\frac34" /> at each stage.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
c_n&=3^n \\
A_n&=32\left(\frac34\right)^n
\end{aligned}" />

The area removed at a stage is the middle triangle of each shaded triangle from the stage before. It is a quarter of the shaded area of that earlier stage, so <InlineMath math="A_n-A_{n+1}=\frac14A_n" />. For stage 1, the removed area is <InlineMath math="\frac14\cdot32=8\text{ cm}^2" />. For stage 2, it is <InlineMath math="\frac14\cdot24=6\text{ cm}^2" />, because stage 1 has a shaded area of <InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}^2" />.

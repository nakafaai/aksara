Two quantities change from one stage to the next. **The number of colored triangles triples**, because each colored triangle produces three new colored triangles. **The colored area is multiplied by <InlineMath math="\frac34" />**, because the four new triangles have equal areas and only one of the four parts loses its color.

At stage <InlineMath math="0" />, the triangle has area <InlineMath math="\frac12\times8\times8=32\text{ cm}^2" />. Let <InlineMath math="N_n" /> be the number of colored triangles and <InlineMath math="L_n" /> the colored area in <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^2" /> at stage <InlineMath math="n" />. Both form geometric sequences.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
N_n&=3^n \\
L_n&=32\left(\frac34\right)^n \\
L_{n-1}-L_n&=\frac14\,L_{n-1}
\end{aligned}" />

The last line gives the area that loses its color when stage <InlineMath math="n-1" /> becomes stage <InlineMath math="n" />, which is one quarter of the colored area at the previous stage.

1. The statement that stage <InlineMath math="n" /> has <InlineMath math="3n" /> colored triangles is false. That formula fits only <InlineMath math="n=1" />. Stage <InlineMath math="2" /> has <InlineMath math="3^2=9" /> triangles, not <InlineMath math="3\times2=6" />, because the count is multiplied by <InlineMath math="3" /> at each stage, not increased by <InlineMath math="3" />.
2. The statement that stage <InlineMath math="4" /> contains <InlineMath math="81" /> colored triangles is true, because <InlineMath math="N_4=3^4=81" />.
3. The statement that the first two changes remove equal areas is false. Going from stage <InlineMath math="0" /> to stage <InlineMath math="1" /> removes <InlineMath math="\frac14\times32=8\text{ cm}^2" />, while going from stage <InlineMath math="1" /> to stage <InlineMath math="2" /> removes <InlineMath math="\frac14\times24=6\text{ cm}^2" />. The removed part shrinks because the quarter is taken from a colored area that keeps getting smaller.
4. The statement that the colored area at stage <InlineMath math="3" /> is <InlineMath math="13.5\text{ cm}^2" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="L_3=32\times\frac{27}{64}=\frac{27}{2}=13.5" />.
5. The statement about stage <InlineMath math="5" /> is true. The colored area keeps decreasing, <InlineMath math="L_4=\frac{81}{8}=10.125" /> is still more than <InlineMath math="10" />, and <InlineMath math="L_5=\frac{243}{32}\approx7.59" /> is already less than <InlineMath math="10" />.

The true statements are the ones about <InlineMath math="81" /> triangles at stage <InlineMath math="4" />, an area of <InlineMath math="13.5\text{ cm}^2" /> at stage <InlineMath math="3" />, and stage <InlineMath math="5" /> as the first stage with a colored area below <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}^2" />.

# Counting solutions with a horizontal line

# Judging each statement

1. **False**. <InlineMath math="f(f(x))=0" /> means <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> has to be one of the zeros of <InlineMath math="f" />, which are <InlineMath math="-3" />, <InlineMath math="-1" /> and <InlineMath math="2" />. Count each case with the table. <InlineMath math="f(x)=-3" /> has no solution, because the smallest value of <InlineMath math="f" /> is <InlineMath math="-2" />. <InlineMath math="f(x)=-1" /> has <InlineMath math="2" /> solutions, <InlineMath math="x=-2.5" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-1.5" />. <InlineMath math="f(x)=2" /> has <InlineMath math="3" /> solutions, <InlineMath math="x=-4" />, <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />. That's <InlineMath math="0+2+3=5" /> in total, not <InlineMath math="3" />. The number <InlineMath math="3" /> is the count for <InlineMath math="f(x)=0" />, not for <InlineMath math="f(f(x))=0" />.
2. **True**. The value <InlineMath math="1" /> lies between <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="2" />, so there are <InlineMath math="4" /> solutions: <InlineMath math="-2x-6=1" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=-3.5" />, <InlineMath math="2x+2=1" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=-0.5" />, <InlineMath math="2-x=1" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=1" />, and <InlineMath math="x-2=1" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=3" />.
3. **True**. Work from the inside out: <InlineMath math="f(-3)=0" />, then <InlineMath math="f(0)=2" />, then <InlineMath math="f(2)=0" />.
4. **True**. By the table, exactly three solutions happen only at <InlineMath math="c=0" /> and <InlineMath math="c=2" />. For <InlineMath math="c=2" />, two of the three solutions are the endpoints <InlineMath math="x=-4" /> and <InlineMath math="x=4" />, which are **easy to miss**.
5. **True**. From the first statement, the solutions of <InlineMath math="f(f(x))=0" /> are <InlineMath math="-4" />, <InlineMath math="-2.5" />, <InlineMath math="-1.5" />, <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />. Check <InlineMath math="x=4" />: <InlineMath math="f(4)=2" /> and <InlineMath math="f(2)=0" />. No solution can be bigger, because the domain stops at <InlineMath math="4" />.

So the true statements are the second, third, fourth and fifth.

# Height of the large pyramid

Pyramid <InlineMath math="P.ABCD" /> has the same base as pyramid <InlineMath math="T.ABCD" />, so what you really need is **the height of point <InlineMath math="P" /> above the base**. For that, first find the height of <InlineMath math="T.ABCD" />.

The apex <InlineMath math="T" /> sits straight above the centre <InlineMath math="O" /> of the base. The distance from <InlineMath math="O" /> to a corner is half the diagonal of the square, not half a side.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
OA&=\tfrac12\cdot12\sqrt2=6\sqrt2 \\
TO&=\sqrt{TA^2-OA^2}=\sqrt{121-72}=\sqrt{49}=7
\end{aligned}" />

# Height of point P

<InlineMath math="P" /> is the midpoint of <InlineMath math="TC" />. As you walk from <InlineMath math="C" /> on the base up to <InlineMath math="T" />, the height grows in proportion, so halfway along **the height is exactly half the height of <InlineMath math="T" />**, which is <InlineMath math="3.5" />.

<BlockMath math="V_{P.ABCD}=\tfrac13\cdot12^2\cdot3.5=\tfrac13\cdot144\cdot3.5=168\text{ cm}^3" />

As a check, that's exactly half the volume of <InlineMath math="T.ABCD" />, which is <InlineMath math="\tfrac13\cdot144\cdot7=336" />.

So the volume of pyramid <InlineMath math="P.ABCD" /> is <InlineMath math="168\text{ cm}^3" />.

- <InlineMath math="336\text{ cm}^3" /> is the volume of <InlineMath math="T.ABCD" />, not <InlineMath math="P.ABCD" />.
- <InlineMath math="264\text{ cm}^3" /> uses the edge <InlineMath math="11" /> as the pyramid's height and then halves it.
- <InlineMath math="24\sqrt{85}\text{ cm}^3" /> uses half a side, <InlineMath math="6" />, as the distance from the centre to a corner, giving a height of <InlineMath math="\sqrt{85}" />.

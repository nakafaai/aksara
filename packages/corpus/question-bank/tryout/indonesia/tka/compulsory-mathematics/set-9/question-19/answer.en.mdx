The ball touches the bottom, the lid, and the wall, so **the cylinder has radius <InlineMath math="r" /> and height <InlineMath math="2r" />**. The sand fills the cylinder minus the ball.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{cylinder}}&=\pi r^2\cdot2r=2\pi r^3 \\
V_{\text{ball}}&=\tfrac43\pi r^3 \\
V_{\text{sand}}&=2\pi r^3-\tfrac43\pi r^3=\tfrac23\pi r^3 \\
V_{\text{sand}}:V_{\text{ball}}&=\tfrac23:\tfrac43=1:2
\end{aligned}" />

The factor <InlineMath math="\pi r^3" /> cancels, so the ratio holds for a ball of any size.

So the volume of sand to the volume of the ball is <InlineMath math="1:2" />.

- <InlineMath math="1:1" /> assumes the sand fills half the cylinder.
- <InlineMath math="1:3" /> is the ratio of sand to cylinder.
- <InlineMath math="2:1" /> turns the ratio upside down.
- <InlineMath math="2:3" /> is the ratio of ball to cylinder.

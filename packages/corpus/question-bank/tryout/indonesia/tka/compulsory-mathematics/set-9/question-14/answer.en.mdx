The centre of the dilation and the translation vector are unknown, so you can't run <InlineMath math="T" /> step by step. The key: **a dilation followed by a translation is still one dilation with the same factor**, just about a different centre. The plan: find <InlineMath math="k" />, find the new centre from the pair <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="A'" />, then use that centre for <InlineMath math="B" />.

# The general form of the transformation

Say the centre of the dilation is <InlineMath math="P" /> and the translation vector is <InlineMath math="\vec v" />. A point <InlineMath math="X" /> first goes to <InlineMath math="P+k(X-P)" />, then moves by <InlineMath math="\vec v" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T(X)&=P+k(X-P)+\vec v \\
&=kX+\big((1-k)P+\vec v\big)=kX+\vec c
\end{aligned}" />

Two things follow. First, for any two points <InlineMath math="T(X)-T(Y)=k(X-Y)" />, so **every segment gets multiplied by <InlineMath math="k" />**, and the translation doesn't change its direction. Second, if <InlineMath math="k\ne1" />, there's exactly one point <InlineMath math="Q" /> that doesn't move, and <InlineMath math="T" /> is the dilation with factor <InlineMath math="k" /> about <InlineMath math="Q" />.

# The scale factor from the area and the position of a point

A dilation with factor <InlineMath math="k" /> multiplies areas by <InlineMath math="k^2" />. So <InlineMath math="k^2=9" />, which leaves **two possibilities**, <InlineMath math="k=3" /> or <InlineMath math="k=-3" />. Point <InlineMath math="C" /> decides. Since <InlineMath math="C-A=(0,3)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
C'&=A'+k(0,3)=(-2,\ 9+3k) \\
k=3&\ \Rightarrow\ C'=(-2,18),\ \text{above } A' \\
k=-3&\ \Rightarrow\ C'=(-2,0),\ \text{below } A'
\end{aligned}" />

The question says <InlineMath math="C'" /> is below <InlineMath math="A'" />, so <InlineMath math="k=-3" />.

# The single centre of dilation

The centre <InlineMath math="Q" /> is the point that doesn't move. The dilation with factor <InlineMath math="-3" /> about <InlineMath math="Q" /> sends <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="A'" />, so <InlineMath math="A'-Q=-3(A-Q)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A'-Q&=-3A+3Q \\
4Q&=A'+3A=(-2,9)+(6,3)=(4,12) \\
Q&=(1,3)
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="A-Q=(1,-2)" /> and <InlineMath math="A'-Q=(-3,6)" />, exactly <InlineMath math="-3" /> times as much. **The point <InlineMath math="Q" /> lies between <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="A'" />**, as it should for a negative factor.

# The image of the point asked for

Now dilate <InlineMath math="B" /> about <InlineMath math="Q" /> with factor <InlineMath math="-3" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
B-Q&=(4,0)-(1,3)=(3,-3) \\
B'&=Q-3(B-Q)=(1,3)+(-9,9)=(-8,12)
\end{aligned}" />

Check with the segment rule: <InlineMath math="B'-A'=(-6,3)" />, and <InlineMath math="-3(B-A)=-3(2,-1)=(-6,3)" />. **It matches**.

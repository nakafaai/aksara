The question asks for the product of all values of <InlineMath math="m" /> that make the graph of <InlineMath math="f" /> touch the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis. Two conditions have to hold together: **the discriminant is zero**, and <InlineMath math="f" /> really is a quadratic function.

# The touching condition

The discriminant <InlineMath math="D=b^2-4ac" /> is the number that decides how many times the graph meets the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis. The graph touches the axis when there is **exactly one common point**, that is, when <InlineMath math="D=0" />. Since <InlineMath math="b" /> has a factor <InlineMath math="2" />, it's shorter to use <InlineMath math="\tfrac{D}{4}=\left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2-ac" />.

<BlockMath math="a=m+2,\qquad \tfrac{b}{2}=-(m+2),\qquad c=m^2-7m+17" />

# The possible parameter values

Notice that <InlineMath math="m+2" /> shows up in two terms, so **take it out as a factor** and don't throw it away.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac{D}{4}&=(m+2)^2-(m+2)\left(m^2-7m+17\right) \\
&=(m+2)\left[(m+2)-\left(m^2-7m+17\right)\right] \\
&=(m+2)\left(-m^2+8m-15\right) \\
&=-(m+2)(m-3)(m-5)
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="D=0" /> for <InlineMath math="m=-2" />, <InlineMath math="m=3" />, or <InlineMath math="m=5" />.

# Checking each value

Now check whether each value really gives a parabola that touches the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m=-2&:\ f(x)=0\cdot x^2-0\cdot x+35=35 \\
m=3&:\ f(x)=5x^2-10x+5=5(x-1)^2 \\
m=5&:\ f(x)=7x^2-14x+7=7(x-1)^2
\end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="m=-2" />, the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x^2" /> becomes zero, so <InlineMath math="f" /> **isn't a quadratic function anymore**. Its graph is the horizontal line <InlineMath math="y=35" />, which never reaches the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis. Throw this value out. The other two values give parabolas that touch the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at <InlineMath math="(1,0)" />.

<BlockMath math="3\cdot5=15" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-30" /> is <InlineMath math="(-2)\cdot3\cdot5" />. You also get it from the product-of-roots rule on the cubic <InlineMath math="-m^3+6m^2+m-30" />. Both ways **wrongly include** <InlineMath math="m=-2" />.
- <InlineMath math="-15" /> comes from <InlineMath math="-m^2+8m-15=0" /> if you take the product of the roots as <InlineMath math="-15" /> without dividing by the leading coefficient <InlineMath math="-1" />. The right product is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{-15}{-1}=15" />.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> comes from putting the whole <InlineMath math="b=-2(m+2)" /> into the <InlineMath math="\tfrac{D}{4}" /> formula. That gives <InlineMath math="(m+2)\left(-m^2+11m-9\right)" />, whose roots multiply to <InlineMath math="9" />. The <InlineMath math="\tfrac{D}{4}" /> formula uses <InlineMath math="\tfrac{b}{2}" />.
- <InlineMath math="30" /> keeps <InlineMath math="m=-2" /> and also gets the sign wrong in the product-of-roots rule for the cubic.

So the product of all the values of <InlineMath math="m" /> that work is <InlineMath math="15" />.

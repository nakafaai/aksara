The question asks how long it takes until the water at the deep end is <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ m}" /> deep. The trap is the shape of the water: the bottom slopes, so at that moment **the water doesn't cover the whole bottom yet**. The water isn't a cuboid.

# Flow rate from the pipe

The flow rate is the volume of water coming out each second: the pipe's cross-section area times the water speed. The pipe's radius is <InlineMath math="3.5\text{ cm}" /> and the speed is <InlineMath math="2\text{ m/s}=200\text{ cm/s}" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A&=\frac{22}{7}\cdot3.5^2=38.5\text{ cm}^2 \\
Q&=38.5\cdot200=7{,}700\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}=7.7\text{ litres/s}
\end{aligned}" />

Notice that **you square the radius**, which is half of the <InlineMath math="7\text{ cm}" /> diameter.

# The shape of the water in the pool

The bottom drops <InlineMath math="2.0-0.4=1.6\text{ m}" /> over <InlineMath math="11\text{ m}" />. The water at the deep end is only <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ m}" />, less than <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ m}" />, so the surface hasn't reached the shallow end yet. Seen from the side, the water forms a **right triangle similar** to the triangle of the bottom's drop (base <InlineMath math="11" />, height <InlineMath math="1.6" />).

Similar triangles give the surface length <InlineMath math="\ell" />, and the water's volume is the triangle's area times the pool's width:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{\ell}{11}&=\frac{1.2}{1.6}\ \Rightarrow\ \ell=8.25\text{ m} \\
V&=\tfrac12\cdot8.25\cdot1.2\cdot7=34.65\text{ m}^3=34{,}650\text{ litres}
\end{aligned}" />

# Filling time

**Divide the volume by the flow rate**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{34{,}650}{7.7}=4{,}500\text{ s} \\
&=75\text{ minutes}=1\text{ hour }15\text{ minutes}
\end{aligned}" />

Check: in <InlineMath math="4{,}500" /> seconds the pipe delivers <InlineMath math="7.7\cdot4{,}500=34{,}650" /> litres, exactly the volume of the water triangle.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="18" /> minutes <InlineMath math="45" /> seconds comes from using the <InlineMath math="7\text{ cm}" /> diameter as the radius. The cross-section becomes four times as big, so the time is a quarter.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> hour comes from using the deep-end depth <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m}" /> in the similar triangles, when the bottom only drops <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ m}" />. That gives <InlineMath math="\ell=6.6\text{ m}" />, which is too short.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> hour <InlineMath math="40" /> minutes comes from stretching the water triangle over the whole <InlineMath math="11\text{ m}" />. That's only true at the moment the water reaches the shallow end.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> hours <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes comes from treating the water as an <InlineMath math="11\times7\times1.2" /> cuboid, as if **the bottom were flat**.

So the time needed is <InlineMath math="1" /> hour <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes.

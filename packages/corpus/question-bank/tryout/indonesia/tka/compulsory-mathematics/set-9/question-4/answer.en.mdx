The question asks for the smallest order size that makes the profit per shirt **more than** <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of the average cost. The data are average costs, but what's linear is the total cost. So the first step is turning averages into totals.

# Total cost from average cost

The average cost per shirt is the total cost divided by the number of shirts. So **the total cost is the average times the number of shirts**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
B(40)&=40\cdot65{,}000=2{,}600{,}000 \\
B(100)&=100\cdot47{,}000=4{,}700{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

The total cost is linear, <InlineMath math="B(n)=a+bn" />, where <InlineMath math="a" /> is the fixed cost and <InlineMath math="b" /> the cost per shirt. Get the slope from those two points.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
b&=\frac{4{,}700{,}000-2{,}600{,}000}{100-40}=\frac{2{,}100{,}000}{60}=35{,}000 \\
a&=2{,}600{,}000-40\cdot35{,}000=1{,}200{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="B(n)=1{,}200{,}000+35{,}000n" />, and the average cost is <InlineMath math="R(n)=35{,}000+\dfrac{1{,}200{,}000}{n}" />.

# The limit on the average cost

The profit per shirt is the selling price minus the average cost, <InlineMath math="54{,}000-R" />. It has to be more than <InlineMath math="20\%" /> **of the average cost**, not of the selling price.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
54{,}000-R&>0.2R \\
54{,}000&>1.2R \\
R&<45{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

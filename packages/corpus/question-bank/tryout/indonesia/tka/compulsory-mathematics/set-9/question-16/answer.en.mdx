# From the map to real sizes

The bar scale says <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> on the map stands for <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" />, so <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> stands for <InlineMath math="25\text{ m}" />. Convert the three lengths you need, then work out the trapezium's area:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AB&=11\cdot25=275\text{ m} \\
DC&=5\cdot25=125\text{ m} \\
AD&=8\cdot25=200\text{ m} \\
A&=\frac{275+125}{2}\cdot200=200\cdot200=40{,}000\text{ m}^2
\end{aligned}" />

Another route gives the same result. The area of the drawing is <InlineMath math="\frac{11+5}{2}\cdot8=64\text{ cm}^2" />. Each <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}^2" /> on the map is a <InlineMath math="25\text{ m}\times25\text{ m}" /> square on the ground, which is <InlineMath math="625\text{ m}^2" />. **For areas, the scale gets squared**: <InlineMath math="64\cdot625=40{,}000\text{ m}^2" />.

Finally, <InlineMath math="1" /> hectare is <InlineMath math="10{,}000\text{ m}^2" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{40{,}000}{10{,}000}=4\text{ hectares}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="5" /> hectares comes from using <InlineMath math="BC=10" /> cm as the height. **That side is slanted**, so it's longer than the real height.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> hectares comes from forgetting to divide by <InlineMath math="2" /> in the trapezium formula.
- <InlineMath math="40" /> hectares comes from treating <InlineMath math="1" /> hectare as <InlineMath math="1{,}000\text{ m}^2" />.
- <InlineMath math="64" /> hectares comes from reading the bar scale as <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> for <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" />, when the <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> belongs to the whole <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> bar.

So the real area of the garden is <InlineMath math="4" /> hectares.

# Judging each statement

First statement: the three points <InlineMath math="D" /> are different, so there are exactly three possibilities. This statement is **True**.

Second statement: check the angle at <InlineMath math="B" /> with the dot product.

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{BA}\cdot\overrightarrow{BC}=(-2,4)\cdot(6,3)=-12+12=0" />

Angle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is a right angle, so parallelogram <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> with <InlineMath math="D(7,7)" /> is **a rectangle**. This statement is **True**.

Third statement: each parallelogram uses the two sides of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> that meet at the vertex opposite <InlineMath math="D" />. The triangle's sides are <InlineMath math="AB=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5" />, <InlineMath math="BC=\sqrt{45}=3\sqrt5" /> and <InlineMath math="AC=\sqrt{65}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
K_1&=2(AB+BC)=2(2\sqrt5+3\sqrt5)=10\sqrt5 \\
K_2&=2(CA+CB)=2(\sqrt{65}+3\sqrt5) \\
K_3&=2(AB+AC)=2(2\sqrt5+\sqrt{65})
\end{aligned}" />

The three perimeters are different. What they do share is the area, twice the area of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />. This statement is **False**.

So the answers are True, True, False.

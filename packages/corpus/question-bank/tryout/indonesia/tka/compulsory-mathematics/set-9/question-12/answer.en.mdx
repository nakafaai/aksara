In an isosceles triangle **the base angles are equal**, and the bisector of the top angle is also an altitude and a median.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\angle ABC=\angle ACB&=\frac{180^\circ-40^\circ}{2}=70^\circ \\
\angle BAD=\angle CAD&=\frac{40^\circ}{2}=20^\circ \\
\angle ADB&=180^\circ-70^\circ-20^\circ=90^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

- <InlineMath math="\angle ABC=70^\circ" />, as worked out. This statement is true.
- <InlineMath math="\angle ADB=90^\circ" />, so <InlineMath math="AD" /> is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="BC" />. This statement is true.
- The bisector halves <InlineMath math="40^\circ" />, so <InlineMath math="\angle BAD=20^\circ" />. This statement is false.
- <InlineMath math="AB=AC" />, <InlineMath math="\angle BAD=\angle CAD" />, and <InlineMath math="AD" /> is shared, so the triangles are congruent by side, angle, side. This statement is true.

The question asks for the height of a pole standing on a slope. The distance is measured along the slope, but **the angle of elevation is measured from the horizontal**, not from the slope. So you need two right triangles that share one horizontal side.

# Where the foot of the pole is

Call the foot of the pole <InlineMath math="F" />, its top <InlineMath math="T" />, and let <InlineMath math="Q" /> be the point directly below <InlineMath math="F" /> at the same level as <InlineMath math="P" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="PQF" /> has its right angle at <InlineMath math="Q" />, hypotenuse <InlineMath math="PF=16" /> m, and the angle at <InlineMath math="P" /> is the slope angle <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
PQ&=16\cos30^\circ=16\cdot\tfrac12\sqrt3=8\sqrt3\text{ m} \\
QF&=16\sin30^\circ=16\cdot\tfrac12=8\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

That means the foot of the pole is **already <InlineMath math="8" /> m higher** than <InlineMath math="P" />.

# The height of the top and of the pole

Triangle <InlineMath math="PQT" /> also has its right angle at <InlineMath math="Q" />, with the same horizontal side <InlineMath math="PQ=8\sqrt3" /> and the <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> angle of elevation at <InlineMath math="P" />. The top is <InlineMath math="QT" /> above <InlineMath math="P" />, and the pole is the part from <InlineMath math="F" /> to <InlineMath math="T" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
QT&=PQ\cdot\tan60^\circ=8\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt3=24\text{ m} \\
FT&=QT-QF=24-8=16\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Check with triangle <InlineMath math="PFT" />. The angle at <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="60^\circ-30^\circ=30^\circ" />. The angle at <InlineMath math="F" />, between the slope going down to <InlineMath math="P" /> and the pole going up, is <InlineMath math="90^\circ+30^\circ=120^\circ" />. That leaves <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> at <InlineMath math="T" /> too. The triangle is isosceles, so <InlineMath math="FT=FP=16" /> m. **It matches**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="8\text{ m}" /> comes from taking the angle at the foot of the pole as <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />. The pole is vertical and the slope goes down towards <InlineMath math="P" />, so that angle is <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{16}{3}\sqrt3\text{ m}" /> comes from treating the pole as perpendicular to the slope, so <InlineMath math="FT=16\tan30^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="24\text{ m}" /> is the height of the top above <InlineMath math="P" />. **It stops one step early** by forgetting to subtract the <InlineMath math="8" /> m rise of the foot.
- <InlineMath math="16\sqrt3\text{ m}" /> comes from treating the ground as flat and using <InlineMath math="16" /> m as a horizontal distance.

So the pole is <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}" /> tall.

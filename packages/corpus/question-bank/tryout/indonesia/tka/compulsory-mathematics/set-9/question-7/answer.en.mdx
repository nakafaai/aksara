The question asks for every number of bags <InlineMath math="x" /> that gives a profit of **at least** <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}600{,}000" />, so exactly <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}600{,}000" /> counts too. # The profit formula

Work with all money **in thousands of rupiah**. Revenue is the number of bags times the price per bag, and profit is revenue minus cost.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x\le32&:\ K(x)=x(120-x)-(900+40x)=-x^2+80x-900 \\
x\ge33&:\ K(x)=-x^2+80x-900-84=-x^2+80x-984
\end{aligned}" />

# The case without the rental fee

For <InlineMath math="x\le32" />, solve <InlineMath math="K(x)\ge600" />. Multiply by <InlineMath math="-1" /> and **flip the inequality sign**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
-x^2+80x-900&\ge600 \\
x^2-80x+1500&\le0 \\
(x-30)(x-50)&\le0 \\
30\le x&\le50
\end{aligned}" />

This case only covers <InlineMath math="x\le32" />, so what's left is <InlineMath math="x=30,\ 31,\ 32" />.

# The case with the rental fee

For <InlineMath math="x\ge33" />, the rental fee is subtracted too.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
-x^2+80x-984&\ge600 \\
x^2-80x+1584&\le0 \\
(x-36)(x-44)&\le0 \\
36\le x&\le44
\end{aligned}" />

All of these values really are above <InlineMath math="32" />, so **every one of them counts**: <InlineMath math="x=36,\ 37,\ \ldots,\ 44" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="30\le x\le44" /> merges the two cases into one interval without checking the gap. For <InlineMath math="x=33,\ 34,\ 35" /> the profit is **below** <InlineMath math="600" />.
- <InlineMath math="30\le x\le50" /> forgets the rental fee altogether.
- <InlineMath math="31\le x\le32" /> or <InlineMath math="37\le x\le43" /> drops the values whose profit is exactly <InlineMath math="600" />. At least means greater than or equal to.

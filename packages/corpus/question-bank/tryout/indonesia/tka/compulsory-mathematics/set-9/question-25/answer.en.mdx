A sum of two numbers is even when **both are even or both are odd**. From <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="20" /> there are <InlineMath math="10" /> even and <InlineMath math="10" /> odd numbers. The two cards are drawn together, so use combinations.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(S)&=\binom{20}{2}=190 \\
n(A)&=\binom{10}{2}+\binom{10}{2}=45+45=90 \\
P(A)&=\frac{90}{190}=\frac{9}{19}
\end{aligned}" />

The probability is a little below <InlineMath math="\frac12" />, because two cards can't carry the same number. There are more even-odd pairs, <InlineMath math="10\cdot10=100" />, than matching pairs.

So the probability that the sum is even is <InlineMath math="\frac{9}{19}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{9}{38}" /> counts only the pairs where both numbers are even.
- <InlineMath math="\frac14" /> uses <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot\frac12" />, as if the first card went back and only the even-even case counted.
- <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> assumes even and odd sums are equally likely.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{10}{19}" /> is the probability that the sum is odd.

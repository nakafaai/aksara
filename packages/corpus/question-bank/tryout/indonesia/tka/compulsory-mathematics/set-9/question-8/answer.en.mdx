The question asks for the largest number of the arithmetic sequence **before it was changed**. Two things decide it: the geometric condition gives two possible common differences, and the word naik (increasing) throws one of them out.

# Writing the three numbers

In an arithmetic sequence, **the middle term is the average of the three terms**. The sum is <InlineMath math="45" />, so the middle term is <InlineMath math="15" />. Write the three numbers with common difference <InlineMath math="d" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{original}&:\ 15-d,\ \ 15,\ \ 15+d \\
\text{after the change}&:\ 12-d,\ \ 15,\ \ 22+d
\end{aligned}" />

# The geometric condition

Three numbers form a geometric sequence when **the square of the middle term equals the product of the two outer terms**, because the ratio of the second term to the first equals the ratio of the third to the second.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
15^2&=(12-d)(22+d) \\
225&=264-10d-d^2 \\
d^2+10d-39&=0 \\
(d+13)(d-3)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="d=3" /> or <InlineMath math="d=-13" />.

# Choosing the right difference

Try both.

| Difference | Arithmetic sequence | Geometric sequence | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="d=3" /> | <InlineMath math="12,\ 15,\ 18" /> | <InlineMath math="9,\ 15,\ 25" /> | <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5}{3}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="d=-13" /> | <InlineMath math="28,\ 15,\ 2" /> | <InlineMath math="25,\ 15,\ 9" /> | <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{5}" /> |

Both satisfy the geometric condition, but the question says the arithmetic sequence is **increasing**. The sequence <InlineMath math="28,\ 15,\ 2" /> goes down, so <InlineMath math="d=-13" /> is out. The original sequence is <InlineMath math="12,\ 15,\ 18" />, and its largest number is <InlineMath math="18" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="25" /> is the largest term of the geometric sequence. That's a number **after the change**, so the added <InlineMath math="7" /> hasn't been taken back.
- <InlineMath math="32" /> comes from undoing the added <InlineMath math="7" /> the wrong way, <InlineMath math="25+7" />. You get the original third term by subtracting <InlineMath math="7" />.
- <InlineMath math="28" /> is the largest number of the decreasing sequence <InlineMath math="28,\ 15,\ 2" />, which the word increasing rules out.
- <InlineMath math="22" /> comes from using the decreasing geometric sequence <InlineMath math="25,\ 15,\ 9" /> and then undoing its first term the wrong way, <InlineMath math="25-3" />. That sequence is already ruled out, and its original first term would be <InlineMath math="25+3=28" />.

So the largest number of the original arithmetic sequence is <InlineMath math="18" />.

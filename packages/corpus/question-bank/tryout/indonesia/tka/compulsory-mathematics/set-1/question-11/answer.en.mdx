The lamp, Rani's head and the tip of the shadow lie on one line. So the triangle formed by the post and the whole shadow tip and the triangle formed by Rani and her shadow are **similar**. Let <InlineMath math="d" /> be Rani's distance from the post and <InlineMath math="s" /> the length of her shadow.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{s}{1.6}&=\frac{d+s}{4.8} \\
4.8s&=1.6d+1.6s \\
3.2s&=1.6d \\
s&=\frac d2
\end{aligned}" />

So the shadow is always half as long as the distance to the post. At <InlineMath math="d=10" />, <InlineMath math="s=5" />. For a <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" /> shadow Rani needs <InlineMath math="d=12\text{ m}" />, so she has to walk <InlineMath math="12-10=2\text{ m}" /> further.

As a check, <InlineMath math="\frac{6}{1.6}=3.75" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac{12+6}{4.8}=3.75" />.

So Rani has to walk <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> further.

- <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> assumes the shadow grows as fast as the distance. It grows only half as fast.
- <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> uses the height ratio <InlineMath math="4.8:1.6=3" /> directly as the answer.
- <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" /> is the new shadow length, not the extra distance walked.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}" /> is the new distance from the post, not the extra distance walked.

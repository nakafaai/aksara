# The shared part

The question asks how much Eka pays. The key is the peak-hour rule: **only the part of the fare charged per km is multiplied by <InlineMath math="1.2" />**, while the door fare stays <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}10{,}000" />.

The cleanest way is to cut the <InlineMath math="22\text{ km}" /> fare into the two parts they agreed on. Start with the first <InlineMath math="8\text{ km}" />. The door fare already covers the first <InlineMath math="2\text{ km}" />, so only km <InlineMath math="2" /> to km <InlineMath math="8" /> are charged per km. That's <InlineMath math="6\text{ km}" /> at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}4{,}500" /> per km.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{shared part}&=10{,}000+1.2\cdot4{,}500\cdot6 \\
&=10{,}000+32{,}400 \\
&=42{,}400
\end{aligned}" />

They split this part equally, so Dina and Eka each pay <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}21{,}200" />.

# The rest of the ride belongs to Eka

The rest of the ride, from km <InlineMath math="8" /> to km <InlineMath math="22" />, runs through two price levels. From km <InlineMath math="8" /> to km <InlineMath math="12" /> there are <InlineMath math="4\text{ km}" /> at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}4{,}500" />, and from km <InlineMath math="12" /> to km <InlineMath math="22" /> there are <InlineMath math="10\text{ km}" /> at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}3{,}500" />. **Both still get the factor <InlineMath math="1.2" />**, because the ride happens at peak hour.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{rest}&=1.2\,(4{,}500\cdot4+3{,}500\cdot10) \\
&=1.2\cdot53{,}000 \\
&=63{,}600
\end{aligned}" />

Eka pays half of the shared part plus the whole rest:

<BlockMath math="21{,}200+63{,}600=84{,}800" />

Check it against the total fare. A <InlineMath math="22\text{ km}" /> peak-hour ride has a per-km part of <InlineMath math="4{,}500\cdot10+3{,}500\cdot10=80{,}000" />, so the fare is <InlineMath math="10{,}000+1.2\cdot80{,}000=106{,}000" />. Dina pays <InlineMath math="21{,}200" /> and Eka <InlineMath math="84{,}800" />, which add up to exactly <InlineMath math="106{,}000" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}53{,}000" /> comes from splitting the whole <InlineMath math="106{,}000" /> in half. But **only the first <InlineMath math="8\text{ km}" /> are split**.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}63{,}600" /> is just the rest of the ride. It's what you get if Dina pays the whole shared part alone.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}71{,}500" /> comes from forgetting the factor <InlineMath math="1.2" />, so every part is priced at the off-peak rate.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}85{,}800" /> comes from multiplying the door fare by <InlineMath math="1.2" /> too. The rule says the door fare stays <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}10{,}000" />.

So Eka pays <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}84{,}800" />.

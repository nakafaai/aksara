Let the cuboid edge lengths be <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" />. Pythagoras on its three face types gives:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2+b^2&=41 \\
a^2+c^2&=52 \\
b^2+c^2&=61
\end{aligned}" />

Add the two equations containing the desired squared edge and subtract the third. The result is twice that squared edge.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2&=\frac{41+52-61}{2}=16 \\
b^2&=\frac{41+61-52}{2}=25 \\
c^2&=\frac{52+61-41}{2}=36
\end{aligned}" />

Edge lengths are positive, so <InlineMath math="a=4" />, <InlineMath math="b=5" />, and <InlineMath math="c=6" />.

<BlockMath math="a+b+c=4+5+6=15" />

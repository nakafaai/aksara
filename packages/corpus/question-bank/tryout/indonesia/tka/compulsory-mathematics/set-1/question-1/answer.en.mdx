Find <InlineMath math="s" /> before calculating <InlineMath math="s^2" />. Each radical contains a <Highlight>perfect-square factor</Highlight>, so all three can be written as multiples of <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{50}&=\sqrt{25\cdot2}=5\sqrt2 \\
\sqrt8&=\sqrt{4\cdot2}=2\sqrt2 \\
\sqrt{18}&=\sqrt{9\cdot2}=3\sqrt2
\end{aligned}" />

A negative exponent means taking the reciprocal of the corresponding positive power. Therefore,

<BlockMath math="2^{-2}=\frac{1}{2^2}=\frac14" />

Substitute the simplified radicals and power. Since <InlineMath math="3\sqrt2\ne0" />, the equal numerator and denominator in the first fraction give one.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
s&=\frac{5\sqrt2-2\sqrt2}{3\sqrt2}+\frac14 \\
&=\frac{(5-2)\sqrt2}{3\sqrt2}+\frac14 \\
&=\frac{3\sqrt2}{3\sqrt2}+\frac14 \\
&=1+\frac14=\frac44+\frac14=\frac54
\end{aligned}" />

The question asks for the <Highlight>square of the fraction</Highlight>. Square both the numerator and the denominator.

<BlockMath math="s^2=\left(\frac54\right)^2=\frac{5^2}{4^2}=\frac{25}{16}" />

The result is <InlineMath math="s^2=\frac{25}{16}" />. To check, <InlineMath math="s=1.25" /> gives <InlineMath math="s^2=1.5625" />, which equals <InlineMath math="\frac{25}{16}" />.

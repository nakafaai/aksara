Let <InlineMath math="n" /> be the number of rent rises of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000" />. The revenue is **rent per room times rooms let**, a quadratic function of <InlineMath math="n" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
R(n)&=(1{,}200{,}000+100{,}000n)(40-2n) \\
&=200{,}000\,(12+n)(20-n)
\end{aligned}" />

The zeros are <InlineMath math="n=-12" /> and <InlineMath math="n=20" />. The vertex of this downward parabola lies exactly halfway, at <InlineMath math="n=\frac{-12+20}{2}=4" />. The rent is then <InlineMath math="1{,}200{,}000+4\cdot100{,}000=1{,}600{,}000" />, and <InlineMath math="40-8=32" /> rooms are let.

<BlockMath math="R(4)=1{,}600{,}000\cdot32=51{,}200{,}000" />

As a check, <InlineMath math="n=3" /> and <InlineMath math="n=5" /> both give <InlineMath math="1{,}500{,}000\cdot34=1{,}700{,}000\cdot30=51{,}000{,}000" />, which is less.

So the largest revenue comes from a rent of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}600{,}000" />.

# Distance as a function of time

The question asks for the smallest distance between two cars that keep moving. The distance changes all the time, so **first write it as a function of time**, then find its smallest value.

Set up axes with <InlineMath math="O" /> at the origin, east as the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, and north as the positive <InlineMath math="y" />-axis. Let <InlineMath math="t" /> be the time in hours after <InlineMath math="07.00" />.

<BlockMath math="A=(0,\ 30-40t),\qquad B=(-5+30t,\ 0)" />

These formulas still work after a car passes <InlineMath math="O" />. For example, a negative <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate for car A just means A is south of <InlineMath math="O" />. The two roads are perpendicular, so the distance <InlineMath math="AB" /> comes from Pythagoras:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
d^2&=(-5+30t)^2+(30-40t)^2 \\
&=(25-300t+900t^2)+(900-2{,}400t+1{,}600t^2) \\
&=2{,}500t^2-2{,}700t+925
\end{aligned}" />

# The vertex of the quadratic

The distance <InlineMath math="d" /> is never negative, so <InlineMath math="d" /> is smallest exactly when <InlineMath math="d^2" /> is smallest. <InlineMath math="d^2" /> is a quadratic with a positive <InlineMath math="t^2" /> coefficient, so **its smallest value sits at the vertex**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{2{,}700}{2\cdot2{,}500}=0.54\text{ hours} \\
d^2_{\min}&=925-\frac{2{,}700^2}{4\cdot2{,}500}=925-729=196 \\
d_{\min}&=14
\end{aligned}" />

Check it with the positions at <InlineMath math="t=0.54" />, which is about <InlineMath math="07.32" />. Car A is <InlineMath math="30-21.6=8.4\text{ km}" /> north of <InlineMath math="O" />, and car B is <InlineMath math="-5+16.2=11.2\text{ km}" /> east of <InlineMath math="O" />.

<BlockMath math="\sqrt{8.4^2+11.2^2}=\sqrt{70.56+125.44}=\sqrt{196}=14" />

At that moment B has already passed the crossing and A hasn't reached it yet. So the closest moment **doesn't happen when one car is right at the crossing**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\sqrt{2}\text{ km}" /> comes from assuming the cars are closest when they're equally far from <InlineMath math="O" />, at <InlineMath math="t=0.5" />. That distance is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{10^2+10^2}\approx14.14" />, **a little more than <InlineMath math="14" />**.
- <InlineMath math="17.5\text{ km}" /> is the distance when A arrives at <InlineMath math="O" />, at <InlineMath math="t=0.75" />. By then B is already <InlineMath math="17.5\text{ km}" /> east of <InlineMath math="O" />.
- <InlineMath math="18\text{ km}" /> comes from starting B right at <InlineMath math="O" />, which forgets its <InlineMath math="5\text{ km}" /> to the west.
- <InlineMath math="22\text{ km}" /> comes from placing B <InlineMath math="5\text{ km}" /> east of <InlineMath math="O" />, so the sign of its position is flipped.

So the smallest distance between the two cars is <InlineMath math="14\text{ km}" />.

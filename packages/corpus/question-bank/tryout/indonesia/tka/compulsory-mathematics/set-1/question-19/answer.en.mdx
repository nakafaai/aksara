# The shape of the fuel cross-section

The question asks for the volume of the empty space inside the tank. The plan: find the volume of fuel already there, then subtract it from the full tank. To get litres straight away, work in decimetres, since <InlineMath math="1\text{ dm}^3=1" /> litre. The radius is <InlineMath math="6\text{ dm}" />, the tank is <InlineMath math="20\text{ dm}" /> long, and the fuel is <InlineMath math="3\text{ dm}" /> deep.

Because the tank lies on its side, its vertical cross-section is a circle of radius <InlineMath math="6" />. The fuel fills the bottom part of the circle cut off by a horizontal chord, which is a **circular segment**. The area of a segment is the area of the sector minus the area of the triangle formed by the centre and the two ends of the chord.

The distance from the centre to the fuel surface is <InlineMath math="6-3=3" />. Draw a radius from the centre to one end of the chord. You get a right triangle with hypotenuse <InlineMath math="6" /> and a leg of <InlineMath math="3" /> next to the angle, so half the central angle, <InlineMath math="\alpha" />, satisfies <InlineMath math="\cos\alpha=\tfrac12" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\cos\alpha&=\frac{3}{6}=\frac12\;\Rightarrow\;\alpha=60^\circ,\quad\text{central angle}=120^\circ \\
\text{half chord}&=6\sin60^\circ=3\sqrt{3},\quad\text{chord}=6\sqrt{3} \\
L_{\text{sector}}&=\frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ}\cdot\pi\cdot6^2=12\pi \\
L_{\text{triangle}}&=\tfrac12\cdot6\sqrt{3}\cdot3=9\sqrt{3} \\
L_{\text{segment}}&=12\pi-9\sqrt{3}\text{ dm}^2
\end{aligned}" />

The fuel's volume is the segment area times the length of the tank.

<BlockMath math="V_{\text{fuel}}=20\,(12\pi-9\sqrt{3})=240\pi-180\sqrt{3}\text{ litres}" />

# The space that's still empty

A full tank holds the circle's area times the length. **The empty space is the full volume minus the fuel**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{full}}&=\pi\cdot6^2\cdot20=720\pi\text{ litres} \\
V_{\text{empty}}&=720\pi-(240\pi-180\sqrt{3}) \\
&=480\pi+180\sqrt{3}\text{ litres}
\end{aligned}" />

Check that it makes sense. The fuel already there is about <InlineMath math="754-312=442" /> litres, while a quarter of the tank is about <InlineMath math="565" /> litres. So a depth of a quarter of the diameter does **not** mean a quarter full. That fits, because the circle is narrow at the bottom and widens towards the middle.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option comes from one slip in the segment or in the last step. Watch **the sign of the triangle and the quantity being asked for**.

- <InlineMath math="(240\pi-180\sqrt{3})" /> litres is the fuel already in the tank, not the empty space.
- <InlineMath math="(480\pi-180\sqrt{3})" /> litres comes from taking the segment as sector plus triangle. The triangle sits above the fuel surface, so it has to be subtracted.
- <InlineMath math="480\pi" /> litres comes from using only the sector and forgetting to subtract the triangle.
- <InlineMath math="540\pi" /> litres comes from reading a depth of <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> of the diameter as <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> of the volume. A lying tank's cross-section isn't a rectangle, so the ratio of depths isn't the ratio of volumes.

So the volume of fuel that can still be added is <InlineMath math="(480\pi+180\sqrt{3})" /> litres, about <InlineMath math="1{,}820" /> litres.

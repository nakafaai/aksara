A face area depends on its two edge lengths, while volume depends on all three. Multiply the corresponding scale factors.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
k_{pl}&=2\cdot3=6 \\
k_{lt}&=3\cdot\frac12=\frac32 \\
k_V&=2\cdot3\cdot\frac12=3
\end{aligned}" />

The directional scale factors differ, so the space diagonal and total surface area do not always change by one fixed factor. For a counterexample, take <InlineMath math="p=l=t=1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
d^2&=1^2+1^2+1^2=3 \\
(d')^2&=2^2+3^2+\left(\frac12\right)^2 \\
&=\frac{53}{4}\ne4d^2 \\
A&=6 \\
A'&=2\left(6+1+\frac32\right)=17 \\
A'&\ne3A
\end{aligned}" />

The three claims about face-area and volume factors are true. The claims that the diagonal always doubles and the total surface area always triples are false.

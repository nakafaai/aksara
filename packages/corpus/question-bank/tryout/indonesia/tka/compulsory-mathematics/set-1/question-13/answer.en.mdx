# Two kinds of belt pieces

The belt is made of **two straight pieces of equal length and two arcs**, one arc on each wheel. So you need two things: the length of one straight piece and the angle of each arc.

# The length of a straight piece

A straight piece touches both wheels, so it's perpendicular to the radius at each point of contact. Slide that piece toward the centre of the small wheel by <InlineMath math="2" /> cm. **You get a right triangle** with hypotenuse <InlineMath math="12" /> (the distance between centres) and one side <InlineMath math="8-2=6" /> (the difference of the radii).

<BlockMath math="t=\sqrt{12^2-6^2}=\sqrt{108}=6\sqrt3" />

# The angle of each arc

In that triangle, the side <InlineMath math="6" /> is half of the hypotenuse <InlineMath math="12" />, so **a <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> angle appears**. That tilts each radius to a contact point <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> away from the half-circle position. The belt wraps more than half of the big wheel and less than half of the small one.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{big wheel}&:\ 180^\circ+2\cdot30^\circ=240^\circ \\
\text{small wheel}&:\ 180^\circ-2\cdot30^\circ=120^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

# The length of the belt

An arc is the share of the circumference that matches its angle. **Add the two straight pieces and the two arcs**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{big arc}&=\tfrac{240}{360}\cdot2\pi\cdot8=\tfrac{32\pi}{3} \\
\text{small arc}&=\tfrac{120}{360}\cdot2\pi\cdot2=\tfrac{4\pi}{3} \\
\text{belt length}&=2\cdot6\sqrt3+\tfrac{32\pi}{3}+\tfrac{4\pi}{3}=12\sqrt3+12\pi
\end{aligned}" />

So the belt is <InlineMath math="(12\sqrt3+12\pi)" /> cm long.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option **gets one part of the belt wrong**.

- <InlineMath math="(12\sqrt3+8\pi)" /> cm swaps the arc angles, giving the big wheel <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> and the small wheel <InlineMath math="240^\circ" />. But the belt hugs the big wheel more.
- <InlineMath math="(6\sqrt3+12\pi)" /> cm counts only one straight piece, but there are two.
- <InlineMath math="(4\sqrt{11}+12\pi)" /> cm uses the sum of the radii, <InlineMath math="8+2" />. That's for a crossed belt.
- <InlineMath math="(12\sqrt3+10\pi)" /> cm assumes the belt wraps exactly half of each wheel. That's only true when both wheels are the same size.

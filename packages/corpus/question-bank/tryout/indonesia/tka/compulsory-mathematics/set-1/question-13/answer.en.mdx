# The shape of APQB

A radius drawn to a point of tangency is always **perpendicular to the tangent line**. So <InlineMath math="AP" /> and <InlineMath math="BQ" /> are both perpendicular to <InlineMath math="PQ" />, which makes them parallel. The quadrilateral <InlineMath math="APQB" /> is a right trapezoid with parallel sides <InlineMath math="9" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />, and its height is <InlineMath math="PQ" />.

# The length of the tangent PQ

The circles touch on the outside, so **the distance between the centres is the sum of the radii**, <InlineMath math="AB=9+4=13\text{ cm}" />. Draw a line from <InlineMath math="B" /> parallel to <InlineMath math="PQ" /> until it meets <InlineMath math="AP" />. That makes a right triangle with hypotenuse <InlineMath math="13" /> and one leg <InlineMath math="9-4=5" />:

<BlockMath math="PQ=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{169-25}=\sqrt{144}=12\text{ cm}" />

# The area of the trapezoid

<BlockMath math="\text{area}=\frac{AP+BQ}{2}\cdot PQ=\frac{9+4}{2}\cdot12=78\text{ cm}^2" />

So **the area of <InlineMath math="APQB" /> is <InlineMath math="78\text{ cm}^2" />**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="156\text{ cm}^2" /> forgets to halve in the trapezoid area formula.
- <InlineMath math="65\text{ cm}^2" /> is <InlineMath math="13\cdot5" />, the product of two sides of the helper triangle. **The area of the quadrilateral isn't the product of any two segments**.
- <InlineMath math="54\text{ cm}^2" /> is only the triangle <InlineMath math="\frac12\cdot9\cdot12" />. The part below <InlineMath math="B" /> is missing.
- <InlineMath math="84.5\text{ cm}^2" /> uses <InlineMath math="AB=13" /> as the height. The trapezoid's height is <InlineMath math="PQ" />, which is perpendicular to both parallel sides.

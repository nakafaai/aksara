In <InlineMath math="(g\circ f)(a)=g(f(a))" />, **<InlineMath math="f" /> acts first**, and its result is then substituted into <InlineMath math="g" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
g(f(a))&=(2a+5)^2-4=45 \\
(2a+5)^2&=49 \\
2a+5=7\ \text{or}\ 2a+5&=-7 \\
a=1\ \text{or}\ a&=-6
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, <InlineMath math="f(1)=7" /> and <InlineMath math="g(7)=45" />, and <InlineMath math="f(-6)=-7" /> and <InlineMath math="g(-7)=45" />.

So the sum of all solutions is <InlineMath math="1+(-6)=-5" />.

- <InlineMath math="5" /> appears if the <InlineMath math="5" /> moves to the other side with the wrong sign: <InlineMath math="2a=\pm7+5" /> gives <InlineMath math="6" /> and <InlineMath math="-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> is only the positive solution. The equation <InlineMath math="(2a+5)^2=49" /> has two solutions.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> comes from the wrong order: <InlineMath math="f(g(a))=2a^2-3=45" /> has two solutions of opposite sign.
- <InlineMath math="-6" /> is only the negative solution. By coincidence it is also the product of the two solutions.

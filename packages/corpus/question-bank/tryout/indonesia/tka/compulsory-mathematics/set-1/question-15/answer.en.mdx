Here you only judge whether the information is **enough for a definite answer**, not what the answer is. Test each statement on its own first, then both together. A statement is enough when it answers the question the same way in every possible case.

A rotation by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> anticlockwise about the origin maps <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-b,a)" />. The image lies in quadrant II exactly when <InlineMath math="-b<0" /> and <InlineMath math="a>0" />, that is, when <InlineMath math="a>0" /> **and** <InlineMath math="b>0" />.

- **Statement (1) alone**: with <InlineMath math="A(1,1)" />, <InlineMath math="A'(-1,1)" /> is in quadrant II, but with <InlineMath math="A(1,-1)" />, <InlineMath math="A'(1,1)" /> is in quadrant I. The answer depends on <InlineMath math="b" />, so the statement is not enough.
- **Statement (2) alone**: with <InlineMath math="A(1,1)" />, <InlineMath math="A'" /> is in quadrant II, but with <InlineMath math="A(-1,1)" />, <InlineMath math="A'(-1,-1)" /> is in quadrant III. This statement is not enough either.
- **Both together**: <InlineMath math="a>0" /> and <InlineMath math="b>0" /> give <InlineMath math="-b<0" /> and <InlineMath math="a>0" />, so <InlineMath math="A'" /> is certainly in quadrant II.

So only both statements together are enough, and neither one alone is.

- The option that statement (1) alone is enough is wrong, because statement (1) alone allows two different answers.
- The option that statement (2) alone is enough is wrong, because statement (2) alone allows two different answers.
- The option that each statement alone is enough is wrong, because statement (1) alone allows two different answers.
- The option that even both together are not enough is wrong, because both statements together answer the question definitely.

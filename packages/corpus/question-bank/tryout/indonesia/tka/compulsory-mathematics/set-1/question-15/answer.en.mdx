# Finding both images

The question asks for the area of the quadrilateral made by segment <InlineMath math="AB" /> and its image. The centre of rotation is <InlineMath math="P(1,1)" />, **not the origin**, so you can't apply the rule <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)" /> straight to the coordinates of <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />.

It takes three steps. First, find each point's position relative to <InlineMath math="P" />. Second, turn that relative position with <InlineMath math="(u,v)\mapsto(-v,u)" />. Third, add the coordinates of <InlineMath math="P" /> back.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A-P&=(3,1)\;\mapsto\;(-1,3), & A'&=(1-1,\;1+3)=(0,4) \\
B-P&=(5,3)\;\mapsto\;(-3,5), & B'&=(1-3,\;1+5)=(-2,6)
\end{aligned}" />

# Working out the area

The vertices, in order, are <InlineMath math="A(4,2)" />, <InlineMath math="B(6,4)" />, <InlineMath math="B'(-2,6)" /> and <InlineMath math="A'(0,4)" />. A safe method is **the bounding rectangle minus the triangles outside**. The smallest rectangle around the four points runs from <InlineMath math="x=-2" /> to <InlineMath math="x=6" /> and from <InlineMath math="y=2" /> to <InlineMath math="y=6" />, so its area is <InlineMath math="8\cdot4=32" />.

The part of the rectangle outside the quadrilateral splits into four triangles, each with a horizontal or vertical base and height.

| Triangle | Base | Height | Area |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <InlineMath math="A,\,(6,2),\,B" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |
| <InlineMath math="B,\,(6,6),\,B'" /> | <InlineMath math="8" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="8" /> |
| <InlineMath math="B',\,(-2,2),\,A'" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(-2,2),\,A,\,A'" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> |

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{ABB'A'}&=32-(2+8+4+6) \\
&=12\text{ square units}
\end{aligned}" />

There's a quick way to check. Triangles <InlineMath math="PAA'" /> and <InlineMath math="PBB'" /> are right isosceles triangles at <InlineMath math="P" />, because a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> rotation keeps every distance to the centre. Triangle <InlineMath math="PAB" /> and its image <InlineMath math="PA'B'" /> are congruent, so their contributions cancel. What's left is the difference of the two right triangles.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
PA^2&=3^2+1^2=10, \qquad PB^2=5^2+3^2=34 \\
L_{ABB'A'}&=\tfrac12 PB^2-\tfrac12 PA^2=17-5=12
\end{aligned}" />

Both ways give the same result.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option comes from a slip in the rotation or in the area step. The **centre of rotation and the minus sign** are the two places to watch.

- <InlineMath math="22" /> comes from adding the area of triangle <InlineMath math="PAA'" />, <InlineMath math="17+5" />. It has to be subtracted.
- <InlineMath math="17" /> is only the area of triangle <InlineMath math="PBB'" />. Triangle <InlineMath math="PAA'" /> was never removed.
- <InlineMath math="16" /> comes from rotating about the origin, which gives <InlineMath math="A'=(-2,4)" /> and <InlineMath math="B'=(-4,6)" />. The centre is <InlineMath math="P(1,1)" />.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> comes from treating <InlineMath math="ABB'A'" /> as a square with side <InlineMath math="AB=2\sqrt{2}" />. But <InlineMath math="AA'=\sqrt{20}" />, which isn't equal to <InlineMath math="AB" />.

So the area of quadrilateral <InlineMath math="ABB'A'" /> is <InlineMath math="12" /> square units.

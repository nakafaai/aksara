# Probabilities in terms of r

Since <InlineMath math="r" /> is unknown, **write each probability as a formula in <InlineMath math="r" />**. The second ball can be red after a red first ball or after a white first ball.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(A)&=\frac{r}{8} \\
P(A\cap B)&=\frac{r}{8}\cdot\frac{r-1}{7}=\frac{r(r-1)}{56} \\
P(B)&=\frac{r(r-1)}{56}+\frac{8-r}{8}\cdot\frac{r}{7}=\frac{7r}{56}=\frac{r}{8} \\
P(B\mid A)&=\frac{r-1}{7}
\end{aligned}" />

So **<InlineMath math="P(B)" /> equals <InlineMath math="P(A)" />**. That makes sense: before you see anything, the second ball is just as random as the first.

# Judging each statement

1. **False**. The two probabilities are equal, <InlineMath math="P(B)=P(A)=\frac r8" />.
2. **True**. <InlineMath math="\frac{r-1}{7}<\frac r8" /> is the same as <InlineMath math="8r-8<7r" />, which is <InlineMath math="r<8" />. That always holds.
3. **False**. The chance of the same colour is <InlineMath math="\frac{r(r-1)+(8-r)(7-r)}{56}" />. For <InlineMath math="r=4" /> it's <InlineMath math="\frac{24}{56}=\frac37" />, but for <InlineMath math="r=1" /> it's <InlineMath math="\frac{42}{56}=\frac34" />. In fact <InlineMath math="r=4" /> gives the smallest chance.
4. **True**. Independent would mean <InlineMath math="P(B\mid A)=P(B)" />. But <InlineMath math="\frac{r-1}{7}" /> is always smaller than <InlineMath math="\frac r8" />, as in the second statement.
5. **True**. <InlineMath math="P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}" /> and <InlineMath math="P(B\mid A)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(A)}" />. The numerators match, and so do the denominators, because <InlineMath math="P(A)=P(B)" />.

So the true statements are the second, fourth, and fifth.

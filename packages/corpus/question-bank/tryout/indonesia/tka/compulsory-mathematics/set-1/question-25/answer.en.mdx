The order of selection does not matter, so count with **binomial coefficients**. At least two girls means exactly two or exactly three.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{all selections}&=\binom93=84 \\
\text{exactly 2 girls}&=\binom42\binom51=6\cdot5=30 \\
\text{exactly 3 girls}&=\binom43=4 \\
P&=\frac{30+4}{84}=\frac{34}{84}=\frac{17}{42}
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, no girl or exactly one girl gives <InlineMath math="\binom53+\binom41\binom52=10+40=50" /> selections, and <InlineMath math="34+50=84" />.

So the probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{17}{42}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{21}=\frac{4}{84}" /> counts only the selection with three girls.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{14}=\frac{30}{84}" /> counts only exactly two girls and forgets the case with three.
- <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> would need <InlineMath math="42" /> favourable selections, but there are <InlineMath math="34" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{25}{42}=\frac{50}{84}" /> is the complement, at most one girl.

For events that do not distinguish order, there are <InlineMath math="\binom92=36" /> equally likely pairs. Count same-color pairs by selecting two balls of one color.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
N&=\binom42+\binom32+\binom22 \\
&=6+3+1=10 \\
P&=\frac{10}{36}=\frac5{18}
\end{aligned}" />

Exactly one red means choosing one of the four red balls and one of the five other balls.

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{4\cdot5}{36}=\frac59" />

If the first ball is green, eight balls remain, including three blue balls. The conditional probability of a blue second ball is <InlineMath math="\frac38" />. The first draw changes the second-draw probabilities. For example, after a blue ball is drawn, the next blue probability is <InlineMath math="\frac28" />, compared with <InlineMath math="\frac39" /> before drawing.

Thus the three numerical probability statements are true, while independence of the two drawn colors is false.

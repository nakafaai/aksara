For events that **do not distinguish order**, there are <InlineMath math="\binom92=\frac{9\cdot8}{2}=36" /> equally likely pairs. Count same-color pairs by selecting two balls of one color.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
N&=\binom42+\binom32+\binom22 \\
&=\frac{4\cdot3}{2}+\frac{3\cdot2}{2}+\frac{2\cdot1}{2} \\
&=6+3+1=10 \\
P&=\frac{10}{36}=\frac5{18}
\end{aligned}" />

Exactly one red means choosing one of the four red balls and one of the five other balls.

<BlockMath math="P=\frac{4\cdot5}{36}=\frac{20}{36}=\frac59" />

If the first ball is green, eight balls remain, including three blue balls. The conditional probability of a blue second ball is <InlineMath math="\frac38" />. The first draw changes the second-draw probabilities. For example, after a blue ball is drawn, the next blue probability is <InlineMath math="\frac28" />, compared with <InlineMath math="\frac39" /> before drawing.

Thus the three numerical probability statements are true, while independence of the two drawn colors is false.

The changing contents of the bag show why the second colour depends on the first draw.

| State before the next draw | Red | Blue | Green | Total | Blue probability |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Before the first draw | 4 | 3 | 2 | 9 | <InlineMath math="\frac39" /> |
| After drawing one green ball | 4 | 3 | 1 | 8 | <InlineMath math="\frac38" /> |
| After drawing one blue ball | 4 | 2 | 2 | 8 | <InlineMath math="\frac28" /> |

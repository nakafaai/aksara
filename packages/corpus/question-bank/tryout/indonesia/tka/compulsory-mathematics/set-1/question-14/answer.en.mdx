In a dilation with centre <InlineMath math="M" /> and factor <InlineMath math="k" />, **the vector from <InlineMath math="M" /> to the point is multiplied by <InlineMath math="k" />**. Areas are multiplied by <InlineMath math="k^2" />, because every length is multiplied by <InlineMath math="|k|" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
B'&=M+k\,(B-M)=(1,2)+(-2)\,(0,3)=(1,-4)
\end{aligned}" />

The area of the original triangle comes from the determinant formula.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_{ABC}&=\frac12\,\bigl|(1-3)(1-2)-(-1-3)(5-2)\bigr|=\frac12\,|2+12|=7 \\
L_{A'B'C'}&=(-2)^2\cdot7=28
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, <InlineMath math="A'=(1,2)+(-2)(2,0)=(-3,2)" /> and <InlineMath math="C'=(1,2)+(-2)(-2,-1)=(5,4)" />. The determinant formula for <InlineMath math="A'B'C'" /> gives <InlineMath math="\frac12\,|4\cdot2-8\cdot(-6)|=\frac12\cdot56=28" />.

So <InlineMath math="B'(1,-4)" />, and the image triangle has area <InlineMath math="28" />.

- The area <InlineMath math="14" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="|k|=2" /> instead of <InlineMath math="k^2=4" />.
- <InlineMath math="B'(1,8)" /> uses the factor <InlineMath math="+2" /> instead of <InlineMath math="-2" />.
- <InlineMath math="B'(-2,-10)" /> dilates from the origin instead of from <InlineMath math="M" />.
- The area <InlineMath math="7" /> belongs to the original triangle. A dilation with <InlineMath math="|k|\neq1" /> changes the area.

# Finding the radius

The question asks for the length of the path made by every point <InlineMath math="C" /> that sees segment <InlineMath math="AB" /> under an angle of <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />. Those points lie on one circle through <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />. So you need two things: the radius of that circle, and which part of the circle actually counts.

The tool is **an inscribed angle is half the central angle** on the same arc. Let <InlineMath math="O" /> be the centre of the circle. The inscribed angle <InlineMath math="\angle ACB" /> stands on the arc <InlineMath math="AB" /> that doesn't contain <InlineMath math="C" />, so the central angle is twice as big.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\angle AOB&=2\cdot\angle ACB=2\cdot45^\circ=90^\circ \\
AB^2&=OA^2+OB^2=2R^2 \\
64&=2R^2 \\
R&=4\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Triangle <InlineMath math="AOB" /> has a right angle at <InlineMath math="O" />, and both legs equal the radius <InlineMath math="R" />. Segment <InlineMath math="AB" /> is the hypotenuse, not a leg.

# Choosing the right arc

Chord <InlineMath math="AB" /> splits the circle into two arcs. The minor arc measures <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />: it's the arc the central angle stands on. The major arc measures <InlineMath math="360^\circ-90^\circ=270^\circ" />.

A point on the major arc sees <InlineMath math="AB" /> under <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />. A point on the minor arc sees it under <InlineMath math="180^\circ-45^\circ=135^\circ" />, because opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />. The minor arc also sits on the other side of line <InlineMath math="AB" />. So **the points on one side make up only the major arc**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{arc length}&=\frac{270^\circ}{360^\circ}\cdot2\pi R \\
&=\frac{3}{4}\cdot2\pi\cdot4\sqrt{2} \\
&=6\sqrt{2}\,\pi\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Check it with one special point. Take <InlineMath math="C" /> so that <InlineMath math="AC" /> is a diameter. The angle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> stands on a diameter, so it's <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. With <InlineMath math="\angle ACB=45^\circ" />, triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is a right isosceles triangle, so <InlineMath math="BC=AB=8" /> and <InlineMath math="AC=8\sqrt{2}=2R" />. That matches <InlineMath math="R=4\sqrt{2}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option comes from one step that slipped. Watch **which arc and which radius** you use.

- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\text{ cm}" /> is the minor arc of <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. On that arc the angle <InlineMath math="ACB" /> is <InlineMath math="135^\circ" />, not <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="8\sqrt{2}\,\pi\text{ cm}" /> is the whole circumference. It counts the minor arc too, where the points don't meet the condition.
- <InlineMath math="12\pi\text{ cm}" /> comes from treating <InlineMath math="AB=8" /> as the radius. In triangle <InlineMath math="AOB" />, <InlineMath math="AB" /> is the hypotenuse, so <InlineMath math="R=\frac{8}{\sqrt{2}}" />.
- <InlineMath math="12\sqrt{2}\,\pi\text{ cm}" /> counts a major arc on both sides of line <InlineMath math="AB" />. The question only wants the points on one side.

So the arc that holds every point <InlineMath math="C" /> has length <InlineMath math="6\sqrt{2}\,\pi\text{ cm}" />.

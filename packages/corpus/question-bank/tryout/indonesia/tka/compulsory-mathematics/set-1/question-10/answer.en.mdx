# Splitting the lane into simple pieces

Lane 1 is made of **two rectangles on the straights and two half rings on the bends**. The lane is <InlineMath math="1.25\text{ m}" /> wide. On the bends, its inner edge has radius <InlineMath math="36\text{ m}" /> and its outer edge, the inner edge of lane 2, has radius <InlineMath math="37.25\text{ m}" />.

# The area of each piece

**The two half rings make one full ring**, a big circle minus a small one:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{straights}&=2\cdot85\cdot1.25=212.5 \\
\text{bends}&=3.14\cdot\left(37.25^2-36^2\right)=3.14\cdot91.5625=287.50625 \\
\text{lane area}&=212.5+287.50625=500.00625\approx500.01
\end{aligned}" />

# A quick check

A ring's area can be written <InlineMath math="\pi\left(R^2-r^2\right)=2\pi\cdot\frac{R+r}{2}\cdot(R-r)" />, the length of the middle circle times the width. So **a lane's area equals the length of its middle line times its width**. Lane 1's middle line has radius <InlineMath math="36.625" /> on the bends:

<BlockMath math="\left(170+2\cdot3.14\cdot36.625\right)\cdot1.25=400.005\cdot1.25=500.00625" />

Same result. So lane 1 covers about <InlineMath math="500.01\text{ m}^2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="495.10\text{ m}^2" /> is the length of lane 1's inner edge times the width. **The inner edge is the shortest path in the lane**, so this is too small.
- <InlineMath math="504.91\text{ m}^2" /> uses the outer edge, so it's too big.
- <InlineMath math="287.51\text{ m}^2" /> is only the bends.
- <InlineMath math="212.50\text{ m}^2" /> is only the straights.

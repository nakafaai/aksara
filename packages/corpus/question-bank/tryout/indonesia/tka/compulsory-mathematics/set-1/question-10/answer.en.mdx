From one vertex, an <InlineMath math="n" />-gon splits into <InlineMath math="n-2" /> triangles. Its interior angle sum is therefore <InlineMath math="(n-2)\cdot180^\circ" />. Use it to find **the number of sides first**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(n-2)\cdot180^\circ&=3{,}240^\circ \\
n-2&=18 \\
n&=20
\end{aligned}" />

The exterior angles of a polygon always add up to <InlineMath math="360^\circ" />. In a regular 20-gon they are all equal.

<BlockMath math="\frac{360^\circ}{20}=18^\circ" />

As a check, each interior angle is <InlineMath math="180^\circ-18^\circ=162^\circ" />, and <InlineMath math="20\cdot162^\circ=3{,}240^\circ" />.

So each exterior angle measures <InlineMath math="18^\circ" />.

- <InlineMath math="162^\circ" /> is the interior angle of the 20-gon, not its exterior angle.
- <InlineMath math="160^\circ" /> is the interior angle of the 18-gon from the same mistake.
- <InlineMath math="20^\circ" /> comes from taking <InlineMath math="n=\frac{3{,}240}{180}=18" /> and forgetting the <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="12^\circ" /> belongs to a regular 30-gon, whose interior angle sum is <InlineMath math="5{,}040^\circ" />.

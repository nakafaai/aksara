#### Domain and Asymptotes

Write the numerator as <InlineMath math="2(x-1)+5" />. This form exposes the **nonzero denominator condition** and the lines approached by the graph.

<BlockMath math="f(x)=\frac{2(x-1)+5}{x-1}=2+\frac5{x-1}" />

The domain is <InlineMath math="\mathbb R\setminus\{1\}" />. As <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches one, the denominator approaches zero while the numerator of the fraction stays five. Thus <InlineMath math="x=1" /> is a vertical asymptote. As <InlineMath math="|x|" /> grows, the fraction approaches zero, so <InlineMath math="y=2" /> is a horizontal asymptote. The two lines intersect at <InlineMath math="(1,2)" />.

#### Decreasing on Each Interval

When <InlineMath math="x>1" />, the fraction <InlineMath math="5/(x-1)" /> is positive, giving <InlineMath math="f(x)>2" />. The graph has **two separate intervals**: <InlineMath math="(-\infty,1)" /> and <InlineMath math="(1,\infty)" />. Choose <InlineMath math="a<b" /> within the same interval. The difference between the outputs is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f(b)-f(a)&=\frac5{b-1}-\frac5{a-1} \\
&=\frac{5(a-1)-5(b-1)}{(a-1)(b-1)} \\
&=\frac{5(a-b)}{(a-1)(b-1)}<0
\end{aligned}" />

The two denominator factors have the same sign, so their product is positive. The numerator is negative because <InlineMath math="a-b<0" />. Therefore <InlineMath math="f(b)-f(a)<0" />, proving strict decrease on each interval. This statement does not combine the two branches into one interval.

#### Finding the Inverse

To find the **inverse function**, write <InlineMath math="y=f(x)" /> and solve for <InlineMath math="x" />. The fraction <InlineMath math="5/(x-1)" /> is never zero, so <InlineMath math="y\ne2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y&=\frac{2x+3}{x-1} \\
y(x-1)&=2x+3 \\
xy-y&=2x+3 \\
xy-2x&=y+3 \\
x(y-2)&=y+3 \\
x&=\frac{y+3}{y-2}
\end{aligned}" />

Every <InlineMath math="y\ne2" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=1+5/(y-2)\ne1" />. Thus every real value except two belongs to the range. Rename the variables to write the inverse:

<BlockMath math="f^{-1}(x)=\frac{x+3}{x-2},\qquad x\ne2" />

The claims about the asymptote intersection, decreasing behavior, values to the right of the asymptote, and the inverse are true. The claim <InlineMath math="f(1)=0" /> is false because division by zero makes <InlineMath math="f(1)" /> undefined.

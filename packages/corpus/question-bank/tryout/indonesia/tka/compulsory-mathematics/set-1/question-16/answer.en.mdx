# How the point and its image are linked

The question asks for the largest possible distance between point <InlineMath math="P" /> and its image <InlineMath math="Q" /> while <InlineMath math="P" /> moves around the circle. There are two steps: turn <InlineMath math="PQ" /> into a distance that's easier to handle, then find the point on the circle that makes it largest.

A dilation with centre <InlineMath math="C" /> and factor <InlineMath math="-2" /> means the vector from <InlineMath math="C" /> to <InlineMath math="Q" /> is <InlineMath math="-2" /> times the vector from <InlineMath math="C" /> to <InlineMath math="P" />. The minus sign puts <InlineMath math="Q" /> **on the far side of <InlineMath math="C" />**, on the same line as <InlineMath math="P" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
Q-C&=-2\,(P-C) \\
P-Q&=(P-C)-(Q-C)=(P-C)+2\,(P-C)=3\,(P-C) \\
PQ&=3\cdot CP
\end{aligned}" />

In other words, <InlineMath math="PQ=PC+CQ=CP+2\,CP" />. So <InlineMath math="PQ" /> is largest exactly when <InlineMath math="CP" /> is largest.

# The farthest point of the circle

Since <InlineMath math="OC=13" /> is bigger than the radius <InlineMath math="3" />, point <InlineMath math="C" /> is outside the circle. <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}

- <InlineMath math="39" /> uses <InlineMath math="OC=13" />, the distance to the centre of the circle, instead of the farthest point on it. The radius <InlineMath math="3" /> was never added.

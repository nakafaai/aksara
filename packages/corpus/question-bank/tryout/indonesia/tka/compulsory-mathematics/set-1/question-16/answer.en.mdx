The volume of a cylinder is **base area times height**. The base area depends on the radius, which is half the diameter.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
r&=\frac{1.4}{2}=0.7\text{ m} \\
V&=\pi r^2h=\frac{22}{7}\cdot0.49\cdot2=3.08\text{ m}^3
\end{aligned}" />

One cubic metre holds <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> litres, because <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}^3=1{,}000\text{ dm}^3" />. So <InlineMath math="3.08\text{ m}^3=3{,}080" /> litres.

As a check, <InlineMath math="\frac{22}{7}\cdot0.49=1.54" />, and <InlineMath math="1.54\cdot2=3.08" />.

So the tank holds <InlineMath math="3{,}080" /> litres.

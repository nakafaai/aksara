# Two equations for three numbers

Let <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" /> be the numbers of A, B, and C coupons sold. The question gives two equations, but there are three numbers. **Two equations alone don't pin down one answer**, so the other conditions have to choose it.

Write both equations with prices in thousands of rupiah, then subtract <InlineMath math="20" /> times the first equation from the second so that <InlineMath math="c" /> disappears:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+b+c&=120 \\
50a+30b+20c&=4{,}200 \\
30a+10b&=4{,}200-2{,}400=1{,}800 \\
b&=180-3a
\end{aligned}" />

Put this into the first equation to get <InlineMath math="c" />:

<BlockMath math="c=120-a-(180-3a)=2a-60" />

Now both <InlineMath math="b" /> and <InlineMath math="c" /> are fixed by <InlineMath math="a" />.

# Conditions that limit the A coupons

There are three conditions. Every type sells at least one coupon, and there are **more** A coupons than B coupons. All the numbers also have to be whole numbers.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
b\ge1&\iff 180-3a\ge1\iff a\le59 \\
c\ge1&\iff 2a-60\ge1\iff a\ge31 \\
a>b&\iff a>180-3a\iff 4a>180\iff a>45
\end{aligned}" />

The last inequality is **strict**. For <InlineMath math="a=45" /> you get <InlineMath math="b=45" />, so the A coupons aren't more, just equal. The smallest whole number that meets every condition is <InlineMath math="a=46" />.

# The fewest C coupons

The formula <InlineMath math="c=2a-60" /> grows as <InlineMath math="a" /> grows, so <InlineMath math="c" /> is smallest when <InlineMath math="a" /> is smallest, at <InlineMath math="a=46" />:

<BlockMath math="b=180-138=42,\qquad c=2\cdot46-60=32" />

Check it: <InlineMath math="46+42+32=120" /> and <InlineMath math="50\cdot46+30\cdot42+20\cdot32=2{,}300+1{,}260+640=4{,}200" /> thousand rupiah. The condition <InlineMath math="46>42" /> holds too, so **this sale really can happen**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="30" /> comes from <InlineMath math="a=45" />. Then <InlineMath math="a=b=45" />, but the A coupons must be **more**, not equal.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> comes from forgetting the condition <InlineMath math="a>b" />, which lets <InlineMath math="a" /> drop to <InlineMath math="31" />.
- <InlineMath math="46" /> is the number of A coupons, not C coupons.
- <InlineMath math="58" /> is the **largest** possible number of C coupons, at <InlineMath math="a=59" />, but the question asks for the smallest.

So the fewest C coupons sold is <InlineMath math="32" />.

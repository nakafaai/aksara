We need the sum of every real root of the fractional equation. **A denominator cannot be zero**, so <InlineMath math="x\ne1" /> and <InlineMath math="x\ne-1" />.

Multiply both sides by <InlineMath math="2(x-1)(x+1)" /> to clear the denominators.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2\cdot 2(x+1)+3\cdot 2(x-1) &= 5(x-1)(x+1) \\
4(x+1)+6(x-1) &= 5(x^2-1) \\
4x+4+6x-6 &= 5x^2-5 \\
10x-2 &= 5x^2-5 \\
5x^2-10x-3 &= 0
\end{aligned}" />

Calculate the discriminant.

<BlockMath math="\Delta=(-10)^2-4(5)(-3)=160>0" />

Because the discriminant is positive, the quadratic equation has two real roots. Since neither <InlineMath math="1" /> nor <InlineMath math="-1" /> is a root of that quadratic, both roots satisfy the denominator condition. Vieta's formula gives **the sum of the valid roots**.

<BlockMath math="x_1+x_2=-\frac{-10}{5}=2" />

Therefore, the sum of all real values of <InlineMath math="x" /> satisfying the equation is <InlineMath math="2" />.

There are <InlineMath math="99-10+1=90" /> equally likely numbers. For an **or** between two events, count both sets and subtract the numbers that lie in both, otherwise they are counted twice.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{divisible by 4}&:\ 12,16,\dots,96\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{96-12}{4}+1=22 \\
\text{divisible by 6}&:\ 12,18,\dots,96\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{96-12}{6}+1=15 \\
\text{divisible by 12}&:\ 12,24,\dots,96\ \Rightarrow\ 8 \\
P&=\frac{22+15-8}{90}=\frac{29}{90}
\end{aligned}" />

A number is divisible by both <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="6" /> exactly when it is divisible by their least common multiple <InlineMath math="12" />.

As a check, <InlineMath math="29" /> is less than <InlineMath math="22+15=37" />, because the eight multiples of <InlineMath math="12" /> may count only once.

So the probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{29}{90}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{45}=\frac{8}{90}" /> counts only the numbers divisible by both, an and instead of an or.
- <InlineMath math="\frac16=\frac{15}{90}" /> counts only the multiples of <InlineMath math="6" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{11}{45}=\frac{22}{90}" /> counts only the multiples of <InlineMath math="4" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{37}{90}" /> adds both sets without subtracting the multiples of <InlineMath math="12" /> once.

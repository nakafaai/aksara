# All the ways to draw

The four socks are taken at once, so order doesn't matter. **The number of ways to choose <InlineMath math="4" /> from <InlineMath math="10" />** is:

<BlockMath math="C(10,4)=\frac{10\cdot9\cdot8\cdot7}{4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1}=210" />

# Through the opposite event

At least one pair covers exactly one pair and exactly two pairs. It's easier to **count the opposite**: no pair at all, which means all four socks have different patterns.

For that, first choose <InlineMath math="4" /> patterns out of <InlineMath math="5" />. Then from each chosen pattern take one of its two socks, left or right.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{no pair}&=C(5,4)\cdot2^4=5\cdot16=80 \\
P(\text{no pair})&=\frac{80}{210}=\frac{8}{21}
\end{aligned}" />

# The probability you want

**Subtract from one**:

<BlockMath math="P(\text{at least one pair})=1-\frac{8}{21}=\frac{13}{21}" />

So the probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{13}{21}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

Each wrong option **stops too early or counts something twice**.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{8}{21}" /> is the probability of no pair, before subtracting it from one.
- <InlineMath math="\frac47" /> counts only exactly one pair, <InlineMath math="120" /> ways, and forgets the <InlineMath math="10" /> ways that give two pairs.
- <InlineMath math="\frac23" /> picks one pair and then any two of the other <InlineMath math="8" /> socks, <InlineMath math="5\cdot28=140" />. Draws with two pairs get counted twice.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{41}{42}" /> forgets that each chosen pattern has two socks to pick from, so it counts only <InlineMath math="5" /> ways with no pair.

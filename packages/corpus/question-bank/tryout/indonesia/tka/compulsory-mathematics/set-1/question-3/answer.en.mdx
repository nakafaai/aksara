#### Square Identities

Square the given equation using the **square-of-a-sum identity**. The cross term equals <InlineMath math="2" /> because <InlineMath math="x\cdot\frac1x=1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\left(x+\frac1x\right)^2&=3^2 \\
x^2+2+\frac1{x^2}&=9 \\
x^2+\frac1{x^2}&=9-2=7 \\
\left(x-\frac1x\right)^2&=x^2+\frac1{x^2}-2 \\
&=7-2=5
\end{aligned}" />

#### Quadratic Equation and Root Type

Since <InlineMath math="x\ne0" />, multiply the original equation by <InlineMath math="x" />. Move all terms to the left, then use the quadratic formula.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x\left(x+\frac1x\right)&=3x \\
x^2+1&=3x \\
x^2-3x+1&=0 \\
x&=\frac{3\pm\sqrt{(-3)^2-4(1)(1)}}{2(1)} \\
&=\frac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}
\end{aligned}" />

Both roots are **irrational** because <InlineMath math="\sqrt5" /> is irrational. Adding a rational number and dividing by two do not remove the irrational part.

#### Cube Identity

Apply <InlineMath math="(a+b)^3=a^3+b^3+3ab(a+b)" /> with <InlineMath math="a=x" /> and <InlineMath math="b=\frac1x" />. Since <InlineMath math="ab=1" />, the **mixed terms** give <InlineMath math="3(x+\frac1x)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^3+\frac1{x^3}
&=\left(x+\frac1x\right)^3-3\left(x+\frac1x\right) \\
&=3^3-3(3) \\
&=27-9=18
\end{aligned}" />

Both square identities, the quadratic equation for <InlineMath math="x" />, and the cube identity are true. The claim that <InlineMath math="x" /> is rational is false.

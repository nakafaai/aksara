Square the given equation. The cross term equals <InlineMath math="2" /> because <InlineMath math="x\cdot\frac1x=1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2+2+\frac1{x^2}&=9 \\
x^2+\frac1{x^2}&=7 \\
\left(x-\frac1x\right)^2&=7-2=5
\end{aligned}" />

Multiply the original equation by <InlineMath math="x" /> to determine what kind of number it is.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-3x+1&=0 \\
x&=\frac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}
\end{aligned}" />

Both roots are irrational because <InlineMath math="\sqrt5" /> is irrational. For the cubes, expand the cube of the sum.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^3+\frac1{x^3}&=\left(x+\frac1x\right)^3 \\
&\quad-3\left(x+\frac1x\right) \\
&=27-9=18
\end{aligned}" />

Both square identities, the quadratic equation for <InlineMath math="x" />, and the cube identity are true. The claim that <InlineMath math="x" /> is rational is false.

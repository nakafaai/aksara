In a geometric sequence, the **ratio of terms** three positions apart is the cube of the common ratio <InlineMath math="r" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
r^3&=\frac{u_5}{u_2}=\frac{162}{6}=27 \\
r&=\sqrt[3]{27}=3 \\
u_1&=\frac{u_2}{r}=\frac63=2
\end{aligned}" />

Use <InlineMath math="u_n=2\cdot3^{n-1}" /> and add the first four terms to find <InlineMath math="S_4" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
u_3&=2\cdot3^2=18 \\
u_4&=2\cdot3^3=2(27)=54 \\
S_4&=2+6+18+54=80 \\
u_6&=2\cdot3^5=2(243)=486 \\
\frac{u_6}{u_3}&=\frac{486}{18}=27
\end{aligned}" />

The statements about <InlineMath math="u_4" />, <InlineMath math="S_4" />, and <InlineMath math="\frac{u_6}{u_3}" /> are true. The claim <InlineMath math="u_6=243" /> is false.

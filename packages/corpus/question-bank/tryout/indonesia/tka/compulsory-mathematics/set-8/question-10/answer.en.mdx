# Side lengths from the angle bisector

The question asks for the length of the angle bisector <InlineMath math="AD" />. The first step is to find the sides <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="AC" />, because neither is given.

An angle bisector splits the opposite side **in the ratio of the two sides next to the angle**. So <InlineMath math="AB:AC=BD:DC=6:10=3:5" />. The perimeter is <InlineMath math="48" /> and <InlineMath math="BC=6+10=16" />, so <InlineMath math="AB+AC=48-16=32" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AB&=\tfrac{3}{8}\cdot32=12 \\
AC&=\tfrac{5}{8}\cdot32=20
\end{aligned}" />

<BlockMath math="AD=\sqrt{12^2+6^2}=\sqrt{180}=6\sqrt5\text{ cm}" />

So <InlineMath math="AD" /> is <InlineMath math="6\sqrt5\text{ cm}" /> long.

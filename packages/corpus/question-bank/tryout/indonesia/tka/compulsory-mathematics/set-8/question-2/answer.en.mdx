# Two possible totals after the promo

This question runs backwards: from a bill of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}380{,}000" />, find every possible number of bottles. A member's bill depends on the total after the promo, call it <InlineMath math="T" />. **A bill below <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}400{,}000" /> doesn't have to mean there was no discount**, so check both cases.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}

Both cases agree with the rules. So there are two possible totals after the promo: <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}380{,}000" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}400{,}000" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="25" /> bottles only comes from assuming a bill below <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}400{,}000" /> means no discount. The discount rule is checked on the total after the promo, not on the bill.
- <InlineMath math="26" /> bottles only comes from undoing the discount and forgetting that a total of <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}380{,}000" /> with no discount is also possible.
- <InlineMath math="19" /> or <InlineMath math="20" /> bottles comes from **forgetting the buy 3 get 1 promo** and paying for every bottle.
- <InlineMath math="26" /> or <InlineMath math="27" /> bottles comes from applying the promo proportionally, paying <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{4}" /> of the bottles rounded down. The promo only works on complete groups of <InlineMath math="4" /> bottles.

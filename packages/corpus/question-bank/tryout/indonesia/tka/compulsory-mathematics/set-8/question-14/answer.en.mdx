# Net turn per two steps

All turns use the same centre, so you can add the angles. Count counterclockwise turns as positive and clockwise turns as negative. One odd turn followed right away by one even turn gives

<BlockMath math="(+72^\circ)+(-144^\circ)=-72^\circ" />

So **every pair of turns works like one clockwise turn of** <InlineMath math="72^\circ" />. Turn <InlineMath math="2026" /> is an even turn, so the <InlineMath math="2026" /> turns split exactly into pairs:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{number of pairs}&=\frac{2026}{2}=1013 \\
\text{total turn}&=1013\cdot(-72^\circ)
\end{aligned}" />

# Removing full turns

Five pairs give <InlineMath math="5\cdot(-72^\circ)=-360^\circ" />, a full turn, and the board looks exactly as it did before. So only the remainder of <InlineMath math="1013" /> divided by <InlineMath math="5" /> matters:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1013&=5\cdot202+3 \\
1013\cdot(-72^\circ)&=202\cdot(-360^\circ)+3\cdot(-72^\circ) \\
3\cdot(-72^\circ)&=-216^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

A clockwise turn of <InlineMath math="216^\circ" /> leaves the board in the same state as a counterclockwise turn of <InlineMath math="360^\circ-216^\circ=144^\circ" />. So **the end result is one counterclockwise turn of** <InlineMath math="144^\circ" />, which moves every vertex two places that way.

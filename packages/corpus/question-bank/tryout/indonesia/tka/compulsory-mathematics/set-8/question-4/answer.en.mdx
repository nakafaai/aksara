# Setting up the equations

Call the hundreds, tens, and units digits <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" />. The original number is <InlineMath math="100a+10b+c" />, and the reversed number is <InlineMath math="100c+10b+a" />. The word **difference** doesn't say which number is bigger, so the sign is still unknown.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(100a+10b+c)-(100c+10b+a)&=99(a-c) \\
|99(a-c)|=396 \Rightarrow |a-c|&=4
\end{aligned}" />

Now combine the other two conditions. The tens digit is <InlineMath math="b=2a+1" />, so put that into the digit sum.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+(2a+1)+c&=13 \\
3a+c&=12
\end{aligned}" />

# Checking both cases

Solve once for each sign of the difference.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a-c=4&:\quad c=a-4,\ 4a-4=12 \Rightarrow a=4,\ c=0,\ b=9 \\
c-a=4&:\quad c=a+4,\ 4a+4=12 \Rightarrow a=2,\ c=6,\ b=5
\end{aligned}" />

The first case gives <InlineMath math="490" />. Reversed, it becomes <InlineMath math="094" />, which is just <InlineMath math="94" />, and that's **not a three-digit number**. The question rules this case out. The second case gives <InlineMath math="256" />, whose reversal is <InlineMath math="652" />.

Check <InlineMath math="256" />: <InlineMath math="2+5+6=13" />, <InlineMath math="5=2(2)+1" />, and <InlineMath math="652-256=396" />. Every condition holds.

So the product of the hundreds digit and the tens digit is <InlineMath math="2\times5=10" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="36" /> comes from <InlineMath math="490" />. That happens if you **assume the original number is the bigger one** and don't check that its reversal must have three digits.
- <InlineMath math="21" /> comes from <InlineMath math="733" />, which you get by flipping the "twice" sentence into <InlineMath math="a=2b+1" />.
- <InlineMath math="30" /> comes from <InlineMath math="652" />, which you get by tying the tens digit to the units digit, <InlineMath math="b=2c+1" />. That number is the reversal, not the original.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> comes from <InlineMath math="337" />, which you get by putting the condition on the units digit, <InlineMath math="c=2a+1" />, even though it's about the tens digit.

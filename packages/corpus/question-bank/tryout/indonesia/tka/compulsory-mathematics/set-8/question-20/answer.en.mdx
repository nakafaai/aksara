The question asks for the floor area where **the roof is at least** <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> **overhead**, not the whole floor. So you need two things: the size of the triangular cross-section, and the floor points where the roof is exactly <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> high.

# Height and base of the cross-section

Draw the height from <InlineMath math="A" /> down to the base and call its foot <InlineMath math="M" />. Because the triangle is isosceles, <InlineMath math="M" /> is the midpoint of <InlineMath math="BC" />. In the right triangle <InlineMath math="ABM" />, the hypotenuse is <InlineMath math="AB=6" /> and the angle at <InlineMath math="B" /> is <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AM&=AB\sin30^\circ=6\cdot\tfrac12=3\text{ m} \\
BM&=AB\cos30^\circ=6\cdot\tfrac12\sqrt3=3\sqrt3\text{ m} \\
BC&=2\cdot BM=6\sqrt3\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Sine gives the height because the height is opposite the <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> angle, while **cosine gives half the base** because half the base is next to that angle.

# Where you can stand

Take a floor point <InlineMath math="P" /> that's <InlineMath math="x" /> metres from <InlineMath math="B" />, with <InlineMath math="x\le3\sqrt3" />. The vertical line from <InlineMath math="P" /> up to the roof, the segment <InlineMath math="BP" />, and the roof <InlineMath math="AB" /> form a right triangle with a <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> angle at <InlineMath math="B" />. The roof height above <InlineMath math="P" /> is opposite that angle and <InlineMath math="BP" /> is next to it, so use tangent:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{roof height above }P&=x\tan30^\circ=\frac{x}{\sqrt3} \\
\frac{x}{\sqrt3}\ge1.5\quad&\Leftrightarrow\quad x\ge1.5\sqrt3
\end{aligned}" />

The same holds from the <InlineMath math="C" /> side. So **a strip** <InlineMath math="1.5\sqrt3" /> **metres wide near each end is too low to stand in**, and the usable width is

<BlockMath math="6\sqrt3-2\cdot1.5\sqrt3=3\sqrt3\text{ m}" />

Check with similar triangles: the part of the triangle above height <InlineMath math="1.5" /> is similar to triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />, and its height is <InlineMath math="3-1.5=1.5" />, half of <InlineMath math="3" />. So its base is half too, <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot6\sqrt3=3\sqrt3" />. It matches.

# Area of the floor part

The usable floor is a rectangle <InlineMath math="3\sqrt3\text{ m}" /> wide running the attic's full <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" /> length. **Its area is the width times the attic's length**:

<BlockMath math="3\sqrt3\cdot10=30\sqrt3\approx51.96\text{ m}^2" />

That is exactly half the whole floor, which fits because the usable width is half the base.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60\sqrt3\text{ m}^2" /> is the whole floor, <InlineMath math="6\sqrt3\cdot10" />. **The** <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> **height condition is ignored**.
- <InlineMath math="50\sqrt3\text{ m}^2" /> comes from flipping the tangent: <InlineMath math="x=1.5\tan30^\circ=\tfrac12\sqrt3" />, when it should be <InlineMath math="x=1.5\div\tan30^\circ=1.5\sqrt3" />.
- <InlineMath math="\left(60\sqrt3-60\right)\text{ m}^2" /> comes from subtracting the length along the roof up to height <InlineMath math="1.5" />, which is <InlineMath math="1.5\div\sin30^\circ=3\text{ m}" />. That distance is measured along the slope, not across the floor.
- <InlineMath math="\left(60-10\sqrt3\right)\text{ m}^2" /> comes from swapping sine and cosine, which gives height <InlineMath math="3\sqrt3" /> and base <InlineMath math="6" />. The roof then acts as if it sloped at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />.

So the floor area where you can stand is <InlineMath math="30\sqrt3\text{ m}^2" />.

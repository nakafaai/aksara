# Three conditions for a logarithm

The question asks when <InlineMath math="{}^{5-x}\log\left(x^2-x-6\right)" /> makes sense. A logarithm <InlineMath math="{}^{a}\log b" /> is defined only when the argument is positive, <InlineMath math="b>0" />, and the base is positive but **not <InlineMath math="1" />**, so <InlineMath math="a>0" /> and <InlineMath math="a\neq1" />. Here the base also contains <InlineMath math="x" />, so all three conditions have to hold together.

# The argument condition

Factor the quadratic, then find where it's positive.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-x-6&>0 \\
(x-3)(x+2)&>0
\end{aligned}" />

A product of two factors is positive when both factors have the same sign, which happens **outside the two roots**. So <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> or <InlineMath math="x>3" />. Try <InlineMath math="x=0" />: <InlineMath math="0-0-6=-6" />, which is negative, so the part between <InlineMath math="-2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> really is left out.

# The base condition

The base is <InlineMath math="5-x" />, and **the base must be positive and must not equal one**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
5-x>0 &\Rightarrow x<5 \\
5-x\neq1 &\Rightarrow x\neq4
\end{aligned}" />

The first condition throws out every <InlineMath math="x" /> that's <InlineMath math="5" /> or more, because there the base is zero or negative. The second one throws out a single point, <InlineMath math="x=4" />.

# Putting it all together

Intersecting <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> or <InlineMath math="x>3" /> with <InlineMath math="x<5" /> gives <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> or <InlineMath math="3<x<5" />. The point <InlineMath math="x=4" /> **sits inside <InlineMath math="3<x<5" />**, so it has to go. Check: at <InlineMath math="x=4" /> the base is <InlineMath math="1" />, and a logarithm with base <InlineMath math="1" /> isn't defined. At <InlineMath math="x=-3" /> the argument is <InlineMath math="6" /> and the base is <InlineMath math="8" />, both fine.

So <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> is defined for <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> or <InlineMath math="3<x<5" />, with <InlineMath math="x\neq4" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-2<x<3" /> comes from flipping the sign of the quadratic inequality. The positive part lies outside the roots, not between them.
- <InlineMath math="3<x<5" /> with <InlineMath math="x\neq4" /> throws away <InlineMath math="x<-2" />. There, both the argument and the base are positive, and the base is bigger than <InlineMath math="1" />.
- <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> or <InlineMath math="3<x<5" /> forgets that **the base can't equal <InlineMath math="1" />**.
- <InlineMath math="x<-2" /> or <InlineMath math="x>3" />, with <InlineMath math="x\neq4" />, forgets that the base must be positive. For <InlineMath math="x\ge5" />, the base <InlineMath math="5-x" /> is zero or negative.

# The ladder length from two positions

The question asks how high a point on the ladder is after it slips. For that you need **the ladder's length and its new position**, but the length isn't given.

Let the top of the ladder start at height <InlineMath math="y" /> metres. At first the foot is <InlineMath math="7" /> from the wall. After slipping, the foot is <InlineMath math="7+8=15" /> from the wall and the top is at <InlineMath math="y-4" />. The ladder has the same length in both positions, so use Pythagoras twice.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
7^2+y^2&=15^2+(y-4)^2 \\
49+y^2&=225+y^2-8y+16 \\
8y&=192 \\
y&=24
\end{aligned}" />

The ladder is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{7^2+24^2}=25" /> metres long. After slipping, its top is at height <InlineMath math="24-4=20" /> metres, and indeed <InlineMath math="15^2+20^2=625=25^2" />.

# Height of the paint mark

The mark is <InlineMath math="5" /> metres from the top, so it's <InlineMath math="25-5=20" /> metres from the foot. Along the ladder, the height grows **in proportion to the distance from the foot**, because the ladder and the wall form similar triangles.

<BlockMath math="\text{height of the mark}=\frac{20}{25}\cdot20=16\text{ m}" />

So after the ladder slips, the paint mark is <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}" /> above the floor.

- <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" /> subtracts <InlineMath math="5" /> straight from <InlineMath math="20" />, as if the ladder stood vertically.
- <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" /> is the height of the ladder's midpoint, not the mark.
- <InlineMath math="4\text{ m}" /> measures <InlineMath math="5" /> metres from the foot instead of from the top.

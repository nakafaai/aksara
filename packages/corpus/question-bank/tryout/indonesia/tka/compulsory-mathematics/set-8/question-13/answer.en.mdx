# Finding the mirror line

The question doesn't give the mirror line's equation, so the first step is **finding the line <InlineMath math="g" /> from a point and its image**. Only then do you reflect <InlineMath math="B" />.

The mirror line is always the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="AA'" />: the line through its midpoint, perpendicular to it. The midpoint of <InlineMath math="AA'" /> is <InlineMath math="M(4,2)" />. Line <InlineMath math="g" /> goes through <InlineMath math="O" /> and <InlineMath math="M" />, so its gradient is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2}{4}=\tfrac12" />.

<BlockMath math="g:\ y=\tfrac12x" />

Check: the gradient of <InlineMath math="AA'" /> is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4-0}{3-5}=-2" />, and <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\cdot(-2)=-1" />, so they really are perpendicular.

# Reflecting B

The image <InlineMath math="B'" /> has to meet two conditions: the midpoint of <InlineMath math="BB'" /> lies on <InlineMath math="g" />, and <InlineMath math="BB'" /> is **perpendicular to the line <InlineMath math="g" />**. A direction perpendicular to <InlineMath math="g" /> is <InlineMath math="(1,-2)" />, so write <InlineMath math="B'=(0,5)+t(1,-2)=(t,\ 5-2t)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{midpoint}&=\left(\tfrac{t}{2},\ \tfrac{10-2t}{2}\right)=\left(\tfrac{t}{2},\ 5-t\right) \\
5-t&=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{t}{2} \\
20-4t&=t \\
t&=4
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="B'=(4,\ 5-8)=(4,-3)" />. As a check, <InlineMath math="|OB'|=\sqrt{16+9}=5=|OB|" />, which fits, because reflecting in a line through <InlineMath math="O" /> keeps the distance to <InlineMath math="O" />.

So the image of <InlineMath math="B" /> is <InlineMath math="(4,-3)" />.

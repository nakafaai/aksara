The question asks for the price of a plot whose drawing is on the **photocopy**, not on the original map. So you need to sort out two things: the scale of the photocopy, and how that scale blows up an area.

# Scale of the photocopy

On the original map, <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> stands for <InlineMath math="2{,}000\text{ cm}" /> on the ground. After the reduction, the same segment is only <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ cm}" /> on the copy, but it still stands for <InlineMath math="2{,}000\text{ cm}" /> on the ground:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.8\text{ cm}&\;\leftrightarrow\;2{,}000\text{ cm} \\
1\text{ cm}&\;\leftrightarrow\;\frac{2{,}000}{0.8}\text{ cm}=2{,}500\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

So the photocopy's scale is <InlineMath math="1:2{,}500" />. Shrinking the drawing makes **the scale's second number bigger**, because one centimetre of drawing now stands for a longer distance.

# The real area

Every length is multiplied by <InlineMath math="2{,}500" />. Area is length times length, so **the area is multiplied by the square of the scale**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{real area}&=40\cdot2{,}500^2\text{ cm}^2=40\cdot6{,}250{,}000\text{ cm}^2 \\
&=250{,}000{,}000\text{ cm}^2 \\
&=\frac{250{,}000{,}000}{10{,}000}\text{ m}^2=25{,}000\text{ m}^2 \\
&=\frac{25{,}000}{10{,}000}\text{ hectares}=2.5\text{ hectares}
\end{aligned}" />

Check through the original map: the drawing there has area <InlineMath math="40\div0.8^2=40\div0.64=62.5\text{ cm}^2" />, and <InlineMath math="62.5\cdot2{,}000^2=62.5\cdot4{,}000{,}000=250{,}000{,}000\text{ cm}^2" />. Same result.

# Price of the plot

The price is given per hectare, so **multiply the area in hectares by the price per hectare**. Make sure the unit is already hectares before you multiply, not square centimetres on the drawing or square metres.

<BlockMath math="2.5\cdot\text{Rp}250\text{ million}=\text{Rp}625\text{ million}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}256\text{ million}" /> comes from taking the copy's scale as <InlineMath math="1:(2{,}000\cdot0.8)=1:1{,}600" />. **Shrinking makes the scale's second number bigger**, not smaller.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}320\text{ million}" /> comes from taking the original drawing's area as <InlineMath math="40\cdot0.8=32\text{ cm}^2" />. The direction is reversed, and the length factor <InlineMath math="0.8" /> isn't squared.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}400\text{ million}" /> comes from using the original scale <InlineMath math="1:2{,}000" /> on the copy, which gives only <InlineMath math="1.6" /> hectares.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}500\text{ million}" /> comes from taking the original drawing's area as <InlineMath math="40\div0.8=50\text{ cm}^2" />. Shrinking lengths by <InlineMath math="0.8" /> shrinks areas by <InlineMath math="0.8^2=0.64" />, so you divide by <InlineMath math="0.64" />.

So the whole plot costs <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}625" /> million.

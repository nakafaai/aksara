The question asks for the largest planting area. Three conditions work together: the perimeter is fixed at <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" />, the sides are whole numbers, and the garden's area is **less than** <InlineMath math="150\text{ m}^2" />, so exactly <InlineMath math="150" /> isn't allowed.

# Length and width

Call the garden's length <InlineMath math="p" /> and its width <InlineMath math="l" />, with <InlineMath math="p\ge l" />. **The whole fence goes around the perimeter**, so

<BlockMath math="2(p+l)=50\quad\Rightarrow\quad p+l=25" />

So every choice of length fixes the width, and all you need is a pair of whole numbers that add up to <InlineMath math="25" />.

# The planting area

The <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> path runs along all four sides, so the planting part loses <InlineMath math="1" /> metre at the left end and <InlineMath math="1" /> metre at the right end, and its width loses two metres the same way. The planting part is a <InlineMath math="(p-2)\times(l-2)" /> rectangle:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_\text{plant}&=(p-2)(l-2)=pl-2(p+l)+4 \\
&=pl-2\cdot25+4=pl-46
\end{aligned}" />

So for this perimeter, **the planting area is always the garden's area minus** <InlineMath math="46" />. The largest planting area comes from the largest garden area that's still allowed.

The pairs after that have even smaller areas, so the largest allowed garden area is <InlineMath math="144\text{ m}^2" />, from a <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}\times9\text{ m}" /> garden.

<BlockMath math="A_\text{plant}=(16-2)(9-2)=14\cdot7=98\text{ m}^2" />

Check with the formula from before: <InlineMath math="144-46=98" />. It matches.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="104\text{ m}^2" /> comes from the <InlineMath math="15\times10" /> garden. Its area is exactly <InlineMath math="150" />, but **the rule says less than** <InlineMath math="150" />.
- <InlineMath math="110\text{ m}^2" /> comes from the <InlineMath math="13\times12" /> garden, whose area is <InlineMath math="156" />. That ignores the village rule.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ m}^2" /> comes from taking the path off each measurement only once, giving <InlineMath math="15\times8" />. The path is at both ends, so each measurement loses <InlineMath math="2" /> metres.
- <InlineMath math="144\text{ m}^2" /> is the area of the whole garden, path included.

So the largest area that can be planted with vegetables is <InlineMath math="98\text{ m}^2" />.

# Given

We are given a **radical rational function** <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> with both a square root and a denominator.

# Asked

The **natural domain** where both the square root and denominator stay defined.

# Solution

> Two safety rules apply together: keep the inside of the square root nonnegative and keep the denominator nonzero.

The formula has two parts that limit the domain, so **both conditions must hold at once**: the number under the root is not negative, and the denominator is not zero.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
4-x^2\ge0&\iff x^2\le4\iff -2\le x\le2 \\
x-1\ne0&\iff x\ne1
\end{aligned}" />

Draw both conditions at once. **The square root is defined only under the half circle from <InlineMath math="-2" /> to <InlineMath math="2" />**, and the line <InlineMath math="x=1" /> must be removed because the denominator is zero there.

The endpoints <InlineMath math="-2" /> and <InlineMath math="2" /> are included, because there <InlineMath math="\sqrt0=0" /> is defined and the denominator is not zero. For example <InlineMath math="f(2)=0" />.

<BlockMath math="f(2)=\frac{0}{1}=0" />

So the natural domain is <InlineMath math="\{x\mid -2\le x\le2,\ x\ne1\}" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid -2\le x\le2\}" /> forgets that <InlineMath math="x=1" /> makes the denominator zero.
- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\ne1\}" /> only checks the denominator and forgets the root.
- The expression below drops the endpoints <InlineMath math="\pm2" />, but <InlineMath math="\sqrt0" /> is defined.

  <BlockMath math="\{x\mid -2<x<2,\ x\ne1\}" />

- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\le-2\text{ or }x\ge2\}" /> reverses the inequality <InlineMath math="x^2\le4" />.

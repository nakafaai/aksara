# Solution

> Spot one integer root from the constant term divisors and factor the remaining quadratic so every root is visible.

Factor <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> first, because **the sign of <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> on each interval** is easiest to read from its factors. Possible integer roots divide <InlineMath math="6" />. Try <InlineMath math="x=-1" />: <InlineMath math="0" />.

\end{gathered}" />

1. The statement that <InlineMath math="x-1" /> is a factor is false, because <InlineMath math="P(1) \ne 0" />.

   <BlockMath math="P(1)=1+2-5-6=-8\ne0" />

2. The statement about the factorisation is true. Check: the equation below, and the equation below.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} (x+1)(x-2)=x^2-x-2 \\ (x^2-x-2)(x+3)=x^3+2x^2-5x-6 \end{gathered}" />

3. The statement that the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x+2" /> is <InlineMath math="-4" /> is false. The remainder is <InlineMath math="4" />.

   <BlockMath math="P(-2)=-8+8+10-6=4" />

4. The statement about the sum of the roots is true: <InlineMath math="-2" />, matching Vieta's the equation below.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} -1+2-3=-2 \\ -\frac{b}{a}=-2 \end{gathered}" />

5. The statement about the sign of <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is true. The roots split the number line into intervals. The leading coefficient is positive, so <InlineMath math="P(x)>0" /> for <InlineMath math="x>2" />, and the sign flips at each simple root: negative on <InlineMath math="2" />, positive on <InlineMath math="-1" />, negative on <InlineMath math="x<-3" />. For example <InlineMath math="P(0)<0" />.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} -1<x<2 \\ -3<x<-1 \\ P(0)=-6<0 \end{gathered}" />

# Answer

So the true statements are the factorisation the expression below, the root sum <InlineMath math="-2" />, and <InlineMath math="P(x)<0" /> on <InlineMath math="2" />.

-1<x<2
\end{gathered}" />

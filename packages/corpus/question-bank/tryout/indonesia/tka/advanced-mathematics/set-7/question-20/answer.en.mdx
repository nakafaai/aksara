Points on the mirror do not move under the reflection. So the line <InlineMath math="y=mx" /> is **the set of points that <InlineMath math="M" /> maps to themselves**. Find a nonzero vector <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> with <InlineMath math="M\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.6x+0.8y&=x\ \Rightarrow\ 0.8y=0.4x\ \Rightarrow\ y=\frac12x \\
0.8x-0.6y&=y\ \Rightarrow\ 0.8x=1.6y\ \Rightarrow\ y=\frac12x
\end{aligned}" />

Both equations give the same line, <InlineMath math="y=\frac12x" />. Check: <InlineMath math="M\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 1.2+0.8 \\ 1.6-0.6 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}" />. The direction perpendicular to the mirror is reversed instead: <InlineMath math="M\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}" />.

So <InlineMath math="m=\frac12" />.

- <InlineMath math="2" /> flips the slope, as if <InlineMath math="x=\frac12y" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac43" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{0.8}{0.6}" />, which is <InlineMath math="\tan2\theta" /> for the mirror's angle <InlineMath math="\theta" />. The mirror's slope is <InlineMath math="\tan\theta" />, half that angle.
- <InlineMath math="\frac34" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{0.6}{0.8}" />, the ratio of the entries turned around.
- <InlineMath math="-2" /> is the slope of the direction <InlineMath math="(1,-2)" />, which is perpendicular to the mirror and reversed by <InlineMath math="M" />.

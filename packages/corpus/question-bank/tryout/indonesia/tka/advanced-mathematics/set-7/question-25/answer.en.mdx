Each statement compares **how fast the numerator and denominator grow or shrink**. Judge each one with the right tool.

1. The first statement is true. Numerator and denominator both have degree two, so the limit is the ratio of the <InlineMath math="x^2" />-coefficients: <InlineMath math="\frac12" />.
2. The second statement is false. Multiply by the conjugate: <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x+1}-\sqrt x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt x}" />, which tends to <InlineMath math="0" /> because the denominator grows without bound.
3. The third statement is true. <InlineMath math="\frac{3x+\sin x}{x}=3+\frac{\sin x}{x}" />. Since <InlineMath math="-1\le\sin x\le1" />, the term <InlineMath math="\frac{\sin x}{x}" /> is squeezed between <InlineMath math="-\frac1x" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac1x" />, so it tends to <InlineMath math="0" />.
4. The fourth statement is false. <InlineMath math="\frac{\sin x}{x^2}=\frac{\sin x}{x}\cdot\frac1x" />. The first factor tends to <InlineMath math="1" />, but <InlineMath math="\frac1x\to\infty" /> as <InlineMath math="x\to0^+" />, so the product grows without bound.
5. The fifth statement is true. Let <InlineMath math="t=\frac1x\to0^+" />. With <InlineMath math="1-\cos t=2\sin^2\frac t2" />, the expression becomes the following.

<BlockMath math="x\left(1-\cos\frac1x\right)=\frac{1-\cos t}{t}=\frac{2\sin^2\frac t2}{t}=\sin\frac t2\cdot\frac{\sin\frac t2}{\frac t2}\to0\cdot1=0" />

So the true statements are the ones about <InlineMath math="\frac{x^2+1}{2x^2-x}" />, about <InlineMath math="\frac{3x+\sin x}{x}" /> and about <InlineMath math="x\left(1-\cos\frac1x\right)" />.

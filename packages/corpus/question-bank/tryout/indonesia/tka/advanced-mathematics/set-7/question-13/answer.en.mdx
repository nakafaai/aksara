Start from two basic facts: <InlineMath math="|\vec{u}|=|\vec{v}|=3" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=2+2-4=0" />. So the vectors are **equally long and perpendicular**, like two sides of a square. The vectors <InlineMath math="\vec{u}+\vec{v}" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{u}-\vec{v}" /> are the two diagonals of that square.

<BlockMath math="\vec{u}+\vec{v}=3\vec{i}+3\vec{j}+0\vec{k},\qquad \vec{u}-\vec{v}=\vec{i}-\vec{j}+4\vec{k}" />

1. The statement that the diagonals are perpendicular is true: <InlineMath math="(\vec{u}+\vec{v})\cdot(\vec{u}-\vec{v})=|\vec{u}|^2-|\vec{v}|^2=0" />. In components: <InlineMath math="3-3+0=0" />.
2. The statement that the diagonals have equal length is true. Because <InlineMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=0" />, <InlineMath math="|\vec{u}\pm\vec{v}|^2=|\vec{u}|^2+|\vec{v}|^2=18" /> for both signs.
3. The statement <InlineMath math="|\vec{u}+\vec{v}|=3\sqrt2" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2" />.
4. The statement <InlineMath math="\vec{u}-\vec{v}=\vec{i}-\vec{j}" /> is false, because its <InlineMath math="\vec{k}" />-component is <InlineMath math="2-(-2)=4" />, not <InlineMath math="0" />.
5. The statement about a <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> angle is false. A square's diagonal makes <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> with its sides: <InlineMath math="\cos\theta=\frac{\vec{u}\cdot(\vec{u}+\vec{v})}{|\vec{u}||\vec{u}+\vec{v}|}=\frac{9}{3\cdot3\sqrt2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}" />.

So the true statements are that <InlineMath math="\vec{u}+\vec{v}" /> is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="\vec{u}-\vec{v}" />, that the two have equal length, and that <InlineMath math="|\vec{u}+\vec{v}|=3\sqrt2" />.

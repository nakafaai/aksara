A translation moves every point by its vector. Two translations in a row **equal one translation by the sum of the vectors**, because each shift adds to the coordinates.

<BlockMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(x+2,\ y-3)\mapsto(x+2-5,\ y-3+1)=(x-3,\ y-2)" />

<BlockMath math="T=T_1+T_2=\begin{pmatrix} 2-5 \\ -3+1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}" />

The order of the two translations does not matter, because vector addition is commutative.

So the single translation is <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}" /> is <InlineMath math="T_1-T_2" />, a difference instead of a sum.
- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -7 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}" /> is <InlineMath math="T_2-T_1" />.
- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}" /> is the correct sum with the sign reversed, the translation that moves the figure back.
- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -10 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}" /> multiplies matching components, but successive translations add.

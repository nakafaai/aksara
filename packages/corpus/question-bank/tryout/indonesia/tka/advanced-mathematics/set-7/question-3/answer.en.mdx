Both matrices are **upper triangular**: their bottom-left entry is zero. The determinant of such a matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, so <InlineMath math="\det A=1" /> and <InlineMath math="\det B=4" />.

1. The statement <InlineMath math="\det(A+B)=8" /> is false. <InlineMath math="A+B=\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}" />, so its determinant is <InlineMath math="3\cdot3-4\cdot0=9" />. The determinant of a sum is not computed from the separate determinants.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="AB=BA" /> is true. Compute both.

<BlockMath math="AB=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3+2 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 5 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix},\qquad BA=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2+3 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 5 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}" />

Matrix multiplication is usually not commutative, but this particular pair happens to commute.

3. The statement about <InlineMath math="A^3" /> is true. Each multiplication by <InlineMath math="A" /> adds <InlineMath math="1" /> to the top right entry: <InlineMath math="A^2=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" /> and <InlineMath math="A^3=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />.

The statement about <InlineMath math="B^{-1}" /> is false. <InlineMath math="B^{-1}=\frac14\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} \frac12 & -\frac34 \\ 0 & \frac12 \end{pmatrix}" />. The statement divides the top right entry by <InlineMath math="2" /> instead of by the determinant <InlineMath math="4" />.

So the statements about <InlineMath math="AB=BA" /> and <InlineMath math="A^3" /> are true, and the statements about <InlineMath math="\det(A+B)" /> and <InlineMath math="B^{-1}" /> are false.

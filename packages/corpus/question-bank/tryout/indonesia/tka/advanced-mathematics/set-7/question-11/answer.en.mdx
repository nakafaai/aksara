# Given

Plane vector <InlineMath math="\vec{c}" /> with positive first component is **perpendicular** to <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" /> with known length.

# Asked

The **components of vector** <InlineMath math="\vec{c}" /> satisfying both conditions.

# Solution

> Perpendicularity sets the dot product to zero, and the given length scales the vector to its exact size.

There are **two conditions for the two unknowns**: perpendicular means the dot product is zero, and the length is <InlineMath math="10" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
3x+4y&=0\ \Rightarrow\ y=-\frac34x \\
x^2+y^2&=100\ \Rightarrow\ x^2+\frac{9}{16}x^2=100\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{25}{16}x^2=100
\end{aligned}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="\vec a" />, the line perpendicular to <InlineMath math="\vec a" />, and the circle of radius <InlineMath math="10" />. **The vector you need ends where that line meets the circle**, and the condition <InlineMath math="x>0" /> picks the point on the right.

So <InlineMath math="x^2=64" />. The condition <InlineMath math="x>0" /> picks <InlineMath math="x=8" />, so <InlineMath math="y=-6" />.

Check: <InlineMath math="3(8)+4(-6)=0" /> and <InlineMath math="10" />. Without the condition <InlineMath math="x>0" />, the vector <InlineMath math="(-8,6)" /> would also work, since it points exactly the opposite way.

<BlockMath math="\sqrt{64+36}=10" />

So the matrix below.

<BlockMath math="\vec{c}=\begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ -6 \end{pmatrix}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="(6,8)" /> points along <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" />, not perpendicular to it, because <InlineMath math="(6,8)=2\vec{a}" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-8,6)" /> is perpendicular with length <InlineMath math="10" />, but breaks the condition <InlineMath math="x>0" />.
- <InlineMath math="(4,-3)" /> is perpendicular, but its length is only <InlineMath math="5" />.
- <InlineMath math="(6,-8)" /> swaps the components the wrong way, so <InlineMath math="3(6)+4(-8)=-14\ne0" />.

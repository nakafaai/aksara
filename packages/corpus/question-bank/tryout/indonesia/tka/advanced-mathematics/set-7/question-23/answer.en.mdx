"A very large number of shirts" means <InlineMath math="x\to\infty" />. Split the fraction into two terms to see **which part fades away**.

<BlockMath math="R(x)=\frac{2{,}000{,}000}{x}+15{,}000" />

The fixed cost is spread over more and more shirts, so its share per shirt shrinks toward zero, while the cost per shirt stays the same.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}R(x)=0+15{,}000=15{,}000" />

For example, for <InlineMath math="x=1{,}000" /> the average cost is <InlineMath math="2{,}000+15{,}000=17{,}000" /> rupiah, and for <InlineMath math="x=100{,}000" /> it becomes <InlineMath math="15{,}020" /> rupiah.

So the average cost per shirt approaches <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}15{,}000.00" />.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}0.00" /> assumes the whole fraction vanishes because the denominator grows. The numerator grows in proportion to <InlineMath math="x" /> too.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}000{,}000.00" /> is the fixed cost, which is exactly the part spread over all shirts.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}015{,}000.00" /> is the total cost of a single shirt.
- "Infinite" mixes up the total cost, which does keep growing, with the average cost.

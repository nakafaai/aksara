# Given

Point <InlineMath math="(a,2)" /> with <InlineMath math="a>0" /> **lies on the given circle**.

# Asked

The **positive value** of <InlineMath math="a" /> satisfying the circle equation.

# Solution

> Since the point sits right on the circle, plugging in its coordinates yields a simple quadratic for the unknown.

A point lies on a circle exactly when **its coordinates satisfy the circle's equation**. Substitute <InlineMath math="x=a" /> and <InlineMath math="y=2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2+4-4a+4-20&=0 \\
a^2-4a-12&=0 \\
(a-6)(a+2)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the circle and the horizontal line <InlineMath math="y=2" />. **That line cuts the circle at two points**, and only the one on the right has a positive <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate.

The circle has two points with <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate <InlineMath math="2" />, namely <InlineMath math="(6,2)" /> and <InlineMath math="(-2,2)" />. The condition <InlineMath math="a>0" /> picks <InlineMath math="a=6" />.

Check with centre and radius: the circle is the equation below, and <InlineMath math="25" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
(x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=25 \\
(6-2)^2+(2+1)^2=16+9=25
\end{gathered}" />

So <InlineMath math="a=6" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="2" /> is the absolute value of the other root, <InlineMath math="-2" />, which is rejected because it is negative.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> is the sum of the two roots of the quadratic, not one of them.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> is the absolute value of the product of the roots.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{20}" /> ignores the term <InlineMath math="-4a" /> and solves <InlineMath math="a^2=20" />.

# Given

We are given the **base-two logarithm function** <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> for <InlineMath math="x>0" />.

# Asked

The **truth value of each statement** about logarithm laws, monotonicity, and its inverse.

# Solution

> A logarithm with base above one always increases and turns input multiplication into output addition.

Every statement tests **the laws of logarithms**. The true laws are the equation below, the equation below and <InlineMath math="{}^{a}\log p^n=n\,{}^{a}\log p" />. Here <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log" /> is the base-2 logarithm.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
{}^{a}\log(pq)={}^{a}\log p+{}^{a}\log q \\
{}^{a}\log\frac pq={}^{a}\log p-{}^{a}\log q
\end{gathered}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="f(x)={}^{2}\log x" /> with a few key points. **Every time <InlineMath math="x" /> is multiplied by <InlineMath math="2" />, the height goes up by <InlineMath math="1" />**, so the height at <InlineMath math="8" /> minus the height at <InlineMath math="2" /> equals the height at <InlineMath math="4" />.

1. The statement <InlineMath math="f(8)-f(2)=f(4)" /> is true. A difference of logarithms is the logarithm of the quotient: <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log\frac82={}^{2}\log4" />. In numbers: <InlineMath math="3-1=2" />.
2. The statement the equation below is false. A sum of logarithms is the logarithm of a product, not of a sum. One counterexample is enough: <InlineMath math="1" />, while <InlineMath math="0" />.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y) \\ f(1+1)=f(2)=1 \\ f(1)+f(1)=0+0=0 \end{gathered}" />

3. The statement <InlineMath math="f\left(\frac14\right)=2" /> is false. Since <InlineMath math="\frac14=2^{-2}" />, <InlineMath math="f\left(\frac14\right)=-2" />.
4. The statement <InlineMath math="f\left(x^2\right)=2f(x)" /> is true for <InlineMath math="x>0" />, by the power law <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log x^2=2\,{}^{2}\log x" />.

# Answer

So the statements about <InlineMath math="f(8)-f(2)" /> and <InlineMath math="f\left(x^2\right)" /> are true, and the statements about <InlineMath math="f(x+y)" /> and <InlineMath math="f\left(\frac14\right)" /> are false.

Every statement tests **the laws of logarithms**. The true laws are <InlineMath math="{}^{a}\log(pq)={}^{a}\log p+{}^{a}\log q" />, <InlineMath math="{}^{a}\log\frac pq={}^{a}\log p-{}^{a}\log q" /> and <InlineMath math="{}^{a}\log p^n=n\,{}^{a}\log p" />. Here <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log" /> is the base-2 logarithm.

1. The statement <InlineMath math="f(8)-f(2)=f(4)" /> is true. A difference of logarithms is the logarithm of the quotient: <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log\frac82={}^{2}\log4" />. In numbers: <InlineMath math="3-1=2" />.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)" /> is false. A sum of logarithms is the logarithm of a product, not of a sum. One counterexample is enough: <InlineMath math="f(1+1)=f(2)=1" />, while <InlineMath math="f(1)+f(1)=0+0=0" />.
3. The statement <InlineMath math="f\left(\frac14\right)=2" /> is false. Since <InlineMath math="\frac14=2^{-2}" />, <InlineMath math="f\left(\frac14\right)=-2" />.
4. The statement <InlineMath math="f\left(x^2\right)=2f(x)" /> is true for <InlineMath math="x>0" />, by the power law <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log x^2=2\,{}^{2}\log x" />.

So the statements about <InlineMath math="f(8)-f(2)" /> and <InlineMath math="f\left(x^2\right)" /> are true, and the statements about <InlineMath math="f(x+y)" /> and <InlineMath math="f\left(\frac14\right)" /> are false.

# Given

A **radio tower** stands at <InlineMath math="M(10,20)" /> on a map with a circular broadcast coverage area.

# Asked

The **coverage equation** or signal reception condition from the tower.

# Solution

> A location receives the broadcast whenever its distance to the tower stays within the coverage radius.

The places that receive the broadcast are the points inside or on the circle with centre <InlineMath math="M(10,20)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="25" />. To test a point, **compare its squared distance to <InlineMath math="M" /> with <InlineMath math="25^2=625" />**. Comparing squares avoids roots and gives the same conclusion.

Only village <InlineMath math="D" /> has a squared distance not above <InlineMath math="625" />. Its distance is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{549}\approx23.4" /> km.

So the village that receives the broadcast is <InlineMath math="D(25,38)" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- Villages <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> are both <InlineMath math="\sqrt{800}\approx28.3" /> km away. Coordinate differences of <InlineMath math="20" /> and <InlineMath math="20" /> look small, but the distance still exceeds <InlineMath math="25" /> km.
- Village <InlineMath math="C" /> differs by exactly <InlineMath math="25" /> in <InlineMath math="y" />, but also by <InlineMath math="10" /> in <InlineMath math="x" />, so it is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{725}\approx26.9" /> km away.
- Village <InlineMath math="E" /> is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{709}\approx26.6" /> km away.

# Answer

Therefore, the village within broadcast range is **Village <InlineMath math="D(25,38)" />**.

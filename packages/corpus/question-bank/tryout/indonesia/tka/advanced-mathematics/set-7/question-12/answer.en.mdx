# Given

Space line <InlineMath math="g" /> passes through **origin** <InlineMath math="O(0,0,0)" /> along direction vector <InlineMath math="\vec{d}" />.

# Asked

The **projection point or distance** along line <InlineMath math="g" />.

# Solution

> Projecting onto the direction vector drops a perpendicular straight onto the closest point on the line.

The distance from a point to a line is measured along **the perpendicular from the point to the line**. The vector <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{OP}" /> splits into a part parallel to <InlineMath math="g" /> and a part perpendicular to <InlineMath math="g" />. The length of the perpendicular part is the distance, and Pythagoras links all three.

The length of the parallel part is the scalar projection of <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{OP}" /> onto <InlineMath math="\vec{d}" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{OP}\cdot\vec{d}&=2+6+2=10,\qquad |\vec{d}|=\sqrt{1+4+4}=3 \\
\text{projection}&=\frac{10}{3},\qquad |\overrightarrow{OP}|^2=4+9+1=14
\end{aligned} \\
\text{distance}^2=|\overrightarrow{OP}|^2-\left(\frac{10}{3}\right)^2=14-\frac{100}{9}=\frac{26}{9}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{distance}=\frac{\sqrt{26}}{3}
\end{gathered}" />
</MathContainer>

Check: the foot of the perpendicular is <InlineMath math="Q" />, and the equation below is indeed perpendicular to <InlineMath math="\vec{d}" />, since <InlineMath math="0" />. Its length is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{26}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
Q=\frac{10}{9}(1,2,2) \\
\overrightarrow{QP}=\left(\frac89,\frac79,-\frac{11}{9}\right) \\
8+14-22=0 \\
\frac{\sqrt{64+49+121}}{9}=\frac{1}{9}\sqrt{234}=\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{26}
\end{gathered}" />

So the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="g" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{26}" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{10}{3}" /> is the length of the part parallel to the line, not the distance.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{14}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to the point <InlineMath math="O" />, not to the line.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{26}{9}" /> is the squared distance before taking the root.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{6}" /> subtracts <InlineMath math="\frac{10}{3}" /> without squaring it, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{14-\frac{10}{3}}" />.

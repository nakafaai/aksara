1. The statement that the equation below cuts <InlineMath math="L" /> in two points is false. Its distance from the centre is <InlineMath math="r" />, so the line is tangent and touches the circle at exactly one point.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} 3x+4y+14=0 \\ \frac{|3+8+14|}{\sqrt{9+16}}=\frac{25}{5}=5=r \end{gathered}" />

2. The statement that <InlineMath math="(4,6)" /> lies on <InlineMath math="L" /> is true: <InlineMath math="25" />.

   <BlockMath math="(4-1)^2+(6-2)^2=9+16=25" />

3. The statement about the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis is false. Substituting <InlineMath math="y=0" /> gives the equation below, and its roots are <InlineMath math="9.17" /> apart, not <InlineMath math="8" />.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} x^2-2x-20=0 \\ \sqrt{(-2)^2-4(1)(-20)}=\sqrt{84}=2\sqrt{21}\approx9.17 \end{gathered}" />

4. The statement about the tangent at <InlineMath math="(4,6)" /> is true. The tangent is perpendicular to the radius from <InlineMath math="M" /> to <InlineMath math="(4,6)" />, which points along <InlineMath math="(3,4)" />, so its equation is the equation below, that is <InlineMath math="3x+4y=36" />. It is parallel to <InlineMath math="3x+4y=0" /> because the coefficients of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> are the same.

   <BlockMath math="3(x-4)+4(y-6)=0" />

# Answer

So the statements about the point <InlineMath math="(4,6)" /> and its tangent are true, and the statements about the line given below and the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis are false.

<BlockMath math="3x+4y+14=0" />

# Given

The team doctor caps **total consumption** at most <InlineMath math="6" /> sachets weekly while nutrition goals stay fixed.

# Asked

The **new minimum cost** or feasibility under the six-sachet cap.

# Solution

> The new upper cap trims the feasible region from above, so recheck which corners remain legal and cheapest.

Before looking for the cheapest plan, check **whether any plan still meets every condition**. A tidy way is to find the fewest sachets that can meet both nutrition needs.

In the previous question the calcium and protein limits met at <InlineMath math="(3,4)" />, that is <InlineMath math="7" /> sachets. The region meeting both nutrition needs is open upward with corners <InlineMath math="(0,10)" />, <InlineMath math="(3,4)" /> and <InlineMath math="(15,0)" />. The numbers of sachets <InlineMath math="x+y" /> at those corners are <InlineMath math="10" />, <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="15" />.

<BlockMath math="\min(x+y)=7\quad\text{at }(3,4)" />

So every plan with enough calcium and protein needs at least <InlineMath math="7" /> sachets. The new condition <InlineMath math="x+y\le6" /> cannot hold together with both needs. Checking the points that touch the new limit shows the same.

So no plan meets every condition.

Therefore, **no consumption plan satisfies all constraints simultaneously**.

# Given

We are given a **sum of two fractions** each undefined at <InlineMath math="x=-2" /> whose combined sum has a limit.

# Asked

The **limit of the combined sum** as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="-2" />.

# Solution

> Combine the two fractions over one common denominator first so the shared zero factor cancels cleanly.

As <InlineMath math="x\to-2" />, both fractions grow without bound with opposite signs, so the form is <InlineMath math="\infty-\infty" /> and the limit is not visible yet. **Use a common denominator** first, since the equation below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
x^2-4=(x+2)(x-2) \\
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{x+2}+\frac{4}{(x+2)(x-2)}&=\frac{(x-2)+4}{(x+2)(x-2)} \\
&=\frac{x+2}{(x+2)(x-2)}=\frac{1}{x-2},\qquad x\neq-2
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Once the factor <InlineMath math="x+2" /> is cancelled, direct substitution is safe.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to-2}\frac{1}{x-2}=\frac{1}{-4}=-\frac14" />

Draw the sum of the two fractions after simplifying. **The two parts that blow up cancel each other, and what's left is the curve the expression below** with one hole at <InlineMath math="x=-2" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{x-2}" />

Numerical check: for <InlineMath math="x=-1.99" />, <InlineMath math="-0.25" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{0.01}+\frac{4}{-0.0399}\approx100-100.25=-0.25" />

So the limit is <InlineMath math="-\frac14" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> assumes the two unbounded parts cancel completely.
- <InlineMath math="\infty" /> only looks at the first fraction growing.
- <InlineMath math="\frac14" /> factors <InlineMath math="x^2-4" /> wrongly as <InlineMath math="(x+2)(2-x)" />, leaving the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{1}{2-x}" />

- <InlineMath math="-4" /> substitutes into <InlineMath math="x-2" /> and forgets that what remains is the fraction the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{1}{x-2}" />

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="-\frac14" />**.

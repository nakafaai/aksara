# Given

We are given a **square matrix** <InlineMath math="A" /> containing real variables <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />.

# Asked

The **values of** <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> or their requested combination.

# Solution

> Match entries in identical slots to turn the matrix condition into ordinary number equations.

Transposing **turns rows into columns**: the entry in row <InlineMath math="i" />, column <InlineMath math="j" /> moves to row <InlineMath math="j" />, column <InlineMath math="i" />. Write <InlineMath math="A^T" /> first, then match it with <InlineMath math="B" /> entry by entry.

<BlockMath math="A^T=\begin{pmatrix} 2x & 3 \\ y+1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 6 & 3 \\ 5 & 4 \end{pmatrix}" />

The diagram below tracks where each entry of <InlineMath math="A" /> moves under the transpose. **The diagonal entries stay put, while the other two swap places**, and only then are they matched with <InlineMath math="B" />.

The entries without variables already match: <InlineMath math="3=3" /> and <InlineMath math="4=4" />. The other two entries give the variables.

<BlockMath math="2x=6\ \Rightarrow\ x=3,\qquad y+1=5\ \Rightarrow\ y=4" />

So <InlineMath math="7" />.

<BlockMath math="x+y=3+4=7" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="5" /> sets <InlineMath math="A" /> itself equal to <InlineMath math="B" />, giving <InlineMath math="y+1=3" />. But then the entries <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> would not match.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> is the product <InlineMath math="xy" />.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> is the value of <InlineMath math="2x" /> before dividing by <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> is only the value of <InlineMath math="y" />.

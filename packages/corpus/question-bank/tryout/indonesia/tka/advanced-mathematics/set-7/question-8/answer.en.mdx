# Given

We are given a **symmetric exponential function** <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> adding opposite powers of <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Asked

The **function value at a multiple input** using a clean algebraic squaring identity.

# Solution

> Square the sum of reciprocal powers because the middle product cancels cleanly to one.

You do not need to find <InlineMath math="a" /> first. Notice that <InlineMath math="f(2a)=2^{2a}+2^{-2a}" /> is **the sum of the squares of the two terms of <InlineMath math="f(a)" />**. Square <InlineMath math="f(a)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\left(2^a+2^{-a}\right)^2&=2^{2a}+2\cdot2^a\cdot2^{-a}+2^{-2a} \\
&=2^{2a}+2+2^{-2a}
\end{aligned}" />

The middle term is <InlineMath math="2" />, because <InlineMath math="1" />. So.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
2^a\cdot2^{-a}=2^0=1 \\
f(2a)=\big(f(a)\big)^2-2=\left(\frac52\right)^2-2=\frac{25}{4}-\frac84=\frac{17}{4}
\end{gathered}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="f" />. **The graph is symmetric about the vertical axis, so <InlineMath math="a=1" /> and <InlineMath math="a=-1" /> both give <InlineMath math="f(a)=\tfrac52" />**, and both give <InlineMath math="f(2a)=\tfrac{17}{4}" />.

Check: <InlineMath math="2^a+2^{-a}=\frac52" /> holds for <InlineMath math="2^a=2" />, that is <InlineMath math="a=1" />. Then <InlineMath math="\frac{17}{4}" />. The value <InlineMath math="a=-1" /> also works and gives the same result.

<BlockMath math="f(2)=4+\frac14=\frac{17}{4}" />

So <InlineMath math="f(2a)" />.

<BlockMath math="f(2a)=\frac{17}{4}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{25}{4}" /> forgets to subtract the middle term <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> assumes <InlineMath math="f(2a)=2f(a)" />, as if <InlineMath math="f" /> were linear.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{21}{4}" /> subtracts <InlineMath math="1" /> instead of <InlineMath math="2" />, forgetting the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> in the middle term.
- <InlineMath math="\frac94" /> subtracts <InlineMath math="4" />, as if the middle term were <InlineMath math="2\cdot2" />.

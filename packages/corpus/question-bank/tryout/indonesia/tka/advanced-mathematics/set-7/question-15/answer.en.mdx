# Given

Two circles <InlineMath math="L_1" /> and <InlineMath math="L_2" /> **intersect** at two points in the plane.

# Asked

The **common chord length** or radical line joining the two intersection points.

# Solution

> Subtracting one circle equation from the other wipes out both squares and immediately reveals the common chord line.

Both intersection points satisfy both equations, so they also satisfy **the difference of the equations**. That difference is the equation of the line containing the common chord.

<BlockMath math="\left(x^2+y^2\right)-\left(x^2+y^2-12x+11\right)=25-0\ \Rightarrow\ 12x-11=25\ \Rightarrow\ x=3" />

Substitute <InlineMath math="x=3" /> into <InlineMath math="L_1" /> to get both intersection points.

<BlockMath math="9+y^2=25\ \Rightarrow\ y=\pm4" />

Draw both circles. **They're the same size with centres <InlineMath math="6" /> apart, so the common chord sits exactly halfway, on the line <InlineMath math="x=3" />**.

The intersection points are <InlineMath math="(3,4)" /> and <InlineMath math="(3,-4)" />, so the chord has length <InlineMath math="4-(-4)=8" />. Check on <InlineMath math="L_2" />: <InlineMath math="0" />. Notice that <InlineMath math="L_2" /> is the equation below, a circle of the same radius whose centre is <InlineMath math="6" /> away, so the chord sits exactly halfway, at <InlineMath math="x=3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
9+16-36+11=0 \\
(x-6)^2+y^2=25
\end{gathered}" />

So the common chord has length <InlineMath math="8" />.

- <InlineMath math="6" /> is the distance between the centres.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> is the diameter of either circle.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> is the radius of either circle.

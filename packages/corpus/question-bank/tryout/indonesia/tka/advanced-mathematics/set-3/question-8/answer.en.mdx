For <InlineMath math="(f\circ g)(x)=f\big(g(x)\big)" /> to be defined, **two conditions must hold together**: <InlineMath math="x" /> must lie in the domain of <InlineMath math="g" />, and the value <InlineMath math="g(x)" /> must lie in the domain of <InlineMath math="f" />.

First condition: <InlineMath math="x\ne3" />. Second condition: the number under the square root is not negative, so <InlineMath math="g(x)-1\ge0" />. Simplify the left side.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{x+2}{x-3}-1&\ge0 \\
\frac{x+2-(x-3)}{x-3}&\ge0 \\
\frac{5}{x-3}&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The numerator <InlineMath math="5" /> is always positive, so the fraction is non-negative exactly when the denominator is positive, <InlineMath math="x-3>0" />. This already includes <InlineMath math="x\ne3" />.

Check two points: for <InlineMath math="x=4" />, <InlineMath math="g(4)=6" /> and <InlineMath math="f(6)=\sqrt5" /> is defined. For <InlineMath math="x=0" />, <InlineMath math="g(0)=-\frac23" /> and <InlineMath math="f\left(-\frac23\right)=\sqrt{-\frac53}" /> is not defined.

So the natural domain of <InlineMath math="f\circ g" /> is <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x>3\}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\ge1\}" /> is the domain of <InlineMath math="f" />, not of the composition.
- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\ne3\}" /> only checks the domain of <InlineMath math="g" />.
- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x\le-2\text{ or }x>3\}" /> solves <InlineMath math="g(x)\ge0" />, but the condition is <InlineMath math="g(x)\ge1" />.
- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid -2\le x<3\}" /> reverses the inequality <InlineMath math="g(x)\ge0" />.

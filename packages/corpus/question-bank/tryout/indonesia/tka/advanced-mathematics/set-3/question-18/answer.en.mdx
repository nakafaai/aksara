A rotation about a point other than the origin takes three steps: **shift the centre to the origin, rotate, then shift back**. A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise rotation about the origin maps <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> to <InlineMath math="(b,-a)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P-C&=(3-1,\ -2-1)=(2,-3) \\
\text{rotated}&:\ (2,-3)\mapsto(-3,-2) \\
P'&=(-3+1,\ -2+1)=(-2,-1)
\end{aligned}" />

Check: the distance to the centre is unchanged, since <InlineMath math="|CP|^2=4+9=13" /> and <InlineMath math="|CP'|^2=9+4=13" />. The vectors <InlineMath math="(2,-3)" /> and <InlineMath math="(-3,-2)" /> are also perpendicular, because <InlineMath math="-6+6=0" />.

So the image of <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="(-2,-1)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(4,3)" /> rotates anticlockwise, <InlineMath math="(a,b)\mapsto(-b,a)" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-2,-3)" /> rotates about the origin and ignores the centre <InlineMath math="C" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-3,-2)" /> forgets to shift back by adding the coordinates of <InlineMath math="C" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-1,4)" /> is the <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation about <InlineMath math="C" />.

Reflection in the line <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> **swaps the two coordinates and changes both signs**: <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(-y,-x)" />. In matrix form:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}" />

Check: the line <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> must be the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="PP'" />. The midpoint <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{-3-5}{2},\frac{5+3}{2}\right)=(-4,4)" /> lies on <InlineMath math="y=-x" />, and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PP'}=(-2,-2)" /> is perpendicular to the line's direction <InlineMath math="(1,-1)" />, since <InlineMath math="-2+2=0" />.

So the image of <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="(-5,3)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(3,-5)" /> is the <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation about the origin, which only changes the signs.
- <InlineMath math="(5,3)" /> is the <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise rotation, <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(y,-x)" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-3,-5)" /> is the reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

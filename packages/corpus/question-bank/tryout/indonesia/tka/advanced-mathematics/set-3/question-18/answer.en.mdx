# Given

Point <InlineMath math="P(-3,5)" /> undergoes a **reflection** across the diagonal line <InlineMath math="y=-x" />.

# Asked

The **image coordinates** of point <InlineMath math="P" /> after reflection.

# Solution

> Reflecting across the downward diagonal simply swaps the two coordinates and flips both of their signs.

Reflection in the line <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> **swaps the two coordinates and changes both signs**: <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(-y,-x)" />. In matrix form:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="P" />, its image, and the mirror line. **The line <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> cuts the segment <InlineMath math="PP'" /> at a right angle, exactly at its midpoint**.

Check: the line <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> must be the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="PP'" />. The midpoint the equation below lies on <InlineMath math="y=-x" />, and the equation below is perpendicular to the line's direction <InlineMath math="(1,-1)" />, since <InlineMath math="-2+2=0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\left(\frac{-3-5}{2},\frac{5+3}{2}\right)=(-4,4) \\
\overrightarrow{PP'}=(-2,-2)
\end{gathered}" />

So the image of <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="(-5,3)" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="(5,-3)" /> is the image under reflection in <InlineMath math="y=x" />, which only swaps the coordinates.
- <InlineMath math="(3,-5)" /> is the <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation about the origin, which only changes the signs.
- <InlineMath math="(5,3)" /> is the <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise rotation, <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(y,-x)" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-3,-5)" /> is the reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="(-5,3)" />**.

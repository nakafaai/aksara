Each statement asks how <InlineMath math="f" /> behaves near a point or for very large <InlineMath math="x" />. Factor first to see **which factor tends to zero** at each point: <InlineMath math="f(x)=\frac{x(2x+3)}{(x-1)(x+1)}" />.

1. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=2" /> is true. Numerator and denominator both have degree two, so the limit is the ratio of the <InlineMath math="x^2" />-coefficients, <InlineMath math="\frac21" />.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to1^+}f(x)=\infty" /> is true. The numerator tends to <InlineMath math="1\cdot5=5>0" />. For <InlineMath math="x" /> slightly above <InlineMath math="1" />, the denominator <InlineMath math="x^2-1" /> is positive and tends to zero, so the fraction becomes a very large positive number.
3. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to-1}f(x)=-\frac12" /> is false. The numerator tends to <InlineMath math="2-3=-1\ne0" /> while the denominator tends to zero, so <InlineMath math="f" /> grows without bound and has no real limit there.
4. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\frac{f(x)}{x}=-3" /> is true. For <InlineMath math="x\ne0" /> the factor <InlineMath math="x" /> cancels: <InlineMath math="\frac{f(x)}{x}=\frac{2x+3}{x^2-1}" />, which tends to <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{-1}=-3" />.
5. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}x\big(f(x)-2\big)=0" /> is false. Compute the difference first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f(x)-2&=\frac{2x^2+3x-2(x^2-1)}{x^2-1}=\frac{3x+2}{x^2-1} \\
\lim_{x\to\infty}x\cdot\frac{3x+2}{x^2-1}&=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^2+2x}{x^2-1}=3
\end{aligned}" />

The difference does tend to zero, but multiplied by <InlineMath math="x" /> it tends to <InlineMath math="3" />, not <InlineMath math="0" />.

So the true statements are <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=2" />, <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to1^+}f(x)=\infty" /> and <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\frac{f(x)}{x}=-3" />.

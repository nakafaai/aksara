How two circles sit relative to each other depends on **the distance between the centres compared with the sum and difference of the radii**. So find each centre and radius.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
L_1&:\ (x-1)^2+(y-2)^2=1+4+4=9 &&\Rightarrow\ M_1(1,2),\ r_1=3 \\
L_2&:\ (x-5)^2+(y-4)^2=25+16-37=4 &&\Rightarrow\ M_2(5,4),\ r_2=2
\end{aligned}" />

The centres are <InlineMath math="|M_1M_2|=\sqrt{4^2+2^2}=\sqrt{20}\approx4.47" /> apart.

1. The statement that they touch externally is false. External tangency means <InlineMath math="|M_1M_2|=r_1+r_2=5" />. Here <InlineMath math="r_1-r_2=1<\sqrt{20}<5" />, so the circles meet in two points.
2. The statement that the centre of <InlineMath math="L_2" /> lies inside <InlineMath math="L_1" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="|M_1M_2|=\sqrt{20}>3=r_1" />.
3. The statement about the common chord is true. Both intersection points satisfy both equations, so they also satisfy their difference. Subtract the equation of <InlineMath math="L_2" /> from that of <InlineMath math="L_1" />.

4. The statement that <InlineMath math="y=6" /> is tangent to <InlineMath math="L_2" /> is true. The distance from <InlineMath math="M_2(5,4)" /> to that line is <InlineMath math="6-4=2=r_2" />.

So the statements about the common chord and the line <InlineMath math="y=6" /> are true, and the statements about external tangency and the position of the centre of <InlineMath math="L_2" /> are false.

# Given

Triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> in space has **three vertices** <InlineMath math="A(1,-2,3)" />, <InlineMath math="B(5,4,-1)" />, and <InlineMath math="C(3,1,7)" />.

# Asked

The **centroid coordinates** <InlineMath math="G" /> or requested position vector of the triangle.

# Solution

> A triangle centroid is its balance point, found simply by averaging the coordinates of all three corners.

The position vector of the centroid is **the average of the three vertices' position vectors**. This comes from the centroid dividing every median in the ratio <InlineMath math="2:1" /> from its vertex.

<BlockMath math="\vec{g}=\frac{\vec{a}+\vec{b}+\vec{c}}{3}=\frac{(1+5+3,\ -2+4+1,\ 3-1+7)}{3}=\frac{(9,3,9)}{3}=(3,1,3)" />

Compute <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AG}" /> and its length.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AG}&=(3-1,\ 1+2,\ 3-3)=(2,3,0) \\
|\overrightarrow{AG}|&=\sqrt{4+9+0}=\sqrt{13}
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the triangle in its own plane, with its three medians. **The centroid <InlineMath math="G" /> sits two thirds of the way from <InlineMath math="A" /> to the midpoint <InlineMath math="M" />**, so the equation below.

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{AG}=\tfrac23\overrightarrow{AM}" />

Check with the median: the midpoint of <InlineMath math="BC" /> is <InlineMath math="M\left(4,\frac52,3\right)" />, and the equation below. Indeed the equation below.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{19}" /> is the length of <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{OG}" />, the centroid's distance from the origin.
- <InlineMath math="\frac32\sqrt{13}" /> is the whole median <InlineMath math="AM" />, not two thirds of it.
- <InlineMath math="5\sqrt5" /> uses <InlineMath math="\vec{a}+\vec{b}+\vec{c}" /> without dividing by <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{13}" /> doubles the correct answer, for example by dividing <InlineMath math="\vec{a}+\vec{b}+\vec{c}" /> by <InlineMath math="3" /> and then applying the <InlineMath math="2:1" /> ratio a second time.

# Given

We know the **remainders of polynomial** <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> when divided by two different quadratic factors.

# Asked

The **remainder** when <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by the combined quadratic divisor.

# Solution

> A quadratic divisor always leaves a linear remainder, and plugging in the divisor roots immediately pins it down.

A divisor of degree two always leaves a remainder of degree at most one, so write the remainder you want as <InlineMath math="ax+b" />. To find <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b" /> you need <InlineMath math="P(1)" /> and <InlineMath math="P(3)" />. The key is that **each value must come from the division that contains that factor**.

From <InlineMath math="P(x)" />, substituting <InlineMath math="x=1" /> makes the first term zero. From <InlineMath math="P(x)" />, substituting <InlineMath math="x=3" /> also makes the first term zero.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
P(x)=(x-1)(x-2)H_1(x)+4x-1 \\
P(x)=(x-2)(x-3)H_2(x)+7x-7 \\
P(1)=4(1)-1=3,\qquad P(3)=7(3)-7=14
\end{gathered}" />

Both divisions also give <InlineMath math="P(2)=7" />, so the data are consistent. Now <InlineMath math="P(x)" />, so <InlineMath math="P(1)=a+b" /> and <InlineMath math="P(3)=3a+b" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
P(x)=(x-1)(x-3)H_3(x)+ax+b \\
\begin{aligned}
a+b&=3 \\
3a+b&=14 \\
2a&=11\ \Rightarrow\ a=\frac{11}{2},\quad b=3-\frac{11}{2}=-\frac52
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

The remainder on division by <InlineMath math="(x-a)(x-b)" /> is the line through the points of <InlineMath math="P" /> at <InlineMath math="x=a" /> and <InlineMath math="x=b" />. **The remainder you need is the line through <InlineMath math="(1,\ 3)" /> and <InlineMath math="(3,\ 14)" />**.

So the remainder is the equation below.

<BlockMath math="\frac{11}{2}x-\frac52=\frac{11x-5}{2}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="11x-5" /> forgets to divide by <InlineMath math="2" /> when solving <InlineMath math="2a=11" />.
- <InlineMath math="4x-1" /> takes <InlineMath math="P(3)" /> from the first remainder, <InlineMath math="4(3)-1=11" />. That remainder only holds at <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=2" />.
- <InlineMath math="7x-7" /> takes <InlineMath math="P(1)" /> from the second remainder, <InlineMath math="7(1)-7=0" />. That remainder only holds at <InlineMath math="x=2" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" />.
- The expression below slips a sign when computing <InlineMath math="b" />.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} \frac{11x+5}{2} \\ b=3-\frac{11}{2} \end{gathered}" />

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is given below.

<BlockMath math="\frac{11x-5}{2}" />

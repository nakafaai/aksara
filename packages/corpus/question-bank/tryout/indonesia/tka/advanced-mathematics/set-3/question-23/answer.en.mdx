# Given

We are given a **limit at infinity** involving a difference with a square root.

# Asked

The **limit value** as <InlineMath math="x" /> grows without bound.

# Solution

> Multiply by the conjugate to turn the subtracting square root into a fraction whose leading terms compare cleanly.

The factor <InlineMath math="x" /> grows without bound, while the difference in brackets shrinks toward <InlineMath math="0" />. This <InlineMath math="\infty\cdot0" /> form does not show the limit yet, so **multiply by the conjugate** <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x^2+6}+x" /> to remove the difference of roots.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x\left(\sqrt{x^2+6}-x\right)&=x\cdot\frac{(x^2+6)-x^2}{\sqrt{x^2+6}+x} \\
&=\frac{6x}{\sqrt{x^2+6}+x}
\end{aligned}" />

Divide numerator and denominator by <InlineMath math="x" />. For <InlineMath math="x>0" />, the equation below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\frac{\sqrt{x^2+6}}{x}=\sqrt{1+\frac{6}{x^2}} \\
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{6}{\sqrt{1+\frac{6}{x^2}}+1}=\frac{6}{1+1}=3
\end{gathered}" />

Draw the product for large <InlineMath math="x" />. **The growing factor <InlineMath math="x" /> and the shrinking difference balance out**, so the curve hugs the line <InlineMath math="y=3" />.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> only sees that the bracket tends to <InlineMath math="0" /> and ignores the factor <InlineMath math="x" />.

"Divisible" means the remainder is zero. By **the factor theorem**, <InlineMath math="P(1)=0" /> and <InlineMath math="P(-2)=0" />. Those two equations are enough for the two unknown numbers.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(1)&=1+a+b-6=0 &&\Rightarrow\ a+b=5 \\
P(-2)&=-8+4a-2b-6=0 &&\Rightarrow\ 2a-b=7
\end{aligned}" />

Adding the equations gives <InlineMath math="3a=12" />, so <InlineMath math="a=4" /> and <InlineMath math="b=1" />. The polynomial is <InlineMath math="P(x)=x^3+4x^2+x-6" />. Divide by <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x+2)=x^2+x-2" /> to get the third factor.

<BlockMath math="x^3+4x^2+x-6=(x^2+x-2)(x+3)=(x-1)(x+2)(x+3)" />

1. The statement <InlineMath math="a=3" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="a=4" />.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="b=1" /> is true.
3. The statement that <InlineMath math="x+3" /> is a factor of <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is true, as the factorisation shows. Check: <InlineMath math="P(-3)=-27+36-3-6=0" />.
4. The statement about the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x-2" /> is false. The remainder is <InlineMath math="P(2)=8+16+2-6=20" />.
5. The statement about the sum of the squares of the roots is true. The roots are <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="-2" /> and <InlineMath math="-3" />, so <InlineMath math="1+4+9=14" />.

So the true statements are <InlineMath math="b=1" />, that <InlineMath math="x+3" /> is a factor of <InlineMath math="P(x)" />, and that the squares of the roots add up to <InlineMath math="14" />.

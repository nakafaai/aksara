# Given

A cubic polynomial <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> has **unknown coefficients** <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b" /> with known factor and remainder facts.

# Asked

The **unknown coefficient values** or requested combination from <InlineMath math="P(x)" />.

# Solution

> The remainder theorem turns each division clue into a simple equation for the two unknown numbers.

Turn both facts into equations with **the remainder theorem**: divisible by <InlineMath math="x-2" /> means <InlineMath math="P(2)=0" />, and remainder <InlineMath math="-6" /> on division by <InlineMath math="x+1" /> means <InlineMath math="P(-1)=-6" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(2)&=16+4a+2b-4=0 &&\Rightarrow\ 2a+b=-6 \\
P(-1)&=-2+a-b-4=-6 &&\Rightarrow\ a-b=0
\end{aligned}" />

The second equation gives <InlineMath math="a=b" />. Substituting into the first: <InlineMath math="3a=-6" />, so <InlineMath math="a=-2" />. Factor by dividing by <InlineMath math="x-2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
a=b=-2 \\
P(x)=2x^3-2x^2-2x-4=2(x-2)(x^2+x+1)
\end{gathered}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="P(x)" />. **The graph crosses the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis only once, at <InlineMath math="x=2" />**, because the quadratic factor is always positive. The points at <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=1" /> show both remainders of <InlineMath math="-6" />.

<BlockMath math="P(x)=2(x-2)(x^2+x+1)" />

1. The statement <InlineMath math="a=2" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="a=-2" />.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="a+b=-4" /> is true.
3. The statement about exactly one real root is true. The factor <InlineMath math="x^2+x+1" /> has discriminant <InlineMath math="0" />, so it has no real roots. The only real root is <InlineMath math="x=2" />.

   <BlockMath math="1-4=-3<0" />

4. The statement that <InlineMath math="x+1" /> is a factor of <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is false, because the remainder is <InlineMath math="-6\ne0" />.
5. The statement about the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x-1" /> is true: <InlineMath math="P(1)=-6" />.

   <BlockMath math="P(1)=2-2-2-4=-6" />

# Answer

Therefore, the correct choices are **statements <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="3" />, and <InlineMath math="5" />**.

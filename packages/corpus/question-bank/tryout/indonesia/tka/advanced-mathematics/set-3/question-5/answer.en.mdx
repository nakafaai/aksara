# Given

# Asked

# Solution

> Adding a new constraint slices off part of the old region, so we just test the new corners along that boundary line.

The order adds a new limit <InlineMath math="x\ge12" />. The old best plan <InlineMath math="(10,20)" /> **no longer meets that limit**, so the optimum has to be found again on the smaller feasible region.

With <InlineMath math="x\ge12" />, the assembly limit <InlineMath math="2x+y\le40" /> becomes the tightest. Find the new corners.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x=12,\ 2x+y=40&\ \Rightarrow\ y=16 \\
y=0,\ 2x+y=40&\ \Rightarrow\ x=20
\end{aligned}" />

The point <InlineMath math="(12,16)" /> also meets the other limits: <InlineMath math="12+16=28\le30" /> testing hours and <InlineMath math="16\le25" /> fans. The corners of the new region are <InlineMath math="(12,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(20,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(12,16)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(12,0)&=720 \\
P(20,0)&=1{,}200 \\
P(12,16)&=720+640=1{,}360
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the new limit <InlineMath math="x\ge12" /> over the old region. **All that's left is a small triangle to the right of the line <InlineMath math="x=12" />**, and the old optimum <InlineMath math="(10,\ 20)" /> is left outside it.

# Given

A **circle** is reflected twice in a row across two parallel vertical mirror lines.

# Asked

The **equation of the image circle** after both reflections.

# Solution

> Mirrors never stretch or shrink a circle, so we only track where the center moves while keeping the same radius.

A reflection does not change size, so the radius stays the same. You only need to track **where the centre goes**. Reflection in the vertical line <InlineMath math="x=h" /> maps <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> to <InlineMath math="(2h-a,b)" />.

Complete the square to get the centre and radius of the original circle.

<BlockMath math="(x-1)^2+(y-3)^2=1+9-6=4" />

The centre is <InlineMath math="(1,3)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=2" />. Track the centre in the given order.

<BlockMath math="(1,3)\ \xrightarrow{\ x=2\ }\ (4-1,3)=(3,3)\ \xrightarrow{\ x=5\ }\ (10-3,3)=(7,3)" />

Track the circle's centre through both mirrors. **Two reflections in parallel lines <InlineMath math="3" /> apart shift the circle <InlineMath math="6" /> units to the right**, and the radius stays <InlineMath math="2" />.

Two reflections in parallel lines <InlineMath math="3" /> apart equal one translation of <InlineMath math="2\cdot3=6" /> units to the right, from the first line toward the second. The result agrees: <InlineMath math="1+6=7" />.

<BlockMath math="(x-7)^2+(y-3)^2=4\iff x^2+y^2-14x-6y+54=0" />

So the image circle is the equation below.

<BlockMath math="x^2+y^2-14x-6y+54=0" />

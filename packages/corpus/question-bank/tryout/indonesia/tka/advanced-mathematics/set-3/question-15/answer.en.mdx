# Given

From outer point <InlineMath math="P(7,1)" />, a **tangent segment** touches a given circle at point <InlineMath math="S" />.

# Asked

The **length of the tangent segment** <InlineMath math="PS" />.

# Solution

> The radius meets the tangent at a right angle, forming a neat right triangle with the center and the outside point.

The radius to the point of tangency is **always perpendicular to the tangent**. So the centre <InlineMath math="M" />, the point of tangency <InlineMath math="S" /> and the point <InlineMath math="P" /> form a right triangle at <InlineMath math="S" />, with hypotenuse <InlineMath math="MP" />.

Complete the square to get the centre and radius.

<BlockMath math="(x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=4+1+4=9" />

The centre is <InlineMath math="M(2,-1)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=3" />. Compute the squared distance <InlineMath math="MP" /> and apply Pythagoras.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
|MP|^2&=(7-2)^2+(1+1)^2=25+4=29 \\
|PS|^2&=|MP|^2-r^2=29-9=20 \\
|PS|&=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5
\end{aligned}" />

The point <InlineMath math="P" /> is outside the circle, since <InlineMath math="|MP|^2>9" />, so a tangent from <InlineMath math="P" /> exists.

<BlockMath math="|MP|^2=29>9" />

So the tangent segment <InlineMath math="PS" /> has length <InlineMath math="2\sqrt5" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{29}" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to the centre, the hypotenuse.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{38}" /> adds <InlineMath math="29+9" />, but the squared radius must be subtracted.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt7" /> uses <InlineMath math="r^2=1" />, a sign slip on the constant.

  <BlockMath math="r^2=4+1-4=1" />

- <InlineMath math="4" /> uses only the <InlineMath math="x" />-difference, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{25-9}" />, and forgets the <InlineMath math="y" />-difference.

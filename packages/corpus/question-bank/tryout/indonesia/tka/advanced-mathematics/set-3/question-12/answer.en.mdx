The dot product gives <InlineMath math="\cos\theta" /> directly, not <InlineMath math="\sin\theta" />. So find **the cosine first**, then use <InlineMath math="\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}&=2\cdot3+1\cdot(-4)+(-2)\cdot0=2 \\
|\vec{u}|&=\sqrt{4+1+4}=3,\qquad |\vec{v}|=\sqrt{9+16+0}=5 \\
\cos\theta&=\frac{\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}}{|\vec{u}||\vec{v}|}=\frac{2}{15}
\end{aligned}" />

The angle between two vectors lies between <InlineMath math="0^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, so <InlineMath math="\sin\theta\ge0" />. Take the positive root.

<BlockMath math="\sin\theta=\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{2}{15}\right)^2}=\sqrt{\frac{225-4}{225}}=\frac{\sqrt{221}}{15}" />

So <InlineMath math="\sin\theta=\frac{\sqrt{221}}{15}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac{\sqrt5}{3}" /> comes from a dot product of <InlineMath math="10" />, a sign slip in <InlineMath math="1\cdot(-4)" />, which gives <InlineMath math="\cos\theta=\frac23" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac23" /> is the cosine with that same sign slip.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{\sqrt{229}}{15}" /> adds <InlineMath math="1+\cos^2\theta" /> instead of subtracting.

Combine the two transformations into one rule, then **write the old coordinates in terms of the image coordinates** and substitute into the line.

Reflection in <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-y,-x)" />. The dilation <InlineMath math="[O,2]" /> doubles both coordinates. So the image is <InlineMath math="(x',y')=(-2y,-2x)" />, which means <InlineMath math="x=-\frac{y'}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="y=-\frac{x'}{2}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2\left(-\frac{y'}{2}\right)-\left(-\frac{x'}{2}\right)+4&=0 \\
-y'+\frac{x'}{2}+4&=0 \\
x'-2y'+8&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Check with one point: <InlineMath math="(0,4)" /> lies on the original line. The reflection sends it to <InlineMath math="(-4,0)" />, the dilation to <InlineMath math="(-8,0)" />, and <InlineMath math="-8-0+8=0" />.

So the image line is <InlineMath math="x-2y+8=0" />.

- <InlineMath math="x-2y+4=0" /> only reflects and forgets the dilation.
- <InlineMath math="x-2y+2=0" /> substitutes <InlineMath math="x=-2y'" /> and <InlineMath math="y=-2x'" />, multiplying by <InlineMath math="2" /> where it should divide.
- <InlineMath math="2x-y+8=0" /> only dilates and forgets the reflection.

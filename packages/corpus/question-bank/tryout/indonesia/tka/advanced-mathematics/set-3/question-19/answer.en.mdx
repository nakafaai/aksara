# Given

A **straight line** is reflected across <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> and then dilated from the origin <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />.

# Asked

The **equation of the image line** after both transformations.

# Solution

> Combine both moves into one point rule, then write old coordinates in terms of new ones to transform the line equation.

Combine the two transformations into one rule, then **write the old coordinates in terms of the image coordinates** and substitute into the line.

Reflection in <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-y,-x)" />. The dilation <InlineMath math="[O,2]" /> doubles both coordinates. So the image is <InlineMath math="(x',y')=(-2y,-2x)" />, which means <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
x=-\frac{y'}{2} \\
y=-\frac{x'}{2} \\
\begin{aligned}
2\left(-\frac{y'}{2}\right)-\left(-\frac{x'}{2}\right)+4&=0 \\
-y'+\frac{x'}{2}+4&=0 \\
x'-2y'+8&=0
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Follow the line and one of its points through both steps. **The reflection turns the line into the gentler line the equation below, then the dilation pushes it away from <InlineMath math="O" />** without changing its slope.

<BlockMath math="x-2y+4=0" />

Check with one point: <InlineMath math="(0,4)" /> lies on the original line. The reflection sends it to <InlineMath math="(-4,0)" />, the dilation to <InlineMath math="(-8,0)" />, and <InlineMath math="0" />.

<BlockMath math="-8-0+8=0" />

So the image line is the equation below.

<BlockMath math="x-2y+8=0" />

# Given

We are given a **linear rational function** <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> defined for <InlineMath math="x\ne-2" />.

# Asked

The **formula for the inverse function** <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(x)" /> and its domain.

# Solution

> Finding an inverse means running the function machine backward by solving for the original input in terms of the output.

An inverse function **swaps the roles of input and output**. Write <InlineMath math="y=f(x)" />, solve for <InlineMath math="x" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="y" />, then rename the variable.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y(x+2)&=3x-1 \\
xy+2y&=3x-1 \\
xy-3x&=-1-2y \\
x(y-3)&=-(2y+1) \\
x&=\frac{2y+1}{3-y}
\end{aligned}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="f" /> and <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> with the line <InlineMath math="y=x" />. **The graph of the inverse is the mirror image of <InlineMath math="f" /> in <InlineMath math="y=x" />**, so the horizontal line <InlineMath math="y=3" /> for <InlineMath math="f" /> becomes the vertical line <InlineMath math="x=3" /> for <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" />.

The last step divides by <InlineMath math="y-3" />, so it needs <InlineMath math="y\ne3" />. Renaming <InlineMath math="y" /> as <InlineMath math="x" /> gives the equation below for <InlineMath math="x\ne3" />.

<BlockMath math="f^{-1}(x)=\frac{2x+1}{3-x}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="f(1)=\frac23" />, and <InlineMath math="1" />.

<BlockMath math="f^{-1}\left(\frac23\right)=\frac{\frac43+1}{3-\frac23}=\frac{7/3}{7/3}=1" />

So the equation below with <InlineMath math="x\ne3" />.

<BlockMath math="f^{-1}(x)=\frac{2x+1}{3-x}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- The expression below is the reciprocal <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{f(x)}" />, not the inverse function.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{x+2}{3x-1}" />

- The expression below slips a sign when moving the <InlineMath math="-1" /> to the right side.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{2x-1}{x-3}" />

- The expression below has the wrong sign in the denominator, as if the factor were <InlineMath math="y+3" />.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{2x+1}{x+3}" />

- The expression below turns the correct inverse upside down.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{3-x}{2x+1}" />

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is given below.

<BlockMath math="f^{-1}(x)=\frac{2x+1}{3-x},\ x\ne3" />

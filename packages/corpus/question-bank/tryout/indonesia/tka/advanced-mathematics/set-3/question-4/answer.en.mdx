# Given

A workshop assembles **lamps and fans** under weekly limits on assembly hours, testing hours, and parts, with known unit profits.

# Asked

The **maximum weekly profit** the workshop can earn.

# Solution

> Maximum profit always sits at one of the corners of the region that respects every time and material limit.

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the number of lamps and <InlineMath math="y" /> the number of fans per week. There are **three limits**: assembly hours, testing hours, and the stock of fan blades.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x+y&\le40 \\
x+y&\le30 \\
y&\le25 \\
x\ge0,\quad y&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The profit objective (each unit standing for <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}000" />) is <InlineMath math="P=60x+40y" />. Its maximum sits at a corner of the feasible region. Find the corners where neighbouring boundary lines cross.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x+y=40,\ x+y=30&\ \Rightarrow\ x=10,\ y=20 \\
x+y=30,\ y=25&\ \Rightarrow\ x=5,\ y=25
\end{aligned}" />

The other corners are <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(20,0)" /> on the line <InlineMath math="2x+y=40" />, and <InlineMath math="(0,25)" />. The point <InlineMath math="(10,20)" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="y\le25" />, and <InlineMath math="(5,25)" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="2\cdot5+25=35\le40" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(20,0)&=1{,}200 \\
P(10,20)&=600+800=1{,}400 \\
P(5,25)&=300+1{,}000=1{,}300 \\
P(0,25)&=1{,}000
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the feasible region. **The three limit lines cut it into a pentagon**, so you only need to compare the profit at its five corners.

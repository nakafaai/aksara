# Given

We are given a **general circle equation** and a **test point** <InlineMath math="T(2,-1)" />.

# Asked

The **position of point** <InlineMath math="T" /> inside, on, or outside the circle.

# Solution

> Simply plug the point coordinates into the circle expression because the sign immediately reveals inside, on, or outside.

Compare **the distance from the point to the centre with the radius**. Complete the square first.

<BlockMath math="(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=9+4-9=4" />

The centre is <InlineMath math="P(3,-2)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=2" />. The distance from <InlineMath math="T" /> to the centre:

<BlockMath math="|PT|=\sqrt{(2-3)^2+(-1+2)^2}=\sqrt{1+1}=\sqrt2\approx1.41" />

Draw the circle and the point <InlineMath math="T" />. **The segment from the centre to <InlineMath math="T" /> is shorter than the radius**, so <InlineMath math="T" /> is inside the circle.

Since <InlineMath math="\sqrt2<2" />, the point <InlineMath math="T" /> lies inside the circle. A shortcut agrees: substituting <InlineMath math="T" /> into the left side of the equation gives <InlineMath math="0" />.

<BlockMath math="4+1-12-4+9=-2<0" />

So <InlineMath math="T" /> lies inside the circle, <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" /> from the centre.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- Outside at distance <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" /> computes the distance correctly but compares it with the radius the wrong way.
- Exactly on the circle would need a distance of exactly <InlineMath math="2" />, or a substitution result of zero.
- A distance of <InlineMath math="2" /> is the radius, not the distance from <InlineMath math="T" /> to the centre.
- Outside at distance <InlineMath math="2" /> swaps distance and radius and draws the wrong conclusion.

# Answer

Therefore, point <InlineMath math="T" /> lies **inside the circle at distance <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2}" /> from the center**.

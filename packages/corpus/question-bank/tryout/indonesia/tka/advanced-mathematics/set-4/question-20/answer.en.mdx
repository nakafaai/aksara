# Given

Square <InlineMath math="ABCD" /> with **four known vertices** is transformed by a matrix.

# Asked

The **area of the image shape** after the matrix transformation.

# Solution

> The absolute value of the matrix determinant is the area scaling factor, so just multiply the starting area by it.

There are two ways to see this, and both give the same result. The first step is still to find <InlineMath math="k" /> from the image of <InlineMath math="C" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 1 & k \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2+2k \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\ \Rightarrow\ k=3" />

Draw the square and its image. **The shear slides each horizontal row according to its height**, so the base and height stay <InlineMath math="2" /> and the area stays <InlineMath math="4" />.

**The determinant route.** A linear transformation multiplies every area by <InlineMath math="|\det M|" />. Here <InlineMath math="\det M=1" /> for every value of <InlineMath math="k" />. So the image has the same area as the square, <InlineMath math="2\cdot2=4" />.

<BlockMath math="\det M=1\cdot1-k\cdot0=1" />

**The picture route.** The images of the corners are <InlineMath math="A'(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="B'(2,0)" />, <InlineMath math="C'(8,2)" /> and <InlineMath math="D'(6,2)" />. The shape is a parallelogram with base <InlineMath math="A'B'=2" /> on the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis and height <InlineMath math="2" />, the distance between the lines <InlineMath math="y=0" /> and <InlineMath math="y=2" />. Its area is <InlineMath math="2\cdot2=4" />.

A matrix like this is a shear: each point slides sideways by <InlineMath math="k" /> times its height, so the shape slants but its area stays the same.

So the image of the square has area <InlineMath math="4" /> square units.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="16" /> is the area of the bounding rectangle from <InlineMath math="x=0" /> to <InlineMath math="x=8" /> and <InlineMath math="y=0" /> to <InlineMath math="y=2" />, not the parallelogram.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> multiplies the square's area by <InlineMath math="k=3" />, as if <InlineMath math="k" /> were an area factor.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> is the area of triangle <InlineMath math="A'B'C'" />, half the parallelogram.
- <InlineMath math="4\sqrt{10}" /> takes the slanted side <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{10}" /> as the base with height <InlineMath math="2" />. That height is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="A'B'" />, not to <InlineMath math="A'D'" />.

  <BlockMath math="A'D'=\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}" />

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="4" />**.

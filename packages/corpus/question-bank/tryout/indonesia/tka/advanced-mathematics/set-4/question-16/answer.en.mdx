# Given

Two **circular pulleys** with radii <InlineMath math="11" /> cm and <InlineMath math="2" /> cm have centers <InlineMath math="15" /> cm apart.

# Asked

The **length of the common tangent** connecting the two pulleys.

# Solution

> Slide the tangent line parallel until it hits the smaller center to create one clean right triangle.

Both radii to the points of tangency are **perpendicular to the rope**, so they are parallel. Slide the rope parallel to itself until it passes through the centre of the small pulley. This makes a right triangle with hypotenuse equal to the distance between the centres, <InlineMath math="d=15" /> cm, one leg <InlineMath math="9" /> cm, and the other leg equal to the rope length you want.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
R-r=11-2=9 \\
\begin{aligned}
\ell^2&=d^2-(R-r)^2 \\
&=15^2-9^2=225-81=144 \\
\ell&=12
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Slide the rope parallel to itself until it passes through the small pulley's centre. **That makes a right triangle with hypotenuse <InlineMath math="15" />, one side <InlineMath math="R-r=9" />, and a third side as long as the rope**.

The pulleys are apart because <InlineMath math="d=13" />, so the external common tangent exists.

<BlockMath math="d=15>R+r=13" />

So the rope between the two points of tangency is <InlineMath math="12" /> cm long.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="9" /> cm is the difference of the radii, a leg of the triangle, not the rope.
- <InlineMath math="15" /> cm is the distance between the centres, the hypotenuse.
- <InlineMath math="3\sqrt{34}" /> cm adds <InlineMath math="15^2+9^2" />, but a leg comes from subtracting.
- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{14}" /> cm uses <InlineMath math="R+r=13" />, the length of the internal common tangent, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{225-169}" />.

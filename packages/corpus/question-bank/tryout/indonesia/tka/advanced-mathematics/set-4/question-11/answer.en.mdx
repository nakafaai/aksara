The boat's velocity over the ground is **the sum of two perpendicular vectors**: its velocity relative to the water, perpendicular to the banks, and the current, parallel to the banks. Put the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis along the current and the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis across the river.

<BlockMath math="\vec{v}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\text{ m/s}" />

The crossing time depends only on the component across the river, because only that component brings the boat closer to the other bank. During that time, the component along the banks carries the boat downstream.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{60\text{ m}}{4\text{ m/s}}=15\text{ s} \\
\text{drift}&=3\text{ m/s}\cdot15\text{ s}=45\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Check with the path: the boat travels <InlineMath math="|\vec{v}|\cdot t=5\cdot15=75" /> m, and indeed <InlineMath math="45^2+60^2=2{,}025+3{,}600=5{,}625=75^2" />.

So the boat lands <InlineMath math="45" /> m from the point directly opposite its start.

- <InlineMath math="60" /> m is the river's width, as if the current did not move the boat.
- <InlineMath math="75" /> m is the length of the slanted path, not the drift along the bank.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> m divides the width by the current's speed.
- <InlineMath math="36" /> m uses <InlineMath math="60\cdot\frac35" />, as if the boat travelled <InlineMath math="60" /> m along its slanted path.

# Given

An **origin-centered circle** is dilated by scale factor <InlineMath math="2" /> and then shifted by a translation vector.

# Asked

The **equation of the image circle** after dilation and translation.

# Solution

> Dilation simply scales the circle radius, while translation only slides the center to its new home.

A circle is fixed by its centre and radius, so **track those two quantities** through each transformation. The original circle has centre <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=3" />.

The dilation <InlineMath math="[O,2]" /> keeps the centre at <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> and doubles the radius to <InlineMath math="6" />. The translation moves the centre to <InlineMath math="(1,-3)" /> without changing the radius.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x-1)^2+(y+3)^2&=36 \\
x^2-2x+1+y^2+6y+9-36&=0 \\
x^2+y^2-2x+6y-26&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Track the centre and radius through both steps. **The dilation only enlarges the circle from <InlineMath math="r=3" /> to <InlineMath math="r=6" />, then the translation shifts it whole** to the centre <InlineMath math="(1,-3)" />.

A circle tangent to the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis is <InlineMath math="r" /> away from it, and a circle tangent to the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis is <InlineMath math="r" /> away from that axis. In the first quadrant **the centre must be** <InlineMath math="(r,r)" />, so the equation is <InlineMath math="(x-r)^2+(y-r)^2=r^2" />.

The point <InlineMath math="(2,1)" /> lies on the circle, so substitute it.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(2-r)^2+(1-r)^2&=r^2 \\
4-4r+r^2+1-2r+r^2&=r^2 \\
r^2-6r+5&=0 \\
(r-1)(r-5)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

There are two circles, with <InlineMath math="r=1" /> and <InlineMath math="r=5" />. Both are valid: <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2+(y-1)^2=1" /> contains <InlineMath math="(2,1)" /> because <InlineMath math="1+0=1" />, and <InlineMath math="(x-5)^2+(y-5)^2=25" /> contains it because <InlineMath math="9+16=25" />.

So the largest radius is <InlineMath math="5" />.

- <InlineMath math="1" /> is the radius of the smaller circle.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> is the average of the two radii, not the radius of either circle.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> is the sum of the two radii, the coefficient of <InlineMath math="r" /> in the quadratic.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt5" /> is the distance from <InlineMath math="(2,1)" /> to the origin.

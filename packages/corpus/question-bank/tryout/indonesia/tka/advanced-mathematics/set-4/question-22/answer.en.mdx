# Given

We are given a **trigonometric limit** near <InlineMath math="x=0" /> involving secant and tangent.

# Asked

The **value of the trigonometric limit** as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches zero.

# Solution

> Rewrite secant and tangent in terms of sine and cosine first so the zero-over-zero form simplifies cleanly.

Direct substitution gives the equation below. **Write <InlineMath math="\sec(2x)" /> as <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{\cos2x}" />** so the numerator takes the form <InlineMath math="1-\cos2x" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\frac{1-1}{0}=\frac00 \\
\sec(2x)-1=\frac{1-\cos2x}{\cos2x}=\frac{2\sin^2x}{\cos2x}
\end{gathered}" />

That line uses <InlineMath math="1-\cos2x=2\sin^2x" />. Now split it into standard limits.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{2\sin^2x}{\cos2x\cdot x\tan x}&=2\cdot\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}\cdot\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{\tan x}\cdot\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1}{\cos2x} \\
&=2\cdot1\cdot1\cdot1=2
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the function on both sides of zero. **Both branches come down to the same point, <InlineMath math="(0,\ 2)" />**, even though the function isn't defined at <InlineMath math="x=0" />.

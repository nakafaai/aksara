This piecewise function is built from rules that are each continuous. So the interesting limits are only **at the joins**, <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" />, where you compare the left and right limits.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\to1^-}f(x)&=1^2-1=0, &\lim_{x\to1^+}f(x)&=2(1)-2=0, &f(1)&=0 \\
\lim_{x\to3^-}f(x)&=2(3)-2=4, &\lim_{x\to3^+}f(x)&=8-3=5, &f(3)&=4
\end{aligned}" />

1. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to1}f(x)=0" /> is true, because the left and right limits are both <InlineMath math="0" />.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to3}f(x)=4" /> is false. The left limit is <InlineMath math="4" /> and the right limit is <InlineMath math="5" />, so the limit does not exist. The value <InlineMath math="f(3)=4" /> does not help, because a limit ignores the value at the point.
3. The statement that <InlineMath math="f" /> is continuous at <InlineMath math="x=1" /> is true: the limit exists and equals <InlineMath math="f(1)=0" />.
4. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to3^+}f(x)=4" /> is false. From the right the rule is <InlineMath math="8-x" />, so the limit is <InlineMath math="5" />.
5. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x}=-1" /> is true. For <InlineMath math="x>3" />, <InlineMath math="\frac{f(x)}{x}=\frac{8-x}{x}=\frac8x-1" />, which tends to <InlineMath math="0-1=-1" />.

So the true statements are <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to1}f(x)=0" />, the continuity of <InlineMath math="f" /> at <InlineMath math="x=1" />, and <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x}=-1" />.

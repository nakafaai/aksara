A logarithm is only defined for **positive** numbers, so the condition is <InlineMath math="x^2-5x+6>0" />. Zero is excluded, because <InlineMath math="\log0" /> is undefined.

Factor, then find the sign of the product.

<BlockMath math="x^2-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)>0" />

A product of two factors is positive when both have the same sign. Both are positive for <InlineMath math="x>3" />, and both are negative for <InlineMath math="x<2" />. Between <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> the signs differ, for example at <InlineMath math="x=2.5" /> the value is <InlineMath math="(0.5)(-0.5)<0" />.

So the natural domain of <InlineMath math="f" /> is <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x<2\text{ or }x>3\}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid 2<x<3\}" /> is where the quadratic is negative.
- The set with <InlineMath math="\le" /> and <InlineMath math="\ge" /> includes <InlineMath math="x=2" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" />, where the quadratic is zero.
- <InlineMath math="\{x\mid x>3\}" /> forgets the interval <InlineMath math="x<2" />, which also makes the quadratic positive.
- All real numbers cannot work, because the quadratic is negative between <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />.

The columns of a transformation matrix are **the images of the two unit vectors**. The first column, <InlineMath math="(0,1)" />, is the image of <InlineMath math="(1,0)" />. The second column, <InlineMath math="(-1,0)" />, is the image of <InlineMath math="(0,1)" />.

The point <InlineMath math="(1,0)" /> on the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis moves to the positive <InlineMath math="y" />-axis, and <InlineMath math="(0,1)" /> moves to the negative <InlineMath math="x" />-axis. Both turn a quarter turn anticlockwise. Lengths and angles are kept, because the rule is <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)" /> and <InlineMath math="(-y)^2+x^2=x^2+y^2" />.

Example: <InlineMath math="(3,1)" /> maps to <InlineMath math="(-1,3)" />, which lies in quadrant II, one quadrant anticlockwise from quadrant I.

So the transformation is a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> anticlockwise rotation about <InlineMath math="O" />.

- Reflection in <InlineMath math="y=x" /> has matrix <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />, with no minus sign.
- A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise rotation has matrix <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />, with the minus sign in the second row.
- Reflection in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis has matrix <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />.
- A <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation has matrix <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />.

# Given

We are given a **linear transformation rule** on the coordinate plane mapping each point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" />.

# Asked

The **parameter value** or image point produced by the transformation.

# Solution

> Match the transformation rule with the input and image coordinates so each unknown falls out immediately.

The columns of a transformation matrix are **the images of the two unit vectors**. The first column, <InlineMath math="(0,1)" />, is the image of <InlineMath math="(1,0)" />. The second column, <InlineMath math="(-1,0)" />, is the image of <InlineMath math="(0,1)" />.

The point <InlineMath math="(1,0)" /> on the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis moves to the positive <InlineMath math="y" />-axis, and <InlineMath math="(0,1)" /> moves to the negative <InlineMath math="x" />-axis. Both turn a quarter turn anticlockwise. Lengths and angles are kept, because the rule is <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)" /> and the equation below.

<BlockMath math="(-y)^2+x^2=x^2+y^2" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- Reflection in <InlineMath math="y=x" /> has matrix the matrix below, with no minus sign.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />

- A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise rotation has matrix the matrix below, with the minus sign in the second row.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />

- Reflection in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis has matrix the matrix below.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

- A <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation has matrix the matrix below.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

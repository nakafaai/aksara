An exponential model <InlineMath math="N(t)=N_0\cdot2^{t/k}" /> has two meanings you can read off directly: <InlineMath math="N_0" /> is the starting value, and <InlineMath math="k" /> is **the time it takes to double**. Here <InlineMath math="N_0=500" /> and <InlineMath math="k=3" />.

1. The statement about the start is false. At <InlineMath math="t=0" />, <InlineMath math="N(0)=500\cdot2^0=500" /> users.
2. The statement about doubling every <InlineMath math="3" /> months is true, because <InlineMath math="N(t+3)=500\cdot2^{(t+3)/3}=2\cdot500\cdot2^{t/3}=2N(t)" />.
3. The statement <InlineMath math="N(6)=1{,}500" /> is false. Six months contain two doublings: <InlineMath math="N(6)=500\cdot2^2=2{,}000" />. The value <InlineMath math="1{,}500" /> treats the growth as adding a fixed amount, but this model multiplies.
4. The statement about <InlineMath math="t=12" /> is true: <InlineMath math="N(12)=500\cdot2^4=500\cdot16=8{,}000" />.

So the statements about doubling every <InlineMath math="3" /> months and about <InlineMath math="t=12" /> are true, and the statements about the start and <InlineMath math="N(6)" /> are false.

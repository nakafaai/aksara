All three vectors have the same length, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{1+1+0}=\sqrt2" />, and every pair has dot product <InlineMath math="1" />. **This symmetry** makes every judgement quick.

<BlockMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=\vec{v}\cdot\vec{w}=\vec{w}\cdot\vec{u}=1,\qquad |\vec{u}|=|\vec{v}|=|\vec{w}|=\sqrt2" />

1. The statement that <InlineMath math="\vec{u}" /> is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" /> is false, because their dot product is <InlineMath math="1\ne0" />.
2. The statement about the <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> angle is true: <InlineMath math="\cos\theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2}=\frac12" />, so <InlineMath math="\theta=60^\circ" />.
3. The statement <InlineMath math="\vec{u}+\vec{v}=\vec{w}" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="\vec{u}+\vec{v}=(1,2,1)" />.
4. The statement that <InlineMath math="UVW" /> is equilateral is true. Its sides are the lengths of the differences of the position vectors.

<BlockMath math="|\vec{u}-\vec{v}|=|(1,0,-1)|=\sqrt2,\qquad |\vec{v}-\vec{w}|=|(-1,1,0)|=\sqrt2,\qquad |\vec{w}-\vec{u}|=|(0,-1,1)|=\sqrt2" />

5. The statement about <InlineMath math="\vec{s}=\vec{u}+\vec{v}+\vec{w}=(2,2,2)" /> is true. Its dot product with each vector is the same, <InlineMath math="\vec{s}\cdot\vec{u}=\vec{s}\cdot\vec{v}=\vec{s}\cdot\vec{w}=4" />, and the three vectors have equal lengths, so the cosines of the angles agree: <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{2\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt2}" />.

So the true statements are the <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> angle, the equilateral triangle <InlineMath math="UVW" />, and the equal angles of <InlineMath math="\vec{u}+\vec{v}+\vec{w}" />.

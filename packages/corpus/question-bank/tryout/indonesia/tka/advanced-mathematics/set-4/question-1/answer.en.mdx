Multiply the two matrices on the left, then match the result with the right side **entry by entry**. Each entry of a product is a row of the first matrix times a column of the second.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} a & 2 \\ 1 & b \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} a+4 & 3a-2 \\ 1+2b & 3-b \end{pmatrix}" />

Solve the entries with only one unknown first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+4&=5\ \Rightarrow\ a=1 \\
3-b&=4\ \Rightarrow\ b=-1
\end{aligned}" />

The other two entries now give <InlineMath math="c" /> and <InlineMath math="d" />: <InlineMath math="c=3a-2=1" /> and <InlineMath math="d=1+2b=-1" />.

So <InlineMath math="c-d=1-(-1)=2" />.

- <InlineMath math="-2" /> is <InlineMath math="d-c" />, the subtraction in the wrong order.
- <InlineMath math="-1" /> multiplies in the reverse order, right matrix times left matrix, which gives <InlineMath math="a=2" />, <InlineMath math="b=0" />, <InlineMath math="c=2" /> and <InlineMath math="d=3" />.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> is <InlineMath math="a+b+c+d" />, not <InlineMath math="c-d" />.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> is the value of <InlineMath math="d" /> from the product in the reverse order.

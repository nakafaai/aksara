# Given

We are given a **matrix equation** containing real unknowns <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, <InlineMath math="c" />, and <InlineMath math="d" />.

# Asked

The **requested value** found by matching corresponding matrix entries.

# Solution

> Two matrices are equal only when every matching slot holds the exact same number.

Multiply the two matrices on the left, then match the result with the right side **entry by entry**. Each entry of a product is a row of the first matrix times a column of the second.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} a & 2 \\ 1 & b \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} a+4 & 3a-2 \\ 1+2b & 3-b \end{pmatrix}" />

The diagram below shows the order in which to use the four entries. **Entries with only one unknown come first**, and their results fill in the other two entries.

<Mermaid
  title={<>The order for solving the entries</>}
  description={<>The top-left entry gives <InlineMath math="a=1" />, and the bottom-right entry gives <InlineMath math="b=-1" />. Those values then give <InlineMath math="c=1" /> from the top-right entry and <InlineMath math="d=-1" /> from the bottom-left entry, so <InlineMath math="c-d=2" />.</>}
  chart={`flowchart TD
  E11["entry (1,1): a + 4 = 5"] --> A["a = 1"]
  E22["entry (2,2): 3 - b = 4"] --> B["b = -1"]
  A --> C["entry (1,2): c = 3a - 2 = 1"]
  B --> D["entry (2,1): d = 1 + 2b = -1"]
  C --> R["c - d = 2"]
  D --> R`}
/>

Solve the entries with only one unknown first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+4&=5\ \Rightarrow\ a=1 \\
3-b&=4\ \Rightarrow\ b=-1
\end{aligned}" />

The other two entries now give <InlineMath math="c" /> and <InlineMath math="d" />: <InlineMath math="c=1" /> and <InlineMath math="d=-1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
c=3a-2=1 \\
d=1+2b=-1
\end{gathered}" />

So <InlineMath math="2" />.

<BlockMath math="c-d=1-(-1)=2" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="-2" /> is <InlineMath math="d-c" />, the subtraction in the wrong order.
- <InlineMath math="-1" /> multiplies in the reverse order, right matrix times left matrix, which gives <InlineMath math="a=2" />, <InlineMath math="b=0" />, <InlineMath math="c=2" /> and <InlineMath math="d=3" />.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> is the expression below, not <InlineMath math="c-d" />.

  <BlockMath math="a+b+c+d" />

- <InlineMath math="3" /> is the value of <InlineMath math="d" /> from the product in the reverse order.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="2" />**.

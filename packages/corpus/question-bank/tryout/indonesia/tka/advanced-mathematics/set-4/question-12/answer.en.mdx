# Given

We are given two **space vectors** <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> with one unknown component <InlineMath math="m" />.

# Asked

The **parameter value** <InlineMath math="m" /> satisfying the vector condition.

# Solution

> Write the geometric condition using the dot product so the unknown parameter appears in one clean equation.

The scalar projection of <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" /> onto <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> is the signed length of the shadow of <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" /> along <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" />. Its formula **divides the dot product by the length** of <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" />, not by its square.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}&=2\cdot1+(-1)\cdot2+m\cdot2=2m \\
|\vec{b}|&=\sqrt{1+4+4}=3 \\
\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}&=\frac{2m}{3}=2\ \Rightarrow\ m=3
\end{aligned}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="\vec a" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec b" /> for <InlineMath math="m=3" /> in the plane that holds both. **The scalar projection is the length of the shadow of <InlineMath math="\vec a" /> on the line of <InlineMath math="\vec b" />**, and here that length is <InlineMath math="2" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="9" /> divides by <InlineMath math="|\vec{b}|^2=9" />, which belongs to the vector projection, not the scalar one.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> slips a sign in <InlineMath math="(-1)\cdot2" />, making the dot product <InlineMath math="4+2m" />.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt5" /> uses <InlineMath math="|\vec{b}|" /> and forgets the <InlineMath math="\vec{k}" />-component.

  <BlockMath math="|\vec{b}|=\sqrt{1+4}" />

- <InlineMath math="-3" /> makes the projection <InlineMath math="-2" />, pointing against <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="3" />**.

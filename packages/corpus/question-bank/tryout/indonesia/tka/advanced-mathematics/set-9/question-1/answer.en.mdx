# Given

Two **secret numbers** <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" /> are encoded into <InlineMath math="(u,v)" /> via a matrix rule.

# Asked

The **original numbers** <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" /> recovered by decoding.

# Solution

> Decoding simply multiplies the coded pair by the inverse of the encoding matrix.

Let the matrix below. To decode, **multiply the code on the left by the inverse of <InlineMath math="K" />**, since <InlineMath math="K^{-1}K=I" />.

<BlockMath math="K=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\det K=2\cdot1-1\cdot1=1,\qquad K^{-1}=\frac{1}{1}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \\
\begin{pmatrix} p \\ q \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 17 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 17-10 \\ -17+20 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}
\end{gathered}" />
</MathContainer>

Each row of the code is a line in the <InlineMath math="(p,q)" /> plane. **The secret pair is the only point on both lines**, which is where they cross.

So <InlineMath math="(p,q)=(7,3)" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="(3,7)" /> swaps <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" />. The code for that pair is <InlineMath math="(13,10)" />.
- <InlineMath math="(44,27)" /> multiplies the code by <InlineMath math="K" /> again instead of by <InlineMath math="K^{-1}" />.
- <InlineMath math="(27,37)" /> swaps the diagonal entries without changing the signs of the others, using the matrix below.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}" />

- <InlineMath math="(-7,-3)" /> flips the sign of the whole inverse.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="(7,3)" />**.

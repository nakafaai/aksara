The area of a triangle can be found from two side vectors that start at the same vertex, without finding a height first. Take <InlineMath math="\vec{u}=\overrightarrow{AB}=(2,1,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{v}=\overrightarrow{AC}=(0,2,2)" />. Since <InlineMath math="\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta" />, **the area can be written with lengths and the dot product**.

<BlockMath math="L=\frac12\left|\vec{u}\right|\left|\vec{v}\right|\sin\theta=\frac12\sqrt{\left|\vec{u}\right|^2\left|\vec{v}\right|^2-\left(\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}\right)^2}" />

<BlockMath math="\left|\vec{u}\right|^2=5,\qquad \left|\vec{v}\right|^2=8,\qquad \vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=0+2+0=2" />

<BlockMath math="L=\frac12\sqrt{5\cdot8-2^2}=\frac12\sqrt{36}=3" />

Check with the cross product: <InlineMath math="\vec{u}\times\vec{v}=(2,-4,4)" />, whose length is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4+16+16}=6" />, and half of that is <InlineMath math="3" />.

So the shade sail covers <InlineMath math="3" /> square metres.

- <InlineMath math="2\sqrt{10}" /> assumes a right angle and also forgets the factor <InlineMath math="\frac12" />.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> forgets the square root: it is the square of the area.

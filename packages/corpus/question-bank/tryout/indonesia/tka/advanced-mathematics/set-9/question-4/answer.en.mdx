Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the number of hand-drawn batik pieces and <InlineMath math="y" /> the number of stamped batik pieces per week. The dyeing time and the fabric stock each give **one constraint**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x+y&\le40\quad(\text{dyeing}) \\
x+y&\le26\quad(\text{fabric}) \\
x\ge0,\quad y&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The profit, in thousands of rupiah, is <InlineMath math="P=100x+80y" />. The corner where both constraints are used up comes from subtracting the two equations.

<BlockMath math="(2x+y)-(x+y)=40-26\ \Rightarrow\ x=14,\quad y=12" />

The other corners are <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(20,0)" /> on the dyeing line and <InlineMath math="(0,26)" /> on the fabric line.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(20,0)&=2{,}000 \\
P(14,12)&=1{,}400+960=2{,}360 \\
P(0,26)&=2{,}080
\end{aligned}" />

So the maximum profit is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}360{,}000.00" />, with <InlineMath math="14" /> hand-drawn and <InlineMath math="12" /> stamped pieces.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}000{,}000.00" /> is the profit at <InlineMath math="(20,0)" />, hand-drawn batik only.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}080{,}000.00" /> is the profit at <InlineMath math="(0,26)" />, stamped batik only.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}600{,}000.00" /> assumes all <InlineMath math="26" /> pieces of fabric can become hand-drawn batik. That plan needs <InlineMath math="52" /> hours of dyeing.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}3{,}200{,}000.00" /> spends all <InlineMath math="40" /> hours on <InlineMath math="40" /> stamped pieces and forgets that the fabric covers only <InlineMath math="26" /> pieces.

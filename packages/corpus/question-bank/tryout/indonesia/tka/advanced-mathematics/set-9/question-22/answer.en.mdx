# Given

We are given a **trigonometric limit** in radians whose numerator sums two terms approaching zero.

# Asked

The **trigonometric limit value** as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches zero.

# Solution

> Split the fraction into two simpler terms so each piece uses a standard small-angle limit cleanly.

Direct substitution gives <InlineMath math="\frac00" />. The numerator is a sum, so **split the fraction into two parts** and take each limit.

<BlockMath math="\frac{x^2+\sin^23x}{x\tan2x}=\frac{x}{\tan2x}+\frac{\sin3x}{x}\cdot\frac{\sin3x}{\tan2x}" />

Use the value <InlineMath math="\frac1a" />, the value <InlineMath math="a" /> and the expression below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x}{\tan ax}=\frac1a \\
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin ax}{x}=a \\
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin ax}{\tan bx}=\frac ab \\
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x^2+\sin^23x}{x\tan2x}=\frac12+3\cdot\frac32=\frac12+\frac92=5
\end{gathered}" />

Draw the two pieces and their sum for small <InlineMath math="x" />. As <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="0" />, the first piece heads to <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />, the second to <InlineMath math="\tfrac92" />, and **their sum to <InlineMath math="5" />**.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> only counts the part the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{x^2}{x\tan2x}" />

- <InlineMath math="\frac92" /> only counts the part the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{\sin^23x}{x\tan2x}" />

- <InlineMath math="10" /> treats <InlineMath math="\tan2x" /> like <InlineMath math="x" />, losing the factor <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> takes <InlineMath math="\sin^23x" /> to behave like <InlineMath math="3x^2" />, but it behaves like <InlineMath math="(3x)^2=9x^2" />.

# Given

Polynomial <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> has known **remainders** modulo a squared linear factor and another linear factor.

# Asked

The **remainder** when <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by the combined cubic divisor.

# Solution

> Write the remainder as a multiple of the squared divisor plus its known remainder, then plug in the other root to find that one constant.

The divisor has degree three, so the remainder <InlineMath math="R(x)" /> has degree at most two. The key: **the remainder of <InlineMath math="R(x)" /> on division by <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2" /> must also be <InlineMath math="3x-1" />**, because <InlineMath math="P(x)-R(x)" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2(x+2)" />, hence also by <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2" />. So <InlineMath math="R(x)" /> can be written as follows.

<BlockMath math="R(x)=a(x-1)^2+3x-1" />

One condition is left, the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x+2" />: <InlineMath math="2" />.

<BlockMath math="R(-2)=P(-2)=2" />

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
a(-3)^2+3(-2)-1=2\ \Rightarrow\ 9a-7=2\ \Rightarrow\ a=1 \\
R(x)=(x-1)^2+3x-1=x^2-2x+1+3x-1=x^2+x
\end{gathered}" />
</MathContainer>

Draw the remainder <InlineMath math="R(x)=x^2+x" /> with the line <InlineMath math="y=3x-1" />. **The parabola touches the line at <InlineMath math="x=1" />**, because their difference is <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2" />, and it also passes through <InlineMath math="(-2,\ 2)" />.

Check: <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2" />, so the remainder of <InlineMath math="R(x)" /> on division by <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2" /> is indeed <InlineMath math="3x-1" />. Also <InlineMath math="2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
x^2+x-(3x-1)=x^2-2x+1=(x-1)^2 \\
R(-2)=4-2=2
\end{gathered}" />

So the remainder is <InlineMath math="x^2+x" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="3x-1" /> only meets the first condition. Its value at <InlineMath math="x=-2" /> is <InlineMath math="-7" />, not <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> only uses <InlineMath math="P(1)=2" /> and <InlineMath math="P(-2)=2" /> and then assumes a constant remainder.
- <InlineMath math="x^2+5x" /> expands <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2" /> as <InlineMath math="x^2+2x+1" />.
- <InlineMath math="-x^2+5x-2" /> takes <InlineMath math="(-3)^2" /> to be <InlineMath math="-9" />, which gives <InlineMath math="a=-1" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="x^2+x" />**.

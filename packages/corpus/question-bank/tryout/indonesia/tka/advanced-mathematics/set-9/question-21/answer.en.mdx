# Given

We are given a **ratio of exponential terms** as <InlineMath math="x" /> grows without bound.

# Asked

The **limit at infinity** of the exponential fraction.

# Solution

> Divide top and bottom by the dominant exponential term so all smaller constants shrink straight to zero.

As <InlineMath math="x\to\infty" />, <InlineMath math="2^x" /> grows without bound. Just as with rational functions, **divide top and bottom by the fastest-growing term**, <InlineMath math="2^x" />.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3\cdot2^x+5}{2^x-1}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3+\frac{5}{2^x}}{1-\frac{1}{2^x}}=\frac{3+0}{1-0}=3" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> turns the ratio of the <InlineMath math="2^x" /> coefficients upside down.
- <InlineMath math="-5" /> compares the constants <InlineMath math="5" /> and <InlineMath math="-1" />, which are both outgrown by <InlineMath math="2^x" />.
- <InlineMath math="\infty" /> only looks at the numerator growing, but the denominator grows at the same rate.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> assumes the denominator grows faster than the numerator.

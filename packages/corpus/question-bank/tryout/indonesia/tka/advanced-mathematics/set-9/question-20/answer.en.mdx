# Given

Point <InlineMath math="A(4,1)" /> is reflected across the **shifted diagonal line** <InlineMath math="y=x+1" />.

# Asked

The **image coordinates** of point <InlineMath math="A" /> after reflection.

# Solution

> Slide the mirror down by one to the main diagonal, swap the coordinates, then slide right back up by one.

The rule <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)" /> only works for the mirror <InlineMath math="y=x" />, which passes through the origin. The mirror <InlineMath math="y=x+1" /> is <InlineMath math="y=x" /> shifted up by <InlineMath math="1" />, so **shift everything down, reflect, then shift back up**.

<BlockMath math="A(4,1)\ \xrightarrow{\ \text{shift }(0,-1)\ }\ (4,0)\ \xrightarrow{\ y=x\ }\ (0,4)\ \xrightarrow{\ \text{shift }(0,1)\ }\ (0,5)" />

Check: the midpoint of <InlineMath math="A(4,1)" /> and <InlineMath math="A'(0,5)" /> is <InlineMath math="(2,3)" />, which lies on <InlineMath math="y=x+1" />. The vector the equation below is perpendicular to the mirror's direction <InlineMath math="(1,1)" />, since <InlineMath math="-4+4=0" />.

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{AA'}=(-4,4)" />

So the image is <InlineMath math="(0,5)" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="(1,4)" /> reflects in <InlineMath math="y=x" /> and ignores the shift of the mirror.
- <InlineMath math="(0,4)" /> shifts down and reflects, but forgets to shift back.
- <InlineMath math="(2,3)" /> shifts the wrong way, which actually reflects in <InlineMath math="y=x-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="(0,-3)" /> uses the rule for the mirror <InlineMath math="y=-x" />, so it reflects in <InlineMath math="y=-x+1" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="(0,5)" />**.

The identity flips the sign each time the input increases by <InlineMath math="2" />. Apply it twice and **the two minus signs cancel**.

<BlockMath math="f(x+4)=-f(x+2)=-\bigl(-f(x)\bigr)=f(x)" />

So <InlineMath math="f" /> repeats every <InlineMath math="4" />, while a step of <InlineMath math="2" /> flips the sign.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f(7)&=f(3)=-f(1)=-3 \\
f(10)&=f(6)=f(2)=-1
\end{aligned}" />

Check step by step: <InlineMath math="f(3)=-3" />, <InlineMath math="f(5)=3" />, <InlineMath math="f(7)=-3" />, and <InlineMath math="f(4)=1" />, <InlineMath math="f(6)=-1" />, <InlineMath math="f(8)=1" />, <InlineMath math="f(10)=-1" />.

So <InlineMath math="f(7)+f(10)=-3+(-1)=-4" />.

- <InlineMath math="2" /> assumes <InlineMath math="f" /> repeats every <InlineMath math="2" />, giving <InlineMath math="f(7)=3" /> and <InlineMath math="f(10)=-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> flips the sign of both values: <InlineMath math="f(7)=3" /> and <InlineMath math="f(10)=1" />.
- <InlineMath math="-2" /> gets <InlineMath math="f(7)" /> right but miscounts the steps from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="10" />. There are four steps, an even number, so the sign stays.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> assumes the sign changes make any two values of the function cancel.

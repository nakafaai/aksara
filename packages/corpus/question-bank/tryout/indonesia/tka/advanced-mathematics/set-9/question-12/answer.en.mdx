The projection of <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" /> onto <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> is a vector **in the direction of <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" />**, given by the formula below. Note that the denominator is <InlineMath math="\left|\vec{b}\right|^2" />, not <InlineMath math="\left|\vec{b}\right|" />.

<BlockMath math="\operatorname{proj}_{\vec{b}}\vec{a}=\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{\left|\vec{b}\right|^2}\,\vec{b}" />

<BlockMath math="\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=5\cdot2+0\cdot(-1)+4\cdot2=18,\qquad \left|\vec{b}\right|^2=4+1+4=9" />

<BlockMath math="\operatorname{proj}_{\vec{b}}\vec{a}=\frac{18}{9}(2,-1,2)=(4,-2,4)" />

Check: the remainder <InlineMath math="\vec{a}-(4,-2,4)=(1,2,0)" /> must be perpendicular to <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" />, and indeed <InlineMath math="2-2+0=0" />.

So the projection is <InlineMath math="(4,-2,4)" />, or <InlineMath math="4\vec{i}-2\vec{j}+4\vec{k}" />.

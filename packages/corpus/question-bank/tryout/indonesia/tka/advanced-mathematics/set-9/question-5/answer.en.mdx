# Given

The owner wants producing <InlineMath math="26" /> **stamped batik** sheets alone to be at least as profitable as any other plan.

# Asked

The **unit profit condition** keeping the stamped-batik corner optimal.

# Solution

> A corner stays optimal as long as the profit line slope matches or beats the active boundary slope.

Let the profit on one stamped piece be <InlineMath math="p" /> thousand rupiah, so <InlineMath math="P=100x+py" />. The feasible region does not change, with corners <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(20,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(14,12)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,26)" />. A linear function always reaches its maximum at a corner, so <InlineMath math="(0,26)" /> **only has to be compared with the other corners**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(0,26)\ge P(14,12)&\ \Leftrightarrow\ 26p\ge1{,}400+12p\ \Leftrightarrow\ p\ge100 \\
P(0,26)\ge P(20,0)&\ \Leftrightarrow\ 26p\ge2{,}000\ \Leftrightarrow\ p\ge76.9\ldots
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the profit line for <InlineMath math="p=100" />. **That line lies along the fabric edge from <InlineMath math="(0,\ 26)" /> to <InlineMath math="(14,\ 12)" />**, so <InlineMath math="(0,\ 26)" /> only joins the best points once <InlineMath math="p" /> reaches <InlineMath math="100" />.

Both conditions must hold, so <InlineMath math="p\ge100" />. At <InlineMath math="p=100" />, the plans <InlineMath math="(0,26)" /> and <InlineMath math="(14,12)" /> both give <InlineMath math="2{,}600" /> thousand rupiah. The profit line <InlineMath math="100x+100y" /> is parallel to the fabric line <InlineMath math="x+y=26" />, so every plan on that edge is equally good, and no other plan is better.

So the smallest profit per stamped piece is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000.00" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}50{,}000.00" /> makes the profit line parallel to the dyeing line, so the best plans lie on the edge from <InlineMath math="(20,0)" /> to <InlineMath math="(14,12)" />, far from <InlineMath math="(0,26)" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}77{,}000.00" /> only compares with <InlineMath math="(20,0)" />. At that value, <InlineMath math="(14,12)" /> gives <InlineMath math="2{,}324" /> thousand, more than the <InlineMath math="2{,}002" /> thousand at <InlineMath math="(0,26)" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}80{,}000.00" /> is the original profit. At that value, <InlineMath math="(14,12)" /> is still better.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}120{,}000.00" /> does make <InlineMath math="(0,26)" /> the best plan, but it is not the smallest such value.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100.000.00" />**.

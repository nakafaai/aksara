# Solution

> With zero constant term, the circle passes through the origin, making its radius equal to the center distance from the origin.

Complete the squares: the equation below, so the centre is <InlineMath math="M(3,1)" /> and <InlineMath math="r" />. For a point outside the circle, **the value of the left-hand side at that point equals the square of the tangent length**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
(x-3)^2+(y-1)^2=10 \\
r=\sqrt{10}
\end{gathered}" />

1. **True.** <InlineMath math="0" />, so <InlineMath math="P" /> is outside <InlineMath math="L" />. Alternatively, <InlineMath math="MP^2>10" />.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} 64+16-48-8=24>0 \\ MP^2=25+9=34>10 \end{gathered}" />

2. **False.** The value <InlineMath math="24" /> is the square of the tangent length. The length itself is the equation below, matching the equation below.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} \sqrt{24}=2\sqrt6 \\ \sqrt{MP^2-r^2}=\sqrt{34-10} \end{gathered}" />

3. **True.** The origin lies on <InlineMath math="L" />, since <InlineMath math="0" />. The tangent there is perpendicular to the radius the equation below, so its equation is <InlineMath math="-3x-y=0" />, that is <InlineMath math="3x+y=0" />.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} 0+0-0-0=0 \\ \overrightarrow{MO}=(-3,-1) \end{gathered}" />

4. **False.** Substituting <InlineMath math="y=0" /> gives <InlineMath math="x^2-6x=0" />, with two roots <InlineMath math="x=0" /> and <InlineMath math="x=6" />. The circle cuts the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis in two points rather than touching it.

# Answer

So the answers are True, False, True, False.

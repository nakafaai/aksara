A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise rotation is a rotation by <InlineMath math="-90^\circ" />. **Its rule is <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(y,-x)" />**, with the matrix below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} \cos(-90^\circ) & -\sin(-90^\circ) \\ \sin(-90^\circ) & \cos(-90^\circ) \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="P" /> is in quadrant IV. Turning it <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise brings it to quadrant III, where both coordinates are negative. Its distance from <InlineMath math="O" /> stays <InlineMath math="5" />.

So the image is <InlineMath math="(-4,-3)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(4,3)" /> is the result of a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> anticlockwise rotation.
- <InlineMath math="(-3,4)" /> is the result of a <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation.
- <InlineMath math="(3,4)" /> is the reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.
- <InlineMath math="(-4,3)" /> only swaps the coordinates, which is the reflection in <InlineMath math="y=x" />.

A circle tangent to two parallel lines fits exactly between them. **The distance between the lines equals the diameter**, and the centre lies midway between the lines.

<BlockMath math="2r=7-1=6\ \Rightarrow\ r=3,\qquad y_{\text{centre}}=\frac{1+7}{2}=4" />

The centre is also on <InlineMath math="x=2" />, so it is <InlineMath math="(2,4)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x-2)^2+(y-4)^2&=9 \\
x^2+y^2-4x-8y+20-9&=0 \\
x^2+y^2-4x-8y+11&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Check: substituting <InlineMath math="y=1" /> gives <InlineMath math="x^2-4x+4=(x-2)^2=0" />, with a single root, so the line <InlineMath math="y=1" /> is indeed tangent at <InlineMath math="(2,1)" />. The same holds for <InlineMath math="y=7" /> at <InlineMath math="(2,7)" />.

So the equation is <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2-4x-8y+11=0" />.

- <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2-4x-8y-16=0" /> uses the distance between the lines, <InlineMath math="6" />, as the radius, but it is the diameter.
- <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2+4x+8y+11=0" /> has centre <InlineMath math="(-2,-4)" />, a sign slip when expanding.
- <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2-4x-8y+17=0" /> subtracts <InlineMath math="r=3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="r^2=9" />.
- <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2-4x-8y+4=0" /> uses the centre's <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate, <InlineMath math="4" />, as the radius, as if the circle touched the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

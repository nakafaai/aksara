As <InlineMath math="n\to\infty" />, the factor <InlineMath math="\frac n2" /> grows while <InlineMath math="\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}" /> shrinks towards <InlineMath math="0" />. To see what their product does, **let <InlineMath math="t=\frac{2\pi}{n}" />**, so <InlineMath math="t\to0^+" /> and <InlineMath math="n=\frac{2\pi}{t}" />.

<BlockMath math="L=\frac{n}{2}\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}=\frac{\pi}{t}\sin t=\pi\cdot\frac{\sin t}{t}" />

<BlockMath math="\lim_{n\to\infty}L(n)=\pi\cdot\lim_{t\to0^+}\frac{\sin t}{t}=\pi\cdot1=\pi" />

This makes sense: with more and more sides, the polygon looks more and more like its circle, whose area is <InlineMath math="\pi\cdot1^2=\pi" />. For example, <InlineMath math="L(6)=3\sin\frac{\pi}{3}\approx2.598" /> and <InlineMath math="L(100)\approx3.1395" />, while <InlineMath math="\pi\approx3.1416" />.

So <InlineMath math="L(n)" /> approaches <InlineMath math="\pi" />.

# Given

We are given a **circle equation** whose squared terms have leading coefficient <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Asked

The **radius of the circle** after normalizing the leading coefficient.

# Solution

> Divide the whole equation by two first so the squared terms have coefficient one before completing the square.

The centre the expression below and radius <InlineMath math="r" /> apply to the form the equation below, where the coefficients of <InlineMath math="x^2" /> and <InlineMath math="y^2" /> are <InlineMath math="1" />. So **divide the whole equation by <InlineMath math="2" /> first**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\left(-\frac A2,-\frac B2\right) \\
r=\sqrt{\frac{A^2}{4}+\frac{B^2}{4}-C} \\
x^2+y^2+Ax+By+C=0 \\
x^2+y^2-4x+6y-3=0
\end{gathered}" />

Now <InlineMath math="A=-4" />, <InlineMath math="B=6" /> and <InlineMath math="C=-3" />, so the centre is <InlineMath math="(2,-3)" />.

<BlockMath math="r=\sqrt{4+9-(-3)}=\sqrt{16}=4" />

Draw the circle after dividing its equation by <InlineMath math="2" />. **Dividing both sides by <InlineMath math="2" /> doesn't change the circle**, so the centre and radius are read from the divided form.

Check: the equation below expands to the equation below, and multiplying by <InlineMath math="2" /> gives back the original equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
(x-2)^2+(y+3)^2=16 \\
x^2+y^2-4x+6y-3=0
\end{gathered}" />

So the radius is <InlineMath math="4" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{58}" /> uses the coefficients <InlineMath math="-8" />, <InlineMath math="12" /> and <InlineMath math="-6" /> without dividing by <InlineMath math="2" />.

- <InlineMath math="16" /> is <InlineMath math="r^2" />, before taking the square root.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> only uses the equation below and forgets the other two terms.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="4" />**.

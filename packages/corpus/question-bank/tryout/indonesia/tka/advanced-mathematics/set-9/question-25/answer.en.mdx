# Given

Two functions <InlineMath math="f" /> and <InlineMath math="g" /> have **known limit values** near <InlineMath math="x=2" />.

# Asked

The **truth value of each statement** about combined limits of both functions.

# Solution

> Limit laws work smoothly for sums and products, while division always requires checking that the denominator limit is nonzero.

The limit laws for sums, differences, products and powers apply whenever the limit of each part exists. For a quotient, **the limit of the denominator must not be zero**.

Draw the example <InlineMath math="f(x)=4" /> and <InlineMath math="g(x)=x-2" /> with their quotient. **As <InlineMath math="g" /> approaches <InlineMath math="0" />, the quotient <InlineMath math="\tfrac fg" /> shoots up to the right of <InlineMath math="2" /> and down to the left of <InlineMath math="2" />**, so it doesn't head to <InlineMath math="0" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- **True.** <InlineMath math="4" />.

  <BlockMath math="\lim\bigl(f+g\bigr)=4+0=4" />

- **True.** The value <InlineMath math="0" />.

  <BlockMath math="\lim f\,g=4\cdot0=0" />

- **True.** <InlineMath math="16" />.

  <BlockMath math="\lim\bigl(f^2-g\bigr)=4^2-0=16" />

- **False.** The denominator tends to <InlineMath math="0" /> while the numerator tends to <InlineMath math="4" />, so the quotient law does not apply and the size of the expression below grows without bound. For example, <InlineMath math="f(x)=4" /> and <InlineMath math="g(x)=x-2" /> give the expression below, which does not tend to <InlineMath math="0" />.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} \frac fg \\ \frac{4}{x-2} \end{gathered}" />

- **False.** A limit only describes the values of <InlineMath math="f" /> near <InlineMath math="2" />, not at <InlineMath math="2" /> itself. For example, <InlineMath math="f(x)=4" /> for <InlineMath math="x\neq2" /> with <InlineMath math="f(2)=0" /> still has limit <InlineMath math="4" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the correct choices are **statements <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, and <InlineMath math="3" />**.

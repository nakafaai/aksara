The inverse of a product is the product of the inverses **in reverse order**: <InlineMath math="(AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}" />. So you do not need <InlineMath math="A" /> itself, only <InlineMath math="B^{-1}" />.

<BlockMath math="\det B=1\cdot1-0\cdot2=1,\qquad B^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

<BlockMath math="B^{-1}A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -4+1 & -2+1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="A=\left(A^{-1}\right)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}" />, so <InlineMath math="AB=\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}" />. Multiplying <InlineMath math="AB" /> by the matrix above gives <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -2+3 & -1+1 \\ 6-6 & 3-2 \end{pmatrix}=I" />.

So <InlineMath math="(AB)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" /> is <InlineMath math="A^{-1}B^{-1}" />, without reversing the order.
- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" /> is <InlineMath math="A^{-1}B" />: it forgets to invert <InlineMath math="B" /> and does not reverse the order.
- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}" /> is <InlineMath math="AB" /> itself, not yet inverted.
- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}" /> is <InlineMath math="BA^{-1}" />, using <InlineMath math="B" /> in place of <InlineMath math="B^{-1}" />.

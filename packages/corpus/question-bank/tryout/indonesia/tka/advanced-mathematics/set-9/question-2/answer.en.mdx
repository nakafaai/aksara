# Given

We are given the **inverse matrix** <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> and a matrix equation linking <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />.

# Asked

The **requested matrix or determinant** derived from <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />.

# Solution

> Since the inverse is already handed to us, we can apply it straight away without inverting anything first.

The inverse of a product is the product of the inverses **in reverse order**: <InlineMath math="(AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}" />. So you do not need <InlineMath math="A" /> itself, only <InlineMath math="B^{-1}" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\det B=1\cdot1-0\cdot2=1,\qquad B^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \\
B^{-1}A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -4+1 & -2+1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}
\end{gathered}" />
</MathContainer>

The diagram below shows why the order reverses. **The matrix <InlineMath math="AB" /> applies <InlineMath math="B" /> first, then <InlineMath math="A" />**, so to undo it, you undo <InlineMath math="A" /> first, then <InlineMath math="B" />.

<Mermaid
  title={<>Undoing <InlineMath math="AB" /> in reverse order</>}
  description={<>Solid arrows: the vector <InlineMath math="v" /> is multiplied by <InlineMath math="B" />, then by <InlineMath math="A" />, giving <InlineMath math="ABv" />. Dashed arrows: <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> undoes the last step first, then <InlineMath math="B^{-1}" /> returns to <InlineMath math="v" />. So <InlineMath math="(AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}" />.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR
  X["v"] -->|"B"| Y["Bv"]
  Y -->|"A"| Z["ABv"]
  Z -.->|"A⁻¹"| Y
  Y -.->|"B⁻¹"| X`}
/>

Check: the matrix below, so the matrix below. Multiplying <InlineMath math="AB" /> by the matrix above gives the matrix below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
A=\left(A^{-1}\right)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \\
AB=\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \\
\begin{pmatrix} -2+3 & -1+1 \\ 6-6 & 3-2 \end{pmatrix}=I
\end{gathered}" />

So the matrix below.

<BlockMath math="(AB)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- The matrix below is <InlineMath math="A^{-1}B^{-1}" />, without reversing the order.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

- The matrix below is <InlineMath math="A^{-1}B" />: it forgets to invert <InlineMath math="B" /> and does not reverse the order.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

- The matrix below is <InlineMath math="AB" /> itself, not yet inverted.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}" />

- The matrix below is <InlineMath math="BA^{-1}" />, using <InlineMath math="B" /> in place of <InlineMath math="B^{-1}" />.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}" />

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is given below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

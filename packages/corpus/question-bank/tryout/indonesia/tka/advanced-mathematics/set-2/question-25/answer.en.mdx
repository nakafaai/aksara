Factor the numerator and the denominator first, because **the common factor and the factors that remain** decide how the limit behaves at each point.

<BlockMath math="f(x)=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{(x-2)(x+1)}=\frac{x+2}{x+1},\qquad x\ne-1,\ x\ne2" />

The simplification is valid at every point of the domain, because there <InlineMath math="x-2\ne0" />.

1. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to2}f(x)=\frac43" /> is true. A limit only uses values near <InlineMath math="2" />, so <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x+2}{x+1}=\frac{4}{3}" />.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=1" /> is true. Numerator and denominator have the same degree and leading coefficient <InlineMath math="1" />, so the limit is <InlineMath math="\frac11=1" />.
3. The statement that <InlineMath math="f" /> is continuous at <InlineMath math="x=2" /> is false. The limit exists, but <InlineMath math="f(2)" /> is undefined, so the condition for continuity fails.
4. The statement that <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to-1}f(x)" /> exists is false. As <InlineMath math="x\to-1" />, the numerator <InlineMath math="x+2\to1" /> while the denominator <InlineMath math="x+1\to0" />. From the right the values go to <InlineMath math="+\infty" />, from the left to <InlineMath math="-\infty" />.
5. The statement <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to0}f(x)=2" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="f" /> is continuous at <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="f(0)=\frac{-4}{-2}=2" />.

So the true statements are <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to2}f(x)=\frac43" />, <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=1" /> and <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to0}f(x)=2" />.

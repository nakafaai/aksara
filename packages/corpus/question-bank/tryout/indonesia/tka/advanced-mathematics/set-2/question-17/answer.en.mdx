The centre is <InlineMath math="M(-1,2)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=\sqrt{10}" />.

1. The statement about the line <InlineMath math="x+3y-15=0" /> is true. A line is tangent exactly when its distance from the centre equals the radius: <InlineMath math="d=\frac{|-1+6-15|}{\sqrt{1+9}}=\frac{10}{\sqrt{10}}=\sqrt{10}=r" />.
2. The statement that <InlineMath math="(2,3)" /> lies inside <InlineMath math="L" /> is false. Its squared distance from the centre is <InlineMath math="(2+1)^2+(3-2)^2=10=r^2" />, so the point lies on the circle, not inside it.
3. The statement about the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis is false. Substituting <InlineMath math="x=0" /> gives <InlineMath math="y^2-4y-5=0" />, that is <InlineMath math="(y-5)(y+1)=0" />. The intersection points are <InlineMath math="(0,5)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,-1)" />, which are <InlineMath math="6" /> apart.
4. The statement about the circle <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2=25" /> is true. That circle has centre <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="5" />. The centres are <InlineMath math="|OM|=\sqrt{1+4}=\sqrt5" /> apart. Two circles meet in two points exactly when <InlineMath math="|R-r|<|OM|<R+r" />.

<BlockMath math="5-\sqrt{10}\approx1.84<\sqrt5\approx2.24<5+\sqrt{10}\approx8.16" />

So the statements about the tangent line and about meeting <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2=25" /> are true, and the statements about the point <InlineMath math="(2,3)" /> and about the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis are false.

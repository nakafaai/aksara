# Given

The <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> satisfies the **matrix equation** <InlineMath math="A^2 = pA + qI" /> for real numbers <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" />.

# Asked

The **product** <InlineMath math="pq" />.

# Solution

> Compute A squared first, then match corresponding entries with pA plus qI to solve for p and q immediately.

First compute <InlineMath math="A^2" />, then write <InlineMath math="pA+qI" /> as one matrix. Two matrices are equal when **every pair of matching entries is equal**, so you get four equations for the two unknown numbers.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A^2&=\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 9-2 & 3+1 \\ -6-2 & -2+1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 7 & 4 \\ -8 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \\
pA+qI&=\begin{pmatrix} 3p+q & p \\ -2p & p+q \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}" />

The diagram below shows the order in which to use the four entries. **The off-diagonal entries contain only <InlineMath math="p" />**, so start there, then the diagonal entries give and check <InlineMath math="q" />.

<Mermaid
  title={<>Four entries, two numbers</>}
  description={<>The entry in row one, column two gives <InlineMath math="p=4" />, and the entry in row two, column one checks it. The top-left entry then gives <InlineMath math="q=-5" />, and the bottom-right entry checks it. The product is <InlineMath math="pq=-20" />.</>}
  chart={`flowchart TD
  M["A² = pA + qI"] --> E12["entry (1,2): 4 = p"]
  M --> E21["entry (2,1): -8 = -2p"]
  M --> E11["entry (1,1): 7 = 3p + q"]
  M --> E22["entry (2,2): -1 = p + q"]
  E12 --> P["p = 4"]
  E21 --> P
  P --> E11
  E11 --> Q["q = -5"]
  Q --> E22
  Q --> R["pq = -20"]`}
/>

The entry in row one, column two gives <InlineMath math="p=4" /> at once. The entry in row two, column one checks it, because <InlineMath math="-2\cdot4=-8" />. The top left entry gives <InlineMath math="q" />.

<BlockMath math="3(4)+q=7\ \Rightarrow\ q=-5" />

The bottom right entry matches as well, since <InlineMath math="-1" />. All four equations hold, so <InlineMath math="p=4" /> and <InlineMath math="q=-5" /> is the right pair.

<BlockMath math="p+q=4-5=-1" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="9" /> is <InlineMath math="p-q" />, not the product.
- <InlineMath math="-1" /> is <InlineMath math="p+q" />.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> comes from taking <InlineMath math="q=5" />, for example by writing <InlineMath math="q=12-7" />.
- <InlineMath math="-5" /> is only the value of <InlineMath math="q" />.

# Answer

So <InlineMath math="pq=-20" />.

<BlockMath math="pq=4\cdot(-5)=-20" />

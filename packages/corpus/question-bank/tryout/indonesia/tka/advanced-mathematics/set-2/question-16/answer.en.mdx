A chord is a segment whose two end points lie on the circle. A tidy method uses **the distance from the centre to the line**. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord cuts the chord in half, which creates a right triangle with hypotenuse <InlineMath math="r" />.

The circle has centre <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=5" />. Write the line in general form <InlineMath math="x-y+1=0" /> and compute its distance from the centre.

<BlockMath math="d=\frac{|0-0+1|}{\sqrt{1^2+(-1)^2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}" />

Half of the chord is a leg of that right triangle.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\left(\tfrac12 t\right)^2&=r^2-d^2=25-\frac12=\frac{49}{2} \\
\tfrac12 t&=\frac{7}{\sqrt2}=\frac72\sqrt2 \\
t&=7\sqrt2
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, substitute <InlineMath math="y=x+1" /> into the circle: <InlineMath math="2x^2+2x-24=0" />, that is <InlineMath math="x^2+x-12=0" />, so <InlineMath math="x=3" /> or <InlineMath math="x=-4" />. The intersection points are <InlineMath math="(3,4)" /> and <InlineMath math="(-4,-3)" />, and their distance is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{7^2+7^2}=7\sqrt2" />.

So the chord has length <InlineMath math="7\sqrt2" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac72\sqrt2" /> is only half of the chord.
- <InlineMath math="7" /> is only the difference of the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinates, but the <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinates also differ by <InlineMath math="7" />.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> is the diameter. This chord does not pass through the centre, so it is shorter.
- <InlineMath math="14" /> adds the two coordinate differences instead of using Pythagoras.

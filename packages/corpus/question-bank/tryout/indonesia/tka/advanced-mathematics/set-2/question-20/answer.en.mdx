The matrix of <InlineMath math="T" /> is unknown, but two pairs of points are enough to find it. Put the two original points as the columns of a matrix <InlineMath math="P" /> and their images as the columns of <InlineMath math="Q" />. Each column is mapped separately, so <InlineMath math="TP=Q" />, which means **<InlineMath math="T=QP^{-1}" />, with the inverse on the right**.

<BlockMath math="P=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix},\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 11 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det P=5-6=-1" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P^{-1}&=\frac{1}{-1}\begin{pmatrix} 5 & -3 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -5 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \\
T&=\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 11 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -5 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -20+22 & 12-11 \\ -5+4 & 3-2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="T(1,2)=(2+2,\ -1+2)=(4,1)" /> and <InlineMath math="T(3,5)=(6+5,\ -3+5)=(11,2)" />. Now apply <InlineMath math="T" /> to <InlineMath math="(2,-1)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 4-1 \\ -2-1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}" />

A route without the matrix gives the same answer. Write <InlineMath math="(2,-1)=-13(1,2)+5(3,5)" />. Because <InlineMath math="T" /> is linear, the image is <InlineMath math="-13(4,1)+5(11,2)=(3,-3)" />.

So the image of <InlineMath math="(2,-1)" /> is <InlineMath math="(3,-3)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(15,-6)" /> multiplies in the reverse order, <InlineMath math="T=P^{-1}Q" />.

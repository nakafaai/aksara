# Given

**Matrix transformation** <InlineMath math="T" /> maps **<InlineMath math="(1, 2)" /> to <InlineMath math="(4, 1)" />** and **<InlineMath math="(3, 5)" /> to <InlineMath math="(11, 2)" />**.

# Asked

The **image of point** <InlineMath math="(2, -1)" /> under <InlineMath math="T" />.

# Solution

> Pack the two input points into matrix P and their outputs into Q to recover T as Q times P inverse, then multiply by the target point.

The matrix of <InlineMath math="T" /> is unknown, but two pairs of points are enough to find it. Put the two original points as the columns of a matrix <InlineMath math="P" /> and their images as the columns of <InlineMath math="Q" />. Each column is mapped separately, so <InlineMath math="TP=Q" />, which means **<InlineMath math="T=QP^{-1}" />, with the inverse on the right**.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
P=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix},\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 11 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det P=5-6=-1 \\
\begin{aligned}
P^{-1}&=\frac{1}{-1}\begin{pmatrix} 5 & -3 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -5 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \\
T&=\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 11 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -5 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -20+22 & 12-11 \\ -5+4 & 3-2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />
</MathContainer>

Check: <InlineMath math="T(1,2)=(4,1)" /> and <InlineMath math="T(3,5)=(11,2)" />. Now apply <InlineMath math="T" /> to <InlineMath math="(2,-1)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
T(1,2)=(2+2,\ -1+2)=(4,1) \\
T(3,5)=(6+5,\ -3+5)=(11,2) \\
\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 4-1 \\ -2-1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}
\end{gathered}" />

A route without the matrix gives the same answer. Write <InlineMath math="(2,-1)=-13(1,2)+5(3,5)" />. Because <InlineMath math="T" /> is linear, the image is <InlineMath math="-13(4,1)+5(11,2)=(3,-3)" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="(-3,3)" /> flips every sign, for example by taking <InlineMath math="\det P=1" />, which gives <InlineMath math="T" /> the opposite sign.
- <InlineMath math="(15,-6)" /> multiplies in the reverse order, <InlineMath math="T=P^{-1}Q" />.
- <InlineMath math="(5,1)" /> uses the transpose of <InlineMath math="T" />.
- <InlineMath math="(3,3)" /> has a sign slip in the second component, <InlineMath math="-2-1" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="(3,-3)" />**.

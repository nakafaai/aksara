Bring the equation to the standard form <InlineMath math="(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2" /> by **completing the square** in <InlineMath math="x" /> and in <InlineMath math="y" />. The standard form shows the centre <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> and the radius <InlineMath math="r" /> directly.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x^2-4x+4)+(y^2+10y+25)&=-20+4+25 \\
(x-2)^2+(y+5)^2&=9
\end{aligned}" />

The numbers <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="25" /> are added to both sides. They are the squares of half the <InlineMath math="x" />- and <InlineMath math="y" />-coefficients. Since <InlineMath math="y+5=y-(-5)" />, the centre is <InlineMath math="(2,-5)" />, and <InlineMath math="r=\sqrt9=3" />.

The shortcut gives the same result. For <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2+Ax+By+C=0" />, the centre is <InlineMath math="\left(-\frac A2,-\frac B2\right)=(2,-5)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=\sqrt{4+25-20}=3" />.

So the circle has centre <InlineMath math="(2,-5)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="3" />.

- The centre <InlineMath math="(-2,5)" /> drops the minus signs in <InlineMath math="\left(-\frac A2,-\frac B2\right)" />.
- The radius <InlineMath math="9" /> is <InlineMath math="r^2" />, before taking the square root.
- The centre <InlineMath math="(4,-10)" /> forgets to halve the coefficients.
- The radius <InlineMath math="7" /> comes from <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4+25+20}" />, a sign slip on the constant <InlineMath math="C" />.

# Given

A circle is given in general form as **the equation below**.

<BlockMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 10y + 20 = 0" />

# Asked

The **center and radius** of the circle.

# Solution

> Complete the square for x and for y separately so the center and squared radius pop right out in standard form.

Bring the equation to the standard form the equation below by **completing the square** in <InlineMath math="x" /> and in <InlineMath math="y" />. The standard form shows the centre <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> and the radius <InlineMath math="r" /> directly.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2 \\
\begin{aligned}
(x^2-4x+4)+(y^2+10y+25)&=-20+4+25 \\
(x-2)^2+(y+5)^2&=9
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Draw the circle from its standard form. **Its centre is at <InlineMath math="(2,\ -5)" />, below the <InlineMath math="X" />-axis**, and its radius is <InlineMath math="3" />.

The numbers <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="25" /> are added to both sides. They are the squares of half the <InlineMath math="x" />- and <InlineMath math="y" />-coefficients. Since the equation below, the centre is <InlineMath math="(2,-5)" />, and <InlineMath math="r=3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
y+5=y-(-5) \\
r=\sqrt9=3
\end{gathered}" />

The shortcut gives the same result. For the equation below, the centre is the equation below and <InlineMath math="r=3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
x^2+y^2+Ax+By+C=0 \\
\left(-\frac A2,-\frac B2\right)=(2,-5) \\
r=\sqrt{4+25-20}=3
\end{gathered}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- The centre <InlineMath math="(-2,5)" /> drops the minus signs in the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\left(-\frac A2,-\frac B2\right)" />

- The radius <InlineMath math="9" /> is <InlineMath math="r^2" />, before taking the square root.
- The centre <InlineMath math="(4,-10)" /> forgets to halve the coefficients.
- The radius <InlineMath math="7" /> comes from <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4+25+20}" />, a sign slip on the constant <InlineMath math="C" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the circle has **center <InlineMath math="(2,-5)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="3" />**.

# Given

**The cubic polynomial** **<InlineMath math="P(x)" />** comes with statements about its factors and roots.

<BlockMath math="P(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 11x + 6" />

# Asked

**All true statements** about the polynomial <InlineMath math="P(x)" />.

# Solution

> Test divisors of the constant term to catch one clean root, divide it out, and factor the leftover quadratic to see every root.

The quickest route is to **factor** <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> **completely**, because almost every statement can be judged from the roots. Possible integer roots divide <InlineMath math="6" />. Try <InlineMath math="x=3" />.

<BlockMath math="P(3)=2(27)-3(9)-11(3)+6=54-27-33+6=0" />

So <InlineMath math="x-3" /> is a factor. Use synthetic division to get the quotient, then factor the quadratic.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(x)&=(x-3)(2x^2+3x-2) \\
&=(x-3)(2x-1)(x+2)
\end{aligned}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="P(x)" />. **The graph crosses the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at <InlineMath math="-2" />, <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />, and <InlineMath math="3" />**, and its height at <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> is the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x+1" />.

<BlockMath math="P(x)=(x-3)(2x-1)(x+2)" />

The roots are <InlineMath math="3" />, <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> and <InlineMath math="-2" />.

1. The statement that <InlineMath math="x-3" /> is a factor of <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="P(3)=0" />.
2. The statement about the sum of the roots is true: the equation below. Vieta's formula gives the same, the equation below.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} 3+\frac12-2=\frac32 \\ -\frac{-3}{2}=\frac32 \end{gathered}" />

3. The statement about the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x+1" /> is false. The remainder is <InlineMath math="12" />, not <InlineMath math="8" />.

   <BlockMath math="P(-1)=-2-3+11+6=12" />

4. The statement that <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="2x-1" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="2x-1" /> appears as a factor, or because <InlineMath math="P\left(\frac12\right)=0" />.
5. The statement about the product of the roots is false. The product is the equation below, matching Vieta's the equation below. The value <InlineMath math="3" /> drops the minus sign in that formula.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} 3\cdot\frac12\cdot(-2)=-3 \\ -\frac{d}{a}=-\frac{6}{2} \end{gathered}" />

# Answer

So the true statements are that <InlineMath math="x-3" /> is a factor of <InlineMath math="P(x)" />, that the roots add up to <InlineMath math="\frac32" />, and that <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="2x-1" />.

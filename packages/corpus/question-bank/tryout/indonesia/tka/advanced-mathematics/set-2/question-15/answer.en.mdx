# Given

**The tangent line** to the circle at point <InlineMath math="T" /> crosses the **<InlineMath math="X" />-axis at <InlineMath math="P" />** and **<InlineMath math="Y" />-axis at <InlineMath math="Q" />** with origin <InlineMath math="O" />.

# Asked

The **area of right triangle** <InlineMath math="OPQ" />.

# Solution

> Write the tangent line at T first, read off both axis intercepts, and take half the product of those two perpendicular legs.

There are two stages: find the tangent line at <InlineMath math="T" />, then find where it meets both axes. Triangle <InlineMath math="OPQ" /> has its right angle at <InlineMath math="O" />, so its area is half the product of the two intercepts.

Complete the square to get the centre and radius.

<BlockMath math="(x-1)^2+(y+2)^2=1+4+20=25" />

The centre is <InlineMath math="M(1,-2)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=5" />. The point <InlineMath math="T" /> is on the circle, since <InlineMath math="25" />. The tangent at <InlineMath math="T" /> is **perpendicular to the radius** <InlineMath math="MT" />, so you can write it with the tangent formula for a point on the circle.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
(4-1)^2+(2+2)^2=9+16=25 \\
\begin{aligned}
(4-1)(x-1)+(2+2)(y+2)&=25 \\
3x-3+4y+8&=25 \\
3x+4y&=20
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

The <InlineMath math="x" />-intercept comes from <InlineMath math="y=0" />, and the <InlineMath math="y" />-intercept from <InlineMath math="x=0" />.

<BlockMath math="P\left(\frac{20}{3},0\right),\qquad Q(0,5),\qquad A=\frac12\cdot\frac{20}{3}\cdot5=\frac{50}{3}" />

# Answer

So the area of triangle <InlineMath math="OPQ" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{50}{3}" /> square units.

Three statements depend on the inverse of <InlineMath math="f" />, so find it first. Write <InlineMath math="y=f(x)" /> and solve for <InlineMath math="x" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="y" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y(x-1)&=x+2 \\
yx-x&=y+2 \\
x(y-1)&=y+2 \\
x&=\frac{y+2}{y-1}
\end{aligned}" />

It turns out that <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(x)=\frac{x+2}{x-1}" />, **the same rule as** <InlineMath math="f" /> **itself**. The last step only works when <InlineMath math="y\ne1" />, and that matters for the first statement.

1. The statement about the range is false. The value <InlineMath math="y=1" /> is never reached, since <InlineMath math="\frac{x+2}{x-1}=1" /> gives <InlineMath math="x+2=x-1" />, that is <InlineMath math="2=-1" />. The range is every real number except <InlineMath math="1" />.
2. The statement about <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> is true, as shown above.
3. The statement <InlineMath math="(f\circ f)(5)=5" /> is true. Because <InlineMath math="f=f^{-1}" />, applying <InlineMath math="f" /> twice returns the starting value. By direct computation, <InlineMath math="f(5)=\frac74" /> and <InlineMath math="f\left(\frac74\right)=\frac{15/4}{3/4}=5" />.
4. The statement that <InlineMath math="x=3" /> solves <InlineMath math="f(x)=4" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="f(3)=\frac52" />. The actual solution is <InlineMath math="x=f^{-1}(4)=\frac{6}{3}=2" />.

So the statements about <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> and <InlineMath math="(f\circ f)(5)" /> are true, and the statements about the range and <InlineMath math="x=3" /> are false.

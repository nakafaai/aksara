Apply the transformations **in the given order**: the reflection first, then the translation. At each step, express the old coordinates in terms of the new ones and substitute into the equation.

Reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(x,-y)" />. So replace <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="-y" />.

<BlockMath math="-y=x^2-2x\iff y=-x^2+2x" />

Translation by <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}" /> maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(x+3,y+1)" />. So replace <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="x-3" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="y-1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y-1&=-(x-3)^2+2(x-3) \\
y&=-(x^2-6x+9)+2x-6+1 \\
y&=-x^2+8x-14
\end{aligned}" />

Check with the vertex. The vertex of <InlineMath math="y=x^2-2x" /> is <InlineMath math="(1,-1)" />. The reflection sends it to <InlineMath math="(1,1)" />, and the translation to <InlineMath math="(4,2)" />. In the final equation, <InlineMath math="-16+32-14=2" />, so the vertex is indeed <InlineMath math="(4,2)" />.

So the image has equation <InlineMath math="y=-x^2+8x-14" />.

- <InlineMath math="y=x^2-8x+16" /> only translates and forgets the reflection.

# Given

Parabola **<InlineMath math="y = x^2 - 2x" />** is reflected across the <InlineMath math="X" /> **-axis** and then translated by vector **<InlineMath math="T" />**.

# Asked

The **equation of the image parabola** after both steps in order.

# Solution

> Reflect first by flipping the sign of y, then apply the shift in exact order because swapping reflection and translation changes the vertex.

Apply the transformations **in the given order**: the reflection first, then the translation. At each step, express the old coordinates in terms of the new ones and substitute into the equation.

Reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(x,-y)" />. So replace <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="-y" />.

<BlockMath math="-y=x^2-2x\iff y=-x^2+2x" />

Translation by the matrix below maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(x+3,y+1)" />. So replace <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="x-3" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="y-1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\
\begin{aligned}
y-1&=-(x-3)^2+2(x-3) \\
y&=-(x^2-6x+9)+2x-6+1 \\
y&=-x^2+8x-14
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Follow the vertex through both steps. **The reflection flips the parabola, then the translation shifts it whole, <InlineMath math="3" /> right and <InlineMath math="1" /> up**.

Check with the vertex. The vertex of <InlineMath math="y=x^2-2x" /> is <InlineMath math="(1,-1)" />. The reflection sends it to <InlineMath math="(1,1)" />, and the translation to <InlineMath math="(4,2)" />. In the final equation, <InlineMath math="2" />, so the vertex is indeed <InlineMath math="(4,2)" />.

<BlockMath math="-16+32-14=2" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="y" /> translates first and reflects second. Its vertex is <InlineMath math="(4,0)" />.

  <BlockMath math="y=-x^2+8x-16" />

- <InlineMath math="y" /> only translates and forgets the reflection.

  <BlockMath math="y=x^2-8x+16" />

- <InlineMath math="y" /> replaces <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="x+3" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="y+1" />, which shifts the parabola by the matrix below, the opposite way.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} y=-x^2-4x-4 \\ \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \end{gathered}" />

- <InlineMath math="y" /> reflects in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis instead of the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

  <BlockMath math="y=x^2-4x+4" />

# Answer

So the image has equation <InlineMath math="y" />.

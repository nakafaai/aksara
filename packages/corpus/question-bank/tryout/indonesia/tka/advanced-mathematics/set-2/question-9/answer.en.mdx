# Given

**The logarithmic function** **<InlineMath math="f(x)" />** is defined for <InlineMath math="x > -3" />.

<BlockMath math="f(x) = \log_2(x + 3) - 1" />

# Asked

The **x-intercept** where the graph of <InlineMath math="f" /> crosses the <InlineMath math="X" />-axis.

# Solution

> A graph hits the x-axis when its height equals zero, then rewrite the log as a power of two to solve for x.

The graph crosses the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis where the function value is zero. So solve <InlineMath math="f(x)=0" /> with the **definition of a logarithm**: <InlineMath math="{}^{a}\log b=c" /> means <InlineMath math="a^c=b" />. Here <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log" /> is the base-2 logarithm.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
{}^{2}\log(x+3)-1&=0 \\
{}^{2}\log(x+3)&=1 \\
x+3&=2^1 \\
x&=-1
\end{aligned}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="f" /> with the line <InlineMath math="x=-3" />. **The graph rises from near that vertical line and crosses the <InlineMath math="X" />-axis exactly once**, at <InlineMath math="x=-1" />.

The value <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> lies in the domain because <InlineMath math="-1>-3" />. Check: <InlineMath math="0" />.

<BlockMath math="f(-1)={}^{2}\log2-1=1-1=0" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="(-3,0)" /> is not on the graph. The line <InlineMath math="x=-3" /> is its vertical asymptote, and <InlineMath math="f(-3)" /> is undefined.
- <InlineMath math="(1,0)" /> comes from <InlineMath math="x+3=2^2" />, as if the logarithm had to equal <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="\left(-\frac52,0\right)" /> comes from <InlineMath math="x+3=2^{-1}" />, a sign slip when moving the <InlineMath math="-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-2,0)" /> ignores the <InlineMath math="-1" /> and solves <InlineMath math="{}^{2}\log(x+3)=0" /> instead.

# Answer

So the graph of <InlineMath math="f" /> crosses the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at <InlineMath math="(-1,0)" />.

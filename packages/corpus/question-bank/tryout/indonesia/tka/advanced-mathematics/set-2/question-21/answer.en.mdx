# Given

**The rational limit** at infinity is **the expression below**.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{6x^2 - x + 4}{3x - 2x^2}" />

# Asked

The **value of the limit** as <InlineMath math="x" /> tends to infinity.

# Solution

> When x grows huge only the highest power matters, so divide the leading x-squared coefficients while watching the minus sign in the denominator.

For very large <InlineMath math="x" />, **the highest power dominates** both the numerator and the denominator. Divide every term by <InlineMath math="x^2" />, the highest power in the denominator.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{6x^2-x+4}{3x-2x^2}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{6-\frac1x+\frac{4}{x^2}}{\frac3x-2}=\frac{6-0+0}{0-2}=-3" />

Draw the function for large <InlineMath math="x" />. **The curve rises and hugs the line <InlineMath math="y=-3" />**, the ratio of the <InlineMath math="x^2" /> coefficients.

Every term of the form <InlineMath math="\frac{c}{x}" /> or the expression below tends to <InlineMath math="0" />. Numerator and denominator have the same degree, so the limit is the ratio of the <InlineMath math="x^2" />-coefficients, the expression below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\frac{c}{x^2} \\
\frac{6}{-2}
\end{gathered}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="3" /> ignores the minus sign of <InlineMath math="-2x^2" /> in the denominator.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> compares <InlineMath math="6" /> with the coefficient of <InlineMath math="3x" />, which is written first but is not the highest power.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> treats the denominator as having the higher degree.
- <InlineMath math="\infty" /> treats the numerator as having the higher degree. Both have degree two.

# Answer

So the limit is <InlineMath math="-3" />.

For very large <InlineMath math="x" />, **the highest power dominates** both the numerator and the denominator. Divide every term by <InlineMath math="x^2" />, the highest power in the denominator.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{6x^2-x+4}{3x-2x^2}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{6-\frac1x+\frac{4}{x^2}}{\frac3x-2}=\frac{6-0+0}{0-2}=-3" />

Every term of the form <InlineMath math="\frac{c}{x}" /> or <InlineMath math="\frac{c}{x^2}" /> tends to <InlineMath math="0" />. Numerator and denominator have the same degree, so the limit is the ratio of the <InlineMath math="x^2" />-coefficients, <InlineMath math="\frac{6}{-2}" />.

So the limit is <InlineMath math="-3" />.

- <InlineMath math="3" /> ignores the minus sign of <InlineMath math="-2x^2" /> in the denominator.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> compares <InlineMath math="6" /> with the coefficient of <InlineMath math="3x" />, which is written first but is not the highest power.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> treats the denominator as having the higher degree.
- <InlineMath math="\infty" /> treats the numerator as having the higher degree. Both have degree two.

Every statement about the shape of the triangle can be judged from **its side vectors**. Compute each side vector as end point minus start point, then the lengths and the dot product.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AB}&=(2,2,1),\qquad |\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{4+4+1}=3 \\
\overrightarrow{AC}&=(-1,2,-2),\qquad |\overrightarrow{AC}|=\sqrt{1+4+4}=3 \\
\overrightarrow{BC}&=(-3,0,-3),\qquad |\overrightarrow{BC}|=\sqrt{9+0+9}=3\sqrt2 \\
\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC}&=-2+4-2=0
\end{aligned}" />

1. The statement that <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is equilateral is false. Two sides are <InlineMath math="3" />, but the third is <InlineMath math="3\sqrt2" />.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{BC}=9" /> is false. The value is <InlineMath math="2(-3)+2\cdot0+1\cdot(-3)=-9" />. It is negative because <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{BC}" /> are placed head to tail, so the angle between them is the exterior angle of the triangle at <InlineMath math="B" />, namely <InlineMath math="135^\circ" />.
3. The statement that angle <InlineMath math="BAC" /> is a right angle is true, because <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC}=0" /> and neither vector is zero.
4. The statement about the area is true. Sides <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="AC" /> are perpendicular, so they serve as base and height: <InlineMath math="A=\frac12\cdot3\cdot3=\frac92" />.
5. The statement about the parallelogram <InlineMath math="ABDC" /> is true. The vertices go <InlineMath math="A\to B\to D\to C" />, so side <InlineMath math="CD" /> must equal <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}" />: <InlineMath math="D=C+\overrightarrow{AB}=(1+2,\ 3+2,\ -2+1)=(3,5,-1)" />.

So the true statements are that angle <InlineMath math="BAC" /> is a right angle, that the area is <InlineMath math="\frac92" /> square units, and that <InlineMath math="D(3,5,-1)" /> makes <InlineMath math="ABDC" /> a parallelogram.

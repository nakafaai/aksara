Direct substitution of <InlineMath math="x=\frac{\pi}{4}" /> gives <InlineMath math="\cos\frac{\pi}{2}=0" /> on top and <InlineMath math="\frac{\sqrt2}{2}-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}=0" /> below. The form <InlineMath math="\frac00" /> signals a common factor. That factor appears once you write <InlineMath math="\cos2x" /> with **the double-angle formula as a difference of squares**.

<BlockMath math="\cos2x=\cos^2x-\sin^2x=(\cos x-\sin x)(\cos x+\sin x)" />

Near <InlineMath math="\frac{\pi}{4}" />, but not at that point itself, <InlineMath math="\cos x-\sin x\ne0" />, so you may cancel the factor.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\frac{\pi}{4}}\frac{(\cos x-\sin x)(\cos x+\sin x)}{\cos x-\sin x}=\lim_{x\to\frac{\pi}{4}}(\cos x+\sin x)=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}=\sqrt2" />

So the limit is <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" />.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> only notices that the numerator is zero. The denominator is zero too, so the form is indeterminate.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> uses <InlineMath math="\cos\frac{\pi}{4}=\sin\frac{\pi}{4}=1" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac12\sqrt2" /> keeps only one term, <InlineMath math="\cos\frac{\pi}{4}" />, of the factor <InlineMath math="\cos x+\sin x" />.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> uses <InlineMath math="\cos2x=1-2\sin^2x" /> and then treats the denominator as equal to the numerator.

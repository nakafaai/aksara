# Given

**The trigonometric function** **<InlineMath math="h(x)" />** is defined near <InlineMath math="x = \pi" />.

<BlockMath math="h(x) = \frac{1 + \cos x}{(x - \pi)\sin x}" />

# Asked

The **value of** the expression below.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\pi} h(x)" />

# Solution

> Shift the variable by letting t equal x minus pi so the limit moves to zero where standard sine and cosine limits apply directly.

At <InlineMath math="x=\pi" />, the numerator <InlineMath math="1+\cos\pi=0" /> and the denominator is <InlineMath math="0" /> too. To use the basic limits near zero, **shift the point to zero** with <InlineMath math="t=x-\pi" />. As <InlineMath math="x\to\pi" />, <InlineMath math="t\to0" />.

The angle formulas for <InlineMath math="\pi+t" /> give <InlineMath math="\cos(\pi+t)=-\cos t" /> and <InlineMath math="\sin(\pi+t)=-\sin t" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\to\pi}h(x)&=\lim_{t\to0}\frac{1-\cos t}{t\cdot(-\sin t)} \\
&=-\lim_{t\to0}\frac{2\sin^2\frac t2}{t\sin t}
\end{aligned}" />

The second line uses the equation below. Split it into basic limits.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
1-\cos t=2\sin^2\frac t2 \\
-\lim_{t\to0}\frac{2\sin^2\frac t2}{t\sin t}=-2\cdot\lim_{t\to0}\left(\frac{\sin\frac t2}{t}\right)^2\cdot\lim_{t\to0}\frac{t}{\sin t}=-2\cdot\left(\frac12\right)^2\cdot1=-\frac12
\end{gathered}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="h" /> on both sides of <InlineMath math="x=\pi" />. **Both branches rise to the same point at height <InlineMath math="-\tfrac12" />**, even though <InlineMath math="h(\pi)" /> itself isn't defined.

<BlockMath math="\frac{0.0000500}{0.01\cdot(-0.0099998)}\approx-0.50" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> takes <InlineMath math="\sin(\pi+t)=\sin t" />, which loses the minus sign.
- <InlineMath math="-1" /> uses <InlineMath math="1-\cos t\approx t^2" />, but the approximation is the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{t^2}{2}" />

- <InlineMath math="0" /> only looks at the numerator tending to zero.
- <InlineMath math="-2" /> turns the fraction <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> upside down at the end.

# Answer

So the limit is <InlineMath math="-\frac12" />.

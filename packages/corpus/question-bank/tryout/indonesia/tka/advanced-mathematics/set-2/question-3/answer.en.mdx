The four statements test **basic rules of matrix operations**: the transpose, the determinant of an inverse, the determinant of a multiple, and the fact that matrix multiplication is not commutative. First compute the two determinants you will use more than once.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\det A&=2\cdot3-(-1)\cdot4=6+4=10 \\
\det B&=0\cdot5-1\cdot(-2)=2
\end{aligned}" />

1. The statement about <InlineMath math="(A+B)^T" /> is true. Add matching entries, then turn rows into columns: <InlineMath math="A+B=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 8 \end{pmatrix}" />, so its transpose is <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 8 \end{pmatrix}" />.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="\det\left(A^{-1}\right)=10" /> is false. Since <InlineMath math="AA^{-1}=I" />, you get <InlineMath math="\det A\cdot\det\left(A^{-1}\right)=1" />, so <InlineMath math="\det\left(A^{-1}\right)=\frac{1}{10}" />.
3. The statement <InlineMath math="\det(2B)=8" /> is true. The factor <InlineMath math="2" /> sits in both rows of a <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> matrix, so <InlineMath math="\det(2B)=2^2\det B=4\cdot2=8" />.
4. The statement <InlineMath math="AB=BA" /> is false. Compute both products.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AB&=\begin{pmatrix} 2\cdot0+(-1)(-2) & 2\cdot1+(-1)\cdot5 \\ 4\cdot0+3\cdot(-2) & 4\cdot1+3\cdot5 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ -6 & 19 \end{pmatrix} \\
BA&=\begin{pmatrix} 0\cdot2+1\cdot4 & 0\cdot(-1)+1\cdot3 \\ -2\cdot2+5\cdot4 & -2\cdot(-1)+5\cdot3 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 3 \\ 16 & 17 \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}" />

The entries differ, so <InlineMath math="AB\ne BA" />. Notice that both products still have determinant <InlineMath math="20" />, because <InlineMath math="\det(AB)=\det(BA)=\det A\cdot\det B" />. Equal determinants do not make the matrices equal.

So the statements about <InlineMath math="(A+B)^T" /> and <InlineMath math="\det(2B)" /> are true, and the statements about <InlineMath math="\det\left(A^{-1}\right)" /> and <InlineMath math="AB=BA" /> are false.

# Given

Two **given functions** <InlineMath math="f(x) = 2x + 1" /> and <InlineMath math="g(x)" /> form an invertible composite <InlineMath math="f \circ g" />.

<BlockMath math="g(x) = \frac{x - 3}{x}" />

# Asked

The **value of** <InlineMath math="(f \circ g)^{-1}(5)" />.

# Solution

> Skip building the whole inverse formula and simply solve for the input x that the composite maps to five.

The value <InlineMath math="(f\circ g)^{-1}(5)" /> is **the number <InlineMath math="x" /> that <InlineMath math="f\circ g" /> sends to <InlineMath math="5" />**. So you do not need a formula for the inverse. Just solve <InlineMath math="f\big(g(x)\big)=5" /> from the outside in.

The outer layer is <InlineMath math="f" />. Find the value of <InlineMath math="g(x)" /> that makes <InlineMath math="f" /> equal <InlineMath math="5" />.

<BlockMath math="2\,g(x)+1=5\ \Rightarrow\ g(x)=2" />

The inner layer is <InlineMath math="g" />. Solve <InlineMath math="g(x)=2" /> with <InlineMath math="x\ne0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{x-3}{x}&=2 \\
x-3&=2x \\
x&=-3
\end{aligned}" />

The diagram below shows both directions. **The forward path goes through <InlineMath math="g" /> first, then <InlineMath math="f" />**, so the way back undoes <InlineMath math="f" /> first, then <InlineMath math="g" />.

<Mermaid
  title={<>Undoing the composition from the outside in</>}
  description={<>The solid arrows are the forward path: <InlineMath math="g" /> takes <InlineMath math="-3" /> to <InlineMath math="2" />, then <InlineMath math="f" /> takes <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="5" />. The dashed arrows are the way back: <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> takes <InlineMath math="5" /> to <InlineMath math="2" />, then <InlineMath math="g^{-1}" /> takes <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="-3" />.</>}
  chart={`flowchart LR
  X["x = -3"] -->|"g"| G["g(x) = 2"]
  G -->|"f"| F["f(g(x)) = 5"]
  F -.->|"f⁻¹"| G
  G -.->|"g⁻¹"| X`}
/>

Check: <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="f(2)=5" />, so <InlineMath math="(f\circ g)(-3)=5" />.

<BlockMath math="g(-3)=\frac{-6}{-3}=2" />

Another route uses the equation below: <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(5)=2" />, then <InlineMath math="g^{-1}(2)=-3" />. The order is the reverse of the original composition.

<BlockMath math="(f\circ g)^{-1}=g^{-1}\circ f^{-1}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\frac95" /> computes <InlineMath math="(f\circ g)(5)=f\left(\frac25\right)" />, the function rather than its inverse.
- <InlineMath math="-\frac78" /> uses the wrong order, the equation below.

  <BlockMath math="f^{-1}\big(g^{-1}(5)\big)=f^{-1}\left(-\frac34\right)" />

- <InlineMath math="2" /> stops at <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(5)" /> and never undoes <InlineMath math="g" />.
- <InlineMath math="-\frac34" /> is <InlineMath math="g^{-1}(5)" />, without undoing <InlineMath math="f" /> first.

# Answer

So <InlineMath math="(f\circ g)^{-1}(5)=-3" />.

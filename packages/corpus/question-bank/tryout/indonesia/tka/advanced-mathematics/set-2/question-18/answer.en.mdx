A dilation with centre <InlineMath math="M" /> and factor <InlineMath math="k" /> multiplies **the vector from the centre to the point** by <InlineMath math="k" /> and then attaches the result back at the centre.

<BlockMath math="P'=M+k\,\overrightarrow{MP}" />

Compute <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{MP}" />, multiply by <InlineMath math="-2" />, then add the centre's coordinates.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{MP}&=(4-1,\ -1-2)=(3,-3) \\
-2\,\overrightarrow{MP}&=(-6,6) \\
P'&=(1-6,\ 2+6)=(-5,8)
\end{aligned}" />

A negative factor puts the image on the other side of the centre. Indeed, <InlineMath math="P" /> lies to the lower right of <InlineMath math="M" />, while <InlineMath math="P'" /> lies to the upper left, twice as far away.

So the image of <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="(-5,8)" />.

- <InlineMath math="(7,-4)" /> uses the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> without the minus sign.
- <InlineMath math="(-8,2)" /> dilates about the origin instead of about <InlineMath math="M" />.
- <InlineMath math="(-6,6)" /> stops at <InlineMath math="-2\,\overrightarrow{MP}" /> and forgets to add the centre.
- <InlineMath math="(-7,4)" /> multiplies the coordinates of <InlineMath math="P" /> by <InlineMath math="-2" /> directly and then adds the centre.

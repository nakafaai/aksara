# Given

All feed constraints **keep the same three corner points**, while the **price per sack of feed II** can vary.

# Asked

The **highest price per sack of feed II** that still keeps <InlineMath math="6" /> sacks of feed I and <InlineMath math="8" /> sacks of feed II optimal.

# Solution

> For the middle corner to remain cheapest, its cost simply must stay less than or equal to the cost at both neighboring corners.

The limits do not change, so the corners of the feasible region are still <InlineMath math="(0,20)" />, <InlineMath math="(6,8)" /> and <InlineMath math="(18,0)" />. Only the cost function changes. Let the price of feed II be <InlineMath math="h" /> thousand rupiah per sack, so <InlineMath math="C=300x+hy" />.

The corner <InlineMath math="(6,8)" /> is where the lines <InlineMath math="2x+y=20" /> and <InlineMath math="2x+3y=36" /> cross. It stays cheapest as long as **the slope of the cost lines lies between the slopes of those two boundary lines**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m_{\text{protein}}&=-2,\qquad m_{\text{fat}}=-\frac23,\qquad m_{\text{cost}}=-\frac{300}{h} \\
-2\le-\frac{300}{h}&\le-\frac23 \iff \frac23\le\frac{300}{h}\le2
\end{aligned}" />

Because <InlineMath math="h>0" />, you can multiply by <InlineMath math="h" /> without flipping the inequality.

<BlockMath math="\frac{300}{h}\ge\frac23\iff h\le450,\qquad \frac{300}{h}\le2\iff h\ge150" />

Rotate the cost line about <InlineMath math="(6,\ 8)" />. **The cost line may turn from the slope of the protein line to the slope of the fat line**, and the gentlest of those limits is reached at <InlineMath math="h=450" />.

So <InlineMath math="(6,8)" /> is cheapest for <InlineMath math="150\le h\le450" />. Check the upper end: for <InlineMath math="h=450" />, the cost at <InlineMath math="(6,8)" /> is <InlineMath math="1{,}800+3{,}600=5{,}400" /> and the cost at <InlineMath math="(18,0)" /> is also <InlineMath math="5{,}400" />, so both are cheapest. For <InlineMath math="h=460" />, the cost at <InlineMath math="(6,8)" /> becomes <InlineMath math="5{,}480" />, more than <InlineMath math="5{,}400" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}150{,}000.00" /> is the lower end of the price interval, not the upper end.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}200{,}000.00" /> comes from writing the slope of <InlineMath math="2x+3y=36" /> as <InlineMath math="-\frac32" />, the <InlineMath math="y" />-coefficient over the <InlineMath math="x" />-coefficient.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}250{,}000.00" /> is the original price. It still keeps <InlineMath math="(6,8)" /> cheapest, but it is not the highest such price.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}300{,}000.00" /> just makes both feeds cost the same without checking slopes.

# Answer

So the highest price for one sack of feed II is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}450{,}000.00" />.

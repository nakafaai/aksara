# Given

Piecewise function **<InlineMath math="f(x)" />** with parameters <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b" /> is **continuous everywhere** and satisfies **<InlineMath math="f(3) = 8" />**.

# Asked

The **sum** <InlineMath math="a + b" />.

# Solution

> Use the right branch at three to find b first, then match both branches right at the boundary two so the graph joins without a jump.

There are two pieces of information, so you get two equations. The value <InlineMath math="f(3)" /> uses the rule for <InlineMath math="x\ge2" />, because <InlineMath math="3\ge2" />.

<BlockMath math="f(3)=3b-1=8\ \Rightarrow\ b=3" />

Each rule is continuous on its own, so the only point to check is where they join, <InlineMath math="x=2" />. There, **the left limit, the right limit and the function value must agree**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\to2^-}f(x)&=2^2+a=4+a \\
\lim_{x\to2^+}f(x)&=f(2)=2b-1=2(3)-1=5
\end{aligned}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="f" /> with <InlineMath math="a=1" /> and <InlineMath math="b=3" />. **The two pieces meet with no gap at <InlineMath math="(2,\ 5)" />**, and the right piece passes through <InlineMath math="(3,\ 8)" />.

Setting the limits equal gives <InlineMath math="4+a=5" />, so <InlineMath math="a=1" />. Check: with <InlineMath math="a=1" /> and <InlineMath math="b=3" />, both rules give <InlineMath math="5" /> at <InlineMath math="x=2" />, and <InlineMath math="f(3)=8" />.

<BlockMath math="f(3)=9-1=8" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="1" /> puts <InlineMath math="f(3)" /> into the rule <InlineMath math="x^2+a" />, which gives <InlineMath math="a=-1" /> and then <InlineMath math="b=2" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> is the difference <InlineMath math="b-a" />, not the sum.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> writes the right limit as <InlineMath math="2b+1=7" />, which gives <InlineMath math="a=3" />.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> is only the value of <InlineMath math="b" />.

# Answer

So <InlineMath math="4" />.

<BlockMath math="a+b=1+3=4" />

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the number of sacks of feed I and <InlineMath math="y" /> the number of sacks of feed II. The protein and fat needs give **two lower limits**, and a number of sacks cannot be negative. The protein limit can be divided by <InlineMath math="2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
4x+2y\ge40\iff 2x+y&\ge20 \\
2x+3y&\ge36 \\
x\ge0,\quad y&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The cost, in thousands of rupiah, is <InlineMath math="C=300x+250y" />. The feasible region is open upward, because adding sacks always still meets the needs. The minimum cost sits at one of the corners of that region.

The corner where both limits are met exactly comes from subtracting the two equations.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(2x+3y)-(2x+y)&=36-20 \\
2y&=16\ \Rightarrow\ y=8,\quad x=\frac{20-8}{2}=6
\end{aligned}" />

The two other corners lie on the axes. On the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis, the line <InlineMath math="2x+y=20" /> gives <InlineMath math="(0,20)" />, which also meets the fat limit because <InlineMath math="3\cdot20=60\ge36" />. On the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, the line <InlineMath math="2x+3y=36" /> gives <InlineMath math="(18,0)" />, which meets the protein limit because <InlineMath math="2\cdot18=36\ge20" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
C(0,20)&=250\cdot20=5{,}000 \\
C(6,8)&=1{,}800+2{,}000=3{,}800 \\
C(18,0)&=300\cdot18=5{,}400
\end{aligned}" />

So the minimum weekly cost is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}3{,}800{,}000.00" />, by buying <InlineMath math="6" /> sacks of feed I and <InlineMath math="8" /> sacks of feed II.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000{,}000.00" /> is the cost at the corner <InlineMath math="(0,20)" />, feed II only.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}400{,}000.00" /> is the cost at the corner <InlineMath math="(18,0)" />, feed I only.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}3{,}000{,}000.00" /> is the cost of <InlineMath math="10" /> sacks of feed I alone. That meets the protein need, but gives only <InlineMath math="20" /> kg of fat.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}4{,}250{,}000.00" /> is the cost of <InlineMath math="(6,10)" />. That plan meets the needs, but buys two more sacks of feed II than necessary.

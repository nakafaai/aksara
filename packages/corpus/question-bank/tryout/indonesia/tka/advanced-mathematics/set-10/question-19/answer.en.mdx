A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> anticlockwise rotation sends <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(x',y')=(-y,x)" />. For the image equation, **write <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="x'" /> and <InlineMath math="y'" />** and substitute.

<BlockMath math="x=y',\qquad y=-x'" />

<BlockMath math="-x'=(y')^2-2y'\ \Leftrightarrow\ x'=-(y')^2+2y'" />

Check with two points on the parabola: <InlineMath math="(2,0)" /> maps to <InlineMath math="(0,2)" />, and <InlineMath math="-4+4=0" />. The point <InlineMath math="(3,3)" /> maps to <InlineMath math="(-3,3)" />, and <InlineMath math="-9+6=-3" />.

So the image is <InlineMath math="x=-y^2+2y" />, a parabola opening to the left.

- <InlineMath math="x=y^2+2y" /> is the result of a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise rotation.
- <InlineMath math="x=y^2-2y" /> only swaps <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, which is the reflection in <InlineMath math="y=x" />.
- <InlineMath math="x=-y^2-2y" /> has the wrong sign on the <InlineMath math="2y" /> term.
- <InlineMath math="y=-x^2+2x" /> is the reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, not a rotation.

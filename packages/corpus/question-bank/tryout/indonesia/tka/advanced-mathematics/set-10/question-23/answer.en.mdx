# Given

A **brine tank** starts with <InlineMath math="100" /> liters containing <InlineMath math="30" /> grams of salt as a steady-concentration brine flows in.

# Asked

The **long-run salt concentration** as time grows without bound.

# Solution

> Over time the incoming brine overwhelms the initial water, driving tank concentration straight to the inflow concentration.

The question asks for the expression below. Numerator and denominator both have degree one, so **divide both by <InlineMath math="t" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\lim_{t\to\infty}K(t) \\
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{30+20t}{100+10t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\frac{30}{t}+20}{\frac{100}{t}+10}=\frac{20}{10}=2
\end{gathered}" />

Draw the salt concentration against time. **The curve rises from the starting concentration and levels off just under the line <InlineMath math="K=2" />**, the concentration of the solution flowing in.

This makes sense: over time almost all of the tank's contents come from the incoming solution, so the concentration approaches that solution's concentration, <InlineMath math="2" /> grams per litre. For example, <InlineMath math="K(10)=1.15" /> and <InlineMath math="K(1000) \approx 1.98" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
K(10)=\frac{230}{200}=1.15 \\
K(1000)=\frac{20{,}030}{10{,}100}\approx1.98
\end{gathered}" />

So the salt concentration approaches <InlineMath math="2" /> grams per litre.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="0.3" /> is the starting concentration, <InlineMath math="K(0)" />.

  <BlockMath math="K(0)=\frac{30}{100}" />

- <InlineMath math="3" /> divides the starting salt, <InlineMath math="30" /> grams, by the flow rate, <InlineMath math="10" /> litres per minute.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> is the amount of salt flowing in each minute, in grams per minute, not a concentration.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> assumes the solution keeps getting weaker, but the incoming liquid contains salt too.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="2" />**.

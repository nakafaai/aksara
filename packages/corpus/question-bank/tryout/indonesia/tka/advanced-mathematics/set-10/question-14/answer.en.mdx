# Given

A **circle** has center <InlineMath math="(-1,4)" /> and passes through point <InlineMath math="(2,0)" />.

# Asked

The **equation of the circle** from its center and one boundary point.

# Solution

> The distance from the center to the given boundary point is the radius, ready to drop straight into standard circle form.

The radius is **the distance from the centre to the point on the circle**.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
r=\sqrt{(2-(-1))^2+(0-4)^2}=\sqrt{9+16}=5 \\
\begin{aligned}
(x+1)^2+(y-4)^2&=25 \\
x^2+2x+1+y^2-8y+16-25&=0 \\
x^2+y^2+2x-8y-8&=0
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />
</MathContainer>

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- The equation below has centre <InlineMath math="(1,-4)" />, a sign slip when expanding.

  <BlockMath math="x^2+y^2-2x+8y-8=0" />

- The equation below forgets to subtract <InlineMath math="r^2=25" />.

  <BlockMath math="x^2+y^2+2x-8y+17=0" />

- The equation below uses <InlineMath math="r=7" />, adding the coordinate differences instead of using Pythagoras.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} x^2+y^2+2x-8y-32=0 \\ r=3+4=7 \end{gathered}" />

- The equation below subtracts <InlineMath math="r=5" /> instead of <InlineMath math="r^2=25" />.

  <BlockMath math="x^2+y^2+2x-8y+12=0" />

# Given

We are given a **trigonometric fraction limit** in radians with tangent in the numerator and cosine in the denominator.

# Asked

The **trigonometric limit value** near zero.

# Solution

> Rewrite the cosine difference using half-angle sine squared so matching zero factors simplify cleanly.

Direct substitution gives <InlineMath math="\frac00" />. Rewrite the denominator with the equation below, then **pair each trigonometric function with its own angle**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
1-\cos3x=2\sin^2\frac{3x}{2} \\
\frac{x\tan6x}{2\sin^2\frac{3x}{2}}=\frac12\cdot\frac{\tan6x}{x}\cdot\left(\frac{x}{\sin\frac{3x}{2}}\right)^2
\end{gathered}" />

Use the value <InlineMath math="6" /> and the value <InlineMath math="\frac23" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\tan6x}{x}=6 \\
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x}{\sin\frac{3x}{2}}=\frac23 \\
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x\tan6x}{1-\cos3x}=\frac12\cdot6\cdot\left(\frac23\right)^2=\frac12\cdot6\cdot\frac49=\frac43
\end{gathered}" />

Draw the graph of the fraction near <InlineMath math="x=0" />. **From the left and from the right, the curve drops towards height <InlineMath math="\tfrac43" />**, even though the fraction is undefined at <InlineMath math="x=0" /> itself.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\frac23" /> takes <InlineMath math="1-\cos3x" /> to behave like <InlineMath math="9x^2" />, but it behaves like the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{9x^2}{2}" />

- <InlineMath math="0" /> only looks at the numerator tending to <InlineMath math="0" />, but the denominator tends to <InlineMath math="0" /> too.

  <BlockMath math="1-\cos3x\approx\frac{9x^2}{4}" />

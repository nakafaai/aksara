# Given

We are given a **base-two logarithmic function** <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> on domain <InlineMath math="x" />.

<BlockMath math="x>-\frac{4}{3}" />

# Asked

The **inverse function formula** <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(x)" />.

# Solution

> Convert the base-two logarithm back into a power of two to free the original variable cleanly.

The value <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(2)" /> is **the number <InlineMath math="x" /> that <InlineMath math="f" /> sends to <InlineMath math="2" />**. So solve <InlineMath math="f(x)=2" />.

<BlockMath math="\log_2(3x+4)-1=2\ \Leftrightarrow\ \log_2(3x+4)=3\ \Leftrightarrow\ 3x+4=2^3=8\ \Leftrightarrow\ x=\frac43" />

Check: <InlineMath math="2" />, and <InlineMath math="\frac43>-\frac43" /> lies in the domain.

<BlockMath math="f\left(\frac43\right)=\log_28-1=3-1=2" />

So <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(2)=\frac43" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\log_210-1" /> is <InlineMath math="f(2)" />, not <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(2)" />.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> solves <InlineMath math="3x+4=8" /> but forgets to divide by <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> stops at <InlineMath math="2^3=8" />, the value of <InlineMath math="3x+4" />, not of <InlineMath math="x" />.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> forgets the <InlineMath math="-1" />, so it solves <InlineMath math="3x+4=2^2" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="\frac43" />**.

# Given

We are given a **reflection transformation** across the vertical <InlineMath math="y" />-axis in the coordinate plane.

# Asked

The **<InlineMath math="2\times2" /> transformation matrix** representing reflection across the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis.

# Solution

> Track where the two unit basis arrows land: flipping across the vertical axis reverses horizontal direction while keeping vertical fixed.

The columns of a transformation matrix are **the images of the unit vectors <InlineMath math="(1,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,1)" />**. Reflection in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis flips the sign of the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate and keeps the <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate, <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)" />.

<BlockMath math="(1,0)\mapsto(-1,0),\qquad (0,1)\mapsto(0,1)\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

Draw both unit vectors and their images in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis. **The vector <InlineMath math="(1,0)" /> flips to <InlineMath math="(-1,0)" />, while <InlineMath math="(0,1)" />, lying on the mirror, stays put**, and those two images are the columns of the matrix.

Check: the point <InlineMath math="(3,2)" /> maps to <InlineMath math="(-3,2)" />, its mirror image across the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis.

So the matrix is the matrix below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- The matrix below is the reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

- The matrix below is the reflection in the line <InlineMath math="y=x" />.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />

- The matrix below is the <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> anticlockwise rotation about <InlineMath math="O" />.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />

- The matrix below is the <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation, the reflection in the origin.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is given below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

The columns of a transformation matrix are **the images of the unit vectors <InlineMath math="(1,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,1)" />**. Reflection in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis flips the sign of the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate and keeps the <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate, <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)" />.

<BlockMath math="(1,0)\mapsto(-1,0),\qquad (0,1)\mapsto(0,1)\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

Check: the point <InlineMath math="(3,2)" /> maps to <InlineMath math="(-3,2)" />, its mirror image across the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis.

So the matrix is <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" /> is the reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.
- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" /> is the reflection in the line <InlineMath math="y=x" />.
- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" /> is the <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> anticlockwise rotation about <InlineMath math="O" />.
- <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" /> is the <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation, the reflection in the origin.

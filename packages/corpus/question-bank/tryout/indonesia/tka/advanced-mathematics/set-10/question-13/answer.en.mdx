The length of the vector is the key to almost every statement: <InlineMath math="\left|\vec{v}\right|=\sqrt{4+1+4}=3" />. The angle with an axis is decided by **the sign of the matching component**, since <InlineMath math="\vec{v}\cdot\vec{i}=-2" />.

Draw <InlineMath math="\vec v" /> in the plane that holds <InlineMath math="\vec v" /> and the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis. **The negative <InlineMath math="x" />-component tilts <InlineMath math="\vec v" /> to the left**, so its angle with <InlineMath math="\vec i" /> is obtuse.

- **True.** <InlineMath math="\left|\vec{v}\right|=\sqrt{(-2)^2+1^2+2^2}=3" />.
- **False.** A unit vector comes from dividing by the length, <InlineMath math="3" />, not by its square. The unit vector is <InlineMath math="\frac13\left(-2\vec{i}+\vec{j}+2\vec{k}\right)" />.
- **False.** <InlineMath math="4\vec{i}-2\vec{j}-4\vec{k}=-2\vec{v}" />, a negative multiple, so it points the opposite way.
- **True.** <InlineMath math="\cos\alpha=\frac{\vec{v}\cdot\vec{i}}{\left|\vec{v}\right|}=-\frac23<0" />, so the angle is obtuse.
- **True.** <InlineMath math="\vec{v}\cdot\vec{w}=\left|\vec{v}\right|\left|\vec{w}\right|\cos\theta=3\cos\theta\le3" />, because <InlineMath math="\cos\theta\le1" />. The value <InlineMath math="3" /> is reached when <InlineMath math="\vec{w}" /> points the same way as <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" />.

So the true statements are the first, fourth and fifth.

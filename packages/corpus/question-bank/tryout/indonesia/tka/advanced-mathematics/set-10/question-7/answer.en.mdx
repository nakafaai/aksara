Two polynomials are identical when **the coefficients of matching powers are equal**. Expand the left-hand side.

<BlockMath math="(x-1)(ax^2+bx+c)=ax^3+(b-a)x^2+(c-b)x-c" />

Compare with <InlineMath math="2x^3-x^2-4x+3" />: <InlineMath math="a=2" />, <InlineMath math="b-a=-1" /> so <InlineMath math="b=1" />, and <InlineMath math="-c=3" /> so <InlineMath math="c=-3" />. The <InlineMath math="x" /> coefficient fits: <InlineMath math="c-b=-4" />.

<BlockMath math="2x^3-x^2-4x+3=(x-1)(2x^2+x-3)=(x-1)(x-1)(2x+3)" />

- **False.** <InlineMath math="c=-3" />, not <InlineMath math="3" />.
- **True.** <InlineMath math="a+b+c=2+1-3=0" />. This is the value of <InlineMath math="2x^2+x-3" /> at <InlineMath math="x=1" />.
- **True.** Since <InlineMath math="2x^2+x-3" /> is also zero at <InlineMath math="x=1" />, the factor <InlineMath math="x-1" /> appears twice, so <InlineMath math="x=1" /> is a double root.
- **False.** The roots are <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="-\frac32" />, so there are only two different real roots.
- **True.** <InlineMath math="1\cdot1\cdot\left(-\frac32\right)=-\frac32" />, matching minus the constant divided by the <InlineMath math="x^3" /> coefficient, <InlineMath math="-\frac32" />.

So the true statements are the second, third and fifth.

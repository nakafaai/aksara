# Given

We are given a **trigonometric limit** in radians combining <InlineMath math="x" /> and sine in both numerator and denominator.

# Asked

The **limit value** as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches zero.

# Solution

> Divide top and bottom by the variable so every sine fraction immediately turns into its angle coefficient.

Direct substitution gives <InlineMath math="\frac00" />. Every term behaves like a multiple of <InlineMath math="x" /> near zero, so **divide top and bottom by <InlineMath math="x" />**.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x+\sin x}{x-\tan2x}=\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1+\frac{\sin x}{x}}{1-\frac{\tan2x}{x}}=\frac{1+1}{1-2}=-2" />

Draw the graph of the fraction near <InlineMath math="x=0" />. **Both branches of the curve drop towards height <InlineMath math="-2" />**, so the limit is negative.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1 \\
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\tan2x}{x}=2
\end{gathered}" />

So the limit is <InlineMath math="-2" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="2" /> reverses the denominator to <InlineMath math="\tan2x-x" />.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> only looks at the numerator tending to <InlineMath math="0" />, but the denominator tends to <InlineMath math="0" /> too.
- <InlineMath math="\frac23" /> slips the sign in the denominator, as if it were <InlineMath math="x+\tan2x" />.
- <InlineMath math="-\frac12" /> turns the fraction upside down.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="-2" />**.

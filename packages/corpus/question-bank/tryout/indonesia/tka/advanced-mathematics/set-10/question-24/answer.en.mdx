Direct substitution gives <InlineMath math="\frac00" />. Every term behaves like a multiple of <InlineMath math="x" /> near zero, so **divide top and bottom by <InlineMath math="x" />**.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x+\sin x}{x-\tan2x}=\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1+\frac{\sin x}{x}}{1-\frac{\tan2x}{x}}=\frac{1+1}{1-2}=-2" />

That line uses <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1" /> and <InlineMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\tan2x}{x}=2" />. Numerical check: at <InlineMath math="x=0.01" /> the value is about <InlineMath math="-1.9995" />.

So the limit is <InlineMath math="-2" />.

- <InlineMath math="2" /> reverses the denominator to <InlineMath math="\tan2x-x" />.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> only looks at the numerator tending to <InlineMath math="0" />, but the denominator tends to <InlineMath math="0" /> too.
- <InlineMath math="\frac23" /> slips the sign in the denominator, as if it were <InlineMath math="x+\tan2x" />.
- <InlineMath math="-\frac12" /> turns the fraction upside down.

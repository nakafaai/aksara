A right angle at <InlineMath math="C" /> means **the vectors <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{CA}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{CB}" /> are perpendicular**, so their dot product is zero.

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{CA}=(1-t,\,2,\,2),\qquad \overrightarrow{CB}=(5-t,\,2,\,-2)" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(1-t)(5-t)+2\cdot2+2\cdot(-2)&=0 \\
(1-t)(5-t)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="t=1" /> or <InlineMath math="t=5" />, and their sum is <InlineMath math="6" />.

Check: for <InlineMath math="t=1" />, <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{CA}=(0,2,2)" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{CB}=(4,2,-2)" />, with dot product <InlineMath math="0+4-4=0" />. For <InlineMath math="t=5" />, <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{CA}=(-4,2,2)" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{CB}=(0,2,-2)" />, again <InlineMath math="0+4-4=0" />.

So the sum of all values of <InlineMath math="t" /> is <InlineMath math="6" />.

- <InlineMath math="5" /> and <InlineMath math="1" /> are each only one of the two values that work.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> is the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate of the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AB" />. The point <InlineMath math="(3,0,0)" /> does not give a right angle, since the dot product is <InlineMath math="(-2)(2)=-4" />.
- <InlineMath math="-6" /> slips a sign when using the sum of the roots of <InlineMath math="t^2-6t+5=0" />.

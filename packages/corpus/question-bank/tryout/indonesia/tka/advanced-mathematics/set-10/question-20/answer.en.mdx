# Given

A dilation with **center** <InlineMath math="P" /> and **scale factor** <InlineMath math="k" /> maps two known points to their images.

# Asked

The **dilation center** <InlineMath math="P" /> or scale factor <InlineMath math="k" />.

# Solution

> Comparing the image segment vector with the original segment vector gives the scale factor immediately, before even finding the center.

A dilation multiplies every vector by <InlineMath math="k" />, including the vector between two points. So **the scale factor comes from the equation below**, without knowing the centre.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\overrightarrow{A'B'}=k\,\overrightarrow{AB} \\
\overrightarrow{AB}=(2,-1),\qquad \overrightarrow{A'B'}=(4,-2)=2(2,-1)\ \Rightarrow\ k=2
\end{gathered}" />

The centre <InlineMath math="P" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="A'-P=2(A-P)" />, so <InlineMath math="P=2A-A'" />.

<BlockMath math="P=(2-3,\ 4-4)=(-1,0)" />

<BlockMath math="B'-P=(8,2)=2(4,1)" />

So <InlineMath math="P(-1,0)" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> assumes the centre is the origin. A dilation by <InlineMath math="2" /> about <InlineMath math="O" /> sends <InlineMath math="A" /> to <InlineMath math="(2,4)" />.
- <InlineMath math="(2,3)" /> is the midpoint of <InlineMath math="AA'" />, which would be the centre for factor <InlineMath math="-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="(5,6)" /> swaps the roles of <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="A'" />, using <InlineMath math="P=2A'-A" />.
- <InlineMath math="(1,0)" /> slips a sign, using <InlineMath math="P=A'-2A" />.

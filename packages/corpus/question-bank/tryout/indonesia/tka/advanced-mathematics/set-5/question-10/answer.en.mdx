# Given

We are given a **quadratic function** <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> restricted to the right branch <InlineMath math="x\ge2" />.

# Asked

The **truth value of each statement** about its inverse formula, domain, and range.

# Solution

> Completing the square exposes the vertex, and the right-branch domain tells us to pick the plus sign on the square root.

A quadratic function only has an inverse once its domain is limited to one side of the vertex. Here the vertex is at <InlineMath math="x=2" />, so <InlineMath math="f" /> increases on <InlineMath math="x\ge2" />. **Complete the square** so that <InlineMath math="x" /> is easy to isolate.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
y&=(x-2)^2-3 \\
(x-2)^2&=y+3 \\
x-2&=\sqrt{y+3}
\end{aligned}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="f" /> on <InlineMath math="x\ge2" /> and its mirror image in <InlineMath math="y=x" />. **The vertex <InlineMath math="(2,\ -3)" /> mirrors to <InlineMath math="(-3,\ 2)" />**, so the graph of the inverse only starts at <InlineMath math="x=-3" />.

The positive root is the right one, because <InlineMath math="x\ge2" /> makes <InlineMath math="x-2\ge0" />. So the equation below, which needs <InlineMath math="x+3\ge0" />.

<BlockMath math="f^{-1}(x)=2+\sqrt{x+3}" />

1. The statement about the formula for <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> is true.
2. The statement that the domain of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> is <InlineMath math="x\ge2" /> is false. The domain of the inverse is the range of <InlineMath math="f" />. The smallest value of <InlineMath math="f" /> is <InlineMath math="f(2)=-3" />, so the domain of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> is <InlineMath math="x\ge-3" />. The number <InlineMath math="2" /> bounds the domain of <InlineMath math="f" />, which becomes the bound of the range of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" />.
3. The statement <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(6)=5" /> is true: <InlineMath math="2+\sqrt9=5" />. Check: <InlineMath math="f(5)=6" />.

   <BlockMath math="f(5)=25-20+1=6" />

4. The statement <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(-3)=0" /> is false: <InlineMath math="2" />. The value <InlineMath math="0" /> is not even in the domain of <InlineMath math="f" />.

   <BlockMath math="f^{-1}(-3)=2+\sqrt0=2" />

# Answer

Therefore, **statements <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> are True**, while **statements <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> are False**.

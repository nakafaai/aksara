# Given

We are given an **algebraic radical function** <InlineMath math="r(x)" /> for <InlineMath math="x\ge0" /> and <InlineMath math="x\ne1" />.

# Asked

The **limit of** <InlineMath math="r(x)" /> as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="1" />.

# Solution

> Factor the numerator and rationalize the root in the denominator so the shared zero factor cancels cleanly.

Direct substitution gives <InlineMath math="\frac00" />. The numerator contains the factor <InlineMath math="x-1" />, while the denominator is <InlineMath math="\sqrt x-1" />. Connect them with **a difference of squares**: the equation below for <InlineMath math="x\ge0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
x-1=(\sqrt x-1)(\sqrt x+1) \\
x^3-1=(x-1)(x^2+x+1)=(\sqrt x-1)(\sqrt x+1)(x^2+x+1)
\end{gathered}" />

For <InlineMath math="x\ne1" />, the factor <InlineMath math="\sqrt x-1" /> is not zero and may be cancelled.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to1}\frac{(\sqrt x-1)(\sqrt x+1)(x^2+x+1)}{\sqrt x-1}=\lim_{x\to1}(\sqrt x+1)(x^2+x+1)=2\cdot3=6" />

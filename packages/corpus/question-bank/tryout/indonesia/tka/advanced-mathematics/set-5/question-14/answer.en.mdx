# Given

A **circle** has center <InlineMath math="(-2,5)" /> and is tangent to the vertical <InlineMath math="y" />-axis.

# Asked

The **equation of the circle** matching this center and axis tangency.

# Solution

> The horizontal distance from the center to the vertical axis immediately gives the radius of the tangent circle.

A circle tangent to the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis touches the line <InlineMath math="x=0" /> at exactly one point. So **its radius equals the distance from the centre to the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis**, the absolute value of the centre's <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate: <InlineMath math="r=2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
r=|-2|=2 \\
\begin{aligned}
(x+2)^2+(y-5)^2&=4 \\
x^2+4x+4+y^2-10y+25-4&=0 \\
x^2+y^2+4x-10y+25&=0
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Draw the circle touching the <InlineMath math="Y" />-axis. **The radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the <InlineMath math="Y" />-axis**, so its length equals the distance from the centre to that axis, which is <InlineMath math="2" />.

Check: substituting <InlineMath math="x=0" /> gives <InlineMath math="0" />, which has only one solution, <InlineMath math="y=5" />. So the circle does touch the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis at one point, <InlineMath math="(0,5)" />.

<BlockMath math="y^2-10y+25=(y-5)^2=0" />

So the circle is the equation below.

<BlockMath math="x^2+y^2+4x-10y+25=0" />

## Checking common traps

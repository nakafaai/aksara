# Given

Reflection across a **mirror line** <InlineMath math="g" /> sends point <InlineMath math="P(1,4)" /> to its image <InlineMath math="P'(5,2)" />.

# Asked

The **equation of the mirror line** <InlineMath math="g" />.

# Solution

> A mirror line always cuts straight through the midpoint of the segment joining a point and its image at a right angle.

In a reflection, the mirror always **cuts the segment from a point to its image at right angles, exactly in the middle**. So <InlineMath math="g" /> is the perpendicular bisector of <InlineMath math="PP'" />: it passes through the midpoint and is perpendicular to the segment.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
M&=\left(\frac{1+5}{2},\ \frac{4+2}{2}\right)=(3,3) \\
\overrightarrow{PP'}&=(5-1,\ 2-4)=(4,-2)
\end{aligned}" />

A line through <InlineMath math="M" /> perpendicular to <InlineMath math="(4,-2)" /> uses that vector as its normal vector.

<BlockMath math="4(x-3)-2(y-3)=0\iff 4x-2y-6=0\iff 2x-y-3=0" />

Draw <InlineMath math="P" />, its image, and the mirror line. **The mirror cuts the segment <InlineMath math="PP'" /> exactly at its midpoint and at a right angle**, so you only need the line through <InlineMath math="M(3,3)" /> perpendicular to <InlineMath math="(4,-2)" />.

Check: <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="P'" /> must be equally far from <InlineMath math="g" />. Substituting into the left side gives <InlineMath math="-5" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />. Equal absolute values with opposite signs mean equal distances on opposite sides.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
2-4-3=-5 \\
10-2-3=5
\end{gathered}" />

So <InlineMath math="g" /> is the equation below.

<BlockMath math="2x-y-3=0" />

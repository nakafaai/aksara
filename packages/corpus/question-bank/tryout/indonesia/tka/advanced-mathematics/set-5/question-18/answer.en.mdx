# Given

Point <InlineMath math="P(4,0)" /> is rotated **twice in a row** counterclockwise about the origin <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />.

# Asked

The **final image coordinates** of point <InlineMath math="P" /> after both rotations.

# Solution

> Two rotations around the same center combine into one single rotation by simply adding their angles.

Two rotations about the same centre **equal one rotation through the sum of the angles**: the equation below anticlockwise. A rotation through <InlineMath math="\theta" /> about <InlineMath math="O" /> sends a point <InlineMath math="(r,0)" /> on the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis to <InlineMath math="(r\cos\theta,\ r\sin\theta)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
70^\circ+50^\circ=120^\circ \\
P'=\left(4\cos120^\circ,\ 4\sin120^\circ\right)=\left(4\cdot\left(-\frac12\right),\ 4\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)=\left(-2,\ 2\sqrt3\right)
\end{gathered}" />

Follow the point <InlineMath math="P" /> on the circle of radius <InlineMath math="4" />. **An arc of <InlineMath math="70^\circ" /> followed by an arc of <InlineMath math="50^\circ" /> join into a single arc of <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />**.

Check: the distance to <InlineMath math="O" /> stays <InlineMath math="4" />, and the point lies in quadrant II, matching an angle of <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> between <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.

So the final image of <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="\left(-2,2\sqrt3\right)" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\left(2,2\sqrt3\right)" /> is a <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> rotation, for example from using <InlineMath math="\cos120^\circ=\frac12" />.
- <InlineMath math="\left(-2,-2\sqrt3\right)" /> is a <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> clockwise rotation.
- <InlineMath math="\left(2\sqrt3,-2\right)" /> swaps sine and cosine and writes <InlineMath math="(4\sin120^\circ,\ 4\cos120^\circ)" />.
- <InlineMath math="\left(-2\sqrt3,2\right)" /> is a <InlineMath math="150^\circ" /> rotation.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="\left(-2,2\sqrt3\right)" />**.

# Given

We are given a **radical rational function** <InlineMath math="q(x)" /> as <InlineMath math="x" /> heads toward negative infinity.

# Asked

The **limit value** as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches negative infinity.

# Solution

> A square root is always positive, so pulling a negative variable out of a square root must introduce a minus sign.

For <InlineMath math="x" /> very large in absolute value, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x^2+1}" /> behaves like <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x^2}" />. The key is that **the equation below, which equals <InlineMath math="-x" /> for <InlineMath math="x<0" />**, not <InlineMath math="x" />.

<BlockMath math="\sqrt{x^2}=|x|" />

Divide numerator and denominator by <InlineMath math="x" />. Since <InlineMath math="x<0" />, moving <InlineMath math="x" /> inside the root needs a minus sign: the expression below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x}=-\sqrt{\frac{x^2+1}{x^2}}=-\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{x^2}} \\
\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{2x-3}=\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{-\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{x^2}}}{2-\frac3x}=\frac{-1}{2}=-\frac12
\end{gathered}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="q" /> for negative <InlineMath math="x" /> heading further left. **The curve stays below the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis and hugs the line <InlineMath math="y=-\tfrac12" />**, because the numerator is positive and the denominator negative.

Sign check: for negative <InlineMath math="x" />, the numerator is positive and the denominator negative, so the fraction is negative. For example <InlineMath math="-0.4926" />.

<BlockMath math="q(-100)=\frac{\sqrt{10{,}001}}{-203}\approx-0.4926" />

So the limit is <InlineMath math="-\frac12" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> writes the equation below, which only holds for <InlineMath math="x\ge0" />. That is the limit at <InlineMath math="+\infty" />.

  <BlockMath math="\sqrt{x^2}=x" />

- <InlineMath math="0" /> assumes the root lowers the degree of the numerator. In fact <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x^2+1}" /> grows like <InlineMath math="|x|" />, the same degree as the denominator.
- <InlineMath math="-\infty" /> assumes the numerator grows much faster than the denominator.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> forgets both the coefficient <InlineMath math="2" /> in the denominator and the sign.

# Given

We are given a **trigonometric fraction** <InlineMath math="k(x)" /> in radians with a cosine difference near zero.

# Asked

The **trigonometric limit value** as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches zero.

# Solution

> Turn the difference of cosines into a product of sines so the zero factors cancel neatly with the denominator.

Direct substitution gives the equation below. **Turn the difference of cosines into a product of sines**, because the basic limit the value <InlineMath math="1" /> only works on sines.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\frac{1-1}{0}=\frac00 \\
\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\sin u}{u}=1 \\
\cos A-\cos B=-2\sin\frac{A+B}{2}\sin\frac{A-B}{2}\ \Rightarrow\ \cos3x-\cos x=-2\sin2x\sin x
\end{gathered}" />

Now split into basic limits.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\frac{-2\sin2x\sin x}{x^2}=-2\cdot\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin2x}{x}\cdot\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=-2\cdot2\cdot1=-4" />

Draw <InlineMath math="k" /> on both sides of zero. **Both branches come down to the same lowest point, <InlineMath math="(0,\ -4)" />**, even though <InlineMath math="k(0)" /> isn't defined.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="4" /> drops the minus sign in the difference formula.
- <InlineMath math="-2" /> uses <InlineMath math="\frac{\sin2x}{x}\to1" />, but that limit is <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="-8" /> uses <InlineMath math="\cos u\approx1-u^2" />, but the approximation is the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="1-\frac{u^2}{2}" />

- <InlineMath math="0" /> only looks at the numerator tending to zero.

# Given

Successive divisions of polynomial <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> give **quotient** <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> and two consecutive remainders.

# Asked

The **remainder** when <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by the product of both linear divisors.

# Solution

> Write the second division equation and drop it right inside the first so the combined remainder appears immediately.

Write both divisions as equations, then **substitute the second division into the first**. That makes the factor <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x+2)" /> appear on its own.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(x)&=(x-1)Q(x)+3 \\
Q(x)&=(x+2)H(x)+4
\end{aligned}" />

Replace <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> in the first equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(x)&=(x-1)\big[(x+2)H(x)+4\big]+3 \\
&=(x-1)(x+2)H(x)+4(x-1)+3 \\
&=(x-1)(x+2)H(x)+4x-1
\end{aligned}" />

The remainder on division by <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x+2)" /> is the line through the points of <InlineMath math="P" /> at <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=-2" />. **Those points are <InlineMath math="(1,\ 3)" /> and <InlineMath math="(-2,\ -9)" />**, and the line through both is <InlineMath math="y=4x-1" />.

The part <InlineMath math="4x-1" /> has degree one, less than the divisor's degree, so it is the remainder.

Check with the remainder theorem: <InlineMath math="P(1)=3" />, matching the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x-1" />. The first equation also gives <InlineMath math="-9" />, and <InlineMath math="4(-2)-1=-9" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
P(1)=4-1=3 \\
P(-2)=(-3)Q(-2)+3=(-3)(4)+3=-9
\end{gathered}" />

So the remainder when <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x+2)" /> is <InlineMath math="4x-1" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="4x+3" /> forgets to expand <InlineMath math="4(x-1)" /> into <InlineMath math="4x-4" />.
- <InlineMath math="7" /> adds the two remainders, but the second one is still multiplied by <InlineMath math="x-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="4x+11" /> multiplies the remainder <InlineMath math="4" /> by <InlineMath math="x+2" /> instead of <InlineMath math="x-1" />.
- <InlineMath math="3x+4" /> swaps the roles of the two remainders.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="4x-1" />**.

# Given

Two **square matrices** <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> of size <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> have <InlineMath math="\det A=3" /> and <InlineMath math="\det B=-2" />.

# Asked

The **determinant value** of the combined product, scalar multiple, and inverse.

# Solution

> Determinants split products into multiplication, flip inverses into reciprocals, and square scalars for two-by-two matrices.

You do not need the entries of <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />. **Three determinant rules** for <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> matrices are enough.

<BlockMath math="\det(kM)=k^2\det M,\qquad \det\left(M^{-1}\right)=\frac{1}{\det M},\qquad \det\left(M^T\right)=\det M" />

The determinant of a product is the product of the determinants, so each factor can be handled on its own.

<BlockMath math="\det\left(2A^{-1}B^T\right)=2^2\cdot\frac{1}{\det A}\cdot\det B=4\cdot\frac13\cdot(-2)=-\frac83" />

The diagram below shows what each factor contributes. **Each part of <InlineMath math="2A^{-1}B^T" /> multiplies the determinant by its own number**, and then they're all multiplied in turn.

  title={<>What each factor contributes to the determinant</>}

The factor <InlineMath math="2" /> is squared because it multiplies both rows of the matrix.

So <InlineMath math="\det\left(2A^{-1}B^T\right)=-\frac83" />.

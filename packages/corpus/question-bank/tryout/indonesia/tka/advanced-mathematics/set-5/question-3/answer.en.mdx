Start with the determinant, because three statements depend on it: <InlineMath math="\det A=4\cdot1-3\cdot1=1" />. A matrix with **determinant <InlineMath math="1" />** has an inverse with integer entries.

1. The statement <InlineMath math="\det\left(A^T\right)=-1" /> is false. Transposing does not change the determinant, so <InlineMath math="\det\left(A^T\right)=\det A=1" />.
2. The statement about <InlineMath math="A+A^T" /> is false. The transpose is <InlineMath math="A^T=\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />, so <InlineMath math="A+A^T=\begin{pmatrix} 8 & 4 \\ 4 & 2 \end{pmatrix}" />. A matrix plus its transpose is always symmetric, and the matrix in the statement is not.
3. The statement about <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> is true. Swap the main diagonal entries, change the signs of the others, and divide by <InlineMath math="1" />. Check: <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 3 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ -1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />.
4. The statement <InlineMath math="\det\left(A^2\right)=1" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="\det\left(A^2\right)=(\det A)^2=1" />.

So the statements about <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> and <InlineMath math="\det\left(A^2\right)" /> are true, and the statements about <InlineMath math="\det\left(A^T\right)" /> and <InlineMath math="A+A^T" /> are false.

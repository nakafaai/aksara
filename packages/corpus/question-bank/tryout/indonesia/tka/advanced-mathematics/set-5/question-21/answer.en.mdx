# Given

We are given a **piecewise function** <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> with a special value defined at <InlineMath math="x=2" />.

# Asked

The **limit of** <InlineMath math="f" /> as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Solution

> A limit only cares where the curve heads from the left and right, completely ignoring any isolated dot at the point itself.

A limit at <InlineMath math="x=2" /> only looks at the values of <InlineMath math="f" /> **near** <InlineMath math="2" />, not at <InlineMath math="f(2)" /> itself. So compare the left and right limits.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\to2^-}f(x)&=\lim_{x\to2^-}(x+1)=3 \\
\lim_{x\to2^+}f(x)&=\lim_{x\to2^+}\left(x^2-1\right)=4-1=3
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the three parts of <InlineMath math="f" />. **Both pieces of the graph head to the same point, <InlineMath math="(2,\ 3)" />**, while the point <InlineMath math="f(2)=5" /> floats on its own above it and doesn't affect the limit.

Both one-sided limits agree, so the limit exists and equals <InlineMath math="3" />. The value <InlineMath math="f(2)=5" /> does not affect the limit. It only makes <InlineMath math="f" /> discontinuous at <InlineMath math="x=2" />.

So the value <InlineMath math="3" />.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to2}f(x)=3" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="5" /> uses <InlineMath math="f(2)" />, but a limit does not depend on the value at the point.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> averages <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />, mixing the limit with the function value.
- "Tidak ada" (does not exist) would be right if the left and right limits differed. Here both are <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> adds the left and right limits.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="3" />**.

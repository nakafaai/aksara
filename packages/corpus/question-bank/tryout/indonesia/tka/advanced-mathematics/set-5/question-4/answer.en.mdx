# Given

A cooperative makes **cassava and banana chips** under ingredient and production limits with known profit per kilogram.

# Asked

The **maximum daily profit** the cooperative can achieve.

# Solution

> Map out the production limits and check profit only at the corner points of the feasible region.

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the cassava chips and <InlineMath math="y" /> the banana chips, in kilograms per day. There are **three limits**: frying capacity, cooking oil, and banana-chip packaging.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x+y&\le50 \\
2x+y&\le80 \\
y&\le40 \\
x\ge0,\quad y&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The profit objective (each unit standing for <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}000" />) is <InlineMath math="P=12x+10y" />. Find the corners where neighbouring boundary lines cross.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x+y=50,\ 2x+y=80&\ \Rightarrow\ x=30,\ y=20 \\
x+y=50,\ y=40&\ \Rightarrow\ x=10,\ y=40
\end{aligned}" />

The other corners are <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(40,0)" /> on the oil line, and <InlineMath math="(0,40)" />. The point <InlineMath math="(30,20)" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="y\le40" />, and <InlineMath math="(10,40)" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="20+40=60\le80" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(40,0)&=480 \\
P(30,20)&=360+200=560 \\
P(10,40)&=120+400=520 \\
P(0,40)&=400
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the feasible region. **The three limits cut it into a pentagon**, and the largest profit is at the corner where the frying limit and the oil limit meet.

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the cassava chips and <InlineMath math="y" /> the banana chips, in kilograms per day. There are **three limits**: frying capacity, cooking oil, and banana-chip packaging.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x+y&\le50 \\
2x+y&\le80 \\
y&\le40 \\
x\ge0,\quad y&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The objective, in thousands of rupiah, is <InlineMath math="P=12x+10y" />. Find the corners where neighbouring boundary lines cross.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x+y=50,\ 2x+y=80&\ \Rightarrow\ x=30,\ y=20 \\
x+y=50,\ y=40&\ \Rightarrow\ x=10,\ y=40
\end{aligned}" />

The other corners are <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(40,0)" /> on the oil line, and <InlineMath math="(0,40)" />. The point <InlineMath math="(30,20)" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="y\le40" />, and <InlineMath math="(10,40)" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="20+40=60\le80" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(40,0)&=480 \\
P(30,20)&=360+200=560 \\
P(10,40)&=120+400=520 \\
P(0,40)&=400
\end{aligned}" />

So the maximum profit is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}560{,}000.00" />, from <InlineMath math="30" /> kg of cassava chips and <InlineMath math="20" /> kg of banana chips.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}480{,}000.00" /> is the profit at <InlineMath math="(40,0)" />, cassava chips only.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}400{,}000.00" /> is the profit at <InlineMath math="(0,40)" />, banana chips only.

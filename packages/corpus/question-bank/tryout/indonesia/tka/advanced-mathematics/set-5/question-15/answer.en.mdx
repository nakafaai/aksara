The tangent to <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2=r^2" /> at a point <InlineMath math="(x_1,y_1)" /> on the circle is <InlineMath math="x_1x+y_1y=r^2" />. The formula comes from the tangent being **perpendicular to the radius** at the point of tangency, and that radius points along <InlineMath math="(x_1,y_1)" />.

<BlockMath math="6x=50\ \Rightarrow\ x=\frac{25}{3},\qquad 4y=25-3\cdot\frac{25}{3}=0\ \Rightarrow\ y=0" />

This makes sense: the two points of tangency are symmetric about the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, so the tangents meet on the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis too.

So <InlineMath math="g" /> and <InlineMath math="h" /> meet at <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{25}{3},0\right)" />.

- <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{25}{4},0\right)" /> swaps the coordinates in the formula and uses <InlineMath math="4x+3y=25" /> and <InlineMath math="4x-3y=25" />.

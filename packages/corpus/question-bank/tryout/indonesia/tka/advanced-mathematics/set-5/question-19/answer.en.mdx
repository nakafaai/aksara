The rule is <InlineMath math="x'=x+2y" /> and <InlineMath math="y'=y" />. To get the image equation, **write <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="x'" /> and <InlineMath math="y'" />** and substitute into the original line.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="y=y',\qquad x=x'-2y'" />
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2(x'-2y')-y'+3&=0 \\
2x'-5y'+3&=0
\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

This matrix slides each point horizontally by twice its height. **Points on the <InlineMath math="X" />-axis stay put, and a point at height <InlineMath math="3" /> slides <InlineMath math="6" /> to the right**, so the line becomes flatter.

Check with two points on the original line: <InlineMath math="(0,3)" /> maps to <InlineMath math="(6,3)" />, and <InlineMath math="12-15+3=0" />. The point <InlineMath math="\left(-\frac32,0\right)" /> maps to itself because <InlineMath math="y=0" />, and <InlineMath math="-3-0+3=0" />.

So the image line is <InlineMath math="2x-5y+3=0" />.

- <InlineMath math="2x-y+3=0" /> assumes the line does not change. Only points on the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis stay fixed under this transformation.
- <InlineMath math="2x+3y+3=0" /> substitutes the wrong way, <InlineMath math="x=x'+2y'" />.
- <InlineMath math="4x-y+3=0" /> uses the transpose of the matrix, so <InlineMath math="y'=2x+y" />.
- <InlineMath math="2x-5y-3=0" /> has the wrong sign on the constant.

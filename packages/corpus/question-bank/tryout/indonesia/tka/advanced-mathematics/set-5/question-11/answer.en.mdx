Work is <InlineMath math="W=\vec{F}\cdot\vec{s}" />, where <InlineMath math="\vec{s}" /> is the displacement vector. **Displacement is the end point minus the starting point**, not the position vector of either point.

<BlockMath math="\vec{s}=\overrightarrow{AB}=(5-1)\vec{i}+(3-2)\vec{j}=4\vec{i}+\vec{j}" />

Multiply matching components and add.

<BlockMath math="W=\vec{F}\cdot\vec{s}=3\cdot4+4\cdot1=12+4=16" />

The unit is newton times metre, the joule. The result is positive because force and displacement form an acute angle.

So the force does <InlineMath math="16" /> J of work.

- <InlineMath math="5\sqrt{17}" /> J multiplies the force's size <InlineMath math="5" /> by the distance <InlineMath math="\sqrt{17}" />, as if the force pointed along the displacement.
- <InlineMath math="27" /> J uses the position vector of <InlineMath math="B" />, <InlineMath math="3\cdot5+4\cdot3" />, instead of the displacement.
- <InlineMath math="38" /> J adds the position vectors of <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />, <InlineMath math="3\cdot6+4\cdot5" />.
- <InlineMath math="11" /> J uses the position vector of <InlineMath math="A" />, <InlineMath math="3\cdot1+4\cdot2" />.

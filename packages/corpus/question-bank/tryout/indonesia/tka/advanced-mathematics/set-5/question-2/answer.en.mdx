# Given

Subscribers of **two internet providers** <InlineMath math="P" /> and <InlineMath math="Q" /> switch each month at fixed transition rates.

# Asked

The **subscriber count** after the given months using the transition matrix.

# Solution

> Pack the staying and switching fractions into a transition matrix, then multiply by the starting counts month by month.

Each month the vector of customer numbers is multiplied by the transition matrix. The first row counts next month's customers of <InlineMath math="P" />: **those who stay with <InlineMath math="P" /> plus those who switch from <InlineMath math="Q" />**. Do it twice.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\begin{pmatrix} p_1 \\ q_1 \end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix} 0.8\cdot500+0.3\cdot500 \\ 0.2\cdot500+0.7\cdot500 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 550 \\ 450 \end{pmatrix} \\
\begin{pmatrix} p_2 \\ q_2 \end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix} 0.8\cdot550+0.3\cdot450 \\ 0.2\cdot550+0.7\cdot450 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 440+135 \\ 110+315 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 575 \\ 425 \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the number of customers of both providers each month. **Customers move from <InlineMath math="Q" /> to <InlineMath math="P" />, but the total stays <InlineMath math="1{,}000" />**, so the two lines are always symmetric about <InlineMath math="500" />.

Check: the total stays <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> every month, because each column of the matrix adds up to <InlineMath math="1" />.

So after <InlineMath math="2" /> months, <InlineMath math="P" /> has <InlineMath math="575" /> customers.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="550" /> is only one month.
- <InlineMath math="500" /> uses the transpose of the matrix, as if <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of <InlineMath math="Q" />'s customers switched to <InlineMath math="P" />. With that transpose the numbers never move from the start.
- <InlineMath math="600" /> is the long-run balance, the number of <InlineMath math="P" /> customers after very many months, not after two.
- <InlineMath math="425" /> is the number of <InlineMath math="Q" /> customers after two months.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="575" />**.

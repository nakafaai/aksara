# Given

A linear function <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> with **positive slope** <InlineMath math="a>0" /> satisfies a self-composition identity.

# Asked

The **formula or requested value** of <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> with positive slope.

# Solution

> Plug the linear formula into itself, then match the slope term and constant term on both sides.

The rule for <InlineMath math="f" /> is unknown, so **build <InlineMath math="f\big(f(x)\big)" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b" />** and match its coefficients with <InlineMath math="4x-9" />.

<BlockMath math="f\big(f(x)\big)=a(ax+b)+b=a^2x+ab+b" />

Two polynomials are equal for every <InlineMath math="x" /> exactly when their coefficients are equal.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2&=4\ \Rightarrow\ a=2\quad(\text{since }a>0) \\
ab+b&=-9\ \Rightarrow\ 3b=-9\ \Rightarrow\ b=-3
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="f(x)=2x-3" />. Check: <InlineMath math="f(f(x))=4x-9" />. The value <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(5)" /> is the number that <InlineMath math="f" /> sends to <InlineMath math="5" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
f(f(x))=2(2x-3)-3=4x-9 \\
2x-3=5\ \Rightarrow\ x=4
\end{gathered}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="f" /> and <InlineMath math="f\circ f" />. **The line <InlineMath math="f\circ f" /> is twice as steep as <InlineMath math="f" />**, because the slope <InlineMath math="2" /> is applied twice. The horizontal line <InlineMath math="y=5" /> hits <InlineMath math="f" /> at <InlineMath math="x=4" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="7" /> is <InlineMath math="f(5)" />, not <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(5)" />.
- <InlineMath math="-3" /> is the value of <InlineMath math="b" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> uses <InlineMath math="a=-2" />, which gives <InlineMath math="b=9" /> and <InlineMath math="f(x)=-2x+9" />. The condition <InlineMath math="a>0" /> rules that out.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> is the value of <InlineMath math="b" /> in the rejected case <InlineMath math="a=-2" />.

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="4" />**.

# Given

We are given a **cubic rational function** <InlineMath math="v(x)" /> as <InlineMath math="x" /> grows without bound.

# Asked

The **limit at infinity** of <InlineMath math="v(x)" />.

# Solution

> Expand the highest-power terms in the numerator first because leading terms dominate for huge inputs.

The numerator looks like degree three, but **the <InlineMath math="x^3" /> terms cancel**. Expand before comparing degrees.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x+1)^3&=x^3+3x^2+3x+1 \\
(x-1)^3&=x^3-3x^2+3x-1 \\
(x+1)^3-(x-1)^3&=6x^2+2
\end{aligned}" />

Now numerator and denominator both have degree two. Divide every term by <InlineMath math="x^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{6x^2+2}{3x^2+1}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{6+\frac{2}{x^2}}{3+\frac{1}{x^2}}=\frac63=2" />

Draw the expanded numerator together with the denominator. **The numerator curve is always exactly twice as high as the denominator curve**, so the quotient equals <InlineMath math="2" /> for every <InlineMath math="x" />, including very large <InlineMath math="x" />.

In fact the equation below for every <InlineMath math="x" />, because the numerator is exactly twice the denominator.

<BlockMath math="\frac{6x^2+2}{3x^2+1}=2" />

So the limit is <InlineMath math="2" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> treats the denominator as the higher degree, after wrongly cancelling every squared term in the numerator.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> compares the <InlineMath math="x^3" /> coefficients of the two cubes and forgets the denominator.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> takes the <InlineMath math="x^2" />-coefficient of the numerator and forgets to divide by <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="\infty" /> treats the numerator as degree three without expanding it.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="2" />**.

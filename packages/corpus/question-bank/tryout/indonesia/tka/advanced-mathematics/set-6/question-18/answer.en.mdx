# Given

A triangle of area <InlineMath math="6" /> square units undergoes a **dilation about the origin** with scale factor <InlineMath math="-3" />.

# Asked

The **area of the image triangle** after dilation.

# Solution

> Scaling side lengths multiplies area by the square of the scale factor, and area is always positive.

A dilation with factor <InlineMath math="k" /> multiplies every length by <InlineMath math="|k|" />. Area is a product of two lengths, so **area is multiplied by <InlineMath math="k^2" />**.

<BlockMath math="A'=k^2\cdot A=(-3)^2\cdot6=9\cdot6=54" />

Draw one example triangle of area <InlineMath math="6" /> and its image. **Every side becomes <InlineMath math="3" /> times longer in both directions**, so the area becomes <InlineMath math="9" /> times larger, and the minus sign only flips it to the other side of <InlineMath math="O" />.

The negative sign of the factor only puts the image on the other side of the centre, turned around. The area stays positive. In matrix form the dilation is the matrix below with determinant <InlineMath math="9" />, and areas are multiplied by the absolute value of the determinant.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -3 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix}" />

So the image triangle has area <InlineMath math="54" /> square units.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="-18" /> multiplies the area by <InlineMath math="k=-3" /> without squaring and forgets that area is never negative.
- <InlineMath math="18" /> multiplies the area by <InlineMath math="|k|=3" />, as if area changed like length.
- <InlineMath math="-54" /> squares correctly but gives the area a negative sign.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> assumes a dilation does not change area.

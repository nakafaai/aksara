# Given

A pond fence forms an **origin-centered circle** of radius <InlineMath math="5" /> m with a lamp standing outside it.

# Asked

The **nearest distance, farthest distance, or tangent length** from the lamp to the pond.

# Solution

> Draw a straight line from the lamp through the pond center because it hits both the nearest and farthest rim points.

The fence point closest to <InlineMath math="L" /> lies **on the line joining the centre to <InlineMath math="L" />**. So the shortest distance is the distance from <InlineMath math="L" /> to the centre minus the radius, provided <InlineMath math="L" /> is outside the circle.

The centre is <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=5" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
|OL|&=\sqrt{9^2+12^2}=\sqrt{81+144}=\sqrt{225}=15 \\
d_{\min}&=|OL|-r=15-5=10
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the pond and the lamp. **The nearest point of the fence lies on the line from the centre to the lamp**, so the shortest distance is <InlineMath math="15" /> minus the radius <InlineMath math="5" />.

Since <InlineMath math="15>5" />, the lamp is outside the fence. The nearest fence point is the equation below, and its distance to <InlineMath math="L" /> is <InlineMath math="10" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\frac{5}{15}(9,12)=(3,4) \\
\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10
\end{gathered}" />

So the shortest distance from the lamp to the pond fence is <InlineMath math="10" /> m.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="15" /> m is the distance from the lamp to the centre of the pond, not to the fence.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> m is the longest distance, <InlineMath math="15+5" />, to the fence point on the far side.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> m is the radius of the pond.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> m uses only the lamp's <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate, its distance to the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

Compute the side vectors, then **judge each angle by the sign of the dot product** of the two vectors leaving that vertex. A positive dot product means the angle is acute.

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}=(-1,2,0),\qquad \overrightarrow{AC}=(-1,0,3),\qquad \overrightarrow{BC}=(0,-2,3)" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{at }A&:\ \overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC}=1+0+0=1 \\
\text{at }B&:\ \overrightarrow{BA}\cdot\overrightarrow{BC}=(1,-2,0)\cdot(0,-2,3)=4 \\
\text{at }C&:\ \overrightarrow{CA}\cdot\overrightarrow{CB}=(1,0,-3)\cdot(0,2,-3)=9
\end{aligned}" />

1. The statement that triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> has a right angle is false, because none of the dot products is zero.
2. The statement about the area is true. With <InlineMath math="|\overrightarrow{AB}|^2=5" /> and <InlineMath math="|\overrightarrow{AC}|^2=10" />, the triangle's area is half the area of the parallelogram on those two sides.

<BlockMath math="A=\frac12\sqrt{|\overrightarrow{AB}|^2|\overrightarrow{AC}|^2-\left(\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC}\right)^2}=\frac12\sqrt{50-1}=\frac72" />

3. The statement that all three angles are acute is true, because all three dot products above are positive.
4. The statement <InlineMath math="|\overrightarrow{AB}|=|\overrightarrow{AC}|" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="\sqrt5\ne\sqrt{10}" />.
5. The statement about the vector <InlineMath math="6\vec{i}+3\vec{j}+2\vec{k}" /> is true: <InlineMath math="(6,3,2)\cdot(-1,2,0)=-6+6=0" /> and <InlineMath math="(6,3,2)\cdot(-1,0,3)=-6+6=0" />.

So the true statements are the area of <InlineMath math="\frac72" /> square units, the three acute angles, and the vector <InlineMath math="6\vec{i}+3\vec{j}+2\vec{k}" /> perpendicular to both sides.

# Given

We are given a **combined sine and radical limit** in radians as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches zero.

# Asked

The **limit value** after resolving the zero-over-zero indeterminate form.

# Solution

> Rationalize the square root first so a clean linear factor appears to pair up with the sine term.

Direct substitution gives <InlineMath math="\frac00" />. The denominator is a difference involving a root, so **multiply top and bottom by the conjugate <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4+x^2}+2" />**.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\left(\sqrt{4+x^2}-2\right)\left(\sqrt{4+x^2}+2\right)=4+x^2-4=x^2 \\
\frac{x\sin3x}{\sqrt{4+x^2}-2}=\frac{x\sin3x\left(\sqrt{4+x^2}+2\right)}{x^2}=\frac{\sin3x}{x}\cdot\left(\sqrt{4+x^2}+2\right)
\end{gathered}" />
</MathContainer>

Now both factors have clear limits: the value <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin3x}{x}=3 \\
\sqrt{4+0}+2=4 \\
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x\sin3x}{\sqrt{4+x^2}-2}=3\cdot4=12
\end{gathered}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="3" /> takes <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4+x^2}-2" /> to behave like <InlineMath math="x^2" />, but it behaves like the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{x^2}{4}" />

- <InlineMath math="6" /> uses <InlineMath math="2" /> as the value of the conjugate, but <InlineMath math="\sqrt4+2=4" />.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> only looks at the numerator tending to <InlineMath math="0" />, but the denominator tends to <InlineMath math="0" /> as well.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> treats <InlineMath math="\sin3x" /> like <InlineMath math="x" />, losing the factor <InlineMath math="3" />.

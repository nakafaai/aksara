# Given

An artisan makes **two craft products** under fabric and sewing-hour limits with known unit profits.

# Asked

The **maximum weekly profit** the artisan can earn.

# Solution

> Find every corner of the region meeting both fabric and time limits, then pick the highest profit.

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the number of bags and <InlineMath math="y" /> the number of wallets per week. There are **two resource limits**: cloth and sewing hours.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x+y&\le40 \\
x+2y&\le50 \\
x\ge0,\quad y&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The profit objective (each unit standing for <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}000" />) is <InlineMath math="P=30x+20y" />. The corner where both resources run out comes from substituting <InlineMath math="y=40-2x" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x+2(40-2x)&=50 \\
80-3x&=50\ \Rightarrow\ x=10,\quad y=20
\end{aligned}" />

The other corners are <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(20,0)" /> on the cloth line, and <InlineMath math="(0,25)" /> on the sewing line.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(20,0)&=600 \\
P(10,20)&=300+400=700 \\
P(0,25)&=500
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the feasible region. **The two limit lines cut it into a quadrilateral**, and the largest profit is at the corner where the fabric and the sewing hours both run out.

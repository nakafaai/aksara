# Given

We are given a **square matrix** <InlineMath math="A" /> and the <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> identity matrix <InlineMath math="I" />.

# Asked

The **matrix operation result** or requested entry from <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="I" />.

# Solution

> Compute the matrix square by row-times-column multiplication first, then combine matching slots entry by entry.

Find the inverse of <InlineMath math="A" /> first, then add it to <InlineMath math="A" />. The determinant is <InlineMath math="2\cdot2-1\cdot3=1" />, so the inverse only needs you to **swap the main diagonal and change the signs of the other diagonal**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A^{-1}&=\frac11\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -3 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \\
A+A^{-1}&=\begin{pmatrix} 2+2 & 1-1 \\ 3-3 & 2+2 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}=4I
\end{aligned}" />

The diagram below adds <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> entry by entry. **The off-diagonal entries cancel, and both diagonal entries are equal**, so the result is a multiple of <InlineMath math="I" />.

The off-diagonal entries cancel and both diagonal entries are <InlineMath math="4" />, so the result really is a multiple of <InlineMath math="I" />. Check: the matrix below.

<BlockMath math="AA^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 4-3 & -2+2 \\ 6-6 & -3+4 \end{pmatrix}=I" />

So <InlineMath math="k=4" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="2" /> only reads a diagonal entry of <InlineMath math="A" /> without adding the inverse.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> is the sum of the off-diagonal entries, not a diagonal entry.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> adds the determinant <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="4" />, but the determinant only divides the adjugate.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> is the determinant of <InlineMath math="A" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="4" />**.

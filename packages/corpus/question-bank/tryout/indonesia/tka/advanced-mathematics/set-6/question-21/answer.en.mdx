Use **the basic limit** <InlineMath math="\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\sin u}{u}=1" />. For the forms to match, the angle inside the sine must equal the denominator. Multiply and divide by <InlineMath math="4" />.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin4x}{3x}=\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin4x}{4x}\cdot\frac{4x}{3x}=1\cdot\frac43=\frac43" />

As <InlineMath math="x\to0" />, <InlineMath math="u=4x\to0" /> too, so <InlineMath math="\frac{\sin4x}{4x}\to1" />. The factor <InlineMath math="\frac{4x}{3x}=\frac43" /> is constant for every <InlineMath math="x\ne0" />.

So the limit is <InlineMath math="\frac43" />.

- <InlineMath math="\frac34" /> flips the ratio of the coefficients.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> forgets the coefficient <InlineMath math="3" /> in the denominator.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> assumes <InlineMath math="\frac{\sin ax}{bx}" /> always tends to <InlineMath math="1" />, whatever the coefficients.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> multiplies the coefficients instead of dividing.

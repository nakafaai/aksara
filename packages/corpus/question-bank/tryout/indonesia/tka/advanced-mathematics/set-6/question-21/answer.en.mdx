# Given

We are given a **basic sine limit** in radians as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches zero.

# Asked

The **value of the sine ratio limit** near zero.

# Solution

> For tiny angles in radians, the sine of a multiple of the angle behaves just like that multiple itself.

Use **the basic limit** the value <InlineMath math="1" />. For the forms to match, the angle inside the sine must equal the denominator. Multiply and divide by <InlineMath math="4" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\sin u}{u}=1 \\
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin4x}{3x}=\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin4x}{4x}\cdot\frac{4x}{3x}=1\cdot\frac43=\frac43
\end{gathered}" />

Draw the function on both sides of zero. **Both branches rise to the same point, <InlineMath math="\left(0,\ \tfrac43\right)" />**, even though the function isn't defined at <InlineMath math="x=0" />.

As <InlineMath math="x\to0" />, <InlineMath math="u=4x\to0" /> too, so <InlineMath math="\frac{\sin4x}{4x}\to1" />. The factor the equation below is constant for every <InlineMath math="x\ne0" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{4x}{3x}=\frac43" />

So the limit is <InlineMath math="\frac43" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="\frac34" /> flips the ratio of the coefficients.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> forgets the coefficient <InlineMath math="3" /> in the denominator.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> assumes <InlineMath math="\frac{\sin ax}{bx}" /> always tends to <InlineMath math="1" />, whatever the coefficients.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> multiplies the coefficients instead of dividing.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="\frac43" />**.

Every statement can be judged from **the values of <InlineMath math="P" /> at a few points**. Compute them first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(1)&=1-3+2+5-6=-1 \\
P(-1)&=1+3+2-5-6=-5 \\
P(2)&=16-24+8+10-6=4 \\
P(3)&=81-81+18+15-6=27
\end{aligned}" />

1. The statement about the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x-2" /> is true, because the remainder is <InlineMath math="P(2)=4" />.
2. The statement that <InlineMath math="x+1" /> is a factor is false, because <InlineMath math="P(-1)=-5\ne0" />.
3. The statement about the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x^2-1" /> is true. The remainder has the form <InlineMath math="ax+b" /> with <InlineMath math="a+b=P(1)=-1" /> and <InlineMath math="-a+b=P(-1)=-5" />. Adding gives <InlineMath math="2b=-6" />, so <InlineMath math="b=-3" /> and <InlineMath math="a=2" />.
4. The statement about the quotient on division by <InlineMath math="x-1" /> is true. Synthetic division with coefficients <InlineMath math="1,-3,2,5,-6" /> at <InlineMath math="x=1" /> gives the row <InlineMath math="1,-2,0,5" /> with remainder <InlineMath math="-1" />. The quotient is <InlineMath math="x^3-2x^2+0x+5" />.
5. The statement <InlineMath math="P(3)=21" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="P(3)=27" />.

So the true statements are the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x-2" />, the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x^2-1" />, and the quotient on division by <InlineMath math="x-1" />.

The area of a parallelogram is base times height, <InlineMath math="|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\sin\theta" />, where <InlineMath math="\theta" /> is the angle between the sides. **The sine comes from the cosine**, and the cosine comes from the dot product.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}&=2-2+4=4,\qquad |\vec{a}|=|\vec{b}|=\sqrt{1+4+4}=3 \\
\cos\theta&=\frac49,\qquad \sin\theta=\sqrt{1-\frac{16}{81}}=\frac{\sqrt{65}}{9}
\end{aligned}" />

The angle between two vectors lies between <InlineMath math="0^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, so its sine is positive.

<BlockMath math="A=|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\sin\theta=3\cdot3\cdot\frac{\sqrt{65}}{9}=\sqrt{65}" />

The same result without the angle: <InlineMath math="A=\sqrt{|\vec{a}|^2|\vec{b}|^2-(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b})^2}=\sqrt{81-16}=\sqrt{65}" />.

So the area of <InlineMath math="OPRQ" /> is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{65}" /> square units.

- <InlineMath math="4" /> is the dot product, which belongs to the cosine, not the area.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> is <InlineMath math="|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|" />, as if the sides were perpendicular.
- <InlineMath math="\sqrt{77}" /> subtracts <InlineMath math="\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=4" /> without squaring it, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{81-4}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac12\sqrt{65}" /> is the area of triangle <InlineMath math="OPQ" />, half the parallelogram.

The dilation <InlineMath math="\left[O,\frac12\right]" /> sends <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(x',y')=\left(\frac x2,\frac y2\right)" />. To get the image equation, **write the old coordinates in terms of the image coordinates**: <InlineMath math="x=2x'" /> and <InlineMath math="y=2y'" />. Then substitute into the original equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2y'&=(2x')^2-4 \\
2y'&=4x'^2-4 \\
y'&=2x'^2-2
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the starting parabola and its image. **The half dilation pulls every point to half its distance from <InlineMath math="O" />**, so the vertex and the intercepts move in too, and the parabola looks narrower.

A function is continuous at <InlineMath math="x=0" /> exactly when **its value equals its limit**, <InlineMath math="k=\lim_{x\to0}f(x)" />. So you need the limit of the part for <InlineMath math="x\ne0" />.

Use the identity <InlineMath math="1-\cos x=2\sin^2\frac x2" /> so that the basic sine limit appears.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^2}=\lim_{x\to0}\frac{2\sin^2\frac x2}{x^2}=2\left(\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin\frac x2}{x}\right)^2=2\cdot\left(\frac12\right)^2=\frac12" />

Numerical check: for <InlineMath math="x=0.1" />, <InlineMath math="\frac{1-\cos0.1}{0.01}\approx0.4996" />.

So <InlineMath math="k=\frac12" />.

- <InlineMath math="1" /> uses <InlineMath math="1-\cos x\approx x^2" />, but the approximation is <InlineMath math="\frac{x^2}{2}" />.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> only looks at the numerator tending to zero.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> turns the fraction <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> upside down.
- <InlineMath math="-\frac12" /> has a sign slip in the identity, as if <InlineMath math="1-\cos x=-2\sin^2\frac x2" />.

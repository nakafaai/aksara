# Given

Two vectors <InlineMath math="\vec{p}" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{q}" /> have known **sum length** and **difference length**.

# Asked

The **dot product** <InlineMath math="\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}" /> between the two vectors.

# Solution

> Subtract the squared difference length from the squared sum length so both squares cancel and leave only the dot product.

Lengths of vectors cannot be added directly, but **squared lengths expand with the dot product**: <InlineMath math="|\vec{u}|^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
|\vec{u}|^2=\vec{u}\cdot\vec{u} \\
\begin{aligned}
|\vec{p}+\vec{q}|^2&=|\vec{p}|^2+2\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}+|\vec{q}|^2=49 \\
|\vec{p}-\vec{q}|^2&=|\vec{p}|^2-2\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}+|\vec{q}|^2=9
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Subtract the second equation from the first. The terms <InlineMath math="|\vec{p}|^2" /> and <InlineMath math="|\vec{q}|^2" /> cancel, so you never need the individual lengths.

<BlockMath math="4\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}=49-9=40\ \Rightarrow\ \vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}=10" />

- <InlineMath math="29" /> is half the sum of the two squares, which is <InlineMath math="|\vec{p}|^2+|\vec{q}|^2" />, not the dot product.

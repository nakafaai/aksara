Lengths of vectors cannot be added directly, but **squared lengths expand with the dot product**: <InlineMath math="|\vec{u}|^2=\vec{u}\cdot\vec{u}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
|\vec{p}+\vec{q}|^2&=|\vec{p}|^2+2\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}+|\vec{q}|^2=49 \\
|\vec{p}-\vec{q}|^2&=|\vec{p}|^2-2\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}+|\vec{q}|^2=9
\end{aligned}" />

Subtract the second equation from the first. The terms <InlineMath math="|\vec{p}|^2" /> and <InlineMath math="|\vec{q}|^2" /> cancel, so you never need the individual lengths.

<BlockMath math="4\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}=49-9=40\ \Rightarrow\ \vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}=10" />

An example that fits: <InlineMath math="\vec{p}=(5,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{q}=(2,0)" /> give <InlineMath math="|\vec{p}+\vec{q}|=7" />, <InlineMath math="|\vec{p}-\vec{q}|=3" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}=10" />.

So <InlineMath math="\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}=10" />.

- <InlineMath math="40" /> forgets to divide by <InlineMath math="4" />.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> divides by <InlineMath math="8" />.
- <InlineMath math="29" /> is half the sum of the two squares, which is <InlineMath math="|\vec{p}|^2+|\vec{q}|^2" />, not the dot product.

# Given

From exterior point <InlineMath math="P(1,7)" />, **two tangent lines** are drawn to the origin-centered circle of radius <InlineMath math="5" />.

# Asked

The **truth value of each statement** about tangent length, angle, and contact points.

# Solution

> Two tangents from one outside point always match in length and form two symmetric right triangles with the center.

The circle has centre <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="r=5" />. Start from **the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to the centre**, because it decides where <InlineMath math="P" /> lies and how long the tangents are.

<BlockMath math="|OP|^2=1^2+7^2=50" />

Draw the circle, the point <InlineMath math="P" />, and both tangents. **Each tangent is perpendicular to the radius at its point of contact**, and the two tangent segments from <InlineMath math="P" /> are equal, both <InlineMath math="5" />.

1. The statement about the tangent length is true. The radius to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent, so the length is <InlineMath math="5" />.

   <BlockMath math="\sqrt{|OP|^2-r^2}=\sqrt{50-25}=5" />

2. The statement that <InlineMath math="P" /> lies inside the circle is false, because <InlineMath math="|OP|^2>25" />. Tangents from a point exist only when the point is outside.

   <BlockMath math="|OP|^2=50>25" />

3. The statement that <InlineMath math="x=1" /> is a tangent is false. Its distance from the centre is <InlineMath math="1<5" />, so it cuts the circle in two points.
4. The statement about the point <InlineMath math="(4,3)" /> is true. The point is on the circle, since <InlineMath math="16+9=25" />. The tangent there is <InlineMath math="4x+3y=25" />, and <InlineMath math="P" /> satisfies it: <InlineMath math="4\cdot1+3\cdot7=25" />. So the tangent at <InlineMath math="(4,3)" /> passes through <InlineMath math="P" />.

As an extra check, the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="(4,3)" /> is <InlineMath math="5" />, matching the first statement.

<BlockMath math="\sqrt{9+16}=5" />

# Answer

Therefore, **statements <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> are True**, while **statements <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> are False**.

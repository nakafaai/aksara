The area of a sector with central angle <InlineMath math="\alpha" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{\alpha}{360^\circ}\pi r^2" />. Both sectors use the same angle, so the factor <InlineMath math="\frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ}=\frac13" /> can be taken out.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A&=\frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ}\pi\cdot60^2-\frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ}\pi\cdot15^2 \\
&=\frac13\pi\left(60^2-15^2\right) \\
&=\frac13\pi(3{,}600-225) \\
&=\frac13\pi\cdot3{,}375=1{,}125\pi\text{ cm}^2
\end{aligned}" />

The result makes sense: it is less than the area of the large sector, <InlineMath math="\frac13\pi\cdot3{,}600=1{,}200\pi\text{ cm}^2" />, and the difference, <InlineMath math="75\pi\text{ cm}^2" />, equals the small sector <InlineMath math="\frac13\pi\cdot225" />.

So the area of glass swept by the wiper rubber in one sweep is <InlineMath math="1{,}125\pi\text{ cm}^2" />.

- <InlineMath math="675\pi\text{ cm}^2" /> comes from subtracting the radii first and then squaring: <InlineMath math="\frac13\pi(60-15)^2" />. The squares of the radii must be subtracted instead.
- <InlineMath math="1{,}200\pi\text{ cm}^2" /> is the large sector alone. It includes the part near the pivot that the rubber never touches.
- <InlineMath math="2{,}250\pi\text{ cm}^2" /> comes from writing the angle fraction as <InlineMath math="\frac{120^\circ}{180^\circ}" />, although a full turn is <InlineMath math="360^\circ" />.
- <InlineMath math="3{,}375\pi\text{ cm}^2" /> is the area of a full ring. The factor <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> for the angle <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> is missing.

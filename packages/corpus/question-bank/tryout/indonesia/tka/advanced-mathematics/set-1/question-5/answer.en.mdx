The cutting and painting limits do not change, so **the feasible region stays the same**. Only the objective function changes. Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the number of chairs and <InlineMath math="y" /> the number of tables in one month.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x+3y&\le120 \\
x+3y&\le90 \\
x\ge0,\quad y&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

With the new table profit, the monthly profit is <InlineMath math="g(x,y)=150{,}000x+500{,}000y" /> rupiah. The maximum of a linear function on the feasible region occurs at a corner point. Eliminating between the two boundary lines gives the intersection <InlineMath math="(30,20)" />, because <InlineMath math="(2x+3y)-(x+3y)=120-90" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=30" />, and then <InlineMath math="30+3y=90" /> gives <InlineMath math="y=20" />. The other corner points are <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(60,0)" />, and <InlineMath math="(0,30)" />.

Compute the new profit at every corner point.

| Corner point | New profit |
| --- | ---: |
| <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> | <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}0" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(60,0)" /> | <InlineMath math="150{,}000\cdot60=\text{Rp}9{,}000{,}000" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(30,20)" /> | <InlineMath math="150{,}000\cdot30+500{,}000\cdot20=\text{Rp}14{,}500{,}000" /> |
| <InlineMath math="(0,30)" /> | <InlineMath math="500{,}000\cdot30=\text{Rp}15{,}000{,}000" /> |

The largest profit is now at <InlineMath math="(0,30)" />. The trade along the painting line <InlineMath math="x+3y=90" /> shows why. Moving from <InlineMath math="(0,30)" /> to <InlineMath math="(30,20)" /> adds <InlineMath math="30" /> chairs and removes <InlineMath math="10" /> tables. The extra chair profit is only <InlineMath math="30\cdot150{,}000=4{,}500{,}000" />, while the lost table profit is <InlineMath math="10\cdot500{,}000=5{,}000{,}000" />. That move therefore lowers the profit.

So, for maximum profit, the workshop makes <InlineMath math="0" /> chairs and <InlineMath math="30" /> tables in one month, earning <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}15{,}000{,}000" />.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> chairs and <InlineMath math="40" /> tables fit the cutting limit, but painting them needs <InlineMath math="3\cdot40=120" /> hours, more than <InlineMath math="90" /> hours.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> chairs and <InlineMath math="30" /> tables need <InlineMath math="2\cdot20+3\cdot30=130" /> cutting hours, more than <InlineMath math="120" /> hours. This combination comes from swapping the coordinates of the corner point <InlineMath math="(30,20)" />.
- <InlineMath math="30" /> chairs and <InlineMath math="20" /> tables is the optimum for the old table profit. With the new profit, it gives only <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}14{,}500{,}000" />.
- <InlineMath math="60" /> chairs and <InlineMath math="0" /> tables give only <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}9{,}000{,}000" />, because no tables are made although tables are now the most profitable product.

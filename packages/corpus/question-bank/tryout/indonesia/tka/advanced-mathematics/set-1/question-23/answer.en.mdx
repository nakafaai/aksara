# Given

**The limit at** infinity **the expression below** takes the indeterminate infinity-minus-infinity form.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty} \left(\frac{3x^2 + 7x}{x - 1} - 3x\right)" />

# Asked

The **value of the limit** as <InlineMath math="x" /> grows without bound.

# Solution

> Combine both terms over one common denominator first so the highest-degree terms cancel and the remaining ratio stands out clearly.

Both parts grow without bound, and the form <InlineMath math="\infty-\infty" /> says nothing yet about the limit. So **combine everything into a single fraction first**. The squared terms then cancel.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{3x^2+7x}{x-1}-3x&=\frac{3x^2+7x-3x(x-1)}{x-1} \\
&=\frac{3x^2+7x-3x^2+3x}{x-1}=\frac{10x}{x-1}
\end{aligned}" />

Divide the numerator and the denominator by <InlineMath math="x" />.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{10}{1-\frac1x}=\frac{10}{1}=10" />

Draw the combined difference, the expression below, for large <InlineMath math="x" />. **The curve falls and hugs the line <InlineMath math="y=10" />** without crossing it.

<BlockMath math="\frac{10x}{x-1}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> treats <InlineMath math="\infty-\infty" /> like an ordinary difference of equal numbers.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> expands <InlineMath math="-3x(x-1)" /> wrongly as <InlineMath math="-3x^2-3x" /> (correct: <InlineMath math="-3x^2+3x" />) and gets the numerator <InlineMath math="4x" />.
- <InlineMath math="7" /> divides <InlineMath math="3x^2+7x" /> by <InlineMath math="x" /> instead of <InlineMath math="x-1" /> and gets the equation below.

  <BlockMath math="3x+7-3x=7" />

- <InlineMath math="\infty" /> overlooks that the <InlineMath math="x^2" /> terms cancel.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="10" />**.

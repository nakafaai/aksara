Both roots grow without bound, and the form <InlineMath math="\infty-\infty" /> says nothing yet about the limit. So **multiply by the conjugate**. The difference of the roots then becomes the difference of the radicands.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x^2+5x}-\sqrt{x^2-3x}&=\frac{(x^2+5x)-(x^2-3x)}{\sqrt{x^2+5x}+\sqrt{x^2-3x}} \\
&=\frac{8x}{\sqrt{x^2+5x}+\sqrt{x^2-3x}}
\end{aligned}" />

Divide the numerator and the denominator by <InlineMath math="x" />. For <InlineMath math="x>0" />, <InlineMath math="\frac{\sqrt{x^2+5x}}{x}=\sqrt{1+\frac5x}" />.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{8}{\sqrt{1+\frac5x}+\sqrt{1-\frac3x}}=\frac{8}{1+1}=4" />

As a check, at <InlineMath math="x=10{,}000" /> the expression is about <InlineMath math="10{,}002.50-9{,}998.50=4.00" />.

So the limit is <InlineMath math="4" />.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> treats <InlineMath math="\infty-\infty" /> like an ordinary difference of equal numbers.
- <InlineMath math="1" /> computes <InlineMath math="5x-3x=2x" /> in the numerator. Subtracting <InlineMath math="x^2-3x" /> adds <InlineMath math="+3x" />, though.
- <InlineMath math="\frac52" /> replaces <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x^2-3x}" /> by <InlineMath math="x" />. That loses the contribution <InlineMath math="\frac32" /> of the second root.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> forgets that the denominator tends to <InlineMath math="2" />, not to <InlineMath math="1" />.

The question asks for the sum of the **different** values of <InlineMath math="k" /> that leave <InlineMath math="M" /> without an inverse. A square matrix has no inverse exactly when its determinant is zero, so you need to solve <InlineMath math="\det M=0" />.

# Work out the determinant with Sarrus

For a <InlineMath math="3\times3" /> matrix, Sarrus's rule adds the three products along the down-right diagonals and then **subtracts the three products along the down-left diagonals**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{down-right}&=(k+1)\cdot k\cdot(k+2)+(-2)(-2)(6)+2(-3)(-4) \\
&=k^3+3k^2+2k+24+24 \\
\text{down-left}&=2\cdot k\cdot6+(k+1)(-2)(-4)+(-2)(-3)(k+2) \\
&=12k+8k+8+6k+12=26k+20 \\
\det M&=k^3+3k^2+2k+48-(26k+20) \\
&=k^3+3k^2-24k+28
\end{aligned}" />

# Factor the cubic

Try divisors of <InlineMath math="28" />. At <InlineMath math="k=2" /> you get <InlineMath math="8+12-48+28=0" />, so <InlineMath math="k-2" /> is a factor. Divide with synthetic division, then **factor the quotient as well**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
k^3+3k^2-24k+28&=(k-2)(k^2+5k-14) \\
&=(k-2)(k-2)(k+7) \\
&=(k-2)^2(k+7)
\end{aligned}" />

The determinant is zero for <InlineMath math="k=2" /> or <InlineMath math="k=-7" />. The root <InlineMath math="k=2" /> shows up twice in the factorization, but as a **value** of <InlineMath math="k" /> it's still just one value. So the different values are only <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="-7" />.

<BlockMath math="2+(-7)=-5" />

# Check both values

For <InlineMath math="k=2" />, the first row <InlineMath math="(3,-2,2)" /> and the second row <InlineMath math="(-3,2,-2)" /> are negatives of each other, so the determinant has to be zero. For <InlineMath math="k=-7" />, substituting into <InlineMath math="(k-2)^2(k+7)" /> gives <InlineMath math="81\cdot0=0" />. Both work, and **there's no other value** because the factorization is complete.

# Why the other options are wrong

The main trap is **a double root counted twice**.

- <InlineMath math="-3" /> comes from the sum-of-roots formula <InlineMath math="-\frac{b}{a}=-3" /> on the cubic. That formula counts the double root <InlineMath math="k=2" /> twice, as <InlineMath math="2+2+(-7)" />. You get the same number if you add the trial root <InlineMath math="k=2" /> to both roots of <InlineMath math="k^2+5k-14" />, even though one of those roots is also <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> comes from stopping after finding <InlineMath math="k=2" /> by trial without factoring the rest.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> comes from the sum-of-roots formula without its minus sign, <InlineMath math="\frac{b}{a}" />, on top of counting the double root twice.
- <InlineMath math="9" /> comes from solving <InlineMath math="k+7=0" /> as <InlineMath math="k=7" />. The correct root is <InlineMath math="k=-7" />.

So the sum of all different values of <InlineMath math="k" /> that make <InlineMath math="M" /> non-invertible is <InlineMath math="-5" />.

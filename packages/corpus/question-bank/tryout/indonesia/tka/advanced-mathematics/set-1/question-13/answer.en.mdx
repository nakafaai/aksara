# Given

Two 3D vectors **<InlineMath math="\vec{u}" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" /> containing real parameter <InlineMath math="k" />** come with five geometric statements.

# Asked

**All true statements** about perpendicularity, acute angles, lengths, and parallelism.

# Solution

> Write the dot product and lengths in terms of k once, then substitute each candidate value of k to judge every statement cleanly.

Three statements depend on the **dot product** <InlineMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}" />, one on vector lengths, and one on parallelism. Compute both in general form, then substitute the value of <InlineMath math="k" /> that each statement names.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}&=1\cdot2+2\cdot k+(-2)\cdot1=2k \\
|\vec{u}|&=\sqrt{1^2+2^2+(-2)^2}=\sqrt9=3 \\
|\vec{v}|&=\sqrt{2^2+k^2+1^2}=\sqrt{k^2+5}
\end{aligned}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="\vec u\cdot\vec v" /> and <InlineMath math="|\vec v|" /> as functions of <InlineMath math="k" />. **The sign of the dot product decides the kind of angle**: zero at <InlineMath math="k=0" />, positive for <InlineMath math="k>0" />. The length curve meets the line at <InlineMath math="3" /> at <InlineMath math="k=\pm2" />.

Two facts decide the statements. Two nonzero vectors are perpendicular exactly when their dot product is zero. The angle between them is acute exactly when the dot product is positive, because the equation below and lengths are always positive.

<BlockMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=|\vec{u}||\vec{v}|\cos\theta" />

1. The statement that some <InlineMath math="k" /> makes <InlineMath math="\vec{u}" /> parallel to <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" /> is false. Parallel means <InlineMath math="\vec{v}=c\vec{u}" /> for some number <InlineMath math="c" />. The first component forces <InlineMath math="2=c\cdot1" />, so <InlineMath math="c=2" />. The third component would then need <InlineMath math="1=-4" />, which is impossible for every <InlineMath math="k" />.

   <BlockMath math="1=2\cdot(-2)=-4" />

2. The statement about <InlineMath math="k=0" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="0" /> and neither vector is zero.

   <BlockMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=2\cdot0=0" />

3. The statement about <InlineMath math="k=4" /> is true. The dot product is <InlineMath math="0" />, so <InlineMath math="\cos\theta>0" /> and the angle is acute.

   <BlockMath math="2\cdot4=8>0" />

4. The statement about <InlineMath math="k=2" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="|\vec{v}|=|\vec{u}|" />.

   <BlockMath math="|\vec{v}|=\sqrt{4+5}=3=|\vec{u}|" />

5. The statement about <InlineMath math="k=-1" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="-2" />, not <InlineMath math="2" />. The value <InlineMath math="2" /> comes from giving the term <InlineMath math="(-2)\cdot1" /> a positive sign.

   <BlockMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=2\cdot(-1)=-2" />

# Answer

Therefore, the correct choices are **statements <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="3" />, and <InlineMath math="4" />**.

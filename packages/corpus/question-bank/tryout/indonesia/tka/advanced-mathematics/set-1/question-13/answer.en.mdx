Four statements depend on the **dot product** <InlineMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}" />, and one depends on vector lengths. Compute both in general form, then substitute the value of <InlineMath math="k" /> that each statement names.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}&=1\cdot2+2\cdot k+(-2)\cdot1=2k \\
|\vec{u}|&=\sqrt{1^2+2^2+(-2)^2}=\sqrt9=3 \\
|\vec{v}|&=\sqrt{2^2+k^2+1^2}=\sqrt{k^2+5}
\end{aligned}" />

Two facts decide the statements. Two nonzero vectors are perpendicular exactly when their dot product is zero. The angle between them is acute exactly when the dot product is positive, because <InlineMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=|\vec{u}||\vec{v}|\cos\theta" /> and lengths are always positive.

1. The statement that some <InlineMath math="k" /> makes <InlineMath math="\vec{u}" /> parallel to <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" /> is false. Parallel means <InlineMath math="\vec{v}=c\vec{u}" /> for some number <InlineMath math="c" />. The first component forces <InlineMath math="2=c\cdot1" />, so <InlineMath math="c=2" />. The third component would then need <InlineMath math="1=2\cdot(-2)=-4" />, which is impossible for every <InlineMath math="k" />.
2. The statement about <InlineMath math="k=0" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=2\cdot0=0" /> and neither vector is zero.
3. The statement about <InlineMath math="k=4" /> is true. The dot product is <InlineMath math="2\cdot4=8>0" />, so <InlineMath math="\cos\theta>0" /> and the angle is acute.
4. The statement about <InlineMath math="k=2" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="|\vec{v}|=\sqrt{4+5}=3=|\vec{u}|" />.
5. The statement about <InlineMath math="k=-1" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=2\cdot(-1)=-2" />, not <InlineMath math="2" />. The value <InlineMath math="2" /> comes from giving the term <InlineMath math="(-2)\cdot1" /> a positive sign.

So the true statements are those about <InlineMath math="k=0" /> (perpendicular), <InlineMath math="k=4" /> (acute angle), and <InlineMath math="k=2" /> (equal lengths).

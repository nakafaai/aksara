# Given

**The quartic polynomial** **<InlineMath math="Q(x)" />** comes with five statements about its factors, roots, and remainders.

<BlockMath math="Q(x) = x^4 - 5x^3 + 5x^2 + 5x - 6" />

# Asked

**All true statements** about the polynomial <InlineMath math="Q(x)" />.

# Solution

> Once you split the polynomial into four clean linear factors, every claim about roots, factors, and remainders can be checked on sight.

Most of the statements depend on the roots of <InlineMath math="Q(x)" />, so the most efficient step is to **factor <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> completely**. The leading coefficient is <InlineMath math="1" />, so every integer root divides the constant term <InlineMath math="-6" />: it is <InlineMath math="\pm1" />, <InlineMath math="\pm2" />, <InlineMath math="\pm3" />, or <InlineMath math="\pm6" />.

## Factoring

Test <InlineMath math="x=1" />: <InlineMath math="Q(1)=0" />, so **<InlineMath math="x-1" /> is a factor** of <InlineMath math="Q(x)" />. Dividing <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> by <InlineMath math="x-1" /> leaves a cubic quotient. Look for the next integer root in that quotient: <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> works because <InlineMath math="0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
Q(1)=1-5+5+5-6=0 \\
-1-4-1+6=0 \\
\begin{aligned}
Q(x)&=(x-1)(x^3-4x^2+x+6) \\
&=(x-1)(x+1)(x^2-5x+6) \\
&=(x-1)(x+1)(x-2)(x-3)
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="Q(x)" />. **The graph crosses the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at exactly four points, three of them on the positive side**, and the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x-4" /> is the graph's height at <InlineMath math="x=4" />.

<BlockMath math="Q(x)=(x-1)(x+1)(x-2)(x-3)" />

The roots of <InlineMath math="Q(x)=0" /> are <InlineMath math="-1" />, <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, and <InlineMath math="3" />. Multiplying the four factors back together does give the expression below.

<BlockMath math="x^4-5x^3+5x^2+5x-6" />

## Judging each statement

1. The statement that <InlineMath math="x+1" /> is a factor of <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> is true. It appears in the factorization above, and <InlineMath math="0" />.

   <BlockMath math="Q(-1)=1+5+5-5-6=0" />

2. The statement that <InlineMath math="x+2" /> is a factor of <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> is false, because <InlineMath math="0" />.

   <BlockMath math="Q(-2)=16+40+20-10-6=60\ne0" />

3. The statement about the remainder on division by <InlineMath math="x-4" /> is true. By the remainder theorem, the remainder is <InlineMath math="Q(4)=30" />.

   <BlockMath math="Q(4)=(4-1)(4+1)(4-2)(4-3)=3\cdot5\cdot2\cdot1=30" />

4. The statement that there are exactly two positive roots is false. There are **three** positive roots: <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, and <InlineMath math="3" />.
5. The statement about the product of the roots is true. The product of the four roots is <InlineMath math="-6" />. This agrees with the product-of-roots formula for a quartic, <InlineMath math="-6" />.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} (-1)\cdot1\cdot2\cdot3=-6 \\ \frac{e}{a}=\frac{-6}{1}=-6 \end{gathered}" />

# Answer

Therefore, the correct choices are **statements <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="3" />, and <InlineMath math="5" />**.

# Given

A circle has **center** <InlineMath math="(3, -2)" /> and is **tangent to the line** the equation below.

<BlockMath math="4x - 3y + 2 = 0" />

# Asked

The **equation of the circle**.

# Solution

> A tangent line touches the circle at one point, so the radius equals the exact perpendicular distance from the center to that line.

The center is already known, so only **the radius** is missing. A tangent line touches the circle at one point, and the radius to that point is perpendicular to the line. So the radius equals **the distance from the center to the tangent line**.

The distance from a point <InlineMath math="(x_0,y_0)" /> to the line given below is the expression below. For the center <InlineMath math="(3,-2)" /> and the line given below, watch the sign of the term <InlineMath math="-3y" /> when <InlineMath math="y=-2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
ax+by+c=0 \\
\frac{|ax_0+by_0+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}} \\
4x-3y+2=0 \\
r=\frac{|4\cdot3-3\cdot(-2)+2|}{\sqrt{4^2+(-3)^2}}=\frac{|12+6+2|}{\sqrt{25}}=\frac{20}{5}=4
\end{gathered}" />

A circle with center <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r" /> has the equation below. Substitute the center and radius, then expand into general form.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2 \\
\begin{aligned}
(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2&=16 \\
x^2-6x+9+y^2+4y+4&=16 \\
x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3&=0
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

As a check, the point of tangency is <InlineMath math="\left(-\frac15,\frac25\right)" />. It lies on the line, because <InlineMath math="0" />, and its distance from the center is <InlineMath math="4" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
4\left(-\frac15\right)-3\cdot\frac25+2=-\frac45-\frac65+2=0 \\
\sqrt{\left(\frac{16}{5}\right)^2+\left(\frac{12}{5}\right)^2}=\sqrt{\frac{400}{25}}=4
\end{gathered}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- the equation below is the circle with center <InlineMath math="(-3,2)" />. The signs of the center coordinates were reversed when writing the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} x^2+y^2+6x-4y-3=0 \\ (x-a)^2+(y-b)^2 \end{gathered}" />

- the equation below comes from the equation below, with the radius <InlineMath math="4" /> written on the right side without squaring it.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} x^2+y^2-6x+4y+9=0 \\ (x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=4 \end{gathered}" />

- the equation below comes from <InlineMath math="r^2=20" />. The numerator of the distance formula, <InlineMath math="20" />, was used as <InlineMath math="r^2" /> without dividing by <InlineMath math="5" />.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} x^2+y^2-6x+4y-7=0 \\ \sqrt{25}=5 \end{gathered}" />

- the equation below comes from <InlineMath math="r=5" />, mistaking the length <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4^2+(-3)^2}" /> in the denominator of the distance formula for the radius.

  <BlockMath math="x^2+y^2-6x+4y-12=0" />

# Answer

So the equation of the circle is the equation below.

<BlockMath math="x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3=0" />

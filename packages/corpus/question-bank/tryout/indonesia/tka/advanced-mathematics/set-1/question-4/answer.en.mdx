This is a **linear programming** problem: the profit must be as large as possible while cutting hours and painting hours are limited. Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the number of chairs and <InlineMath math="y" /> the number of tables made in one month.

Each limit on working hours becomes one inequality. A chair uses <InlineMath math="2" /> cutting hours and a table uses <InlineMath math="3" />, so the total cutting time is <InlineMath math="2x+3y" />. In the same way, the total painting time is <InlineMath math="x+3y" />. The numbers of pieces cannot be negative either.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2x+3y&\le120 \\
x+3y&\le90 \\
x\ge0,\quad y&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The profit to maximize is the objective function <InlineMath math="f(x,y)=150{,}000x+250{,}000y" /> rupiah. The maximum of a linear function on the feasible region is always reached at one of its **corner points**, so find those corner points.

The intersection of the lines <InlineMath math="2x+3y=120" /> and <InlineMath math="x+3y=90" /> comes from elimination. Subtract the second equation from the first, then substitute the value of <InlineMath math="x" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(2x+3y)-(x+3y)&=120-90 \\
x&=30 \\
30+3y&=90 \\
y&=20
\end{aligned}" />

The line <InlineMath math="2x+3y=120" /> meets the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at <InlineMath math="(60,0)" />, and the line <InlineMath math="x+3y=90" /> meets the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis at <InlineMath math="(0,30)" />. The point <InlineMath math="(0,40)" /> on the cutting line is not feasible, because its painting needs <InlineMath math="3\cdot40=120" /> hours, more than <InlineMath math="90" /> hours.

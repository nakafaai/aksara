The image line consists of the points <InlineMath math="(x',y')" /> whose **preimage lies on <InlineMath math="g" />**. So express <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="x'" /> and <InlineMath math="y'" /> with the inverse matrix.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\det M&=3\cdot2-1\cdot5=1 \\
M^{-1}&=\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -5 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \\
x=2x'-y',&\qquad y=-5x'+3y'
\end{aligned}" />

Substitute both into the equation of <InlineMath math="g" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2(2x'-y')-(-5x'+3y')+1&=0 \\
4x'-2y'+5x'-3y'+1&=0 \\
9x'-5y'+1&=0
\end{aligned}" />

- <InlineMath math="x+1=0" /> substitutes <InlineMath math="M" /> instead of <InlineMath math="M^{-1}" />, that is, <InlineMath math="x=3x'+y'" /> and <InlineMath math="y=5x'+2y'" />.

- <InlineMath math="x+y-1=0" /> comes from a wrong inverse in which the off-diagonal entries keep their signs.
- <InlineMath math="5x-9y-1=0" /> swaps the roles of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> in the correct equation.

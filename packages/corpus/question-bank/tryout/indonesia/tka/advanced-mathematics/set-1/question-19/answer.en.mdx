Each point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> on the line <InlineMath math="g" /> is mapped to a point <InlineMath math="(x',y')" />. The image equation is the condition that <InlineMath math="(x',y')" /> satisfies. The known condition is written in <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, so **express <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="x'" /> and <InlineMath math="y'" />**, then substitute.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} x+2y \\ y \end{pmatrix}" />

The second row gives <InlineMath math="y=y'" />. The first row gives <InlineMath math="x=x'-2y=x'-2y'" />. This is the same as multiplying by <InlineMath math="M^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />. Substitute into the equation of <InlineMath math="g" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2(x'-2y')-y'+4&=0 \\
2x'-4y'-y'+4&=0 \\
2x'-5y'+4&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Check with two points on <InlineMath math="g" />. The point <InlineMath math="(0,4)" /> maps to <InlineMath math="(0+2\cdot4,4)=(8,4)" />, and <InlineMath math="2\cdot8-5\cdot4+4=0" />. The point <InlineMath math="(-2,0)" /> maps to itself, and <InlineMath math="2\cdot(-2)-5\cdot0+4=0" />. Both images lie on the line found.

So the equation of the image of <InlineMath math="g" /> is <InlineMath math="2x-5y+4=0" />.

- <InlineMath math="2x+3y+4=0" /> comes from applying <InlineMath math="M" /> itself to <InlineMath math="(x',y')" />, that is, <InlineMath math="x=x'+2y'" />, although the inverse is needed.
- <InlineMath math="2x-5y-4=0" /> has the wrong sign on the constant term. The linear transformation does not change the constant term of this equation.
- <InlineMath math="5x-2y+4=0" /> swaps the coefficients of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />. The point <InlineMath math="(8,4)" /> does not satisfy it, because <InlineMath math="40-8+4\ne0" />.
- <InlineMath math="4x-y+4=0" /> comes from using the transpose <InlineMath math="M^T=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />, so that <InlineMath math="y'=2x+y" />.

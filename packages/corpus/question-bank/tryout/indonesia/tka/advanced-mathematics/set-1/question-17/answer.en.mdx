1. The statement that <InlineMath math="L_1" /> and <InlineMath math="L_2" /> meet at two points is false. Two intersection points need a center distance smaller than <InlineMath math="r_1+r_2" />, and here the distance is exactly <InlineMath math="r_1+r_2" />.
2. The statement about the point of tangency <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{12}{5},\frac95\right)" /> is true. The point of tangency lies on the segment <InlineMath math="OP" /> at distance <InlineMath math="r_1=3" /> from <InlineMath math="O" />, that is, at <InlineMath math="\frac35(4,3)=\left(\frac{12}{5},\frac95\right)" />. It satisfies both equations: <InlineMath math="\frac{144}{25}+\frac{81}{25}=9" /> and <InlineMath math="\left(-\frac85\right)^2+\left(-\frac65\right)^2=\frac{100}{25}=4" />.
3. The statement about the line <InlineMath math="4x+3y=15" /> is true. The distance from <InlineMath math="O" /> to the line is <InlineMath math="\frac{|0+0-15|}{5}=3=r_1" />, and the distance from <InlineMath math="P" /> to the line is <InlineMath math="\frac{|16+9-15|}{5}=2=r_2" />.

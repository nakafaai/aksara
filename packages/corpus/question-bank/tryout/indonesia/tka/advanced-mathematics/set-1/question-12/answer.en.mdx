The given facts are the lengths of three vectors: <InlineMath math="|\vec{F}_1|=30" />, <InlineMath math="|\vec{F}_2|=50" />, and <InlineMath math="|\vec{F}_1+\vec{F}_2|=70" />, all in newtons. The relation that links the length of the resultant to the angle between the two vectors is **the squared length of a sum of two vectors**, which contains the dot product <InlineMath math="\vec{F}_1\cdot\vec{F}_2=|\vec{F}_1||\vec{F}_2|\cos\theta" />.

<BlockMath math="|\vec{F}_1+\vec{F}_2|^2=|\vec{F}_1|^2+|\vec{F}_2|^2+2|\vec{F}_1||\vec{F}_2|\cos\theta" />

Substitute the three magnitudes and solve for <InlineMath math="\cos\theta" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
70^2&=30^2+50^2+2\cdot30\cdot50\cos\theta \\
4{,}900&=3{,}400+3{,}000\cos\theta \\
\cos\theta&=\frac{1{,}500}{3{,}000}=\frac12
\end{aligned}" />

The angle between two vectors lies between <InlineMath math="0^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, and on that interval only <InlineMath math="\theta=60^\circ" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="\cos\theta=\frac12" />. The result makes sense. If the two forces were perpendicular, the resultant would be <InlineMath math="\sqrt{3{,}400}\approx58\text{ N}" />. A resultant of <InlineMath math="70\text{ N}" /> is larger than that, so the angle must be acute.

So the angle between <InlineMath math="\vec{F}_1" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{F}_2" /> is <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />.

- <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> comes from linking the value <InlineMath math="\frac12" /> to sine, because <InlineMath math="\sin30^\circ=\frac12" />. Here it is the cosine that equals <InlineMath math="\frac12" />.
- <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> assumes that the forces are perpendicular. At that angle the resultant is only <InlineMath math="\sqrt{30^2+50^2}=\sqrt{3{,}400}\approx58\text{ N}" />, not <InlineMath math="70\text{ N}" />.
- <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> comes from using the law of cosines for an interior angle of a triangle, with a minus sign before <InlineMath math="2|\vec{F}_1||\vec{F}_2|\cos\theta" />. That <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> is the angle of the triangle opposite the resultant, not the angle between the forces.
- <InlineMath math="150^\circ" /> has the same sine as <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />, since <InlineMath math="\sin150^\circ=\frac12" />. The same sine mix-up gives <InlineMath math="150^\circ" /> when the obtuse angle is taken.

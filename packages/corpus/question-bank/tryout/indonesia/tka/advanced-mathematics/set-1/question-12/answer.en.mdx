Only lengths are given. The bridge to the dot product is the **square of the length of a vector sum**, because <InlineMath math="|\vec u+\vec v|^2=(\vec u+\vec v)\cdot(\vec u+\vec v)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
|\vec u+\vec v|^2&=|\vec u|^2+2\,\vec u\cdot\vec v+|\vec v|^2 \\
(2\sqrt7)^2&=16+2\,\vec u\cdot\vec v+36 \\
28&=52+2\,\vec u\cdot\vec v \\
\vec u\cdot\vec v&=-12
\end{aligned}" />

The angle follows from <InlineMath math="\vec u\cdot\vec v=|\vec u||\vec v|\cos\theta" />.

<BlockMath math="\cos\theta=\frac{-12}{4\cdot6}=-\frac12\quad\Rightarrow\quad\theta=120^\circ" />

As a check with coordinates, for <InlineMath math="\vec u=(4,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec v=(-3,3\sqrt3)" />, <InlineMath math="\vec u+\vec v=(1,3\sqrt3)" />, so <InlineMath math="|\vec u+\vec v|^2=1+27=28" />.

So the dot product is <InlineMath math="-12" /> and the angle is <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.

- <InlineMath math="12" /> and <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> come from the wrong sign, that is, from <InlineMath math="|\vec u|^2+|\vec v|^2-2\,\vec u\cdot\vec v" />. That form belongs to <InlineMath math="|\vec u-\vec v|^2" />.
- <InlineMath math="-12" /> and <InlineMath math="150^\circ" /> have the right dot product, but <InlineMath math="\cos150^\circ=-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}" />, not <InlineMath math="-\frac12" />.
- <InlineMath math="-24" /> and <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> forget the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> in front of <InlineMath math="\vec u\cdot\vec v" />.
- <InlineMath math="24" /> and <InlineMath math="0^\circ" /> combine both mistakes.

The four statements test **rules for <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> matrices**: the determinant of a multiple, a sum, the inverse and the determinant of a product. Compute the two determinants first, because they are used more than once.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\det A&=2\cdot3-(-1)\cdot1=7 \\
\det B&=1\cdot0-2\cdot(-1)=2
\end{aligned}" />

1. The statement <InlineMath math="\det(2A)=14" /> is false. The factor <InlineMath math="2" /> sits in both rows, so <InlineMath math="\det(2A)=2^2\det A=4\cdot7=28" />.
2. The statement about <InlineMath math="A+B" /> is true, because matrices are added entry by entry.
3. The statement about <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> is false. For the inverse, the entries on the main diagonal swap places, and the entries on the other diagonal only change sign.
4. The statement <InlineMath math="\det(AB)=14" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="\det(AB)=\det A\cdot\det B=7\cdot2=14" />.

The inverse and the product as a check:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A^{-1}&=\frac17\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \\
AB&=\begin{pmatrix} 2\cdot1+(-1)\cdot(-1) & 2\cdot2+(-1)\cdot0 \\ 1\cdot1+3\cdot(-1) & 1\cdot2+3\cdot0 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det(AB)=6+8=14
\end{aligned}" />

So the statements about <InlineMath math="A+B" /> and <InlineMath math="\det(AB)" /> are true, and the statements about <InlineMath math="\det(2A)" /> and <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> are false.

All three statements are about the matrices <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> **given in the question**, not about any matrices at all. So a general rule isn't enough: a statement that's usually false can hold for one particular pair, and the other way round. First work out the pieces you'll use more than once.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
AB&=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\3&4\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}4&6\\11&14\end{pmatrix} \\
BA&=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\3&4\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}8&9\\10&10\end{pmatrix} \\
\det A&=2\cdot4-1\cdot3=5,\qquad \det B=1\cdot2-2\cdot2=-2
\end{aligned}" />

# Transpose of a product

First statement: **False**. The rule that always holds is <InlineMath math="(AB)^T=B^TA^T" />, with the order reversed. The right side of the statement, <InlineMath math="A^TB^T" />, equals <InlineMath math="(BA)^T" />. So the statement is only true when <InlineMath math="AB=BA" />, and the products above show <InlineMath math="AB\neq BA" />.

<BlockMath math="(AB)^T=\begin{pmatrix}4&11\\6&14\end{pmatrix},\qquad A^TB^T=(BA)^T=\begin{pmatrix}8&10\\9&10\end{pmatrix}" />

# Determinant of a sum

Second statement: **True**. In general a determinant doesn't split over a sum, but for these two matrices the equality happens to hold. Just compute both sides.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A+B&=\begin{pmatrix}3&3\\5&6\end{pmatrix} \\
\det(A+B)&=3\cdot6-3\cdot5=3 \\
\det A+\det B&=5+(-2)=3
\end{aligned}" />

Both sides equal <InlineMath math="3" />. If you answered false straight away because you remembered the general rule, you missed that **the question is about these exact matrices**.

# True for every real number

Third statement: **False**. The words **for every** mean one failing example is enough to knock it down. Work out <InlineMath math="\det(A+kB)" /> as a function of <InlineMath math="k" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A+kB&=\begin{pmatrix}2+k&1+2k\\3+2k&4+2k\end{pmatrix} \\
\det(A+kB)&=(2+k)(4+2k)-(1+2k)(3+2k) \\
&=(8+8k+2k^2)-(3+8k+4k^2) \\
&=5-2k^2
\end{aligned}" />

The right side of the statement is <InlineMath math="\det A+k\det B=5-2k" />. The two agree only when <InlineMath math="2k^2=2k" />, that is, <InlineMath math="k=0" /> or <InlineMath math="k=1" />. The value <InlineMath math="k=1" /> is exactly the second statement, so its truth doesn't carry over to every <InlineMath math="k" />. A counterexample is <InlineMath math="k=2" />:

<BlockMath math="\det(A+2B)=5-8=-3,\qquad \det A+2\det B=5-4=1" />

So the first statement is false, the second is true, and the third is false.

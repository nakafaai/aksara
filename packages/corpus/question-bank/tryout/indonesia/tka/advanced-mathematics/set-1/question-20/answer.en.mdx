A transformation by a matrix <InlineMath math="M" /> multiplies every area by **the absolute value of the determinant** of <InlineMath math="M" />. So it is enough to find the area of the original triangle and the determinant of the matrix. The absolute value is needed because a negative determinant only signals that the order of the vertices is reversed, while an area is always positive.

The side <InlineMath math="AB" /> is horizontal, because <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> both have y-coordinate <InlineMath math="1" />. Its length is <InlineMath math="4-1=3" />, and the height of the triangle on that side is the distance from <InlineMath math="C" /> to the line <InlineMath math="y=1" />, that is, <InlineMath math="5-1=4" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{ABC}&=\frac12\cdot3\cdot4=6 \\
\det M&=3\cdot(-1)-1\cdot1=-4 \\
A_{A'B'C'}&=|\det M|\cdot A_{ABC}=4\cdot6=24
\end{aligned}" />

Check by computing the image of each vertex and using the coordinate formula for the area of a triangle.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A'&=(3\cdot1+1,\ 1-1)=(4,0) \\
B'&=(3\cdot4+1,\ 4-1)=(13,3) \\
C'&=(3\cdot2+5,\ 2-5)=(11,-3) \\
A_{A'B'C'}&=\frac12\left|4(3-(-3))+13(-3-0)+11(0-3)\right|=\frac12|24-39-33|=24
\end{aligned}" />

So the area of the image triangle is <InlineMath math="24" /> square units.

- <InlineMath math="-24" /> uses the determinant without the absolute value. An area is never negative.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> is the area of the original triangle. The effect of the transformation is missing.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> comes from **adding** the area and the size of the determinant, <InlineMath math="6+4" />. The determinant is a factor that multiplies the area.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> uses the sum of the main diagonal, <InlineMath math="3+(-1)=2" />, as the factor. The area factor is <InlineMath math="|\det M|" />, not the diagonal sum.

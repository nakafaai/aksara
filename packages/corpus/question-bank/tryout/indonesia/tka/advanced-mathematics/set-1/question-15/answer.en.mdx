A line touches a circle exactly when **its distance from the centre equals the radius**. So first find the centre and the radius by completing the square.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x^2-6x+9)+(y^2+2y+1)&=15+9+1 \\
(x-3)^2+(y+1)^2&=25
\end{aligned}" />

The centre is <InlineMath math="(3,-1)" /> and the radius is <InlineMath math="5" />. The given line has slope <InlineMath math="\frac34" />, so a perpendicular line has slope <InlineMath math="-\frac43" />. Such lines have the form <InlineMath math="4x+3y+c=0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{|4\cdot3+3\cdot(-1)+c|}{\sqrt{4^2+3^2}}&=5 \\
|9+c|&=25 \\
c=16\ \text{or}\ c&=-34
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, the point of contact <InlineMath math="(7,2)" /> lies on the circle, because <InlineMath math="4^2+3^2=25" />, and on the tangent, because <InlineMath math="28+6-34=0" />.

So among the options, <InlineMath math="4x+3y-34=0" /> is one of the required tangents.

- <InlineMath math="4x+3y+34=0" /> has the wrong sign. Its distance from the centre is <InlineMath math="\frac{43}{5}" />, not <InlineMath math="5" />.
- <InlineMath math="3x-4y+12=0" /> does touch the circle, but it is parallel to the given line instead of perpendicular.
- <InlineMath math="4x+3y-9=0" /> passes through the centre and therefore cuts the circle at two points.
- <InlineMath math="4x+3y-4=0" /> comes from <InlineMath math="|9+c|=5" />, when the radius is not multiplied by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5" />. Its distance from the centre is only <InlineMath math="1" />.

A line touches a circle exactly when **its distance from the centre equals the radius**. So first find the centre and the radius by completing the square.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x^2+6x+9)+(y^2-8y+16)&=9+16 \\
(x+3)^2+(y-4)^2&=25
\end{aligned}" />

The centre is <InlineMath math="(-3,4)" /> and the radius is <InlineMath math="5" />. The given line has slope <InlineMath math="\frac34" />, so a perpendicular line has slope <InlineMath math="-\frac43" />. Such lines have the form <InlineMath math="4x+3y+c=0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{|4\cdot(-3)+3\cdot4+c|}{\sqrt{4^2+3^2}}&=5 \\
|c|&=25 \\
c=25\ \text{or}\ c&=-25
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, the point of contact <InlineMath math="(1,7)" /> lies on the circle, because <InlineMath math="(1+3)^2+(7-4)^2=16+9=25" />, and on the tangent, because <InlineMath math="4+21-25=0" />.

So among the options, <InlineMath math="4x+3y-25=0" /> is one of the required tangents.

- <InlineMath math="3x-4y=0" /> does touch the circle at the origin, but it is parallel to the given line instead of perpendicular.
- <InlineMath math="4x+3y=0" /> passes through the centre, because <InlineMath math="-12+12=0" />, and therefore cuts the circle at two points.
- <InlineMath math="4x+3y-5=0" /> comes from <InlineMath math="|c|=5" />, when the radius is not multiplied by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5" />. Its distance from the centre is only <InlineMath math="1" />.
- <InlineMath math="4x-3y-25=0" /> has slope <InlineMath math="\frac43" /> instead of <InlineMath math="-\frac43" />. It is not perpendicular to the given line, and its distance from the centre is <InlineMath math="\frac{49}{5}" />.

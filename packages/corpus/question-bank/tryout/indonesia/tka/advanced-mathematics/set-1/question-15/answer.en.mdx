A line touches a circle exactly when **its distance from the centre equals the radius**. So first find the centre and the radius by completing the square.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(x^2+6x+9)+(y^2-8y+16)&=9+16 \\
(x+3)^2+(y-4)^2&=25
\end{aligned}" />

The centre is <InlineMath math="(-3,4)" /> and the radius is <InlineMath math="5" />. The given line has slope <InlineMath math="\frac43" />, so a perpendicular line has slope <InlineMath math="-\frac34" />. Such lines have the form <InlineMath math="3x+4y+c=0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{|3\cdot(-3)+4\cdot4+c|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}&=5 \\
|7+c|&=25 \\
c=18\ \text{or}\ c&=-32
\end{aligned}" />

As a check, the point of contact <InlineMath math="(0,8)" /> lies on the circle, because <InlineMath math="(0+3)^2+(8-4)^2=9+16=25" />, and on the tangent, because <InlineMath math="0+32-32=0" />.

So among the options, <InlineMath math="3x+4y-32=0" /> is one of the required tangents.

- <InlineMath math="3x+4y+32=0" /> has the wrong sign. Its distance from the centre is <InlineMath math="\frac{39}{5}" />, not <InlineMath math="5" />.
- <InlineMath math="4x-3y-1=0" /> does touch the circle, but it is parallel to the given line instead of perpendicular.
- <InlineMath math="3x+4y-7=0" /> passes through the centre and therefore cuts the circle at two points.
- <InlineMath math="3x+4y-2=0" /> comes from <InlineMath math="|7+c|=5" />, when the radius is not multiplied by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5" />. Its distance from the centre is only <InlineMath math="1" />.

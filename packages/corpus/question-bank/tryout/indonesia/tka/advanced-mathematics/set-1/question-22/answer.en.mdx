# Given

**The radical limit** **the expression below** produces zero over zero at <InlineMath math="x = 4" />.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to 4} \frac{x - 4}{\sqrt{2x + 1} - 3}" />

# Asked

The **value of the limit** as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="4" />.

# Solution

> Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the square-root denominator so the root clears and the shared zero factor cancels cleanly.

Substituting <InlineMath math="x=4" /> gives the numerator <InlineMath math="0" /> and the denominator <InlineMath math="\sqrt9-3=0" />. The denominator contains a root, so its factor <InlineMath math="x-4" /> is not visible yet. The right move is to **multiply by the conjugate** <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2x+1}+3" />, because the equation below, with <InlineMath math="p=2x+1" />, removes the root.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
(\sqrt{p}-3)(\sqrt{p}+3)=p-9 \\
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x-4}{\sqrt{2x+1}-3}&=\frac{x-4}{\sqrt{2x+1}-3}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2x+1}+3}{\sqrt{2x+1}+3} \\
&=\frac{(x-4)(\sqrt{2x+1}+3)}{(2x+1)-9} \\
&=\frac{(x-4)(\sqrt{2x+1}+3)}{2(x-4)}
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

The denominator <InlineMath math="2x-8" /> turns out to be <InlineMath math="2(x-4)" />. For <InlineMath math="x\ne4" />, the factor <InlineMath math="x-4" /> may be cancelled, and then direct substitution is safe.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to4}\frac{\sqrt{2x+1}+3}{2}=\frac{\sqrt9+3}{2}=\frac{3+3}{2}=3" />

Draw the original function near <InlineMath math="x=4" />. **After multiplying by the conjugate, its graph is the same as the expression below for <InlineMath math="x\ne4" />**, so its value heads to <InlineMath math="3" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{\sqrt{2x+1}+3}{2}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="0" /> comes from looking only at the numerator. The denominator also approaches zero, so the form is <InlineMath math="\frac00" />, not zero.
- <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> is the reciprocal of the result, the limit of the expression below.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{\sqrt{2x+1}-3}{x-4}" />

- <InlineMath math="\frac32" /> comes from writing the remaining expression as the expression below, forgetting the <InlineMath math="+3" /> of the conjugate.

  <BlockMath math="\frac{\sqrt{2x+1}}{2}" />

- <InlineMath math="6" /> comes from treating <InlineMath math="(2x+1)-9" /> as <InlineMath math="x-4" />, which loses the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> in the denominator.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="3" />**.

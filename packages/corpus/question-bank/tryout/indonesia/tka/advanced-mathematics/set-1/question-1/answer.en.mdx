The equation compares two determinants, so the first step is to **turn each determinant into an algebraic expression in** <InlineMath math="x" />. After that, the determinant equation becomes an ordinary quadratic equation.

The determinant of a <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> matrix is the product of the main diagonal minus the product of the other diagonal.

<BlockMath math="\begin{vmatrix} x & 2 \\ 3 & x-1 \end{vmatrix}=x(x-1)-2\cdot3=x^2-x-6" />

Expand the <InlineMath math="3\times3" /> determinant along its first row. The middle entry of that row is <InlineMath math="0" />, so only two cofactors need to be calculated. The cofactor signs along the first row are positive, negative, positive.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & x & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix}&=1\begin{vmatrix} x & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix}-0\begin{vmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix}+2\begin{vmatrix} 0 & x \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} \\
&=1(3x-1)-0+2(0\cdot1-x\cdot1) \\
&=3x-1-2x=x-1
\end{aligned}" />

Set the two results equal and move every term to the left side.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x^2-x-6&=x-1 \\
x^2-2x-5&=0
\end{aligned}" />

The discriminant of this quadratic is <InlineMath math="D=(-2)^2-4(1)(-5)=24" />. Since <InlineMath math="D>0" />, there are two different real values of <InlineMath math="x" />, namely <InlineMath math="x=1+\sqrt6" /> and <InlineMath math="x=1-\sqrt6" />. The sum-of-roots formula gives <InlineMath math="-\frac{b}{a}=-\frac{-2}{1}=2" />, and adding the roots directly gives the same value, <InlineMath math="(1+\sqrt6)+(1-\sqrt6)=2" />.

So the sum of all real values of <InlineMath math="x" /> that satisfy the equation is <InlineMath math="2" />.

Write both reflections as matrices. In a composition, **the matrix applied first stands on the right**, because it acts on the point first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S_x&=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix},\qquad S_{y=-x}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \\
T&=S_{y=-x}\,S_x=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}" />

The matrix <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" /> maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(y,-x)" />. That is the rotation by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise. Two reflections in lines through <InlineMath math="O" /> always give a rotation, here by twice the <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> angle between the mirror lines.

As a check with one point, <InlineMath math="P(3,1)\to P'(3,-1)\to P''(1,-3)" />, and the clockwise rotation also sends <InlineMath math="(3,1)" /> to <InlineMath math="(1,-3)" />.

So <InlineMath math="T" /> is the rotation by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> clockwise about <InlineMath math="O" />.

- The anticlockwise rotation comes from the wrong order, that is, from <InlineMath math="S_x\,S_{y=-x}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />.
- It cannot be a reflection, because <InlineMath math="\det T=1" />. Two reflections reverse the orientation twice and so keep it.
- A rotation by <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> sends <InlineMath math="P(3,1)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-3,-1)" />, not to <InlineMath math="(1,-3)" />.
- The reflection in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis sends <InlineMath math="P(3,1)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-3,1)" /> and has determinant <InlineMath math="-1" />.

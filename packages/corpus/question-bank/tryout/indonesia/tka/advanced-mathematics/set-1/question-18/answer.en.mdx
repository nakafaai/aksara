Write both transformations as matrices. In a composition, **the matrix applied first stands on the right**, because it acts on the point first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S_{y=x}&=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},\qquad R_{90^\circ}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \\
T&=R_{90^\circ}\,S_{y=x}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}" />

The matrix <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" /> maps <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-x,y)" />. That is the reflection in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis. Its determinant is <InlineMath math="-1" />, as it must be for a reflection combined with a rotation: the result is always a reflection, never a rotation.

As a check with one point, <InlineMath math="P(3,1)\to P'(1,3)\to P''(-3,1)" />, and the reflection in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis also maps <InlineMath math="(3,1)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-3,1)" />.

So <InlineMath math="T" /> is the reflection in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis.

- The reflection in the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis comes from the reversed order, <InlineMath math="S_{y=x}\,R_{90^\circ}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />.
- The clockwise rotation by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> has determinant <InlineMath math="1" />, but <InlineMath math="\det T=-1" />.
- The reflection in the line <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> maps <InlineMath math="P(3,1)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-1,-3)" />, not to <InlineMath math="(-3,1)" />.
- The rotation by <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> maps <InlineMath math="P(3,1)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-3,-1)" /> and has determinant <InlineMath math="1" />.

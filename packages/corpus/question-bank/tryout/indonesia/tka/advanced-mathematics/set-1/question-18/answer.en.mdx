Write both maps as matrices. In a composition, **the matrix applied first stands on the right**, because it acts on the point first.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S_x&=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix},\qquad R_{90^\circ}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \\
T&=R_{90^\circ}\,S_x=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}" />

The matrix <InlineMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" /> swaps the coordinates, so it is the reflection in the line <InlineMath math="y=x" />.

As a check with one point, <InlineMath math="P(3,1)\to P'(3,-1)\to P''(1,3)" />. The result has exactly the swapped coordinates of <InlineMath math="P" />.

So <InlineMath math="T" /> is the reflection in the line <InlineMath math="y=x" />.

- The reflection in <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> comes from the wrong order, that is, from <InlineMath math="S_x\,R_{90^\circ}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />.
- A clockwise rotation by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> keeps the orientation. A reflection followed by a rotation reverses it, because <InlineMath math="\det T=-1" />.
- A rotation by <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> also has determinant <InlineMath math="1" /> and sends <InlineMath math="P(3,1)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-3,-1)" />.
- The reflection in the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis sends <InlineMath math="P(3,1)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-3,1)" />, not to <InlineMath math="(1,3)" />.

# Given

# Asked

# Solution

> Compare the distance between centers against the sum and difference of radii to classify how the circles meet.

Read off the centres and radii. <InlineMath math="L_1" /> has centre <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="r_1=3" />. For <InlineMath math="L_2" />, complete the square: the equation below, so its centre is <InlineMath math="M(5,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="r_2=2" />.

<BlockMath math="(x-5)^2+y^2=4" />

The distance between the centres is <InlineMath math="OM=5" />, exactly the equation below. **A centre distance equal to the sum of the radii** means the circles touch externally.

<BlockMath math="r_1+r_2=3+2" />

Draw both circles and every line that touches both. **The circles touch at <InlineMath math="(3,0)" />, so there are three common tangents**: two outer lines and one vertical line at the point of contact.

1. **True.** <InlineMath math="OM=r_1+r_2" />, so the circles touch externally.
2. **True.** The point of contact lies on segment <InlineMath math="OM" />, <InlineMath math="3" /> units from <InlineMath math="O" />, at <InlineMath math="(3,0)" />. It satisfies both equations: <InlineMath math="9+0=9" /> and <InlineMath math="0" />.

   <BlockMath math="9+0-30+21=0" />

3. **False.** Three lines touch both circles at once: two external tangents and one tangent at the point <InlineMath math="(3,0)" />, the line <InlineMath math="x=3" />.
4. **False.** Substituting <InlineMath math="x=3" /> into <InlineMath math="L_2" /> gives <InlineMath math="4+y^2=4" />, so only <InlineMath math="y=0" />. The line <InlineMath math="x=3" /> just touches <InlineMath math="L_2" /> at <InlineMath math="(3,0)" />.

# Answer

So the answers are True, True, False, False.

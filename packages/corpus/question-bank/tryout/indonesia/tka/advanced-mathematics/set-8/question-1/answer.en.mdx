# Given

We are given a **matrix multiplication equation** involving <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> matrices <InlineMath math="A" />, <InlineMath math="B" />, and <InlineMath math="C" />.

# Asked

The **unknown matrix** <InlineMath math="B" /> or its requested entry.

# Solution

> Multiply by the inverse on the exact same side as the factor because matrix multiplication order cannot be swapped.

You do not need to find <InlineMath math="B" />. Use **the determinant of a product**: it equals the product of the determinants, and the equation below.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\det\left(A^{-1}\right)=\frac{1}{\det A} \\
\det C=\det A\cdot\det B\cdot\frac{1}{\det A}=\det B
\end{gathered}" />

The diagram below follows the determinant factors from left to right. **The factors <InlineMath math="\det A" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{\det A}" /> cancel**, so only <InlineMath math="\det B" /> is left, and it must equal <InlineMath math="\det C" />.

The factors <InlineMath math="\det A" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{\det A}" /> cancel, whatever <InlineMath math="\det A" /> is. So just compute <InlineMath math="\det C" />.

<BlockMath math="\det B=\det C=2\cdot2-1\cdot1=3" />

The data on <InlineMath math="A" /> still matters to make sure <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> exists, since <InlineMath math="\det A \ne 0" />.

<BlockMath math="\det A=6-4=2\ne0" />

So <InlineMath math="\det B=3" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="12" /> multiplies <InlineMath math="\det C" /> by <InlineMath math="(\det A)^2" />, as if <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" /> also contributed a factor <InlineMath math="\det A" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac34" /> divides <InlineMath math="\det C" /> by <InlineMath math="(\det A)^2" />.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> multiplies <InlineMath math="\det C" /> by <InlineMath math="\det A" /> and forgets the factor from <InlineMath math="A^{-1}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac32" /> divides <InlineMath math="\det C" /> by <InlineMath math="\det A" /> and forgets the factor from <InlineMath math="A" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="3" />**.

# Given

Polynomial <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> has **two known remainders** when divided by two different linear factors.

# Asked

The **remainder** when <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by the product of both linear divisors.

# Solution

> A quadratic divisor leaves a linear remainder that two function values determine uniquely.

The divisor has degree two, so the remainder has the form <InlineMath math="ax+b" />, and it is fixed by <InlineMath math="Q(1)" /> and <InlineMath math="Q(3)" />. **You can find those values without knowing all of <InlineMath math="P(x)" />**, just from <InlineMath math="P(1)=2" /> and <InlineMath math="P(3)=6" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
Q(1)&=(1+1)\cdot P(1)=2\cdot2=4 \\
Q(3)&=(9+1)\cdot P(3)=10\cdot6=60
\end{aligned}" />

The remainder <InlineMath math="ax+b" /> must take the same values at those points.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+b&=4 \\
3a+b&=60 \\
2a&=56\ \Rightarrow\ a=28,\quad b=-24
\end{aligned}" />

The remainder on division by <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x-3)" /> is the line through the points of the graph of <InlineMath math="Q" /> at <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=3" />. **Those points are <InlineMath math="(1,\ 4)" /> and <InlineMath math="(3,\ 60)" />**, and the line through both is <InlineMath math="y=28x-24" />.

So the remainder when <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x-3)" /> is <InlineMath math="28x-24" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="2x" /> is the remainder of <InlineMath math="P(x)" />, not of <InlineMath math="Q(x)" />, because the factor <InlineMath math="x^2+1" /> was forgotten.
- <InlineMath math="2x^3+2x" /> multiplies the remainder of <InlineMath math="P" /> by <InlineMath math="x^2+1" />, but the result has degree three, so it is not yet a remainder on division by a quadratic.
- <InlineMath math="29x-27" /> forgets to multiply <InlineMath math="P(1)" /> by <InlineMath math="1^2+1=2" /> and uses <InlineMath math="Q(1)=2" />.
- <InlineMath math="28x+24" /> slips a sign when computing <InlineMath math="b=4-28" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="28x-24" />**.

So <InlineMath math="f(x)=7" /> or <InlineMath math="f(x)=-3" />. How many solutions each equation has depends on **the range of <InlineMath math="f" />**. Since <InlineMath math="f(x)" />, the smallest value of <InlineMath math="f" /> is <InlineMath math="-3" />, reached only at <InlineMath math="x=2" />.

<BlockMath math="f(x)=(x-2)^2-3" />

- <InlineMath math="f(x)=-3" /> means <InlineMath math="(x-2)^2=0" />, so there is exactly one solution, <InlineMath math="x=2" />.
- <InlineMath math="f(x)=7" /> means <InlineMath math="(x-2)^2=10" />, so there are two solutions, <InlineMath math="x" />.

  <BlockMath math="x=2\pm\sqrt{10}" />

All three values are different. Check: <InlineMath math="f(2)=-3" /> and <InlineMath math="22" />, while <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="f(7)=22" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
f(-3)=9+12+1=22 \\
f\left(2+\sqrt{10}\right)=10-3=7 \\
f(7)=49-28+1=22
\end{gathered}" />

So <InlineMath math="3" /> real numbers satisfy the equation.

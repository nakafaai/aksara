Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the packs of exercise books and <InlineMath math="y" /> the packs of drawing books per month. Each machine gives **one limit on working hours**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x+2y&\le80\quad(\text{cutting}) \\
3x+2y&\le120\quad(\text{binding}) \\
x\ge0,\quad y&\ge0
\end{aligned}" />

The profit, in thousands of rupiah, is <InlineMath math="P=50x+40y" />. The corner where both machines run at full capacity comes from subtracting the equations.

<BlockMath math="(3x+2y)-(x+2y)=120-80\ \Rightarrow\ 2x=40\ \Rightarrow\ x=20,\quad y=30" />

The other corners are <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="(40,0)" /> on the binding line, and <InlineMath math="(0,40)" /> on the cutting line.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(40,0)&=2{,}000 \\
P(20,30)&=1{,}000+1{,}200=2{,}200 \\
P(0,40)&=1{,}600
\end{aligned}" />

So the maximum profit is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}200{,}000.00" />, from <InlineMath math="20" /> packs of exercise books and <InlineMath math="30" /> packs of drawing books.

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}000{,}000.00" /> is the profit at <InlineMath math="(40,0)" />, exercise books only.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}600{,}000.00" /> is the profit at <InlineMath math="(0,40)" />, drawing books only.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}500{,}000.00" /> is the profit of <InlineMath math="(30,25)" />. That plan needs <InlineMath math="140" /> binding hours.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}3{,}600{,}000.00" /> adds the profits of <InlineMath math="(40,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,40)" />, as if both plans could run at once.

The notation <InlineMath math="(g\circ f)(x)" /> means <InlineMath math="g(f(x))" />: **<InlineMath math="f" /> acts first**, and its output becomes the input of <InlineMath math="g" />. So replace every <InlineMath math="x" /> in the rule for <InlineMath math="g" /> with <InlineMath math="f(x)=x^2+1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(g\circ f)(x)&=2\left(x^2+1\right)-3 \\
&=2x^2+2-3 \\
&=2x^2-1
\end{aligned}" />

Check at <InlineMath math="x=2" />: <InlineMath math="f(2)=5" /> and <InlineMath math="g(5)=7" />, while <InlineMath math="2\cdot4-1=7" />.

So <InlineMath math="(g\circ f)(x)=2x^2-1" />.

- <InlineMath math="2x^3-3x^2+2x-3" /> is the product <InlineMath math="f(x)\cdot g(x)" />, not the composition.
- <InlineMath math="2x^2-2" /> multiplies only <InlineMath math="x^2" /> by <InlineMath math="2" /> and leaves the <InlineMath math="1" /> alone.
- <InlineMath math="x^2-2" /> is <InlineMath math="f(x)-3" />, forgetting the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> in <InlineMath math="g" />.

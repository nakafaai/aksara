# Given

A **right-triangular plot** has vertices at <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="P(12,0)" />, and a third corner on the vertical axis.

# Asked

The **circumcircle or incircle** of the right-triangular plot.

# Solution

> In any right triangle, the hypotenuse is the circumcircle diameter, putting the center right at its midpoint.

The largest circle inside a triangle touching all three sides is **the incircle**. Triangle <InlineMath math="OPQ" /> has a right angle at <InlineMath math="O" />, legs <InlineMath math="12" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />, and hypotenuse <InlineMath math="PQ=13" />.

<BlockMath math="PQ=\sqrt{144+25}=13" />

The inradius equals the area divided by the semi-perimeter.

<BlockMath math="r=\frac{\frac12\cdot12\cdot5}{\frac12(12+5+13)}=\frac{30}{15}=2" />

Draw the plot and the largest pond inside it. **The pond touches all three sides, so its centre is the same distance, <InlineMath math="r=2" />, from every side**, including both axes.

The circle touches both axes, so its centre is <InlineMath math="2" /> units from each of them, at <InlineMath math="(2,2)" />.

Check against the hypotenuse: line <InlineMath math="PQ" /> is the equation below, and its distance from <InlineMath math="(2,2)" /> is <InlineMath math="2" />, equal to the radius.

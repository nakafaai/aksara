For a circle <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2+Ax+By+C=0" />, a point lies **inside the circle when the left-hand side is negative** after substituting its coordinates. Zero means exactly on the circle, and a positive value means outside.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1+k^2-4+2k-5&<0 \\
k^2+2k-8&<0 \\
(k+4)(k-2)&<0
\end{aligned}" />

The product is negative when <InlineMath math="k" /> lies between the two roots, so <InlineMath math="-4<k<2" />.

Another way: the circle has centre <InlineMath math="(2,-1)" /> and <InlineMath math="r^2=4+1+5=10" />. Being inside means <InlineMath math="(1-2)^2+(k+1)^2<10" />, that is <InlineMath math="(k+1)^2<9" />, so <InlineMath math="-3<k+1<3" />.

So <InlineMath math="-4<k<2" />.

- <InlineMath math="k<-4" /> or <InlineMath math="k>2" /> is the condition for the point to lie outside.
- <InlineMath math="-2<k<4" /> gets the signs wrong when factoring, as if it were <InlineMath math="(k-4)(k+2)" />.
- <InlineMath math="-4\leq k\leq2" /> also includes points exactly on the circle.
- <InlineMath math="k=-4" /> or <InlineMath math="k=2" /> is the condition for the point to lie exactly on the circle.

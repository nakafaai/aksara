# Given

Point <InlineMath math="P(1,k)" /> sits **strictly inside a circle** with a known general equation.

# Asked

The **range of** <InlineMath math="k" /> keeping <InlineMath math="P" /> inside the circle.

# Solution

> A point lies inside the circle precisely when plugging its coordinates into the circle expression gives a negative result.

For a circle the equation below, a point lies **inside the circle when the left-hand side is negative** after substituting its coordinates. Zero means exactly on the circle, and a positive value means outside.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
x^2+y^2+Ax+By+C=0 \\
\begin{aligned}
1+k^2-4+2k-5&<0 \\
k^2+2k-8&<0 \\
(k+4)(k-2)&<0
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Draw the circle and the vertical line <InlineMath math="x=1" />, where every point <InlineMath math="P(1,k)" /> lies. **The part of the line inside the circle runs from <InlineMath math="k=-4" /> to <InlineMath math="k=2" />**, without the two ends, which sit exactly on the circle.

The product is negative when <InlineMath math="k" /> lies between the two roots, so <InlineMath math="2" />.

<BlockMath math="-4<k<2" />

Another way: the circle has centre <InlineMath math="(2,-1)" /> and <InlineMath math="r^2=10" />. Being inside means the equation below, that is <InlineMath math="(k+1)^2<9" />, so <InlineMath math="3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
r^2=4+1+5=10 \\
(1-2)^2+(k+1)^2<10 \\
-3<k+1<3
\end{gathered}" />

So <InlineMath math="2" />.

<BlockMath math="-4<k<2" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="k<-4" /> or <InlineMath math="k>2" /> is the condition for the point to lie outside.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> gets the signs wrong when factoring, as if it were <InlineMath math="(k-4)(k+2)" />.

  <BlockMath math="-2<k<4" />

- <InlineMath math="-4\leq k\leq2" /> also includes points exactly on the circle.
- <InlineMath math="k=-4" /> or <InlineMath math="k=2" /> is the condition for the point to lie exactly on the circle.

# Given

A steel frame has **three space joints** at <InlineMath math="A(2,3,0)" />, <InlineMath math="B(1,2,0)" />, and <InlineMath math="C(1,1,1)" />.

# Asked

The **joint angle** or bar length formed by the three space points.

# Solution

> Build two rod vectors starting at the shared joint, then use their dot product to find the angle.

Angle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is the angle at <InlineMath math="B" /> between two vectors that **both start at <InlineMath math="B" />**, namely <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{BA}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{BC}" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\overrightarrow{BA}=A-B=(1,1,0),\qquad \overrightarrow{BC}=C-B=(0,-1,1) \\
\begin{aligned}
\cos\angle ABC&=\frac{\overrightarrow{BA}\cdot\overrightarrow{BC}}{\left|\overrightarrow{BA}\right|\left|\overrightarrow{BC}\right|} \\
&=\frac{0-1+0}{\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2}=-\frac12
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />
</MathContainer>

Draw the two bars in the plane that holds them. **Both bars have length <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" /> and the cosine of the angle is negative**, so the angle at <InlineMath math="B" /> is obtuse, <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.

The angle between <InlineMath math="0^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> with cosine <InlineMath math="-\frac12" /> is <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.

Check with the cosine rule: <InlineMath math="AC^2=6" />, and <InlineMath math="6" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
AC^2=1+4+1=6 \\
BA^2+BC^2-2\cdot BA\cdot BC\cos120^\circ=2+2+2=6
\end{gathered}" />

So angle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> is <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> uses <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{BC}" />. The vector <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}" /> does not start at <InlineMath math="B" />, so the sign of the cosine flips.
- <InlineMath math="135^\circ" /> uses the equation below, but <InlineMath math="\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2=2" />.

  <BlockMath math="\left|\overrightarrow{BA}\right|\left|\overrightarrow{BC}\right|=\sqrt2" />

- <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> makes both of those slips at once.
- <InlineMath math="150^\circ" /> mixes up the value <InlineMath math="\cos150^\circ" />.

  <BlockMath math="\cos150^\circ=-\frac12\sqrt3" />

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is **<InlineMath math="120^\circ" />**.

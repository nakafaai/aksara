# Given

A **straight line** is dilated from center <InlineMath math="(0,1)" /> with scale factor <InlineMath math="3" />.

# Asked

The **equation of the image line** after dilation about <InlineMath math="(0,1)" />.

# Solution

> Dilation keeps every line parallel to itself, so the slope never changes and tracking one point pins down the image.

A dilation with centre <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> and factor <InlineMath math="k" /> scales **the distance from the centre**, not the coordinates themselves.

<BlockMath math="x'-0=3(x-0),\qquad y'-1=3(y-1)" />

Write <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> in terms of <InlineMath math="x'" /> and <InlineMath math="y'" />.

<BlockMath math="x=\frac{x'}{3},\qquad y=\frac{y'-1}{3}+1=\frac{y'+2}{3}" />

Substitute into the original line and multiply through by <InlineMath math="3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{x'}{3}+2\cdot\frac{y'+2}{3}-4&=0 \\
x'+2y'+4-12&=0 \\
x'+2y'-8&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Draw the starting line, the centre of dilation, and the images of two points. **Every point is pulled away from <InlineMath math="(0,1)" /> until its distance is three times as large**, so the image is a parallel line farther from the centre.

Check: the point <InlineMath math="(4,0)" /> on the original line maps to <InlineMath math="(12,-2)" />, and <InlineMath math="0" />. The point <InlineMath math="(0,2)" /> maps to <InlineMath math="(0,4)" />, and <InlineMath math="0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
12-4-8=0 \\
0+8-8=0
\end{gathered}" />

So the image is the equation below.

<BlockMath math="x+2y-8=0" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- The equation below puts the centre of dilation at the origin.

  <BlockMath math="x+2y-12=0" />

- The equation below assumes the line does not move. A dilation leaves a line in place only if the line passes through the centre, and <InlineMath math="(0,1)" /> is not on this line.

  <BlockMath math="x+2y-4=0" />

- The equation below forgets to add the <InlineMath math="1" /> back and uses <InlineMath math="y" />.

  <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} x+2y-14=0 \\ y=\frac{y'-1}{3} \end{gathered}" />

- The equation below uses the factor <InlineMath math="\frac13" /> with the centre at the origin.

  <BlockMath math="3x+6y-4=0" />

# Given

A ball rolls straight with **position function** <InlineMath math="s(t)" /> after <InlineMath math="t" /> seconds.

# Asked

The **instantaneous velocity** found as the limit of average velocity.

# Solution

> Instantaneous velocity is simply average velocity over a time window shrunk down toward zero.

Work out the numerator first. Putting <InlineMath math="h=0" /> straight in gives <InlineMath math="\frac00" />, so **expand <InlineMath math="s(2+h)" /> so that the factor <InlineMath math="h" /> can cancel**.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
\begin{aligned}
s(2+h)&=2(4+4h+h^2)+3(2+h)=14+11h+2h^2 \\
s(2)&=8+6=14 \\
s(2+h)-s(2)&=11h+2h^2=h(11+2h)
\end{aligned} \\
\lim_{h\to0}\frac{h(11+2h)}{h}=\lim_{h\to0}(11+2h)=11
\end{gathered}" />
</MathContainer>

Draw the ball's distance against time. **The instantaneous velocity at <InlineMath math="t=2" /> is the slope of the tangent at the point <InlineMath math="(2,\ 14)" />**, and that tangent has gradient <InlineMath math="11" />.

Check: the average velocity on <InlineMath math="[2,\,2.1]" /> is <InlineMath math="11.2" />, and the shorter the interval, the closer it gets to <InlineMath math="11" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{s(2.1)-s(2)}{0.1}=\frac{15.12-14}{0.1}=11.2" />

So the ball's instantaneous velocity at <InlineMath math="t=2" /> is <InlineMath math="11" /> metres per second.

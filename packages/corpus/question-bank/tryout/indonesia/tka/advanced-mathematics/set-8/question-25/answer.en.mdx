# Given

We are given a **rational absolute-value function** <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> for all <InlineMath math="x\ne2" />.

# Asked

The **truth value of each statement** about left limit, right limit, and overall limit at <InlineMath math="x=2" />.

# Solution

> To the right the absolute value stays positive while to the left it flips negative, so check whether both sides match.

The absolute value makes the rule for <InlineMath math="f" /> depend on the sign of <InlineMath math="x-2" />, so **rewrite <InlineMath math="f" /> without the absolute value on each side of <InlineMath math="x=2" />**.

<BlockMath math="f(x)=\begin{cases} x+1, & x>2 \\ -(x+1), & x<2 \end{cases}" />

Draw <InlineMath math="f" /> on both sides of <InlineMath math="x=2" />. **From the right the graph heads to <InlineMath math="3" />, from the left to <InlineMath math="-3" />**, so the limit at <InlineMath math="x=2" /> doesn't exist, while at <InlineMath math="x=-1" /> the graph passes smoothly through zero.

For <InlineMath math="x>2" />, <InlineMath math="|x-2|" />, so the factor cancels. For <InlineMath math="x<2" />, <InlineMath math="|x-2|" />, which brings in a minus sign.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
|x-2|=x-2 \\
|x-2|=-(x-2)
\end{gathered}" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- **True.** From the right, <InlineMath math="f(x)=x+1\to3" />.
- **True.** From the left, <InlineMath math="f(x) \to -3" />. The one-sided limits differ, <InlineMath math="3\neq-3" />, so the two-sided limit at <InlineMath math="x=2" /> does not exist.

  <BlockMath math="f(x)=-(x+1)\to-3" />

- **False.** The left-hand limit at <InlineMath math="x=2" /> is <InlineMath math="-3" />, not <InlineMath math="3" />. For example, <InlineMath math="f(1.99)=-2.99" />.
- **False.** The limit does not exist when the one-sided limits differ. Their average, <InlineMath math="0" />, is not a limit value.
- **True.** Near <InlineMath math="x=-1" />, which is less than <InlineMath math="2" />, we have <InlineMath math="f(x)=-(x+1)" />, a continuous function. So the limit is <InlineMath math="-(-1+1)=0" />.

# Answer

Therefore, the correct choices are **statements <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, and <InlineMath math="5" />**.

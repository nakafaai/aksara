For a rational function at infinity, **compare the highest powers in the numerator and denominator**. The numerator has degree <InlineMath math="3" />, the denominator degree <InlineMath math="4" />. Divide top and bottom by the highest power in the denominator, <InlineMath math="x^4" />.

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5x^3-2x}{x^4+3}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\frac5x-\frac{2}{x^3}}{1+\frac{3}{x^4}}=\frac{0-0}{1+0}=0" />

The denominator grows faster than the numerator, so the fraction shrinks towards <InlineMath math="0" />. For example, at <InlineMath math="x=100" /> it is about <InlineMath math="0.05" />, and at <InlineMath math="x=1000" /> about <InlineMath math="0.005" />.

So the limit is <InlineMath math="0" />.

- <InlineMath math="5" /> compares the leading coefficients as if both degrees were equal.
- <InlineMath math="\infty" /> only looks at the numerator growing, without comparing it with the faster-growing denominator.
- <InlineMath math="-\frac23" /> compares terms that are not the leading terms, <InlineMath math="-2x" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac15" /> turns the ratio of the leading coefficients upside down.

# Given

We are given a **square matrix** <InlineMath math="S" /> and the <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> identity matrix <InlineMath math="I" />.

# Asked

The **truth value of each statement** about determinant, inverse, and powers of <InlineMath math="S" />.

# Solution

> Square the matrix first because returning to the identity makes every even and odd power trivial.

The matrix <InlineMath math="S" /> **swaps the two components** of a vector: the matrix below. Geometrically, it is the reflection in the line <InlineMath math="y=x" />.

<BlockMath math="S\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} b \\ a \end{pmatrix}" />

See <InlineMath math="S" /> as reflection in <InlineMath math="y=x" />. **A shape is flipped like a mirror image, so its orientation is reversed**, which is why <InlineMath math="\det S=-1" />, and reflecting twice puts the shape back.

1. The statement <InlineMath math="S^2=I" /> is true. Swapping twice restores the original order: the matrix below.

   <BlockMath math="S^2=\begin{pmatrix} 0\cdot0+1\cdot1 & 0\cdot1+1\cdot0 \\ 1\cdot0+0\cdot1 & 1\cdot1+0\cdot0 \end{pmatrix}=I" />

2. The statement <InlineMath math="\det S=1" /> is false: <InlineMath math="\det S=-1" />. A negative determinant signals a reflection, which reverses orientation.

   <BlockMath math="\det S=0\cdot0-1\cdot1=-1" />

3. The statement <InlineMath math="S^{-1}=S" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="S\cdot S=I" /> from the first statement.
4. The statement that <InlineMath math="S" /> commutes with every matrix is false. Counterexample: for the matrix below, the matrix below while the matrix below.

   <BlockMath math="\begin{gathered} A=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \\ SA=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \\ AS=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \end{gathered}" />

# Answer

Therefore, **statements <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> are True**, while **statements <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> are False**.

The matrix <InlineMath math="S" /> **swaps the two components** of a vector: <InlineMath math="S\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} b \\ a \end{pmatrix}" />. Geometrically, it is the reflection in the line <InlineMath math="y=x" />.

1. The statement <InlineMath math="S^2=I" /> is true. Swapping twice restores the original order: <InlineMath math="S^2=\begin{pmatrix} 0\cdot0+1\cdot1 & 0\cdot1+1\cdot0 \\ 1\cdot0+0\cdot1 & 1\cdot1+0\cdot0 \end{pmatrix}=I" />.
2. The statement <InlineMath math="\det S=1" /> is false: <InlineMath math="\det S=0\cdot0-1\cdot1=-1" />. A negative determinant signals a reflection, which reverses orientation.
3. The statement <InlineMath math="S^{-1}=S" /> is true, because <InlineMath math="S\cdot S=I" /> from the first statement.
4. The statement that <InlineMath math="S" /> commutes with every matrix is false. Counterexample: for <InlineMath math="A=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}" />, <InlineMath math="SA=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" /> while <InlineMath math="AS=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />.

So the statements about <InlineMath math="S^2" /> and <InlineMath math="S^{-1}" /> are true, and the statements about <InlineMath math="\det S" /> and commuting are false.

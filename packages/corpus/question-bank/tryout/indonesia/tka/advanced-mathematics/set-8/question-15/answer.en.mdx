# Given

A **city roundabout** forms a circle touched tangentially by a straight road on the map.

# Asked

The **tangent road equation** or point of contact on the roundabout.

# Solution

> A tangent road stands perpendicular to the radius at contact, so its slope is the negative reciprocal of the radius slope.

Complete the squares: the equation below, so the centre is <InlineMath math="(1,-2)" /> and <InlineMath math="r" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
(x-1)^2+(y+2)^2=10 \\
r=\sqrt{10}
\end{gathered}" />

A line parallel to <InlineMath math="3x+y=0" /> has the form the equation below. It is tangent exactly when **its distance from the centre equals the radius**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
3x+y+c=0 \\
\begin{aligned}
\frac{|3\cdot1+(-2)+c|}{\sqrt{3^2+1^2}}&=\sqrt{10} \\
|1+c|&=10
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}" />

Draw the roundabout and both tangents parallel to <InlineMath math="3x+y=0" />. **There are two tangents, one on each side of the roundabout**, and only the lower-left one crosses the <InlineMath math="Y" />-axis below the origin.

So <InlineMath math="c=9" /> or <InlineMath math="c=-11" />. There are two parallel tangents, the equation below and the equation below. The first meets the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis at <InlineMath math="(0,-9)" />, below the origin. The second meets it at <InlineMath math="(0,11)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{gathered}
3x+y+9=0 \\
3x+y-11=0
\end{gathered}" />

Check: substituting <InlineMath math="y=-3x-9" /> into the circle gives <InlineMath math="0" />, with a single root. So the line touches the circle exactly at <InlineMath math="(-2,-3)" />.

<BlockMath math="10x^2+40x+40=10(x+2)^2=0" />

So the road is the equation below.

<BlockMath math="3x+y+9=0" />

## Checking common traps

Let us check why these **common trap choices** miss the mark.

- The equation below is also tangent and parallel, but it meets the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis above the origin.

  <BlockMath math="3x+y-11=0" />

- The equation below uses the centre <InlineMath math="(-1,2)" />, a sign slip when reading the centre. Its distance from the actual centre is not <InlineMath math="\sqrt{10}" />.

  <BlockMath math="3x+y+11=0" />

- The equation below is tangent, but it is perpendicular to <InlineMath math="3x+y=0" />, not parallel.

  <BlockMath math="x-3y-17=0" />

- The equation below passes through the centre, so it cuts the circle in two points.

  <BlockMath math="3x+y-1=0" />

# Answer

Therefore, the **correct answer** is given below.

<BlockMath math="3x+y+9=0" />

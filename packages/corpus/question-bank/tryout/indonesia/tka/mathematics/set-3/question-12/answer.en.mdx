Let the cuboid edge lengths be <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" />. Pythagoras on its three face types gives:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2+b^2&=85 \\
a^2+c^2&=100 \\
b^2+c^2&=113
\end{aligned}" />

Add the two equations containing the desired squared edge and subtract the third. The result is twice that squared edge.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a^2&=\frac{85+100-113}{2}=36 \\
b^2&=\frac{85+113-100}{2}=49 \\
c^2&=\frac{100+113-85}{2}=64
\end{aligned}" />

Edge lengths are positive, so <InlineMath math="a=6" />, <InlineMath math="b=7" />, and <InlineMath math="c=8" />.

<BlockMath math="V=abc=6\cdot7\cdot8=336" />

Let <InlineMath math="u=\sqrt a" /> and <InlineMath math="v=\sqrt b" />. Their sum is <InlineMath math="7" /> and their product is <InlineMath math="10" />. They are therefore the roots of the following equation.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t^2-7t+10&=0 \\
(t-5)(t-2)&=0
\end{aligned}" />

Thus <InlineMath math="\{u,v\}=\{5,2\}" /> and <InlineMath math="\{a,b\}=\{25,4\}" />. Their order does not change the sum or the absolute difference.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
a+b&=25+4=29 \\
|a-b|&=25-4=21 \\
(a-b)^2&=21^2=441
\end{aligned}" />

For the sum of the reciprocal roots, use a common denominator.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac1{\sqrt a}+\frac1{\sqrt b}&=\frac{u+v}{uv} \\
&=\frac7{10}
\end{aligned}" />

These four results are true. The statement <InlineMath math="\sqrt{a+b}=7" /> is false because <InlineMath math="\sqrt{a+b}=\sqrt{29}" />.

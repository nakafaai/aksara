Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the number of packages A and <InlineMath math="y" /> the number of packages B. Both quantities must satisfy <InlineMath math="x \geq 0" /> and <InlineMath math="y \geq 0" />. Because the packages are indivisible, <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> are integers.

This gives the following mathematical model:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="5x + 2y \geq 60 \quad \text{...(i)}" />
<BlockMath math="2x + 2y \geq 30 \quad \text{...(ii)}" />
</MathContainer>

#### Finding the Intersection Point

If both line equations are eliminated, we get the intersection point

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{rcrcrcl} 5x & + & 2y & = & 60 & & \\ 2x & + & 2y & = & 30 & & - \\ \hline 3x & & & = & 30 & & \\ x & & & = & 10 & \Rightarrow & y = 5 \end{array}" />

#### Constraint Boundaries

The graph shows both boundary lines and the three vertices on the lower boundary of the feasible region. Because both inequalities use <InlineMath math="\geq" />, the feasible region lies on or above that boundary.

#### Determining Minimum Cost

The cost function is <InlineMath math="f(x, y) = 1{,}000x + 800y" />. Since both coefficients are positive, a minimum must lie on the lower boundary of the feasible region.

Evaluate the cost at its three vertices:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="f(0, 30) = 1{,}000(0) + 800(30) = 24{,}000" />
<BlockMath math="f(10, 5) = 1{,}000(10) + 800(5) = 14{,}000" />
<BlockMath math="f(15, 0) = 1{,}000(15) + 800(0) = 15{,}000" />
</MathContainer>

The intersection <InlineMath math="(10,5)" /> has integer coordinates, so it is also valid for indivisible packages. Therefore, the minimum cost is <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}14{,}000" />.

Given two quadratic functions

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="f(x) = (k - 1)x^2 + kx - 1, \quad k \neq 1" />
<BlockMath math="g(x) = (k - 2)x^2 + x + 2k, \quad k \neq 2" />
</MathContainer>

#### Finding the Condition for Intersection

Eliminate <InlineMath math="y" /> from both functions by equating <InlineMath math="f(x) = g(x)" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="(k - 1)x^2 + kx - 1 = (k - 2)x^2 + x + 2k" />
<BlockMath math="x^2 + (k - 1)x - (2k + 1) = 0 \quad ...(1)" />
</MathContainer>

The condition for both functions to intersect at two different points is that the discriminant must be positive, namely <InlineMath math="D > 0" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="D > 0" />
<BlockMath math="(k - 1)^2 - 4(1)(-2k - 1) > 0" />
<BlockMath math="k^2 - 2k + 1 + 8k + 4 > 0" />
<BlockMath math="k^2 + 6k + 5 > 0" />
<BlockMath math="(k + 5)(k + 1) > 0" />
</MathContainer>

From the inequality <InlineMath math="(k + 5)(k + 1) > 0" />, we get <InlineMath math="k < -5" /> or <InlineMath math="k > -1" />.

Since <InlineMath math="k \neq 1" /> and <InlineMath math="k \neq 2" />, the smallest positive integer that satisfies the condition is <InlineMath math="k = 3" />.

#### Substituting the Constant Value

Substitute the value <InlineMath math="k = 3" /> into the first equation to find the sum of roots <InlineMath math="x" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="x^2 + (k - 1)x - (2k + 1) = 0" />
<BlockMath math="x^2 + (3 - 1)x - (2(3) + 1) = 0" />
<BlockMath math="x^2 + 2x - 7 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

From this quadratic equation, the sum of its roots is

<BlockMath math="x_1 + x_2 = -2" />

#### Finding the Sum of the Ordinates

For <InlineMath math="k=3" />, either function gives the ordinates at the intersection points. Use

<BlockMath math="y_i=f(x_i)=2x_i^2+3x_i-1" />

Let <InlineMath math="S_x=x_1+x_2=-2" /> and <InlineMath math="P_x=x_1x_2=-7" />. First compute the sum of the squared abscissas.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="x_1^2+x_2^2=(x_1+x_2)^2-2x_1x_2" />
<BlockMath math="x_1^2+x_2^2=S_x^2-2P_x=(-2)^2-2(-7)=18" />
</MathContainer>

Now add <InlineMath math="y_1" /> and <InlineMath math="y_2" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="y_1+y_2=2(x_1^2+x_2^2)+3(x_1+x_2)-2" />
<BlockMath math="y_1+y_2=2(18)+3(-2)-2=28" />
</MathContainer>

#### Forming the New Quadratic Equation

The new roots are <InlineMath math="r_1=x_1+x_2=-2" /> and <InlineMath math="r_2=y_1+y_2=28" />. Use a new variable <InlineMath math="t" /> to avoid confusing this equation with the original abscissas.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="t^2-(r_1+r_2)t+r_1r_2=0" />
<BlockMath math="t^2-(-2+28)t+(-2)(28)=0" />
<BlockMath math="t^2-26t-56=0" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, the requested equation is <InlineMath math="x^2-26x-56=0" />, matching the variable used in the answer choices.

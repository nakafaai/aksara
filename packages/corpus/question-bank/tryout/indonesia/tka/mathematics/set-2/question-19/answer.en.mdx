Both dimensions are positive, so area and perimeter increase when either dimension increases. Use the lower bounds for minima and the upper bounds for maxima.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_{\min}&=9.9(7.9) \\
&=78.21\,\mathrm{cm}^2 \\
A_{\max}&=10.1(8.1) \\
&=81.81\,\mathrm{cm}^2 \\
U_{\min}&=2(9.9+7.9) \\
&=35.6\,\mathrm{cm} \\
U_{\max}&=2(10.1+8.1) \\
&=36.4\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Relative to the nominal area <InlineMath math="80\,\mathrm{cm}^2" />, the downward error is <InlineMath math="80-78.21=1.79\,\mathrm{cm}^2" /> and the upward error is <InlineMath math="81.81-80=1.81\,\mathrm{cm}^2" />. The greatest absolute error is therefore <InlineMath math="1.81\,\mathrm{cm}^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1.81}{80}\cdot100\%=2.2625\%" />

The claims about minimum area, maximum area, perimeter range, and relative error exceeding <InlineMath math="2.2\%" /> are true. The claimed maximum absolute error of <InlineMath math="1.8\,\mathrm{cm}^2" /> is false.

Because <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b" /> are roots of <InlineMath math="2x^2 + kx + 6 = 0" />, Vieta's formulas give

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="ab = \frac{6}{2} = 3" />
<BlockMath math="a \neq 0 \quad \text{and} \quad b \neq 0" />
</MathContainer>

The first three terms <InlineMath math="a, b, b^2" /> form a geometric sequence, so consecutive ratios are equal.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{b}{a} = \frac{b^2}{b} = b" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{a} = 1" />
<BlockMath math="a = 1" />
</MathContainer>

The division by <InlineMath math="b" /> is valid because <InlineMath math="b \neq 0" />. Use the product of the roots again to find <InlineMath math="b" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="ab = 3" />
<BlockMath math="(1)b = 3" />
<BlockMath math="b = 3" />
</MathContainer>

The sum of the roots determines <InlineMath math="k" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="x_1 + x_2 = \frac{-k}{2}" />
<BlockMath math="1 + 3 = \frac{-k}{2}" />
<BlockMath math="4 = \frac{-k}{2}" />
<BlockMath math="k = -8" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore the geometric sequence is

<BlockMath math="a, b, b^2 = 1, 3, 9, ..." />

Then the <InlineMath math="4" />th term is

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="u_4 = ar^3" />
<BlockMath math="u_4 = (1)(3)^3" />
<BlockMath math="u_4 = 27" />
</MathContainer>

Thus the values of <InlineMath math="u_4" /> and <InlineMath math="k" /> respectively are <InlineMath math="27" /> and <InlineMath math="-8" />.

# Given

In the **SMP Harapan silent reading** debate, Text 1 (literacy team) says **nothing builds readers like daily practice** and **<InlineMath math="15" /> minutes a day is a small price**.

Text 2 (Bu Yuni) notes **both pilot teachers already ran book clubs**, **the library received two hundred new books**, **the first lesson is mathematics on four days of the week**, and proposes that **Year 8 read at the start of the long break**.

# Asked

Whether each of the six statements is **Stated as a fact**, **Stated as an opinion**, or **Not stated in either text**.

# Solution

Sort cleanly into three boxes: **verifiable school details** are facts, **value claims** (*nothing builds readers like*, *small price*) are opinions, and **two traps** (saying new books *caused* higher borrowing, or saying Year 8 *already* reads during break) appear in neither text.

> Never mistake a *possible alternative factor* for a claimed cause, or a *proposal* for a current habit.

## Sorting all six statements into three categories

Text 2 explicitly writes <Highlight>both teachers already ran book clubs</Highlight> and <Highlight>mathematics on four days of the week</Highlight>, while proposing Year 8 read during break. Checking **all six statements**:

| Statement | Category | Textual basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Both pilot teachers already ran book clubs | **Stated as a fact** | Verifiable detail in Text 2 |
| Daily practice builds readers best | **Stated as an opinion** | Literacy team's value claim in Text 1 |
| The two hundred new books raised borrowing | **Not stated in either text** | Mentioned only as an alternative factor, not proven cause |
| First lesson is mathematics four days a week | **Stated as a fact** | Timetable fact in Text 2 |
| Fifteen minutes a day is a small price | **Stated as an opinion** | Literacy team's judgement in Text 1 |
| Year 8 already reads during the long break | **Not stated in either text** | Proposed for the future, not current practice |

# Answer

Statements **one and four** are **Stated as a fact**, statements **two and five** are **Stated as an opinion**, and statements **three and six** are **Not stated in either text**.

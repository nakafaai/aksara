# Given

In the **egg incubator** recount, the narrator openly admits: **"I numbered that first Monday as day <InlineMath math="1" /> in my record. So I stopped the turning one day early, and on the Saturday after the candling on day <InlineMath math="14" />, I told the whole class that the chicks would come the next morning."**

# Asked

Which statements among I through V about the narrator are supported by the account.

# Solution

Judge the narrator fairly from the text: it was an **honest off-by-one counting slip** that shifted both the turning deadline and the expected hatch day (Sunday = chart **day <InlineMath math="20" />**), and the narrator **admits it openly** rather than hiding it or breaking the rule deliberately.

> Never infer deliberate disobedience or secrecy when a narrator openly confesses an accidental slip.

## Checking statements I through V about the narrator

The narrator explains plainly <Highlight>I numbered that first Monday as day <InlineMath math="1" /> in my record. So I stopped the turning one day early</Highlight>. Testing **all five statements**:

- **I (supported)**: The narrator followed the day-<InlineMath math="18" /> rule using their own shifted day count.
- **II (false)**: Nothing hints the day-<InlineMath math="0" /> rule was ignored deliberately.
- **III (supported)**: That single off-by-one slip shifted both stopping turning and expecting the hatch one day early.
- **IV (false)**: The narrator confesses the mistake directly to the reader.
- **V (supported)**: Expecting chicks on Sunday meant expecting them on **day <InlineMath math="20" />** of Bu Ratna's chart.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I and III only** and **III and V only** drop one directly verified statement.
- **I, II, and III only** invents intentional rule-breaking in II.
- **I, III, IV, and V only** contradicts the narrator's candid admission in IV.

# Answer

The supported statements about the narrator are **I, III, and V only**.

# Given

A neighbour places their rain gauge **right under the house eaves** claiming it prevents wind tipping and improves accuracy. Step two states **pebbles keep the bottle from blowing over**, and step four requires **open ground at least as far from any tree or wall as the tree or wall is tall**.

# Asked

Which replies among I through IV are supported by the instructions.

# Solution

Putting a rain gauge under eaves defeats the whole purpose because the roof blocks falling rain. The instructions already solve wind stability with **pebbles** (step two) and require **open ground** for every gauge regardless of funnel size (step four).

> Follow every setup rule together: careful reading technique cannot cancel out a sheltered location.

## Checking all four replies against steps two and four

The guide writes <Highlight>The pebbles keep the bottle from blowing over</Highlight> and <Highlight>Set the gauge in open ground, at least as far from any tree or wall as the tree or wall is tall</Highlight>. Testing **replies I through IV**:

- **I (supported)**: Step four explicitly requires open ground well clear of walls.
- **II (supported)**: Pebbles already stabilize the bottle against wind, so no wall shelter is needed.
- **III (false)**: Reading at eye level cannot fix missing rain blocked by eaves.
- **IV (false)**: Open-ground placement applies to **every** gauge, not just wide funnels.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I only** leaves out the pebble stability answer in II.
- Options with **III** mistakenly endorse placing the gauge under eaves.
- **II and IV only** invents an exception for narrow funnels.

# Answer

The supported replies are **I and II only**.

# Given

Over a **stormy month**, a home-made gauge recorded **far less rain** than the nearby official school gauge. Five instruction details (I through V) are tested to see which mistakes would cause a large **under-reading**.

# Asked

Which details among I through V point to mistakes that could explain recording **far less rain**.

# Solution

Check the **direction and size** of each error. Forgetting to divide a wide funnel (**II**) pushes readings **too high**, not too low, and small parallax errors (**III**) fluctuate slightly both ways. Only **shelter from trees or walls (I)** and **skipping the 19:00 emptying so heavy rain overflows the <InlineMath math="100" /> mm ruler (IV)** cause large under-readings.

> Always check which *direction* breaking a rule pushes a measurement (too low versus too high).

## Checking all five details for large under readings

Step four requires open ground away from shelter (<Highlight>at least as far from any tree or wall as the tree or wall is tall</Highlight>) and step six requires <Highlight>On a day of very heavy rain, also read and empty the gauge at 19:00</Highlight>. Testing **I through V**:

- **I (explains far less rain)**: Trees or walls block rain from reaching the funnel.
- **II (wrong direction)**: Failing to divide a wide funnel makes rainfall look **higher**, not lower.
- **III (too small and two-sided)**: Slight eye-level shifts nudge readings marginally up or down, not *far less* across a whole month.
- **IV (explains far less rain)**: Skipping 19:00 emptying during heavy storms lets water exceed the <InlineMath math="100" /> mm ruler, losing excess rainfall.
- **V (unrelated)**: Pebbles merely prevent tipping over.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I only** and **IV only** each miss one major cause of lost rain.
- **I, II, and IV only** and **I, III, and IV only** include errors that push readings too high or only shift them slightly.

# Answer

The details pointing to mistakes that could explain far less rain are **I and IV only**.

At SMP Harapan, every student has a cleaning duty day once a week. Before lessons start, the duty team sweeps the floor, wipes the whiteboard and desks, and empties the bins. Some parents have recently asked the school to end this practice and hire more cleaners instead. I believe the school should keep student cleaning duty.

First, it teaches responsibility for shared spaces. When students clean their own classroom, they see how much rubbish they and their friends leave behind. Many start to pick up after themselves without being told.

Second, the duty takes very little time. The school timed duty teams for two weeks in August.

| Wiping the whiteboard and desks | <InlineMath math="4" /> |
| Emptying the bins | <InlineMath math="2" /> |

That adds up to about twelve minutes, once a week, before the first bell.

Third, the money saved can be used for something else. Hiring two extra cleaners would cost the school about 60 million rupiah a year, roughly what it spends on new library books.

The parents are right that the school's cleaners are still needed. Toilets, gutters, and the hall require equipment and training that students do not have, and those jobs should stay with the professional staff. Student duty should cover only the classroom. With that limit, the habit is worth keeping.

# Given

In the **bottle rain gauge** guide, a funnel **twice as wide** as the bottle collects <InlineMath math="4" /> times the rain depth (divide rise by **<InlineMath math="4" />**), and a funnel **three times as wide** collects <InlineMath math="9" /> times the depth (divide rise by **<InlineMath math="9" />**).

In the same field, Rina's twice-as-wide gauge shows a rise of **<InlineMath math="36" /> mm**.

# Asked

The rise in millimetres that Dimas's three-times-as-wide gauge should show that morning.

# Solution

Use **actual rainfall** as the bridge between the two bottles. Because circular catch area grows with the **square of width** (<InlineMath math="2^2 = 4" /> and <InlineMath math="3^2 = 9" />), divide Rina's rise by <InlineMath math="4" /> to get rainfall, then multiply by <InlineMath math="9" /> for Dimas.

> Convert the first gauge reading into true rainfall first, then scale up by the second gauge's area factor.

## Finding rainfall from Rina and scaling to Dimas

Step seven gives the exact factors: <Highlight>divide the rise: by <InlineMath math="4" /> for a funnel twice as wide as the bottle, and by <InlineMath math="9" /> for one three times as wide</Highlight>. Computing **rainfall and Dimas's rise**:

<BlockMath math="\text{Rainfall} = \frac{36}{4} = 9\text{ mm}, \qquad \text{Dimas's rise} = 9 \times 9 = 81\text{ mm}" />

## Why the other four numbers fail

- **<InlineMath math="54" />** scales linearly by width (<InlineMath math="36 \times \frac{3}{2}" />) instead of by area factors <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="9" />.
- **<InlineMath math="9" />** stops at the true rainfall instead of computing Dimas's bottle rise.
- **<InlineMath math="324" />** multiplies <InlineMath math="36 \times 9" /> without first dividing by <InlineMath math="4" />.
- **<InlineMath math="48" />** multiplies <InlineMath math="36" /> by <InlineMath math="\frac{4}{3}" />.

# Answer

Dimas's gauge should show a rise of **<InlineMath math="81" />** millimetres.

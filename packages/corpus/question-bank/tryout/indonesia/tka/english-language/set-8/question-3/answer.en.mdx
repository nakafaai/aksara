# Given

At **Puskesmas Kenanga**, weekdays have **two doctors** (<InlineMath math="23" /> paired turns = <InlineMath math="46" /> numbered patients) and Saturdays have **one doctor** (<InlineMath math="23" /> single turns = <InlineMath math="23" /> numbered patients). Registration costs **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}10{,}000" />**, and **national health card holders pay nothing** with no day restriction.

# Asked

Which of statements I through IV comparing weekdays with Saturdays are supported by the text.

# Solution

Check each claim against the **two-doctor versus one-doctor ratio** and the **fee paragraph**. Both days share the exact same <InlineMath math="23" /> time slots, so weekdays handle twice the patients and move any queue number along twice as fast, while fees never vary by day.

> Unless a passage specifies a weekend discount or surcharge, treat its stated fee rule as identical across every open day.

## Checking all four comparisons between weekday and Saturday

The clinic states <Highlight>Patients with a national health card pay nothing</Highlight>. Computing **number <InlineMath math="20" />'s start time on both days**:

<BlockMath math="\text{Weekday (pair 10): } 9 \times 12 = 108\text{ min} = 1\text{ h } 48\text{ min}, \qquad \text{Saturday (slot 20): } 19 \times 12 = 228\text{ min} = 3\text{ h } 48\text{ min}" />

Measured from 07:30, ticket <InlineMath math="20" /> enters at **09:18** on weekdays and **11:18** on Saturdays.

- **I (supported)**: Weekdays see <InlineMath math="46" /> numbered patients versus <InlineMath math="23" /> on Saturday (exactly twice as many).
- **II (supported)**: Number <InlineMath math="20" /> enters at 11:18 on Saturday, two hours later than 9:18 on a weekday.
- **III (false)**: No Saturday registration discount is mentioned anywhere.
- **IV (supported)**: National health card holders pay nothing on every open day.

## Why the other four options fail

- Options including **III** invent a Saturday fee discount absent from the text.
- **I and II only** omits IV, while **II and IV only** drops the proven two-to-one capacity in I.

# Answer

The supported comparisons are **I, II, and IV only**.

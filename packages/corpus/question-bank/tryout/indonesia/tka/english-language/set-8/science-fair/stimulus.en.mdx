Last Saturday our class held its first science fair in the school hall. Each group of three had four weeks to plan and carry out a small experiment.

My group, made up of Nadia, Bayu, and me, wanted to find out which material keeps ice from melting the longest. We wrapped identical ice cubes in newspaper, aluminum foil, and a wool sock, and left one cube unwrapped. Then we timed how long each one took to melt completely. We repeated the test three times to make sure our results were not just luck.

At first, Bayu was sure the foil would win, because it looks like the material inside cooler bags. To our surprise, the wool sock kept the ice the longest, about 95 minutes on average, while the unwrapped cube melted in about 40 minutes. The foil came third, just behind the newspaper.

On the day of the fair, visitors kept asking the same question: “Isn't wool supposed to make things warm?” We explained that wool does not create heat. It traps air, and trapped air slows down heat moving in either direction, from a warm body out into the cold or from a warm room into the ice.

The judges gave us second place. One of them told us that our idea was simple but our method was careful. I think that was the best compliment we could have received.

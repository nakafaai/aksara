# Given

Bu Yuni doubts that the **<InlineMath math="15" />-minute silent reading time** caused the rise in library borrowing because both pilot teachers **already ran book clubs** and the library received **two hundred new books** that semester. Four hypothetical facts (I through IV) are tested to see which would **weaken her doubt about borrowing**.

# Asked

Which facts among I through IV would weaken Bu Yuni's doubt that reading time caused the rise in borrowing.

# Solution

To **weaken** an alternative-cause doubt, knock down the **alternative causes** (book clubs and new books). Showing non-book-club classes saw the same jump (**I**) knocks out book clubs, and showing the new books arrived only in the **last week** (**II**) knocks out new books.

> Weakening a critic's doubt means ruling out the competing explanations they proposed, not strengthening them or switching topics.

## Evaluating how I through IV affect Bu Yuni doubt

Text 2 raises two competing causes for borrowing: <Highlight>both teachers already ran book clubs. The library also received two hundred new books that semester</Highlight>. Checking **facts I through IV**:

- **I (weakens doubt)**: Classes **without book clubs** seeing the same rise eliminates book clubs as the necessary cause.
- **II (weakens doubt)**: New books arriving only in the **last week** could not have caused a semester-long rise.
- **III (strengthens doubt)**: Book-club classes borrowing more *without* reading time credits book clubs instead.
- **IV (off-topic and strengthens doubt)**: Easier test formats concern **test scores**, not library borrowing.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I only** omits knocking down the new-books explanation in II.
- Options with **III** or **IV** select facts that reinforce Bu Yuni's skepticism or address test scores instead of borrowing.

# Answer

The facts that weaken Bu Yuni's doubt are **I and II only**.

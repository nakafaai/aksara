**Making and reading a bottle rain gauge**

1. Cut the top off a straight-sided plastic bottle and turn it upside down into the bottle as a funnel.
2. Put a layer of pebbles in the bottom, then pour in water up to the top of the pebbles. Mark this level as the zero line. The pebbles keep the bottle from blowing over.
3. Stick a ruler to the side with its <InlineMath math="0" /> at the zero line. The ruler shows up to <InlineMath math="100" /> millimetres.
4. Set the gauge in open ground, at least as far from any tree or wall as the tree or wall is tall.
5. Read the water level at 7:00 every morning, with your eye level with the water, and write down the rise in millimetres. Then pour water out until the level is back at the zero line.
6. On a day of very heavy rain, also read and empty the gauge at 19:00, and add the two readings for that day.
7. If the funnel is as wide as the bottle, the rise in millimetres is the rainfall in millimetres. A funnel wider than the bottle catches more rain, so divide the rise: by <InlineMath math="4" /> for a funnel twice as wide as the bottle, and by <InlineMath math="9" /> for one three times as wide.

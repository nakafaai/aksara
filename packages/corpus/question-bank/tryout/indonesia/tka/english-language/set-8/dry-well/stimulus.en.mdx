The old well behind SD Tunas has been dry for three years. Pak Udin, the caretaker, keeps it shut inside a little brick well-house with a padlocked door, and the only key hangs on his belt. Once a month he lowers a weighted rope to the bottom and writes "dry" in his book. The well-house bucket hangs on a hook inside.

That dry season, strange things happened around the well-house. In the afternoons we heard voices from it, until Pak Udin showed us that an old drain pipe carried the sound from the staff room. Every Monday there was a coin on the doorstep, and one day I caught a boy from class two leaving it there because his sister had dared him. On hot days cold air came under the door, and our science teacher said that deep stone shafts stay cool all year.

In September the tanker that brought the village its water broke down, and for six days every family had only what was left in its drums. On the sixth morning I found the well-house bucket on the doorstep, full to the brim with cold, clear water. The padlock was closed, and Pak Udin said the key had not left his belt, not even at night. His book, written the day before, said "dry".

Our neighbour said that someone must have carried the water from home as a joke. Pak Udin looked at the bucket for a long time and said nothing. Then he gave everyone in the queue a cupful, starting with the oldest.

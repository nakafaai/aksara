The old railway station in Kota Lama stopped serving passengers in 1985, when a new station was built on the edge of town. For years it stood empty. Today it is the Railway Heritage Hall, and it looks much as it did a century ago.

The building is long and low, with thick white walls and a red tiled roof that stretches over the platform to keep off the rain. Above the main door hangs a round clock with Roman numerals. It no longer works, and its hands have been stopped at 7:15, the departure time of the last passenger train.

Inside, the old waiting room has become the main gallery. Wooden benches still line the walls, and the ticket window, with its small arched opening and brass bars, has been kept exactly as it was. Glass cases display old tickets, conductors' caps, and the lanterns that were once used to signal to drivers at night.

The most popular exhibit stands on the single remaining track beside the platform: a black steam locomotive built in 1921. Visitors can climb into the driver's cab and look into the firebox, where coal was once burned to heat the water.

Behind the station, a small garden has been planted where the goods yard used to be. On weekend mornings, a volunteer who once worked as a station master tells visitors about the trains he remembers.

# Given

Five scenarios inspired by **The Dry Well** are judged for **real-world possibility**: sound travelling along an old pipe (I), a deep stone shaft staying cool on a hot day (II), and a bucket appearing outside a locked door while the padlock stays shut, the sole key never leaves its owner's belt, and nobody opens the door (III).

The remaining two scenarios are a caretaker writing "dry" when water exists (IV) and someone filling a bucket from a home drum (V).

# Asked

Which of scenarios I through V could happen in the real world.

# Solution

Test every scenario **outside the story** by everyday reality. Even though we trust Pak Udin inside the story, a real-world caretaker **can make a mistake or write the wrong entry** (**IV is possible**), whereas a solid bucket passing out through a permanently locked door (**III**) breaks physical reality.

> Separate whether a person *could* err in real life from whether a specific story character seems trustworthy.

## Judging real world possibility for I through V

Testing **each scenario independently**:

- **I (possible)**: Hollow pipes readily carry sound between rooms.
- **II (possible)**: Deep underground stone stays cool during hot afternoons.
- **III (impossible)**: With <Highlight>the padlock stays closed, the only key never leaves its owner's belt, and nobody opens the door</Highlight>, no physical process moves the bucket outside.
- **IV (possible)**: Any human caretaker can misread a rope or write an inaccurate note.
- **V (possible)**: Carrying water from a home drum in a bucket is completely ordinary.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I, II, and V only** and **I and II only** wrongly judge IV by Pak Udin's honesty in the story instead of real-world possibility.
- **I, II, III, IV, and V** accepts a physically impossible locked-room escape (III).
- **II, IV, and V only** wrongly drops acoustic transmission through a pipe (I).

# Answer

The events that could happen in the real world are **I, II, IV, and V only**.

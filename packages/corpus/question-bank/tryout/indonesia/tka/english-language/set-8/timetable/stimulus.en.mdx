**Text 1**

**Keep the Four-Day Week**

From July to December last year, Danau Teduh Senior High School tried a new timetable. Students had classes from Monday to Thursday, from 7:00 to 15:45, and stayed at home on Fridays, which teachers used for planning and marking. Having studied the results, the school committee believes that the four-day week should become permanent from next July.

The first gain is rested students. Average daily attendance rose from <InlineMath math="91\%" /> in the same term a year earlier to <InlineMath math="95\%" /> during the trial, and the largest rise came on Mondays, once the weakest day of the week. Teachers gained as well. Most of the teachers we surveyed called the free Friday the most useful change the school has made in years.

We will not pretend that every number improved. The average term-end mark moved from <InlineMath math="71.2" /> to <InlineMath math="71.6" />, which is no real change. Even so, that result is reassuring, because it shows that the longer days did not harm learning. The school buses, meanwhile, ran on four days instead of five, so the term's fuel bill fell by about one fifth. A rise of <InlineMath math="4" /> percentage points in attendance is worth more than any single test result.

**Text 2**

**A Reply from the Parents' Forum**

The Parents' Forum thanks the committee for publishing its figures, and we have no quarrel with the figures themselves. Marks did hold steady. We simply read that result differently. Steady marks after a single term tell us little. One term is too short for marks to show either harm or benefit.

We do not dispute that attendance rose. Children plainly enjoyed their long weekends. Whether they came back rested is another matter, because the attendance figure counts only the four days the school was open. Friday is where the real cost of the trial landed. In our survey of <InlineMath math="212" /> families, <InlineMath math="64" /> said that an older child had spent Fridays minding younger brothers and sisters so that the parents could work, and <InlineMath math="39" /> said that their child had taken a paid Friday job.

We are not asking the school to return to five days. We ask for a second trial term with a supervised Friday programme, paid for from the bus savings, and with Fridays counted in the results. Only then will the committee know what the four-day week costs as well as what it saves.

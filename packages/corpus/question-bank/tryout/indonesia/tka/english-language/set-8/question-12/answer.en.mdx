# Given

**The Dry Well** has four paragraphs: paragraph one introduces the **padlocked well-house and key on Pak Udin's belt**, and paragraph two covers three strange clues explained by **Pak Udin** (voices), **the narrator** (coins), and **the science teacher** (cold air).

Paragraph three reveals the **full bucket, closed padlock, and key staying on his belt**, and paragraph four gives the **neighbour's joke theory** while **Pak Udin says nothing** and shares cups of water.

# Asked

Which of statements I through V accurately describe how the story is organized.

# Solution

Check both **paragraph order** and **who explains what**. Notice that the closed padlock comes in paragraph **three** *before* the neighbour's theory in paragraph **four** (so V flips the order), and Pak Udin stays completely silent at the end (ruling out III).

> Structural questions often test subtle reversals between paragraphs three and four as well as who actually speaks each explanation.

## Checking the four paragraph structure for I through V

Paragraph four ends with <Highlight>Pak Udin looked at the bucket for a long time and said nothing</Highlight>. Checking **statements I through V**:

- **I (correct)**: The locked well-house and belt key appear in paragraph one before any strange event in paragraph two.
- **II (wrong)**: Pak Udin explains the voices and the science teacher explains the cold air, while the narrator explains only the coins.
- **III (wrong)**: Pak Udin never explains where the water came from.
- **IV (correct)**: The key appears on Pak Udin's belt in paragraph one and again in paragraph three.
- **V (wrong)**: The closed padlock is revealed in paragraph three, **before** the neighbour's theory in paragraph four.

## Why the other four options fail

- Options containing **V** reverse paragraphs three and four.
- Options containing **II** credit the narrator with explanations given by Pak Udin and the science teacher.

# Answer

The accurate descriptions of how the story is organized are **I and IV only**.

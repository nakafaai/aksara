# Given

Pak Dimas uses a funnel **twice as wide** as his bottle (divide rise by **<InlineMath math="4" />**) with a ruler up to **<InlineMath math="100" /> mm**. On a stormy day he reads **<InlineMath math="64" /> mm** at 19:00, empties to zero, and reads **<InlineMath math="44" /> mm** at 7:00 the next morning.

# Asked

Which of statements I through IV are supported by the instructions.

# Solution

Add the two stormy-day readings first (**<InlineMath math="108" /> mm** total). Notice two things immediately: **<InlineMath math="108" /> mm** exceeds the <InlineMath math="100" /> mm ruler (proving why emptying at 19:00 mattered!), and dividing by **<InlineMath math="4" />** yields **<InlineMath math="27" /> mm** of true rainfall.

> Sum split readings before dividing by the funnel area factor, and compare the combined rise directly against the ruler's <InlineMath math="100" /> mm ceiling.

## Computing combined rise and true rainfall

Step six instructs to <Highlight>add the two readings for that day</Highlight>, and step seven requires dividing by **<InlineMath math="4" />** for a twice-as-wide funnel:

<BlockMath math="\text{Total rise} = 64 + 44 = 108\text{ mm} > 100\text{ mm}, \qquad \text{Rainfall} = \frac{108}{4} = 27\text{ mm}" />

- **I (supported)**: True rainfall between the two 7:00 checks was **<InlineMath math="27" /> mm**.
- **II (supported)**: Without emptying at 19:00, the <InlineMath math="108" /> mm rise would have passed the <InlineMath math="100" /> mm ruler top.
- **III (false)**: <InlineMath math="54" /> mm wrongly divides by width (<InlineMath math="2" />) instead of <InlineMath math="4" />.
- **IV (supported)**: A matching-width funnel rises <InlineMath math="1" /> mm per <InlineMath math="1" /> mm of rain, so **<InlineMath math="27" /> mm**.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I, II, III, and IV** wrongly includes III (dividing by <InlineMath math="2" />).
- **I and II only**, **II and IV only**, and **I and IV only** each drop one verified statement.

# Answer

The supported statements are **I, II, and IV only**.

# Given

In **The Dry Well**, Pak Udin lowers a weighted rope monthly and **writes "dry" in his book**. After the full bucket appears and a neighbour guesses someone carried water from home as a joke, **Pak Udin looks at the bucket for a long time, says nothing, and gives everyone in the queue a cupful, starting with the oldest**.

# Asked

Which statements among I through V about Pak Udin are supported by the story.

# Solution

Check carefully whether Pak Udin's logbook really **contradicts** the neighbour's guess (**II**): his book says the *well* was dry, and the neighbour says water came *from someone's home*. Both statements actually fit together, so **II is false**! Also note the first cup goes to **the oldest**, not the narrator.

> Two different statements contradict each other only when they cannot both be true at the same time.

## Testing statements I through V about Pak Udin

The ending reads <Highlight>Pak Udin looked at the bucket for a long time and said nothing. Then he gave everyone in the queue a cupful, starting with the oldest</Highlight>. Checking **all five claims**:

- **I (supported)**: He keeps a monthly written logbook for the well.
- **II (false)**: A dry well does **not** contradict someone carrying water from home, as both could be true together.
- **III (supported)**: He stays silent about the water's source and immediately shares it.
- **IV (false)**: He gives the first cup to **the oldest** person in line, not to the narrator.
- **V (supported)**: By saying nothing, he neither endorses nor dismisses the neighbour's theory.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I and III only** and **III and V only** drop one directly supported statement.
- **I, II, III, and V only** mistakes compatibility for contradiction in II.
- **I, III, IV, and V only** gives the narrator the cup meant for the oldest villager.

# Answer

The supported statements about Pak Udin are **I, III, and V only**.

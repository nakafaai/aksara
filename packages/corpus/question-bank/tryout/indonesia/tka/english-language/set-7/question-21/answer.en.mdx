# Given

In the **Jalan Pasar** debate, Text 1 (traders) argues a motor-free street is **simply a pleasure** and **nothing else could bring so many shoppers back so cheaply**, while Text 2 (Pak Hendra) notes **Kota Lama opened a free parking lot at the same time**, **nobody compared takings with the year before**, and **ambulances must reach the southern clinic**.

# Asked

Whether each of the six statements is **Stated as a fact**, **Stated as an opinion**, or **Not stated in either text**.

# Solution

Apply a quick three-bucket test: **verifiable events** go into facts, **value judgements** (*pleasure*, *nothing else could*) go into opinions, and **unstated claims** (saying parking *caused* the rise, or inventing the clinic's *opinion*) belong in neither text.

> Raising a possible alternative factor or mentioning a building's emergency access never states a proven cause or that building's opinion.

## Sorting all six statements into three categories

Checking **each statement against both texts**:

| Statement | Category | Textual basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A street without engines is a pleasure to shop in | **Stated as an opinion** | Traders' personal taste in Text 1 |
| Kota Lama opened a free parking lot at the same time | **Stated as a fact** | Verifiable event cited by Pak Hendra |
| Free parking caused the rise in shoppers | **Not stated in either text** | Mentioned only as an extra factor, not proven cause |
| Nobody compared takings with last year's Sundays | **Stated as a fact** | Direct factual check in Text 2 |
| Nothing else could bring shoppers back so cheaply | **Stated as an opinion** | Traders' sweeping value judgement |
| The clinic opposes closing the street | **Not stated in either text** | Text 2 only notes ambulances must reach the clinic |

# Answer

Statements **two and four** are **Stated as a fact**, statements **one and five** are **Stated as an opinion**, and statements **three and six** are **Not stated in either text**.

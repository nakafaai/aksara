The town of Wiraguna has fourteen bus stops on its main route, but only three of them have a roof. At the others, passengers stand in the open, holding umbrellas in the rain or squinting in the afternoon sun. I believe the town council should build simple shelters at the five busiest uncovered stops next year.

The first reason is comfort and health, especially for older passengers. During the rainy season, many of them choose to stay home rather than wait in the rain, even when they have a doctor's appointment.

The second reason is that shelters would make the bus more attractive. When the stop outside the market got a roof two years ago, the bus company's ticket records showed that boardings there rose from about 210 to about 260 a day over the following year. The company believes that other changes, such as a new timetable, may also have played a part, but the rise was larger than at any stop without a roof.

The third reason is cost. A simple steel shelter with a bench costs about 25 million rupiah, so five shelters would cost about 125 million rupiah. The council could spread that amount over two years.

Some people worry that shelters would attract litter and graffiti. This is possible, but each shelter can have a bin, and local schools have offered to adopt a shelter and help keep it clean.

Shelters will not solve every problem with the town's buses. But they are a small, affordable step that would make waiting easier for thousands of people.

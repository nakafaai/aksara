# Given

A neighbour claims airing the dyed cloth is wasted time because it already looks coloured out of the bath. Steps three and four state that the cloth **comes out green and turns blue as it meets the air**, **one dip plus one airing makes one round**, and light, mid, and dark blue require **<InlineMath math="2" />**, **<InlineMath math="4" />**, and **<InlineMath math="6" /> rounds**.

# Asked

Which replies among I through IV are supported by the instructions.

# Solution

Indigo dyeing depends on **air to turn green dye blue** in every single round, like letting each coat of paint dry before adding the next. Skipping airing even for light blue breaks the recipe.

> Every round includes its own airing phase, so shortcuts that drop early airings contradict the procedure for all shades.

## Evaluating all four replies against steps three and four

Step three states <Highlight>It comes out of the bath green and turns blue as it meets the air. One dip and one airing make one round</Highlight>. Checking **replies I through IV**:

- **I (supported)**: Airing is what changes the green cloth into blue.
- **II (supported)**: Every round pairs a dip with an airing, and round count controls shade depth.
- **III (false)**: Light blue still requires **<InlineMath math="2" /> full rounds**, each with its own airing.
- **IV (false)**: Airing happens after **every** dip, never just after the final one.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I only** omits the round-building role of airing in II.
- **II and IV only** wrongly limits airing to the final dip.
- **I, II, and III only** and **I and III only** wrongly excuse light blue from airing.

# Answer

The supported replies are **I and II only**.

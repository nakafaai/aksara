The lighthouse at Tanjung Batu has been dark for six years, since the new beacon was built on the breakwater. Its power line was cut that year, its only door is locked, and the only key hangs on a hook in my grandmother's kitchen, because my grandfather was the last keeper.

The summer I stayed with Nenek, strange things happened around the tower. Some nights a light seemed to move in the lamp room, until my cousin Arif showed me that it was the lamps of the fishing boats, caught in the big lens as they came round the point. On windy nights the tower moaned, and Pak Darto, the harbour master, told us the wind was blowing through a broken vent pipe. One morning there were wet footprints on the tower steps, and after two days Arif admitted that he had been swimming off the rocks at night.

On the night of the big storm, the beacon on the breakwater went out. From Nenek's window I watched the fishing boats come in late and slow, feeling for the harbour mouth. Then the old lighthouse lit up, one long flash every ten seconds, as it had in my grandfather's time, and it shone until the last boat was in. All that night the key hung on its hook in the kitchen; I could see it from where I stood.

In the morning Pak Darto said that a fisherman must have carried a generator up into the lamp room. Arif nodded. Nenek said nothing. She took the key down, wiped it on her sleeve and hung it back on its hook.

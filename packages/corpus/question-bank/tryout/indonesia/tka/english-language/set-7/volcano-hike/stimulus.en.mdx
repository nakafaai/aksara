At two in the morning last July, my cousin Fajar and I started up Mount Kembang with a guide and eight other hikers. We wanted to reach the summit before sunrise.

The first part of the trail ran through pine forest. It was so dark that we could see only the circles of light from our headlamps and the boots of the person in front. Our guide, Bu Endah, stopped us every forty minutes for water and a short rest. At first I thought the breaks were too frequent, but by the third one my legs were shaking.

Above the forest, the path turned into loose gravel, and a cold wind began to blow. When Fajar slipped, his water bottle rolled away into the dark, so we shared mine for the rest of the climb. Bu Endah reminded everyone to step only where she stepped.

We reached the summit at a quarter past five. The sky turned orange behind a line of other mountains, and below us the clouds covered the villages like a white sea. Bu Endah told us we could stay for only twenty minutes, because the wind usually gets stronger after sunrise and clouds can roll in quickly.

On the way down, I finally saw the trail we had climbed in the dark. It was much steeper than I had imagined. I am glad I did not see it at two in the morning.

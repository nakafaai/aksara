# Given

Pak Hendra doubts that **closing the street** caused Kota Lama's jump in shoppers because the trial coincided with **school holidays** and a **new free parking lot**. Four hypothetical facts (I through IV) are tested to see which would **strengthen his doubt**.

# Asked

Which facts among I through IV would strengthen Pak Hendra's doubt that street closure caused the rise in shoppers.

# Solution

To strengthen doubt against **street closure**, look for evidence that gives **stronger credit to school holidays or free parking** instead. Any fact that rules out parking or removes school holidays actually **weakens** Pak Hendra's doubt.

> A fact strengthens an alternative-cause critique only when it shows the competing factor alone explains the outcome or directly drove people's choices.

## Evaluating how I through IV affect Pak Hendra doubt

Text 2 cites two competing causes: <Highlight>It ran during the school holidays, when markets are busy anyway, and the town opened a free parking lot at the edge of the market at the same time</Highlight>. Checking **all four hypothetical facts**:

- **I (strengthens doubt)**: Earlier holiday Sundays drawing larger crowds even **with the street open** shows holidays alone could explain the boost.
- **II (weakens doubt)**: If free parking opened only **after** the trial ended, parking cannot explain the trial's rise.
- **III (strengthens doubt)**: Shoppers stating they came **because of free parking** directly points away from the street closure.
- **IV (does not strengthen)**: Keeping crowds high **after holidays ended** rules out holidays while leaving closure and parking tangled together.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I only** drops the direct parking evidence in III.
- Options with **II** or **IV** pick facts that eliminate one of Pak Hendra's own alternative explanations.

# Answer

The facts that strengthen Pak Hendra's doubt are **I and III only**.

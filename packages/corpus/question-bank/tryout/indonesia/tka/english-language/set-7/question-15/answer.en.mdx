# Given

Five scenarios from **The Dark Lighthouse** are tested for **real-world possibility**: boat lights in a lens (I), wind moaning in a broken pipe (II), a lamp run by a portable generator (III), a lamp shining all night with cut wires, no generator or battery, and a locked door (IV), and a storm knocking out a beacon (V).

# Asked

Which of scenarios I through V could happen in the real world.

# Solution

Step **outside the story's plot** and test each scenario strictly by **everyday physics**. Even though Pak Darto's generator theory feels doubtful inside the story's locked-door mystery, running a lamp off a portable generator (**III**) is completely possible in real life, whereas a lamp glowing with **zero power source** (**IV**) is impossible.

> Distinguish whether an idea is physically possible in the real world from whether it fits a particular story's clues.

## Checking physical realism for I through V

Testing **each event purely on real-world physics**:

- **I (possible)**: Large lighthouse lenses refract and reflect light from passing fishing boats.
- **II (possible)**: Wind rushing across a broken vent pipe produces a moaning sound.
- **III (possible)**: A portable generator can easily power a lighthouse lamp off the grid.
- **IV (impossible)**: With <Highlight>power line cut, no generator or battery inside</Highlight>, an electric lamp has zero energy source to shine all night.
- **V (possible)**: Severe storms routinely knock out coastal beacons.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I, II, and V only** wrongly treats carrying a generator (III) as physically impossible instead of merely doubtful in this plot.
- **I, II, III, IV, and V** accepts a powerless lamp shining all night (IV).
- **I and II only** and **II, III, and V only** drop clearly real-world phenomena.

# Answer

The events that could happen in the real world are **I, II, III, and V only**.

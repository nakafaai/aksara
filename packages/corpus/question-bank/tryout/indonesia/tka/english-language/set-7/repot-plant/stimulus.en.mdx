How to Repot a Houseplant

When roots start growing out of the holes in the bottom of a pot, the plant needs a bigger home. Repotting gives the roots fresh soil and more space. The best time to do it is early in the rainy season, when plants are growing actively.

You will need a new pot one size larger with drainage holes, fresh potting soil, a small trowel, a watering can, and an old newspaper to keep the floor clean.

1. Water the plant lightly the day before. Damp soil holds together and slides out of the pot more easily.
2. Spread the newspaper on the floor and put a layer of fresh soil, about three centimeters deep, in the bottom of the new pot.
3. Turn the old pot on its side, hold the plant at the base of its stems, and gently pull it out. If it is stuck, tap the sides of the pot.
4. Loosen the outer roots with your fingers. Cut off any roots that are black and soft.
5. Place the plant in the new pot so that the top of the root ball sits about two centimeters below the rim.
6. Fill the space around the roots with fresh soil and press it down lightly.
7. Water slowly until water drains out of the holes at the bottom.

Keep the plant out of strong sunlight for about a week while its roots settle. In my experience, a plant that has just been repotted always looks a little tired for a few days, so there is no need to worry.

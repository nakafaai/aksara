The night before the class trip, Sari could not find the key to her bicycle lock. She needed it in the morning to ride to school, where the bus would leave at six. She searched her bag, her desk drawer, and every pocket of her jacket. Nothing.

Her little brother, Dito, watched from the doorway. “Did you check the fridge?” he asked. Sari rolled her eyes. Last week Dito had hidden her headphones in the rice jar as a joke, and she was not in the mood.

At ten o'clock, she gave up and asked her father to drive her in the morning. He agreed, but he had to leave for work early, so they would have to go at half past five. Sari set two alarms and went to bed angry, mostly at herself.

In the morning, she opened the fridge to take out her lunch box. There, on the shelf next to the eggs, lay the key. Now she remembered. She had come home the day before carrying a bag of groceries in one hand and the key in the other, and she had put everything away at the same time.

Dito appeared behind her in his pajamas. He did not say anything. He just raised his eyebrows.

“Fine,” Sari said, handing him one of the two chocolate bars from her lunch box. “You were right. But you are still not touching my headphones.”

She rode her bicycle to school after all, and her father had a quiet breakfast for once.

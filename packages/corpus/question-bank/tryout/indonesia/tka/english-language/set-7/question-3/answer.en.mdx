# Given

The *Kapal Bunga* fare rules state: adult **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}40{,}000" />**, child <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="11" /> **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}20{,}000" />**, motorbike with rider **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}75{,}000" />**, bicycle **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}10{,}000" />**, return ticket equals **two one-way tickets**, resident card halves **only the adult fare**, and public holidays add **<InlineMath math="25\%" />** to every fare.

# Asked

Which of statements I through V about the ferry fares are supported by the text.

# Solution

Treat each Roman-numeral claim like a quick **receipt check** on both sides of the comparison. Computing each exact rupiah total exposes which two claims twist the rules.

> When a passage restricts a discount with phrases like *does not change any other fare*, never apply that discount to vehicle fares.

## Pricing all five statements against the fare rules

The passage writes <Highlight>A return ticket costs the same as two one-way tickets</Highlight> (**I is true**) and halves only the adult ticket (<Highlight>does not change any other fare</Highlight>). Key **fare comparisons**:

<BlockMath math="\text{Resident + bicycle} = 20{,}000 + 10{,}000 = \text{Rp}30{,}000, \qquad \text{Adult + bicycle} = 40{,}000 + 10{,}000 = \text{Rp}50{,}000" />

- **I (supported)**: A return ticket equals two one-way tickets.
- **II (supported)**: Resident adult plus bicycle pays <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}30{,}000" />, exceeding an <InlineMath math="8" />-year-old's <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}20{,}000" />.
- **III (false)**: Motorbike with rider costs <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}75{,}000" />, which is **more**, not less, than <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}50{,}000" />.
- **IV (false)**: Resident cards do not halve motorbike fares, so it stays <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}75{,}000" />.
- **V (supported)**: On a holiday, an <InlineMath math="8" />-year-old pays <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}25{,}000" /> (<InlineMath math="25\%" /> above <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}20{,}000" />).

## Why the other four options fail

- **I and V only** misses statement II (<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}30{,}000 > \text{Rp}20{,}000" />).
- **I, II, IV, and V only** wrongly applies the resident discount to a motorbike.
- **II, III, and V only** and **I, II, III, and V only** reverse the comparison in III.

# Answer

The supported statements are **I, II, and V only**.

# Given

In Text 2 of **Jalan Pasar**, Pak Hendra agrees the street is **too crowded on Sunday mornings**, calls Kota Lama's shopper count **impressive**, points out two extra factors (**school holidays** and a **free parking lot**), notes the **<InlineMath math="20\%" /> takings rise** was never compared with the prior year, and proposes closing **only the northern half** for four Sundays.

# Asked

Which argumentative moves among I through V Text 2 actually makes.

# Solution

Read Pak Hendra's reply like a polite, sharp reviewer: he **accepts the problem and the shopper numbers**, questions **the cause and the takings figure**, and trims the trial down to **half the street** so ambulances can still reach the clinic.

> Spot the subtle difference between doubting *what caused* a number and disputing *whether the number itself happened*.

## Checking all five moves in the reply

Text 2 opens with <Highlight>I agree with the traders that Jalan Pasar is too crowded on Sunday mornings</Highlight> and proposes closing <Highlight>only the northern half of the street</Highlight>. Checking **moves I through V**:

- **I (made)**: He explicitly grants that Sunday mornings are too crowded.
- **II (made)**: He points to school holidays and a newly opened free parking lot.
- **III (not made)**: He calls the shopper count *impressive* rather than disputing it.
- **IV (made)**: He notes the <InlineMath math="20\%" /> figure is only remembered and was never compared with the same Sundays a year earlier.
- **V (not made)**: He proposes closing only the **northern half**, not the whole street.

## Why the other four options fail

- **I, II, and V only** doubles his half-street proposal into the whole street.
- **I, II, III, and IV only** confuses doubting the cause with disputing the shopper count.
- **II and IV only** and **I and IV only** drop one explicit move made in Text 2.

# Answer

Text 2 makes argumentative moves **I, II, and IV only**.

# Given

A cotton cloth received **<InlineMath math="4" /> full rounds** (the exact number specified for mid blue), yet came out **lighter than mid blue**. Five details from the indigo dyeing instructions are offered as possible explanations.

# Asked

Which details among I through V could explain why the cloth came out lighter than mid blue.

# Solution

To explain why dye came out **too pale** even with the right number of rounds, look solely for things that **block dye absorption or weaken the dye bath**. Standard rules the cloth already followed cannot explain the flaw.

> Troubleshooting questions isolate variables that weaken or block the desired outcome, ruling out constant rules that stayed normal.

## Testing all five details as causes of pale colour

Step one warns <Highlight>Starch left in new cloth stops the dye from taking</Highlight> (**I**) and step five warns <Highlight>The bath weakens with use</Highlight> without the extra paste (**II**). Checking **details I through V**:

- **I (explains it)**: Skipping the initial wash leaves starch that stops dye from binding.
- **II (explains it)**: Reusing a bath past <InlineMath math="3" /> cloths without adding <InlineMath math="25" /> g makes the bath too weak.
- **III (cannot explain)**: <InlineMath math="15" />-minute airings are normal for every round and darken cloth properly.
- **IV (cannot explain)**: Dark blue taking <InlineMath math="6" /> rounds has no bearing on why <InlineMath math="4" /> rounds fell short of mid blue.
- **V (cannot explain)**: An <InlineMath math="8" />-litre bath is properly proportioned at <InlineMath math="50" /> g per <InlineMath math="2" /> litres.

## Why the other four options fail

- Every distractor includes normal standard parameters (**III**, **IV**, or **V**) that do not weaken dye uptake.

# Answer

The details that could explain the lighter shade are **I and II only**.

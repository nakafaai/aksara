# Given

From the *Kapal Bunga* passage: a return trip must occur within **<InlineMath math="7" /> days, counting the outward day as day <InlineMath math="1" />**, the ferry makes **two round trips** daily with **<InlineMath math="90" />-minute** crossings and <InlineMath math="30" />-minute pier waits, low tide can shift departures **<InlineMath math="1" /> hour later**, and adults are aged **<InlineMath math="12" /> or over**.

# Asked

All statements among the five options that are supported by the passage.

# Solution

Watch two classic boundary traps: **inclusive day counting** (Monday is day <InlineMath math="1" />, so Sunday is day <InlineMath math="7" /> and the next Monday is day <InlineMath math="8" />) and **sea time versus pier wait time** (four crossings of <InlineMath math="90" /> minutes equal <InlineMath math="6" /> hours at sea).

> Counting the starting day as day <InlineMath math="1" /> always shifts a <InlineMath math="7" />-day window one day earlier than counting from zero.

## Checking all five statements against the rules

The passage defines <Highlight>counting the day of the outward trip as the first day</Highlight> and sets two round trips of <InlineMath math="90" />-minute crossings (<InlineMath math="4 \times 90 = 360" /> minutes, or **<InlineMath math="6" /> hours at sea**). Checking **all five choices**:

- **Monday outward, back following Sunday**: **Supported**, because Monday is day <InlineMath math="1" /> and Sunday is day <InlineMath math="7" />.
- **Monday outward, back following Monday**: **Not supported**, since that Monday is day <InlineMath math="8" />.
- **<InlineMath math="6" /> hours at sea on a normal day**: **Supported**, as <InlineMath math="4 \times 90 = 360" /> minutes equals <InlineMath math="6" /> hours (waits happen at the pier).
- **First ferry leaves Pulau Lima at 8:00 every day**: **Not supported**, because low-tide days shift departure to 9:00.
- **Child of <InlineMath math="12" /> pays child fare**: **Not supported**, since adults are aged <InlineMath math="12" /> or over.

# Answer

The supported statements are:
- **A passenger whose outward trip is on a Monday can still make the trip back on the following Sunday with a return ticket.**
- **On a normal day, the ferry spends <InlineMath math="6" /> hours at sea.**

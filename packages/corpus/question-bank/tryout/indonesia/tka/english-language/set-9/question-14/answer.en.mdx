# Given

Recurring patterns and fixed facts established in the story:
- Evening tinkling comes from the neighbour's porch wind chimes.
- The glass cabinet is always locked, and its only key stays on Ibu's key ring.
- The front room cools at night, causing moisture to mist the cabinet glass.
- On returning home, Nenek gazes at the music box and smiles.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral predictions are reasonable based on the story.

# Solution

Accept predictions grounded directly in established physical patterns or fixed facts, and reject guesses about future character actions that have no textual support.

> Reasonable predictions extend a proven pattern or fixed condition from the story instead of guessing an unsupported change in character behaviour.

## Evaluating each prediction

| Statement | Reasonable? | Grounding in passage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Bayu proved the evening tinkling came from <Highlight>the wind chimes on the neighbour's porch</Highlight>. |
| II | **Yes** | Because <Highlight>its only key is on Ibu's key ring</Highlight>, taking the box out requires Ibu's keys. |
| III | No | Nenek smiles fondly at the box, giving zero hint she would throw it away. |
| IV | **Yes** | Ibu explained the repeatable cause: <Highlight>the room cools at night and the glass sweats</Highlight>. |
| V | No | Nothing in the text hints that Bayu plans to borrow Ibu's keys again. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options with III** contradict Nenek's affectionate smile toward her music box.
- **Options with V** speculate about a repeat action by Bayu with no textual clue.
- **Options missing I or IV** drop a repeatable physical pattern directly established in the story.

# Answer

The reasonable predictions are **I, II, and IV only**.

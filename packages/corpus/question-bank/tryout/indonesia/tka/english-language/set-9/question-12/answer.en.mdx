# Given

The story of Nenek's music box unfolds across four paragraphs:
- Paragraph one introduces the broken spring and the always-locked glass cabinet.
- Paragraph two describes three strange incidents explained by **Bayu** and **Ibu**.
- Paragraph three presents the midnight tune while the cabinet remains locked.
- Paragraph four brings Ibu's dream explanation, the narrator's silence, and Nenek's quiet smile.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral statements accurately describe how the story is organised.

# Solution

Track **the job of each paragraph in order**, checking carefully who explains the incidents in paragraph two and how the final paragraph closes without an explanation.

> Repeating that the cabinet is locked in paragraphs one and three ensures the reader already doubts Ibu's dream theory before she even speaks it.

## Checking the paragraph structure

| Statement | Correct? | Structural evidence in passage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Paragraph one sets up the broken box and locked cabinet, and <Highlight>strange things happened</Highlight> opens paragraph two. |
| II | No | Bayu explains the chimes and moved box while Ibu explains the misted glass, never the narrator. |
| III | No | Nenek offers no explanation and simply <Highlight>looked at the box for a long time, and smiled</Highlight>. |
| IV | **Yes** | The cabinet is <Highlight>always locked</Highlight> in paragraph one and again <Highlight>The cabinet was locked</Highlight> on the night of the tune. |
| V | **Yes** | The locked cabinet appears in paragraph three, before Ibu's <Highlight>must have dreamed it</Highlight> in paragraph four. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options with II** credit the narrator with explanations actually given by Bayu and Ibu.
- **Options without V** overlook that the reader receives the locked-cabinet proof before hearing Ibu dismiss it as a dream.

# Answer

The statements that correctly describe the story's organisation are **I, IV, and V only**.

Last semester, our class turned a gray wall behind the school canteen into a mural. The idea came from our art teacher, Bu Sinta, after students complained that the wall made the area feel gloomy.

First, we voted on a theme. “Our River” won by a narrow margin over “Future City.” Each group of four then drew a small design on paper, and the class chose parts from three of them to combine into one picture.

Before painting, we had to prepare the wall. We scraped off flaking paint, filled two cracks with cement, and covered the whole surface with a white base coat. This took two Saturdays, longer than we had expected, and some of us grumbled that we wanted to start the “real” painting.

The painting itself took four more Saturdays. We sketched the outline in chalk, then filled in large areas of color before adding details such as fish, boats, and a fisherman's net. The hardest part was the river's surface. Our first attempt looked flat, so Bu Sinta showed us how to add thin white lines to make the water seem to move.

When the mural was finished, the canteen owner told us that students had started eating lunch at the tables next to the wall instead of carrying it back to class. Months later, the colors are still bright, while the paint on the gate next to it has already started peeling. Now I understand why Bu Sinta made us spend so long on the base coat.

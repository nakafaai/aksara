In the village of Lembah Tinggi, the oldest houses stand on wooden posts about two meters above the ground. My grandmother's house is one of them. It was built more than sixty years ago from ironwood, a timber so hard that termites rarely damage it.

The space under the house is open on all sides. During the day it is the coolest place in the village, and my grandmother keeps her loom there, along with stacks of firewood and a pair of bicycles. In the rainy season, when the nearby river rises, the water flows under the floor instead of into the rooms.

A wooden ladder with seven steps leads up to a wide veranda. Most visitors are greeted there rather than inside. The front wall is made of woven bamboo panels, and small gaps between the strips let the breeze pass through, so the rooms stay cool without a fan.

Inside, there are only two rooms. The larger one is for sitting, eating, and sleeping on mats, and the smaller one, at the back, is the kitchen. The roof is steep and covered with palm fiber, which keeps out heavy rain and softens its noise.

I have stayed in many houses, but none feels as calm as my grandmother's house when the rain is falling and the river is singing underneath.

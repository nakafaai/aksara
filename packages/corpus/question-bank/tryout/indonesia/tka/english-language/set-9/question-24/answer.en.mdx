# Given

The student council (Text 1) and Bu Rosa (Text 2) share several points of agreement while **disagreeing over causality** and nurse-visit data from SMP Mutiara.

# Asked

All points accepted by **both writers** (select every correct option).

# Solution

Verify that each statement appears with approval in **both** Text 1 and Text 2, ruling out any claim that only the student council makes and Bu Rosa challenges.

> Bu Rosa accepts the sugar problem, the sales figures, and the value of fountains, while rejecting the unmeasured headache claim and questioning the price-charge cause.

## Checking shared ground between both texts

| Statement | Accepted by both? | Evidence across Text 1 and Text 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Students drink too much sugar | **Yes** | Text 1 says <Highlight>Our students drink far too much sugar</Highlight>, and Text 2 replies <Highlight>I agree with the student council that our students drink too much sugar</Highlight>. |
| The charge caused the sales drop | No | Only the council claims this, while Bu Rosa cites the fountain and rainy months. |
| SMP Mutiara sold <InlineMath math="540" /> sweetened drinks a week during the trial | **Yes** | Text 1 reports <Highlight>fell from <InlineMath math="900" /> to <InlineMath math="540" /> a week</Highlight>, and Bu Rosa repeats the exact numbers as striking. |
| Headache visits to the nurse fell | No | Only the council claims this, while Bu Rosa replies <Highlight>we cannot say they fell</Highlight>. |
| Drinking-water fountains are worth installing | **Yes** | The council wants charge revenue spent on fountains, and Bu Rosa proposes installing two fountains first. |

## Why the other two options fail

- Both **causal credit to the charge** and **falling headache visits** are directly challenged by Bu Rosa in Text 2.

# Answer

Both writers accept **Students drink too much sugar**, **During the trial, SMP Mutiara's canteen sold <InlineMath math="540" /> sweetened drinks a week**, and **Drinking-water fountains are worth installing**.

# Given

Five Roman-numeral events (I through V) draw on **elements from Nenek's music-box story**: a secretly repaired box playing at night, wind chimes heard through a wall, a box with a snapped spring playing by itself, a vivid dream of a tune, and cabinet glass misting over on a cool night.

# Asked

Which of these events could happen in the real world.

# Solution

Test each event independently against **real-world physics and everyday human experience** rather than the mysterious mood of the story.

> Separating realism from fantasy comes down to whether a real physical mechanism can drive the event.

## Separating realistic events from fantasy

| Event | Realistically possible? | Physical or everyday explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Fitting a new spring and winding key restores the mechanical power needed to play the tune. |
| II | **Yes** | Sound waves from a neighbour's wind chimes easily travel through house walls. |
| III | No | A music box with <Highlight>its spring snapped</Highlight> and no key has no mechanical energy source to play on its own. |
| IV | **Yes** | Vivid dreams that feel completely real upon waking happen routinely in real life. |
| V | **Yes** | Condensation naturally mists glass whenever air cools overnight. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options without I** wrongly treat a mechanically repaired music box as impossible.
- **Options with III** include the only supernatural event that lacks any power source.
- **Options without IV** overlook that lifelike dreams occur in ordinary experience.

# Answer

The events that could happen in the real world are **I, II, IV, and V only**.

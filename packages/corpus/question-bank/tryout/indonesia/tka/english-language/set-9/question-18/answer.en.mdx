Small, heavy rolls mean the dough didn't rise enough. A detail can explain that only if skipping it weakens the rise.

- **I can explain it**. On a cool evening, a rise kept to the warm-kitchen time is cut short.
- **II can explain it**. Water above <InlineMath math="45" /> degrees "kills the yeast", and dead yeast can't raise the dough.
- **III can explain it**. Salt that touches the yeast "slows it down".
- IV can't. The rolls baked for the full time at the right heat.
- V can't. Equal pieces affect the size of each roll against the others, not whether the dough rose.

The options without I or III leave out a step that slows the rise. The option with V treats even shaping as a cause of heavy bread.

So the details that could explain it are I, II and III only.

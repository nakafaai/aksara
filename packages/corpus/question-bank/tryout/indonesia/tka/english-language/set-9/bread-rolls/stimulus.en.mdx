**Soft bread rolls (makes twelve)**

1. Mix <InlineMath math="500" /> g of flour, <InlineMath math="7" /> g of dried yeast, <InlineMath math="30" /> g of sugar and <InlineMath math="1" /> teaspoon of salt. Put the salt and the yeast on opposite sides of the bowl before you mix, because salt touching yeast directly slows it down.
2. Add <InlineMath math="300" /> ml of warm water. The water must not be above <InlineMath math="45" /> degrees, because hotter water kills the yeast. Knead for <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes.
3. Cover the dough and leave it to rise until it has doubled in size. At about <InlineMath math="28" /> degrees this takes <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes; in a room below <InlineMath math="24" /> degrees, allow twice as long.
4. Divide the dough into <InlineMath math="12" /> pieces of the same weight, shape them, and leave them to rise again for half as long as the first rise took.
5. Bake at <InlineMath math="180" /> degrees for <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes. The rolls are done when they sound hollow when tapped underneath.

To make more rolls, multiply every amount and the number of pieces by the same number, except the yeast: a double batch needs only <InlineMath math="1.5" /> times the yeast.

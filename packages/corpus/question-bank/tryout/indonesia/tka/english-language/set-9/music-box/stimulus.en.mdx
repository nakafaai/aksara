Nenek's music box has not played since its spring snapped, the year before she came to live with us. Its winding key was lost long ago, and the box sits in the glass cabinet in the front room. The cabinet is always locked, and its only key is on Ibu's key ring, which she keeps in her bag.

The month Nenek was in hospital, strange things happened in the front room. Some evenings I heard a tinkling tune, until my brother Bayu showed me that it was the wind chimes on the neighbour's porch. One morning the cabinet glass was misted over, and Ibu said that the room cools at night and the glass sweats. And once the music box had moved to the front of its shelf; Bayu admitted that he had borrowed Ibu's keys to look at it and put it back in a hurry.

On the night before Nenek came home, I woke at two o'clock to a tune I knew at once: the slow song Nenek used to hum while she sewed. It came from the front room. In the cabinet, the lid of the music box was open and its little dancer was turning. The cabinet was locked, Ibu's bag hung on its hook beside her bed, and Bayu was asleep across the room from mine. The tune played to the end and stopped.

In the morning Ibu said I must have dreamed it, and Bayu agreed. I didn't argue. When Nenek came home that afternoon, she went straight to the cabinet, looked at the box for a long time, and smiled.

# Given

Bu Rosa doubts that the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}000" /> charge caused SMP Mutiara's sales drop because of two competing explanations:
- A **free cold-water fountain** installed during the same term.
- **Rainy months**, when cold drinks normally sell less anyway.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral facts, if true, would **weaken Bu Rosa's doubt** that the price charge caused the fall in sales.

# Solution

A fact weakens Bu Rosa's doubt whenever it **rules out a rival explanation** (rain or the fountain) or shows the **same sales drop occurring without those rival factors**.

> Eliminating rain or the water fountain leaves the price charge standing as the strongest explanation for the sales drop.

## Testing each fact against the rival causes

Bu Rosa bases her doubt on <Highlight>a free cold-water fountain</Highlight> and the fact that the trial ran <Highlight>through the rainy months</Highlight>.

| Fact | Weakens doubt? | Effect on rival explanations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | If earlier rainy months showed no sales dip, rain cannot explain the drop. |
| II | **Yes** | Seeing the same drop at another school without any fountain points directly toward the price charge. |
| III | No | Students saying they used the fountain instead of buying drinks **strengthens** Bu Rosa's doubt rather than weakening it. |
| IV | **Yes** | A fountain broken all term could not have drawn buyers away from sweetened drinks. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options with III** include evidence that supports Bu Rosa's fountain explanation instead of undermining it.
- **Options without IV** miss the clearest way to eliminate the water fountain as a competing cause.

# Answer

The facts that would weaken Bu Rosa's doubt are **I, II, and IV only**.

Every year, our school takes students on trips, holds a sports day, and runs practicals in the science lab. Yet if someone gets hurt, most students would not know what to do in the first few minutes. I believe our school should give every Grade 10 student a short course in basic first aid.

First, students are often the first people on the scene. Teachers cannot be everywhere at once. During last year's trip to a waterfall, a student cut her hand on a rock while the teachers were at the front of the group. Her friends wanted to help, but they did not know whether to wash the cut, press on it, or simply wait.

Second, the basic skills are quick to learn. Last semester, the school's youth health club ran a trial course, teaching one class of thirty students over two afternoons. Among other things, the students learned how to turn an unconscious person who is still breathing onto their side, which is called the recovery position. The class took the same ten-question quiz before the course and again right after it.

Because the second quiz came right after the course, it shows what the students remembered that day, not how they would act in a real emergency. A short refresher every year would help them keep the skills.

Third, first aid is useful outside school as well. A trained student can help a younger brother who burns a finger in the kitchen or a neighbor who slips on a wet road.

Some parents worry that a student who knows only a little might make an injury worse. This worry deserves attention. However, the course teaches students to call an adult or an ambulance first and to do only simple, safe things: pressing a clean cloth on a bleeding cut, cooling a burn under running water, and placing a breathing, unconscious person in the recovery position. It also teaches them never to move someone who may have hurt their neck. A student who knows these limits is more useful than a frightened one who guesses.

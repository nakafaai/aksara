# Given

A neighbour claims: *"Hot water makes yeast work faster, so I use boiling water to save time."*

Relevant recipe rules:
- Step <InlineMath math="2" />: water must not exceed **<InlineMath math="45" /> degrees**, because hotter water kills the yeast.
- Step <InlineMath math="3" />: dough rises in **<InlineMath math="60" /> minutes** at about <InlineMath math="28" /> degrees, and takes **twice as long** below <InlineMath math="24" /> degrees.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral replies to the neighbour are supported by the instructions.

# Solution

Check which replies correct the boiling-water mistake using step two's temperature limit and offer step three's safe warm-room way to speed up rising.

> Boiling water kills yeast outright, whereas keeping the dough in a warm room around twenty-eight degrees is the recipe's real way to save rising time.

## Evaluating each reply against the recipe

| Statement | Supported? | Evidence from recipe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Step <InlineMath math="2" /> bans water above <InlineMath math="45" /> degrees because <Highlight>hotter water kills the yeast</Highlight>. |
| II | **Yes** | Step <InlineMath math="3" /> gives the safe speed-up: <Highlight>At about 28 degrees this takes 60 minutes; in a room below 24 degrees, allow twice as long.</Highlight> |
| III | No | Kneading longer cannot revive yeast already killed by boiling water. |
| IV | No | The <InlineMath math="45" />-degree limit applies to every batch, not only double batches. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Option I only** misses the valid warm-room advice in step three.
- **Options with III** approve boiling water with a kneading condition the recipe never supports.
- **Options with IV** restrict the water-temperature rule to double batches alone.

# Answer

The supported replies are **I and II only**.

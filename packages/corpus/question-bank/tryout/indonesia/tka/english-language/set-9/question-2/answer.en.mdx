# Given

Five scenarios pair a **Harian** (<InlineMath math="1" /> day), **Mingguan** (<InlineMath math="7" /> days), or **Bulanan** (<InlineMath math="30" /> days) purchase time with a later check time.

Kilat's validity rule:
- Every package counts **the purchase date as day one** and **ends at 23:59 on its last calendar day**, regardless of purchase hour.

# Asked

Whether each package is **Still valid** or **Already ended** at the time of the check.

# Solution

Find the final calendar day by counting the purchase day as day one, then compare the check time against **23:59** on that final day.

> Validity follows calendar days ending at 23:59, never exact twenty-four-hour multiples from the purchase clock time.

## Checking each package against its cutoff

The passage sets the exact boundary: <Highlight>ends at 23:59 on its last day, counting the day of purchase as the first day</Highlight>.

| Statement | Expiry cutoff | Check time | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harian, Monday 23:00 | Monday 23:59 (day <InlineMath math="1" />) | Tuesday 0:30 | **Already ended** |
| Mingguan, Wednesday 8:00 | Tuesday 23:59 (day <InlineMath math="7" />) | Tuesday 22:00 | **Still valid** |
| Mingguan, Wednesday 8:00 | Tuesday 23:59 (day <InlineMath math="7" />) | Wednesday 7:00 | **Already ended** |
| Bulanan, 1 March 10:00 | 30 March 23:59 (day <InlineMath math="30" />) | 30 March 23:00 | **Still valid** |
| Bulanan, 1 March 10:00 | 30 March 23:59 (day <InlineMath math="30" />) | 31 March 0:10 | **Already ended** |

## Common trap to avoid

- Counting exact <InlineMath math="24" />-hour blocks from 8:00 on Wednesday or 10:00 on 1 March falsely marks statements 3 and 5 as still valid.

# Answer

Statements **2 and 4** are **Still valid**, while statements **1, 3, and 5** are **Already ended**.

# Given

Details spread across the story of Nenek's music box:
- Paragraph one: **the box's spring snapped** before Nenek moved in, and its winding key was lost long ago.
- Paragraph two: Bayu admitted borrowing Ibu's keys earlier that month to look at the box.
- Paragraph three: at two in the morning Nenek's slow sewing tune came from the front room while Bayu slept across the room.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral conclusions the reader can draw by combining details across paragraphs.

# Solution

Link **the broken spring** in paragraph one with Bayu's earlier handling and his whereabouts during the midnight tune, while separating the front-room song from the neighbour's wind chimes.

> Keeping the neighbour's wind chimes separate from Nenek's sewing song prevents confusing an earlier evening with the night before Nenek returned.

## Combining evidence across paragraphs

| Statement | Valid conclusion? | Evidence from passage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | The box <Highlight>has not played since its spring snapped</Highlight>, so its own spring could not have driven the tune. |
| II | **Yes** | While the tune played, <Highlight>Bayu was asleep across the room from mine</Highlight>. |
| III | No | The narrator recognised Nenek's slow sewing song coming from the front room, not the neighbour's porch. |
| IV | **Yes** | Earlier that month Bayu admitted <Highlight>he had borrowed Ibu's keys to look at it and put it back in a hurry</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options with III** carry the earlier wind-chime explanation over to a completely different night and sound.
- **Options without I** omit the snapped spring that makes the midnight tune impossible on its own mechanism.

# Answer

The reader can conclude **I, II, and IV only**.

Nadia had taken the city bus to her aunt's house a dozen times, always with her mother. This Saturday was the first time she went alone. Her mother had written the route on a card: Bus 7 to the clock tower, then walk two blocks to the bakery with the green awning.

Nadia got on the first bus with a 7 on its sign and sat by the window, reading her book. When she looked up twenty minutes later, there was no clock tower. There were rice fields. The sign above the driver read 7B.

Her stomach dropped. She wanted to call her mother, but her phone battery was almost dead. Instead, she walked to the front and asked the driver, in a smaller voice than she intended, whether this bus went to the clock tower. The driver laughed kindly. “That's the 7. This is the 7B. Get off at the next stop and cross the road. The 7B going back will take you to the market, and the 7 stops there too.”

Nadia followed his instructions, holding the card so tightly that it bent. At the market, she found a Bus 7, checked the sign twice, and this time kept her book in her bag. She reached the bakery with the green awning forty minutes late.

Her aunt was waiting outside, phone in hand. Nadia expected a scolding. Instead, her aunt asked how she had found her way back. When Nadia told her, her aunt smiled and took a pen from her pocket. On the back of the card, she wrote: “Check the letter, not just the number.”

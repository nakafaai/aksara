# Given

Kakek's actions across the story:
- He covers and seals the seed-rice jar with cloth and candle wax to keep out damp and insects.
- On planting morning, he and the narrator unlock the storeroom together.
- When the aunt guesses he soaked the seed and forgot, Kakek says nothing and carries the jar to the field to plant every shoot before noon.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral statements about Kakek are supported by the story.

# Solution

Keep Kakek's documented actions separate from the **aunt's unconfirmed guess**, and never treat silently planting the shoots as agreement with her.

> Kakek's silence leaves the aunt's guess unanswered, and planting the shoots on planting day is practical action rather than agreement.

## Evaluating each statement about Kakek

| Statement | Supported? | Evidence from passage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | The jar is <Highlight>sealed all round with candle wax, so that no damp and no insect could get in</Highlight>. |
| II | No | Planting the shoots does not signal agreement because the text says <Highlight>Kakek didn't answer</Highlight>. |
| III | **Yes** | On planting morning, <Highlight>Kakek and I unlocked the storeroom together</Highlight>. |
| IV | No | Nothing confirms Kakek soaked the seed, as that is only the aunt's guess and is undercut by the dry rice. |
| V | **Yes** | <Highlight>Kakek didn't answer</Highlight>, leaving her explanation unanswered. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options with II** misread planting the sprouts as agreeing with the aunt.
- **Options with IV** treat the aunt's unsupported guess as an established fact.
- **Options without I** omit Kakek's careful dry-storage method from paragraph one.

# Answer

The supported statements about Kakek are **I, III, and V only**.

# Given

Details spread across the story of Kakek's seed-rice jar:
- Paragraph one: the jar mouth is covered with cloth and sealed round with candle wax, while the storeroom's only key hangs on a nail above Kakek's bed every evening.
- Paragraph two: Dimas admitted only that he pressed the cloth one morning to test the wax.
- Paragraph three: on planting morning the wax seal was whole, the rice was dry as sand with no water near it, and the key had hung above Kakek's bed all night while the narrator slept beside him.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral conclusions the reader can draw by combining details from different parts of the story.

# Solution

Join the single-key setup in paragraph one with the overnight watch, unbroken wax seal, and bone-dry rice in paragraph three, while keeping Dimas's earlier wax test separate.

> Linking the untouched key, intact wax seal, and sand-dry rice establishes that no one unlocked the door overnight and the shoots appeared without water.

## Combining evidence across paragraphs

| Statement | Valid conclusion? | Evidence from passage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Kakek hangs <Highlight>the only key on a nail above his bed</Highlight>, and <Highlight>All night the key had hung above Kakek's bed</Highlight> with the narrator beside him. |
| II | **Yes** | <Highlight>The wax seal was whole</Highlight> right up until Kakek <Highlight>cut the wax</Highlight>. |
| III | No | Dimas only <Highlight>pressed it to test the wax</Highlight> on an earlier morning, never making the seeds sprout as a joke. |
| IV | **Yes** | The text states directly that <Highlight>the rice was as dry as sand and no water had been near it</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options with III** stretch Dimas's earlier touch on the cloth into a prank causing the sprouting.
- **Options missing I or II** drop a key fact that makes the planting-day discovery inexplicable.

# Answer

The reader can conclude **I, II, and IV only**.

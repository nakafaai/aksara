# Given

Chili seedlings planted out during the **dry season** **wilted badly in their first week**.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral steps from the instructions, if skipped, could explain this wilting.

# Solution

Wilting happens when seedlings **lose water faster than their roots can absorb it**, so pick only steps that protect root uptake, dry-season water supply, or shade from harsh sun.

> Only steps affecting root soil, dry-season watering volume, and sun shade explain first-week wilting, never row-spacing or edge-placement rules.

## Checking how skipping each step affects water loss

| Step | Explains wilting? | Consequence if skipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Skipping tray watering (<Highlight>so that the roots come out with their soil</Highlight>) strips soil from the roots and weakens water uptake. |
| II | **Yes** | Skipping the dry-season <Highlight><InlineMath math="500" /> ml</Highlight> morning watering leaves each seedling with half the water it needs. |
| III | **Yes** | Skipping <Highlight>Shade the bed with palm leaves for the first <InlineMath math="3" /> days</Highlight> exposes tender seedlings to drying sunlight. |
| IV | No | Putting a row every <InlineMath math="70" /> cm governs layout spacing rather than first-week water balance. |
| V | No | Starting at one end of the row has no effect on whether seedlings dry out. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options without I** overlook how bare roots struggle to absorb water after transplanting.
- **Options with IV or V** treat spacing geometry as a direct cause of rapid wilting.

# Answer

The steps whose omission could explain the wilting are **I, II, and III only**.

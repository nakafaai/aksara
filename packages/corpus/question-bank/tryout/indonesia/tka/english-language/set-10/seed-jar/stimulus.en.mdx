Kakek kept the seed rice for next season in a clay jar in the storeroom. The mouth of the jar was covered with a cloth and sealed all round with candle wax, so that no damp and no insect could get in. The storeroom has one door, and every evening Kakek locks it and hangs the only key on a nail above his bed.

In the weeks before planting, strange things happened around the storeroom. At night there was rustling behind the door, until Kakek found a gecko living in the roof beams. One morning the cloth on the jar looked loose, and my cousin Dimas admitted that he had pressed it to test the wax. And after every storm the storeroom smelled of wet earth, because rain gets in under the tiles.

On the morning of planting day, Kakek and I unlocked the storeroom together. The wax seal was whole and the cloth was tight. When Kakek cut the wax and lifted the cloth, the jar was full of green shoots, every grain sprouted a finger's length, though the rice was as dry as sand and no water had been near it. All night the key had hung above Kakek's bed, and I had slept on the mat beside him.

My aunt said Kakek must have soaked the seed and forgotten. Kakek didn't answer. He carried the jar to the field and planted every shoot before noon.

In January, heavy rain made the river behind our neighborhood overflow. My older brother and I volunteered at the emergency kitchen that was set up in the village hall.

On the first morning, the coordinator, Pak Yusuf, gave each volunteer a job. My brother joined the team cooking rice in huge pots, while I was put at the table where food was packed into boxes. Before we started, Pak Yusuf made everyone wash their hands with soap and wear masks and gloves. He explained that stomach illness spreads quickly when many people share one kitchen.

By noon we had packed four hundred boxes. A motorbike team took them to families who could not leave their homes because the water was still knee-deep. In the afternoon, some families came to the hall themselves, and we served them at long tables.

On the third day, the water went down, but our work changed rather than ended. Instead of cooking, volunteers carried mud out of houses and washed furniture. My hands were sore by the evening, and I was surprised at how heavy wet mud is.

Looking back, I remember the packing table most. I had never thought about how much planning a single box of rice and vegetables needs. Someone has to count the families, order the food, cook it safely, and deliver it before it gets cold.

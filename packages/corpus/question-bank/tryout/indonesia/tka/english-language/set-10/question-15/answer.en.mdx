# Given

Five Roman-numeral events (I through V) test realism against fantasy: rice secretly watered and resealed a week earlier, pressed cloth under intact wax, dry rice sprouting overnight without water, a duplicate key opening the door, and a rustling gecko.

# Asked

Which of these events could happen in the real world.

# Solution

Judge each event purely by real-world biology and physics rather than the story's plot constraints about how many keys existed or whether anyone opened the jar.

> Testing realism against fantasy asks whether science allows the event in real life, never whether the story's specific plot allowed it.

## Separating realistic events from fantasy

| Event | Realistically possible? | Biological or physical reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Damp rice watered a week earlier can sprout a finger's length, and fresh wax can reseal the jar. |
| II | **Yes** | Cloth beneath a rim of wax can be pressed lightly and smoothed back without breaking the seal. |
| III | No | Dry seed with zero moisture (<Highlight>as dry as sand and no water had been near it</Highlight>) cannot biologically sprout a finger's length overnight. |
| IV | **Yes** | In real life a duplicate key opens a lock even while the first key remains hanging on its nail. |
| V | **Yes** | Geckos commonly live in roof beams and rustle at night. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options without I or IV** reject physically realistic scenarios merely because the fictional story stated there was only one key or nobody opened the jar.
- **Options with III** accept the one biologically impossible event.

# Answer

The events that could happen in the real world are **I, II, IV, and V only**.

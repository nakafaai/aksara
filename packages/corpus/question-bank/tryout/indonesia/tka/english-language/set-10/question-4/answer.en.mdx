# Given

Town library rules:
- Borrowing day is day <InlineMath math="0" />, and a book loan lasts <InlineMath math="14" /> days.
- The library closes on Sundays and charges no fine for a Sunday.
- Renewals happen before the due date **online or at the desk** and run from the old due date.
- Every member shares the <InlineMath math="6" />-item cap, while members under <InlineMath math="12" /> pay half the fine.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral cause-and-effect statements are supported by the passage.

# Solution

Verify that both the stated rule and the claimed consequence hold under the text, watching closely for the **online-renewal option** and for unrelated rules falsely linked together.

> Online renewals allow Sunday renewals even while the building is closed, and half-price fines for children never change the universal six-item borrowing limit.

## Checking each cause and effect claim

| Statement | Supported? | Evidence from passage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Counting borrowing day as day <InlineMath math="0" /> puts <InlineMath math="14" /> days later on the same weekday (<Highlight>a book borrowed on a Monday is due on the Monday two weeks later</Highlight>). |
| II | **Yes** | Because <Highlight>no fine is charged for a Sunday</Highlight>, a DVD due Saturday and returned Monday counts only <InlineMath math="1" /> late day (Monday). |
| III | **Yes** | Because <Highlight>each renewal runs from the old due date</Highlight>, renewing early never forfeits remaining days. |
| IV | No | Items may be renewed <Highlight>online or at the desk</Highlight>, so Sunday closure does not block online renewals. |
| V | No | Paying half the fine under age <InlineMath math="12" /> has no effect on the universal <InlineMath math="6" />-item borrowing cap. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options with IV** overlook that members can renew online even when the desk is closed.
- **Options with V** invent a link between fine discounts and the item limit.
- **Options missing I or III** omit clear consequences stated directly in the rules.

# Answer

The supported cause-and-effect statements are **I, II, and III only**.

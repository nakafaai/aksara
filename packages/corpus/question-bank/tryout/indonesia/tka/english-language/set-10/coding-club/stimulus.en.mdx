Our school offers clubs in football, music, and journalism, but nothing in computing. I believe the school should start a coding club that meets once a week after school.

First, coding teaches a way of thinking that helps in other subjects. To write a program, students must break a big problem into small steps and test each one. That is the same skill they need to solve word problems in mathematics or to plan a science experiment.

Second, students are already interested. When the ICT teacher ran a free six-week trial last term, he expected about fifteen students. Thirty-two signed up, and most of them kept coming.

The trial was free and ran during the dry season, when fewer students had other activities, so attendance in a regular club might be lower. Still, most students kept coming until the end.

Third, a coding club needs little equipment. Free beginner programs run on the school's existing computers, and students can practice on their phones at home.

Some parents worry that more screen time is bad for their children. This concern is reasonable. However, the club would meet for only ninety minutes a week, and much of that time would be spent planning on paper and discussing ideas in groups. The school could run the club for one semester and ask students and parents for feedback at the end.

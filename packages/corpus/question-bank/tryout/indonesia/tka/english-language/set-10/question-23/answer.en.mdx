# Given

Pak Yudi doubts that the bike racks caused the jump in cycling at SMA Harapan because two other changes occurred in the same term:
- A **new cycle lane** opened on the main road to SMA Harapan.
- **Fuel prices rose**.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral facts, if true, would **weaken Pak Yudi's doubt** that the racks caused the rise in cycling.

# Solution

A fact weakens Pak Yudi's doubt whenever it **shows the same cycling increase with racks alone** or **undermines the new cycle lane** as the explanation for the new cyclists.

> Evidence pointing away from the cycle lane weakens Pak Yudi's doubt, whereas evidence crediting the cycle lane strengthens his doubt instead.

## Testing each fact against the rival causes

Pak Yudi's doubt rests on the fact that <Highlight>the town opened a cycle lane on the main road to SMA Harapan, and fuel prices rose</Highlight>.

| Fact | Weakens doubt? | Effect on rival explanations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Matching cycling growth at a school with racks and no new cycle lane strengthens the case for racks. |
| II | No | Cycling rising only after the lane opened points to the lane, **strengthening** Pak Yudi's doubt. |
| III | No | New cyclists crediting the lane directly **strengthens** Pak Yudi's doubt. |
| IV | **Yes** | If most new cyclists live far from the cycle lane, the lane is unlikely to explain their switch to cycling. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options with II or III** select facts that reinforce Pak Yudi's cycle-lane explanation rather than weakening it.
- **Options without IV** miss the geographic evidence discounting the cycle lane.

# Answer

The facts that would weaken Pak Yudi's doubt are **I and IV only**.

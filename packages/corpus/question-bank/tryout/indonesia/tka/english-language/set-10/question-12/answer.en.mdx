# Given

The four-paragraph structure of Kakek's seed-jar story:
- Paragraph one describes the wax-sealed jar, locked storeroom door, and key above Kakek's bed.
- Paragraph two recounts three strange happenings explained via **Kakek** (the gecko), **Dimas** (pressing the cloth), and rain under the tiles.
- Paragraph three reveals the dry rice full of green shoots and repeats that the key hung above Kakek's bed.
- Paragraph four gives the aunt's guess, Kakek's silence, and his planting every shoot before noon.

# Asked

Which Roman-numeral statements accurately describe how the story is organised.

# Solution

Check each statement against the **sequence of the four paragraphs**, noting who uncovered the causes in paragraph two and what the reader already knows before the aunt speaks.

> Revealing the bone-dry rice in paragraph three ensures the reader already sees the flaw in the aunt's soaking guess in paragraph four.

## Checking the four paragraph structure

| Statement | Correct? | Structural evidence in passage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | The wax seal and locked door appear in paragraph one, before <Highlight>strange things happened</Highlight> opens paragraph two. |
| II | No | <Highlight>Kakek found a gecko</Highlight> and Dimas admitted pressing the cloth, so the narrator did not uncover everything unaided. |
| III | No | <Highlight>Kakek didn't answer</Highlight>, ending the story in action rather than explanation. |
| IV | **Yes** | The key above Kakek's bed appears in paragraph one and returns in paragraph three (<Highlight>All night the key had hung above Kakek's bed</Highlight>). |
| V | **Yes** | Readers learn the rice was <Highlight>as dry as sand</Highlight> in paragraph three before hearing the aunt's guess in paragraph four. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options with II** credit the narrator alone with discoveries made by Kakek and confessed by Dimas.
- **Options without V** overlook the deliberate ordering that undercuts the aunt's explanation as soon as she speaks.

# Answer

The statements that correctly describe the story's organisation are **I, IV, and V only**.

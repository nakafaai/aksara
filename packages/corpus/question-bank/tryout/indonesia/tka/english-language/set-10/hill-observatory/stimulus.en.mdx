On a hill above the town of Sukaluhur stands a small observatory run by the local astronomy club. From the road it looks like a white water tank with a round metal roof. The roof can turn, though, and at night a narrow strip of it slides open so that a telescope can look out at the sky.

The building has two floors. On the ground floor is a classroom with twenty folding chairs and a screen where club members show photographs of the Moon, the planets, and star clusters. A spiral staircase leads to the upper floor, where the main telescope sits on a heavy concrete pillar. The pillar is not connected to the floor around it, so footsteps do not shake the telescope.

Outside, a flat lawn surrounds the building. On clear weekend nights, members set up smaller telescopes there so that visitors can take turns looking through them. Red lamps line the path from the gate, because red light does not spoil the eyes' ability to see in the dark as much as white light does.

The observatory is open to the public on the first Saturday of every month, from seven to ten at night. Entry is free, but visitors are asked to turn their phone screens down to the lowest brightness.

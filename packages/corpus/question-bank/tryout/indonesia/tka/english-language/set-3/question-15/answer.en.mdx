# Given

**Four events from Uncle Bonar's shop** are listed: (I) a silent radio bursting into sound when lifted, (II) the cat sitting by the door a minute before the motorbike appears, (III) Grandmother's valve radio playing from the top shelf, and (IV) the wall clock reading five early.

# Asked

Which numbered events in the story **could not happen in the real world**.

# Solution

Put on a physicist's hat and test each incident. Three of them receive **completely ordinary real-world explanations** right in the text, leaving **only one event that breaks basic laws of electricity**.

> Do not judge by first impressions of mystery: check whether the passage supplies an everyday cause or deliberately rules out every ordinary explanation.

## Checking each event against reality

| Event | Possible in reality? | Direct evidence in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Yes | A loose connection (<Highlight>a wire whose end had worked loose</Highlight>) briefly closed the circuit when the radio tipped. |
| II | Yes | Cats hear faint sounds well before humans: Belang <Highlight>simply heard the engine at the corner, long before we could</Highlight>. |
| III | **No** | Grandmother's radio <Highlight>ran only on mains power, and that morning he had pulled its plug from the wall</Highlight>, yet the song came from the top shelf and <Highlight>its speaker cloth trembled under my fingers</Highlight>. An unplugged mains-only radio cannot power itself. |
| IV | Yes | Uncle intentionally <Highlight>set the wall clock ten minutes fast</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Any option including I, II, or IV** ignores the ordinary physical explanations explicitly given in the story.

# Answer

The only event that could not happen in the real world is **III only**.

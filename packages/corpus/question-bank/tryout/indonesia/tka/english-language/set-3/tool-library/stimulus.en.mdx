The Selaras Tool Library fills the ground floor of a former printing house on Jalan Melati. Instead of books, its shelves hold drills, ladders, sewing machines, pressure washers and about <InlineMath math="300" /> other tools. Each tool carries a coloured band that sets the rules for borrowing it.

Anyone who lives in the district can join. A basic membership costs <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}60{,}000" /> a year and allows one tool at a time. A family membership costs <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000" /> a year and allows three tools at a time in total, borrowed on the cards of up to four people in the same household. Members pay nothing to borrow, but a red-band tool needs a deposit, which comes back in full when the tool is returned clean and working.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Green | hand tools, garden tools | <InlineMath math="14" /> days | none | <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2{,}000" /> per day |
| Yellow | drills, sanders, sewing machines | <InlineMath math="7" /> days | none | <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" /> per day |
| Red | pressure washers, tile cutters, generators | <InlineMath math="3" /> days | <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}150{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}20{,}000" /> per day |

The loan period counts every calendar day, and the day a tool leaves the library is day one. So a green-band tool taken out on the 1st is due back on the 14th, and a yellow-band tool taken out on a Wednesday is due back on the Tuesday of the following week. The library is closed on Mondays, and a tool that falls due on a Monday may come back on the Tuesday without a late fee. No loan is ever charged more than ten days of late fees. Volunteers check every returned tool, and one that comes back dirty is cleaned for a flat <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}10{,}000" />. For a red-band tool, this charge and any late fee are taken from the deposit. For other tools, they are added to the member's account.

The library opened in 2021 with <InlineMath math="40" /> tools donated by a retired carpenter, Pak Darmo. From then until the end of last year, it lent tools <InlineMath math="2{,}900" /> times. Its coordinator, Wulan Hartati, says the number she watches most closely is not loans but repairs. About one tool in eight comes back needing some repair, and the Saturday repair table now fixes most of them on the spot. "Lending only works if the tools keep working," she says. A survey of <InlineMath math="210" /> members last year found that <InlineMath math="64\%" /> had put off buying a tool because they could borrow it. The survey did not ask which tools, or whether those members bought them later anyway.

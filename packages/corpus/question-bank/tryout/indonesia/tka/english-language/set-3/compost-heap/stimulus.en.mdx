**Is your compost ready? A guide from the Sido Rukun community garden**

**Building the heap.** Mix about three parts browns (dry leaves, shredded cardboard) to one part greens (vegetable scraps, fresh grass). Measure by the bucketful, not by weight: wet greens weigh far more than dry browns, so a ratio by weight puts in far too few greens. Keep the heap as damp as a wrung-out sponge.

**The hot weeks.** Check the temperature at the centre of the heap with a long thermometer every three days. A working heap reaches <InlineMath math="55" /> to <InlineMath math="65^\circ\text{C}" /> within its first week. During the first six weeks, turn the heap whenever the centre falls below <InlineMath math="45^\circ\text{C}" />. After six weeks, stop turning it and let it cool and mature.

**The ready test.** Compost is ready only when all three of these hold:

1. At least eight weeks have passed since the heap was built.
2. The centre has stayed within <InlineMath math="5^\circ\text{C}" /> of the air temperature on two checks in a row.
3. It smells of earth, not of ammonia or rot.

**Fixing problems.** These fixes apply at any stage, and a turn made to fix a problem is the one exception to the six-week rule.

| What you notice | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smells of ammonia | Too many greens | Add browns |
| Smells sour or rotten, and feels slimy | Too wet | Turn the heap and add browns |
| Stays cold in the first week, and is dry | Too dry | Add water while turning |
| Stays cold in the first week, damp, no smell | Too few greens | Add greens |

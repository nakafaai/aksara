# Given

The Sukamaju trial table (dimmed in <InlineMath math="2025" />) records burglaries moving from **<InlineMath math="18" /> to <InlineMath math="15" />**, crashes from **<InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="5" />**, and lighting complaints from **<InlineMath math="4" /> to <InlineMath math="31" />**. Right below the table, the writer **explicitly narrows what the trial shows**.

# Asked

According to the writer, which numbered statements the Sukamaju trial shows.

# Solution

Focus on **"according to the writer"**. Pair the table figures with **the writer's own disclaimers** in paragraph four, where exaggerated readings of the drop in burglaries are flatly rejected.

> When a writer explicitly disclaims an inference ("I do not claim..."), any option repeating that claim is eliminated immediately.

## Checking each statement against the author view

| Statement | Shown? | Evidence from table and paragraph four |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Yes | Burglaries went from <InlineMath math="18" /> to <InlineMath math="15" />, matching the writer's narrower point: <Highlight>nothing in the trial got worse</Highlight>. |
| II | Yes | Night-time crashes went from <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="5" />, so they did not rise. |
| III | Yes | Complaints rose by <InlineMath math="27" /> (over <InlineMath math="25" />), acknowledged openly: <Highlight>Complaints did jump, from <InlineMath math="4" /> to <InlineMath math="31" /></Highlight>. |
| IV | No | Explicitly disclaimed in <Highlight>I do not claim that darkness keeps burglars away</Highlight>. |
| V | No | Directly contradicted: a drop of three <Highlight>is well within the usual rise and fall of such numbers</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **I and II only** omits the admitted complaint surge in III.
- **Options including IV or V** attribute claims the writer explicitly denies.

# Answer

According to the writer, the Sukamaju trial shows **I, II, and III only**.

**Dim the lights after midnight**

Kotabaru should dim the streetlights on its residential streets to half brightness between midnight and 5:00. It would save money, and the city's own trial suggests that it would not make those streets less safe.

The city spends <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}9.6" /> billion a year on electricity for streetlights, which burn for twelve hours a night. Residential streets carry <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of the lights, and a light dimmed to half brightness uses about half the electricity. Dimming those lights for five of their twelve hours would therefore save about <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}800" /> million a year. A city that lights empty streets at full power all night is wasting money it does not have. The savings, about <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}800" /> million a year, are too large to ignore.

In 2025, the city dimmed the lights on twelve residential streets in Sukamaju for a year and left twelve similar streets in Sukajadi at full brightness.

I do not claim that darkness keeps burglars away. A fall of three in a year is well within the usual rise and fall of such numbers, and the streets left bright saw a fall too. The point is narrower: nothing in the trial got worse. Complaints did jump, from <InlineMath math="4" /> to <InlineMath math="31" />, but most of them came in the first two months, and the trial did not record how many came from people who were actually out after midnight.

The night-shift workers' union replies that walking home under dim lights feels unsafe, whatever the figures say. That feeling matters, which is why the proposal leaves main roads and every bus stop at full brightness. The people the union speaks for walk mostly along those roads.

Dimming is not a gamble. It is a small, tested change that pays for itself from the first night.

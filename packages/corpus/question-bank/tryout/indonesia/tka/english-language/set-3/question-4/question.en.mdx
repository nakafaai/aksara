After an evening shower, the Sora night market changes without becoming quiet. Water collects in the shallow stone channels that run between the food rows. Vendors place narrow wooden bridges across them, leaving bright yellow edges visible under the lamps. The bridges keep shoes dry and also show where the safest crossing points are.

Near the entrance, fruit stalls glow beneath white lights. Sellers wipe rain from clear covers instead of removing them, so customers can still see the colors without touching wet produce. Handwritten price cards hang inside the covers. Farther in, the cooking row uses warmer lamps. Steam rises from soup pots and mixes with the cooler air, making the signs appear and disappear behind a thin cloud.

The craft area is quieter. Fabric bags and paper prints have been moved from open tables to vertical racks beneath deep awnings. Each rack stands a hand's width from the wall, allowing damp air to move behind it. Small clips hold the lower corners when wind enters the lane.

At the center, a covered seating space surrounds a drain marked with a blue circle. Staff keep chairs outside the circle so water can flow freely. A board lists the time of the last floor check and the name of the person responsible.

Rain does not simply make the market shine. It reveals a design built around temporary change. Covers, bridges, raised displays, and clear drainage marks allow trade to continue while reminding visitors that the ground, air, and movement are different from a dry evening.

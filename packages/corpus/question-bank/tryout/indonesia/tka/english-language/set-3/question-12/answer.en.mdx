# Given

Three time clues sit in separate paragraphs of the story:
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="1" />: Uncle Bonar sets the wall clock **ten minutes fast**.
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />: The neighbouring tailor locks up and leaves **at noon**.
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="5" />: The lullaby begins when the wall clock shows **five** (<InlineMath math="5:00" />).

# Asked

Combining details across the story, which numbered statements are true.

# Solution

Picture setting a kitchen clock ten minutes fast so you are never late for dinner. Because the clock runs ahead, **the true time is always ten minutes earlier** than what the clock face displays.

> A fast clock reads ahead of reality, so finding the real time means subtracting the offset rather than adding it.

## Calculating real time and elapsed time

Because Uncle <Highlight>set the wall clock ten minutes fast</Highlight> and the song started <Highlight>When the wall clock said five</Highlight>, the true time and the time elapsed since noon follow directly.

**The real time** sits ten minutes earlier than 5:00, namely 4:50, which is <InlineMath math="4" /> hours <InlineMath math="50" /> minutes after noon.

## Checking each statement

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence and calculation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | True | Ten minutes before a fast <InlineMath math="5:00" /> is about <InlineMath math="4:50" />. |
| II | True | The tailor left <Highlight>At noon</Highlight>, and from <InlineMath math="12:00" /> to <InlineMath math="4:50" /> is <InlineMath math="4" /> hours <InlineMath math="50" /> minutes, or nearly five hours. |
| III | False | <InlineMath math="5:10" /> goes the wrong way by adding ten minutes instead of subtracting. |
| IV | False | The fast clock read <InlineMath math="5:00" />, which is ten minutes **later** than real time (<InlineMath math="4:50" />), not earlier. |

## Why the other options fail

- **I only** overlooks the cross-paragraph calculation for the tailor in II.
- **Options including III or IV** reverse how a fast clock relates to real time.

# Answer

The true statements formed by combining details across the story are **I and II only**.

By Sunday night I had already explained the difference to anyone who would listen, so here is how the day went.

Eight of us from Class 11C rode a relay around the <InlineMath math="1.5" />-kilometre loop road of Taman Kencana, from 6:00 to 10:00. Only one rider was on the loop at a time, and we kept a fixed order, one lap each: Rina, Yusuf, Dimas, Ayu, Bagas, Fitri, Hendra and me. Our sponsors had pledged in two ways. Together, the per-kilometre sponsors promised <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" /> for every kilometre the team rode, and the fixed sponsors promised <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}500{,}000" /> between them, however far we went. Our boast had assumed <InlineMath math="70" /> laps.

Pak Haris, who timed the relay, gave us two rules. The relay was four hours on the clock, whatever happened. And a lap counted only if the rider stayed on the bike all the way round, so a lap with a stop, even a short one, was ridden but not counted. I lost one of mine on my very first lap, when I put a foot down at the corner by the fountain. Rina's chain came off on her third lap, and Bagas stopped to wave a cat off the road on his last.

At 8:40, with Dimas halfway round his fifth lap, the sky opened. Pak Haris waved everyone off the road, and we waited under the bandstand for twenty minutes. On Sunday night I complained to my uncle, one of the per-kilometre sponsors, that four laps had been thrown away. He said he had pledged for kilometres ridden, not for kilometres of standing beside a bike, and that if he had to wonder about every lap, he would not sponsor us next year. I had been thinking of the rule as Pak Haris being fussy. It had never occurred to me that it was the reason the sponsors could trust the number at all.

A fact is a claim you could check against the world, even when nobody in the text has checked it. An opinion is a judgement or a value.

- The people the union speaks for walking mostly along those roads: fact. It's a claim about where people walk, and counting them would confirm or disprove it. A claim doesn't become an opinion **just because the text gives no evidence for it**.
- **"Too large to ignore": opinion**. The <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}800" /> million could be checked, but whether it's too large to ignore is a judgement.
- The <InlineMath math="40\%" /> share of streetlights: fact. You can count the lights.
- **"That feeling matters": opinion**. It states a value, and no count can prove it.
- **"Dimming is not a gamble": opinion**. It's the writer's verdict on the proposal.

So the opinions are the sentence about savings too large to ignore, "That feeling matters", and "Dimming is not a gamble".

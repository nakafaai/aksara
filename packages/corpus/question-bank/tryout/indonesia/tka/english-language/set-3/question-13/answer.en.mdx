# Given

The narrative reveals **Uncle Bonar's character** through his daily habits (the fast clock for Aunt Mira, refusing payment from Bu Lastri), his practical repair rule about "magic", and his wordless response to Grandmother's radio.

# Asked

Which numbered statements about Uncle Bonar are supported by the story.

# Solution

Picture building a character profile strictly from **what Uncle Bonar actually says and does**. Watch two subtle traps: **a running joke is not a literal payment**, and **the narrator's inner excuse is not Uncle's dialogue**.

> Character claims must rest on that character's own deeds and words, never on an unfulfilled joke or another character's private thoughts.

## Checking each statement about Uncle Bonar

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | He opens the back, points to the loose wire, and declares <Highlight>Magic is just a fault you haven't found yet</Highlight>. |
| II | Supported | He sets the clock fast <Highlight>so that he would never keep Aunt Mira waiting at dinner</Highlight>. |
| III | Not supported | He <Highlight>waved away her money</Highlight> and mentions kue lapis as a running joke he says every time, never shown taking cake as payment. |
| IV | Not supported | Thinking the song came from next door is what the narrator <Highlight>told myself</Highlight>. Uncle never says a word. |
| V | Supported | Hearing the lullaby, he stands still, smiles, dusts the radio, and places it on the counter in full view, showing his feeling purely through action. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** treat a warm running joke as literal barter.
- **Options including IV** assign the narrator's private thought to Uncle.
- **I and II only** leave out the defining silent gesture at the climax (V).

# Answer

The statements about Uncle Bonar supported by the story are **I, II, and V only**.

# Given

In the final paragraph, the narrator complains to an uncle (one of the per-kilometre sponsors) about the four uncounted laps. The uncle states his **condition for trusting the count**, prompting the narrator to rethink Pak Haris's rule.

# Asked

Which numbered statements about the final paragraph and what follows from it are supported by the account.

# Solution

Read the exchange between nephew and uncle like a careful listener. Two grammar clues settle the traps: **a conditional "if" is not a final decision**, and **past perfect "had been thinking" signals a view the narrator no longer holds**.

> Watch tense shifts and conditional words closely: they separate an old opinion or a hypothetical warning from a settled fact.

## Computing the cost of the four uncounted laps

**Four uncounted laps** on a <InlineMath math="1.5\text{-km}" /> loop represent:

<BlockMath math="4 \times 1.5 = 6\text{ km}, \qquad 6 \times \text{Rp}5{,}000 = \text{Rp}30{,}000" />

## Checking each statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in the account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | The uncle pledged <Highlight>for kilometres ridden, not for kilometres of standing beside a bike</Highlight>, so by his reasoning stopped laps should not be paid. |
| II | Not supported | He said he would stop sponsoring only <Highlight>if he had to wonder about every lap</Highlight>, which is a condition rather than a decision already made. |
| III | Supported | As calculated above, <InlineMath math="4" /> uncounted laps (<InlineMath math="6\text{ km}" />) cost exactly <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}30{,}000" />. |
| IV | Supported | The final sentence recognises the rule as <Highlight>the reason the sponsors could trust the number at all</Highlight>. |
| V | Not supported | <Highlight>I had been thinking of the rule as Pak Haris being fussy</Highlight> describes a past view that the closing sentence replaces. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including II** turn the uncle's conditional warning into an active decision to quit.
- **Options including V** ignore the narrator's clear change of mind in the closing sentence.
- **I and IV only** or **III and IV only** drop either the verified <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}30{,}000" /> calculation or the uncle's reasoning.

# Answer

The statements supported by the account are **I, III, and IV only**.

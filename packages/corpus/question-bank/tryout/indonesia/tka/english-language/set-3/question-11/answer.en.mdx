# Given

The story in Uncle Bonar's repair shop sets up **several odd events with ordinary explanations** (the student's loose wire, Belang hearing the motorbike early, the fast wall clock) before Grandmother's **unplugged mains-only valve radio** plays her Batak lullaby at closing time.

# Asked

Which numbered statements belong in an accurate summary of the story.

# Solution

Picture summarizing this quiet mystery in three crisp beats. A valid summary captures **both the ordinary explanations and the inexplicable lullaby** while keeping straight who spoke, who stayed silent, and who only thought to herself.

> In first-person fiction, never confuse a narrator's silent self-reassurance with dialogue spoken aloud to another character.

## Checking each summary statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Belongs | The loose wire, Belang at the door, and the fast clock all turn out ordinary, summed up by <Highlight>Magic is just a fault you haven't found yet</Highlight>. |
| II | Belongs | Grandmother's radio <Highlight>ran only on mains power, and that morning he had pulled its plug from the wall</Highlight>, making the lullaby the story's turning point. |
| III | Excluded | The narrator only thinks <Highlight>I told myself, and I went on sweeping</Highlight> and never tells Uncle anything. |
| IV | Belongs | Uncle responds wordlessly as he <Highlight>lifted the radio down, dusted it and set it on the counter, where every customer would see it</Highlight>. |
| V | Excluded | Uncle never claims the song came from next door. That excuse belongs solely to the narrator. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III or V** mistake the narrator's private thought for dialogue with or from Uncle.
- **II and IV only** or **I and II only** omit either the pattern of ordinary explanations (I) or Uncle's meaningful closing gesture (IV).

# Answer

The statements that belong in an accurate summary are **I, II, and IV only**.

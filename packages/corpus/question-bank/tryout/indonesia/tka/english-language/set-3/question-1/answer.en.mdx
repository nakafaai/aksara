You need three things: the due day, the number of late days, and what comes out of the deposit.

**Due day**. A red-band tool has a loan period of <InlineMath math="3" /> days, counted from the day it leaves: Friday, Saturday and Sunday. So it's due on Sunday.

**Late days**. It comes back on Wednesday, so Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday are late. The Monday rule doesn't help here: it only applies to a tool that "falls due on a Monday", and this one fell due on Sunday. "Every calendar day" counts, including the Monday the library is closed.

**Deposit**. For a red-band tool, the cleaning charge and any late fee "are taken from the deposit":

<BlockMath math="\text{Rp}150{,}000 - 3 \times \text{Rp}20{,}000 - \text{Rp}10{,}000 = \text{Rp}80{,}000" />

- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}100{,}000" /> skips Monday because the library is closed, so it counts only <InlineMath math="2" /> late days.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}60{,}000" /> also counts Sunday, the due day itself, as late, which makes <InlineMath math="4" /> late days.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}120{,}000" /> starts counting on Saturday. Then the tool would fall due on Monday, the Tuesday would be free, and only Wednesday would be late.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}90{,}000" /> forgets the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}10{,}000" /> for cleaning the muddy washer.

So the member gets <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}80{,}000" /> of the deposit back.

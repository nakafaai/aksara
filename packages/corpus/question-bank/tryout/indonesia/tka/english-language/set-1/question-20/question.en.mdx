A slow water leak may be difficult to notice, but a simple meter check can show whether water is moving when no one intends to use it. Begin by choosing a quiet period of at least thirty minutes. Tell everyone in the building not to use taps, toilets, washing machines, or other water appliances during the test.

First, inspect the area for anything that must remain in use, such as a medical device connected to water. Do not interrupt essential equipment. If the building has an automatic irrigation system, make sure its timer will not start during the test.

Next, locate the water meter and photograph or write down the complete reading. Some meters have a small flow indicator that moves even when the numbered display changes very slowly. Record its position as well. Do not open the meter box if it is locked or unsafe to reach.

Wait for the chosen period without using water. Then record the meter and flow indicator again. If neither has moved, the test found no continuous flow during that period. If either one has moved, repeat the test once to rule out an accidental use that someone forgot to report.

A second movement suggests that water is flowing somewhere, but it does not identify the location. Check visible taps and toilets for simple signs, then contact the responsible building manager or a qualified plumber. Do not break walls or disconnect pipes based only on this test. Keep both readings, times, and photographs so the next person can review what was observed.

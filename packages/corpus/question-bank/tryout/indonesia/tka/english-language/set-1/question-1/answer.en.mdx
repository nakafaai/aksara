This question wants the statements that join a fact from one paragraph with a fact from **another paragraph** in a way the story really allows. Two statements do that. The other three take real details from the story but put them together wrongly.

- *From the boat, Grandmother named every one of the houses that the map was still missing.* Not correct. The fourth paragraph says she *named the houses that had stood below*. But the last paragraph, which comes after the boat trip, says *there were still* <InlineMath math="11" /> *houses without names*. So the trip didn't cover every house.
- *The tree that bent the river road on the map was the same kind as the seed Grandmother planted.* Correct. In the third paragraph the road curves around *the tamarind*. In the last paragraph Grandmother plants *a tamarind seed*. Both are tamarinds.
- *The minaret that rose in August belonged to the mosque Laras first drew on the wrong side of the school.* Correct, even though it sounds wrong at first. The fourth paragraph calls it *the mosque's minaret*. The second paragraph says Laras first drew that mosque *west of the school*, and Grandmother pointed east. West was the wrong side, so this statement joins the two paragraphs correctly.
- *The seed Grandmother planted had been in her keeping ever since the year the valley filled.* Not correct. She had carried it *since 1987*, the year her family was moved out. The first paragraph says the dam gates closed <InlineMath math="2" /> years later, so the valley filled in 1989 at the earliest, not in 1987.
- *Laras moved the mosque to the east side of the school only after seeing the minaret from the boat.* Not correct. The second paragraph says she *rubbed it out and moved it* right after Grandmother pointed east. That happened long before the August drought.

So the correct statements are the one that links the tamarind on the map to the tamarind seed, and the one that links the August minaret to the mosque Laras first drew on the west side.

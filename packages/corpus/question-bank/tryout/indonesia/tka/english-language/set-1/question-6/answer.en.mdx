# Given

The rain-gauge procedure uses a **funnel opening of <InlineMath math="80\text{ cm}^2" />** feeding into a narrower straight bottle section. The second-trial table shows <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" /> of rain raises the water level by <InlineMath math="2\text{ mm}" />.

# Asked

How the spacing of the scale marks would change if the bottle's straight section had the exact same area as the funnel opening.

# Solution

Imagine pouring one cup of water into a narrow glass and then into a wider glass. In a narrow glass the water climbs fast, whereas in a bottle as wide as the funnel it climbs **at the exact same rate** as rain on flat ground.

> Scale spacing depends on the ratio between funnel area and bottle area, so widening the bottle compresses the height rise.

## Comparing the old and new bottles

- Paragraph one states the bottle's straight section <Highlight>is narrower than the funnel opening</Highlight>, so <Highlight>Water therefore rises faster inside the bottle than rain builds up on the ground</Highlight>.
- The calibration table gives the exact multiplier: <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" /> of rain produces <InlineMath math="2\text{ mm}" /> of height and <InlineMath math="5\text{ mm}" /> produces <InlineMath math="10\text{ mm}" />, meaning the old scale stretched height by **a factor of two**.
- With equal funnel and bottle areas, <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" /> of rain raises the water by just <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" />, making the marks **half as far apart**.

## Why the other options fail

- **Twice as far apart** reverses the physics because a wider bottle makes water rise more slowly, bringing marks closer together.
- **Staying in the exact same spots** ignores that height changes whenever the storage cross-section widens.
- **No longer needing a zero line** confuses width with the domed base mentioned in paragraph two.
- **Each <InlineMath math="8\text{ mL}" /> standing for <InlineMath math="2\text{ mm}" /> of rain** is wrong because collected volume depends only on the unchanged <InlineMath math="80\text{ cm}^2" /> funnel.

# Answer

**Each millimetre of rain would raise the water by one millimetre, so the marks on the gauge would sit half as far apart as before**.

# Given

A reader draws **four lessons** from Radio Sekar's silent Monday broadcast and the club's new Wednesday testing routine.

# Asked

Which numbered lessons are actually supported by the evidence in the account.

# Solution

Imagine testing a microphone only through headphones plugged into your desk mixer without ever checking whether the living-room speakers are on. You could rehearse **a hundred times** and still miss a fault further down the line.

> A test can catch a fault only when it listens at the same output point that real listeners actually hear.

## Checking each lesson

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence and location in the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | Paragraph two notes <Highlight>every rehearsal had sounded perfect</Highlight> through mixer-fed headphones, so extra rehearsals on the same setup would never have spotted an amplifier fault. |
| II | Not supported | Paragraph three defends the caretaker instead of blaming him: <Highlight>Nobody had ever told him that the cable mattered, and nothing beside the sockets said which one to use</Highlight>. |
| III | Supported | Because both headphones and recorder tapped the mixer before the amplifier, studio checks were blind to classroom silence, prompting the new check from <Highlight>Class 11B</Highlight> in paragraph five. |
| IV | Not supported | Paragraph two notes the mixer-fed recording captured <Highlight>four minutes of Rina speaking clearly to nobody</Highlight>, proving it cannot confirm classroom sound. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Any option including II** contradicts the writer's explicit defense of the caretaker.
- **Any option including IV** treats a studio-mixer recording as proof of classroom audio.
- **I only** omits lesson III, which directly explains why the club moved its check into a classroom.

# Answer

The lessons supported by the text are **I and III only**.

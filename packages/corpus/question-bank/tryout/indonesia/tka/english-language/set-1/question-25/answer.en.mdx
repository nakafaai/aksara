# Given

The target idea connects **two halves**: a low grade on weekend work does not automatically mean lower learning. Four details from the text are offered as candidate support.

# Asked

Which numbered details from the text support the idea that a low grade on weekend work need not mean a student learned less.

# Solution

Picture a fast runner finishing late because a bicycle tire went flat on the way to the track, not because she ran slowly. Valid supporting details must show **equal real learning despite a low weekend mark** or name a **non-learning cause** behind that mark.

> A supporting detail must separate the weekend grade from actual learning, rather than merely discussing study habits or forgetting.

## Checking each supporting detail

| Detail | Verdict | Reasoning and textual evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supports | Paragraph three shows students who missed weekend deadlines still earned <Highlight>the same end-of-unit test scores</Highlight>, proving lost weekend marks did not reflect lower learning. |
| II | Supports | Paragraph two notes a student sharing one phone or working Saturdays is graded on <Highlight>time and quiet that she does not have</Highlight>, pinpointing a non-learning cause for a low mark. |
| III | Does not support | Ms Hartono's ten-minute advice explains how to retain skills, not what a grade measures. |
| IV | Does not support | Pak Darmawan's son forgetting <Highlight>half of it</Highlight> by Wednesday illustrates genuinely weaker learning, not an unfair grade. |

## Why the other options fail

- **I only** omits detail II, which supplies the concrete home-condition reason behind the low grade.
- **Any option adding III or IV** pulls in details about practice frequency or forgetting that say nothing about grading bias.

# Answer

The supporting details from the text are **I and II only**.

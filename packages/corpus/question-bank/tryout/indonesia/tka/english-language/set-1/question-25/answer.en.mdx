The idea has **two parts**: a weekend grade that's low, and learning that isn't. A supporting detail must point to a low grade with a cause other than less learning.

- I supports it. Students who missed a weekend deadline lost marks on that work, yet had *the same end-of-unit test scores* as those who met it. Their learning matched, so the low weekend grade came from something else.
- II supports it. It names that something else: a grade on time and quiet *that she does not have*. The low grade comes from home conditions, not from learning less.
- III doesn't support it. It's about how to keep a skill, not about what a grade measures.
- IV points the other way. The son really did learn less, *half of it* forgotten, so this shows learning lost, not a misleading grade.

So the supporting details are I and II. An option with I alone misses the reason the grade can be low, and options with III or IV add details about practice and forgetting that say nothing about what grades measure.

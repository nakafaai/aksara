An outline has to get **both the order and the content** right, so check each paragraph against its label.

1. The first paragraph explains why the scale is made *by testing, not by holding a ruler against the bottle*.
2. The second paragraph sets up the funnel and the zero line.
3. The third paragraph gives the measured pours and the table.
4. The fourth paragraph explains why the first trial failed: the cylinder was read from above.
5. The fifth paragraph gives the rules for using the gauge outdoors every morning.

Only one outline matches all five.

Why the other outlines are wrong:

- It puts the pours before the zero line, but the text sets the zero line first, *before marking anything*.
- It moves the failed trial to the end, after the daily rules, but the text tells it in the fourth paragraph.
- It says rain is measured in millilitres, but the text says *rain is reported as a depth*, in millimetres.
- It traces the failure to a leak, but the text rejects the leak and blames the way the cylinder was read.

So the matching outline is: why the scale must be tested, setting the zero line, the test pours and the table, the failed first trial and its cause, and the rules for daily use.

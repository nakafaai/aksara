# Given

The rain-gauge text spans **five paragraphs**: operating principle (paragraph one), cutting the bottle and setting the zero line (paragraph two), pouring rules and the second-trial table (paragraph three), the failed first trial (paragraph four), and outdoor reading rules (paragraph five).

# Asked

Which numbered statements accurately describe how the rain-gauge text is organised.

# Solution

Think of checking a floor plan of five rooms. To judge text organisation, verify **which paragraph holds each detail** and **whether the narrative steps backward in time**.

> A passage's paragraph order can jump backward in time, so separate where an event appears on the page from when it happened.

## Checking each organisational claim

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence and location in the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Inaccurate | Paragraph three explicitly says <Highlight>The table shows the second trial, which the group used for its final scale</Highlight>, which is the successful trial. |
| II | Accurate | Paragraph four opens with <Highlight>The first trial had failed</Highlight>, stepping back to an earlier trial than the one in the table. |
| III | Accurate | The rule <Highlight>Read the cylinder at eye level</Highlight> appears in paragraph three before the failed-trial account in paragraph four. |
| IV | Inaccurate | Instructions for the zero line sit in paragraph two, whereas outdoor rules come last in paragraph five. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Any option including I** misreads the table as the failed first trial instead of the successful second trial.
- **Any option including IV** reverses paragraphs two and five.
- **II only** drops statement III, even though the eye-level rule clearly precedes the failed trial.

# Answer

The accurate statements about the text's organisation are **II and III only**.

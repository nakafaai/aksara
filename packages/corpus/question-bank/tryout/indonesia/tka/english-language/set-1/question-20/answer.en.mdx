Each place takes **three steps**: name the plant from its description, find its zone, then read how often that zone floods.

- The plant that stores salt in its fleshy stems is glasswort. It covers the middle marsh, *flooded only by the spring tides that come about twice a month*. About twice a month.
- The plant with hollow channels in its stems is the tall, stiff grass of the low marsh, which *the tide covers twice every day*. Twice a day.
- Beyond the last plants is the bare mudflat. *The tide covers it twice a day*. Twice a day.
- Two plants push salt out through their leaves, sea lavender and the low-marsh grass. Only sea lavender *grows around* the white-crusted salt pans, in the high marsh, which the sea reaches *only during the highest tides of the year or in storms*. Only by the highest tides or storms.

So the low marsh and the mudflat flood twice a day, the glasswort ground about twice a month, and the sea lavender ground only by the highest tides or storms.

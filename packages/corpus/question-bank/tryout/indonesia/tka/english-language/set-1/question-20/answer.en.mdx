# Given

The Harrow Creek boardwalk crosses **four ordered zones** with distinct tidal schedules: high marsh (highest tides of the year or storms), middle marsh (spring tides about twice a month), low marsh (twice a day), and bare mudflat (twice a day).

# Asked

How often the sea floods each of the four places described by its plant traits or ground features.

# Solution

Use a simple three-step clue card for every row: **identify the plant or ground**, **locate its marsh zone**, and **read that zone's tide schedule**.

> Whenever two plants share one trait such as leaf salt glands, rely on the second clue like white-crusted salt pans to pin down the exact zone.

## Mapping all four locations to tide frequency

| Place description | Zone match and textual evidence | Flooding frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground with the plant storing salt in fleshy stems | Glasswort in the middle marsh (paragraph three), <Highlight>flooded only by the spring tides that come about twice a month</Highlight>. | Flooded about twice a month |
| Ground with a plant having hollow stem channels | Tall stiff grass in the low marsh (paragraph four), <Highlight>which the tide covers twice every day</Highlight>. | Flooded twice a day |
| Bare ground beyond the last plants at boardwalk's end | Bare mudflat (paragraph five), <Highlight>The tide covers it twice a day</Highlight>. | Flooded twice a day |
| Ground where a salt-expelling plant grows beside white-crusted patches | Both sea lavender and low-marsh grass expel salt, but only sea lavender grows by <Highlight>bare, white-crusted patches called salt pans</Highlight> in the high marsh (paragraph two), reached <Highlight>only during the highest tides of the year or in storms</Highlight>. | Flooded only by the highest tides or storms |

# Answer

The hollow-stemmed grass ground and the bare mudflat are **flooded twice a day**, the fleshy-stemmed glasswort ground is **flooded about twice a month**, and the sea lavender ground beside white-crusted patches is **flooded only by the highest tides or storms**.

At two minutes past seven on Monday, half a minute into the first live broadcast of Radio Sekar, the speakers in every classroom went silent. In the studio we noticed nothing. Rina, our host, could hear herself perfectly in her headphones, so she kept talking for four more minutes, reading the week's exam room changes to a school that heard none of them. At 7:06 Pak Hadi, the physics teacher, knocked on the studio window and mouthed one word: nothing.

We had rehearsed for two weeks, and every rehearsal had sounded perfect. Looking back, that was the problem. Our headphones took their sound straight from the mixer, before the signal left the studio for the amplifier in the office next door. They told us what we were sending, not what anyone was receiving. Our recorder was plugged into the mixer as well, which is why our recording of that Monday is complete: four minutes of Rina speaking clearly to nobody.

The silence itself had a simple cause. On Saturday the caretaker had repainted the office. To move the amplifier away from the wall, he unplugged its input cable, and when he put it back, he pushed it into the socket labelled AUX 2 instead of AUX 1. AUX 2 was turned down to zero. Nothing was broken, so nothing looked wrong. He had not known that the cable mattered, and we had never told anyone that it did.

Our final rehearsal had taken place on Friday afternoon, the day before the painting. On Monday at 6:40, Rina tested her microphone and heard herself clearly in the headphones, and twenty minutes later we went live with total confidence.

Once Pak Hadi's word sank in, I ran next door and found the cable in under a minute. The exam room changes had to be read again at the first break, and two classes still went to the wrong rooms that morning. On Wednesday we broadcast again. This time Dimas sat in Class 11B and sent a message saying he could hear us before Rina said her first word. We now begin every broadcast that way. The amplifier sockets carry labels in red tape, and a note taped above them explains what the cable does.

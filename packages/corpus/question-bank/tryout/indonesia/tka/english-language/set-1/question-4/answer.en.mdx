# Given

Four event statements mix scenes straight from the Karangsari story with **close realistic variations**, to be sorted into events that could happen in real life or could not happen in real life.

# Asked

Whether each of the four events could happen in real life or could not happen in real life.

# Solution

Think of playing a "spot the one magical detail" game. Two statements look like near twins, so read past the familiar topic and check **who causes the action** and **how much time passes**.

> Changing a single magical detail, such as overnight growth or human speech, can turn a fantasy scene into an ordinary real-life event.

## Classifying every statement

| Statement | Category | Reasoning and contrast with the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seedling planted at night reaches a knee by morning | Could not happen | Paragraph six writes <Highlight>That night</Highlight> to <Highlight>by morning</Highlight>, which is biologically impossible in a few hours. |
| Tamarind seedling reaches a knee a year or two later | Could happen | Same plant and height, but stretched across one or two years, which matches ordinary plant growth. |
| Heron on a drowned minaret gives a loud cry as a boat nears | Could happen | In paragraph four the heron speaks a nickname, which is impossible, yet this statement only claims it <Highlight>gives a loud cry</Highlight>, which real herons actually do. |
| Pencil line moves by itself overnight around the tree spot | Could not happen | Paragraph three states <Highlight>Nobody had erased it</Highlight> and <Highlight>The line itself had moved</Highlight>, and graphite on paper cannot redraw itself. |

# Answer

The overnight knee-high seedling and the self-moving pencil line **could not happen in real life**, whereas the seedling reaching knee height in a year or two and the heron giving a loud cry **could happen in real life**.

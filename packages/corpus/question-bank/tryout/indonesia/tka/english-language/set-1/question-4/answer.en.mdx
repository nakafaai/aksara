Two of these statements are **near twins**, so read each one for exactly what it claims: who acts, and how much time passes.

- The seedling that reaches a knee by the next morning: could not happen. In the story Grandmother plants the seed *That night*, and the shoot reaches Laras's knee *by morning*. No seedling grows that much in a few hours.
- The seedling that reaches a knee some months later: could happen. It's the same plant and the same height, but with months to grow instead of one night. That is ordinary growth.
- The heron that calls out as a boat comes near: could happen. In the story the heron also says a nickname, which is impossible, but this statement only says it *calls out*. Real herons do.
- The pencil line that moves by itself: could not happen. The story says *Nobody had erased it* and *The line itself had moved*. A line on paper can't redraw itself.

So the overnight seedling and the self-moving line could not happen in real life, while the slow-growing seedling and the calling heron could.

The last paragraph gives the rule: *a mistake made while marking the scale spoils every reading the gauge will ever give, while a mistake made on one morning spoils only that morning's reading*. So ask of each mistake: **does it happen while the scale is being made**, or on a single morning?

- Looking down at the cylinder while making the marks: spoils every reading. It happens during marking, and it puts every mark in the wrong place, just like the failed first trial.
- Drawing the zero line before the dome is covered: spoils every reading. The zero line is part of the scale, and the text says to cover the dome *before marking anything*.
- Reading three hours late on a hot afternoon: spoils only that morning's reading. Water left in the sun *slowly evaporates*, so that one reading is low, but the scale itself is fine.
- Using a ruler instead of test pours: spoils every reading. The whole text warns against making the scale *by holding a ruler against the bottle*, and this mistake is built into every mark.

So three of the mistakes are built into the scale and spoil every reading, and only the late reading on a hot day spoils a single morning.

# Given

The closing sentence of paragraph five sorts rain-gauge mistakes into **two impact groups**: mistakes made while marking the scale spoil every future reading, whereas mistakes made on one morning spoil only that morning's reading.

# Asked

Which of the two mistake categories each of the four listed errors belongs to.

# Solution

Think of the difference between printing wrong numbers on a ruler and misreading a clock once. A badly printed ruler ruins **every measurement you ever take**, while reading late ruins **only that one day**.

> Paragraph five states the exact dividing rule: <Highlight>a mistake made while marking the scale spoils every reading the gauge will ever give, while a mistake made on one morning spoils only that morning's reading</Highlight>.

## Classifying every mistake

| Mistake | Category | Reason from the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Looking down at the cylinder while making the marks | Spoils every reading | Happens during calibration and shifts every permanent mark on the bottle, just like the failed first trial. |
| Drawing the zero line before water covers the domed base | Spoils every reading | The zero line anchors the entire scale, and paragraph two requires filling the dome <Highlight>Before marking anything</Highlight>. |
| Reading the gauge three hours late on a hot afternoon | Spoils only one morning's reading | Paragraph five warns water in the sun <Highlight>slowly evaporates</Highlight>, lowering that single reading while leaving the marks intact. |
| Drawing marks with a ruler instead of test pours | Spoils every reading | Paragraph one forbids building the scale <Highlight>by holding a ruler against the bottle</Highlight>, because every permanent mark is wrong. |

# Answer

Looking down while marking, drawing the zero line too early, and using a ruler instead of test pours **spoil every reading**, whereas reading three hours late on a hot afternoon **spoils only one morning's reading**.

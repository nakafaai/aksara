# Given

The Harrow Creek boardwalk passage contrasts the **high marsh (paragraph two)** with the **low marsh (paragraph four)** across flooding frequency, salt levels, soil air, and plant salt removal.

# Asked

Which numbered statements comparing the high marsh and the low marsh are supported by the text.

# Solution

Picture two steps by the shore: the upper step (**high marsh**) rarely sees seawater, while the lower step (**low marsh**) floods twice daily. Check each claim directly against paragraphs two and four.

> Comparing two habitats means pulling evidence from both paragraphs so traits from one zone never get flipped onto the other.

## Checking each comparison statement

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence and location in the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | Paragraph two says the sea reaches the high marsh <Highlight>only during the highest tides of the year or in storms</Highlight>, whereas paragraph four says the tide covers the low marsh <Highlight>twice every day</Highlight>. |
| II | Supported | Paragraph two explains trapped water evaporates in hollows so in dry months the salt pans <Highlight>can be saltier than the sea itself</Highlight>. |
| III | Not supported | Paragraph four notes the low-marsh mud smells of rotten eggs, <Highlight>a smell that forms only in mud with no air in it</Highlight>, so airless soil cannot hold more air. |
| IV | Supported | Paragraph two says high-marsh sea lavender <Highlight>pushes it out again through tiny glands on its leaves</Highlight>, and paragraph four says low-marsh grass <Highlight>Like sea lavender, it gets rid of salt through glands on its leaves</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options omitting IV** overlook that both marshes host a plant expelling salt through leaf glands.
- **Options including III** reverse the soil fact because low-marsh mud has no air at all.
- **II and IV only** drops statement I, which states the clearest contrast in tide frequency.

# Answer

The supported comparison statements are **I, II, and IV only**.

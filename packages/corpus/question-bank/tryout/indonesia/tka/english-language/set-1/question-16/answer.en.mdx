To compare the two marshes you need the second and fourth paragraphs together, and **one surprise** in the second.

- High marsh: the sea arrives *only during the highest tides of the year or in storms*. But *in the dry months the opposite is true*: water left in hollows evaporates, so the salt pans *can be saltier than the sea itself*.
- Low marsh: *the tide covers twice every day*, and the soil *is so waterlogged that air cannot get in*.

So the high marsh floods far less often, and still it can end up saltier in dry months.

Why the other options are wrong:

- The high marsh floods less often, not more.
- "Floods less, so less salt" is exactly the expectation the text overturns with *the opposite is true*.
- It swaps the two marshes. The waterlogged, airless soil belongs to the low marsh, and the white crust belongs to the high marsh's salt pans.
- Spring tides *about twice a month* flood the middle marsh, and the low marsh floods twice a day, not once.

So the high marsh is flooded far less often than the low marsh, yet in dry months its salt pans can be saltier than the sea.

# Given

# Asked

Which numbered statements about the failed first calibration trial are supported by the text.

# Solution

Think of solving a mystery with three suspects. Paragraph four **rules out two suspects directly**, and the single change that fixed the second trial points straight at the real culprit.

> When a repeat trial changes only one variable and succeeds, that single variable identifies what went wrong the first time.

## Checking each statement

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence and location in the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | Paragraph four rules out a leak because <Highlight>the outside of the bottle stayed dry and the level did not drop overnight</Highlight>. |
| II | Supported | Paragraph four states <Highlight>Evaporation was ruled out as well, because the whole trial took less than five minutes</Highlight>. |
| III | Not supported | The narrow straight section makes water rise faster, not lower, and both trials used the exact same bottle. |
| IV | Supported | Paragraph three says <Highlight>Read the cylinder at eye level</Highlight>, and paragraph four notes the only change was Dimas <Highlight>knelt beside the table instead of standing over the cylinder</Highlight>, proving viewing from above made each pour fall short (<InlineMath math="8\text{ mm}" /> equals only <InlineMath math="32\text{ mL}" />). |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options omitting IV** miss the real cause established by the one-variable change between trials.
- **Options including III** wrongly blame the narrow bottle section, which accelerates rise and stayed unchanged.
- **IV only** ignores statements I and II, which paragraph four states outright.

# Answer

The statements supported by the text are **I, II, and IV only**.

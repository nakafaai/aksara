# Given

Four numbered statements describe how **Grandmother Warsih's attitude** toward the map of Karangsari shifts from the opening paragraph to the ending.

# Asked

Which combination of statements is supported by the events and timeline of the story.

# Solution

Picture Grandmother's attitude like a closed door that opens step by step. To test each claim, place every action on a **clear timeline** and check whether the text really supports it.

> Evaluating a character's shift requires checking both what happens and exactly when it happens in the story.

## Checking each statement

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence and location in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | Paragraph one shows Grandmother turned up the radio and said <Highlight>There is nothing to map. It is all water</Highlight>. |
| II | Supported | Paragraph three states that the next morning she <Highlight>asked for the pencil and drew the tree herself</Highlight>, well before the August drought in paragraph four. |
| III | Not supported | Reverses the timeline because she drew the tamarind in paragraph three long before the heron called her name in August. |
| IV | Not supported | Paragraph six says she <Highlight>could name a house only from the spot where it had stood</Highlight>, a location limit rather than memory loss, since she named houses from the boat. |

## Why the other options fail

- **I only** leaves out statement II, which is clearly backed by Grandmother picking up the pencil before August.
- **Any option containing III or IV** fails because III gets the order of events wrong and IV misreads a location-based trigger as losing her memory.

# Answer

The supported statements are **I and II only**.

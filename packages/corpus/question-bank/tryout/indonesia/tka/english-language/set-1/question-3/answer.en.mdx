A change needs **a clear starting point and a clear later point**, and both must be about the map.

- The start: when Laras mentions the map, Grandmother *turned up the radio* and said *"There is nothing to map. It is all water."* That's refusal.
- The later point: the next morning, after the road line moved, she *asked for the pencil and drew the tree herself*. Now she's working on the map with her own hand.

Only one pair has a refusal at the start and her own work on the map later.

Why the other pairs don't show the change:

- Answering in steps and pointing east are both small helps given while Laras asks questions. There's no refusal in this pair, so it shows no change.
- The heron and the seed are both late moments, and neither is about the map itself.
- The second moment is a limit of her memory, not a new attitude. It doesn't show her joining in.
- The first half is right, but the second half breaks the timeline. She drew the tamarind long before August, so she didn't wait for the heron.

So the best pair is the radio turned up at the start and Grandmother asking for the pencil to draw the tamarind herself.

A change in attitude needs **two moments** from the story: where Grandmother starts and where she ends up.

- At the start she turns up the radio and says, *"There is nothing to map. It is all water."* That's a refusal.
- In the third paragraph, before any boat trip, she *asked for the pencil and drew the tree herself*.
- In August, from the boat, she *named the houses that had stood below*.

So she moves from refusing to joining in, first on paper and then on the water.

Why the other options are wrong:

- Nothing shows she loses interest. The last paragraph only says she can name a house from its spot, which is a limit, not boredom.
- Staying unwilling fits the first scene only. It ignores her drawing the tamarind and naming houses.
- The heron comes in August, but she already drew the tamarind the morning after Laras's step questions. Her help started earlier.
- When the road line moved, she corrected it from memory: *"The road went around the tamarind."* Her memory fixes the map, not the other way round.

So Grandmother changes from refusing to talk about Karangsari to drawing on the map and naming houses from the boat.

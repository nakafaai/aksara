# Given

The passage quotes **Ms Hartono** in paragraph two (<Highlight>A skill you use once a week fades between lessons. Ten minutes a day keeps it</Highlight>) and **Pak Darmawan** in paragraph four (<Highlight>My son crams for three hours on Sunday night and has forgotten half of it by Wednesday</Highlight> and <Highlight>I would rather he did a little each evening</Highlight>).

# Asked

On which numbered points do Ms Hartono and Pak Darmawan both agree.

# Solution

Picture two overlapping circles, one for each speaker, and keep **only the overlap** backed by both quotations rather than a view held by just one speaker or by the writer.

> True agreement between two quoted speakers includes only ideas supported by both voices, never a number or proposal voiced by one side alone.

## Checking each point of agreement

| Point | Verdict | Side-by-side speaker evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Agree | Ms Hartono backs short daily practice, and Pak Darmawan prefers <Highlight>a little each evening</Highlight> over a three-hour Sunday cram. |
| II | Do not agree | Removing weekend grades is the writer's proposal, and paragraph four notes Pak Darmawan's worry is <Highlight>not whether their weekend practice should be graded</Highlight>. |
| III | Agree | Ms Hartono says an unused skill <Highlight>fades between lessons</Highlight>, and Pak Darmawan observes his son <Highlight>has forgotten half of it by Wednesday</Highlight>. |
| IV | Do not agree | Only Ms Hartono names ten minutes a day, whereas Pak Darmawan never specifies a minute count. |

## Why the other options fail

- **I only** drops point III, even though both speakers highlight rapid forgetting.
- **Any option adding II or IV** credits the writer's grading plan or Ms Hartono's exact ten-minute figure to both speakers.

# Answer

Ms Hartono and Pak Darmawan agree on **I and III only**.

# Given

The Harrow Creek text profiles **three salt-marsh plants** (sea lavender, glasswort, and stiff low-marsh grass) alongside the ground traits of four zones along the <InlineMath math="600\text{ m}" /> boardwalk.

# Asked

Which statements about the plants and ground along the boardwalk are stated in the text.

# Solution

Picture each option as two fact cards tied together with string. Check whether **both cards truly belong to the same plant or zone** without a single swapped duration or habitat.

> Multi-detail statements hold only when every single attribute matches the exact plant, timing, and zone stated in the text.

## Statements stated in the text

- **The plant whose stems swell after flooding keeps salt inside** is stated. Paragraph three explains that glasswort <Highlight>stores salt in its fleshy stems</Highlight> with extra water, <Highlight>which is why the stems swell after each flooding</Highlight>.
- **Sea lavender and low-marsh grass remove salt the same way** is stated. Paragraph four writes explicitly <Highlight>Like sea lavender, it gets rid of salt through glands on its leaves</Highlight>.

## Why the other three options fail

- **The mudflat and low marsh staying under water for the same length of time** contradicts paragraph five, which says the tide covers the mudflat <Highlight>twice a day, as it does the low marsh, but for longer</Highlight>.
- **Glasswort keeping swollen stems through long dry spells** contradicts paragraph three, which states its stems <Highlight>shrivel in long dry spells</Highlight>.
- **Salt pans forming on ground flooded twice a day** swaps zones, because paragraph two places salt pans in the high marsh reached only by the year's highest tides or storms.

# Answer

The two stated facts are that the plant whose stems swell after a flood is the one that keeps salt inside itself, and that sea lavender and the low-marsh grass get rid of salt in the same way.

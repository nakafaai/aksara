The paragraph's main idea is about grading, but it **opens with a concession**. Each statement asks what one part of the paragraph does.

- I is correct. *Practice does matter, and nobody on the committee disputes it* grants a point before the writer's own claim.
- II is not correct. Ms Hartono's words, *A skill you use once a week fades between lessons*, back up the concession that practice matters. She says nothing about grading.
- III is correct. *A grade, however, turns practice into a test*: the turn from what everyone accepts to the writer's claim that *a test taken at home measures the home as much as the student*.
- IV is correct. A student who *shares one phone with three siblings* or *works in the family shop on Saturdays* is graded *on time and quiet that she does not have*. These are the examples of unequal home conditions.

So the correct statements are I, III and IV. Any option with II ties the quotation to the wrong claim, and leaving out I or IV misses the concession or the examples.

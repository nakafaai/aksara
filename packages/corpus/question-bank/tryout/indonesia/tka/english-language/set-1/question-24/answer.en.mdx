# Given

Paragraph two builds its argument in **four distinct moves**: conceding that practice matters, quoting Ms Hartono on regular practice, pivoting with *however* to the grading claim, and giving two examples of unequal home conditions.

# Asked

Which numbered statements accurately describe how paragraph two constructs its main idea.

# Solution

Imagine a speaker saying, "Yes, practice matters, **however**, grading work at home is unfair." Everything before "however" sets up the **concession**, and the writer's own main claim begins right at the pivot word.

> Watch where a pivot word like *however* sits because examples or quotes placed before it support the concession rather than the main claim after it.

## Checking each structural statement

| Statement | Verdict | Role inside paragraph two |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Correct | The opening sentence grants an undisputed point: <Highlight>Practice does matter, and nobody on the committee disputes it</Highlight>. |
| II | Incorrect | Ms Hartono's quote <Highlight>A skill you use once a week fades between lessons</Highlight> backs the concession about practice and says nothing about grading. |
| III | Correct | <Highlight>A grade, however, turns practice into a test</Highlight> marks the exact pivot into the writer's claim about home testing. |
| IV | Correct | A student who <Highlight>shares one phone with three siblings</Highlight> or <Highlight>works in the family shop on Saturdays</Highlight> illustrates unequal home time and quiet. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Any option including II** attaches Ms Hartono's practice quote to the wrong claim.
- **Options dropping I or IV** miss either the opening concession or the concrete examples of home inequality.

# Answer

The statements accurately describing how paragraph two builds its main idea are **I, III, and IV only**.

Two conditions must hold: the sentence is **a judgement rather than a checkable fact**, and it is **the writer's own**, stated with no hedge.

- I meets both. "The only fair way" is a judgement that no record can check, and the writer states it flatly in the last paragraph.
- II meets both, and it's easy to miss. The plan hasn't been tried, so whether it *keeps skills fresh* and *answers Pak Darmawan's worry* is a prediction. The writer states it as certain, with no "should" or "might".
- III fails the first condition. The percentages come from the school's records and can be checked.
- IV fails the second condition. It's a judgement, but it belongs to Pak Darmawan, marked by the quotation and by *I would rather*.

So the writer's own judgements stated as fact are I and II. An option with only I misses the prediction, and options with III or IV mistake a record or someone else's view for the writer's judgement.

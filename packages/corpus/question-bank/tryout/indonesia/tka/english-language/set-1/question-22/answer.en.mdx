The question has **two conditions**: the sentence must be a judgement, and it must be the writer's own, stated without any hedge.

- *It is the only fair way to treat weekend learning.* This judges fairness, and "the only fair way" can't be checked against any record. It's the writer speaking in the last paragraph, with no "I think" and no "perhaps". It meets both conditions.

Why the other sentences don't:

- The <InlineMath math="31\%" /> comes from the school's records. It's a checkable fact, not a judgement.
- This is a judgement, but it's Pak Darmawan's, marked as his by the quotation and by *I would rather*. It isn't the writer's.
- The test scores come from the records too, so they're a checkable fact.
- This is Ms Hartono's claim, in her own quoted words, not the writer's.

So the sentence that states the writer's own judgement as an established fact is the one calling the plan the only fair way to treat weekend learning.

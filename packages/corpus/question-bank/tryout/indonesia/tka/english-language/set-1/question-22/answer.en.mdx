A fact can be **checked against records or events**. An opinion is a judgement, even when it's written like a fact. Test each sentence with one question: could you check it?

- *It is the only fair way to treat weekend learning.* This is a judgement about fairness, and "the only fair way" can't be checked against any record. But it has no "I think" and no "perhaps". It's stated as if it were already settled. That makes it the opinion dressed as a fact.

Why the other sentences are facts:

- The <InlineMath math="31\%" /> figure comes from the school's records and can be checked. A number makes it checkable, not true by magic, but it's still a factual claim.
- This one is tricky. "Short, regular practice beats one long session" is a judgement, but the sentence reports that Pak Darmawan *agrees* with it. Whether he agrees can be checked by asking him, so the sentence is a fact about his view.
- What students take home every Friday is an event anyone at the school can confirm.
- The test scores come from the records, so they can be checked too.

So the opinion presented as a settled fact is the sentence calling the writer's plan the only fair way to treat weekend learning.

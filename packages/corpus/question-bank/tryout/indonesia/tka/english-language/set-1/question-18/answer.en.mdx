A title for the whole text has to cover **every part of it**, not one paragraph. Find the idea that holds the paragraphs together.

- The first paragraph sets the frame: the walk *crosses three different marshes*, and *how often the sea arrives decides what can grow*.
- The next three paragraphs each describe one marsh, its flooding and its plants.
- The last paragraph reaches the mudflat and explains how to tell which zones the sea has just visited.

So the whole text is about three marshes along one short route, each shaped by how often the tide arrives.

Why the other titles fail:

- Glasswort swelling fits only the third paragraph.
- *The saltiest place* takes one detail about the salt pans and makes it sound like the whole text, and the text never compares the whole coast.
- Reading the tide from seaweed fits only the last two sentences.
- The text says the opposite. Sea lavender *takes salt in through its roots*, and glasswort *stores salt in its fleshy stems*.

So the title that fits the whole text is the one about three marshes on one short walk and how the tide shapes what grows.

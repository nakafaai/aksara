The new sentence **points back to two things**, so it must come right after a sentence that mentions both.

- *the patches* needs patches that were just named.
- *that salty* needs a salt level that was just described.
- *bare* has to match how those patches look.

The second paragraph has all three in one sentence: *the bare, white-crusted patches called salt pans can be saltier than the sea itself*. The next sentence, *Sea lavender grows around their edges*, then makes sense: the plants grow at the edges because the middle is too salty.

Why the other places don't fit:

- After the first sentence of the second paragraph, no patches have been mentioned yet, so *the patches* points to nothing.
- The glasswort ground is covered with plants, and glasswort copes with salt. Nothing there is bare.
- The glittering leaves belong to living grass, so *the patches are bare* contradicts that spot.
- The mudflat is bare too, but because *no plant can root in its shifting mud*, not because of salt. It also isn't described as *patches*.

So the sentence fits best in the second paragraph, between the sentence about the salt pans and the sentence about sea lavender.

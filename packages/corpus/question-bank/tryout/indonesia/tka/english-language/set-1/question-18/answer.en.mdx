# Given

Paragraph three focuses on the **middle marsh and glasswort**: flooded only by spring tides roughly twice a month, storing salt and extra water inside fleshy stems without expelling salt, swelling after flooding, and shrivelling in dry spells.

# Asked

Which numbered sentences would fit cleanly at the end of paragraph three in both meaning and factual accuracy.

# Solution

Picture adding one closing sentence to a paragraph about a water-storing sponge. A fitting sentence must **extend glasswort's own behaviour** without borrowing traits from a neighbouring plant or tide zone.

> A valid closing sentence follows naturally from the paragraph's facts and never imports habits belonging to another species or zone.

## Checking each candidate sentence

| Sentence | Verdict | Reasoning and textual evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Fits | Paragraph three notes flooding comes <Highlight>only by the spring tides</Highlight> and stems <Highlight>swell after each flooding</Highlight>, so they are naturally plumpest right after a spring tide. |
| II | Does not fit | Paragraph three explicitly says <Highlight>Glasswort does not push salt out</Highlight>, while glittering salt crystals belong to low-marsh grass in paragraph four. |
| III | Fits | Paragraph three explains glasswort <Highlight>holds extra water</Highlight> in its stems and they <Highlight>shrivel in long dry spells</Highlight>, meaning it survives dry spells on water stored from the last spring tide. |
| IV | Does not fit | Paragraph three states the middle marsh floods <Highlight>about twice a month</Highlight>, not twice a day (which is the low marsh). |

## Why the other options fail

- **Any option including II** gives glasswort the leaf crystals of low-marsh grass.
- **Any option including IV** gives glasswort the twice-daily tides of the low marsh.
- **I only** omits sentence III, which logically extends the stored-water and shrivelling facts.

# Answer

The sentences that fit at the end of paragraph three are **I and III only**.

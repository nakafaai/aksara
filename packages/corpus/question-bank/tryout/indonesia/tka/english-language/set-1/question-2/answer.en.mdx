# Given

The last two paragraphs set **three strict conditions** for finishing the map of Karangsari: where Grandmother can recall house names, the deadline before October rains, and why Laras's step scale was off.

# Asked

Which plan satisfies every condition set by the story without breaking any single one.

# Solution

Picture unlocking a treasure chest with three padlocks. A plan opens the chest only when every key fits at once: **the right person on the water**, **the right time before the rains**, and **the right direction for fixing the scale**.

> A valid plan must obey all three constraints together because breaking even one makes finishing the map impossible.

## Checking the three conditions

- **Where Grandmother remembers** comes from paragraph six, which states <Highlight>Grandmother could name a house only from the spot where it had stood, never from the kitchen table</Highlight>, so she must go out on the boat.
- **The October deadline** in paragraph six warns that rains arrive in the second week of October and once the minaret sinks <Highlight>nobody would find those spots again</Highlight>, requiring a trip before mid-October.
- **Fixing the step scale** in paragraph five reveals the steps were counted <Highlight>by a girl of <InlineMath math="7" /></Highlight> and the minaret stood much closer to the well than Laras drew, meaning Laras must **shorten the step distances**.

## Why the other plans fail

- **Waiting until late October at the kitchen table** breaks both the location rule and the rain deadline.
- **Asking the boatman to name the houses** fails because paragraph five notes he knew the well's spot <Highlight>though not who had lived around it</Highlight>.
- **Lengthening the step distances** corrects the scale backward because a seven-year-old takes shorter strides than Laras, not longer ones.
- **Going out after the October rains raise the water** fails because submerged landmarks wipe out the reference spots completely.

# Answer

The only plan that fits every condition is **to go out by boat with Grandmother before mid-October and shorten the step distances to match a seven-year-old's stride**.

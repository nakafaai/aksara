Ask what the third paragraph changes for the reader before the seventh morning.

- **I is right**. For each oddity the narrator finds or accepts an ordinary reason: cooling teak, jasmine pollen, an unlatched shutter.
- **II is right**. Because everything else gets an explanation, the growth of the cloth, which nobody explains, stands out.
- III is wrong. The paragraph points the other way: each oddity has an ordinary cause.
- IV is wrong. The narrator opens the shutters and touches nothing on the loom. Forgetting one latch is a slip, not ignoring an instruction.
- V is wrong. It turns "these have ordinary causes" into "everything has", and the <InlineMath math="40" /> centimetres of new cloth get no ordinary cause.

The options with V overgeneralise from three explained events. The option with IV mistakes a forgotten latch for disobedience.

So the noises and the dust do I and II only.

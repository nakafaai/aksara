This activity shows how layers of different materials remove dirt from muddy water. It takes about thirty minutes. The filtered water will look clearer, but it is **not safe to drink**, because this filter cannot remove germs or dissolved chemicals.

What you need:

- a clear plastic bottle (1.5 liters) with its cap
- a small square of cotton cloth
- a cup each of clean gravel, clean coarse sand, and clean fine sand
- a handful of crushed charcoal, rinsed
- a jar of muddy water
- scissors and a nail

Steps:

1. Ask an adult to cut the bottle about 10 centimeters from the bottom. Keep both parts.
2. With an adult's help, make five small holes in the cap with the nail, then screw the cap back on.
3. Turn the top part of the bottle upside down and stand it in the bottom part, which will catch the water.
4. Place the cotton cloth inside, just above the cap.
5. Add the layers in this order: charcoal, fine sand, coarse sand, and finally gravel on top.
6. Slowly pour the muddy water onto the gravel. Do not pour too fast, or the water will push through without being filtered.
7. Compare the water in the bottom part with the muddy water in the jar.

If the water still looks cloudy, pour it through the filter a second time. When you finish, throw away the used sand and charcoal, because the dirt is now trapped inside them.

# Given

The exposition **Charge for bags, don't ban them** proposes a **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}500" /> bag fee** instead of a ban, reports a **<InlineMath math="70\%" /> drop** at charging stalls versus **<InlineMath math="85\%" />** under a ban elsewhere, and openly concedes **the trial cannot settle whether shoppers switched stalls**.

# Asked

Which numbered statements belong in an accurate summary of the text.

# Solution

Keep the **core proposal (I)**, **main trial outcome (II)**, and **honest trial limitation (IV)**, while rejecting any statement that turns an open question into proof (III) or swaps the <InlineMath math="85\%" /> and <InlineMath math="70\%" /> figures (V).

> Accurate summaries preserve both the headline numbers and the writer's own caveats without flipping comparisons or turning open questions into conclusions.

## Checking each summary statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Belongs | States the opening proposal: <Highlight>charge <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}500" /> for every plastic bag instead of banning bags outright</Highlight>. |
| II | Belongs | Reports the key trial figure: <Highlight>At the charging stalls, bag use fell by <InlineMath math="70\%" /></Highlight>. |
| III | Excluded | False: charging-stall sales dropped <InlineMath math="3\%" /> and the writer admits <Highlight>The trial cannot settle that</Highlight>. |
| IV | Belongs | Accurately reflects the writer's concession: <Highlight>The trial cannot settle that</Highlight>. |
| V | Excluded | Reversed: the other town's ban cut bags by <InlineMath math="85\%" />, more than the charge's <InlineMath math="70\%" />. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** claim a sales guarantee the writer explicitly says the trial cannot prove.
- **Options including V** swap <InlineMath math="85\%" /> (ban) and <InlineMath math="70\%" /> (charge).
- **I and II only** or **II and IV only** omits either the key caveat (IV) or the main proposal (I).

# Answer

The statements belonging in an accurate summary are **I, II, and IV only**.

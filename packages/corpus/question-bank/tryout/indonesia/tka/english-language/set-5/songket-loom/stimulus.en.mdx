When Bibi Ratih went into hospital in Palembang, she gave me the only key to her weaving room and two instructions: open the shutters every morning so that the damp would not reach the silk, and touch nothing on the loom.

On the loom was a songket she was weaving for my cousin Dewi's wedding, gold thread worked into deep red silk. Dewi herself was in Medan for her final exams and would not be home for a month. The night Bibi Ratih left, I measured the finished cloth with her yardstick, because she wanted to know how far she had got: <InlineMath math="2" /> metres exactly, ending at the third flower of the border.

The weaving room made noises at night. The loom creaked after dark, and our neighbour, Pak Hasan, told me that old teak shrinks as it cools and always talks after sunset. Each morning there was a fine film of gold dust on the windowsill, and I found the reason in the garden: the jasmine below the window drops its pollen at dawn. Once I heard the shuttle rattle, but it was only the wind coming through a shutter I had forgotten to latch.

On the seventh morning I unlocked the door, and the cloth reached the fifth flower of the border. I measured it twice: <InlineMath math="2.4" /> metres. Dewi must have come home early and woven a little of it, I told myself, and I kept the key in my pocket all that day. That evening Bibi Ratih called from the hospital. She asked how far the cloth had got, and when I told her, she was quiet for a long time. Then she said, "Good. Tell no one, and keep opening the shutters."

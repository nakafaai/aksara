We never stood on the summit of Gunung Sindara, and I still think it was the best day of our trip.

Six of us from the school nature club set off from the Pos Cemara trailhead at 5:30 on a Sunday in July, with Pak Bayu as our guide. The trail to the summit is <InlineMath math="9" /> kilometres long. Before we left, Pak Bayu set the one rule he would not bend: whoever was in front at 12:00 would turn the whole group around, wherever we were, because the afternoon clouds on Sindara bring lightning, and tired legs need as long to come down as they needed to go up.

The first <InlineMath math="5" /> kilometres, through the pine forest, took us three hours, including two rests of a quarter of an hour each. [GAP] Above the forest the path turned to loose stones. Laras, the slowest of us, had a blister by then, and Pak Bayu put her at the front so that the group would move at her pace instead of leaving her behind. The <InlineMath math="2" /> kilometres from the edge of the forest to a flat shoulder of rock we called the ridge took us three hours and twenty minutes, stops included.

We reached the ridge at 11:50, <InlineMath math="2" /> kilometres below the summit. We could see the summit cairn. Raka said we could be there in an hour and back at the ridge by 13:30. Pak Bayu looked at his watch, looked at Laras, and said nothing. Ten minutes later, Laras, still in front, turned us around.

The cloud reached the ridge at 13:15. We heard the first thunder when we were back among the pines, and we reached the trailhead at 16:40, soaked and laughing.

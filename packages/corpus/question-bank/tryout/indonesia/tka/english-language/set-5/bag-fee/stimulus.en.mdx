**Charge for bags, don't ban them**

Pasar Baru should charge <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}500" /> for every plastic bag instead of banning bags outright, as the town council proposes. A charge cuts bag use almost as much as a ban, leaves a choice for shoppers who really need a bag, and raises money that the market can spend on its drains.

For three months last year, twenty of the market's sixty stalls tried the charge, while the other forty carried on as before.

| Bags handed out per week | Before the trial | During the trial |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Twenty stalls with the charge | <InlineMath math="4{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="1{,}200" /> |
| Forty stalls without the charge | <InlineMath math="8{,}000" /> | <InlineMath math="7{,}600" /> |

At the charging stalls, bag use fell by <InlineMath math="70\%" />. A town in the same province that banned bags in 2023 reports a fall of <InlineMath math="85\%" />. Fifteen points is a small price for keeping the choice. And every bag the charging stalls handed out was paid for, which brought in about <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}600{,}000" /> a week.

Some stallholders worried that shoppers would walk to stalls that still gave bags away free. The trial cannot settle that. Sales at the charging stalls fell by <InlineMath math="3\%" />, but sales across the whole market fell by <InlineMath math="2\%" /> in the same months because of the rains. I think shoppers adapt quickly, and most of the regulars at the charging stalls were bringing their own bags within a month.

A ban looks tidier on paper. A charge works, and it pays.

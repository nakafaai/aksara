# Given

The four paragraphs of the songket story move from **Bibi Ratih's two instructions** (paragraph <InlineMath math="1" />), to **the initial <InlineMath math="2" />-metre measurement** (paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />), to **three ordinary explanations** (paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />: one from a neighbour, two discovered by the narrator), to **the <InlineMath math="2.4" />-metre re-measurement and secretive phone call** (paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />).

# Asked

Which numbered statements accurately describe how the story is organised.

# Solution

Trace the four paragraphs in sequence. Watch for two classic traps: **Pak Hasan explains only one oddity (the creaking teak)**, never all of them, and **Bibi Ratih gives zero explanation** in her closing call.

> Test words like "every" against all examples in the paragraph, and check whether an ending resolves a mystery or deliberately leaves it unspoken.

## Checking each structural statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct structural evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Correct | Both instructions (<Highlight>open the shutters every morning</Highlight> and <Highlight>touch nothing on the loom</Highlight>) appear in paragraph <InlineMath math="1" />, before paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />'s noises and dust. |
| II | Incorrect | Neighbour Pak Hasan explains only the creaking teak. The narrator uncovers the jasmine pollen and unlatched shutter independently. |
| III | Incorrect | Bibi Ratih explains nothing at the close, saying only <Highlight>Good. Tell no one, and keep opening the shutters</Highlight>. |
| IV | Correct | Cloth length appears near the start (<InlineMath math="2" /> metres) and again near the end (<InlineMath math="2.4" /> metres). |
| V | Correct | The narrator discovers both the jasmine pollen and the unlatched shutter without anyone's help. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including II** overstretch Pak Hasan's single explanation to every strange event.
- **Options including III** read an explanation into a phone call that offers none.
- **I and IV only** or **IV and V only** drops either the narrator's self-found causes (V) or the opening placement of instructions (I).

# Answer

The statements that correctly describe the story's organisation are **I, IV, and V only**.

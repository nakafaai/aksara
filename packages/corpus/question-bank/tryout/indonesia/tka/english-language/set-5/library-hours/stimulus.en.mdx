Our school library closes at two o'clock, right when lessons end. I believe SMA Tunas Muda should keep it open until five in the afternoon, at least from Monday to Thursday, for three reasons.

First, many students have nowhere quiet to study after school. In a survey by the library club, 64 of the 200 students who answered said that they share a room with younger siblings or live next to a busy road. For them, homework at home often means noise and interruptions.

Second, the library is already busiest at the very moment it closes. The librarian's visitor log for September shows how many visits the library receives at different times of the day.

The log counts visits, not individual students, so a student who comes in twice is counted twice. The survey was also answered mostly by students who already use the library. Even so, the pattern is clear: demand rises through the day and is cut off at its highest point.

Third, longer hours would not require a new building or many new books. The library already has tables, fans, and Wi-Fi. What it needs is someone to supervise.

Some teachers point out that the school cannot pay the librarian for three extra hours a day. This is a fair concern. However, members of the library club have offered to help on a rota, and one teacher could supervise each afternoon in turn. The school could try this plan for one semester and count the visits again before deciding whether to keep it.

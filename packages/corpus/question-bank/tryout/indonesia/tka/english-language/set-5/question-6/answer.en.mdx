# Given

The Gunung Sindara climb centres on **Pak Bayu's strict 12:00 turnaround rule** to avoid afternoon lightning, turning back at **noon on the ridge <InlineMath math="2" /> km below the summit**, getting caught in rain on the descent, and the narrator's verdict that it was still the trip's best day.

# Asked

Which numbered statements belong in an accurate summary of the account.

# Solution

Keep only statements that are **both true and central to the climb**. Checking paragraphs four and five quickly eliminates III (Pak Bayu never gave in to Raka) and V (the group heard thunder in the pines and arrived soaked).

> In recount summaries, check whether a character's suggestion actually changed the outcome or whether the original rule held firm.

## Checking each summary statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in the account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Belongs | They reached the ridge <Highlight><InlineMath math="2" /> kilometres below the summit</Highlight> at 11:50 and turned back ten minutes later at noon. |
| II | Belongs | The turnaround rule exists <Highlight>because the afternoon clouds on Sindara bring lightning</Highlight>. |
| III | Excluded | False: after Raka spoke, Pak Bayu <Highlight>said nothing</Highlight> and the group turned around as planned. |
| IV | Belongs | The opening sentence declares <Highlight>I still think it was the best day of our trip</Highlight> despite missing the summit. |
| V | Excluded | False: thunder started while they were among the pines and they reached the trailhead <Highlight>soaked and laughing</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III or V** directly contradict Pak Bayu's silence and the rain-soaked descent.
- **I and II only** omits the narrator's defining personal conclusion in IV.

# Answer

The statements that belong in an accurate summary are **I, II, and IV only**.

# Given

Paragraph <InlineMath math="1" /> sets out the writer's **three-part case** for a <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}500" /> charge over a ban: cutting bag use nearly as much as a ban (<InlineMath math="70\%" /> vs. <InlineMath math="85\%" />), keeping a choice for shoppers, and raising drain funds (<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}600{,}000" /> a week).

# Asked

Which numbered points are part of the writer's case for a charge rather than a ban.

# Solution

Match all five options against **the three opening pillars plus a four-week revenue check**. Reject IV (the writer admits the trial cannot settle customer switching) and V (the writer admits the ban's <InlineMath math="85\%" /> drop beat the charge's <InlineMath math="70\%" />).

> Always convert time units (<InlineMath math="4" /> weeks at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}600{,}000" />/week) before dismissing a figure, and watch for reversed percentages.

## Checking four week revenue and each point

**Four weeks of trial revenue** at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}600{,}000" /> per week equals:

<BlockMath math="4 \times \text{Rp}600{,}000 = \text{Rp}2.4\text{ million}" />

| Point | Part of case? | Direct evidence in the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | First pillar: <Highlight>cuts bag use almost as much as a ban</Highlight> (<InlineMath math="70\%" /> vs. <InlineMath math="85\%" />). |
| II | **Yes** | Second pillar: <Highlight>leaves a choice for shoppers who really need a bag</Highlight>. |
| III | **Yes** | Third pillar (funds): <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}600{,}000" /> weekly brings in about <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}2.4" /> million every four weeks. |
| IV | No | Directly denied: <Highlight>The trial cannot settle that</Highlight>. |
| V | No | Reversed: <InlineMath math="70\%" /> is smaller than <InlineMath math="85\%" />, not larger. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including IV or V** claim proof the writer disclaims or invert <InlineMath math="70\%" /> and <InlineMath math="85\%" />.
- **I and II only** or **II and III only** drops one of the writer's three pillars.

# Answer

The writer's case includes **I, II, and III only**.

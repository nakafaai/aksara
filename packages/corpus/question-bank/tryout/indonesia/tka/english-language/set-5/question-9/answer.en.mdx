# Given

Pak Bayu, the guide, sets one unbending rule that **whoever is in front at 12:00 turns the whole group around**, places Laras (the slowest walker, with a blister) at the front, and **says nothing** when Raka claims the summit is only an hour away.

# Asked

Which descriptions of Pak Bayu are supported by the account (choose all that apply).

# Solution

Check all five options against **Pak Bayu's exact words and actions**. Three distractors collapse on close reading: he **never argues** (he stays silent), **Laras makes the turnaround call** under his rule, and they turn back by the **12:00 clock**, not because they saw the 13:15 cloud.

> Pay attention to silence and delegated authority: Pak Bayu enforces his rule without arguing and lets the front walker make the call.

## Checking all five descriptions of Pak Bayu

| Description | Supported? | Direct evidence in the account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keeps to his rule even when Raka says the summit is an hour away | **Yes** | Pak Bayu <Highlight>looked at his watch, looked at Laras, and said nothing</Highlight>, letting the noon rule stand. |
| Lets the slowest walker set the group's pace | **Yes** | He placed Laras at the front <Highlight>so that the group would move at her pace instead of leaving her behind</Highlight>. |
| Argues with Raka about going on | **No** | He never argues (<Highlight>said nothing</Highlight>). |
| Makes the turning decision himself at noon | **No** | His rule delegates it to whoever leads, and <Highlight>Laras, still in front, turned us around</Highlight>. |
| Turns the group around because he sees the cloud coming | **No** | They turn at 12:00 by the clock, over an hour before the cloud reaches the ridge at 13:15. |

# Answer

The supported descriptions of Pak Bayu are:
- **He keeps to his rule even when Raka says the summit is only an hour away**.
- **He lets the slowest walker set the group's pace**.

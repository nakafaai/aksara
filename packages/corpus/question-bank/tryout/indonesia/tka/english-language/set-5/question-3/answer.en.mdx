# Given

In the last paragraph, the hall bought **<InlineMath math="410" /> kWh from the village grid** (<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}500" />/kWh), with **<InlineMath math="70\%" /> in the rainy months from December to February**. The panels saved about **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5.4" /> million** last year, while the battery needs replacing after **<InlineMath math="8" /> years** for **<InlineMath math="\text{Rp}36" /> million**.

# Asked

Which numbered statements about the council's claim and Pak Wiryo's figures are supported by the text.

# Solution

Test the council's bold label ("self-sufficient") against **Pak Wiryo's actual numbers**. Two quick calculations verify statements II and III, while the <InlineMath math="70\%" /> rainy-season share immediately refutes statement IV.

> Spread a long-term replacement cost across its lifespan to see how much of the yearly saving it really consumes.

## Computing annualised battery cost and grid bill

**Annualised battery replacement** and **last year's village-grid electricity bill** come directly from the text:

<BlockMath math="\text{Battery per year: } \frac{\text{Rp}36\text{ million}}{8} = \text{Rp}4.5\text{ million}, \qquad \text{Grid bill: } 410 \times \text{Rp}1{,}500 = \text{Rp}615{,}000" />

## Checking each statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | Because the hall <Highlight>bought <InlineMath math="410" /> kWh from the grid</Highlight>, calling it "self-sufficient" overstates its record. |
| II | Supported | <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}4.5" /> million a year consumes most of the <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5.4" /> million annual saving. |
| III | Supported | <InlineMath math="410" /> kWh at <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}500" /> per kWh equals exactly <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}615{,}000" />. |
| IV | Not supported | The text states <Highlight><InlineMath math="70\%" /> of it in the rainy months from December to February</Highlight>, so most was bought inside those months, not outside. |

## Why the other options fail

- **I, II, III, and IV** reverses the <InlineMath math="70\%" /> rainy-season share.
- **I and II only**, **II and III only**, and **I and III only** drop a statement proved directly by the paragraph's numbers.

# Answer

The supported statements are **I, II, and III only**.

A narrative is usually built from **orientation, complication, and resolution**. The orientation introduces Raka, the borrowed camera, and the 24-picture rule. The complication starts when a problem appears: *On the second day, disaster struck*, as the lens cap falls into the water and Raka is too worried to shoot. So the complication begins when the cap rolls into the water.

- The aunt lending the camera is part of the orientation.
- Walking the harbor and taking three pictures is still orientation, before any problem.
- Giving away the prize money comes at the end, after the problem is solved.
- The judge's note belongs to the resolution, showing what Raka's patience achieved.

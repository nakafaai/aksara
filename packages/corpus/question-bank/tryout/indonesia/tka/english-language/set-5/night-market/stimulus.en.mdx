Every Saturday evening, the car park behind Sentosa Market turns into the Lantern Night Market. By six o'clock, the painted parking lines have disappeared under rows of folding tables, and strings of paper lanterns hang between the lamp posts.

The market is laid out in three lanes. The east lane belongs to food sellers. Here the air smells of grilled corn and sweet soy sauce, and every stall keeps a bucket of water beside its stove, a rule the organizers check each week. The middle lane is quieter. It is filled with second-hand books, hand-stitched bags, and a repair stall where an elderly man fixes watches under a magnifying lamp. The west lane is kept clear of stalls so that families with strollers and people in wheelchairs can move easily from one end of the market to the other.

At the far end stands a small wooden stage. Local students perform there for half an hour at a time, usually traditional dances or a school band. Benches face the stage, but most people stand at the back with a snack in one hand.

Unlike the day market, the night market accepts only stallholders who live in the district. The organizers say this keeps prices low and helps neighbors get to know one another. There are no plastic bags. Shoppers bring their own baskets or buy a cloth bag for five thousand rupiah at the information booth, which also keeps a box for lost items.

For me, the best time to visit is just before nine, when the lanterns are brightest and the sellers start offering the last of their food at half price.

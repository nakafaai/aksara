At SMA Tunas Muda, lunch break lasts thirty minutes. For many students, those minutes are spent looking down at a screen. I believe our school should ask students to keep their phones in their bags during lunch, for three reasons.

First, lunch is one of the few times in the school day when students can talk freely. Lessons are planned and quiet, but the canteen is where friendships grow. When everyone at a table is scrolling, conversations stop before they start.

Second, a short break from screens may help students return to class more focused. Our student council asked 120 students to describe their afternoons during two weeks in September. In the first week, phones were allowed at lunch. In the second week, the same students agreed to put their phones away. The table shows how many of the 120 students gave each answer.

These numbers come from students' own answers and from only two weeks, so they cannot prove that phones cause tiredness. Still, they suggest that many students noticed a difference.

Third, a phone-free lunch is simple and free. It needs no new rooms or equipment, and students who must contact their families can still use a phone at the school office.

Some students argue that phones help them relax after a hard morning. This is fair, and nobody wants lunch to feel like another lesson. That is why the rule should cover only the thirty-minute lunch break, not the whole school day. For half an hour, let us look up from our screens and at each other.

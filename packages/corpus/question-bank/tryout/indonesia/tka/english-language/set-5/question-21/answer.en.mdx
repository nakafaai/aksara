# Given

The opinion piece **Charge for bags, don't ban them** mixes **verifiable trial figures** (<InlineMath math="70\%" /> drop, <InlineMath math="85\%" /> ban drop, every bag paid for) with **personal value judgements** about what counts as a small price, tidiness on paper, and how quickly shoppers adapt.

# Asked

Which statements the writer states as an **opinion** (choose all that apply).

# Solution

Apply a two-step check: **does the writer actually state this sentence**, and **does it express a judgement or personal belief ("small price", "tidier", "I think")**? Notice the trap in the first choice: fifteen points is arithmetic, yet calling it "a small price" is pure opinion.

> A number inside a sentence does not make it a fact when the sentence's main claim is a value judgement, and an unstated comparison cannot be chosen at all.

## Checking all five statements

| Statement | Stated as opinion? | Evidence and reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Highlight>Fifteen points is a small price for keeping the choice</Highlight> | **Yes** | The fifteen-point gap is arithmetic, yet labelling it *a small price* is the writer's judgement. |
| <Highlight>A ban looks tidier on paper</Highlight> | **Yes** | Calling a policy *tidier* expresses a personal judgement. |
| <Highlight>Every bag the charging stalls handed out was paid for</Highlight> | No (fact) | A checkable factual record backed by the weekly <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}600{,}000" /> collected. |
| A charge would raise more money for the drains than a ban | No (not stated) | The writer says a charge raises money for drains, yet never compares that revenue against a ban. |
| <Highlight>Shoppers adapt quickly</Highlight> | **Yes** | Explicitly flagged as personal belief via <Highlight>I think shoppers adapt quickly</Highlight>. |

# Answer

The statements the writer states as opinions are:
- **Fifteen points is a small price for keeping the choice**.
- **A ban looks tidier on paper**.
- **Shoppers adapt quickly**.

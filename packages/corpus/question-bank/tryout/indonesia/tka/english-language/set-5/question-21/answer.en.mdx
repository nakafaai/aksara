An opinion is the writer's own judgement, which a reader could reasonably reject. A fact can be checked.

- **"Fifteen points is a small price for keeping the choice" is an opinion**. The fifteen points (<InlineMath math="85 - 70" />) is a fact, but calling them "a small price" is the writer's judgement. The number is what makes it look like a fact.
- **"A ban looks tidier on paper" is an opinion**. "Tidier" is a judgement.
- "Every bag the charging stalls handed out was paid for" is a fact. It follows from the charge and can be checked against the money taken.
- "A charge would raise more money for the drains than a ban" isn't stated at all. The writer says the charge raises money for the drains but never compares that with a ban.
- **"Shoppers adapt quickly" is an opinion**. The writer marks it with "I think", and the trial can't check it.

So the opinions are "Fifteen points is a small price for keeping the choice", "A ban looks tidier on paper" and "Shoppers adapt quickly".

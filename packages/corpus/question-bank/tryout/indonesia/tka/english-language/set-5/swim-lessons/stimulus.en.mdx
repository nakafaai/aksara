Our school is a twenty-minute walk from the coast, yet many of its students cannot swim. I believe SMA Pelita Samudra should add swimming lessons to physical education, for three reasons.

First, swimming is a safety skill for students who live near water. Many of us spend weekends at the beach or by the river. In a survey by the PE teacher, 74 of the 180 students who answered said that they could not swim 25 meters without stopping.

Second, students can learn the basics quickly. Last semester, the school's sports club ran a free four-week course for 30 volunteers, with two lessons a week at the town pool.

The volunteers chose to join, so they may have been keener than other students, and four weeks is a short time. Even so, the change was large.

Third, swimming is exercise that students can keep doing for life. It works the whole body and puts little strain on the knees, so people can continue long after they stop playing team sports.

Some parents point out that the school has no pool and that transport costs money. This is a fair concern. However, the town pool is a ten-minute walk from the school, and it offers school groups a lower price on weekday mornings. The school could start with one class per semester and record how many students reach 25 meters by the end.

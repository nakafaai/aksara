Balai Surya, the community hall of Desa Karangjati, has run almost entirely on sunlight since 2023. Twenty-four panels on its south roof feed a battery in the storeroom behind the stage. On a clear day the panels produce about <InlineMath math="30" /> kWh, and on an overcast day about <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of that. During the day the panels power the hall directly and send only the surplus to the battery. After sunset, everything comes from the battery.

The battery holds <InlineMath math="20" /> kWh, but its controller never lets it fall below a fifth of that, because draining it completely would shorten its life. When the battery reaches that floor, the hall switches to the village grid, which charges <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}1{,}500" /> for each kWh.

A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the energy used by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> watts running for one hour, so the ceiling lights use <InlineMath math="3" /> kWh in five hours.

One rule comes from the wiring rather than the battery. The water pump shares a circuit with the stage sockets, so it may not run while the projector or the sound system is on.

Last year the hall bought <InlineMath math="410" /> kWh from the grid, <InlineMath math="70\%" /> of it in the rainy months from December to February. The village council calls the hall "self-sufficient". Pak Wiryo, who manages it, prefers a narrower claim: compared with buying all of its electricity, the panels saved the hall about <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5.4" /> million last year, but the battery will need replacing after about eight years, at a cost of about <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}36" /> million.

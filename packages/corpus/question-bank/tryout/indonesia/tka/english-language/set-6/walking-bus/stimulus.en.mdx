Every school morning, the road in front of SD Taman Kenari turns into a traffic jam. Parents on motorbikes and in cars stop wherever they can, and children squeeze between the vehicles to reach the gate. I believe the school should start a “walking bus”: groups of children who walk to school together along set routes, led by volunteer parents.

The first reason is safety. A group of fifteen children in bright vests, with one adult at the front and another at the back, is far easier for drivers to see than a single child stepping out between parked cars. Fewer vehicles at the gate would also make the area around the school calmer.

The second reason is health. The World Health Organization recommends that children aged five to seventeen be physically active for an average of at least sixty minutes a day. A fifteen-minute walk each way adds up to thirty minutes, half of that amount, and the children get it before and after lessons without needing extra time in the school day.

The third reason is that walking buses have already worked nearby. When SD Lereng Asri started three routes last year, the number of vehicles stopping at its gate each morning fell from about 140 to about 90, according to counts made by the school committee.

Some parents worry that walking is not possible in the rainy season. This is a real problem, but it does not have to stop the idea. Routes can pause on stormy days, and children can bring raincoats on lighter ones. A walking bus does not need to run every day to make a difference.

SD Taman Kenari should start with two routes next semester and invite parents to volunteer.

Kampung Sumber gets its piped water in turns. The village pump fills a single reservoir, and the reservoir cannot supply all four blocks at once, so each block receives water for one fixed stretch of time, on a rota that repeats every four days.

| Day of the rota | Block that receives water | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First day | Block A | 5:00 to 9:00 |
| Second day | Block B | 5:00 to 9:00 |
| Third day | Block C | 5:00 to 9:00 |
| Fourth day | Block D | 5:00 to 9:00, then Block A again from 15:00 to 17:00 |

During its hours, a block's pipes deliver about <InlineMath math="1{,}200" /> litres an hour to each house. Most houses store water in a roof tank. The common tank size is <InlineMath math="1" /> cubic metre, which is <InlineMath math="1{,}000" /> litres, and a few houses have a second tank. A tank that is full when the supply stops has to last until the block's next turn. Bu Narti tells each household to count the days until its next turn begins, counting any part of a day as a whole day.

The village health worker, Bu Narti, uses a figure of <InlineMath math="60" /> litres per person per day for drinking, cooking, washing and the toilet. She warns that a household which runs short tends to buy water from carts, at about <InlineMath math="\text{Rp}5{,}000" /> for every <InlineMath math="20" />-litre container, and that cart water is not always clean. Block A has two turns in each four-day cycle because it sits highest on the hill, where the pressure is weakest. Its afternoon turn was added in 2024, after Block A households complained that their morning water ran thin.

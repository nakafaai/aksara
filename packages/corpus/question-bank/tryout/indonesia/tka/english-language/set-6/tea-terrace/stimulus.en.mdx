The Gunung Hijau tea estate covers the lower slopes of a mountain about 1,400 meters above sea level. From the road, the hills look as if they have been combed. Long, curving rows of tea bushes follow the shape of the land, and each row is cut to about waist height so that pickers can reach the top leaves.

The bushes are planted on terraces, narrow steps cut into the slope. Each terrace is held up by a low wall of stacked stones, and a shallow ditch runs along its inner edge to carry rainwater away before it can wash the soil downhill. Between the rows, the paths are just wide enough for one person with a basket.

Tall silver oak trees stand at regular intervals across the estate. They give the tea bushes light shade during the hottest part of the day, and their fallen leaves are left on the ground to keep the soil moist.

At the top of the estate is the factory, a long white building with rows of windows on both sides. Inside, fresh leaves are spread out on wire racks so that warm air can dry them slowly. On busy mornings, the smell of drying tea drifts down the slope to the visitors' café.

The café's terrace faces east, toward a valley that is often filled with mist until about eight o'clock. In my opinion, there is no better place to drink a cup of tea than that terrace on a clear morning, when the mist lifts and the rows of green appear one by one.

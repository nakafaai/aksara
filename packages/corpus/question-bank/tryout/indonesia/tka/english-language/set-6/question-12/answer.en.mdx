# Given

Kakek's bicycle story follows **four tight structural paragraphs**:
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="1" />: describes chaining through the frame and back wheel.
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />: two oddities explained by the **father** (night lorries) and the **sister** (pumped tyres).
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />: closed chain, key still on neck, bicycle found at the grave.
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />: the uncle's "lifted over the gate" theory, the narrator's silence, and repeating the exact chaining method at the close.

# Asked

Which numbered statements accurately describe how the story is organised.

# Solution

Check paragraph order and who speaks in each paragraph. Catch two clear traps: **the father and sister explain paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />, not the narrator**, and **the ending offers silence rather than an explanation**.

> Framing a story with the exact same physical detail at start and finish quietly reminds the reader why the adults' theory fails.

## Checking each structural statement

| Statement | Correct? | Structural evidence in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Chaining (<Highlight>The chain went through the frame and the back wheel</Highlight>) appears in paragraph <InlineMath math="1" />, before paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />'s strange events. |
| II | No | Neither event is explained by the narrator: the father explains the bell and the sister confesses to the tyres. |
| III | No | The narrator <Highlight>didn't say anything</Highlight> and closes by chaining the bicycle again without explaining. |
| IV | **Yes** | The closing sentence echoes paragraph <InlineMath math="1" />: <Highlight>the chain through the frame and the back wheel, as before</Highlight>. |
| V | **Yes** | The key is confirmed still around the narrator's neck in paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />, before the uncle's theory arrives in paragraph <InlineMath math="4" />. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including II** credit the narrator with explanations supplied by the father and sister.
- **Options omitting V** miss that the reader already knows the lock and key facts before hearing the uncle's theory.

# Answer

The statements that correctly describe the story's organisation are **I, IV, and V only**.

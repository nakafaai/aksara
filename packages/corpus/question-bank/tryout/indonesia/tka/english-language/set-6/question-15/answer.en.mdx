# Given

Five events are tested for **real-world possibility**: (I) lorries shaking a gate bell, (II) someone secretly pumping soft tyres, (III) a bicycle reaching a grave <InlineMath math="12" /> km away with its chain still locked, (IV) an uncle trucking the bike home, and (V) someone cutting the chain first and riding <InlineMath math="12" /> km.

# Asked

Which numbered events **could happen in the real world**.

# Solution

Contrast III and V directly! Both put the bicycle at a grave <InlineMath math="12" /> km away, yet **a bicycle cannot move while its locked chain still binds its frame to a gatepost (III)**, whereas **cutting the chain first makes riding it <InlineMath math="12" /> km completely possible (V)**.

> Evaluate each statement on its own physical terms: adding "who had first cut its chain" removes the impossibility completely.

## Checking all five events against reality

| Event | Possible in reality? | Physical reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Heavy lorries passing nearby can vibrate an iron gate enough to jingle a bell (<Highlight>shook the gate</Highlight>). |
| II | **Yes** | Someone can easily pump tyres without telling the owner. |
| III | **No** | A closed chain running through the frame to a fixed gatepost physically traps the bicycle on the spot. |
| IV | **Yes** | Loading a bicycle into a truck is completely ordinary. |
| V | **Yes** | Once the chain is cut, riding a bicycle <InlineMath math="12" /> km is entirely possible in the real world. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options omitting V** (such as I, II, and IV only) judge V by the story's uncut chain rather than V's own wording.
- **I, II, III, IV, and V** accepts the single physically impossible event (III).

# Answer

The events that could happen in the real world are **I, II, IV, and V only**.

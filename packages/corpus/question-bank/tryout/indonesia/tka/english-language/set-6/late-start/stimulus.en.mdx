**Text 1: a letter from the parents' association of SMA Cahaya**

Our school day should start at 8:00 instead of 6:45. Teenagers' body clocks run late, and asking them to think clearly before seven in the morning wastes the first lesson of every day. Last year, three schools in our district moved their start to 8:00. In the semester after the change, the share of students arriving late at those schools fell from <InlineMath math="14\%" /> to <InlineMath math="6\%" />, and the average mark in first-lesson tests rose by <InlineMath math="5" /> points. No other change could do as much for so little cost.

**Text 2: a reply from Pak Teguh, deputy head of SMA Cahaya**

I share the parents' view that a 6:45 start is hard on teenagers, and the lateness figures are striking. But the three schools did not change only their start time. Two of them also opened a canteen breakfast in the same semester, and none of them published their first-lesson marks for earlier years, so we cannot tell whether <InlineMath math="5" /> points is a rise or a normal swing. Our school also shares its buses with the primary school, which starts at 7:30. Moving us to 8:00 would leave our students waiting at school, or would need new buses that the budget does not cover. I propose a trial: start at 7:30 for one semester, the latest time the buses allow, and record lateness and first-lesson marks before and after.

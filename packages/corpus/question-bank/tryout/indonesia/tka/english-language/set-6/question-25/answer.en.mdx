# Given

**Four candidate statements** summarise the exchange between the SMA Cahaya parents' association (Text <InlineMath math="1" />) and deputy head Pak Teguh (Text <InlineMath math="2" />).

# Asked

Which numbered statements belong in an accurate summary of the two texts.

# Solution

Keep **the parents' 8:00 request and three-school evidence (I)**, **Pak Teguh's agreement that 6:45 is a problem (II)**, and **his 7:30 measured trial (IV)**, while rejecting III, which twists "we cannot isolate the cause" into "he proved the later start did not work".

> Saying evidence is inconclusive ("cannot tell") is never the same as proving a policy failed.

## Checking each summary statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in both texts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Belongs | Captures Text <InlineMath math="1" />'s 8:00 proposal and its evidence from three district schools. |
| II | Belongs | Captures Pak Teguh's opening agreement: <Highlight>I share the parents' view that a 6:45 start is hard on teenagers</Highlight>. |
| III | Excluded | Distorts his point: he argues the trial had confounding changes, never proving that the later start failed to cut lateness. |
| IV | Belongs | Captures his closing counterproposal: <Highlight>start at 7:30 for one semester ... and record lateness and first-lesson marks before and after</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** turn methodological caution into a disproof Pak Teguh never establishes.
- **I and IV only** or **II and IV only** drops either their shared starting point (II) or the parents' proposal (I).

# Answer

The statements belonging in an accurate summary are **I, II, and IV only**.

# Given

The Kali Jernih recount describes **timing floating oranges between two bamboo stakes**, **Pak Rudi's rule dropping the fastest and slowest runs**, and **the narrator's pacing error** (<InlineMath math="9" /> metres instead of <InlineMath math="10" /> metres), which made the first speed answer too high.

# Asked

Which numbered statements belong in an accurate summary of the account.

# Solution

Check both **how a shorter true distance affects speed** and **the closing sentence stating what the group changed** after Pak Rudi measured with a tape.

> Dividing a falsely large distance (<InlineMath math="10" /> m instead of <InlineMath math="9" /> m) by the same average time always overstates the river's speed.

## Checking each summary statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in the account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Belongs | Summarises the float method used to measure river speed between two stakes. |
| II | Belongs | Following Pak Rudi's rule, they crossed out the third (<InlineMath math="31" /> s) and fifth (<InlineMath math="18" /> s) runs. |
| III | Belongs | The stakes were <InlineMath math="9" /> m apart rather than <InlineMath math="10" /> m, so using <InlineMath math="10" /> m made the initial <InlineMath math="0.44" /> m/s answer too high. |
| IV | Excluded | Directly refuted by the final sentence: <Highlight>We kept the times and changed only the distance</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including IV** invent a repeat of all six runs ruled out by the closing sentence.
- **I and II only** or **II and III only** drops either the core distance mistake (III) or the float method (I).

# Answer

The statements belonging in an accurate summary are **I, II, and III only**.

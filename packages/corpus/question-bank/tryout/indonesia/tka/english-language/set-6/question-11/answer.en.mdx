# Given

Details across the paragraphs of Kakek's bicycle lock together tightly:
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="1" />: the chain runs **through the frame and back wheel** to the iron gatepost, and the narrator wears **the only key** around the neck.
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />: on an earlier occasion, the sister pumped the tyres to borrow the bicycle during school hours.
- Paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />: on the hundredth morning, the chain stays on the gatepost, **the padlock stays closed**, **the key stays on the narrator's neck**, and the bicycle rests at Kakek's grave **<InlineMath math="12" /> km** from the yard.

# Asked

Putting together details across the story, which numbered statements the reader can conclude about the morning the bicycle was found at the grave.

# Solution

Join the locked-bicycle clues from paragraphs <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />: **a closed chain through the frame cannot be lifted free**, **the narrator held the only key**, and **the grave sits <InlineMath math="12" /> km away**. Meanwhile, the sister's earlier borrowing belongs to a separate day with no tie to the hundredth morning.

> Never carry a character's earlier ordinary action onto a later mystery unless the text links the two events.

## Checking each cross paragraph conclusion

| Statement | Concluded? | Cross-paragraph evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | The chain ran <Highlight>through the frame and the back wheel</Highlight> and remained <Highlight>still wrapped around the gatepost, the padlock still closed</Highlight>, so lifting the bicycle free was impossible. |
| II | **Yes** | Paragraph <InlineMath math="1" /> states the narrator <Highlight>wore the only key on a string around my neck</Highlight>, still there that morning. |
| III | No | The sister's borrowing happened earlier when the tyres turned hard, nothing connects her to the hundredth morning. |
| IV | **Yes** | The bicycle leaned against Kakek's grave, and <Highlight>The cemetery is twelve kilometres from our yard</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** transfer the sister's earlier borrowing onto the hundredth morning without evidence.
- **I and IV only** or **II and IV only** drops one of the two locked-chain facts that create the central impossibility.

# Answer

The reader can conclude **I, II, and IV only**.

# Given

The story establishes **two recurring everyday patterns** (passing night lorries shaking the gate so the bell rings, the sister secretly pumping tyres to borrow the bicycle) and **consistent physical and character facts** (the narrator keeps the key on a string, stays silent, and chains the frame and back wheel as before).

# Asked

Based on the story, which numbered predictions are reasonable.

# Solution

Accept predictions that extend **proven patterns and physical lock mechanics**, and reject predictions that invent an unmotivated change in the narrator's habits (switching to a drawer or confronting the uncle later).

> Reasonable predictions continue established behaviour and physical constraints rather than inventing sudden character reversals.

## Checking each prediction

| Prediction | Reasonable? | Evidence in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | The father showed the bell <Highlight>only rang when the night lorries went past on the main road and shook the gate</Highlight>, making a passing lorry the likely cause again. |
| II | **Yes** | Since the sister secretly pumped the tyres once to borrow the bicycle, hard tyres again could reasonably be her doing. |
| III | No | The narrator strictly honours Kakek's key rule and chains the bicycle "as before", with zero hint of switching to a drawer. |
| IV | No | The narrator <Highlight>didn't say anything</Highlight> when the uncle spoke, and nothing hints at speaking up later. |
| V | **Yes** | With the chain locked <Highlight>through the frame and the back wheel</Highlight>, taking the bicycle without the key physically requires cutting the chain or breaking the padlock. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III or IV** invent sudden behaviour changes with no textual basis.
- **I and V only** or **II and V only** omits a pattern explicitly established in paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Answer

The reasonable predictions based on the story are **I, II, and V only**.

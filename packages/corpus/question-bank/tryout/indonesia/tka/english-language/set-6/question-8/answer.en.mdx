# Given

The river-speed recount opens with a **retrospective line** ("was wrong for a week") and mentions later that the narrator **had previously measured paces on the classroom floor** before pacing the riverbank on Tuesday.

# Asked

The chronological order in which events P, Q, R, S, and T actually occurred.

# Solution

Rebuild real time rather than paragraph order! Measuring paces indoors (**Q**) happened before Tuesday's riverbank trip (**P**), and writing the retrospective account (**T**) could only begin last, after Pak Rudi's tape check a week later (**R**).

> Past perfect ("had measured") pushes an event earlier than the main timeline, while a retrospective opening line sits at the very end of the timeline.

## Why the other options fail

- **Orders starting with P** overlook the earlier indoor pace measurement (Q).
- **Q, P, R, S, T** puts Pak Rudi's tape check (R) ahead of computing the first answer (S).
- **T, Q, P, S, R** mistakes the retrospective opening sentence (T) for the first event.

# Answer

The events happened in the order **Q, P, S, R, T**.

# Given

Pak Teguh **doubts that the later start caused the drop in lateness** at the three schools because two of them also opened a **canteen breakfast** in the same semester.

# Asked

Which numbered facts, if true, would **weaken Pak Teguh's doubt** that the later start caused the fall in lateness.

# Solution

Follow the logic of his doubt: he suspects a **rival cause (canteen breakfast or another shift)** produced the drop. Any fact that rules out the breakfast or a district-wide trend **weakens his doubt** (I, II, IV), whereas introducing yet another simultaneous change like bus routes (III) **strengthens** his doubt.

> Ruling out rival causes weakens a sceptic's doubt, while adding a brand-new rival cause reinforces it.

## Testing each fact against Pak Teguh doubt

His doubt rests on <Highlight>the three schools did not change only their start time. Two of them also opened a canteen breakfast in the same semester</Highlight>.

| Fact | Weakens doubt? | Effect on causal explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | The school **without a canteen breakfast** saw the exact same drop, showing breakfast was not needed for the fall. |
| II | **Yes** | Lateness stayed unchanged at other district schools keeping their start time, ruling out a district-wide shift. |
| III | No | Changing bus routes in the same semester adds another confounding variable, which **strengthens** his doubt. |
| IV | **Yes** | A breakfast starting only in the final month cannot explain a semester-long drop in lateness. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** treat a brand-new confounding change as weakening doubt when it actually feeds doubt.
- **I only** overlooks that ruling out district trends (II) and late-starting breakfasts (IV) weakens his doubt too.

# Answer

The facts that would weaken Pak Teguh's doubt are **I, II, and IV only**.

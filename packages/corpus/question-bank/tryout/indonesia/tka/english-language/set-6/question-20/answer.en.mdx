# Given

A neighbour dismisses **seven days of damp sacks as extra work**, claiming the concrete path will look identical after two days.

Step <InlineMath math="4" /> requires damp sacks for **<InlineMath math="7" /> days** because concrete that dries too quickly loses **up to a third of its strength**, while Step <InlineMath math="5" /> bars walking for **<InlineMath math="3" /> days** and motorbikes for **<InlineMath math="7" /> days**.

# Asked

Which numbered replies to the neighbour are supported by the instructions.

# Solution

Test each reply against **the real purpose of damp sacks** (preventing strength loss, not preserving appearance) and **how Steps <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> run in parallel**: walking starts after three days, yet the sacks remain damp through day seven.

> Parallel curing rules never trade off against each other: keeping motorbikes off the path cannot replace keeping the sacks damp.

## Checking each reply against the guide

| Reply | Supported? | Direct evidence in Steps 4 and 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Step <InlineMath math="4" /> states the true reason: <Highlight>Concrete that dries too quickly loses up to a third of its strength</Highlight>, not how it looks. |
| II | **Yes** | Step <InlineMath math="5" /> permits walking after <InlineMath math="3" /> days while Step <InlineMath math="4" /> keeps the sacks damp <Highlight>for <InlineMath math="7" /> days</Highlight>. |
| III | No | Keeping motorbikes off for seven days does not replace damp sacks or prevent rapid drying. |
| IV | No | The guide requires <InlineMath math="7" /> full days of damp sacks, never just <InlineMath math="2" />. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** swap the damp-sack curing rule for the motorbike rule.
- **Options including IV** shorten seven days down to the neighbour's two days.
- **I only** overlooks how the three-day and seven-day rules work together in II.

# Answer

The replies supported by the instructions are **I and II only**.

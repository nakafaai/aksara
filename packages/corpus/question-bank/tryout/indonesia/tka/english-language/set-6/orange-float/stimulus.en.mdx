Our measurement of the Kali Jernih was wrong for a week, and the mistake was mine.

The task for our science group was to find how fast the river flows past the footbridge below the school. Pak Rudi showed us the float method: mark two points on the bank, drop an orange in the water upstream of the first mark, and time how long it takes to pass from the first mark to the second. An orange floats low in the water, so the wind barely moves it. He gave us one rule for the results: run the float six times, cross out the fastest and the slowest time, and average the other four, because a single snag or gust can spoil one run.

On Tuesday, Siska and I marked the bank with two bamboo stakes. I paced out what I took to be <InlineMath math="10" /> metres. [GAP] Dodi stood at the first stake with the stopwatch, Siska dropped the oranges, and I fished them out below the second stake with a net.

The following Tuesday, Pak Rudi brought a measuring tape to check the stakes. The distance between them was <InlineMath math="9" /> metres, not <InlineMath math="10" />. My paces were shorter on the muddy bank than on the classroom floor where I had measured them. We kept the times and changed only the distance.

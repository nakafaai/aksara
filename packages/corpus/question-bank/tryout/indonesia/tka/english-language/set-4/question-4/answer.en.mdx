A comparison needs **two sides for every difference**, so collect what the text says about each level before reading the options.

| Feature | Ground floor | Upper platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| View | Narrow viewing slits in the front wall | "the whole lagoon and the mudflats beyond it" |
| Cover | A dim room with a front wall and flaps over the slits | "no walls, only a waist-high rail" |
| Birds and visitors | Herons hunt "only a few meters from the bench" | Birds "can easily see anyone standing up there" |

Read the table row by row. The platform gives the wider view because it has no walls, while the ground floor looks out through narrow slits. The ground floor gives better cover, and on the platform visitors must sit and move slowly because the birds notice them easily. The correct comparison is that the platform gives a wider view, but **birds notice people there more easily**.

Check that both halves of this option come from the text. The wide view is stated in the fourth paragraph, and the same paragraph explains why visitors must sit on the low benches.

- "The platform is busiest in the evening, while the ground floor is busiest at sunrise." The text says the platform is busiest during the first hour after sunrise, and it never says when the ground floor is busiest.
- "The platform is closer to the hunting herons than the bench downstairs is." The text places the herons a few meters from the ground-floor bench and never says that the platform is closer.
- "The platform stays open in strong wind, while the ground floor then closes." The text says the opposite about the platform: it "is closed whenever the wind is strong".
- "The platform has viewing slits with flaps, while the ground floor has an open rail." This swaps the features of the two levels.

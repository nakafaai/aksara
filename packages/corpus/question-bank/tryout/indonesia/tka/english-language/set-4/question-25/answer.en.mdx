A summary of an argument must include **the position, the main evidence, and the answer to the objection**. Check each option for all three.

The position appears at the start and at the end: Kalisari should plant shade trees on the streets that people use most on foot. The evidence is the measurement on one street, which shows that shade cooled every surface and helped most on paved ones. The objection about cost is answered in the fourth paragraph with a plan for species, planting openings, and watering. The last paragraph adds that the data come from one afternoon on one street and that the town should keep measuring.

The option saying that measurements on one street support planting trees on busy walking routes, with a plan for their care, covers the position, the evidence, and the answer to the objection. It also keeps the evidence modest with the words "on one street", which matches **the limits stated in the last paragraph**.

- "Shade trees cost too much to water, so the town should build roofs over its sidewalks instead." The writer argues the opposite and never mentions roofs.
- "Measurements on one street prove that trees would make the whole town cooler and healthier." The writer says the data describe "the ground, not the air or anyone's health", so "prove" and "the whole town" claim too much.
- "Shop owners and the public works office disagree about how hot the streets of the town become." The shop owners object to the costs. They do not dispute the temperatures.
- "An infrared thermometer showed that grass stays cooler than asphalt in every season of the year." The measurements come from one afternoon in September, so they cannot cover every season.

In the last week of June, three months after we planted them, we waded back into the Muara Asri estuary to count our mangroves. That count is the reason I am writing this.

In March, <InlineMath math="36" /> students from SMA Negeri 4 had planted <InlineMath math="600" /> seedlings along the estuary in three zones: <InlineMath math="200" /> by the old sea wall (Zone A), <InlineMath math="250" /> on the open mudflat (Zone B) and <InlineMath math="150" /> at the mouth of the creek (Zone C). Bu Fitri, the ranger, showed us how to tie each seedling to a bamboo stake so that the tide would not pull it out. In May, a flood came down the creek and tore most of the stakes out of Zone C. Two weeks after the flood, we replanted <InlineMath math="50" /> seedlings there.

Bu Fitri's counting rules were strict. A seedling counted as surviving only if it had grown at least two new leaves. A seedling that was alive but had no new leaves was marked "uncertain", because many such seedlings die later. The seedlings replanted in May were left out of the June count altogether, since they had been in the ground for less than two months.

Bu Fitri works out a zone's survival rate as the surviving seedlings divided by the seedlings whose fate is known, so the uncertain ones are left out of the bottom of the fraction as well as the top.

A week after the count, the town newspaper ran a short piece under the headline "Students' mangroves: only <InlineMath math="64\%" /> survive". It had divided <InlineMath math="385" /> by <InlineMath math="600" />. I wrote to the editor, and my letter appeared the following Saturday. I did not argue that the true figure was higher or lower. I argued that a figure which treats the uncertain seedlings as dead is not a survival rate at all, and that the piece never mentioned the flood, which explains most of the losses in one zone and says nothing about the planting.

The decision about where to begin appears at the end: Kalisari "should begin planting on its busiest walking routes this year". To find **the ideas the writer combines**, look for the reasons given in earlier parts of the text for choosing those routes.

| Option | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The hottest air in town is found near the bus terminal. | Do not select | The text never measures air temperature. The last paragraph says the measurements describe "the ground, not the air". |
| Many people walk these routes at midday, mostly without shade. | Select | The first paragraph says residents walk these routes "in the middle of the day" and that "for most of the way there is no shade at all". |
| Shop owners have offered to pay for watering the trees. | Do not select | The shop owners object to the cost of watering. They make no offer to pay. |
| Shade made the biggest difference on paved surfaces. | Select | The third paragraph says the difference "was largest on the paved surfaces where people walk and wait", and the last paragraph repeats that the data show "where shade makes the biggest difference". |

The two selected ideas come from different parts of the text and work together. The idea about the walkers explains **who needs shade**: people on foot when the sun is strongest. The idea about paved surfaces explains where shade does the most good: on the paving they walk on. Together they point to the busiest walking routes.

The option about the walkers says "mostly without shade", and that limit matches the text, where shade is missing "for most of the way" and not along the whole route. The correct options are the people who walk these routes at midday, mostly without shade, and the larger effect of shade on paved surfaces.

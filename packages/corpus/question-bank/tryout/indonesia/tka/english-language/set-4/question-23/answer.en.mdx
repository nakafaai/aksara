# Given

In Text <InlineMath math="2" />, Bu Anjani raises **two distinct kinds of criticism**: some details question **what Text <InlineMath math="1" />'s survey actually measures** (<InlineMath math="2.1" /> hours), while others attack **the council's proposed <InlineMath math="60" />-minute cap**.

# Asked

Which numbered details from Text <InlineMath math="2" /> challenge **what Text <InlineMath math="1" />'s survey can show**.

# Solution

Split the council's argument into two boxes: **Evidence (the survey)** and **Remedy (the flat cap)**. This question asks strictly which details undermine the **Evidence box**, leaving aside objections to the cap itself.

> Separating a critique of evidence from a critique of the proposed remedy instantly filters out tempting true-in-the-text distractors.

## Sorting survey challenges from remedy objections

| Detail from Text 2 | Challenges survey? | Actual target of the critique |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **Yes** | Questions what the survey number represents: <Highlight>measured the time students spent, not the time teachers set</Highlight>. |
| II | **Yes** | Questions whether the <InlineMath math="2.1" /> hours are all homework: <Highlight>time spent at a desk with a phone beside it is not all homework</Highlight>. |
| III | No | Critiques the **flat-cap remedy** (treating <InlineMath math="40" />-minute and two-hour tasks alike), not the survey. |
| IV | No | Critiques the **flat-cap remedy** (failing to prevent five deadlines on one night), not the survey. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III or IV** mix objections to the cap remedy with objections to the survey evidence.
- **I only** drops detail II, which directly challenges what the survey hours actually counted.

# Answer

The details from Text <InlineMath math="2" /> that challenge what Text <InlineMath math="1" />'s survey can show are **I and II only**.

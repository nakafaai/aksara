This question asks why one step **comes before another**. The text puts "stand with your back to the Sun" at the start of the setting-up paragraph, before you hold up the foil card and look for the image on the screen.

Find the reason for this order in the warnings. The first paragraph says it is never safe to look straight at the Sun without eye protection made for that purpose, and the last paragraph adds: "Never look at the Sun through the pinhole, even for a moment." Lining up the foil card and the screen means moving the cards while you watch for the bright circle. If you did that while facing the Sun, your eyes could easily turn toward it. When you turn your back first, **your eyes already face away from the Sun** for the whole time you line up the cards.

Check this reason with the last paragraph. When you lose the image, the text tells you to "check that your back is still turned to the Sun, and then raise them again". The same order is repeated, which shows that it is there to protect your eyes.

You turn your back first because facing away before you line up the cards means you never look toward the Sun.

- "The foil has to warm up in the sunlight before it can form an image." The text never mentions warming the foil. The image forms as soon as sunlight passes through the pinhole.
- "The image appears only after the eclipse has already started." The circle is an image of the Sun at any time. Only the dark curve depends on the eclipse.
- "Turning first makes the image larger than it would be otherwise." According to the text, the size of the image depends on the distance between the screen and the foil.
- "The pinhole must be made outside while you face away from the Sun." The pinhole is made in the second paragraph, before you go outside.

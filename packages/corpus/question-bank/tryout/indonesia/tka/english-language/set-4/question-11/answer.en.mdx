# Given

Two parts of the story must be read side by side:
- **Grandfather's rule** in paragraph <InlineMath math="1" />: a kite always flies downwind of the hand holding it.
- **The last afternoon** in paragraph <InlineMath math="5" />: wind blew off the sea from the west, boat flags streamed towards the village (east), yet **the red kite hung far out over the water to the west**.

# Asked

Combining Grandfather's rule with the last afternoon, which numbered statements are true.

# Solution

Picture a simple compass on the cliff edge: sea to the **west**, village to the **east**. With a west wind blowing toward the village, downwind means **east**, so hanging over the water to the west placed the red kite directly **upwind**.

> Separate wind origin from downwind direction: a west wind blows eastward, so anything sitting west of the flier stands upwind.

## Checking each statement against wind direction

Grandfather's rule states <Highlight>A kite always flies downwind of the hand that holds it</Highlight>, while paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> reports <Highlight>The wind came off the sea from the west</Highlight> and the kite <Highlight>hung far out over the water to the west</Highlight>.

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | True | Stated directly: <Highlight>The wind came off the sea from the west</Highlight>. |
| II | True | Downwind of a west wind is east, over the land and village behind them. |
| III | True | The red kite <Highlight>hung far out over the water to the west</Highlight>. |
| IV | False | The narrator merely guesses the wind turned, and the next sentence refutes it: the flags <Highlight>were still streaming towards the village</Highlight>. |
| V | False | West of Grandfather in a west wind is **upwind**, the exact opposite of downwind. |

## Why the other options fail

- **I and II only** omits the explicitly stated western position of the red kite (III).
- **Options including IV or V** ignore the steady boat flags or confuse upwind with downwind.

# Answer

The true statements combining Grandfather's rule and the last afternoon are **I, II, and III only**.

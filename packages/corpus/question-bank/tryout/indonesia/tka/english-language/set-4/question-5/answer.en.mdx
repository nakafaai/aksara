A fact can be checked by measuring, counting, or observing. An opinion is a judgment that people could reasonably disagree with. Test each sentence by asking **how you could check it**.

Four sentences pass that test. You could measure the boardwalk to check "about two hundred meters", watch the reeds over several mornings to see whether herons often hunt in front of the slits, look at the reserve's records after a Monday, and visit on a windy day to see whether the platform is closed.

The sentence "The ground floor is the most rewarding place for a first visit." fails the test. The phrase "most rewarding" is **the writer's own judgment**. Another visitor might find the platform more rewarding because it shows the whole lagoon, and no measurement could settle that disagreement. The next sentence gives a reason for the view, but a reason does not turn a judgment into a fact.

The opinion is the claim that the ground floor is the most rewarding place for a first visit.

- "The boardwalk is about two hundred meters long." This is a length that can be measured. The word "about" only makes the length approximate.
- "Herons often hunt in the reeds directly in front of the slits, only a few meters from the bench." The word "often" describes how frequently something happens, and repeated observation can check it.
- "Every Monday, a ranger copies the new entries into the reserve's records." This describes a routine that anyone can check by looking at the records.
- "The platform is closed whenever the wind is strong." This states a rule of the reserve that can be checked on a windy day.

A conclusion is supported only if **the data or the text actually show it**. Keep in mind two limits that the writer states in the last paragraph: the measurements come from "a single afternoon on one street", and they describe "the ground, not the air or anyone's health". A conclusion that goes beyond these limits is not supported, even if it sounds likely.

| Statement | Category | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The trees lowered the air temperature across the town center. | Not supported | The table gives surface temperatures on one street, and the text says the measurements describe "the ground, not the air". |
| Asphalt in the sun is hotter than grass in the sun at every hour of the day. | Not supported | Every value was measured "at 1 p.m. on one clear day". The data say nothing about other hours. |
| On the day of the test, concrete in full sun was hotter than grass in full sun. | Supported | In full sun, the table gives <InlineMath math="47" /> for concrete and <InlineMath math="36" /> for grass, and <InlineMath math="47" /> is greater than <InlineMath math="36" />. |

The likeliest confusion is the conclusion about every hour of the day, because asphalt really was hotter than grass in the table. The problem is the phrase "at every hour of the day": measurements taken only at 1 p.m. cannot show what happens at other hours. So the conclusions about the air across the town center and about every hour of the day are not supported, and the conclusion about concrete and grass on the day of the test is supported.

**Text 1: a letter from the student council of SMA Harapan**

We ask the school to cap homework at <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes a school day for every class. In our survey of <InlineMath math="400" /> students last month, the average student spent <InlineMath math="2.1" /> hours a day on homework, and <InlineMath math="58\%" /> slept less than seven hours on school nights. A school that keeps students up past midnight is failing them. Homework that eats our sleep makes us worse learners, not better ones, and a firm cap is the only fair answer, because every teacher thinks their own subject deserves the extra half hour.

**Text 2: a reply from Bu Anjani, mathematics teacher**

The council is right that many students are too tired, and right that the problem starts with homework. But its survey measured the time students spent, not the time teachers set, and time spent at a desk with a phone beside it is not all homework. Last term I timed my own tasks. I designed each to take about <InlineMath math="40" /> minutes, and my students reported <InlineMath math="75" /> minutes on average. Some of that gap is my misjudgement, and some is not homework at all. A flat cap would treat the forty-minute task and the two-hour one alike, and it would not stop five deadlines from falling on the same night. What we need first is a shared calendar, so that no class sets a long task on a day when two others already have.

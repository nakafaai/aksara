The question is about one paragraph, so first work out **what that paragraph does in the argument**. The fourth paragraph opens with an objection: "Some shop owners object that trees cost too much." It names two costs: watering through the dry season and roots that can crack sidewalks.

Then the writer responds: "These are real costs, but the town can plan for them." The rest of the paragraph gives the plan: species that are less likely to lift paving, a wide opening in the sidewalk for each tree, and money for watering until the young trees are established. So the paragraph does two things in order. It **admits a real drawback** and then shows how the town can manage it.

Check this against the writer's wording. The writer calls the costs "real", which rules out any reading in which the writer brushes the shop owners aside.

The main purpose of the paragraph is to admit a real drawback of the plan and show how to manage it.

- "To show that the shop owners' worries about cost are based on false information" contradicts the writer, who calls the costs "real".
- "To explain why the measurements were taken on only one street in the town" does not fit this paragraph, which never mentions where the measurements were taken. The one-street limit is stated only in the last paragraph, and no reason for it is given there.
- "To persuade the shop owners to pay for watering the young trees themselves" is not in the text. The writer says that the town should set aside the money.
- "To list the tree species that grow best along the sidewalks of the town" goes beyond the paragraph, which names no species at all.

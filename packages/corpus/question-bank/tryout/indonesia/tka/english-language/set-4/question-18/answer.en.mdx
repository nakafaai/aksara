# Given

The guide opens with the claim that **new fish die in new tanks because the water lacks cleaning bacteria**, then traces how fish waste creates ammonia and why a sudden fish load overwhelms insufficient bacteria.

# Asked

Which numbered details in the guide support its opening claim that new fish die in new tanks.

# Solution

Ask one focused question for every detail: **does this help explain why fish die in a brand-new tank**? Details explaining missing bacteria or toxic ammonia buildup belong, while facts about harmless nitrate or heater settings do not.

> Being true in the passage is not enough: a supporting detail must actually explain the danger named in the claim.

## Checking each detail against the opening claim

| Detail | Supports claim? | Link to why new fish die |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Yes | States the primary cause outright: <Highlight>New fish die in new tanks because the water has none of the bacteria that clean it</Highlight>. |
| II | Yes | Identifies the toxic substance generated: <Highlight>Fish waste produces ammonia</Highlight>. |
| III | No | Saying nitrate is far less harmful explains why the final product is **safe**, not why new fish die. |
| IV | No | Setting the heater to <InlineMath math="26^\circ\text{C}" /> is a setup instruction, not part of the toxicity mechanism. |
| V | Yes | Explains the fatal overload: <Highlight>a tank that suddenly receives many fish makes more ammonia than its bacteria can handle</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** treat a fact about safe nitrate as evidence for why fish die.
- **I and II only** or **II and V only** drop one link of the bacterial-overload chain.

# Answer

The details supporting the opening claim are **I, II, and V only**.

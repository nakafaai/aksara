Kalisari should plant shade trees along the streets that people use most on foot, starting with the road from the bus terminal to the market. Many residents walk these routes in the middle of the day, when the sun is strongest, and for most of the way there is no shade at all.

Last September, the town's public works office measured surface temperatures with an infrared thermometer at 1 p.m. on one clear day. It compared spots in full sun with spots under the few large trees that already grow along Jalan Melati. The results are shown in the table below.

| Concrete sidewalk | <InlineMath math="47" /> | <InlineMath math="32" /> |
| Grass strip | <InlineMath math="36" /> | <InlineMath math="29" /> |

Shade lowered the temperature of every surface, but the difference was largest on the paved surfaces where people walk and wait, and smallest on the grass. A surface that heats up in the sun also warms the air just above it, so a shaded sidewalk is a more comfortable place to walk.

Some shop owners object that trees cost too much. Young trees must be watered through the dry season, and roots can crack sidewalks. These are real costs, but the town can plan for them. It can choose species that are less likely to lift paving, give each tree a wide opening in the sidewalk, and set aside money for watering until the young trees are established.

The measurements have limits. They come from a single afternoon on one street, and they describe the ground, not the air or anyone's health. Still, they show where shade makes the biggest difference. Kalisari should begin planting on its busiest walking routes this year and keep measuring as the trees grow.

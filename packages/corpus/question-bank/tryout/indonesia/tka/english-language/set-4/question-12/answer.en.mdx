# Given

The summer story with Grandfather unfolds across **five chronological scenes**: the wind lesson on day one, the cliff climb that afternoon, the rainstorm two days later, the snapped string on day five (found that same evening), and the red kite on the last afternoon.

# Asked

Which numbered statements accurately describe how the story is organised.

# Solution

Read the five paragraphs like dated diary entries. Verify **where each explanation sits**, **whether events stay in chronological order**, **whether Grandfather speaks at the end**, and **when the lost kite is recovered**.

> Narrative-structure items are settled cleanly by checking time markers and dialogue paragraph by paragraph from start to finish.

## Checking each structural statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct trace in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | True | Paragraph <InlineMath math="1" /> states <Highlight>On my first day he gave me a lesson before he gave me a kite</Highlight>. |
| II | True | The windy cliff above the calm village is explained in paragraph <InlineMath math="2" />, and the low-flying swallows before rain are explained in paragraph <InlineMath math="3" />. |
| III | False | Paragraph <InlineMath math="5" /> explicitly says <Highlight>Grandfather did not say a word</Highlight>. |
| IV | True | Time markers move steadily forward from the first day through the fifth day to the last afternoon. |
| V | False | The string snapped on day five and they found the kite <Highlight>that evening</Highlight>, on the **same day**, not a different day. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** invent a closing explanation that Grandfather never gives.
- **Options including V** misread *that evening* as a later day.
- **I and II only** or **II and IV only** leave out either chronological order (IV) or the opening lesson (I).

# Answer

The statements that correctly describe the story's organisation are **I, II, and IV only**.

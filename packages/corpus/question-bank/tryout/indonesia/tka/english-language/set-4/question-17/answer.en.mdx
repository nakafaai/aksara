This question gives a new case, so apply **the rule about distance** from the fourth paragraph: "To make the image larger, move the screen farther from the foil. The image will also become fainter".

The rule describes how the image changes with distance: the farther the screen is from the foil, the larger and fainter the image becomes. The same relationship works in the other direction, so the closer the screen is, the smaller and brighter the image becomes. The student holds the screen close, only twenty centimeters below the foil, so the image will be **smaller and brighter**.

Check this with the rest of the same sentence, which goes on: "so find a distance at which the circle is large enough to study and still bright enough to see clearly". That advice only makes sense if a close screen gives a bright but small circle and a distant screen gives a large but faint one.

The image will most likely be smaller and brighter than at a greater distance.

- "It will be larger and fainter than at a greater distance." This describes moving the screen farther away, which is the opposite of what the student does.
- "It will not appear until the screen is at least one meter away." The text sets no minimum distance. The circle appears as soon as the light through the pinhole lands on the screen.
- "It will show the edge of the Moon more clearly than before." A smaller image gives less room for detail, and the text links the Moon's edge with the progress of the eclipse, not with the distance.
- "It will turn into a dark curve instead of a bright circle." The dark curve appears because the Moon covers part of the Sun. Moving the screen does not create it.

# Given

Grandfather establishes two physical rules early in the story:
- **Flight direction**: a kite always flies downwind of the hand holding it.
- **Afternoon cliff breeze**: every afternoon the sea breeze runs up the cliff face while houses and palms shelter the village below in calm air.

# Asked

Following Grandfather's lesson and the cliff description, which numbered predictions are reasonable.

# Solution

Picture wind carrying a kite the way a stream carries a paper boat: **wind from the sea pushes inland**, while **wind from the land pushes out to sea**. Reasonable predictions obey those physical rules, never the single unexplained marvel of the red kite.

> Base predictions strictly on the story's stated physical laws, never on a one-off unexplained exception at the climax.

## Checking each prediction

The two rules are <Highlight>A kite always flies downwind of the hand that holds it</Highlight> and <Highlight>Every afternoon, he explained, the sea breeze runs up the face of the cliff</Highlight>.

| Prediction | Verdict | Reason under Grandfather's rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Reasonable | Sea wind blows inland, so downwind lies over the land behind the flier. |
| II | Reasonable | Land wind blows seaward, so downwind lies out over the water. |
| III | Reasonable | The sea breeze rises up the cliff every afternoon even when palms and houses <Highlight>keep the village below in its own calm</Highlight>. |
| IV | Unreasonable | Hanging over the water in a sea wind is upwind. Only the unexplained red kite did that, contradicting Grandfather's lesson. |

## Why the other options fail

- **I, II, III, and IV** treats the unexplained red-kite anomaly as a normal rule.
- **I and II only**, **I and III only**, and **II and III only** omit a prediction directly guaranteed by the rules.

# Answer

The reasonable predictions are **I, II, and III only**.

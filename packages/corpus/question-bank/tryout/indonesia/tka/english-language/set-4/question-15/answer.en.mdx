A story stays realistic when everything in it **could happen in the real world**. Something strange may appear, but the story must explain it in ordinary terms. So ask what the "tall man" really is and how the story shows it.

The "tall man" appears only while the room is dark and Bayu is frightened: "The coat stand by the door became a tall man with no face". The word "became" describes how the coat stand looked to Bayu, and the curtains move "as if someone were breathing behind them", which is a comparison. When Bayu brings the candle back, the story explains the figure: "In its light, the tall man by the door was only a coat stand with his father's raincoat hanging on it."

So the figure is **a trick of darkness and fear**, and the candlelight reveals the real object. Nothing impossible happens in the story, because a power cut, a candle, a walking stick, and flashlights are all ordinary.

The story remains realistic because Bayu imagines the tall man in the dark, and the candle reveals a coat stand.

- "The tall man is real, but he leaves the house before Bayu brings the candle." The candlelight shows a coat stand in the same place, so there was never a real man.
- "The story is realistic only because it takes place during a heavy rainstorm." Rain is ordinary, but it does not explain the tall man, and the word "only" ignores the candle scene.
- "His grandmother also sees the tall man, which proves that the figure exists." She cannot see well, and she only hears Bayu's funny description of it.
- "The coat stand really moves in the dark, but it stops when the lights return." The coat stand never moves. The story only describes how it looked to Bayu in the dark.

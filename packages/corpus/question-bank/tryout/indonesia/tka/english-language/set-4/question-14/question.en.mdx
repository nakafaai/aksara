The lights went out just after eight, when the rain was loudest on the tin roof. Bayu was alone in the front room with his grandmother, who had fallen asleep in her chair after dinner. His parents had gone to a wedding in the next town and would not be home before midnight.

In the dark, the room began to change. The coat stand by the door became a tall man with no face, and the curtains moved as if someone were breathing behind them. Bayu pulled his knees up onto the sofa and decided to stay there until the power came back. Nothing could make him cross that room.

Then his grandmother woke up. "Bayu? Where are you?" Her voice sounded small and unsure. She could not see well even in daylight, and Bayu remembered that her walking stick was leaning against the kitchen door at the other end of the house.

He stood up before he could think about the tall man. Keeping one hand on the wall, he felt his way to the kitchen, found the stick, and opened the drawer where his mother kept candles and matches. He lit a candle and carried it back to the front room. In its light, the tall man by the door was only a coat stand with his father's raincoat hanging on it. Bayu almost laughed.

He gave his grandmother her stick and sat beside her. When she asked what had taken him so long, he described the "tall man" so dramatically that she laughed until she had to wipe her eyes. The power came back an hour later. Before he went to bed, Bayu put a flashlight in the kitchen drawer and another one beside his grandmother's chair.

A few weeks later, the power goes out again while Bayu is in the kitchen and his grandmother is in her chair. What will Bayu most likely do?

A sentence fits a paragraph when it **matches the paragraph's job**, takes the same form, and agrees with the rest of the text. The paragraph that explains how to make the projector gives instructions for cutting the card, taping the foil, and pushing the pin through it "once to make a small, round hole". A good extra sentence should therefore be an instruction about the same materials, and it must not break the safety rules elsewhere in the text.

The sentence about replacing torn foil or a hole that is too large meets all three conditions. It is an instruction, like the sentences around it. It deals with the foil and the pinhole. It also supports the aim of "a small, round hole", since a torn or oversized hole no longer fits that description.

Check the safety condition as well. This sentence asks you to do nothing that involves looking toward the Sun, so it agrees with **both warnings in the text**.

The sentence that fits best is the one that tells you to replace the foil and try again if it tears or if the hole becomes too large.

- "Hold the foil up to the Sun and look through the hole to check that it is open." This breaks the warning "Never look at the Sun through the pinhole, even for a moment."
- "Long ago, many people were frightened by eclipses and told stories to explain them." This is a piece of history. It does not help anyone make the projector.
- "Dark sunglasses are a good choice when you want to check the hole quickly." The first paragraph says that ordinary sunglasses "are not enough, however dark they are".
- "I made my first projector last year, and it worked much better than I expected." This is a personal story told with "I". The procedure addresses the reader as "you" and never describes the writer's own experience.

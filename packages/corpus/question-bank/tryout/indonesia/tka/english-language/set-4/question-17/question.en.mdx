During a partial solar eclipse, the Moon covers only part of the Sun. Even then, it is never safe to look straight at the Sun without eye protection made for that purpose, and ordinary sunglasses are not enough, however dark they are. A pinhole projector solves this problem. Instead of looking at the Sun, you look at a small image of it on a sheet of card.

To make the projector, you need two sheets of stiff white card, a piece of aluminum foil, tape, scissors, and a pin. Cut a square hole about three centimeters wide in the middle of one card. Tape the foil over the hole so that it lies flat. Then push the pin through the center of the foil once to make a small, round hole. The second card will be your screen.

Go outside and stand with your back to the Sun. Hold the foil card above your shoulder so that sunlight falls on the foil. Hold the screen in your other hand, or lay it on the ground, so that the light passing through the pinhole lands on it. A small, bright circle will appear on the screen. This circle is an image of the Sun.

To make the image larger, move the screen farther from the foil. The image will also become fainter, so find a distance at which the circle is large enough to study and still bright enough to see clearly. As the eclipse goes on, a dark curve will cut into the edge of the bright circle. This curve is the edge of the Moon.

Never look at the Sun through the pinhole, even for a moment. The tiny hole does not make sunlight any safer for your eyes. If you lose the image, lower both cards, check that your back is still turned to the Sun, and then raise them again.

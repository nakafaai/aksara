An outline lists **the main events in the order they happen**. To check an option, test two things: every event must be in the story, and the events must follow the story's order.

Trace the story first. The lights go out, and Bayu is too frightened to move (first and second paragraphs). His grandmother wakes up and calls him (third paragraph). He fetches her stick and a candle, and the candlelight shows that the tall man is a coat stand (fourth paragraph). He sits with her, the power comes back, and he puts out two flashlights for next time (last paragraph).

The option that moves from the power cut that scares Bayu, to his grandmother's call, to fetching light and her stick, and finally to preparing for next time follows this order and leaves out no main stage.

Check the end with care, because it is easy to stop at the candle. The story closes with Bayu putting a flashlight "in the kitchen drawer and another one beside his grandmother's chair", which is **his preparation for the next power cut**.

The outline that matches the story is that a power cut scares Bayu, his grandmother calls, and he fetches light and her stick and prepares for next time.

- "Bayu lights a candle, the tall man appears, his grandmother calls him, and his parents come home early." The tall man appears before the candle is lit, and the story never says that his parents come home early.
- "His parents call from the wedding, Bayu finds the candles, the lights go out, and his grandmother falls asleep." His grandmother is already asleep when the lights go out, and no one calls from the wedding.
- "The lights go out, Bayu's grandmother finds the candles herself, Bayu tells her a story, and the power returns." Bayu finds the candles himself while his grandmother stays in her chair.
- "Bayu waits on the sofa until the power comes back, and only then does he put out two flashlights." He leaves the sofa long before the power comes back.

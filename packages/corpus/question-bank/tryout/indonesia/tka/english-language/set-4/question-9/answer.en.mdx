This question is about **order in time**: does Fajar's pattern come before, during, or after the change of speed? Two sentences decide it. The third paragraph gives the general rule: the drummer "signals each change with his hands before the group follows". The fourth paragraph shows one case: "Fajar leaned forward and played a quick pattern. I slowed down with everyone else".

Put the rule and the case together. The rule says that the signal comes first and the group's change follows. In the case, the quick pattern is the signal, and slowing down is the change. So the pattern comes **just before the group slows down**, and it tells the players that the change is coming. That is also why watching Fajar fixed the writer's timing: the writer could see each change coming.

Check the order of the sentences in the fourth paragraph. The pattern is mentioned first, and "I slowed down with everyone else" follows it.

Fajar's quick pattern comes just before the change, so the players know to slow down.

- "It comes after the group has slowed down, to show that the change is complete." This reverses the rule, in which the signal comes "before the group follows".
- "It comes together with the big gong at the end of each long cycle." The gong marks the end of a cycle, and the text never links the quick pattern to the gong.
- "It comes only at the anniversary concert, after weeks of rehearsals." The pattern appears in the rehearsal right after Bu Ratna's advice, two weeks before the concert.
- "It comes while the writer is still counting the beats." By then the writer had been told to play "without counting at all", and the fourth paragraph opens with how strange it felt "to stop trusting my own counting".

The question asks for the writer's main purpose, so the right option has to fit every paragraph. Start by naming **what each paragraph does**. The first paragraph describes the boardwalk, the reed screens along it, and the sign about talking. The second describes the ground floor with its viewing slits and their flaps. The third describes the bird board and the logbook, the fourth describes the upper platform, and the last gives the writer's view of a first visit.

Most of these parts come with a reason, and the reasons point the same way. The screens are there so that people "cannot be seen from the water". A flap is lowered because birds "can notice movement through an open slit". On the platform, visitors "sit on the low benches and move slowly". The text is therefore **a description of one place and of how its design keeps the birds undisturbed**.

Check this against the whole text. The first four paragraphs each add a part of the hide or a rule for using it. Even the opinion in the last paragraph is about a part of the hide: it recommends the ground floor because herons often hunt only a few meters from its bench. The writer's purpose is to describe how a bird hide lets visitors watch birds without disturbing them.

- "To persuade readers to visit the reserve when large flocks arrive" builds on one detail about the flocks after sunrise. The writer never invites readers to come at that time.
- "To explain why herons prefer to hunt in the reeds near the hide" is too narrow. The text says that herons often hunt in front of the slits, but it gives no reason for it.
- "To report what a group of visitors saw during one morning at the hide" fits a recount. The text tells no visit and no series of events.
- "To teach readers how to build a hide beside a shallow lagoon" fits a procedure. The text describes a finished hide and gives no building steps.

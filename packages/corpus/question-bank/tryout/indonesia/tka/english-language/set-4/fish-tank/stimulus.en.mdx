**Before the fish: setting up the science club tank**

New fish die in new tanks because the water has none of the bacteria that clean it. Fish waste produces ammonia, one kind of bacteria turns ammonia into nitrite, and another turns nitrite into nitrate, which is far less harmful. Growing these bacteria before any fish arrive is called cycling the tank. The bacteria grow only as fast as the waste they are given, so a tank that suddenly receives many fish makes more ammonia than its bacteria can handle.

1. Fill the tank with tap water. Add dechlorinator to every litre of new tap water that goes in, at <InlineMath math="5" /> millilitres for every <InlineMath math="10" /> litres, and never to water that is already in the tank.
2. Run the filter and keep the heater at <InlineMath math="26^\circ\text{C}" />. Feed the empty tank a pinch of fish food every day, so the bacteria have waste to live on.
3. Test the water every two days for ammonia, nitrite and nitrate.
4. The tank is cycled when ammonia and nitrite both read <InlineMath math="0" /> ppm on three tests in a row and nitrate reads above <InlineMath math="0" /> ppm on the last of them. Nitrate above <InlineMath math="0" /> shows that the second kind of bacteria is at work.
5. If nitrate reads above <InlineMath math="40" /> ppm on that last test, change a quarter of the water before adding any fish.
6. Add fish slowly: no more than a third of the planned number of fish in any one week. Test ammonia two days after each addition. If it reads above <InlineMath math="0.25" /> ppm, add no more fish that week and change a quarter of the water.

The club's tank holds <InlineMath math="120" /> litres. It was filled on 1 August.

# Given

Every paragraph of the Bukit Kenanga passage tracks **one part of the former observatory**, connecting its original astronomical design to its current role in the town library.

# Asked

Which sentence does not fit the purpose of the paragraph it appears in.

# Solution

Imagine taking a room-by-room tour of a converted house when the guide suddenly drops a random remark about a gym across town. That stray sentence merely **echoes the words "tall windows"** while wandering off to an unrelated building.

> An off-topic sentence often hides behind a surface word echo from the sentence before it even though it contributes nothing to the paragraph's subject.

## Why the sports hall sentence does not fit

- The sentence <Highlight>The town's new sports hall, finished last year, also has tall windows</Highlight> sits inside paragraph three, which describes the library's first floor.
- Its only link is echoing <Highlight>the same tall windows</Highlight> from the prior sentence, while **introducing a completely different building** unrelated to how the observatory serves as a library.

## Why the other four sentences belong

- **The <InlineMath math="1998" /> move sentence** in paragraph one introduces the essay's central thesis that almost every floor now has an unplanned new job.
- **The no-heat lamp sentence** in paragraph four extends the vault's mission of keeping heat away from fragile records.
- **The wide-doors sentence** in paragraph two directly links an old workshop feature to modern wheelchair and pram access.
- **The no-books-upstairs sentence** in paragraph six explains how the library protects books when the dome slit opens to damp night air.

# Answer

The sentence that does not fit its paragraph is **the one stating that the town's new sports hall, finished last year, also has tall windows**.

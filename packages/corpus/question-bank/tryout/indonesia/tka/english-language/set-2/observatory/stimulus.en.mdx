For ninety years the white dome on Bukit Kenanga held a telescope. Since the observatory moved to a darker hill in 1998, the building has been the town library, and almost every floor now does something its builders never planned.

The ground floor was once the workshop where instrument makers ground lenses and repaired clocks. Its wide doors, made for moving heavy equipment, now let prams and wheelchairs straight in, and the children's section fills the space where the lathes stood. The returns desk sits by the entrance.

On the first floor, the astronomers once sat at long tables working out the positions of stars by hand. The tables are still there, under the same tall windows, and students now use them for homework and exam revision. The town's new sports hall, finished last year, also has tall windows. The reference shelves line the walls.

The second floor is the old vault, where the observatory kept its glass photographic plates. Its walls are almost <InlineMath math="1" /> metre thick and it has no windows, so its temperature barely changes between the wet and dry seasons. The town archive keeps its old maps and land records here. Visitors may read them only at the table by the door, under a lamp that gives off almost no heat.

Two parts of the building still do the job they were built for: the vault still protects fragile paper and glass from heat and damp, and the dome still opens to the sky. Everything else has been given a new job.

The dome itself is the reading room. The telescope was taken away, but the stone pier that held it still stands in the middle, now topped with a glass case of old star charts. On those evenings, no books are carried up, because the night air off the hill is damp.

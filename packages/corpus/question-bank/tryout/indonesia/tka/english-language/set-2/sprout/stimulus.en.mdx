Seeds that have been stored too long, or kept somewhere damp, slowly lose the ability to sprout. A germination test tells you what share of a packet will still grow, so you know how thickly to sow before you waste a whole bed.

Count out <InlineMath math="50" /> seeds from the packet at random, not just the biggest ones. Lay two sheets of paper towel on a tray, dampen them, and spread the seeds so that none touch. Cover the tray to hold in moisture and keep it at room temperature, out of direct sun. Keep the towel damp but never wet, because seeds sitting in standing water may rot. Check the tray every day. A seed counts as germinated once its root is at least <InlineMath math="2\text{ mm}" /> long. Remove each germinated seed as you count it, so that no seed is counted twice.

Stop counting on the last day of the test length printed on the packet. Seeds that sprout after that day are left out, because seeds that slow to start grow too weakly in a garden bed. Then work out the germination rate: the number of germinated seeds divided by the number tested, as a percentage.

Our gardening club tested two packets of the same mung bean variety, each with a seven-day test length. Packet A had been kept in a sealed jar indoors, and packet B in a paper bag in the tool shed. Our rule for using the result is simple. At <InlineMath math="80\%" /> or more, sow as usual. From <InlineMath math="50\%" /> up to <InlineMath math="80\%" />, sow two seeds in every hole instead of one. Below <InlineMath math="50\%" />, buy new seed.

# Given

In Text <InlineMath math="2" />, the head groundskeeper notes that the town has **four gardeners for six parks** right after citing two years of hand weeding and right before proposing a small trial along the edges of two parks.

# Asked

Which numbered points this staffing detail helps the groundskeeper argue.

# Solution

Picture running a four-person crew responsible for six entire parks. Mentioning that ratio forms the **middle link in a clear argument**: heavy hand weeding would stretch the crew, Text <InlineMath math="1" /> understates the labour, and starting small makes practical sense.

> To see what a supporting detail argues, follow the writer's actual chain of reasoning without inventing demands the writer never makes.

## Checking each numbered point

| Point | Verdict | Role in Text 2's argument |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | Sits directly beside <Highlight>For its first two years it needs weeding by hand</Highlight>, showing how thin the team would be stretched. |
| II | Supported | Limited staff directly justifies his closing proposal to <Highlight>start with the edges of two parks</Highlight> rather than converting all six parks at once. |
| III | Not supported | He never asks the council to hire more gardeners, choosing instead to start small and measure work hours. |
| IV | Supported | It backs his opening critique that <Highlight>the article makes the change sound cheap and quick</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Any option including III** assigns him a hiring demand he never makes.
- **Options dropping II or I** break the link between short staffing, heavy hand weeding, and his modest two-park trial.

# Answer

The four-gardeners detail helps him argue **I, II, and IV only**.

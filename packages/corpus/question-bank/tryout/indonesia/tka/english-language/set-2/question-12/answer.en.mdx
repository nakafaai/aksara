Some reasons are stated and some must be **followed one link further**. Check each chain.

- I is supported. The text says to pick seeds *at random, not just the biggest ones*. The point of a test is to tell you *what share of a packet will still grow*. If you picked only big seeds, the result would describe big seeds, not the packet.
- II is supported, as a chain. *Seeds sitting in standing water may rot*. A rotten seed won't sprout, so fewer seeds count as germinated and the rate comes out lower than the seed really deserves.
- III is not supported. Late seeds are left out *because seeds that slow to start grow too weakly in a garden bed*, not because they've rotted. A late sprout has a root, so it hasn't rotted.
- IV is supported. Removing seeds means *no seed is counted twice*. A seed counted twice would add to the germinated number, so the rate would come out too high.

So the supported reasons are I, II and IV. Any option with III gives the wrong reason for leaving out late seeds, and leaving out I misses why the sample must be random.

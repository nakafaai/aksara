# Given

Paragraphs one through three of the germination procedure explain the **purpose behind each step**: picking <InlineMath math="50" /> seeds at random, keeping towels damp rather than wet, removing counted sprouts, and stopping on the packet's final test day.

# Asked

Which numbered statements about the reasons behind these steps are supported by the text.

# Solution

Think of every rule in a lab test as a guardrail protecting an honest score from **drifting artificially high or artificially low**. Follow each rule one logical step forward to see which outcomes match the text.

> Matching a procedural rule to its reason means checking the exact risk the text warns against rather than swapping risks between steps.

## Checking each reason statement

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence and reasoning chain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | Paragraph two directs picking <Highlight>at random, not just the biggest ones</Highlight> so the test reflects <Highlight>what share of a packet will still grow</Highlight> rather than only the biggest seeds. |
| II | Supported | Paragraph two warns <Highlight>seeds sitting in standing water may rot</Highlight>, and rotting seeds fail to sprout, dragging the measured rate too low. |
| III | Not supported | Paragraph three states late sprouts are excluded <Highlight>because seeds that slow to start grow too weakly in a garden bed</Highlight>, not because they rotted (a sprouting seed is alive). |
| IV | Supported | Paragraph two explains removing counted sprouts <Highlight>so that no seed is counted twice</Highlight>, which stops the rate from coming out too high. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Any option including III** confuses weak growth with rotting.
- **Options dropping I or IV** omit why random sampling or removing counted seeds protects the rate.

# Answer

The supported statements about the reasons behind the steps are **I, II, and IV only**.

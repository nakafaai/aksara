# Given

Text <InlineMath math="2" /> (the groundskeeper's reply) follows **three clear beats**: initial agreement on helping insects, three practical maintenance and machinery objections, and a proposal for a measured two-park pilot.

# Asked

Which numbered statements belong in an accurate summary of Text <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Solution

Think of summarizing a balanced reply letter in three bullets: **what the writer agrees with**, **what practical hurdles he flags**, and **what compromise he proposes**.

> A faithful summary of a nuanced reply keeps its agreement, its objections, and its middle-ground proposal without turning caution into rejection.

## Checking each summary statement

| Statement | Verdict | Direct evidence in Text 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Belongs | Opens directly with <Highlight>I agree that more native planting would help insects</Highlight>. |
| II | Belongs | Summarizes all three hurdles: two years of <Highlight>weeding by hand</Highlight>, <Highlight>watering through its first dry season</Highlight>, and <Highlight>heavy equipment that the town does not own</Highlight>. |
| III | Excluded | False because he never asks the council to abandon native planting, only to pilot it first. |
| IV | Belongs | Closes with his plan to <Highlight>start with the edges of two parks</Highlight> and <Highlight>let the council decide from our own numbers</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** twist a careful counterproposal into an outright rejection.
- **Options omitting I, II, or IV** drop one of Text <InlineMath math="2" />'s three structural parts.

# Answer

The statements belonging in a summary of Text <InlineMath math="2" /> are **I, II, and IV only**.

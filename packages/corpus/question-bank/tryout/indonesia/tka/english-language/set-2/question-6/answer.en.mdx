# Given

Four sentences drawn from **Text <InlineMath math="1" /> (the wild-parks article)** and **Text <InlineMath math="2" /> (the groundskeeper's reply)** must each be classified as either a fact or an opinion.

# Asked

Whether each of the four sentences states a fact or an opinion.

# Solution

Imagine walking into the park with a notepad and a measuring cup. A **fact** can be verified by counting, measuring, or observing, whereas an **opinion** makes a value judgement like "smartest" even when a number sits inside the sentence.

> A percentage or number inside a sentence never turns a value judgement into a fact if the core claim cannot be verified by measurement.

## Classifying each sentence

| Sentence | Category | Reasoning from the texts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two-year trial in Lindung found three times as many bee visits on planted strips | Fact | From Text <InlineMath math="1" />, bee visits across two strips can be counted and verified directly. |
| Fast-growing weeds take over before native plants are established | Fact | From Text <InlineMath math="2" />, <Highlight>Fast-growing weeds take over before the native plants are established</Highlight> reports an observable gardening event. |
| Even a <InlineMath math="10\%" /> cut in mowing would be the smartest decision in years | Opinion | From Text <InlineMath math="1" />, calling it <Highlight>the smartest decision the council has made in years</Highlight> is a value judgement no measurement can settle. |
| Mowing alone burns about <InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> litres of fuel a year | Fact | From Text <InlineMath math="1" />, annual mower fuel consumption is directly measurable from town records. |

# Answer

The Lindung bee trial, fast-growing weeds taking over, and burning about <InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> litres of fuel are **facts**, whereas calling a <InlineMath math="10\%" /> cut the smartest decision in years is an **opinion**.

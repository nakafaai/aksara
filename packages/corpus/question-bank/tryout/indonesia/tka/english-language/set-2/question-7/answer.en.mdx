# Given

Text <InlineMath math="1" /> proposes replacing most town lawn with native plants, while Text <InlineMath math="2" /> from the head groundskeeper accepts the insect benefit yet adds **early maintenance and equipment constraints**.

# Asked

Which numbered conclusions are supported when Text <InlineMath math="1" /> and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> are read together.

# Solution

Imagine two people describing the same park project from two angles. A combined conclusion holds only when it **survives both texts together** without skipping a qualifier like "once they are established".

> Cross-text conclusions must combine genuine evidence from both passages while honoring every time limit or qualifier either text sets.

## Checking each combined conclusion

| Conclusion | Verdict | Evidence from Text 1 and Text 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | Text <InlineMath math="1" /> cites <Highlight>three times as many bee visits</Highlight>, and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> opens with <Highlight>I agree that more native planting would help insects</Highlight>. |
| II | Not supported | Text <InlineMath math="1" /> qualifies water savings as <Highlight>once they are established</Highlight>, and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> adds that a new meadow <Highlight>needs watering through its first dry season</Highlight>. |
| III | Supported | Joins Text <InlineMath math="1" /> (lawn cut <Highlight>every week from March to November</Highlight>) with Text <InlineMath math="2" /> (meadow cut <Highlight>once or twice a year with heavy equipment that the town does not own</Highlight>). |
| IV | Not supported | The Lindung trial in Text <InlineMath math="1" /> counted only bee visits and measured nothing about maintenance costs. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including II** ignore the qualifier <Highlight>once they are established</Highlight> and the first dry season's watering.
- **Options including IV** treat a bee count as if it were a cost study.
- **I only** overlooks conclusion III, which directly joins mowing frequency from Text <InlineMath math="1" /> with missing machinery from Text <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Answer

The conclusions supported by reading Text <InlineMath math="1" /> and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> together are **I and III only**.

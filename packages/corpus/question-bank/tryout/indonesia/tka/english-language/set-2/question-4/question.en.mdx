The Riverleaf is a small library boat that visits four villages along a wide river. Its blue roof can be seen above the reeds before the engine is heard. At the front, a folding ramp connects the deck to each landing. The ramp has raised edges and a rough surface, while two crew members stand nearby when visitors board.

Inside, the shelves are lower than those in a normal library. Children's books face outward in bright covers. Larger-print novels and audio players occupy a shelf near the entrance. A narrow central aisle leads to a reading bench beneath three windows. Elastic cords hold the books in place when the river becomes rough.

The rear section is a work area. Returned books go into a dry inspection tray before they are placed back on the shelves. A small fan moves air through the cabin, and sealed boxes protect paper during heavy rain. The boat also carries a portable router, but the crew never promises internet access because the signal changes along the route.

Every landing has a different timetable card. A green mark means the boat can use the regular dock. A yellow mark means the water level must be checked before the ramp is opened. During the dry season, one village is served from a temporary landing farther downstream.

The Riverleaf is compact, but little space is wasted. Each feature responds to movement, moisture, access, or an uncertain connection. The boat does not copy a building and place it on water. It reshapes library work around the river that carries it.

Why does one village use a temporary landing in the dry season?

# Given

Four numbered statements compare **Text <InlineMath math="1" /> (the wild-parks proposal)** with **Text <InlineMath math="2" /> (the parks team's reply)** on insects, water savings, overall stance, and mowing equipment.

# Asked

Which numbered statements accurately compare Text <InlineMath math="1" /> and Text <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Solution

Picture comparing two recipes side by side. A comparison statement holds only when **both halves accurately represent their respective texts** without dropping a qualifier or exaggerating caution into rejection.

> A comparison fails the moment even one half misrepresents its text, such as ignoring a qualifier or turning a cautious counterproposal into a flat rejection.

| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Correct | Text <InlineMath math="1" /> cites Lindung's bee visits, and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> states <Highlight>I agree that more native planting would help insects</Highlight>. |
| II | Incorrect | Trap: Text <InlineMath math="1" /> never claims immediate savings, explicitly qualifying <Highlight>once they are established</Highlight>, which agrees with Text <InlineMath math="2" />. |
| III | Incorrect | Text <InlineMath math="2" /> does not reject native planting, instead recommending a measured start on <Highlight>the edges of two parks</Highlight>. |
| IV | Correct | Text <InlineMath math="1" /> omits meadow-cutting machinery completely, while Text <InlineMath math="2" /> flags <Highlight>heavy equipment that the town does not own</Highlight>. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including II** overlook <Highlight>once they are established</Highlight> in Text <InlineMath math="1" />.
- **Options including III** misread a cautious pilot proposal as outright rejection.
- **I only** omits the clear equipment contrast in statement IV.

# Answer

The accurate comparisons between Text <InlineMath math="1" /> and Text <InlineMath math="2" /> are **I and IV only**.

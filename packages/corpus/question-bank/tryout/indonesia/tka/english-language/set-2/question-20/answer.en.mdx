# Given

Paragraphs one and two trace **Bu Sari's words and actions** with the yellow umbrella from Monday morning through Tuesday when she leaves it at the Jalan Melati bus shelter.

# Asked

Which numbered statements about Bu Sari are supported by the story.

# Solution

Picture someone saying "I never keep what isn't mine," using the umbrella all day and drying it overnight, and then leaving it at the bus stop the next morning. Her **words and actions briefly clash before finally lining up**.

> Reading a character accurately means catching both the small tension between words and actions and how the character resolves it, without inventing scenes that never happened.

## Checking each statement about Bu Sari

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence from paragraphs one and two |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supported | Paragraph one quotes <Highlight>I never keep what isn't mine</Highlight> to her first customer, adding <Highlight>and she said it again at noon</Highlight>. |
| II | Supported | Despite saying that, paragraph one notes <Highlight>She held the umbrella over her pots until dusk, and that night she dried it beside her stove</Highlight>. |
| III | Not supported | Paragraph two shows she left it at the shelter and Raka found it open while <Highlight>nobody stood near it</Highlight>, so they never met. |
| IV | Supported | Paragraph two states that on Tuesday she left it <Highlight>where its owner might look</Highlight>, acting on her own words. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Any option including III** invents a hand-off between Bu Sari and Raka that never occurs.
- **Options omitting II or IV** miss either the temporary gap between her words and actions or how she finally honors them.

# Answer

The statements about Bu Sari supported by the story are **I, II, and IV only**.

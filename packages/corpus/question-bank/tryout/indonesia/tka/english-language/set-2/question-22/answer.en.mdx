# Given

Paragraphs four and five describe the **second-floor old vault**, where the town archive stores fragile old maps and land records that must be shielded from heat, damp, and sunlight.

# Asked

Which numbered details support the idea that the old vault suits the town archive.

# Solution

Picture choosing the safest cabinet for hundred-year-old paper maps. A supporting detail must show **real protection from heat, moisture, or light**, rather than merely stating which floor the room sits on.

> Paragraph five pins down what the archive needs: <Highlight>the vault still protects fragile paper and glass from heat and damp</Highlight>, so floor placement alone does not show suitability.

## Checking each supporting detail

| Detail | Verdict | Reasoning and paragraph-four evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Supports | Almost <InlineMath math="1" />-metre-thick walls ensure <Highlight>its temperature barely changes between the wet and dry seasons</Highlight>, shielding fragile paper. |
| II | Supports | Having <Highlight>no windows</Highlight> blocks damaging sunlight and helps keep temperature and dampness steady. |
| III | Does not support | Sitting on the second floor between study tables and the reading room is purely a location fact that offers no preservation benefit. |
| IV | Supports | Reading under a lamp <Highlight>that gives off almost no heat</Highlight> prevents heat damage even while records are in use. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options omitting IV** overlook the heat protection built into how visitors read the documents.
- **Any option including III** treats a neutral floor location as a preservation advantage.

# Answer

The details supporting that the old vault suits the town archive are **I, II, and IV only**.

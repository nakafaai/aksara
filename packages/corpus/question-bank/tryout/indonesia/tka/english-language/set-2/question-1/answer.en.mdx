A summary statement has to be **true and central to the whole account**. One of them needs the table, not just the prose.

- I belongs. Two pairs counted *birds flying south* and *birds flying north* across the same line, and Nadia *subtracted the northbound birds from the southbound ones* so no flock was counted twice.
- II belongs, but you have to check the table. The third hour has <InlineMath math="690" /> southbound birds, more than any other hour. The squall stopped counting from 9:20 to 9:45, which is <InlineMath math="25" /> minutes, and the rule marks an hour incomplete after *more than* <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes. So that hour was left out.
- III doesn't belong, because it's false. *Nadia wrote "incomplete" beside the hour anyway*, and incomplete hours *are not added to the day's total*.
- IV belongs. After seeing *last year's sheet, where every number meant the same as ours*, the writer decides *a smaller total we can compare is worth more than a bigger one we cannot*.

So an accurate summary contains I, II and IV. Without II it misses why the biggest hour is missing, and with III it adds something the text contradicts.

# Given

The Rindang Shorebird Watch account covers **four hours of shorebird counting** (including one incomplete hour stopped by a squall), the northbound subtraction rule, and a master-sheet table giving a submitted total of <InlineMath math="778" /> birds.

# Asked

Which numbered statements belong in an accurate summary of the bird-count account.

# Solution

Imagine retelling the morning survey to a friend in three crisp sentences. An accurate summary stays **true to both prose and table** while keeping **the counting method, why the biggest hour was dropped, and why the writer accepted the smaller total**.

> Checking a summary of a data-backed recount means verifying every statement against both the narrative rules and the table figures.

## Checking each summary statement

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence from text and table |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Belongs | Paragraph two explains pairs counted <Highlight>birds flying south</Highlight> and <Highlight>birds flying north</Highlight>, and paragraph five notes Nadia <Highlight>subtracted the northbound birds from the southbound ones</Highlight>. |
| II | Belongs | The table shows hour three had <InlineMath math="690" /> southbound birds (the highest), yet the squall halted counting from <InlineMath math="9\text{:}20" /> to <InlineMath math="9\text{:}45" /> (<InlineMath math="25" /> minutes), exceeding the <InlineMath math="15" />-minute limit in paragraph two. |
| III | Excluded | False because paragraph four states <Highlight>Nadia wrote "incomplete" beside the hour anyway</Highlight>, and incomplete hours are left out of the day's total. |
| IV | Belongs | The closing paragraph notes that after seeing <Highlight>last year's sheet, where every number meant the same as ours</Highlight>, the writer accepted why a smaller comparable total matters more. |

## Why the other options fail

- **Options including III** introduce a false claim that Nadia kept the incomplete third hour.
- **Options omitting I, II, or IV** lose a core pillar of the account: the subtraction method, the dropped peak hour, or the lesson on comparable totals.

# Answer

The statements belonging in an accurate summary are **I, II, and IV only**.

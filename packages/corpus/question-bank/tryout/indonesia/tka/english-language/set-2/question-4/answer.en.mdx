A supported response **stays with what the writer shows and finally thinks**, not with how a reader might feel at first.

- Nadia too strict: not supported. The writer *sulked a little*, but then understood Nadia's reason, so the account ends up agreeing with the rule.
- Ilham's figures untrustworthy: not supported. The hour was dropped because counting stopped too long, not because anyone counted badly. Ilham clicking *faster than I had ever seen him* is about the birds, not about mistakes.
- The writer's view changes: supported. She *sulked* over the third hour, then, after seeing last year's sheet, she *understood* that *a smaller total we can compare is worth more than a bigger one we cannot*.
- Staying out in the squall: not supported. Nothing in the text suggests the shelter was a mistake, and the rule exists so that interrupted hours don't distort the comparison.
- The comparable total is more useful: supported. The figures *go to a regional survey that compares sites and years*, and *the survey compares only full hours*. That's why the same rules matter more than a bigger number.

So the supported responses are that the writer's view of the third hour changes, and that a total following the same rules every year is more useful to the survey.

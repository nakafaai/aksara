On Monday morning a yellow umbrella was hooked over the handle of Bu Sari's noodle cart, though she was sure it had not been there when she pushed the cart out of her lane. A paper tag hung from it: *For whoever needs it most.* "I never keep what isn't mine," she told her first customer, and she said it again at noon. She held the umbrella over her pots until dusk, and that night she dried it beside her stove.

On Tuesday she left it at the bus shelter on Jalan Melati, where its owner might look. Raka, running to a mathematics exam with no coat, found it there already open, though the rain had only just begun and nobody stood near it. Halfway across the bridge a gust caught it and bent one rib backwards. He spent the exam worrying about how to explain the damage to an owner he had never met.

On Wednesday, outside the clinic, Raka saw Pak Darmo, who walks with a cane, waiting in the downpour for his grandson. "Borrow it," Raka said. "Just until he comes." The grandson came late, and by then Raka had gone home. When Pak Darmo opened the umbrella that evening, the bent rib was straight again. Nobody had touched it.

On Thursday the rain was the heaviest of the week. Pak Darmo was at the market when a girl named Wulan, whose own umbrella had blown inside out, stood shivering under an awning. He pressed the yellow umbrella into her hands and walked home under a rice sack.

On Friday the sky was clear. Wulan carried the umbrella to the shelter on Jalan Melati and hung it on the hook. Before she left, she took a pen from her bag and wrote a second line under the first on the tag: *Returned dry. Pass it on.*

The fifth paragraph gives the rule: *Two parts of the building still do the job they were built for: the vault ... and the dome ... Everything else has been given a new job.* So **only the vault and the dome itself** go in the first group, and every other part goes in the second, even if its new job looks like its old one.

- The ground-floor workshop: a new job. Lenses were ground there, and now it holds *the children's section*.
- The stone pier in the dome: a new job. This is the trap. The dome kept its job, but the pier is a separate part. It once *held* the telescope, and now it is *topped with a glass case of old star charts*.
- The windowless room on the second floor: the old job. That's the vault, which *still protects fragile paper and glass from heat and damp*.
- The long tables: a new job. Desk work then and desk work now can look alike, but by the text's own rule only the vault and the dome kept their jobs. The astronomers *worked out the positions of stars*, and students now use them *for homework and exam revision*.

So only the windowless second-floor room still does the job it was built for, and the workshop, the pier and the tables have all been given new jobs.

# Given

Paragraph five of the Bukit Kenanga observatory passage divides the building into **two strict groups**: only **the vault and the dome itself** still do their original job, while **everything else has been given a new job**.

# Asked

Which of the two groups each of the four listed parts of the building belongs to.

# Solution

Think of sorting rooms in a converted landmark using only two tags: "old job" and "new job." Paragraph five gives an **exact sorting rule** that settles every item, even look-alikes such as the long tables or the pier inside the dome.

> Paragraph five states the exact rule: <Highlight>Two parts of the building still do the job they were built for: the vault ... and the dome ... Everything else has been given a new job</Highlight>.

## Classifying every building part

| Building part | Group | Textual evidence and reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The ground-floor workshop | Given a new job | Paragraph two shows it once ground lenses and repaired clocks, and now holds <Highlight>the children's section</Highlight>. |
| The stone pier in the middle of the dome | Given a new job | Trap: the dome kept its job, but paragraph six shows the pier once held the telescope and is now <Highlight>topped with a glass case of old star charts</Highlight>. |
| The windowless room on the second floor | Still does its original job | Paragraph four identifies this room as <Highlight>the old vault</Highlight>, which still protects fragile paper and glass from heat and damp. |
| The long tables on the first floor | Given a new job | Paragraph three notes astronomers calculated star positions there while students now use them <Highlight>for homework and exam revision</Highlight>, outside the text's vault-and-dome pair. |

# Answer

Only the **windowless room on the second floor** still does the job it was built for, whereas the **ground-floor workshop, the stone pier inside the dome, and the first-floor long tables** have all been given new jobs.

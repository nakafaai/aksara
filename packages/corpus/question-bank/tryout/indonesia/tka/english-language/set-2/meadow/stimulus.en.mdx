**Text 1**

**Let the Parks Grow Wild**

The six parks of Kembang Town hold about <InlineMath math="4.2" /> hectares of mown grass, cut every week from March to November. The council should replace most of it with native plants. A lawn is the most wasteful surface a town can own. Mowing alone burns about <InlineMath math="3{,}000" /> litres of fuel a year, and a lawn offers almost nothing to the bees and butterflies that native flowers feed. In Lindung, the next town along the river, a two-year trial in one park found three times as many bee visits on the planted strips as on the lawn beside them. Native plants also need less water once they are established, because their roots reach deeper. Children will still have room to play, since every park can keep a mown area for games. Even a <InlineMath math="10\%" /> cut in mowing would be the smartest decision the council has made in years.

**Text 2**

**A Reply from the Parks Team**

As head groundskeeper, I agree that more native planting would help insects, and the Lindung figures match what I see on our own verges. But the article makes the change sound cheap and quick. A new meadow does not look after itself. For its first two years it needs weeding by hand, because fast-growing weeds take over before the native plants are established, and we have four gardeners for six parks. It also needs watering through its first dry season. And a meadow is cut once or twice a year with heavy equipment that the town does not own. I would start with the edges of two parks, record the bee visits and the hours of work, and let the council decide from our own numbers.

# Resolve along the motion, balance, then let the new surplus accelerate

Only the **components along the motion** pull the ship forward, and the sideways components of the two ropes cancel. At constant velocity the forward pull **equals the water resistance**. With the ropes closer to the axis the forward pull grows, the resistance has not yet changed, and the **surplus** gives the acceleration, with the mass in kilograms.

The resistance is about <InlineMath math="9.2\text{ kN}" />. At <InlineMath math="20°" /> the tugs pull forward with about <InlineMath math="11.3\text{ kN}" />, so about <InlineMath math="2.1\text{ kN}" /> is unbalanced, and the <InlineMath math="5.0\times10^{5}\text{ kg}" /> ship starts to accelerate at about <InlineMath math="4.2\times10^{-3}\text{ m s}^{-2}" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{v^2}{2.0^2}=\frac{11.3}{9.2}\Rightarrow v=2.0\sqrt{1.23}\approx2.2\text{ m s}^{-1}" />

As the ship speeds up, the resistance grows until it matches the new <InlineMath math="11.3\text{ kN}" /> pull. Because resistance goes with speed squared, a pull <InlineMath math="23\%" /> larger raises the speed by only about <InlineMath math="11\%" />, to about <InlineMath math="2.2\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

<BlockMath math="2\times7.0\cos20°\approx13.2\text{ kN},\qquad v=2.0\sqrt{\frac{13.2}{9.2}}\approx2.4\text{ m s}^{-1}" />

However hard the tugs work, each rope can carry only <InlineMath math="7.0\text{ kN}" />, so the forward pull can never exceed about <InlineMath math="13.2\text{ kN}" />. The ship settles where the resistance matches that: about <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12\text{ kN}" /> **adds the rope forces as if they were parallel**, so it misses the angle and sees no change in the pull or the speed.
- <InlineMath math="2.3\times10^{-2}\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> divides the **whole new pull** by the mass, forgetting the water resistance still acts.
- <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> makes the resistance **proportional to the speed**, not to its square.

# The source fixes the frequency, so in water the wavelength shrinks and the fringes close up

The fringe spacing follows <InlineMath math="x=\dfrac{\lambda D}{a}" />, so the air readings give the slit separation. In water, light slows by the factor <InlineMath math="n=1.33" />. Its **frequency is fixed by the lamp** and cannot change at a boundary, so the slowing must shorten the wavelength by the same factor, and the fringes close up with it.

The slits are <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ mm}" /> apart. In water the bright fringes are about <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ mm}" /> apart, and the light keeps its frequency of <InlineMath math="4.7\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.2\text{ mm}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.5\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}" /> let the **frequency drop** while the wavelength stays, but the frequency is set by the source.
- <InlineMath math="4.3\text{ mm}" /> **multiplies by the refractive index**, as if light had a longer wavelength in the denser medium.
- <InlineMath math="3.5\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}" /> with <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ mm}" /> shrinks the wavelength and **also lowers the frequency**, but only the speed and the wavelength change.

# Neighbouring loud sounds are half a wavelength apart

Each loud sound has a node at the water and an antinode just above the rim. Going from one loud sound to the next adds one more loop to the column, so **the <InlineMath math="34.0\text{ cm}" /> between the two lengths is exactly half a wavelength**, wherever that antinode sits. The wavelength is <InlineMath math="68.0\text{ cm}" /> and the speed <InlineMath math="512\times0.680=348\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. Treating the first length as a full quarter wavelength gives <InlineMath math="4\times0.155\times512=317\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, but <InlineMath math="15.5\text{ cm}" /> is clearly short of a quarter of <InlineMath math="68.0\text{ cm}" />.

# The bit of column above the rim

A quarter wavelength is <InlineMath math="17.0\text{ cm}" />, yet the first loud column measured only <InlineMath math="15.5\text{ cm}" />. **The missing <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ cm}" /> is the stretch between the rim and the antinode**, the end correction. It belongs to the open end of the tube, so it stays the same whichever fork you hold over it.

# Two loads give two equations for the same emf

Each reading gives a current, <InlineMath math="I=\dfrac VR" />. The emf is the terminal voltage **plus the voltage lost inside**, <InlineMath math="E=V+Ir" />, and it is the same in both cases, so the two equations give <InlineMath math="r" /> and then <InlineMath math="E" />. With a wire of negligible resistance, the **whole emf drives current through <InlineMath math="r" /> alone**.

<BlockMath math="1.40+0.70r=1.20+1.20r\Rightarrow r=\frac{0.20}{0.50}=0.40\,\Omega,\qquad E=1.40+0.70(0.40)=1.68\text{ V}" />

<BlockMath math="I_{\text{short}}=\frac{E}{r}=\frac{1.68}{0.40}=4.2\text{ A}" />

The terminal voltage falls <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ V}" /> when the current rises <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ A}" />, so the internal resistance is <InlineMath math="0.40\,\Omega" /> and the emf is <InlineMath math="1.68\text{ V}" />. A short circuit would draw <InlineMath math="4.2\text{ A}" />.

<BlockMath math="I=\frac{E-V}{r}=\frac{1.68-1.50}{0.40}=0.45\text{ A},\qquad R=\frac{1.50}{0.45}\approx3.3\,\Omega" />

For the terminals to show <InlineMath math="1.50\text{ V}" />, only <InlineMath math="0.18\text{ V}" /> may be lost inside, which limits the current to <InlineMath math="0.45\text{ A}" /> and needs about <InlineMath math="3.3\,\Omega" /> outside.

<BlockMath math="\frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{total}}}=\frac{IV}{IE}=\frac{1.50}{1.68}\approx89\%" />

The same current flows through the cell and the resistor, so the share delivered outside is the terminal voltage over the emf: about <InlineMath math="89\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.5\text{ A}" /> divides the **terminal voltage <InlineMath math="1.40\text{ V}" />** by <InlineMath math="r" />, but with a short circuit the whole emf acts across <InlineMath math="r" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.20\,\Omega" /> divides the voltage change by the **change in resistance**, <InlineMath math="1.0\,\Omega" />, instead of the change in current, and the slip carries into the other values.
- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ A}" /> divides the **other terminal voltage, <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ V}" />**, by <InlineMath math="r" />, again missing the emf.

# Floating means matching the weight

A toy boat in the bath sinks in until the water it pushes aside weighs as much as the boat. **The block has a mass of <InlineMath math="900+60=960\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="960\text{ g}" /> of water fills <InlineMath math="960\text{ cm}^3" />, so that is what it pushes aside**. Its own volume is the ice's <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" /> plus the stone's <InlineMath math="60/3.0=20\text{ cm}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="1020\text{ cm}^3" /> in all, and <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> stays above the water. Leave out the stone's own <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^3" /> and only <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> seems to stick out.

# Melting with a stone on board

Throw a pebble out of a toy boat into the bath and the water level drops a little. Melting does the same thing here. Plain floating ice melts into exactly the water it was pushing aside. **But while the stone rides in the ice it pushes aside its weight in water, <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" />, and lying on the bottom it pushes aside only its own volume, <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^3" />**. The <InlineMath math="900\text{ g}" /> of ice melts into <InlineMath math="900\text{ cm}^3" /> and fills its own dent, so <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> goes missing and the level falls <InlineMath math="40/100=0.40\text{ cm}=4.0\text{ mm}" />. The rule that melting ice leaves the level alone holds only when nothing denser than water rides along.

# The heaviest stone the ice can carry

A swimming float can carry only so much before it goes under. Each extra gram of stone needs <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}^3" /> more water pushed aside but brings only <InlineMath math="\tfrac13\text{ cm}^3" /> of its own volume. **The block only just floats when its whole volume is under water**: <InlineMath math="1000+\tfrac{m}{3}=900+m" />, so <InlineMath math="m=150\text{ g}" />. Treating the ice's spare <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> as the whole allowance stops at <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> and forgets that the stone adds a little volume of its own.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V_1&=\frac{960\text{ g}}{1.0\text{ g cm}^{-3}}=960\text{ cm}^3,\qquad V_0=1000+20=1020\text{ cm}^3\\
\Delta h&=\frac{(900+60)-(900+20)}{100}=0.40\text{ cm}\\
900+m&=1000+\frac{m}{3}\ \Rightarrow\ m=150\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

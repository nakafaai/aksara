# The useful output is lift plus launch, and the supply must cover the losses on top

Every second the pump gives <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ kg}" /> of water **potential energy** for the <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" /> climb and also **kinetic energy**, because the water leaves the pipe still moving. Together they are the useful output. The motor wastes <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of what it takes in, so the electrical input is the output **divided by** <InlineMath math="0.60" />, and the current follows from <InlineMath math="P=VI" />.

The pump gives the water about <InlineMath math="320\text{ W}" /> of useful power, the motor draws about <InlineMath math="2.2\text{ A}" />, and filling the tank takes <InlineMath math="750\text{ s}" /> and about <InlineMath math="0.11\text{ kW h}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="300\text{ W}" /> counts only the **lift**, forgetting that the water leaves the pipe with kinetic energy too.
- <InlineMath math="0.79\text{ A}" /> **multiplies by the efficiency** instead of dividing, as if the motor took in less than it gives out.
- <InlineMath math="6.6\text{ kW h}" /> multiplies kilowatts by **minutes** instead of hours.

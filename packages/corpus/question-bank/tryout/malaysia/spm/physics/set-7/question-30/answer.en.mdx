# Follow the energy from sunlight to storage, losing some at each stage

The sunlight on the panel is intensity times area. The panel keeps <InlineMath math="18\%" /> of it as electrical power. The battery's capacity in **ampere hours** becomes energy through <InlineMath math="E=VIt" />. Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of the panel's output is stored, so the stored power is smaller and the time is **longer** than the simple ratio suggests.

The panel gives about <InlineMath math="170\text{ W}" /> of electrical power, of which about <InlineMath math="138\text{ W}" /> is stored. Storing <InlineMath math="4.32\text{ MJ}" /> at that rate takes about <InlineMath math="8.7" /> hours of full sunlight.

<BlockMath math="E_{\text{day}}=0.69\times4.32\approx2.99\text{ MJ},\qquad E_{\text{fridge}}=60\times12\times3600\approx2.59\text{ MJ},\qquad \frac{2.99-2.59}{4.32}\approx9\%" />

By sunset the battery holds about <InlineMath math="2.99\text{ MJ}" />. The refrigerator uses <InlineMath math="2.59\text{ MJ}" /> overnight, leaving only about <InlineMath math="9\%" /> of a full charge by morning.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="87\%" /> and <InlineMath math="27\%" /> **ignore the charging loss**, as if all the panel's output were stored.
- <InlineMath math="960\text{ W}" /> is the **sunlight falling on the panel**, not the electrical power it produces.
- <InlineMath math="64\%" /> charges the refrigerator for **one hour's energy**, <InlineMath math="60\text{ W h}" />, instead of the twelve hours it runs.

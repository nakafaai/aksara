# Series shares the extension, parallel shares the force

In **series** both springs carry the **whole <InlineMath math="20\text{ N}" />**, and their extensions add. In **parallel** the bar makes both springs stretch **the same amount**, and their forces add to <InlineMath math="20\text{ N}" />, so the stiffer spring carries more.

In series the pair stretches about <InlineMath math="0.17\text{ m}" />, and the softer <InlineMath math="200\text{ N m}^{-1}" /> spring stores <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ J}" />. In parallel both stretch <InlineMath math="0.040\text{ m}" />, so the <InlineMath math="300\text{ N m}^{-1}" /> spring carries <InlineMath math="12\text{ N}" /> and the other <InlineMath math="8\text{ N}" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{F_{200}}{W}=\frac{200}{500}=0.40,\qquad 0.40\,W\le25\Rightarrow W\le\frac{25}{0.40}\approx63\text{ N}" />

In parallel the <InlineMath math="200\text{ N m}^{-1}" /> spring always carries <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of the load. It reaches <InlineMath math="25\text{ N}" /> when the load is about <InlineMath math="63\text{ N}" />, while the stiffer spring then carries <InlineMath math="38\text{ N}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ N}" /> and <InlineMath math="50\text{ N}" /> share the load **equally** in parallel, but equal extensions make the stiffer spring carry more.
- <InlineMath math="25\text{ N}" /> treats the parallel pair **as if it were in series**, where each spring would carry the whole load.
- <InlineMath math="0.040\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="42\text{ N}" /> treat the series pair as **if it were in parallel** and then give the <InlineMath math="200\text{ N m}^{-1}" /> spring the stiffer spring's <InlineMath math="60\%" /> share.

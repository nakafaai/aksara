# The hammer arrives at ten metres per second

Falling freely through <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ m}" />, the hammer gains <InlineMath math="v^2=2gh=100" />, so it hits the pile at <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. **That speed belongs to the hammer alone**, and the pile is still at rest when it arrives.

# The collision shares the momentum

Hammer and pile leave together, so the <InlineMath math="5000\text{ kg m s}^{-1}" /> of momentum is spread over <InlineMath math="2000\text{ kg}" /> and **they start down at only <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" />**. Keeping the full <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> would drive the pile <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ m}" /> in a single blow, as if the pile had no mass to share it with.

# The soil fights the pile and its weight helps

The ground pushes up with <InlineMath math="60\text{ kN}" />, but **hammer and pile still weigh <InlineMath math="20\text{ kN}" /> while they move**, so the net force is <InlineMath math="40\text{ kN}" /> upwards and they slow at <InlineMath math="20\text{ m s}^{-2}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v&=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{2\times10\times5.0}=10\text{ m s}^{-1},\qquad V=\frac{500\times10}{2000}=2.5\text{ m s}^{-1}\\
a&=\frac{60\,000-20\,000}{2000}=20\text{ m s}^{-2},\qquad s=\frac{2.5^2}{2\times20}=0.156\text{ m},\qquad \frac{1.0}{0.156}=6.4
\end{aligned}" />

Leaving the weight out gives <InlineMath math="30\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> and only <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ m}" /> per blow, which would make the job look like ten blows.

# Counting whole blows

Each blow adds <InlineMath math="0.156\text{ m}" />. Six blows reach <InlineMath math="0.94\text{ m}" />, **still short of the metre**, so a seventh blow is needed even though <InlineMath math="6.4" /> rounds down to <InlineMath math="6" />.

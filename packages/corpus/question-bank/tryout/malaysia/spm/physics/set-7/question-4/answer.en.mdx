# Force is the momentum the water loses each second

By Newton's second law, the force on the plate equals the **rate of change of momentum** of the water, and turning the water back changes its velocity by the **sum** of the two speeds. Through the same nozzle, a faster jet also carries **more mass each second**, in proportion to its speed.

At <InlineMath math="30\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> the nozzle passes <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ kg}" /> a second, each stopped from <InlineMath math="30\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, so the force is four times as large, <InlineMath math="120\text{ N}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ N}" /> takes the change of velocity as **<InlineMath math="15-10" />**, ignoring that the water reverses direction.
- <InlineMath math="60\text{ N}" /> doubles the speed but **keeps the mass flow at <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ kg}" /> a second**, though the same nozzle now passes twice as much water.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ N}" /> is the force on the **flat wall**, not on the plate that throws the water back.

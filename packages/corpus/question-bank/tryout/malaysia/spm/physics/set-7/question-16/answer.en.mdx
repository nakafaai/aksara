# Closing speed when boat and waves meet

Heading out, the boat and the crests move towards each other, so **their speeds add**. Crests <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}" /> apart met every <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ s}" /> means a closing speed of <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, and the waves supply <InlineMath math="6.0" /> of it, leaving <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> for the boat. Calling the whole <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> the boat's speed forgets that the crests were coming to meet it.

# Running home with the waves

Back towards the shore, the crests catch the boat from behind at only <InlineMath math="6.0-4.0=2.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, so **a crest passes every <InlineMath math="12/2.0=6.0\text{ s}" />**. Keeping <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ s}" /> ignores that the boat now runs with the waves.

# Shallow water keeps the period

The waves still arrive every <InlineMath math="12/6.0=2.0\text{ s}" />, because **the source sets the period and the shallow water only slows the crests**. At <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> they are <InlineMath math="3.0\times2.0=6.0\text{ m}" /> apart, so the boat meets one every <InlineMath math="6.0/(3.0+4.0)=0.86\text{ s}" />, and a <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ s}" /> interval needs a closing speed of <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, a boat speed of <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. Keeping the <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}" /> spacing gives <InlineMath math="1.7\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> instead.

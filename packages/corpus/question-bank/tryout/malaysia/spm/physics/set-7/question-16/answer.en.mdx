# Use the speed of the crests relative to the boat

The interval between crests reaching the boat is the **crest spacing divided by the relative speed**. Heading out, boat and crests approach each other, so their speeds **add**. Heading to shore, the crests must catch up, so the speeds **subtract**.

The crests close on the boat at <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, of which <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> is their own speed, so the boat moves at <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. Going back, the crests gain on it at only <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, so one passes every <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ s}" /> assumes the interval **does not depend on the direction** of the boat.
- <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> takes the **closing speed as the boat's speed**, forgetting that the crests are moving too.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ s}" /> is the wave's own period, which a **stationary** boat would see.

# Find the heat lost per degree, then use it on the cold night

On average the heater replaces exactly the heat the tank loses, so **<InlineMath math="30\%" /> of <InlineMath math="100\text{ W}" /> is the loss at a <InlineMath math="4" /> °C difference**. That gives the loss per degree. On the cold night the difference is <InlineMath math="10" /> °C, and if the heater stops, that whole loss comes out of the water's stored heat, <InlineMath math="P=mc\,\dfrac{\Delta\theta}{t}" />.

The tank loses <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ W}" /> for every degree above the room. At a <InlineMath math="10" /> °C difference that is <InlineMath math="75\text{ W}" />, so the heater must be on <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of the time. Without it, <InlineMath math="75\text{ W}" /> drawn from <InlineMath math="210\,000\text{ J}" /> per degree cools the water by about <InlineMath math="1.3" /> °C each hour at first.

<BlockMath math="7.5\times(26-12)=105\text{ W}>100\text{ W},\qquad 7.5\,\Delta\theta=100\Rightarrow\Delta\theta=13.3\text{ °C},\qquad 12+13.3\approx25\text{ °C}" />

At <InlineMath math="12" /> °C the tank would lose <InlineMath math="105\text{ W}" /> at <InlineMath math="26" /> °C, more than the heater can give. Running nonstop, the heater balances the loss only when the water is <InlineMath math="13.3" /> °C above the room, about <InlineMath math="25" /> °C.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="45\%" /> adds only the loss from the **extra <InlineMath math="6" /> °C**, forgetting the <InlineMath math="30\text{ W}" /> already lost at <InlineMath math="22" /> °C.
- <InlineMath math="1.7\text{ °C h}^{-1}" /> uses the **heater's full <InlineMath math="100\text{ W}" />** as the rate of loss, not the <InlineMath math="75\text{ W}" /> the tank actually loses.
- <InlineMath math="26" /> °C assumes the thermostat can **always hold its setting**, but at <InlineMath math="12" /> °C the loss would exceed the heater's <InlineMath math="100\text{ W}" />.

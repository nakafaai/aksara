# The cables share the secondary voltage, and the primary pays for everything

The <InlineMath math="12\text{ V}" /> drives current through the cables and the heater **in series**, so the current depends on their total resistance. The heater receives only its own <InlineMath math="I^2R" />. An ideal transformer takes from the mains **exactly the power the secondary delivers**, including the power wasted in the cables.

The secondary current is <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ A}" />. The heater gives about <InlineMath math="46\text{ W}" />, and the cables waste about <InlineMath math="11.5\text{ W}" />. The primary supplies the total <InlineMath math="57.6\text{ W}" />, so it carries <InlineMath math="0.24\text{ A}" />.

<BlockMath math="P_{\max}=I_{\max}^2R_{\text{heater}}=5.5^2\times2.0\approx61\text{ W}" />

Whatever the transformer, the heater and cables share the same current, and the cables limit it to <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ A}" />. The heater can therefore never deliver more than about <InlineMath math="61\text{ W}" /> through these cables.

<BlockMath math="V_s=5.5\times(2.0+0.50)\approx13.8\text{ V},\qquad \frac{N_p}{N_s}=\frac{240}{13.8}\approx17" />

Driving <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ A}" /> through the heater and the cables together needs about <InlineMath math="13.8\text{ V}" /> from the secondary, so the transformer must step <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" /> down about <InlineMath math="17" /> times.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="72\text{ W}" /> and <InlineMath math="20:1" /> **ignore the cables**, putting the full secondary voltage across the heater and never meeting the current limit.
- <InlineMath math="76\text{ W}" /> applies the <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ A}" /> limit to the **heater and cables together**, <InlineMath math="5.5^2\times2.5" />, but only the heater's share heats the greenhouse.
- <InlineMath math="22:1" /> gives the secondary only the **heater's <InlineMath math="11\text{ V}" />**, forgetting that the cables need their own share of the voltage.

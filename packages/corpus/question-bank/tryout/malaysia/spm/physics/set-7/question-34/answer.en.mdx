# The cables share the secondary voltage, and the primary pays for everything

The <InlineMath math="12\text{ V}" /> drives current through the cables and the heater **in series**, so the current depends on their total resistance. The heater receives only its own <InlineMath math="I^2R" />. An ideal transformer takes from the mains **exactly the power the secondary delivers**, including the power wasted in the cables.

The secondary current is <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ A}" />. The heater gives about <InlineMath math="46\text{ W}" />, and the cables waste about <InlineMath math="11.5\text{ W}" />. The primary supplies the total <InlineMath math="57.6\text{ W}" />, so it carries <InlineMath math="0.24\text{ A}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="72\text{ W}" /> with <InlineMath math="0.30\text{ A}" /> **ignores the cables**, putting the full <InlineMath math="12\text{ V}" /> across the heater.
- <InlineMath math="0.19\text{ A}" /> supplies only the **heater's power** from the primary, forgetting the power lost in the cables.
- <InlineMath math="58\text{ W}" /> is the **whole secondary output**, not the share that reaches the heater.

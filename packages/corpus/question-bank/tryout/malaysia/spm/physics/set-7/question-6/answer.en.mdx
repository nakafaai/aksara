# The sandbag starts with the balloon's upward velocity

At the moment it is let go, the sandbag is **moving upwards at <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />**, the same as the balloon. It rises a little, stops, then falls past its starting point to the ground. Take upwards as positive, so the ground is a displacement of <InlineMath math="-40\text{ m}" />, and solve the quadratic for the positive time.

Check with energy: <InlineMath math="v^2=5.0^2+2(10)(40)=825" />, so <InlineMath math="v\approx28.7\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. The sandbag lands after about <InlineMath math="3.4\text{ s}" /> at about <InlineMath math="29\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.8\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="28\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> treat the sandbag as **starting from rest**, ignoring the balloon's upward motion.
- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ s}" /> takes the <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> as **downwards**, which gives the right landing speed but too short a time.
- <InlineMath math="34\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> uses **<InlineMath math="v=gt" />**, as if the sandbag had fallen from rest for the whole <InlineMath math="3.4\text{ s}" />.

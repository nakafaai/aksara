# Take the reaction distance off first, then brake over what is left

During the reaction time the car still moves at full speed, so only the distance **left after reacting** is available for braking. That fixes the deceleration through <InlineMath math="v^2=u^2-2as" />. At the higher speed the reaction distance is **longer**, which leaves even less room to brake, so the car cannot stop.

At <InlineMath math="25\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> only <InlineMath math="31.25\text{ m}" /> remains, and the car is still doing about <InlineMath math="16\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> when it reaches the tree.

With a reaction time of <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ s}" />, the reaction distance and the braking distance together must fit in <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" />. That allows a speed of only about <InlineMath math="17\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, slower than the <InlineMath math="20\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> the alert driver could manage.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> **ignores the reaction distance** and brakes over the full <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" />, and the same slip runs through the other two values.
- <InlineMath math="15\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> keeps the **old reaction distance of <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" />** for the faster car, although it travels farther before braking.
- <InlineMath math="24\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> leaves out the **phone driver's reaction distance**, letting the whole <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" /> be used for braking.

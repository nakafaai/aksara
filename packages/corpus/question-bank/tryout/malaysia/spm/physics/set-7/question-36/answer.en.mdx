# The small base current sets the large collector current

In the base path the supply is shared between the base resistor and the **base-emitter junction**, so the base resistor has <InlineMath math="6.0-0.70" /> volts across it. The collector current is <InlineMath math="150" /> times the base current. That current makes a voltage drop across the collector resistor, and the transistor gets **what is left** of the supply.

A base current of <InlineMath math="20\,\mu\text{A}" /> controls a collector current of <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ mA}" />. The collector resistor takes <InlineMath math="3.6\text{ V}" />, leaving <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ V}" /> between the collector and the emitter.

<BlockMath math="I_B=\frac{4.0\text{ mA}}{150}\approx26.7\,\mu\text{A},\qquad R_B=\frac{6.0-0.70}{26.7\times10^{-6}}\approx2.0\times10^{5}\,\Omega" />

A collector current of <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ mA}" /> needs a base current of about <InlineMath math="26.7\,\mu\text{A}" />. A larger base resistor would give less, so the base resistor may be at most about <InlineMath math="200\text{ k}\Omega" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="23\,\mu\text{A}" /> and <InlineMath math="225\text{ k}\Omega" /> put the **whole <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ V}" />** across the base resistor, ignoring the <InlineMath math="0.70\text{ V}" /> across the junction.
- <InlineMath math="3.6\text{ V}" /> is the voltage across the **collector resistor**, not across the transistor.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ V}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.3\text{ k}\Omega" /> treat the collector current as **equal to the base current**, ignoring the gain of <InlineMath math="150" />.

# If the cooling failed

With no evaporation, the whole <InlineMath math="2.16\times10^{6}\text{ J}" /> would heat <InlineMath math="70\text{ kg}" /> of body, a rise of <InlineMath math="8.8^\circ\text{C}" />. **The rise follows the heat left behind**, so if only half the usual sweat evaporated, half the energy stays in the body and the rise is <InlineMath math="4.4^\circ\text{C}" />. Working with one minute instead of one hour gives <InlineMath math="0.15^\circ\text{C}" />, sixty times too small.

# The price of a cold drink

The cyclist drinks back all <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ kg}" />, and **warming it from <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" /> to <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" /> takes <InlineMath math="1.36\times10^{5}\text{ J}" />** of body heat. At <InlineMath math="600\text{ W}" /> that is <InlineMath math="227\text{ s}" />, close to <InlineMath math="3.8\text{ min}" /> of the excess. Replacing only the evaporated <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ kg}" /> gives <InlineMath math="2.8\text{ min}" />, but the drips leave the body too.

# The work done by the field becomes kinetic energy

Each electron gains energy <InlineMath math="eV" /> from the field, and all of it becomes **kinetic energy**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac12mv^2" />. So <InlineMath math="v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2eV}{m}}" />, and the speed grows with the **square root** of the voltage. To double the speed, the kinetic energy must be four times as large, so the voltage must be **four times** as large.

<BlockMath math="\tfrac12mv^2=eV\Rightarrow v=\sqrt{\frac{2(1.6\times10^{-19})(500)}{9.1\times10^{-31}}}=\sqrt{1.76\times10^{14}}\approx1.3\times10^{7}\text{ m s}^{-1}" />

<BlockMath math="v\propto\sqrt V\Rightarrow V'=2^2\times500=2000\text{ V}" />

The electrons reach about <InlineMath math="1.3\times10^{7}\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, about <InlineMath math="4\%" /> of the speed of light. Arriving twice as fast needs four times the energy, <InlineMath math="2000\text{ V}" />.

<BlockMath math="t_1=\frac{0.050}{\tfrac12(1.33\times10^{7})}\approx7.5\text{ ns},\qquad t_2=\frac{0.20}{1.33\times10^{7}}\approx15\text{ ns},\qquad t_1+t_2\approx23\text{ ns}" />

While it accelerates, the electron's average speed is half its final speed, so the <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ cm}" /> take <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ ns}" />. Beyond the anode it coasts at the full <InlineMath math="1.3\times10^{7}\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, covering <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> in <InlineMath math="15\text{ ns}" />, so it reaches the screen about <InlineMath math="23\text{ ns}" /> after leaving the cathode.

<BlockMath math="v\propto\sqrt V\Rightarrow v'=2v,\qquad t'=\frac{23\text{ ns}}{2}\approx11\text{ ns}" />

Four times the voltage gives four times the energy but only twice the speed, so every stretch of the journey, and the whole trip, takes half as long: about <InlineMath math="11\text{ ns}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.8\text{ ns}" /> and <InlineMath math="19\text{ ns}" /> divide the accelerating gap by the **final speed**, as if the electron moved that fast from the start.
- <InlineMath math="5.6\text{ ns}" /> makes the **speed proportional to the voltage**, quartering the time, but four times the voltage only doubles the speed.
- <InlineMath math="11\text{ ns}" /> and <InlineMath math="32\text{ ns}" /> drop the **<InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />** from the kinetic energy, so every speed is too low and every time too long.

# From the screen to the cups

A merry-go-round that turns ten times a second carries its riders a long way every second. **One wave covers <InlineMath math="5.0" /> divisions of <InlineMath math="20\text{ ms}" />, so one turn takes <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ s}" /> and the cups turn <InlineMath math="10" /> times a second**. Each turn carries a cup centre round <InlineMath math="2\pi\times0.070=0.44\text{ m}" />, so the cups move at <InlineMath math="0.44\times10=4.4\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

# The wind is faster than the cups

A leaf floating down a stream never quite keeps up with the water that carries it. The cups and the wind are the same. **The cups move at one third of the wind speed, so the wind blows at <InlineMath math="3\times4.4=13\text{ m s}^{-1}" />**. Stopping at <InlineMath math="4.4\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> gives the cups' speed, not the wind's.

# A faster wind makes a taller wave

Pedal a bicycle faster and its dynamo lamp glows brighter. **Twice the wind turns the magnet twice as fast, so the peaks double from <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ V}" /> to <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ V}" />**. Only <InlineMath math="4" /> divisions lie above the zero line, so the Y-gain must be at least <InlineMath math="6.0/4=1.5\text{ V}" /> per division, which means the <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ V}" /> setting. Treating all <InlineMath math="8" /> divisions as room for the peak lets <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ V}" /> through, but then the peaks sit <InlineMath math="6" /> divisions up, off the top of the screen. Sticking with the old <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ V}" /> forgets that a faster magnet makes a bigger voltage.

# Find the wavelength, then the spacing, then take half of it

The wavelength comes from <InlineMath math="v=f\lambda" />. The formula gives <InlineMath math="x" />, the spacing **between neighbouring quiet points**. The centre line is loud, so the first quiet point lies **halfway** to the next loud line, at <InlineMath math="\tfrac12x" />. A higher frequency means a shorter wavelength, so everything moves closer to the centre.

<BlockMath math="\lambda=\frac{330}{660}=0.50\text{ m},\qquad x=\frac{\lambda D}{a}=\frac{0.50\times8.0}{2.0}=2.0\text{ m},\qquad \tfrac12x=1.0\text{ m}" />

<BlockMath math="\lambda'=\frac{330}{990}=0.33\text{ m},\qquad x'=\frac{0.33\times8.0}{2.0}\approx1.3\text{ m},\qquad \tfrac12x'\approx0.67\text{ m}" />

At <InlineMath math="660\text{ Hz}" /> the quiet points are <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m}" /> apart, so the first is <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ m}" /> from the centre line. At <InlineMath math="990\text{ Hz}" /> the spacing falls to about <InlineMath math="1.3\text{ m}" /> and the first quiet point is about <InlineMath math="0.67\text{ m}" /> from the centre.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m}" /> is the **spacing between quiet points**, but the centre line is loud, so the first quiet point is only half that distance away.
- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> makes the **spacing grow with frequency**, but a higher frequency gives a shorter wavelength and a smaller spacing.
- <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ m}" /> takes the **wavelength itself** as the distance on the line, without scaling it by <InlineMath math="D/a" />.

# Get the wavelength from the pattern, scale the pattern with distance, then shift it by half

The measured spacing gives the **wavelength**, and <InlineMath math="v=f\lambda" /> gives the frequency. On a line farther away the whole pattern spreads in proportion to <InlineMath math="D" />. Driving the loudspeakers in antiphase **swaps loud and quiet**: the centre line becomes quiet, so the nearest loud point is **half a spacing** from it.

<BlockMath math="\lambda=\frac{ax}{D}=\frac{2.0\times1.5}{8.0}=0.375\text{ m},\qquad f=\frac{330}{0.375}=880\text{ Hz}" />

<BlockMath math="x'=\frac{\lambda D'}{a}=\frac{0.375\times12}{2.0}=2.25\text{ m},\qquad \tfrac12x'\approx1.1\text{ m}" />

The signal is <InlineMath math="880\text{ Hz}" />. At <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}" /> the quiet points are about <InlineMath math="2.3\text{ m}" /> apart, and after the swap the nearest loud point lies about <InlineMath math="1.1\text{ m}" /> from the now-quiet centre line.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> keeps the **spacing the same at every distance**, but it grows in proportion to <InlineMath math="D" />.
- <InlineMath math="2.3\text{ m}" /> for the loud point puts it **a whole spacing** from the centre, but swapping the phase moves the pattern by only half a spacing.
- <InlineMath math="55\text{ Hz}" /> turns the formula **upside down**, <InlineMath math="\lambda=\dfrac{xD}{a}" />, giving a wavelength of <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m}" />.

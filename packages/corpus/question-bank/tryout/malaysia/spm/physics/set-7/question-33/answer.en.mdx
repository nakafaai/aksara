# The first pass

Roll a ball through a patch of sand and it comes out slower, because the sand keeps some of its movement. The coil does the same. **The magnet arrives with <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\times0.040\times2.0^2=0.080\text{ J}" />, and the coil keeps <InlineMath math="30\%" /> of it, <InlineMath math="0.024\text{ J}" />**. The magnet leaves the coil with the other <InlineMath math="0.056\text{ J}" />.

# The way back starts with less

Cut a slice of cake, then the same share of what is left, and the second slice is smaller. **The rubber stop returns all <InlineMath math="0.056\text{ J}" />, so the way back stores <InlineMath math="0.30\times0.056=0.0168\text{ J}" />, and one shake stores <InlineMath math="0.024+0.0168=0.0408\text{ J}" />**. Counting <InlineMath math="0.024\text{ J}" /> twice gives <InlineMath math="0.048\text{ J}" />, as if the coil had taken nothing on the way out. Counting one pass only gives <InlineMath math="0.024\text{ J}" /> and <InlineMath math="150" /> shakes.

# Round the shakes up

If a recipe needs a little more than <InlineMath math="88" /> cups of flour, <InlineMath math="88" /> cups are not enough. **The LED uses <InlineMath math="3.0\times0.020=0.060\text{ W}" />, so one minute needs <InlineMath math="0.060\times60=3.6\text{ J}" />, which is <InlineMath math="3.6/0.0408=88.2" /> shakes, so <InlineMath math="89" /> shakes are needed**. Stopping at <InlineMath math="88" /> leaves the LED dark before the minute is up.

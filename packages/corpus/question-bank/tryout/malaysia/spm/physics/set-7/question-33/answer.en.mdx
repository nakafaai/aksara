# The whole length of wire in the field feels the force

In a radial field every part of every turn is **perpendicular to the field**, and the forces on all parts point the same way along the axis. So the force is <InlineMath math="F=BIL" /> with <InlineMath math="L" /> the **total length of wire**: <InlineMath math="50" /> circumferences. Newton's second law then gives the cone's acceleration, with the mass in kilograms.

The coil holds <InlineMath math="3.14\text{ m}" /> of wire, so it feels about <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ N}" />. On a <InlineMath math="10\text{ g}" /> cone that gives an acceleration of about <InlineMath math="50\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, five times <InlineMath math="g" />.

The amplifier drives <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ A}" /> through the coil, the current used above. Starting from rest at <InlineMath math="50\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, the cone covers <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm}" /> in about <InlineMath math="8.9\text{ ms}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ N}" /> uses the length of **one turn only**, forgetting the other <InlineMath math="49" />, and every later value follows from that slip.
- <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" /> takes the **<InlineMath math="1.6\text{ V}" /> as the current**, forgetting to divide by the coil's <InlineMath math="8.0\,\Omega" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.080\text{ ms}" /> stops at <InlineMath math="\dfrac{2s}{a}" /> and **forgets the square root**, which is in units of seconds squared, not seconds.

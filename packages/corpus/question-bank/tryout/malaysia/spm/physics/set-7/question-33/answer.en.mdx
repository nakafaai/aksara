# The whole length of wire in the field feels the force

In a radial field every part of every turn is **perpendicular to the field**, and the forces on all parts point the same way along the axis. So the force is <InlineMath math="F=BIL" /> with <InlineMath math="L" /> the **total length of wire**: <InlineMath math="50" /> circumferences. Newton's second law then gives the cone's acceleration, with the mass in kilograms.

The coil holds <InlineMath math="3.14\text{ m}" /> of wire, so it feels about <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ N}" />. On a <InlineMath math="10\text{ g}" /> cone that gives an acceleration of about <InlineMath math="50\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, five times <InlineMath math="g" />.

<BlockMath math="F_{\max}=200\times0.0020=0.40\text{ N},\qquad I_{\max}=\frac{0.40}{0.80\times3.14}\approx0.16\text{ A},\qquad V_{\max}=0.16\times8.0\approx1.3\text{ V}" />

At <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ V}" /> the coil carries <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ A}" /> and pushes the cone <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ mm}" />, past its <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm}" /> limit. The limit allows only <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ N}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="0.16\text{ A}" />, so the amplifier may apply at most about <InlineMath math="1.3\text{ V}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ V}" /> never **checks the displacement** against the <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm}" /> limit, but at <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ V}" /> the cone moves <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ mm}" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.25\text{ mm}" /> divides <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ N}" /> by <InlineMath math="200\text{ N m}^{-1}" /> but reads the result in **centimetres instead of metres**, so the limit seems far away.
- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ N}" /> uses the **diameter instead of the radius** in the circumference, doubling the force and halving the safe voltage.

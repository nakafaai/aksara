# Split the flight into the powered climb, the coast to the top and the fall

The rocket does not stop when the fuel runs out: it **keeps rising at <InlineMath math="60\text{ m s}^{-1}" />** and slows only under gravity. So the flight has three stages. Find the height and speed at burnout, then how much higher it coasts, then how long it takes to fall the whole height from rest.

<BlockMath math="H=90+180=270\text{ m},\qquad 270=5t^2\Rightarrow t=7.35\text{ s},\qquad T=3.0+6.0+7.35\approx16\text{ s}" />

The rocket is <InlineMath math="90\text{ m}" /> up when the engine stops and coasts another <InlineMath math="180\text{ m}" />, reaching <InlineMath math="270\text{ m}" /> after <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ s}" />. Falling <InlineMath math="270\text{ m}" /> from rest takes <InlineMath math="7.35\text{ s}" />, so it lands about <InlineMath math="16\text{ s}" /> after launch.

<BlockMath math="v=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{2(10)(270)}\approx73\text{ m s}^{-1}" />

From the top it falls freely through the full <InlineMath math="270\text{ m}" />, so it hits the ground at about <InlineMath math="73\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, faster than the <InlineMath math="60\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> it had when the fuel ran out.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="180\text{ m}" /> counts only the **coasting climb** after burnout and leaves out the <InlineMath math="90\text{ m}" /> gained while the engine burned, and the slip carries into the time and the impact speed.
- <InlineMath math="15\text{ s}" /> assumes the rocket **falls for as long as it coasted up**, but it falls from <InlineMath math="270\text{ m}" />, which takes <InlineMath math="7.3\text{ s}" />, not <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ s}" />.
- <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ s}" /> is the time to **reach the top**, not to come back down.

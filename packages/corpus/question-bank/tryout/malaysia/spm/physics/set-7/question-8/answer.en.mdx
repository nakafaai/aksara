# Balance the inverse-square pulls, then scale g by the square of the distance

Each pull is proportional to **mass divided by distance squared**. For the pulls to be equal, the distances must be in the ratio of the **square roots** of the masses, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{81}:1=9:1" />. Then the Earth's own pull at that distance follows from its surface value, scaled by <InlineMath math="(R/r)^2" />.

The point is <InlineMath math="0.9" /> of the way to the Moon, <InlineMath math="3.5\times10^{8}\text{ m}" /> from the Earth's centre. That is <InlineMath math="54" /> Earth radii out, where the Earth's pull is only <InlineMath math="1/2900" /> of its surface value.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.8\times10^{8}\text{ m}" /> splits the distance in the ratio **<InlineMath math="81:1" /> itself**, as if the pull fell with distance, not distance squared.
- <InlineMath math="1.9\times10^{8}\text{ m}" /> is the **midpoint**, where the Earth's pull is <InlineMath math="81" /> times the Moon's.
- <InlineMath math="0.18\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> scales <InlineMath math="g" /> by **<InlineMath math="R/r" /> once**, not by its square.

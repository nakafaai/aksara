# The balance point needs a square root

Equal pulls mean <InlineMath math="M_E/x^2=M_M/(D-x)^2" />, so <InlineMath math="x/(D-x)=\sqrt{81}=9" /> and the point is nine tenths of the way to the Moon. **The distances go as the square root of the masses**, not as the masses. Using <InlineMath math="81" /> itself would put the point at <InlineMath math="3.79\times10^{8}\text{ m}" />, almost on the Moon.

# The Earth's strength from its own surface

No mass is given, but <InlineMath math="GM_E=gR^2" /> follows from the surface data, <InlineMath math="4.01\times10^{14}\text{ N m}^2\text{ kg}^{-1}" />. **The Moon's <InlineMath math="GM" /> is that divided by <InlineMath math="81" />**, <InlineMath math="4.96\times10^{12}\text{ N m}^2\text{ kg}^{-1}" />.

# Past the balance point the Moon wins

At <InlineMath math="3.6\times10^{8}\text{ m}" /> from the Earth the craft is only <InlineMath math="2.4\times10^{7}\text{ m}" /> from the Moon. The Earth pulls with <InlineMath math="3.10\times10^{-3}\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, the Moon with <InlineMath math="8.60\times10^{-3}\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, and **the two pulls point in opposite directions**, so they subtract.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{D-x}&=\sqrt{81}=9\ \Rightarrow\ x=0.9\times3.84\times10^{8}=3.46\times10^{8}\text{ m},\qquad GM_E=gR^2=4.01\times10^{14}\text{ N m}^2\text{ kg}^{-1}\\
g_E&=\frac{4.01\times10^{14}}{(3.6\times10^{8})^2}=3.10\times10^{-3},\qquad g_M=\frac{4.96\times10^{12}}{(2.4\times10^{7})^2}=8.60\times10^{-3}\\
g_M-g_E&=5.5\times10^{-3}\text{ m s}^{-2},\qquad g_{M,0}=\frac{4.96\times10^{12}}{(1.74\times10^{6})^2}=1.6\text{ m s}^{-2}
\end{aligned}" />

The result, <InlineMath math="5.5\times10^{-3}\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, points to the Moon. Adding the pulls gives <InlineMath math="1.2\times10^{-2}\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, and sending the result towards the heavier Earth ignores where the craft sits.

# The Moon's own surface

On the Moon's surface the distance to its centre is just <InlineMath math="1.74\times10^{6}\text{ m}" />, **far smaller than the Earth's radius**, so the Moon's weak <InlineMath math="GM" /> still gives <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. Dividing <InlineMath math="9.8" /> by <InlineMath math="81" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.12\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> and quietly keeps the Earth's radius.

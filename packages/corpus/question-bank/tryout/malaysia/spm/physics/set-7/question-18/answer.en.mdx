# One product survives every flat boundary

Snell's law at the water surface and again at the glass gives <InlineMath math="1.00\sin50^\circ=1.33\sin r_1=1.50\sin r_2" />. **Because the boundaries are parallel, <InlineMath math="n\sin\theta" /> carries straight through**, and the water drops out of the glass angle: <InlineMath math="\sin r_2=0.766/1.50" />, so <InlineMath math="r_2=30.7^\circ\approx31^\circ" />.

# Adding up the two slanted runs

The ray drifts sideways in each layer by the thickness times the tangent of its angle there. **The water uses <InlineMath math="35.2^\circ" /> and the glass <InlineMath math="30.7^\circ" />**, so the runs are <InlineMath math="7.05\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.56\text{ cm}" />, together about <InlineMath math="11\text{ cm}" />. Carrying the air angle of <InlineMath math="50^\circ" /> into the water makes <InlineMath math="16\text{ cm}" />, and using the glass angle for all <InlineMath math="16\text{ cm}" /> of depth gives <InlineMath math="9.5\text{ cm}" />.

\sin r_1&=\frac{\sin50^\circ}{1.33}=0.576\ \Rightarrow\ r_1=35.2^\circ,\qquad \sin r_2=\frac{\sin50^\circ}{1.50}=0.511\ \Rightarrow\ r_2=30.7^\circ\\
s&=10\tan35.2^\circ+6.0\tan30.7^\circ=7.05+3.56=10.6\text{ cm}\approx11\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

# Glass meets water here

Leaving glass for water, <InlineMath math="\sin c=1.33/1.50=0.887" />, so <InlineMath math="c=62^\circ" />. **The critical angle depends on what lies beyond the glass**. The familiar <InlineMath math="42^\circ" /> belongs to glass meeting air, which is not what sits on top of this block.

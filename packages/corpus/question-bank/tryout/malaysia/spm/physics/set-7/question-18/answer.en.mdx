# Parallel layers pass <InlineMath math="n\sin\theta" /> on unchanged

At each boundary <InlineMath math="n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2" />. With parallel surfaces, the angle leaving the water is the angle entering the glass, so <InlineMath math="n\sin\theta" /> is **the same in every layer** and the water drops out: the glass angle depends only on air and glass. The critical angle at the glass-water boundary uses **both** indices, because the ray would be leaving glass into water, not into air.

<BlockMath math="1.00\sin50°=1.33\sin\theta_w=1.50\sin\theta_g\Rightarrow \sin\theta_g=\frac{0.766}{1.50}=0.511,\qquad \theta_g\approx31°" />

<BlockMath math="\sin c=\frac{1.33}{1.50}=0.887\Rightarrow c\approx62°" />

In the water the ray is at about <InlineMath math="35°" />, and in the glass at about <InlineMath math="31°" />, exactly as if the water were absent. The critical angle from glass into water is about <InlineMath math="62°" />.

<BlockMath math="d=10\tan35°+6.0\tan31°=10(0.705)+6.0(0.594)\approx7.0+3.6\approx11\text{ cm}" />

The ray crosses the water at <InlineMath math="35°" /> and the glass at <InlineMath math="31°" />, so each layer shifts it sideways by its own thickness times the tangent of its own angle. Together that is about <InlineMath math="11\text{ cm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="16\text{ cm}" /> lets the ray cross the water **at <InlineMath math="50°" />**, as if it did not bend at the water surface.
- <InlineMath math="9.5\text{ cm}" /> uses the **glass angle in both layers**, but in the water the ray is at <InlineMath math="35°" />.
- <InlineMath math="42°" /> is the critical angle from **glass into air**, <InlineMath math="\sin c=1/1.50" />, but the glass here meets water.

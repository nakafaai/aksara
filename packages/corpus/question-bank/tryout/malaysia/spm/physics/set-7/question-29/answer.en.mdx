# What each switch position connects

People pass two gates one after the other more slowly than two gates side by side. Current does the same. **Series adds the resistances, and parallel gives less than either coil alone**. LOW has <InlineMath math="60+40=100\,\Omega" /> and <InlineMath math="P=240^2/100=576\text{ W}" />. MEDIUM uses the <InlineMath math="40\,\Omega" /> coil alone for <InlineMath math="1.44\text{ kW}" />, and HIGH has <InlineMath math="24\,\Omega" /> for <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ kW}" />.

# A burnt coil is a fallen bridge

Nobody crosses a bridge that has fallen down. A burnt-through coil is the same for current. **Any position whose only path runs through the <InlineMath math="40\,\Omega" /> coil goes dead**. LOW has both coils in one line and MEDIUM has only the burnt one, so both give nothing. HIGH still has its <InlineMath math="60\,\Omega" /> branch, so the most the heater can give is <InlineMath math="240^2/60=960\text{ W}" />. The <InlineMath math="1.44\text{ kW}" /> of MEDIUM belonged to the coil that is gone.

# Choosing the new coil for the fuse

A fuse is a guard that lets through at most <InlineMath math="13\text{ A}" />. **On HIGH the whole heater must have at least <InlineMath math="240/13=18.5\,\Omega" />, and that means the two coils side by side**. So <InlineMath math="\tfrac1R\le\tfrac1{18.5}-\tfrac1{60}" /> and <InlineMath math="R\ge26.7\,\Omega" />. Choosing <InlineMath math="18.5\,\Omega" /> treats the new coil as if it carried all <InlineMath math="13\text{ A}" /> alone, and the <InlineMath math="4\text{ A}" /> through the <InlineMath math="60\,\Omega" /> coil would then blow the fuse.

# LOW comes back warmer

Swap one slow gate in a row for a quicker one and the whole queue moves faster. **LOW now has <InlineMath math="60+26.7=86.7\,\Omega" />, less than before, so more current flows and <InlineMath math="P=240^2/86.7=665\text{ W}" />**, up from <InlineMath math="576\text{ W}" />. Keeping the old <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ A}" /> in <InlineMath math="I^2R" /> gives only <InlineMath math="499\text{ W}" />, but the current changes when the resistance does.

P_L&=\frac{240^2}{60+40}=576\text{ W},\qquad P_{\max}=\frac{240^2}{60}=960\text{ W}\\
\frac1R&\le\frac{13}{240}-\frac1{60}=0.0375\ \Rightarrow\ R\ge26.7\,\Omega\\
P_L'&=\frac{240^2}{60+26.7}=665\text{ W}
\end{aligned}" />

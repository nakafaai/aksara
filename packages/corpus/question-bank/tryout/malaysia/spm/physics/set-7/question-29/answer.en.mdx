# Work out each position, then ask which ones still have a complete path

At a fixed <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" />, power is <InlineMath math="P=\dfrac{V^2}{R}" />. Series adds the resistances, and parallel gives less than either coil. After the fault, current can flow only through paths that **avoid the broken coil**: the series path runs through it, MEDIUM is the broken coil itself, and only the parallel position still has a path, through the <InlineMath math="60\,\Omega" /> coil.

Before the fault the heater gave <InlineMath math="576\text{ W}" /> on LOW and <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ kW}" /> on HIGH. After it, LOW and MEDIUM are dead, and HIGH gives only <InlineMath math="960\text{ W}" /> through the <InlineMath math="60\,\Omega" /> coil, the most the heater can now give.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="576\text{ W}" /> after the fault assumes the **series path still works**, but it runs through the broken coil, so no current flows.
- <InlineMath math="1.4\text{ kW}" /> uses the **MEDIUM position**, but that position is the broken <InlineMath math="40\,\Omega" /> coil itself.
- <InlineMath math="960\text{ W}" /> on LOW takes the **larger coil alone** as the lowest setting, but the series pair has more resistance and gives less power.

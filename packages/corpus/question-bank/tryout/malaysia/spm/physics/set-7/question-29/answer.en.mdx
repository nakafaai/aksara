# At a fixed voltage, less resistance means more power

With the voltage fixed at <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" />, power is <InlineMath math="P=\dfrac{V^2}{R}" />, so the setting with the **most resistance gives the least power**. Series adds the resistances, and parallel gives a resistance **smaller than either coil**. The current on the highest setting is then <InlineMath math="I=\dfrac VR" />.

<BlockMath math="R_{\text{series}}=60+40=100\,\Omega,\qquad P_{\min}=\frac{240^2}{100}=576\text{ W}" />

<BlockMath math="R_{\text{parallel}}=\frac{60\times40}{60+40}=24\,\Omega,\qquad I_{\max}=\frac{240}{24}=10\text{ A}" />

The lowest setting is the coils in series, <InlineMath math="576\text{ W}" />. The highest is the coils in parallel, <InlineMath math="2400\text{ W}" />, which draws <InlineMath math="10\text{ A}" />.

<BlockMath math="I_3\le13-10=3.0\text{ A},\qquad R_3\ge\frac{240}{3.0}=80\,\Omega,\qquad P'=2400+\frac{240^2}{80}=2400+720\approx3.1\text{ kW}" />

The highest setting already draws <InlineMath math="10\text{ A}" />, so the third coil may add at most <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ A}" />, which needs at least <InlineMath math="80\,\Omega" />. That coil adds <InlineMath math="720\text{ W}" />, raising the highest setting to about <InlineMath math="3.1\text{ kW}" />, exactly the <InlineMath math="13\text{ A}" /> limit at <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="960\text{ W}" /> takes the **larger coil alone** as the lowest setting, but the series pair has even more resistance.
- <InlineMath math="23\,\Omega" /> and <InlineMath math="4.9\text{ kW}" /> start from the current on the **lowest setting**, <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ A}" />, not the highest.
- <InlineMath math="18\,\Omega" /> and <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ kW}" /> give the third coil the **whole <InlineMath math="13\text{ A}" />**, forgetting the <InlineMath math="10\text{ A}" /> the other two coils already draw.

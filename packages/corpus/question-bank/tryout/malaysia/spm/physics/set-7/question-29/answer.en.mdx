# Find which positions still have a complete path, then let the fuse limit the replacement

At a fixed <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" />, power is <InlineMath math="P=\dfrac{V^2}{R}" />. After the fault, current can flow only through paths that **avoid the broken coil**: the series path runs through it, MEDIUM is the broken coil itself, and only HIGH still works, through the <InlineMath math="60\,\Omega" /> coil. A replacement coil sits in parallel with the <InlineMath math="60\,\Omega" /> coil on HIGH, so the two currents add, and together they must stay within <InlineMath math="13\text{ A}" />.

<BlockMath math="I_{60}=\frac{240}{60}=4.0\text{ A},\qquad I_R\le13-4.0=9.0\text{ A}\Rightarrow R\ge\frac{240}{9.0}\approx27\,\Omega" />

Before the fault LOW gave <InlineMath math="576\text{ W}" />. After it only HIGH works, giving <InlineMath math="960\text{ W}" />. The <InlineMath math="60\,\Omega" /> coil already takes <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ A}" /> on HIGH, so a replacement may take at most <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ A}" />, which means at least about <InlineMath math="27\,\Omega" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="576\text{ W}" /> after the fault assumes the **series path still works**, but it runs through the broken coil, so no current flows.
- <InlineMath math="18\,\Omega" /> gives the replacement the **whole <InlineMath math="13\text{ A}" />**, forgetting the <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ A}" /> the <InlineMath math="60\,\Omega" /> coil draws beside it on HIGH.
- <InlineMath math="1.4\text{ kW}" /> uses the **MEDIUM position**, but that position is the broken <InlineMath math="40\,\Omega" /> coil itself.

# Include the internal resistance in the total, then follow the current through

The current depends on the **whole circuit**, including the battery's own <InlineMath math="1.0\,\Omega" />. When the <InlineMath math="3.0\,\Omega" /> branch fails, the parallel pair becomes the <InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" /> resistor alone, so the total resistance rises and the current falls. A smaller current loses less voltage inside the battery, so the **terminal voltage rises**, and the <InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" /> resistor, now carrying the whole current, heats up more.

The battery now delivers about <InlineMath math="1.1\text{ A}" />. At <InlineMath math="7.1\text{ W}" /> the <InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" /> resistor is overloaded. Keeping it at <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ W}" /> limits the current to about <InlineMath math="0.91\text{ A}" />, so the whole circuit, battery included, needs at least <InlineMath math="13.1\,\Omega" />: about <InlineMath math="2.1\,\Omega" /> more than the <InlineMath math="11\,\Omega" /> already there.

With the current cut to about <InlineMath math="0.91\text{ A}" />, only about <InlineMath math="0.9\text{ V}" /> is lost inside the battery, so its terminals give about <InlineMath math="11.1\text{ V}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ A}" />, <InlineMath math="8.6\text{ W}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.1\,\Omega" /> **ignore the internal resistance** throughout, as if the battery's terminals always gave the full emf.
- <InlineMath math="1.7\text{ A}" />, <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ W}" /> and <InlineMath math="0\,\Omega" /> describe the circuit **before the failure**, when the resistor was well within its rating.
- <InlineMath math="3.1\,\Omega" /> with the right current and power **forgets the battery's own <InlineMath math="1.0\,\Omega" />** when working out how much resistance is already in the circuit.

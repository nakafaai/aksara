# A failed branch raises the total resistance and shifts the voltage

With both branches working, the parallel pair is <InlineMath math="2.0\,\Omega" />. When the <InlineMath math="3.0\,\Omega" /> resistor fails, the pair becomes just the <InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" /> resistor, so the **total resistance rises** and the current falls. But the <InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" /> resistor now takes a **larger share of the <InlineMath math="12\text{ V}" />**, so its power goes up, not down.

<BlockMath math="\text{After: } R'=4.0+6.0=10\,\Omega,\quad I'=\frac{12}{10}=1.2\text{ A},\quad P_6'=1.2^2\times6.0\approx8.6\text{ W}" />

The supply current drops from <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ A}" /> to <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ A}" />. The voltage across the <InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" /> resistor rises from <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ V}" /> to <InlineMath math="7.2\text{ V}" />, so its power more than triples, to about <InlineMath math="8.6\text{ W}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ A}" /> with <InlineMath math="2.7\text{ W}" /> is the circuit **before the failure**.
- <InlineMath math="24\text{ W}" /> puts the **full <InlineMath math="12\text{ V}" />** across the <InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" /> resistor, forgetting the <InlineMath math="4.0\,\Omega" /> resistor in series.
- <InlineMath math="2.7\text{ W}" /> with <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ A}" /> assumes the **<InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" /> resistor is unaffected** by the failure of the branch beside it.

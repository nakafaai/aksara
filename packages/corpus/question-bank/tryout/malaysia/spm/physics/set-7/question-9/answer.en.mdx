# Let gravity supply the centripetal force, then divide the mass by the volume

For a low orbit, the planet's pull provides the **centripetal force** at radius <InlineMath math="R" />. That gives the mass in terms of <InlineMath math="R" /> and <InlineMath math="T" />, and dividing by the volume <InlineMath math="\tfrac43\pi R^3" /> makes <InlineMath math="R" /> cancel: the density depends **only on the period**. Kepler's third law then gives the period at twice the radius, <InlineMath math="T\propto r^{3/2}" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{GM}{R^2}=\frac{4\pi^2R}{T^2}\Rightarrow M=\frac{4\pi^2R^3}{GT^2},\qquad \rho=\frac{M}{\tfrac43\pi R^3}=\frac{3\pi}{GT^2}=\frac{3(3.14)}{6.67\times10^{-11}\times5400^2}\approx4.8\times10^{3}\text{ kg m}^{-3}" />

<BlockMath math="T_{2R}=1.5\times2^{1.5}=1.5\times2.83\approx4.2\text{ h}" />

With <InlineMath math="T=5400\text{ s}" />, the planet is about <InlineMath math="4.8" /> times as dense as water. Doubling the orbit's radius multiplies the period by <InlineMath math="2\sqrt2" />, giving about <InlineMath math="4.2\text{ h}" />.

<BlockMath math="v_{2R}=\frac{2\pi(2R)}{T_{2R}}=v\times\frac{2}{2^{1.5}}=\frac{3.0}{\sqrt2}\approx2.1\text{ km s}^{-1}" />

The wider orbit is twice as long but takes <InlineMath math="2.83" /> times as long to go round, so the satellite there moves more slowly, at about <InlineMath math="2.1\text{ km s}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ h}" /> makes the **period proportional to the radius**, which would leave the speed unchanged, but Kepler's third law gives <InlineMath math="T^2\propto r^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ h}" /> makes the **period proportional to <InlineMath math="r^3" />**, forgetting the square root, and so makes the outer satellite far too slow.
- <InlineMath math="1.2\times10^{3}\text{ kg m}^{-3}" /> drops the **factor of <InlineMath math="4" />** in the volume of a sphere, using <InlineMath math="\pi R^3" /> instead of <InlineMath math="\tfrac43\pi R^3" /> together with <InlineMath math="4\pi^2" />.

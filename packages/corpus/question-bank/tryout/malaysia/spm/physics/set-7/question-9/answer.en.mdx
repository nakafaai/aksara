# The orbit measures the planet

The probe skims the surface, so **its orbit radius is the planet's radius**: one lap of <InlineMath math="2\pi R" /> at <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ km s}^{-1}" /> takes <InlineMath math="5400\text{ s}" />, which gives <InlineMath math="R=2.58\times10^{6}\text{ m}" />.

# Density straight from the period

Gravity supplies the centripetal force, <InlineMath math="GM/R^2=4\pi^2R/T^2" />, and <InlineMath math="M=\tfrac43\pi R^3\rho" />. Put them together and **<InlineMath math="R" /> cancels**, so a grazing orbit's period alone fixes the density.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
R&=\frac{vT}{2\pi}=\frac{3000\times5400}{2\times3.14}=2.58\times10^{6}\text{ m},\qquad \rho=\frac{3\pi}{GT^2}=4.8\times10^{3}\text{ kg m}^{-3}\\
M&=\frac{v^2R}{G}=3.5\times10^{23}\text{ kg},\qquad T'=1.5\times2^{1.5}=4.2\text{ h},\qquad v'=\frac{3.0}{\sqrt2}=2.1\text{ km s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

With <InlineMath math="R" /> known, the mass comes out as <InlineMath math="v^2R/G=3.5\times10^{23}\text{ kg}" />. If you take the route through <InlineMath math="GM=4\pi^2R^3/T^2" /> and lose the <InlineMath math="4" />, both answers shrink to a quarter, <InlineMath math="1.2\times10^{3}\text{ kg m}^{-3}" /> and <InlineMath math="8.7\times10^{22}\text{ kg}" />.

# Twice as far out

Kepler's third law, <InlineMath math="T^2\propto r^3" />, makes the period grow by <InlineMath math="2^{1.5}=2.83" />, to <InlineMath math="4.2\text{ h}" />, and the speed drop by <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" />, to <InlineMath math="2.1\text{ km s}^{-1}" />. **Doubling the radius does not double the period**. Keeping the speed at <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ km s}^{-1}" /> round a path twice as long gives <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ h}" />, and <InlineMath math="12\text{ h}" /> with <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ km s}^{-1}" /> comes from <InlineMath math="T\propto r^3" /> and <InlineMath math="v\propto r^{-2}" />, the powers without their square roots.

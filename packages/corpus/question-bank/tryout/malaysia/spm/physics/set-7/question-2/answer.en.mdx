# Read the power from the ratio for a doubling, then scale by that power

In a power law, multiplying <InlineMath math="d" /> by a factor multiplies <InlineMath math="t" /> by **that factor raised to the power <InlineMath math="n" />**. Doubling the diameter from <InlineMath math="2.0" /> to <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ mm}" /> divides the time by <InlineMath math="320\div56.6=5.66" />, and so does the next doubling. Since <InlineMath math="2^{2.5}=5.66" />, the power is <InlineMath math="n=-2.5" />. Then scale from a known point, never by a straight-line guess between points.

<BlockMath math="2^{n}=\frac{56.6}{320}=0.177\ \Rightarrow\ n=-2.5,\qquad t_{5.0}=320\times\left(\frac{5.0}{2.0}\right)^{-2.5}=\frac{320}{2.5^{2.5}}=\frac{320}{9.88}\approx32\text{ s}" />

The hole is <InlineMath math="2.5" /> times as wide as the <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm}" /> hole, so the time is divided by <InlineMath math="2.5^{2.5}\approx9.9" />, about <InlineMath math="32\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="n=-2" /> treats the time as **set by the area of the hole alone**, which the factor of <InlineMath math="5.66" /> per doubling rules out.
- <InlineMath math="45\text{ s}" /> reads <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ mm}" /> off a **straight line drawn between the <InlineMath math="4.0" /> and <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ mm}" /> points**, but the curve is not straight.
- <InlineMath math="n=-3" /> would divide the time by **<InlineMath math="8" /> per doubling**, not by the measured <InlineMath math="5.66" />.

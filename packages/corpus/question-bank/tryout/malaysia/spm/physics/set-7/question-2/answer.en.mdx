# Two errors push the same way, so both must come out before the heat balance

A voltmeter that reads <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ V}" /> with no current has a **positive zero error**: every reading is <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ V}" /> too high, so it must be subtracted. The heater's energy also does not all stay in the oil: some leaks to the room while it warms. Only the energy the oil **keeps** equals <InlineMath math="mc\Delta\theta" />. Both mistakes make the energy look bigger than it is, so the raw value of <InlineMath math="c" /> comes out too high.

The true potential difference is <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ V}" />, and the oil's specific heat capacity is about <InlineMath math="2.1\times10^{3}" /> J per kg per °C. Using the raw voltmeter reading and ignoring the heat loss would give a value about <InlineMath math="16\%" /> too high.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12.8\text{ V}" /> **adds the zero error** instead of subtracting it, as if the meter read too low.
- <InlineMath math="2.4\times10^{3}" /> corrects the voltmeter but **ignores the heat lost** to the room, so it credits the oil with energy it never kept.
- <InlineMath math="13\%" /> compares with a raw value that **corrects the voltmeter**, but the raw reading still carries the zero error.

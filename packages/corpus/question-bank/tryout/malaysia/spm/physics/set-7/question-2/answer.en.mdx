# Read the power from the ratio for a doubling, then scale by that power

In a power law, multiplying <InlineMath math="d" /> by a factor multiplies <InlineMath math="t" /> by **that factor raised to the power <InlineMath math="n" />**. Doubling the diameter from <InlineMath math="2.0" /> to <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ mm}" /> divides the time by <InlineMath math="320\div56.6=5.66" />, and so does the next doubling. Since <InlineMath math="2^{2.5}=5.66" />, the power is <InlineMath math="n=-2.5" />. Then scale from a known point, never by a straight-line guess between points.

<BlockMath math="2^{n}=\frac{56.6}{320}=0.177\ \Rightarrow\ n=-2.5,\qquad t_{5.0}=320\times\left(\frac{5.0}{2.0}\right)^{-2.5}=\frac{320}{2.5^{2.5}}=\frac{320}{9.88}\approx32\text{ s}" />

The hole is <InlineMath math="2.5" /> times as wide as the <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm}" /> hole, so the time is divided by <InlineMath math="2.5^{2.5}\approx9.9" />, about <InlineMath math="32\text{ s}" />.

<BlockMath math="\left(\frac{d}{2.0}\right)^{2.5}=\frac{320}{60}=5.33\Rightarrow d=2.0\times5.33^{0.4}\approx3.9\text{ mm}" />

A run of <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" /> lies between the <InlineMath math="320\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="56.6\text{ s}" /> in the table, so the hole must lie between <InlineMath math="2.0" /> and <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ mm}" />: about <InlineMath math="3.9\text{ mm}" />.

<BlockMath math="\text{rate}=\frac{400\text{ g}}{32\text{ s}}=12.5\text{ g s}^{-1}" />

The <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ kg}" /> of sand runs through the <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ mm}" /> hole in <InlineMath math="32\text{ s}" />, an average of <InlineMath math="12.5\text{ g}" /> every second.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="n=-2" /> lets the time fall **fourfold for each doubling**, but <InlineMath math="320/56.6=5.66=2^{2.5}" />, and the slip carries into the rate and the hole.
- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ mm}" /> turns the **ratio upside down**: a shorter time needs a wider hole, not a narrower one.
- <InlineMath math="n=-3" /> lets the time fall **eightfold for each doubling**, which the readings of <InlineMath math="56.6\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ s}" /> rule out.

# The gradient of the log graph is the power

Taking lg of <InlineMath math="t=kd^{\,n}" /> gives <InlineMath math="\lg t=\lg k+n\lg d" />, a straight line whose **gradient is the power <InlineMath math="n" />**. The end points give <InlineMath math="(1.000-2.505)\div(0.903-0.301)=-2.50" />. Dividing the other way round, <InlineMath math="\Delta\lg d\div\Delta\lg t" />, gives <InlineMath math="-0.40" />, and that slip then sends the <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ mm}" /> time out to about <InlineMath math="220\text{ s}" />.

# Scaling to the wider hole

The first point is the <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm}" /> hole, <InlineMath math="10^{2.505}=320\text{ s}" />. A <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ mm}" /> hole is <InlineMath math="2.5" /> times as wide, so its time is <InlineMath math="320" /> divided by <InlineMath math="2.5^{2.5}=9.88" />, **about <InlineMath math="32\text{ s}" />**.

# Two small holes against one bigger hole

With two <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ mm}" /> holes, **the flow rates add, the times do not**. Each hole alone needs <InlineMath math="56.6\text{ s}" />, so together they pour twice as fast and empty the funnel in <InlineMath math="28.3\text{ s}" />. One hole does that only if its time is halved, so its diameter must grow by <InlineMath math="2^{1/2.5}=1.32" />, to <InlineMath math="5.3\text{ mm}" />.

# How fast the sand pours through it

That hole lets <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ kg}" /> through in <InlineMath math="28.3\text{ s}" />, **twice the rate of one small hole**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n&=\frac{1.000-2.505}{0.903-0.301}=-2.50,\qquad t_{5.0}=320\times2.5^{-2.5}=32\text{ s}\\
t_{2\times4.0}&=\tfrac12\times56.6=28.3\text{ s},\quad d=4.0\times2^{1/2.5}=5.3\text{ mm},\quad \frac{0.40\text{ kg}}{28.3\text{ s}}=14\text{ g s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

Quoting <InlineMath math="7.1\text{ g s}^{-1}" /> gives the rate of just one of the two small holes.

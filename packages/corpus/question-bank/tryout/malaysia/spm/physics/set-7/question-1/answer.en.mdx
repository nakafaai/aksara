# Subtract the zero reading, drop the point past the elastic limit, then use the area

Hooke's law links force to **extension**, not to the pointer reading, so first subtract the reading with no load. The points from <InlineMath math="1.0" /> to <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" /> lie on one straight line, while the <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ N}" /> point jumps well above it: the spring has gone past its elastic limit there, so that point is left out. The energy stored is the **area under the force-extension line**, a triangle.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ J}" /> uses the **pointer reading of <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}" />** as if it were the extension.
- <InlineMath math="43\text{ N m}^{-1}" /> comes from a line through the **<InlineMath math="5.0\text{ N}" /> point**, which lies past the elastic limit.
- <InlineMath math="0.32\text{ J}" /> multiplies force by extension **without the half**, as if the full force acted over the whole stretch.

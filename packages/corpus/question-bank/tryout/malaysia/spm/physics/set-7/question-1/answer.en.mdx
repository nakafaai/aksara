# Subtract the zero reading, drop the point past the elastic limit, then use the area

Hooke's law links force to **extension**, not to the pointer reading, so first subtract the reading with no load. The points from <InlineMath math="1.0" /> to <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" /> lie on one straight line, while the <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ N}" /> point jumps well above it: the spring has gone past its elastic limit there, so that point is left out. The energy stored is the **area under the force-extension line**, a triangle.

The extensions are <InlineMath math="2.0" />, <InlineMath math="4.1" />, <InlineMath math="5.9" /> and <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ cm}" />, close to <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}" /> for every newton, so <InlineMath math="k=50\text{ N m}^{-1}" />. At <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" /> the spring is stretched <InlineMath math="0.080\text{ m}" /> and stores <InlineMath math="0.16\text{ J}" />.

<BlockMath math="k_{\text{half}}=2\times50=100\text{ N m}^{-1},\qquad x=\frac{4.0}{100}=0.040\text{ m},\qquad E=\tfrac12(4.0)(0.040)=0.080\text{ J},\qquad v=\sqrt{\frac{2(0.080)}{0.020}}\approx2.8\text{ m s}^{-1}" />

Half a spring is **twice as stiff**: each coil stretches the same amount under the same force, and there are half as many coils. Under <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" /> it stretches only <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}" />, stores <InlineMath math="0.080\text{ J}" />, and launches the ball at about <InlineMath math="2.8\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="43\text{ N m}^{-1}" /> draws the line through the **<InlineMath math="5.0\text{ N}" /> reading**, which was taken past the elastic limit, so every later value is off.
- <InlineMath math="0.32\text{ J}" /> uses **<InlineMath math="Fx" /> instead of <InlineMath math="\tfrac12Fx" />**: the force grows from zero as the spring stretches, so the average force is half the final one.
- <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> for the half spring assumes **cutting the spring leaves <InlineMath math="k" /> unchanged**, but a half spring is twice as stiff and stores half the energy under the same load.

# Find the reading that broke the pattern

Up to <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" /> every extra newton moves the pointer about <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}" />. The last newton moves it <InlineMath math="3.5\text{ cm}" />, so **the <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ N}" /> reading was taken past the elastic limit** and has to go. Keep it and you get <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ N}" /> over <InlineMath math="11.5\text{ cm}" />, about <InlineMath math="43\text{ N m}^{-1}" />, a stiffness the spring never had while it obeyed Hooke's law.

The clean readings give <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" /> for <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ cm}" />, so <InlineMath math="k=50\text{ N m}^{-1}" />.

# The energy is a triangle, not a rectangle

The force grew from zero to <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" /> while the spring stretched, so the work done is the area of a triangle under the force-extension line, **half of force times extension**: <InlineMath math="0.16\text{ J}" />. Multiplying <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" /> by <InlineMath math="0.080\text{ m}" /> without the half doubles it to <InlineMath math="0.32\text{ J}" />.

# Half a spring is twice as stiff

Each half of the spring carries the full load but has only half the coils to share the stretch, so **the half spring has <InlineMath math="k=100\text{ N m}^{-1}" />** and stretches just <InlineMath math="0.040\text{ m}" /> under <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ N}" />. It stores <InlineMath math="0.080\text{ J}" />, and that is all the ball can get.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac{4.0\text{ N}}{0.080\text{ m}}=50\text{ N m}^{-1},\qquad E=\tfrac12Fx=\tfrac12\times4.0\times0.080=0.16\text{ J}\\
k'&=2k=100\text{ N m}^{-1},\quad x'=0.040\text{ m},\quad E'=0.080\text{ J}=\tfrac12\times0.020\times v^2\ \Rightarrow\ v=2.8\text{ m s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

Handing the ball the whole spring's <InlineMath math="0.16\text{ J}" /> would launch it at <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, but the shorter spring stores only half as much at the same load.

# Reflected and direct waves interfere, so the fading pattern is set by the wavelength

Where the reflected wave meets the direct wave **out of step**, they cancel and the signal fades. These points repeat every **half wavelength** along the road. The car's speed turns that spacing into a rate, and a station with a much longer wavelength spreads its fades much farther apart.

The FM fades are <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> apart, so at <InlineMath math="15\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> the car passes ten every second, a rapid flutter. On AM the fades are <InlineMath math="250\text{ m}" /> apart, so a fade comes only about every <InlineMath math="17\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" /> takes a **whole wavelength** as the spacing, but the fades repeat every half wavelength.
- <InlineMath math="23" /> per second **multiplies** the speed by the spacing instead of dividing, which gives a distance squared over time, not a rate.
- <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ m}" /> takes a **quarter wavelength**, the distance from a fade to the nearest strong point, as the spacing between fades.

# Diffraction is strongest when the wavelength is comparable to the obstacle

Waves bend around an obstacle most when their wavelength is **similar in size to the obstacle**. Work out both wavelengths from <InlineMath math="v=f\lambda" /> and compare them with the hill.

<BlockMath math="\lambda_{AM}=\frac{3.0\times10^{8}}{6.0\times10^{5}}=500\text{ m},\qquad \lambda_{FM}=\frac{3.0\times10^{8}}{1.0\times10^{8}}=3.0\text{ m}" />

The AM wave, <InlineMath math="500\text{ m}" /> long, is about as long as the hill is wide, so it **diffracts** around the hill and fills the valley. The FM wave is far shorter than the hill, travels nearly in straight lines and leaves the valley in a shadow. The village hears the AM station more clearly.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The **FM station** has the shorter wavelength, which bends less around the hill, so it is the weaker signal in the valley.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\times10^{-3}\text{ m}" /> divides the **frequency by the speed** instead of the speed by the frequency.
- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" /> is the **FM wavelength**, not the AM wavelength.

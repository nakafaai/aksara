# The fades sit at fixed places, so count them by distance, not by time

The direct and reflected waves form a **stationary pattern** along the road: the fades stay put, half a wavelength apart, and the car runs through them. At speed <InlineMath math="v" /> it meets <InlineMath math="\dfrac{v}{d}" /> fades a second, so while it accelerates the rate keeps rising. The fades heard separately are the ones the car passes **before reaching the blur speed**, so they must be counted along the distance it covers, not multiplied by a time.

The fades are <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> apart and blur above <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. The car reaches that speed after about <InlineMath math="11.3\text{ m}" />, by which time it has passed the fades at <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" />, <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" />, up to <InlineMath math="10.5\text{ m}" />: seven separate fades. The eighth, at <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ m}" />, comes at <InlineMath math="4.1" /> a second and is already smoothed out.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" /> takes a **whole wavelength** as the spacing, which doubles the blur speed and stretches the distance fourfold.
- <InlineMath math="15" /> fades with the right spacing assumes the car meets **four fades every second** for the whole <InlineMath math="3.75\text{ s}" />, but at the start it is barely moving.
- <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ m}" /> takes a **quarter wavelength**, the distance from a fade to the nearest strong point, as the spacing between fades.

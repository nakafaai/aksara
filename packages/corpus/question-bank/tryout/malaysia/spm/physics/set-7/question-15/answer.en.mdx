# Work out both wavelengths, then compare each with the hill

Waves bend around an obstacle most when their wavelength is **similar in size to the obstacle**. Each statement is checked against <InlineMath math="v=f\lambda" /> and that rule.

<BlockMath math="\lambda_{AM}=\frac{3.0\times10^{8}}{6.0\times10^{5}}=500\text{ m},\qquad \lambda_{FM}=\frac{3.0\times10^{8}}{1.0\times10^{8}}=3.0\text{ m},\qquad f=\frac{3.0\times10^{8}}{500}=6.0\times10^{5}\text{ Hz}" />

Statement I is correct. Statement II is wrong: the FM wavelength is <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" />, not <InlineMath math="0.33\text{ m}" />. Statement III is correct, since the <InlineMath math="500\text{ m}" /> wave is as long as the hill is wide and **diffracts** into the valley. Statement IV is correct: bending as well as the AM wave needs the same <InlineMath math="500\text{ m}" /> wavelength, which means a frequency of <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ MHz}" />, the AM station's own.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **I and III only** misses statement IV, which follows from the same <InlineMath math="v=f\lambda" /> worked backwards.
- **I, II and III only** accepts <InlineMath math="0.33\text{ m}" />, which divides the **frequency by the speed** in the wrong order.
- **II, III and IV only** rejects statement I, although <InlineMath math="\dfrac{3.0\times10^{8}}{6.0\times10^{5}}" /> is exactly <InlineMath math="500\text{ m}" />.

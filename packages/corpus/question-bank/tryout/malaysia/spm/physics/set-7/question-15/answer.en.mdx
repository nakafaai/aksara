# Two waves meeting head on

The FM wavelength is <InlineMath math="3.0\times10^{8}/1.0\times10^{8}=3.0\text{ m}" />. The direct and reflected waves run along the road in opposite directions and set up a standing pattern, and **neighbouring fades in a standing pattern are half a wavelength apart, <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" />**. A full <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" /> spacing pushes the blur speed up to <InlineMath math="12\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, and <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ m}" /> is the gap from a fade to the strong signal beside it, not to the next fade.

# When the fading turns into a hiss

A car at speed <InlineMath math="v" /> crosses <InlineMath math="v/1.5" /> fades every second. **The radio blurs once that rate passes <InlineMath math="4" /> per second**, which happens at <InlineMath math="v=4\times1.5=6.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

# Fades heard on the way to the blur

Accelerating at <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, the car needs <InlineMath math="3.75\text{ s}" /> to reach <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> and covers <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\times1.6\times3.75^2=11.25\text{ m}" />. It started sitting in a fade, so **the fades heard after moving off are the ones at <InlineMath math="1.5" />, <InlineMath math="3.0" /> and so on up to <InlineMath math="10.5\text{ m}" />**, seven of them. Using the final speed for the whole <InlineMath math="3.75\text{ s}" /> pretends the car covered <InlineMath math="22.5\text{ m}" /> and gives fifteen.

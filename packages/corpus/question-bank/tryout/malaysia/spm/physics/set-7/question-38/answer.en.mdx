# Let the absorbers name the radiation, then subtract the background before halving

Paper stops alpha, and a few millimetres of aluminium stop beta. If **neither changes the reading**, the radiation must be **gamma**. For the half-life, the detector's readings include the background, which does not decay, so it must be **subtracted first**. Then count how many times the corrected rate halves in <InlineMath math="18" /> hours.

<BlockMath math="1000-40=960,\qquad 160-40=120,\qquad \frac{960}{120}=8=2^3" />

<BlockMath math="T_{1/2}=\frac{18\text{ h}}{3}=6.0\text{ h}" />

The source emits gamma rays, since paper and aluminium do not reduce the count. The source's own count rate falls from <InlineMath math="960" /> to <InlineMath math="120" /> per minute, three halvings in <InlineMath math="18" /> hours, so the half-life is <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ h}" />.

<BlockMath math="70-40=30,\qquad \frac{960}{30}=32=2^5,\qquad t=5\times6.0=30\text{ h}" />

A reading of <InlineMath math="70" /> per minute means the source itself gives only <InlineMath math="30" /> per minute, one thirty-second of its starting <InlineMath math="960" />. That takes five half-lives, <InlineMath math="30\text{ h}" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{960}{2^2}+40=240+40=280\text{ per minute}" />

Two centimetres of lead halve the source's <InlineMath math="960" /> per minute twice, to <InlineMath math="240" />, but the background still reaches the detector, so the reading is <InlineMath math="280" /> per minute.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **Beta** would be stopped by <InlineMath math="5\text{ mm}" /> of aluminium, but the reading did not fall.
- <InlineMath math="6.8\text{ h}" />, <InlineMath math="26\text{ h}" /> and <InlineMath math="250" /> use the **raw readings** without separating the background, which neither decays nor is blocked by the lead.
- <InlineMath math="23\text{ h}" /> and <InlineMath math="240" /> remove the background but **never add it back** to the readings the detector would show.

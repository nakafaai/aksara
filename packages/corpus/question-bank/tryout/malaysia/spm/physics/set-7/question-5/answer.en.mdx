# Find both speeds, reverse one, and remember the ball's weight

The speeds come from the heights, <InlineMath math="v=\sqrt{2gh}" />. The velocity **reverses**, so the change of momentum uses the sum of the two speeds. During contact two forces act on the ball, the floor's push upwards and its weight downwards, so the floor must supply the **impulse plus the weight**. Kinetic energy follows the heights, since each speed came from a fall or a rise.

The momentum changes by <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ N s}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ s}" />, which needs a net <InlineMath math="45\text{ N}" /> upwards. The floor must push <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ N}" /> more to hold up the weight, <InlineMath math="46\text{ N}" /> in all. The ball keeps <InlineMath math="0.80\div1.25=64\%" /> of its energy, so <InlineMath math="36\%" /> is lost.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="45\text{ N}" /> is only the **net force**, forgetting that the floor must also support the ball's weight.
- <InlineMath math="5\text{ N}" /> subtracts the speeds, **<InlineMath math="5.0-4.0" />**, as if the ball kept moving downwards.
- <InlineMath math="20\%" /> is the fraction of **speed** lost, <InlineMath math="1\div5" />, but kinetic energy depends on the speed squared.

# The loss depends on the current, and the current depends on the power that must cover the loss

The generator must make the village's <InlineMath math="45\text{ kW}" /> **plus** the cable loss <InlineMath math="I^2R" />, but the current <InlineMath math="I=\dfrac PV" /> itself depends on that total power <InlineMath math="P" />. That gives a **quadratic** in <InlineMath math="P" /> with two roots, and only the smaller one is sensible: the larger would waste most of the power in the cables. At <InlineMath math="25\text{ kV}" /> the current is tiny, so the loss barely changes the total.

The generator must produce <InlineMath math="50\text{ kW}" /> at <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ kV}" />, losing <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ kW}" />, a tenth of its output. The <InlineMath math="450\text{ kW}" /> root would lose <InlineMath math="405\text{ kW}" /> in the cables, which no designer would choose. At <InlineMath math="25\text{ kV}" /> the cables waste only about <InlineMath math="6.5\text{ W}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="49\text{ kW}" /> works out the current from the **village's <InlineMath math="45\text{ kW}" />** instead of the generator's output, so it underestimates the current and the loss.
- <InlineMath math="450\text{ kW}" /> takes the **other root** of the quadratic, a solution in which nine tenths of the power is wasted in the cables.
- <InlineMath math="200\text{ W}" /> makes the loss fall in proportion to the voltage, **<InlineMath math="25" /> times instead of <InlineMath math="25^2" /> times**.

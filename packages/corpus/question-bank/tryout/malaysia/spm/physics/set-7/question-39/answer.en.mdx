# A tiny loss of mass

When wood burns, a little of what it was turns into heat and light. In a decay a little mass itself turns into energy. **The helium atom is <InlineMath math="3.016049-3.016029=0.000020\text{ u}" /> lighter, and <InlineMath math="E=mc^2" /> turns that into <InlineMath math="0.000020\times1.66\times10^{-27}\times(3.0\times10^{8})^2=3.0\times10^{-15}\text{ J}" /> per decay**. The atomic masses already count the electrons on both sides, so nothing more is taken away. Forgetting to square <InlineMath math="c" /> gives <InlineMath math="1.0\times10^{-23}\text{ J}" />, far too small.

# Half a half-life is not a quarter gone

Fold a sheet of paper in half, then in half again. It shrinks by the same fraction each time, not by the same amount. Decay works like that. **After <InlineMath math="6.15" /> years, half a half-life, the activity is <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac12\right)^{0.5}=0.71" /> of the start, <InlineMath math="2.6\times10^{11}\text{ Bq}" />**, not three quarters of it. Each decay sends <InlineMath math="0.30\times3.0\times10^{-15}=9.0\times10^{-16}\text{ J}" /> into the phosphor, so the power now is <InlineMath math="2.6\times10^{11}\times9.0\times10^{-16}=2.3\times10^{-4}\text{ W}" />. Assuming a straight-line fall to three quarters gives <InlineMath math="2.5\times10^{-4}\text{ W}" />.

# Count from the filling day

A <InlineMath math="12" />-month guarantee counts from the day you bought the phone, not from today. **One eighth is three halvings, <InlineMath math="3\times12.3=36.9" /> years after filling, and the sign is already <InlineMath math="6.15" /> years old, so it has <InlineMath math="36.9-6.15=30.75" />, about <InlineMath math="31" />, years left**. Counting <InlineMath math="36.9" /> years from today keeps the sign about <InlineMath math="6" /> years too long.

# The missing mass is the energy that holds the nucleus together

A nucleus has **less mass** than its separate protons and neutrons. The difference, the **mass defect**, is the energy released when the nucleus forms, <InlineMath math="E=mc^2" />, and the same energy is needed to pull it apart. Divide by the **four nucleons** for the binding energy per nucleon.

<BlockMath math="E=\Delta m\,c^2=5.04\times10^{-29}\times(3.0\times10^{8})^2\approx4.5\times10^{-12}\text{ J},\qquad \frac{E}{4}\approx1.1\times10^{-12}\text{ J}" />

The nucleus is <InlineMath math="0.03039\text{ u}" /> lighter than its parts. That mass corresponds to about <InlineMath math="4.5\times10^{-12}\text{ J}" /> of binding energy, or about <InlineMath math="1.1\times10^{-12}\text{ J}" /> for each of the four nucleons.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.3\times10^{-12}\text{ J}" /> divides by the **two protons only**, but neutrons are nucleons too.
- <InlineMath math="1.5\times10^{-20}\text{ J}" /> uses **<InlineMath math="E=mc" />**, forgetting to square the speed of light.
- <InlineMath math="1.8\times10^{-11}\text{ J}" /> **multiplies by four** instead of dividing.

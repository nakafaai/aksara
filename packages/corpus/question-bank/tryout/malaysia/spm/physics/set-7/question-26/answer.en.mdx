# The depth below the tower's water surface sets the pressure

The water at a tap is under the column of water from the tap **up to the tower's surface**, so the depth is <InlineMath math="25\text{ m}" /> minus the tap's height. A gauge reads only the pressure **above atmospheric**, <InlineMath math="\rho gh" />. The machine needs a depth of at least <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ m}" />, so its tap may be at most <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> high, and the floor must be **at or below** that height.

The tap on floor <InlineMath math="3" /> reads <InlineMath math="1.6\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" />. Floor <InlineMath math="6" /> is the highest that works.

With the surface <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m}" /> lower, a tap must be at most <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}" /> high. Floor <InlineMath math="5" />, at <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" />, still works, but floor <InlineMath math="6" />, at <InlineMath math="18\text{ m}" />, now does not.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.6\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" /> adds **atmospheric pressure**, but a gauge reads only the pressure above atmospheric.
- Floors <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="6" /> **round <InlineMath math="6.7" /> and <InlineMath math="5.3" /> up**, but those floors are above the highest tap height that still gets enough pressure.
- <InlineMath math="9.0\times10^{4}\text{ Pa}" /> uses the **tap's height above the ground**, <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ m}" />, as if it were the depth of water above the tap.

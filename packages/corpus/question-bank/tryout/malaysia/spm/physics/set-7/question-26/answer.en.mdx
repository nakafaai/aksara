# The depth below the tower's water surface sets the pressure

The water at a tap is under the column of water from the tap **up to the tower's surface**, so the depth is <InlineMath math="25\text{ m}" /> minus the tap's height. A gauge reads only the pressure **above atmospheric**, <InlineMath math="\rho gh" />. The machine needs a depth of at least <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ m}" />, so its tap may be at most <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> high, and the floor must be **at or below** that height.

The tap on floor <InlineMath math="3" /> reads <InlineMath math="1.6\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" />. Floor <InlineMath math="6" /> is at <InlineMath math="18\text{ m}" />, giving <InlineMath math="7.0\times10^{4}\text{ Pa}" />, while floor <InlineMath math="7" /> at <InlineMath math="21\text{ m}" /> would give only <InlineMath math="4.0\times10^{4}\text{ Pa}" />. Floor <InlineMath math="6" /> is the highest that works.

<BlockMath math="\Delta h=\frac{30}{10}=3.0\text{ m},\qquad 25-3.0-5.0=17\text{ m},\qquad \frac{17}{3.0}=5.7\Rightarrow\text{floor }5" />

Drawing <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}^3" /> from a tank <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}^2" /> across lowers its surface by <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" />. A tap must then be at most <InlineMath math="17\text{ m}" /> high, so floor <InlineMath math="5" />, at <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" />, still works but floor <InlineMath math="6" />, at <InlineMath math="18\text{ m}" />, does not.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.6\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" /> adds **atmospheric pressure**, but a gauge reads only the pressure above atmospheric.
- Floors <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="6" /> **round <InlineMath math="6.7" /> and <InlineMath math="5.7" /> up**, but those floors lie above the highest tap that still gets enough pressure.
- **No floor** takes the <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}^3" /> as a **<InlineMath math="30\text{ m}" /> drop**, forgetting to divide the volume by the tank's area.

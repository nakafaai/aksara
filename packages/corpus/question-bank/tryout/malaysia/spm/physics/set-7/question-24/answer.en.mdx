# The air pushes on both sides, so only the water's extra pressure counts for the force

The total pressure on the water side is the **atmosphere plus the water column**, <InlineMath math="p_0+\rho gh" />. The air in the pipe pushes back with <InlineMath math="p_0" />, so the **resultant force** comes from the difference only, <InlineMath math="\rho gh" />, times the area.

The water side is at <InlineMath math="3.0\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" />, three times atmospheric pressure. The air cancels <InlineMath math="1.0\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" /> of it, leaving <InlineMath math="2.0\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" /> across <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ m}^2" />, a resultant force of <InlineMath math="1.0\times10^{5}\text{ N}" /> towards the pipe.

<BlockMath math="F_{\text{bolt}}=\frac{1.0\times10^{5}}{8}\approx13\text{ kN},\qquad \Delta p_{\max}=\frac{8\times15\,000}{0.50}=2.4\times10^{5}\text{ Pa},\qquad h_{\max}=\frac{2.4\times10^{5}}{1000\times10}=24\text{ m}" />

Each bolt carries about <InlineMath math="12.5\text{ kN}" />, inside its limit. Together the bolts can resist a pressure difference of <InlineMath math="2.4\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" />, which the water alone supplies at <InlineMath math="24\text{ m}" />, since the air acts on both sides.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.5\times10^{5}\text{ N}" /> multiplies the **total pressure** by the area, forgetting the air pushing back from the pipe, and the same slip makes the safe depth too shallow.
- <InlineMath math="34\text{ m}" /> gets the force right but **adds the atmosphere back** when turning the bolts' limit into a depth.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\times10^{5}\text{ N}" /> leaves out the **hatch's area**, as if it were <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}^2" />.

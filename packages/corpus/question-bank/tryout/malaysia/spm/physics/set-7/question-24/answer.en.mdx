# The air pushes on both sides, so only the water's extra pressure counts for the force

The total pressure on the water side is the **atmosphere plus the water column**, <InlineMath math="p_0+\rho gh" />. The air in the pipe pushes back with <InlineMath math="p_0" />, so the **resultant force** comes from the difference only, <InlineMath math="\rho gh" />, times the area.

The water side is at <InlineMath math="3.0\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" />, three times atmospheric pressure. The air cancels <InlineMath math="1.0\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" /> of it, leaving <InlineMath math="2.0\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" /> across <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ m}^2" />, a resultant force of <InlineMath math="1.0\times10^{5}\text{ N}" /> towards the pipe.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.0\times10^{5}\text{ Pa}" /> is only the **water's own pressure**, but the total pressure on the water side also includes the atmosphere above the reservoir.
- <InlineMath math="1.5\times10^{5}\text{ N}" /> multiplies the **total pressure** by the area, forgetting the air pushing back from the pipe.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\times10^{5}\text{ N}" /> **divides the pressure by the area** instead of multiplying.

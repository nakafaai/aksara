# Balance where the oil meets the water

Two children on a seesaw balance when they press down equally hard. The two arms of the U-tube balance the same way, at the level where the oil meets the water. **Below that level both arms hold only water, so the pressures there must match**. Above it, the left arm has <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ cm}" /> of oil and the right arm <InlineMath math="12.0-2.0=10.0\text{ cm}" /> of water. So <InlineMath math="\rho_o\times12.0=1000\times10.0" /> and <InlineMath math="\rho_o=833\text{ kg m}^{-3}" />. Flip the fraction and the oil comes out at <InlineMath math="1.2\times10^{3}\text{ kg m}^{-3}" />, heavier than water, so it would sink to the bend instead of resting on top.

# Down on one side, up on the other

Water cannot be squeezed into less room. Whatever goes down on one side has to come up on the other, and in a tube that is equally wide all along, it comes up the same distance. **When the oil pushes the boundary down by <InlineMath math="x" />, the right surface rises by <InlineMath math="x" />**, so the water standing above the boundary level is <InlineMath math="2x" /> tall. That gives <InlineMath math="2x=10.0\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="x=5.0\text{ cm}" />. The <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}" /> adds the drop on the left to the rise on the right, so calling all of it the rise doubles the answer.

# The oil arm runs over first

Pour from one jug into two glasses at different rates and the faster one spills first. For any oil column <InlineMath math="h" />, the right side rises <InlineMath math="\tfrac{833}{1000}\times\tfrac{h}{2}=\tfrac{5h}{12}" /> and the oil top ends <InlineMath math="h-\tfrac{5h}{12}=\tfrac{7h}{12}" /> above its first level. **The oil top climbs faster, so the left arm reaches its rim first**: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{7h}{12}=8.0\text{ cm}" /> gives <InlineMath math="h=13.7\text{ cm}" />. Watching the water arm instead, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5h}{12}=8.0\text{ cm}" /> gives <InlineMath math="19.2\text{ cm}" />, but by then oil has been running over the left rim for a while.

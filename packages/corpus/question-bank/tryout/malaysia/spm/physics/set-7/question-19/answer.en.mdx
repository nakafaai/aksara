# Read the Moon's angle off the objective's image, then choose the eyepiece

The objective forms a real image of the Moon at its focal plane, and the image size is the **focal length times the angle in radians**. That gives the Moon's angle. The magnification needed is the ratio of the two angles, which is also <InlineMath math="\dfrac{f_o}{f_e}" />, so it fixes the eyepiece's focal length, and its power is <InlineMath math="P=\dfrac1f" /> with <InlineMath math="f" /> **in metres**.

The Moon fills <InlineMath math="0.50°" />. To make it fill <InlineMath math="10°" /> the eyepiece needs a focal length of <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}" />, a power of <InlineMath math="+25\text{ D}" />, and in normal adjustment the lenses sit <InlineMath math="84\text{ cm}" /> apart.

<BlockMath math="\frac1v=\frac1{0.80}-\frac1{100}\Rightarrow v\approx0.8065\text{ m},\qquad \Delta=0.8065-0.80\approx6.5\times10^{-3}\text{ m}" />

The Moon is so far away that its image forms at the objective's focal plane. The tree is near enough that its image forms about <InlineMath math="6.5\text{ mm}" /> **beyond** that plane, so the eyepiece must move <InlineMath math="6.5\text{ mm}" /> away from the objective to keep the image at its own focus.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="+0.25\text{ D}" /> takes the reciprocal of the focal length **in centimetres**, but power uses metres.
- **No movement** treats the tree as **infinitely far away** like the Moon, but at <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> its image forms a little beyond the focal plane.
- **Towards the objective** has the direction reversed: a nearer object gives a farther image, so the eyepiece must move out.

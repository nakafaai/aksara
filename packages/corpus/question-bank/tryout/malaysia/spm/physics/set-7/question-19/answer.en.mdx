# How big the Moon looks to the eye

Rays from the edges of the Moon cross at the objective, so **the image on the focal plane spans the same angle the Moon does**: <InlineMath math="7.0\text{ mm}/800\text{ mm}=0.00875\text{ rad}" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.50^\circ" />.

# Choosing the eyepiece

To fill <InlineMath math="10^\circ" /> the telescope must magnify <InlineMath math="10/0.50=20" /> times, and in normal adjustment <InlineMath math="M=f_o/f_e" />, so <InlineMath math="f_e=80/20=4.0\text{ cm}" />. **Power is the reciprocal of the focal length in metres**, <InlineMath math="1/0.040=+25\text{ D}" />. Dividing <InlineMath math="1" /> by <InlineMath math="4.0" /> with centimetres left in gives <InlineMath math="+0.25\text{ D}" />, a lens a hundred times too weak.

# Refocusing on a tree

The tree is far but not infinitely far, so the objective forms its image a little beyond the focal point: <InlineMath math="1/v=1/0.80-1/100" /> gives <InlineMath math="v=0.8065\text{ m}" />, <InlineMath math="6.5\text{ mm}" /> farther from the objective. **The eyepiece must keep its <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}" /> from that image**, so it moves <InlineMath math="6.5\text{ mm}" /> away from the objective. Leaving it in place treats the tree like the Moon, and moving it inwards follows the image the wrong way.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=\frac{7.0}{800}=0.00875\text{ rad}=0.00875\times\frac{180^\circ}{3.14}=0.50^\circ,\quad f_e=\frac{80}{20}=4.0\text{ cm},\quad P=\frac{1}{0.040}=+25\text{ D}\\
\frac1v&=\frac{1}{0.80}-\frac{1}{100}=1.24\text{ m}^{-1}\ \Rightarrow\ v=0.8065\text{ m},\quad \Delta=6.5\text{ mm}
\end{aligned}" />

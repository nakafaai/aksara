# Turn powers into focal lengths, then choose which lens does which job

The power of a lens is <InlineMath math="P=\dfrac1f" /> with <InlineMath math="f" /> in metres. The **weaker lens, with the long focal length, is the objective** and the stronger one is the eyepiece. In normal adjustment the lenses are <InlineMath math="f_o+f_e" /> apart, and the angular magnification <InlineMath math="\dfrac{f_o}{f_e}" /> multiplies the angle the Moon fills.

<BlockMath math="L=80+4.0=84\text{ cm},\qquad M=\frac{80}{4.0}=20,\qquad \theta=20\times0.50°=10°" />

The lenses sit <InlineMath math="84\text{ cm}" /> apart. The telescope magnifies angles <InlineMath math="20" /> times, so the Moon appears to fill <InlineMath math="10°" /> instead of <InlineMath math="0.50°" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="76\text{ cm}" /> **subtracts the focal lengths**, but in normal adjustment their foci meet between the lenses, so the lengths add.
- <InlineMath math="0.025°" /> **divides** by the magnification, as if the eyepiece and objective were swapped.
- <InlineMath math="0.50°" /> assumes a telescope only brings the image closer **without enlarging the angle**, but its whole purpose is angular magnification.

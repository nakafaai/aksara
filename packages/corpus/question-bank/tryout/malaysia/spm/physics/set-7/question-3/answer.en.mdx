# Add or subtract the speeds, never the times

Speeds relative to the ground **add** when she walks with the escalator and **subtract** when she walks against it. Write each speed as a fraction of the escalator's length <InlineMath math="L" /> per second, combine the speeds, then turn the result back into a time.

<BlockMath math="v_e=\frac{L}{60},\quad v_w=\frac{L}{40}\qquad \text{up}: \frac{L}{L/60+L/40}=\frac{1}{5/120}=24\text{ s},\qquad \text{down}: \frac{L}{L/40-L/60}=\frac{1}{1/120}=120\text{ s}" />

Walking with the escalator she covers <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{60}+\tfrac{1}{40}=\tfrac{5}{120}" /> of it each second, so <InlineMath math="24\text{ s}" />. Walking down against it she gains only <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{40}-\tfrac{1}{60}=\tfrac{1}{120}" /> of it each second, so <InlineMath math="120\text{ s}" />.

<BlockMath math="\text{rate}=\frac{60}{40}=1.5\text{ steps s}^{-1},\qquad 1.5\times24=36,\qquad 1.5\times120=180" />

She treads <InlineMath math="1.5" /> steps every second whatever the escalator does. Walking up with it she treads only <InlineMath math="36" /> steps, because the escalator carries her part of the way, and walking down against it she treads <InlineMath math="180" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60" /> steps both ways assumes the **number of steps is fixed** by the escalator's length, but she treads steps for as long as she walks.
- <InlineMath math="36" /> steps going down **adds the speeds in both directions**, but walking down against the escalator the speeds subtract.
- <InlineMath math="50\text{ s}" /> **averages the two times**, but it is the speeds that add, not the times.

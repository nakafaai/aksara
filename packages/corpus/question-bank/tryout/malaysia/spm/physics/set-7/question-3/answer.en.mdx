# Work backwards: friction gives the speed after, momentum gives the speed before

The skid is easy to read backwards. Friction is <InlineMath math="0.60" /> times the weight, so the deceleration is <InlineMath math="0.60g" /> **whatever the mass**, and the skid length gives the speed just after the impact. The impact itself is a collision in which the cars stick: **momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not**, so momentum, not energy, carries the speed back to before the crash.

The locked cars left the impact at <InlineMath math="12\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, so the striking car was doing <InlineMath math="72\text{ km h}^{-1}" />. In the impact <InlineMath math="96\text{ kJ}" /> of kinetic energy became heat, sound and crushed metal, and the skid then removed the remaining <InlineMath math="144\text{ kJ}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="56\text{ km h}^{-1}" /> and <InlineMath math="0\text{ kJ}" /> carry the speed back by **conserving kinetic energy**, but cars that lock together always lose kinetic energy.
- <InlineMath math="9.3\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> applies the friction of **one car's weight** to both cars, but friction grows with the weight of everything skidding.
- <InlineMath math="144\text{ kJ}" /> is the energy **removed by the skid**, after the impact, not the energy converted in the impact.

# Measure every speed in escalator lengths

Call the escalator one length <InlineMath math="L" />. The steps move at <InlineMath math="L" /> per <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" /> and Aina walks at <InlineMath math="L" /> per <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" /> relative to them. Walking up a rising escalator **adds the two speeds**, so she covers <InlineMath math="L" /> in <InlineMath math="24\text{ s}" />. In that time she treads only <InlineMath math="24/40" /> of the <InlineMath math="60" /> steps, <InlineMath math="36" /> steps, because the escalator does part of the climbing.

# Walking down against the steps

Going down, her velocity relative to the ground is the difference of the speeds, <InlineMath math="L" /> per <InlineMath math="120\text{ s}" />. **The steps keep rising under her for the whole two minutes**, and she treads them at her usual rate of <InlineMath math="60" /> steps per <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />, so she treads <InlineMath math="180" /> steps. Using <InlineMath math="36" /> again forgets that the downward trip lasts five times as long as the upward one.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v_{\uparrow}&=\tfrac{L}{60}+\tfrac{L}{40}=\tfrac{L}{24},\qquad v_{\downarrow}=\tfrac{L}{40}-\tfrac{L}{60}=\tfrac{L}{120}\\
N&=\tfrac{120}{40}\times60=180,\qquad v_{b}=\tfrac{L}{60}-\tfrac{L}{75}=+\tfrac{L}{300}
\end{aligned}" />

# The brother who walks too slowly

He walks at <InlineMath math="L" /> per <InlineMath math="75\text{ s}" /> relative to the steps, slower than the steps rise. So **his velocity relative to the ground still points up**, at <InlineMath math="L" /> per <InlineMath math="300\text{ s}" />. He walks downwards the whole time and still arrives at the top after <InlineMath math="300\text{ s}" />. Expecting him to step off at the bottom gets the sign of his velocity wrong, and <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" /> leaves out the walking that slows his ride.

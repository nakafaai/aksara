# Work from the inputs through each gate in turn

The NOT gate gives <InlineMath math="1" /> when the belt is **unfastened**. The OR gate gives <InlineMath math="1" /> **if the belt is unfastened or the door is open**. The AND gate passes that on **only when the ignition is on**. So the buzzer needs <InlineMath math="I=1" /> together with an OR output of <InlineMath math="1" />. The OR gate does not see <InlineMath math="I" /> at all, so its output can be <InlineMath math="1" /> even when the buzzer is silent.

With the ignition off, the buzzer never sounds. With it on, the buzzer sounds in every case except belt fastened and door shut, which is <InlineMath math="3" /> of the <InlineMath math="8" /> combinations. When everything is <InlineMath math="0" />, the OR gate still outputs <InlineMath math="1" />, because the belt is unfastened, but the AND gate blocks it.

<BlockMath math="B'=I\cdot\overline F\cdot D:\qquad (I,F,D)=(1,0,1)\text{ only}" />

With an AND gate the buzzer would need the ignition on, the belt unfastened **and** the door open together, which happens in just <InlineMath math="1" /> of the <InlineMath math="8" /> combinations.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0" /> for the OR gate takes the **buzzer's output** for the OR gate's, but the ignition only enters at the AND gate.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> and <InlineMath math="2" /> count the combinations where the **gate before the final AND** gives <InlineMath math="1" />, forgetting that the final AND gate also needs the ignition on.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> **ignores the door input**, counting only the cases with the belt unfastened and the ignition on.

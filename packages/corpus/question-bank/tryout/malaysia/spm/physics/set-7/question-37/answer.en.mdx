# What the circuit wants

Think of the pump as a gardener who waters when the soil is dry and it is not raining, or whenever you press the button, but never from an empty tank. **That is <InlineMath math="P=(S\cdot\overline{R}+M)\cdot\overline{E}" />**. With <InlineMath math="E=1" /> nothing runs. With <InlineMath math="E=0" /> the pump runs for all four combinations with <InlineMath math="M=1" /> and for one more, dry soil with no rain, so <InlineMath math="5" /> of the <InlineMath math="16" />. Leaving <InlineMath math="E" /> out of the count doubles it to <InlineMath math="10" />.

# The tank decides for itself

A torch whose battery lasts three hours goes dark after three hours of use, not after three hours on the clock. **The pump wants to run in hours <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="3" />, <InlineMath math="4" />, <InlineMath math="5" />, <InlineMath math="6" />, <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="8" />, but hours <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> use up the tank, so hours <InlineMath math="5" /> and <InlineMath math="6" /> start with <InlineMath math="E=1" />**. The refill brings hours <InlineMath math="7" /> and <InlineMath math="8" /> back, so the pump runs for <InlineMath math="5" /> hours. Ignoring the tank gives <InlineMath math="7" />.

# Without the NOT gate

After the refill only hour <InlineMath math="7" /> runs, so the faulty pump runs for <InlineMath math="4" /> hours**.

# Compare the faulty behaviour with the correct table, then rule out each candidate fault

The working circuit gives <InlineMath math="B=I\cdot\left(\overline F+D\right)" />: with <InlineMath math="I=1" /> it should stay silent only when the belt is fastened **and** the door is shut. A buzzer that sounds for every <InlineMath math="I=1" /> means the AND gate is receiving a <InlineMath math="1" /> from the OR side every time. That happens if the **NOT output** or the **OR output** is stuck at <InlineMath math="1" />. The measurement settles it: the NOT gate does give <InlineMath math="0" /> when <InlineMath math="F=1" />, so it is working, and the stuck output must be the OR gate's.

The two tables differ only for <InlineMath math="I=1" />, <InlineMath math="F=1" />, <InlineMath math="D=0" />: ignition on, belt fastened, door shut, when the buzzer should be silent but sounds. That one combination is the one to test.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **NOT output stuck at <InlineMath math="1" />** would give the same buzzer table, but the technician measured the NOT output as <InlineMath math="0" /> with <InlineMath math="F=1" />, so the NOT gate works.
- **<InlineMath math="4" /> combinations** counts every case with <InlineMath math="I=1" /> as wrong, but in three of them the buzzer is meant to sound anyway.
- **AND output stuck at <InlineMath math="1" />** would make the buzzer sound even with the ignition off, which the technician never saw.

# The reflected beam turns through twice the mirror's angle

Turning a mirror by <InlineMath math="\theta" /> turns the normal by <InlineMath math="\theta" />, so the angle of incidence and the angle of reflection **each** grow by <InlineMath math="\theta" />: the reflected beam turns through <InlineMath math="2\theta" />. The spot's shift follows from the right-angled triangle with the wall. When the mirror spins, the beam spins **twice as fast**, so near its original position the spot moves like a point on a circle of radius <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" /> turning at <InlineMath math="4.0" /> revolutions per second.

Turning the mirror <InlineMath math="5.0°" /> swings the beam <InlineMath math="10°" />, which moves the spot about <InlineMath math="0.53\text{ m}" /> along the wall. Spinning at <InlineMath math="2.0" /> revolutions per second, the beam turns at <InlineMath math="4.0" /> revolutions per second, and the spot crosses its starting point at about <InlineMath math="75\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

<BlockMath math="\tan2\theta=\frac{3.0}{3.0}=1\Rightarrow2\theta=45°\Rightarrow\theta=22.5°" />

A spot <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" /> along a wall <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m}" /> away means the beam has turned <InlineMath math="45°" />, so the mirror has turned only half of that, <InlineMath math="22.5°" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.26\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="45°" /> turn the beam through **only the mirror's angle**, and the same slip halves the speed.
- <InlineMath math="38\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> gets the shift right but lets the beam spin at **the mirror's rate** instead of twice it.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> leaves out the **<InlineMath math="2\pi" />** that turns revolutions into distance around the circle.

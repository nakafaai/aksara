# The image runs the same way as the object, faster and faster, until it stops being real

For a convex lens, <InlineMath math="\dfrac1f=\dfrac1u+\dfrac1v" />. As the object comes closer, the real image moves **further away** on the other side, in the same direction as the object travels. The image is real only while the object is **beyond the focal point**: at <InlineMath math="u=f" /> the image goes off to infinity, and closer than that it becomes virtual, on the car's own side, where no screen can catch it.

The screen starts <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> from the lens. In the first <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ s}" /> it must move <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> away from the lens, an average of <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm s}^{-1}" />. After <InlineMath math="10\text{ s}" /> the car reaches the focal point, and from then on no real image forms.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **Towards the lens** gets the direction backwards: as a real object approaches, its real image moves away.
- <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ s}" /> is when the car reaches **twice the focal length**, where the image is the same size as the car, but the image is still real there.
- <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" /> **adds the reciprocals** instead of subtracting, and then assumes the image moves with the car at its own speed.

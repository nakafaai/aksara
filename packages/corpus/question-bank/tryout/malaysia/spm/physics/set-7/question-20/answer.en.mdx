# The source sets the frequency, the glass slows the wave

Refraction changes the **speed** of light, <InlineMath math="v=\dfrac cn" />. The frequency is fixed by the source and **does not change** at a boundary, because every crest that arrives must pass through. With the same frequency and a lower speed, the wavelength shrinks in the same ratio. Colour is set by **frequency**, not by the wavelength in a particular material.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **I, II and IV only** judges colour by the **wavelength in the glass**, but colour follows the frequency, which has not changed.
- **I, II and III only** lets the **frequency fall** too, which would also break <InlineMath math="v=f\lambda" /> with the new speed and wavelength.
- **II and IV only** rejects statement I, although <InlineMath math="v=\dfrac cn" /> gives exactly <InlineMath math="2.0\times10^{8}\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

# Follow the beam into the glass, along its slanting path, and check what the glass can change

Snell's law gives the angle inside the glass. The beam then crosses the block **on a slant**, so its path is longer than the <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ cm}" /> thickness, and the crossing time uses that longer path and the **slower speed** in glass. The glass changes the speed and the wavelength, but not the **frequency**, which sets the colour.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **I, II, III and IV** accepts statement IV, but colour follows the frequency, which no boundary can change.
- **I and II only** rejects statement III, which needs the **slanting <InlineMath math="6.6\text{ cm}" /> path**: using the <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ cm}" /> thickness gives only <InlineMath math="3.0\times10^{-10}\text{ s}" />.
- **I, III and IV only** rejects statement II, although a beam crossing at <InlineMath math="25°" /> must travel farther than the block's thickness.

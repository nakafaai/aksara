# The glass slows the light and shortens its wavelength, but the frequency stays

Refraction changes the **speed** of light, <InlineMath math="v=\dfrac cn" />. The frequency is fixed by the source and **does not change** at a boundary, so the wavelength shrinks in the same ratio as the speed. The crossing time and the number of wavelengths in the block both come from these values in the glass.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **I, II, III and IV** accepts statement IV, but a boundary cannot change the frequency, and colour follows frequency.
- **I and II only** rejects statement III, which needs the wavelength **in the glass**, <InlineMath math="422\text{ nm}" />: using <InlineMath math="633\text{ nm}" /> gives only <InlineMath math="9.5\times10^{4}" />.
- **I, III and IV only** rejects statement II, although <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.060}{2.0\times10^{8}}" /> is exactly <InlineMath math="3.0\times10^{-10}\text{ s}" />.

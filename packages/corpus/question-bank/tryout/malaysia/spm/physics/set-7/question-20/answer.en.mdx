# The source sets the frequency, the glass slows the wave

Refraction changes the **speed** of light, <InlineMath math="v=\dfrac cn" />. The frequency is fixed by the source and **does not change** at a boundary, because every crest that arrives must pass through. With the same frequency and a lower speed, the wavelength shrinks in the same ratio, <InlineMath math="\lambda=\dfrac vf" />.

<BlockMath math="\lambda_g=\frac{v}{f}=\frac{633}{1.50}=422\text{ nm}" />

Inside the glass the light still has frequency <InlineMath math="4.7\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}" />, so its colour is unchanged. It travels at two thirds of its speed in air, so its wavelength is two thirds as long, <InlineMath math="422\text{ nm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="950\text{ nm}" /> **multiplies** by the refractive index, as if the wave sped up in glass.
- <InlineMath math="3.2\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}" /> with <InlineMath math="422\text{ nm}" /> **divides the frequency as well**, which would also break <InlineMath math="v=f\lambda" />.
- <InlineMath math="633\text{ nm}" /> with <InlineMath math="3.2\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}" /> keeps the **wavelength fixed** and changes the frequency, the reverse of what happens.

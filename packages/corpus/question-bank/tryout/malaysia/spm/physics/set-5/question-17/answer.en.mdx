# The rim ray travels at the critical angle, so the radius comes from its tangent

Light skimming the surface from the horizon bends down into the water at the **critical angle**, where <InlineMath math="\sin c = 1 \div 1.33" /> gives <InlineMath math="c = 48.8^\circ" />. No sky light reaches the fish at a larger angle, so beyond that angle the fish sees only reflection.

The rim ray makes <InlineMath math="48.8^\circ" /> with the vertical over a depth of <InlineMath math="2.0" /> m, so the radius is <InlineMath math="2.0 \times \tan 48.8^\circ = 2.3" /> m and the area is <InlineMath math="\pi \times 2.28^2 = 16" /> square metres.

**With the oil on top**: Snell’s law holds at each flat surface, so <InlineMath math="1.33\sin\theta_{\text{water}} = 1.50\sin\theta_{\text{oil}} = 1 \times \sin 90^\circ" /> for light grazing in from the horizon. The oil cancels out, the angle in the water is still <InlineMath math="48.8^\circ" />, and the window **keeps its size**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}^2" /> uses the oil’s refractive index as if the fish were **in the oil**.
- <InlineMath math="46\text{ m}^2" /> uses the ratio of the two indices, which matters only at the oil-water surface, not for the **light from the sky**.
- <InlineMath math="9.6\text{ m}^2" /> uses the angle from the surface instead of the angle from the **vertical**.

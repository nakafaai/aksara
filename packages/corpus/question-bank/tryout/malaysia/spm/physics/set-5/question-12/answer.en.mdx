# Add the heat for all three stages, then divide by the freezer’s rate

Cooling the water to <InlineMath math="0^\circ\text{C}" /> removes <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 4200 \times 20 = 42\,000" /> J, freezing it removes <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 3.34 \times 10^5 = 167\,000" /> J, and cooling the ice removes <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 2100 \times 10 = 10\,500" /> J, using the **specific heat capacity of ice**.

The total is <InlineMath math="219\,500" /> J, so at <InlineMath math="150" /> W it takes <InlineMath math="1463" /> s, about <InlineMath math="24" /> minutes. Freezing alone takes <InlineMath math="167\,000 \div 219\,500 = 76\%" /> of that time, because the **change of state** needs by far the most heat.

**With the leak**: the bottle loses only <InlineMath math="150 - 30 = 120" /> W net, so the job takes <InlineMath math="219\,500 \div 120 = 1830" /> s, about <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes. Every stage slows by the same factor, so freezing is **still** <InlineMath math="76\%" /> of the time.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="29" /> minutes stops at <InlineMath math="0^\circ\text{C}" /> ice and forgets to **cool the ice** to <InlineMath math="-10^\circ\text{C}" />.
- About <InlineMath math="32" /> minutes cools the ice with the specific heat capacity of **water**, which is twice that of ice.
- About <InlineMath math="7.3" /> minutes leaves out the **latent heat**, the largest part of the job.

# The sandbag leaves with the balloon's upward speed, so solve the whole flight at once

At release the sandbag moves **upwards** at <InlineMath math="5.0" /> m/s. Taking up as positive, it reaches the ground when <InlineMath math="-30 = 5.0t - 5t^2" />, which is <InlineMath math="t^2 - t - 6 = 0" />, so <InlineMath math="t = 3.0" /> s.

Its velocity then is <InlineMath math="5.0 - 10 \times 3.0 = -25" /> m/s, so it hits the ground at <InlineMath math="25" /> m/s. Meanwhile the balloon has risen <InlineMath math="5.0 \times 3.0 = 15" /> m more, to <InlineMath math="45" /> m.

**Seen from the balloon**: the bag moves down at <InlineMath math="25" /> m/s while the balloon still rises at <InlineMath math="5.0" /> m/s, so they separate at <InlineMath math="25 + 5.0 = 30" /> m/s. From the balloon the bag simply starts at rest and falls with <InlineMath math="g" /> for <InlineMath math="3.0" /> s.

**The gap at landing**: in the <InlineMath math="3.0" /> s the balloon rises <InlineMath math="15" /> m to <InlineMath math="45" /> m, so the bag lands **<InlineMath math="45" /> metres below** it. From the balloon this is just <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 3.0^2 = 45" /> m of free fall from rest.

**The second sandbag**: it leaves the balloon at <InlineMath math="35" /> m, also moving up at <InlineMath math="5.0" /> m/s. Two seconds later it is at <InlineMath math="35 + 5.0 \times 2.0 - 5 \times 2.0^2 = 25" /> m, because it **starts like the first bag**, only one second later.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="25\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> is the speed **relative to the ground**, not to the rising balloon.
- <InlineMath math="20\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> subtracts the balloon’s speed, but the two move in **opposite directions**.
- <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" /> drops the second bag from rest, forgetting it also starts with the balloon’s **upward speed**.

# The image sits at the focal plane, and the concentration is a ratio of areas

The Sun is effectively at infinity, so its image forms **at the focal plane**, <InlineMath math="0.20" /> m behind the lens. Rays through the centre of the lens are undeviated, so the image and the Sun subtend the same angle: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 1.4 \times 10^9 \div 1.5 \times 10^{11} = 1.87 \times 10^{-3}" /> m, about <InlineMath math="1.9" /> mm.

All the light through a disc <InlineMath math="50" /> mm wide lands on a disc <InlineMath math="1.87" /> mm wide. The widths differ by a factor of <InlineMath math="27" />, so the **areas differ by its square**, about <InlineMath math="720" /> times.

**Power on the paper**: the lens collects <InlineMath math="1000 \times \pi \times 0.025^2 = 1.96" /> W and puts it on a disc of area <InlineMath math="\pi \times (0.933 \times 10^{-3})^2 = 2.74 \times 10^{-6}" /> square metres, giving <InlineMath math="7.2 \times 10^5" /> W per square metre, enough to **char the paper**.

**Heating the spot**: each square metre of paper needs <InlineMath math="0.080 \times 1300 \times 200 = 20\,800" /> J and absorbs <InlineMath math="0.60 \times 7.2 \times 10^5 = 4.3 \times 10^5" /> W, so the spot heats in about <InlineMath math="0.05" /> s, which is why a **lens can burn paper** almost at once.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.7 \times 10^4\text{ W m}^{-2}" /> scales by the ratio of **diameters**, but the light is spread over areas.
- <InlineMath math="1.8 \times 10^5\text{ W m}^{-2}" /> places the image at twice the focal length, which holds only for an object at **twice the focal length**.
- <InlineMath math="2.9 \times 10^6\text{ W m}^{-2}" /> uses the Sun’s **radius** in place of its diameter.

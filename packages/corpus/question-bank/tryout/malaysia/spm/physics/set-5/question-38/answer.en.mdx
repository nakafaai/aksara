# A fourfold treatment time means a quarter of the activity, which is two half-lives

The dose is activity times time, so a treatment four times as long means the activity has fallen to **a quarter**. A quarter is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times \tfrac{1}{2}" />, two half-lives.

Two half-lives take <InlineMath math="2 \times 5.3 = 10.6" /> years, about <InlineMath math="11" /> years. A quarter of the nuclei **remain**, so <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{4}" /> have decayed.

**A longer limit**: going from <InlineMath math="8.0" /> to <InlineMath math="16" /> minutes needs the activity to halve **once more**, so the source lasts one more half-life, <InlineMath math="5.3" /> years.

**The activity at the end**: a <InlineMath math="16" /> minute treatment means the activity is an eighth of new, <InlineMath math="200 \div 8 = 25" /> TBq, after **three half-lives** in all.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="11" /> years longer takes the doubling from eight to sixteen minutes as **two half-lives**.
- <InlineMath math="50\text{ TBq}" /> is the activity at the **eight-minute** limit, not the sixteen-minute one.
- <InlineMath math="100\text{ TBq}" /> halves the new activity only once, but sixteen minutes is **eight times** the first treatment.

# Put the cables in series with the lamp, then size the secondary for the rated current

The secondary gives <InlineMath math="240 \times 60 \div 1200 = 12" /> V. The lamp has <InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" />, so with the cables **in series** the current is <InlineMath math="12 \div 6.5 = 1.85" /> A and the lamp takes <InlineMath math="1.85^2 \times 6.0 = 20" /> W.

At its rating the lamp needs <InlineMath math="2.0" /> A, which needs <InlineMath math="2.0 \times 6.5 = 13" /> V from the secondary. That takes <InlineMath math="1200 \times 13 \div 240 = 65" /> turns, a few more to **make up the cable loss**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="24\text{ W}" /> forgets the **cable resistance** that shares the secondary voltage.
- <InlineMath math="22\text{ W}" /> multiplies the current by the secondary voltage, which includes the **voltage lost in the cables**.
- <InlineMath math="71" /> turns scales the turns with the power, but power goes as the **square of the voltage**.

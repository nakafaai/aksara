# Find the air volume at which the diver just floats, then use Boyle’s law for the pressure

The diver sinks once its **upthrust falls below its weight**, that is once it displaces less than <InlineMath math="6.0" /> cubic centimetres of water. The glass itself always displaces <InlineMath math="2.4" />, so the air must shrink to <InlineMath math="6.0 - 2.4 = 3.6" /> cubic centimetres.

At constant temperature <InlineMath math="pV" /> stays the same: <InlineMath math="100 \times 6.0 = p \times 3.6" /> gives <InlineMath math="p = 167" /> kPa. The squeeze must add <InlineMath math="167 - 100 = 67" /> kPa **above atmospheric**.

**At the bottom**: the water there adds <InlineMath math="1000 \times 9.81 \times 0.30 = 2940" /> Pa, so the air reaches the <InlineMath math="167" /> kPa it needs while the top of the bottle is at only <InlineMath math="167 - 2.9 = 164" /> kPa. The squeeze is <InlineMath math="64" /> kPa, **a little less** than near the top.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="70\text{ kPa}" /> adds the depth to the squeeze, but the water column **helps** compress the air at the bottom.
- <InlineMath math="40\text{ kPa}" /> applies Boyle’s law to the glass and air together, but the **glass does not compress**.
- <InlineMath math="167\text{ kPa}" /> is the full pressure of the air, not the **rise above atmospheric**.

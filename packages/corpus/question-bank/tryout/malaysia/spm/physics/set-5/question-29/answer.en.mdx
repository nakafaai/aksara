# The saving comes from the difference in power, and the payback divides the price by that saving

The LED lamp uses <InlineMath math="51" /> W less. Over a year that is <InlineMath math="0.051 \times 5 \times 365 = 93" /> kilowatt-hours, which costs RM <InlineMath math="93 \times 0.50 = 47" />. The **difference in power** is what the family saves.

The LED lamp pays for itself in <InlineMath math="15 \div 46.5 = 0.32" /> years, about **four months**.

**Over the LED lamp’s life**: in <InlineMath math="15\,000" /> hours it saves <InlineMath math="0.051 \times 15\,000 = 765" /> kilowatt-hours, worth RM <InlineMath math="383" />. It also replaces <InlineMath math="15" /> filament bulbs, worth RM <InlineMath math="45" />. After its own price, the family saves <InlineMath math="383 + 45 - 15 = 413" />, about RM <InlineMath math="410" /> **in all**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About RM <InlineMath math="480" /> counts the bulb’s whole energy as saved, but the LED lamp still uses its own <InlineMath math="9" /> W, so only the **difference** is saved.
- About RM <InlineMath math="370" /> forgets the <InlineMath math="15" /> **filament bulbs** the LED lamp replaces.
- About RM <InlineMath math="430" /> forgets to subtract the **price of the LED lamp**.

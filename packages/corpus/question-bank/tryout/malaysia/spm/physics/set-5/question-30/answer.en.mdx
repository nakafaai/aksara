# Both currents cost volts inside the battery, so solve for the terminal voltage

The headlights have <InlineMath math="12^2 \div 110 = 1.31\,\Omega" />, so at terminal voltage <InlineMath math="V" /> they draw <InlineMath math="V \div 1.31" />. The battery delivers **both currents** through its internal resistance: <InlineMath math="V = 12.6 - 0.020\,(150 + V \div 1.31)" />.

That gives <InlineMath math="1.0153\,V = 9.6" />, so <InlineMath math="V = 9.46" /> V. The headlights give out <InlineMath math="9.46^2 \div 1.31 = 68" /> W, because at a fixed resistance the power goes as the **square of the voltage**.

**Waste inside the battery**: the battery delivers <InlineMath math="150 + 9.46 \div 1.31 = 157" /> A. It produces <InlineMath math="12.6 \times 157 = 1980" /> W, and its internal resistance wastes <InlineMath math="157^2 \times 0.020 = 494" /> W, about <InlineMath math="25\%" />, the same fraction as the **lost volts**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="110\text{ W}" /> ignores the volts lost inside the battery when a **large current** flows.
- <InlineMath math="70\text{ W}" /> and <InlineMath math="24\%" /> leave out the **headlights’ own current**, which also passes through the internal resistance.
- <InlineMath math="87\text{ W}" /> scales the power in proportion to the voltage, but at a fixed resistance it goes as the **square**.

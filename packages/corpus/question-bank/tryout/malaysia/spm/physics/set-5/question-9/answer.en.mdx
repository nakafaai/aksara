# Find the force, give each ball its acceleration, then let both close the gap

<InlineMath math="F = 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 10 \times 10 \div 0.10^2 = 6.7 \times 10^{-7}" /> N. Each <InlineMath math="10" /> kg ball accelerates at <InlineMath math="6.7 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> towards the other.

**Both balls move**, so the gap closes at <InlineMath math="1.33 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. From rest, <InlineMath math="1.0 \times 10^{-3} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1.33 \times 10^{-7} t^2" /> gives <InlineMath math="t = 120" /> s, about <InlineMath math="2.0" /> minutes.

**Closing speed**: each ball has reached <InlineMath math="v = at = 6.67 \times 10^{-8} \times 122 = 8.2 \times 10^{-6}" /> m/s, and they move towards each other, so the gap closes at <InlineMath math="1.6 \times 10^{-5}" /> m/s, **twice** the average closing speed.

**The second millimetre**: with the force still constant, closing <InlineMath math="2.0" /> mm takes <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2}" /> times as long, <InlineMath math="122 \times 1.41 = 173" /> s, so the second millimetre takes only <InlineMath math="173 - 122 = 51" /> s, because the balls are **already moving**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="2.0" /> minutes repeats the first millimetre, but the balls start the second one **already moving**.
- <InlineMath math="8.2 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> is the **average** closing speed, or the speed of one ball.
- About <InlineMath math="61\text{ s}" /> keeps the closing speed fixed, but the balls **keep accelerating**.

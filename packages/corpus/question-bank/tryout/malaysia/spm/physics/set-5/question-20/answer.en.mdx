# Shift both distances to the lens, then use a virtual image at the near point

The lens sits <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cm in front of the eye, so the page is <InlineMath math="23" /> cm and her near point <InlineMath math="73" /> cm **from the lens**. The image must be virtual, on the same side as the page.

Then <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{f} = \tfrac{1}{23} - \tfrac{1}{73}" />, giving <InlineMath math="f = 33.6" /> cm. The focal length is positive, so the lens is **converging**, with power <InlineMath math="100 \div 33.6 = +3.0" /> dioptres.

**How large the print looks**: the image is <InlineMath math="73 \div 23 = 3.2" /> times the size of the print, and it sits at her near point, exactly where she would otherwise have to hold the page. So the print looks **about three times larger**.

**The farthest page**: her eye can relax to focus on very distant objects, so the farthest page is the one whose image is **at infinity**, a page at the focal point, <InlineMath math="33.6" /> cm from the lens. That is <InlineMath math="33.6 + 2.0 = 36" /> cm from her eye.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="+2.7\text{ D}" /> and <InlineMath math="38\text{ cm}" /> measure from the eye and ignore the **gap to the lens**.
- <InlineMath math="34\text{ cm}" /> stops at the focal point and forgets to add the **gap to the eye**.
- <InlineMath math="75\text{ cm}" /> is her near point without glasses, but through the lens a page beyond the focal point gives a **real image** she cannot use.

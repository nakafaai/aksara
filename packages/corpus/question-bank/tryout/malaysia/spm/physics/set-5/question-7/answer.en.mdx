Up the slope positive, <InlineMath math="s = 6.0 \times 6.0 - \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1.5 \times 6.0^2 = 9.0" /> m: the cart ends <InlineMath math="9.0" /> m up the slope.

But it **turned round** on the way. It stops after <InlineMath math="6.0 \div 1.5 = 4.0" /> s, at <InlineMath math="6.0^2 \div (2 \times 1.5) = 12" /> m, and rolls back <InlineMath math="12 - 9.0 = 3.0" /> m in the last <InlineMath math="2.0" /> s. It has travelled <InlineMath math="12 + 3.0 = 15" /> m.

**Averages over six seconds**: average speed uses the **distance travelled**, <InlineMath math="15 \div 6.0 = 2.5" /> m/s, while average velocity uses the displacement, <InlineMath math="9.0 \div 6.0 = 1.5" /> m/s up the slope.

**Back at the start**: the displacement <InlineMath math="6.0t - 0.75t^2" /> is zero again at <InlineMath math="t = 8.0" /> s, when <InlineMath math="v = 6.0 - 1.5 \times 8.0 = -6.0" /> m/s. On a smooth slope the cart returns with **its starting speed**, now heading down.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> uses the displacement for the speed, ignoring the **trip back down**.
- Back after <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ s}" /> mistakes the time of the question for the **return time**, when the cart is still <InlineMath math="9.0" /> m up.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> forgets the cart **started at** <InlineMath math="6.0" /> m/s up the slope.

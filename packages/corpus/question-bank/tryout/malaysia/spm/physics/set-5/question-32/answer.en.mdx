Its moment about the pivot is <InlineMath math="0.030 \times 0.20 = 0.0060" /> N m. The rider sits **half as far** from the pivot, so its weight must be <InlineMath math="0.060" /> N, a mass of <InlineMath math="0.060 \div 9.81 = 6.1" /> g.

**The weaker field**: the rider still needs a moment of <InlineMath math="0.0060" /> N m, so the force must stay <InlineMath math="0.030" /> N. With <InlineMath math="0.20" /> T, <InlineMath math="I = 0.030 \div (0.20 \times 0.050) = 3.0" /> A, **more current** to make up for the weaker field.

**Moving the rider instead**: at <InlineMath math="2.0" /> A in <InlineMath math="0.20" /> T the force is <InlineMath math="0.020" /> N, a moment of <InlineMath math="0.0040" /> N m. The rider’s <InlineMath math="0.060" /> N must balance it at <InlineMath math="0.0040 \div 0.060 = 0.067" /> m, **closer to the pivot**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.3\text{ A}" /> lowers the current with the field, but a weaker field needs **more current** for the same force.
- <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" /> moves the rider outwards, but a smaller magnetic moment needs the rider **nearer the pivot**.
- <InlineMath math="4.4\text{ cm}" /> scales the distance with the square of the field, but the force goes as the field **itself**.

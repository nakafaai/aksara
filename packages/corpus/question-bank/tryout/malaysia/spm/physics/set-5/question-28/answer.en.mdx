# Resistance per metre first, then the heater's total, then double it for each parallel strand

The radius is <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mm, so the cross-section is <InlineMath math="1.26 \times 10^{-7}" /> square metres and **each metre** has <InlineMath math="8.75\,\Omega" />. The resistor needs <InlineMath math="10 \div 8.75 = 1.1" /> m.

The heater draws <InlineMath math="1000" /> W at <InlineMath math="240" /> V, so its element is <InlineMath math="240^2 \div 1000 = 57.6\,\Omega" />. Two equal strands in parallel halve the resistance, so **each strand** must be <InlineMath math="115\,\Omega" />, which is <InlineMath math="13" /> m of wire.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.6\text{ m}" /> makes the whole element from one strand and ignores the **parallel pair**.
- <InlineMath math="4.6\text{ m}" /> uses the **diameter as the radius**, which makes the area four times too large.
- <InlineMath math="3.3\text{ m}" /> halves the strand's resistance, but each of two parallel strands needs **twice** the total.

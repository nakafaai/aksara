# Current flows only while the supply beats the battery and the diode together

The peak is <InlineMath math="12\sqrt{2} = 17.0" /> V. Current flows only while the supply exceeds <InlineMath math="12.0 + 0.7 = 12.7" /> V, which starts when <InlineMath math="17.0 \sin\theta = 12.7" />, at <InlineMath math="\theta = 48.5^\circ" />.

By symmetry it stops at <InlineMath math="131.5^\circ" />, so it flows for <InlineMath math="83^\circ" /> of the <InlineMath math="360^\circ" /> cycle, about <InlineMath math="23\%" />. The diode **blocks the whole negative half**.

**The peak current**: the resistance does not change when current starts, because at that moment no current flows. At the peak the supply exceeds <InlineMath math="12.7" /> V by <InlineMath math="17.0 - 12.7 = 4.3" /> V, which drives <InlineMath math="4.3 \div 0.50 = 8.5" /> A through the **total resistance**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="46\%" /> compares the conducting angle with **half a cycle** instead of a whole cycle.
- Never charging confuses the **r.m.s. value** with the peak, which is larger by a factor of the square root of two.
- <InlineMath math="25\%" /> and <InlineMath math="9.9\text{ A}" /> ignore the **diode’s forward voltage**.

# The spots are antinodes, so double their spacing, then turn the power into photons

Neighbouring antinodes are **half a wavelength** apart, so the wavelength is <InlineMath math="12" /> cm and the frequency is <InlineMath math="3.0 \times 10^8 \div 0.12 = 2.5 \times 10^9" /> Hz.

Each photon carries <InlineMath math="hf = 1.66 \times 10^{-24}" /> J, a tiny amount, so <InlineMath math="800" /> W needs <InlineMath math="800 \div 1.66 \times 10^{-24} = 4.8 \times 10^{26}" /> photons **every second**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ GHz}" /> takes the spot spacing as a **whole wavelength**.
- <InlineMath math="1.25\text{ GHz}" /> doubles the spacing **twice**, taking the wavelength as four spot gaps.
- <InlineMath math="4.8 \times 10^{23}" /> photons uses the power in **kilowatts** instead of watts.

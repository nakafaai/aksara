# Start at the brain and add the pressure of each column of blood going down

The heart must supply the brain's <InlineMath math="10" /> kPa **plus the column of blood** above it: <InlineMath math="10\,000 + 1050 \times 9.81 \times 2.5 = 35\,800" /> Pa, about <InlineMath math="36" /> kPa.

The feet lie a further <InlineMath math="2.1" /> m down, so pressure **keeps rising**: <InlineMath math="35\,800 + 1050 \times 9.81 \times 2.1 = 57\,400" /> Pa, about <InlineMath math="57" /> kPa.

**Head down to drink**: the brain is now below the heart, so the column of blood **adds** pressure: <InlineMath math="35\,800 + 1050 \times 9.81 \times 1.5 = 51\,200" /> Pa, about <InlineMath math="51" /> kPa, five times what the brain needs.

**The valves**: with its head down the brain would see about <InlineMath math="51" /> kPa, so the valves must take away <InlineMath math="51 - 20 = 31" /> kPa, which is why a giraffe needs **special valves** in its neck.

**Lifting the head**: the heart briefly gives only <InlineMath math="0.80 \times 35.8 = 28.6" /> kPa, and the <InlineMath math="2.5" /> m column takes <InlineMath math="25.8" /> kPa of it, leaving about <InlineMath math="3" /> kPa at the brain, **well under what it needs**, which is why a giraffe can faint if it lifts its head too fast.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="21\text{ kPa}" /> lifts the blood to the brain but forgets the <InlineMath math="10" /> kPa the **brain still needs**, so the heart pressure is too low.
- No reduction treats the brain as still above the heart, but lowering the head **adds** a column of blood.
- <InlineMath math="41\text{ kPa}" /> and <InlineMath math="8\text{ kPa}" /> take the cut off the brain’s pressure, but the <InlineMath math="20\%" /> drop is in the **heart’s** pressure.

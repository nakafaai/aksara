# Solve for the virtual image, then reverse the lens formula for the new size

With the face inside the focal length, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{v} = \tfrac{1}{30} - \tfrac{1}{20} = -\tfrac{1}{60}" />, so the image is **virtual**, <InlineMath math="60" /> cm behind the mirror, and the magnification is <InlineMath math="60 \div 20 = 3" />.

An upright image four times larger needs <InlineMath math="v = -4u" />. Then <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{u} - \tfrac{1}{4u} = \tfrac{1}{30}" />, so <InlineMath math="u = 22.5" /> cm: he must **move back**, towards the focal point.

**How far the image moves**: at <InlineMath math="20" /> cm the image is <InlineMath math="60" /> cm behind the mirror. At <InlineMath math="22.5" /> cm it is <InlineMath math="4 \times 22.5 = 90" /> cm behind, so a small step back of <InlineMath math="2.5" /> cm sends the image **thirty centimetres further back**.

**Distance from his eyes**: his eyes are <InlineMath math="22.5" /> cm in front of the mirror and the image <InlineMath math="90" /> cm behind it, so the image is <InlineMath math="22.5 + 90 = 113" /> cm from his eyes, **both distances added** across the mirror.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> closer comes from moving to <InlineMath math="15" /> cm, but inside the focal length a **nearer face** gives a smaller image.
- <InlineMath math="90\text{ cm}" /> from his eyes forgets the <InlineMath math="22.5" /> cm between his **face and the mirror**.
- <InlineMath math="150\text{ cm}" /> in front comes from a real, **inverted image**, which he cannot use to shave.

# The voltages add in series, but every cell carries the whole current

The two cells in series give <InlineMath math="3.0" /> V, so the bulb draws <InlineMath math="0.90 \div 3.0 = 0.30" /> A.

The **same current** flows through each cell, so each one gives up charge at <InlineMath math="0.30" /> A. A <InlineMath math="2.0" /> ampere-hour cell lasts <InlineMath math="2.0 \div 0.30 = 6.7" /> hours, and both cells run flat together.

**The price of the energy**: each cell stores <InlineMath math="2.0 \times 3600 \times 1.5 = 10\,800" /> J, so the pair holds <InlineMath math="21\,600" /> J, which is <InlineMath math="0.0060" /> kilowatt-hours. At RM <InlineMath math="5.00" /> that is about RM <InlineMath math="830" /> per kilowatt-hour, more than **a thousand times** the price of mains electricity.

**The rechargeable pack**: one charge draws <InlineMath math="0.0060 \div 0.80 = 0.0075" /> kilowatt-hours, costing under one sen, so each charge saves almost the full RM <InlineMath math="5.00" /> of a new pair of cells. The pack pays for itself after <InlineMath math="30 \div 5.00 = 6" /> charges, because the **mains energy is almost free** by comparison.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12" /> charges counts only one cell’s price saved each time, but a charge replaces **both cells**.
- RM <InlineMath math="420" /> per kWh adds the capacities, which holds only for cells in **parallel**.
- RM <InlineMath math="1700" /> per kWh counts the energy of one cell but the **price of both**.

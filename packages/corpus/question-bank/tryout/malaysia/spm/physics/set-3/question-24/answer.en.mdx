# The trapped air pushes down on the mercury

**The column is short because the trapped air presses on it**: <InlineMath math="p_{\text{air}}+h=76\text{ cmHg}" />, so at first the air is at <InlineMath math="76-72=4\text{ cmHg}" />.

# Raising the tube gives the air more room

**If the column becomes <InlineMath math="h" />, the tube above the reservoir is <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> longer, so the air's length is <InlineMath math="8.0+10+(72-h)" />**. Boyle's law links its pressure and length.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(76-h)(90-h)&=4\times8.0=32\\
h=74\text{ cm}:&\quad (2)(16)=32
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="72.0\text{ cm}" /> **treats the barometer as if it had a vacuum above the mercury, where raising the tube changes nothing**.
- <InlineMath math="82.0\text{ cm}" /> lets the column rise with the tube, as if the air kept its pressure.
- <InlineMath math="76.0\text{ cm}" /> would need the air's pressure to fall to zero.

# Halve each echo time, then subtract the two depths

An echo travels **down and back**, so each distance uses half the time. Seabed: <InlineMath math="1500 \times 0.40 = 600" /> m. Shoal: <InlineMath math="1500 \times 0.15 = 225" /> m below the ship.

The shoal sits <InlineMath math="600 - 225 = 375" /> m above the seabed. Wavelength: <InlineMath math="\lambda = v \div f = 1500 \div 50\,000 = 0.030" /> m, or <InlineMath math="3.0" /> cm.

**The moving shoal**: the extra <InlineMath math="0.04" /> s is a round trip, so the shoal is <InlineMath math="1500 \times 0.04 \div 2 = 30" /> m deeper than before. Over <InlineMath math="10" /> s that is an average of <InlineMath math="3.0" /> m/s downwards.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="225\text{ m}" /> is the shoal's depth **below the surface**, not its height above the seabed.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> forgets that the extra <InlineMath math="0.04" /> s covers the extra distance twice, down and back.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}" /> divides the speed by <InlineMath math="50" /> Hz instead of <InlineMath math="50\,000" /> Hz.

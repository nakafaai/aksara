# Impulse first cancels the incoming momentum, then reverses it

Impulse is the **area under the force-time graph**: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 600 \times 0.030 = 9.0\text{ N s}" />, away from the wall.

Take away from the wall as positive. Momentum before: <InlineMath math="-0.40 \times 15 = -6.0\text{ kg m s}^{-1}" />. After: <InlineMath math="-6.0 + 9.0 = +3.0\text{ kg m s}^{-1}" />, so <InlineMath math="v = 3.0 \div 0.40 = 7.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> away from the wall.

Kinetic energy before: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.40 \times 15^2 = 45" /> J. After: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.40 \times 7.5^2 = 11.25" /> J. Half the speed keeps only a quarter of the energy, so <InlineMath math="75\%" /> is lost.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="22.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> adds the impulse in the **same direction** as the incoming momentum instead of against it.
- <InlineMath math="50\%" /> uses the ratio of speeds, but kinetic energy goes as the **square** of the speed.
- <InlineMath math="15\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> would need an impulse of <InlineMath math="12\text{ N s}" />, twice the incoming momentum, not the <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ N s}" /> the graph gives.

# Correct the balance, but let the parallax cancel

The balance reads <InlineMath math="0.3" /> g with nothing on it, a **zero error** of <InlineMath math="+0.3" /> g, so the stone's mass is <InlineMath math="41.9 - 0.3 = 41.6" /> g.

The volume comes from a **difference** of two readings. Both are <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ cm}^3" /> too high, so the error cancels: <InlineMath math="58.5 - 42.5 = 16.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

Density: <InlineMath math="41.6 \div 16.0 = 2.60\text{ g cm}^{-3}" />.

**The liquid**: the stone weighs <InlineMath math="0.0416 \times 10 = 0.416" /> N, so the upthrust is the loss in the reading, <InlineMath math="0.416 - 0.250 = 0.166" /> N. It pushes aside its own <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ cm}^3 = 1.60 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="\rho = 0.166 \div (1.60 \times 10^{-5} \times 10) = 1040\text{ kg m}^{-3}" />, or <InlineMath math="1.04\text{ g cm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.06\text{ g cm}^{-3}" /> with the right stone density takes the weight from <InlineMath math="41.9" /> g, carrying the balance's **zero error** into the upthrust.
- <InlineMath math="2.62\text{ g cm}^{-3}" /> forgets the zero error in the density as well as in the weight.
- <InlineMath math="1.56\text{ g cm}^{-3}" /> treats the reading in the liquid as the upthrust, but the upthrust is the **loss** of weight.

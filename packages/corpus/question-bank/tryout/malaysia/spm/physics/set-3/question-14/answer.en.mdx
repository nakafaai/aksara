# The source fixes the frequency, the depth fixes the speed

Deep ocean: <InlineMath math="v = \sqrt{10 \times 4000} = 200\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, and with a period of <InlineMath math="900" /> s the wavelength is <InlineMath math="200 \times 900 = 180\,000" /> m, or <InlineMath math="180" /> km.

The frequency is set by the source and **does not change** when the wave enters shallower water. Near the coast <InlineMath math="v = \sqrt{10 \times 10} = 10\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, so <InlineMath math="\lambda = 10 \times 900 = 9.0" /> km. The speed falls by a factor of <InlineMath math="20" />, the square root of the <InlineMath math="400" /> times fall in depth, and so does the wavelength.

**Crossing time**: the deep ocean takes <InlineMath math="1\,800\,000 \div 200 = 9000" /> s. On the shelf the speed is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{10 \times 40} = 20" /> m/s, so its <InlineMath math="30" /> km take another <InlineMath math="1500" /> s. In all <InlineMath math="10\,500" /> s, about <InlineMath math="2.9" /> h: the short, shallow shelf adds almost half an hour.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The frequency is set by the **source** of the waves, so it cannot change, and the wavelength must change with the speed.
- <InlineMath math="0.45\text{ km}" /> scales the wavelength with the depth, but the speed goes as the square root of the depth.
- <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ h}" /> counts only the deep ocean and leaves out the slow crossing of the shelf.

# Energy of one photon, then power divided by that energy

One photon carries <InlineMath math="E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^8}{650 \times 10^{-9}} = 3.06 \times 10^{-19}" /> J. The power is <InlineMath math="5.0 \times 10^{-3}" /> J each second, so the laser emits <InlineMath math="5.0 \times 10^{-3} \div 3.06 \times 10^{-19} = 1.6 \times 10^{16}" /> photons each second.

A green photon has a shorter wavelength and so **more energy**, <InlineMath math="3.83 \times 10^{-19}" /> J. The same <InlineMath math="5.0" /> mW then buys only <InlineMath math="1.3 \times 10^{16}" /> photons each second.

**The metal surface**: a red photon carries <InlineMath math="3.06 \times 10^{-19}" /> J, less than the <InlineMath math="3.2 \times 10^{-19}" /> J needed, so no electron leaves however many photons arrive. A green photon carries <InlineMath math="3.83 \times 10^{-19}" /> J, so electrons leave with at most <InlineMath math="(3.83 - 3.2) \times 10^{-19} = 6.3 \times 10^{-20}" /> J.

# Why the other options are wrong

- More photons cannot make up for too little energy in each one: emission depends on the energy of **a single photon**.
- <InlineMath math="1.6 \times 10^{19}" /> uses <InlineMath math="5.0" /> W, leaving out the milli in milliwatts.
- <InlineMath math="3.8 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}" /> is the whole photon energy, but the work function must be paid first.

# Metres and kilograms first, then the energy chain

**Convert first**: <InlineMath math="x = 0.20" /> m and <InlineMath math="m = 0.050" /> kg. Stored energy: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 400 \times 0.20^2 = 8.0" /> J.

A quarter is lost, so <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{4} \times 8.0 = 6.0" /> J becomes gravitational potential energy: <InlineMath math="h = 6.0 \div (0.050 \times 10) = 12" /> m.

**Time in the air**: the <InlineMath math="6.0" /> J launches the stone at <InlineMath math="v = \sqrt{2 \times 6.0 \div 0.050} = 15.5" /> m/s. It rises for <InlineMath math="15.5 \div 10 = 1.55" /> s and takes as long to fall back, <InlineMath math="3.1" /> s in all.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.6\text{ s}" /> let **all** the stored energy launch the stone.
- <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ s}" /> keep only the quarter that was lost.
- <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ s}" /> is only the time going up, but the stone must also fall back down.

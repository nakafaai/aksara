# Only the forward parts of the pulls beat the drag

Resolve each pull. The sideways parts, <InlineMath math="800 \sin 30^\circ" /> each way, **cancel**. The forward parts add: <InlineMath math="2 \times 800 \times 0.866 = 1386" /> N.

Net force: <InlineMath math="1386 - 1000 = 386" /> N, so <InlineMath math="a = 386 \div 400 = 0.96\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. From rest, after <InlineMath math="5.0" /> s, <InlineMath math="v = 0.96 \times 5.0 = 4.8\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

**Coasting**: once the ropes are released only the <InlineMath math="1000" /> N resistance acts, a deceleration of <InlineMath math="1000 \div 400 = 2.5\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. The boat stops in <InlineMath math="4.8^2 \div (2 \times 2.5) = 4.6" /> m.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> adds the full <InlineMath math="1600" /> N as if both ropes pulled **straight ahead**.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}" /> keeps the old <InlineMath math="0.96\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> for the coasting, but without the ropes the resistance alone slows the boat.
- <InlineMath math="3.5\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> resolves the pulls correctly but leaves out the resistance while the ropes pull.

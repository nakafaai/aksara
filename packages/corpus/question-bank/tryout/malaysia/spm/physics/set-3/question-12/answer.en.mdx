# Use absolute pressure and absolute temperature

**The gas laws hold for the pressure of the gas itself, not for the gauge reading**, and for temperature in kelvin.

<BlockMath math="p_1=200+100=300\text{ kPa},\qquad T_1=300\text{ K},\qquad T_2=330\text{ K}" />

# Combine the temperature rise and the expansion

<BlockMath math="p_2=p_1\times\frac{T_2}{T_1}\times\frac{V_1}{V_2}=300\times\frac{330}{300}\times\frac{1}{1.02}=324\text{ kPa}" />

**The gauge reads <InlineMath math="324-100=224\text{ kPa}" />**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="216\text{ kPa}" /> **applies the gas law to the gauge reading as if it were the gas pressure**.
- <InlineMath math="230\text{ kPa}" /> ignores the <InlineMath math="2\%" /> growth in volume.
- <InlineMath math="422\text{ kPa}" /> uses degrees Celsius in the ratio.

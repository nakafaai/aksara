# Kepler's third law with the Moon as reference

**Both orbit the same Earth, so <InlineMath math="r^3\propto T^2" />, which gives <InlineMath math="r\propto T^{2/3}" />**.

<BlockMath math="r=3.84\times10^5\times\left(\frac{1}{27.3}\right)^{2/3}=3.84\times10^5\times0.110=4.23\times10^4\text{ km}" />

# Radius is not height

**The law gives the distance from the Earth's centre**, so subtract the Earth's radius.

<BlockMath math="h=42\,300-6370\approx36\,000\text{ km}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="42\,000\text{ km}" /> **is the orbit radius, measured from the centre**.
- <InlineMath math="14\,000\text{ km}" /> scales the radius in proportion to the period.
- <InlineMath math="7\,700\text{ km}" /> scales in proportion to the period and then subtracts the Earth's radius.

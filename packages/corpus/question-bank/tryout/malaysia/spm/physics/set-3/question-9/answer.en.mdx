# Seconds in the period, the orbit radius in the mass, the Earth's radius in the volume

The Moon's mass cancels: <InlineMath math="M = \dfrac{4\pi^2 r^3}{GT^2}" />. The period **must be in seconds**: <InlineMath math="27.3 \times 86\,400 = 2.36 \times 10^6" /> s. Then <InlineMath math="M = \dfrac{4\pi^2 (3.84 \times 10^8)^3}{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times (2.36 \times 10^6)^2} = 6.0 \times 10^{24}" /> kg.

For the density, use the Earth's own radius: <InlineMath math="V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi (6.4 \times 10^6)^3 = 1.10 \times 10^{21}\text{ m}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="\rho = 6.0 \times 10^{24} \div 1.10 \times 10^{21} = 5.5 \times 10^3\text{ kg m}^{-3}" />, about twice the density of surface rock.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.5 \times 10^{34}\text{ kg}" /> puts the period in **days**, which makes the mass <InlineMath math="86\,400^2" /> times too large.
- <InlineMath math="9.6 \times 10^{23}\text{ kg}" /> uses <InlineMath math="2\pi" /> instead of <InlineMath math="4\pi^2" />.
- <InlineMath math="7.3 \times 10^{3}" /> leaves out the <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4}{3}" /> in the volume of a sphere.

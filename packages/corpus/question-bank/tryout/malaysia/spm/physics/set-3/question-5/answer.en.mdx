# Equal momenta, unequal energies

The system starts at rest, so **total momentum stays zero**: <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 3.0 = 1.5 v" />, and the heavier block moves off at <InlineMath math="v = 1.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> the other way.

On a smooth ramp all the kinetic energy becomes potential energy, <InlineMath math="h = v^2 \div 2g" />.

Lighter block: <InlineMath math="3.0^2 \div 20 = 0.45" /> m, or <InlineMath math="45" /> cm. Heavier block: <InlineMath math="1.0^2 \div 20 = 0.05" /> m, or <InlineMath math="5" /> cm. Three times the speed gives **nine times** the height.

**One block clamped**: the spring stored <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}(0.50)(3.0)^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}(1.5)(1.0)^2 = 2.25 + 0.75 = 3.0" /> J. With the heavier block fixed, all <InlineMath math="3.0" /> J goes to the lighter block, which rises <InlineMath math="3.0 \div (0.50 \times 10) = 0.60" /> m, higher than before.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The lighter block does not keep the same speed: with the other block fixed it receives **all** the stored energy.
- <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}" /> adds the two heights, but the energy is <InlineMath math="mgh" /> with each block's own mass.
- <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" /> divides the height by three, as if height followed the speed, but it follows the speed squared.

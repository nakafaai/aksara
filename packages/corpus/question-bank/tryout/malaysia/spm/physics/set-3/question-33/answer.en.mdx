# Find the coil current at each light level, then reshape it with a parallel resistor

At dusk the current is <InlineMath math="6.0 \div (50 + 30) = 0.075" /> A, below <InlineMath math="0.10" /> A. The armature is **released**, so the normally closed contacts already light the lamp.

To hold the armature at dusk, the LDR branch may be at most <InlineMath math="60 - 50 = 10\,\Omega" />. A <InlineMath math="12\,\Omega" /> resistor in parallel with <InlineMath math="30\,\Omega" /> gives <InlineMath math="8.6\,\Omega" />, the current is <InlineMath math="0.102" /> A and the lamp stays off. In darkness the pair is <InlineMath math="12 \parallel 200 = 11.3\,\Omega" />, the current falls to <InlineMath math="0.098" /> A, the armature drops and the lamp lights.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The dusk current is only <InlineMath math="0.075" /> A, so the armature is already released and the lamp is **already lit**.
- A resistor in series with the LDR raises the resistance and lowers the current further, so the lamp would light even earlier.
- <InlineMath math="60\,\Omega" /> in parallel with <InlineMath math="30\,\Omega" /> is <InlineMath math="20\,\Omega" />, giving <InlineMath math="0.086" /> A at dusk, still too small to hold the armature.

Cutting the string removes the pull, but the mass **is still moving upwards**. Now only gravity acts, so it rises a further <InlineMath math="2.0^2 \div (2 \times 10) = 0.20" /> m before it stops. Greatest height: <InlineMath math="1.0 + 0.20 = 1.2" /> m.

**Back past the start**: after the cut the mass rises for <InlineMath math="2 \div 10 = 0.2" /> s to its highest point, <InlineMath math="1.2" /> m up, and then falls from rest: <InlineMath math="1.2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times t^2" /> gives <InlineMath math="t = 0.49" /> s. In all <InlineMath math="1.0 + 0.2 + 0.49 = 1.7" /> s after the release.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ m}" /> stops the mass the moment the string is cut, but it **still has upward speed**.
- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ s}" /> drops the mass from <InlineMath math="1.2" /> m at the moment of the cut, forgetting the <InlineMath math="0.2" /> s it still spends rising.
- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ s}" /> is only the time to the highest point, not back down past the start.

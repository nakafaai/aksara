# The push also presses the block into the slope

A horizontal push has a part along the slope, <InlineMath math="F\cos30^\circ" />, and a part into it, <InlineMath math="F\sin30^\circ" />. **That second part raises the normal force, and with it the friction**.

<BlockMath math="N=mg\cos30^\circ+F\sin30^\circ=17.32+0.5F,\qquad f=0.20N=3.46+0.10F" />

# Balance along the slope

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
F\cos30^\circ&=mg\sin30^\circ+f\\
0.866F&=10.0+3.46+0.10F\ \Rightarrow\ F=\frac{13.46}{0.766}=17.6\text{ N}
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="15.5\text{ N}" /> **takes the friction from the weight alone and forgets the push's part into the slope**.
- <InlineMath math="13.5\text{ N}" /> is the force needed if the push ran along the slope.
- <InlineMath math="11.5\text{ N}" /> ignores friction.

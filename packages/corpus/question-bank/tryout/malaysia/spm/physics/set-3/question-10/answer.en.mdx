Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of <InlineMath math="2000" /> W reaches the water, so the **useful power is <InlineMath math="1600" /> W**. Time: <InlineMath math="811\,500 \div 1600 = 507" /> s, about <InlineMath math="8.5" /> minutes.

**On a lower voltage**: the element keeps its resistance, <InlineMath math="R = 240^2 \div 2000 = 28.8\,\Omega" />, so on <InlineMath math="220" /> V it gives only <InlineMath math="220^2 \div 28.8 = 1681" /> W. The water still needs <InlineMath math="811.5 \div 0.80 = 1014" /> kJ of electrical energy, which takes <InlineMath math="1\,014\,000 \div 1681 = 604" /> s.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="406\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="483\text{ s}" /> forget that only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of the energy **reaches the water**.
- <InlineMath math="553\text{ s}" /> lowers the power in step with the voltage, but with a fixed resistance the power goes as the voltage squared.
- An unchanged <InlineMath math="507\text{ s}" /> treats the <InlineMath math="2.0" /> kW rating as fixed, but it holds only on <InlineMath math="240" /> V.

# Speed and period in a circular orbit

**Gravity provides the centripetal force, so <InlineMath math="v=\sqrt{GM/r}" /> and <InlineMath math="T=2\pi r/v\propto r^{3/2}" />**. A larger <InlineMath math="r" /> means a smaller speed and a longer period, so I and II are correct.

# Energy and the bursts

A smaller speed means less kinetic energy, so III is wrong. **The engine's work goes into gravitational potential energy, which grows by more than the kinetic energy falls**.

The first burst does speed the satellite up, onto a path that climbs away from the planet. As it climbs, gravity slows it, so it reaches the higher orbit slower than it started, and IV is correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only **misses IV, the speed-up that starts the climb**.
- Options with III assume the work done must raise the kinetic energy.

# Rebuild the resistance, add the internal resistance, then find the power

Resistance is proportional to length, so each quarter is <InlineMath math="2.0\,\Omega" />. Each pair in series is <InlineMath math="4.0\,\Omega" />, and two equal <InlineMath math="4.0\,\Omega" /> branches **in parallel** give <InlineMath math="2.0\,\Omega" />.

Current: <InlineMath math="I = 6.0 \div (2.0 + 1.0) = 2.0" /> A. Power in the network: <InlineMath math="I^2 R = 2.0^2 \times 2.0 = 8.0" /> W. The other <InlineMath math="4.0" /> W is wasted inside the cell.

**A broken piece**: the pair with the broken piece carries nothing, so the network is a single <InlineMath math="4.0\,\Omega" /> branch. Then <InlineMath math="I = 6.0 \div (4.0 + 1.0) = 1.2" /> A and the network takes <InlineMath math="1.2^2 \times 4.0 = 5.8" /> W. A break raises the resistance, so the current and the power both fall.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ A}" /> after the break forgets the cell's **internal resistance**, which still takes its share of the e.m.f.
- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ A}" /> forgets the internal resistance from the start.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ W}" /> and <InlineMath math="7.2\text{ W}" /> are the total power from the e.m.f., <InlineMath math="EI" />, which includes the loss inside the cell.

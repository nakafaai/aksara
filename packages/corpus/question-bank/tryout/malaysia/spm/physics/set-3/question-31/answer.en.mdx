# Milliseconds to seconds, then energy to time, then seconds to days

The time must be **in seconds**, <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 10^{-3}" /> s, so <InlineMath math="I = Q \div t = 20 \div 5.0 \times 10^{-4} = 4.0 \times 10^4" /> A.

Energy: <InlineMath math="E = QV = 20 \times 1.0 \times 10^8 = 2.0 \times 10^9" /> J. A <InlineMath math="2000" /> W heater uses that in <InlineMath math="2.0 \times 10^9 \div 2000 = 1.0 \times 10^6" /> s.

A day is <InlineMath math="86\,400" /> s, so <InlineMath math="1.0 \times 10^6" /> s is about <InlineMath math="12" /> days.

**Boiling pond water**: only <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 2.0 \times 10^9 = 1.0 \times 10^8" /> J reaches the water. Each kilogram needs <InlineMath math="4200 \times 70 = 2.94 \times 10^5" /> J to reach <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" /> and <InlineMath math="2.26 \times 10^6" /> J to vaporise, <InlineMath math="2.55 \times 10^6" /> J in all. So <InlineMath math="1.0 \times 10^8 \div 2.55 \times 10^6 \approx 39" /> kg.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40\text{ A}" /> leaves the time in **milliseconds**.
- <InlineMath math="44\text{ kg}" /> uses only the latent heat and forgets the water must first be heated from <InlineMath math="30^\circ\text{C}" /> to <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" />.
- About <InlineMath math="6" /> days and <InlineMath math="20\text{ kg}" /> use <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}QV" />, but the charge falls through the whole potential difference, so the energy is <InlineMath math="QV" />.

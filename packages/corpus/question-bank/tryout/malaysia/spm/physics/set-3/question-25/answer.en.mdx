# Upthrust from the cold air, weight of the hot air and everything else

Upthrust is the weight of the **displaced cold air**: <InlineMath math="1.20 \times 2000 \times 10 = 24\,000" /> N. The hot air inside weighs <InlineMath math="0.90 \times 2000 \times 10 = 18\,000" /> N, so <InlineMath math="6000" /> N is left to hold everything else.

The envelope, basket and burner weigh <InlineMath math="4000" /> N, leaving <InlineMath math="2000" /> N for the load: a mass of <InlineMath math="200" /> kg.

**Accelerating upwards**: the upthrust stays <InlineMath math="2000 \times 1.20 \times 10 = 24\,000" /> N. The moving mass is <InlineMath math="600" /> kg plus the hot air, <InlineMath math="2000\rho" />, so <InlineMath math="24\,000 - (600 + 2000\rho) \times 10 = (600 + 2000\rho) \times 0.50" />. That gives <InlineMath math="17\,700 = 21\,000\rho" /> and <InlineMath math="\rho = 0.84\text{ kg m}^{-3}" />: the hot air must be accelerated too.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="600\text{ kg}" /> spends the whole net lift on the load and **forgets the balloon's own** <InlineMath math="400" /> kg.
- At <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ kg m}^{-3}" /> the forces only balance, so there is no net force to start the balloon moving.
- <InlineMath math="0.89\text{ kg m}^{-3}" /> leaves the hot air out of the mass being accelerated.

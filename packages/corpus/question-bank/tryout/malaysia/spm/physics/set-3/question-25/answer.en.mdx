# Floating means upthrust equals the whole weight

**A floating body is in equilibrium, so the upthrust equals the weight of the ice and the stone**. I is correct.

# What the stone does when the ice melts

While floating, the stone's weight is carried by displaced water, which is more water than the stone's own volume because the stone is denser than water. **Once the ice melts, the stone sinks and displaces only its own volume**, so the level falls. II is correct.

# The other two cases

An air bubble has almost no weight, so the cube behaves like pure ice: the meltwater exactly fills the hole the ice made, and the level stays the same. III is correct.

Pure ice in salt water floats on less displaced liquid than its meltwater needs, because salt water is denser than fresh water. **The meltwater takes up more room than the salt water it displaced**, so the level rises. IV is wrong.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only **misses the air-bubble case in III**.
- Options with IV forget that meltwater is less dense than salt water.

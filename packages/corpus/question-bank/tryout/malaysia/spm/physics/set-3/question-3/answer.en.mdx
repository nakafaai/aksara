# The reaction distance eats into the braking room

At <InlineMath math="20\text{ m s}^{-1}" />: thinking distance <InlineMath math="20 \times 0.60 = 12" /> m, braking distance <InlineMath math="v^2 \div 2a = 400 \div 10 = 40" /> m, so the car stops in <InlineMath math="52" /> m.

At <InlineMath math="30\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> the thinking distance alone is <InlineMath math="30 \times 0.60 = 18" /> m, which leaves **only <InlineMath math="34" /> m** for braking.

Deceleration needed: <InlineMath math="a = v^2 \div 2s = 900 \div 68 = 13.2\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, more than two and a half times the original braking.

**Wet road**: the car still covers <InlineMath math="18" /> m before the brakes act, leaving <InlineMath math="52 - 18 = 34" /> m of braking. Then <InlineMath math="v^2 = 30^2 - 2 \times 4.0 \times 34 = 628" />, so the car reaches the hazard at about <InlineMath math="25" /> m/s.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="8.7\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> and <InlineMath math="22\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> leave out the **reaction distance** each time.
- <InlineMath math="40\text{ m}" /> is the braking distance alone, without the <InlineMath math="12" /> m covered during the reaction time.
- <InlineMath math="22\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> after a correct first part brakes over all <InlineMath math="52" /> m, forgetting the <InlineMath math="18" /> m before the brakes act.

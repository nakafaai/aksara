# Count the path difference in wavelengths, before and after

Path difference at P: <InlineMath math="24 - 18 = 6" /> cm, which is <InlineMath math="6 \div 4 = 1.5" /> wavelengths. A **half-number** of wavelengths means a crest meets a trough: destructive interference.

The depth is unchanged, so the wave speed is unchanged. Doubling the frequency **halves the wavelength** to <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cm. The same <InlineMath math="6" /> cm is now <InlineMath math="3" /> whole wavelengths, so crests meet crests: constructive interference.

**Deeper water**: at the doubled frequency the wavelength was <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cm. Moving <InlineMath math="1.2" /> times as fast at the same frequency stretches it to <InlineMath math="2.4" /> cm, and <InlineMath math="6.0 \div 2.4 = 2.5" /> wavelengths is again an odd number of half wavelengths, so P is a point of destructive interference.

# Why the other options are wrong

- At first <InlineMath math="6.0" /> cm is <InlineMath math="1.5" /> wavelengths, an odd number of half wavelengths, so P starts **destructive**, not constructive.
- Doubling the frequency halves the wavelength to <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cm, which makes <InlineMath math="6.0" /> cm exactly three wavelengths.
- Keeping <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cm in the deeper water ignores that a faster wave at the same frequency has a **longer** wavelength.

# Add the area of each stage of the speed-time graph

**Free fall**: after <InlineMath math="4.0" /> s the speed is <InlineMath math="10 \times 4.0 = 40\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, and the distance is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 4.0^2 = 80" /> m.

**Slowing**: the speed falls uniformly from <InlineMath math="40" /> to <InlineMath math="5\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, so the average is <InlineMath math="22.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> and the distance <InlineMath math="22.5 \times 3.0 = 67.5" /> m.

**Steady**: from <InlineMath math="7" /> s to <InlineMath math="10" /> s, <InlineMath math="5.0 \times 3.0 = 15" /> m. Total: <InlineMath math="80 + 67.5 + 15 = 162.5" /> m.

**The air's force**: the speed falls from <InlineMath math="40" /> to <InlineMath math="5.0" /> m/s in <InlineMath math="3.0" /> s, a deceleration of <InlineMath math="35 \div 3.0 = 11.7\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. The air must hold up the weight and also decelerate: <InlineMath math="F - mg = ma" />, so <InlineMath math="F = 80 \times (10 + 11.7) = 1.73 \times 10^3" /> N.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="155\text{ m}" /> averages <InlineMath math="40" /> and <InlineMath math="0" /> in the slowing stage, but the speed only drops to <InlineMath math="5\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.
- <InlineMath math="930\text{ N}" /> is the resultant force, <InlineMath math="80 \times 11.7" />, but the air must **also hold up the weight**.
- <InlineMath math="110\text{ m}" /> drops the speed to <InlineMath math="5\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> instantly when the parachute opens.

# Turns from the r.m.s. ratio, peak from the square root of two, ripple from the bridge

The turns follow the voltage ratio: <InlineMath math="N_s = 1200 \times 12 \div 240 = 60" />. Both voltages in that ratio are r.m.s., so the ratio must use <InlineMath math="12" /> V, not the peak.

The capacitor charges to the **peak**, not the r.m.s. value, less the two diodes that conduct in each half cycle: <InlineMath math="12 \times \sqrt{2} - 2 \times 0.70 = 15.6" /> V.

A bridge turns every negative half cycle positive, so each <InlineMath math="50" /> Hz cycle gives two pulses, and the ripple is at <InlineMath math="100" /> Hz.

**Running the lamp**: at its rating the lamp takes <InlineMath math="6.0 \div 12 = 0.50" /> A. The resistor must drop the rest of the smoothed voltage, <InlineMath math="15.6 - 12 = 3.6" /> V, at that current, so <InlineMath math="R = 3.6 \div 0.50 = 7.2\,\Omega" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="16.3\text{ V}" /> subtracts one diode drop, but in a bridge the current passes through **two diodes** in each half cycle.
- <InlineMath math="17.0\text{ V}" /> ignores the diode drops, which the question gives as <InlineMath math="0.70" /> V each.
- <InlineMath math="14.2\text{ V}" /> subtracts all four drops, but only two of the four diodes conduct at any moment.

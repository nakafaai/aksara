# The slanting force changes the normal force, so pushing and pulling at the same angle are not equal

Only the horizontal part of the handle force, <InlineMath math="F\cos30°" />, drives the roller forwards, but the vertical part changes how hard the roller presses on the ground. Pushing **down** adds <InlineMath math="F\sin30°" /> to the normal force and so **raises** the resistance. Pulling **up** takes it away and **lowers** the resistance. At a steep enough pushing angle, the extra resistance grows as fast as the forward drive, and no force can win.

<BlockMath math="\text{pull: }F\cos30°=0.15(400-F\sin30°)\Rightarrow F=\frac{60}{0.866+0.075}\approx64\text{ N}" />

<BlockMath math="\cos\theta\le0.15\sin\theta\Rightarrow\tan\theta\ge\frac{1}{0.15}\approx6.7\Rightarrow\theta\ge81°" />

Pushing needs about <InlineMath math="76\text{ N}" /> and pulling only about <InlineMath math="64\text{ N}" />. Push the handle at more than about <InlineMath math="81°" /> below the horizontal, and the extra resistance always matches the forward push, so the roller will not move at all.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="69\text{ N}" /> for both **ignores the change in normal force**, taking the resistance as <InlineMath math="0.15mg" /> in both cases.
- <InlineMath math="64\text{ N}" /> to push and <InlineMath math="76\text{ N}" /> to pull **swap the effects**: pressing down raises the resistance, lifting lowers it.
- <InlineMath math="8.5°" /> solves <InlineMath math="\tan\theta=0.15" />, the angle **of a slope** where friction just holds a block, not the jamming angle of a push.

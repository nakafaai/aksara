# Each failed lamp shortens the chain, so the current rises until the fuse goes

In series, the current is the supply voltage divided by the **total resistance**. A failed lamp's shunt takes its <InlineMath math="12\,\Omega" /> out of the string, so each failure **lowers** the total resistance and **raises** the current through every lamp still working. The fuse limits the current, which sets how many lamps may remain, and the remaining lamps share the full <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" />, so each one runs brighter than before.

With all lamps working <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ A}" /> flows. Up to <InlineMath math="10" /> lamps can fail, leaving <InlineMath math="40" /> that carry <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ A}" />, and each of those then runs at <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ W}" /> instead of about <InlineMath math="1.9\text{ W}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40" /> lamps is the number **still working** at the limit, not the number that have failed.
- <InlineMath math="1.9\text{ W}" /> assumes each lamp **keeps its normal share** of the voltage, but fewer lamps share the same <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" />.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ W}" /> multiplies the current by the **resistance** as if it were a voltage, <InlineMath math="P=IR" />, which is not a power.

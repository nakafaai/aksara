# The delay is distance over the speed of light, and the blocked path forces a longer, bent route

Radio waves travel at the speed of light, so each delay is a **distance divided by <InlineMath math="3.0\times10^{8}\text{ m s}^{-1}" />**. The planets are closest when they are on the same side of the Sun, <InlineMath math="2.3-1.5" /> apart. On opposite sides the straight line would pass through the Sun, so the signal has to go **round** through the relay: Earth and relay are a quarter turn apart on the same circle, and the relay to Mars is the long side of a right-angled triangle with the Sun at the corner. A command and its reply cover that route **twice**.

The shortest one-way delay is about <InlineMath math="4.4\text{ min}" />. With the Sun in the way, the relay route takes about <InlineMath math="27\text{ min}" /> each way, so a reply arrives about <InlineMath math="54\text{ min}" /> after the command is sent.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="21\text{ min}" /> sends the signal **straight through the Sun**, the path the question says is blocked.
- <InlineMath math="13\text{ min}" /> uses **Mars's distance from the Sun** as its shortest distance from the Earth.
- <InlineMath math="32\text{ min}" /> **adds the two sides** of the right angle for the Earth to relay leg instead of using Pythagoras.

# The outlet height sets the speed, and that same speed lowers the pressure at the crest

At the tank's surface and at the open outlet the pressure is atmospheric, so the speed at the outlet comes from the <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m}" /> drop alone. The hose is uniform, so the water moves at **that same speed everywhere** along it, including over the wall. At the crest the pressure is lowered both by the **height** above the tank's surface and by the **speed**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\rho v^2" />. The siphon fails when the crest pressure would reach zero.

The water leaves at about <InlineMath math="6.3\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. Over the wall its pressure is <InlineMath math="5.0\times10^{4}\text{ Pa}" />, and the crest could be raised to <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ m}" /> above the tank's surface before the pressure there fell to zero.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="7.0\times10^{4}\text{ Pa}" /> and <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" /> treat the water at the crest as **still**, ignoring the pressure taken up by its speed.
- <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" /> with the right crest pressure uses the **static limit**, which applies only if the water is not flowing.
- <InlineMath math="4.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> uses <InlineMath math="v=\sqrt{gh}" />, **forgetting the factor of <InlineMath math="2" />**, and the slip carries into the crest pressure and the height.

A workshop has a flat roof <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ m}" /> by <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" /> with a mass of <InlineMath math="1200\text{ kg}" />. Its fixings can hold it down with a force of at most <InlineMath math="30\text{ kN}" /> in addition to its weight. In a storm, wind blows across the roof. Over the roof the air moves <InlineMath math="1.5" /> times as fast as the wind in the open, and its pressure there is below atmospheric pressure by <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\rho v^2" />, where <InlineMath math="v" /> is the speed of the air over the roof and <InlineMath math="\rho=1.2\text{ kg m}^{-3}" />. With every door and window shut, the air inside stays still at atmospheric pressure. If a window in the wall facing the wind breaks, the wind blowing in brings the air inside to rest and raises its pressure above atmospheric pressure by <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\rho u^2" />, where <InlineMath math="u" /> is the speed of the wind in the open. Take <InlineMath math="g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}" />.

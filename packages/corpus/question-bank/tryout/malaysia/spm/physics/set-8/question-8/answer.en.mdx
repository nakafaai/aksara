# Gravity has a second job here

On a merry-go-round you have to hold on, because something must keep pulling you towards the middle to bend your path into a circle. At the equator the visitor rides a giant merry-go-round, and gravity does that pulling. The spin rate is <InlineMath math="\omega=2\pi/10800=5.81\times10^{-4}\text{ rad s}^{-1}" />, and the circle needs <InlineMath math="\omega^2R=1.69\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> of the <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. **Only the rest presses the visitor onto the scale, so it reads <InlineMath math="60\times(8.0-1.69)=379\text{ N}" />, about <InlineMath math="380\text{ N}" />**. Adding the <InlineMath math="1.69" /> instead gives <InlineMath math="580\text{ N}" />, as if the spin pushed the visitor down, and ignoring the spin gives <InlineMath math="480\text{ N}" />.

# The pendulum feels the same weaker pull

When a lift starts to go down, you feel lighter for a moment, and a swing hanging in that lift would sway more slowly. The pendulum hangs in the same spinning place as the scale, so it swings under the same left-over pull, <InlineMath math="g'=8.0-1.69=6.31\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. **Its period is <InlineMath math="T=2\pi\sqrt{L/g'}=2\times3.14\times\sqrt{0.80/6.31}=2.2\text{ s}" />**. With the full <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> it would be <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ s}" />, and with <InlineMath math="9.69\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> it would be <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ s}" />.

# Spin fast enough to float

Swing a bucket of water fast enough and the water stays in even at the top, because gravity is used up bending its path. The visitor floats when all of gravity is needed for the circle, <InlineMath math="\omega^2R=g" />. **That gives <InlineMath math="\omega=\sqrt{8.0/(5.0\times10^{6})}=1.26\times10^{-3}\text{ rad s}^{-1}" /> and a day of <InlineMath math="2\pi/\omega=4.97\times10^{3}\text{ s}=1.4\text{ h}" />**. Stopping at <InlineMath math="1/\omega=791\text{ s}=0.22\text{ h}" /> forgets that one turn is <InlineMath math="2\pi" /> radians.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\omega^2R&=\left(\frac{2\times3.14}{10800}\right)^2\times5.0\times10^{6}=1.69\text{ m s}^{-2}\\
N&=60\times(8.0-1.69)=3.8\times10^{2}\text{ N},\qquad T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.80}{6.31}}=2.2\text{ s}\\
\omega^2R&=g\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{2\pi}{\omega}=2\times3.14\times\sqrt{\frac{5.0\times10^{6}}{8.0}}=4.97\times10^{3}\text{ s}=1.4\text{ h}
\end{aligned}" />

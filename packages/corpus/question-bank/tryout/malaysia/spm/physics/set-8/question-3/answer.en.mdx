# Check what each car is really doing before you set their distances equal

Setting the two distance formulas equal gives a time, but only if both formulas still hold then. Car B's formula <InlineMath math="20t-t^2" /> is valid **only until it stops**: after that it stays put. The police car's <InlineMath math="\tfrac12at^2" /> is valid **only until it reaches <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" />**: after that it moves at a steady speed. So find where each car is when its motion changes, and solve the chase in the right phase.

Car B stops after <InlineMath math="10\text{ s}" />, <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> from the light. At that moment the police car has only just reached its top speed and is <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" /> behind, so it covers the last <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" /> at <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> and reaches car B <InlineMath math="15\text{ s}" /> after the start.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="13\text{ s}" /> solves <InlineMath math="20t-t^2=\tfrac12t^2" />, which **lets car B keep braking after it has stopped** and ignores the speed limit, so the police car seems to pass <InlineMath math="13\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.
- <InlineMath math="14\text{ s}" /> at <InlineMath math="14\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> stops car B correctly but **forgets the speed limit**, letting the police car keep accelerating.
- <InlineMath math="14\text{ s}" /> at <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> keeps the speed limit but **lets car B roll backwards** after stopping, so they seem to meet too early.

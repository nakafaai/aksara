# The gas follows one law while the piston is stuck and another once it slides, and friction can trap it on the way back

While the piston is held, the volume is fixed and the **pressure law** applies, <InlineMath math="\dfrac{p}{T}" /> constant, with temperatures in kelvin. Once the pressure is <InlineMath math="20\text{ kPa}" /> above the outside, the piston slides and the pressure stays at <InlineMath math="120\text{ kPa}" />, so **Charles's law** takes over. On cooling, friction holds the piston again until the pressure is <InlineMath math="20\text{ kPa}" /> **below** the outside, <InlineMath math="80\text{ kPa}" />, so check whether that is reached before <InlineMath math="27" /> °C.

The piston starts to move at <InlineMath math="87" /> °C, and by <InlineMath math="100" /> °C the air fills about <InlineMath math="31\text{ cm}^3" />. On cooling the pressure falls only to about <InlineMath math="97\text{ kPa}" />, never <InlineMath math="20\text{ kPa}" /> below the outside, so the piston stays put and the volume remains about <InlineMath math="31\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}^3" /> after cooling assumes the piston **slides back**, but friction holds it unless the pressure falls to <InlineMath math="80\text{ kPa}" />.
- <InlineMath math="37\text{ cm}^3" /> lets the piston slide **from the start**, as if friction did not hold it until the pressure rose.
- <InlineMath math="32" /> °C uses **degrees Celsius** in the pressure law instead of kelvin.

# The slab acts first and lifts the print, so the lens sees a nearer object than the real one

Light from the print refracts as it leaves the glass, so seen from above the print appears at its **apparent depth**, <InlineMath math="\dfrac{\text{real depth}}{n}" />, nearer than it really is. The magnifier works on that **apparent** position, not on the real one. The print then lies inside the focal length, so the lens forms an enlarged virtual image on the same side, with magnification <InlineMath math="\dfrac{v}{u}" />.

Seen without the lens, the print appears <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}" /> below the top face. The lens forms a virtual image <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}" /> from it, on the print's side, magnified <InlineMath math="5.0" /> times.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="90\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="10" /> **ignore the slab**, using the print's real position <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ cm}" /> from the lens.
- <InlineMath math="4.5\text{ cm}" /> **multiplies** the depth by the refractive index, so the print seems deeper than it is and even lies beyond the focal point.
- <InlineMath math="4.4\text{ cm}" /> **adds the reciprocals** in the lens equation instead of subtracting, which cannot give an enlarged image.

# A mixing answer above the boiling point is a sign that some of the water boils

The usual heat balance assumes both bodies end at one temperature with nothing changing state. Here it gives a temperature **above <InlineMath math="100" /> °C**, impossible for liquid water open to the air. What really happens: the water heats to <InlineMath math="100" /> °C, the iron cools to <InlineMath math="100" /> °C, and the heat the iron still has to give **boils water away** at <InlineMath math="100" /> °C.

The mixing formula would give <InlineMath math="179" /> °C, which is impossible, so the water boils. Iron and water end at <InlineMath math="100" /> °C, and about <InlineMath math="0.11\text{ kg}" /> of water boils away.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="179" /> °C for the final temperature **accepts an impossible answer**: liquid water open to the air cannot rise above <InlineMath math="100" /> °C.
- <InlineMath math="0.13\text{ kg}" /> lets the iron cool all the way to **<InlineMath math="25" /> °C**, but it is sitting in boiling water, so it stops at <InlineMath math="100" /> °C.
- <InlineMath math="0.19\text{ kg}" /> uses all the iron's heat for boiling and **forgets to warm the water** to <InlineMath math="100" /> °C first.

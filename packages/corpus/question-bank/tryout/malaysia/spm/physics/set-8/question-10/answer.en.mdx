# A heater with its own off switch

An electric iron clicks off by itself once it is hot enough. The hand warmer has that switch built into the chemistry: above <InlineMath math="54\text{ °C}" /> crystals melt again, so crystallisation cannot push the pack any hotter. Warming the whole pack from <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="54\text{ °C}" /> takes <InlineMath math="mc\Delta\theta=0.10\times2600\times34=8840\text{ J}" />, and one full crystallisation would release <InlineMath math="0.10\times2.6\times10^{5}=26000\text{ J}" />. **So only <InlineMath math="8840/26000=0.34" /> of the pack, <InlineMath math="34\%" />, crystallises at the click**. If all of it crystallised at once, the pack would have to reach <InlineMath math="20+26000/260=120\text{ °C}" />, which the built-in switch forbids.

# A long, steady plateau

An ice cube keeps a drink at <InlineMath math="0\text{ °C}" /> until its last bit melts. Here crystallising keeps the pack at <InlineMath math="54\text{ °C}" /> until the last bit of liquid is gone. **The liquid left, <InlineMath math="66\%" /> of the pack, still holds <InlineMath math="0.66\times26000=17160\text{ J}" />**, and at <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ W}" /> that lasts <InlineMath math="17160/5.0=3432\text{ s}" />, about <InlineMath math="57\text{ min}" />. Dividing the full <InlineMath math="26000\text{ J}" /> by <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ W}" /> gives <InlineMath math="87\text{ min}" />, but the first <InlineMath math="34\%" /> was already spent on warming the pack.

# What goes round comes round

Walk out of your front door, climb a hill, come back down and go inside, and your height has not changed at all. The pack starts at <InlineMath math="20\text{ °C}" /> and ends at <InlineMath math="20\text{ °C}" />, so none of the released heat stays in it. **The hands get <InlineMath math="17160+8840=26000\text{ J}=26\text{ kJ}" />, exactly the heat of crystallising the whole <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ kg}" />**. Counting only the <InlineMath math="8.8\text{ kJ}" /> of warm-up misses the long middle stage. Adding a full <InlineMath math="26\text{ kJ}" /> to the <InlineMath math="8.8\text{ kJ}" /> of cooling counts the warm-up twice and gives <InlineMath math="35\text{ kJ}" />.

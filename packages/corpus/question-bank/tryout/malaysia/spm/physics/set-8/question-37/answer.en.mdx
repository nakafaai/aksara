# When the line does not pass through the origin, the gain is the slope, not the ratio

The two readings give **different** ratios, <InlineMath math="\dfrac{3.5}{0.020}=175" /> and <InlineMath math="\dfrac{8.0}{0.050}=160" />, so the collector current is not simply proportional to the base current. On a straight line, the gain is the **slope**: the change in collector current divided by the change in base current. The line then meets the axis at a small collector current that flows even with no base current. For the collector resistor, the supply's <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ V}" /> must cover the resistor **and** the <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ V}" /> the transistor must keep.

The current gain is <InlineMath math="150" />, and about <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ mA}" /> flows in the collector with no base current. The collector resistor can be at most <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ k}\Omega" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="160" /> and <InlineMath math="175" /> divide **one reading by itself**, which gives different answers for the two readings and ignores the current at zero base current.
- <InlineMath math="0\text{ mA}" /> assumes the line **passes through the origin**, but the two readings do not lie on a line through it.
- <InlineMath math="1.1\text{ k}\Omega" /> uses the **whole <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ V}" />**, forgetting the <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ V}" /> that must stay across the transistor.

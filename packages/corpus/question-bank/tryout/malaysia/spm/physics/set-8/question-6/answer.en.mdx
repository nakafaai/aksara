# The crate gains on the cab only while the lorry is still braking, then the lorry is simply parked

The lorry brakes harder than friction can slow the crate, so the crate **slides forwards** relative to the bed. While both are slowing, the crate gains on the cab with relative acceleration <InlineMath math="10-4.0=6.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. But the lorry stops after <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ s}" />, and **from then on** the cab stands still while friction keeps slowing the crate. So first check where the crate is when the lorry stops.

The lorry stops in <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" />. At that moment the crate has slid <InlineMath math="12\text{ m}" /> along the bed and is still doing <InlineMath math="12\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. It covers the last <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m}" /> towards the parked cab and hits it about <InlineMath math="2.2\text{ s}" /> after braking began, at about <InlineMath math="11\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="13\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> keeps the **relative acceleration of <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" />** all the way, but after <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ s}" /> the lorry has stopped, so only friction acts on the crate.
- <InlineMath math="1.7\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="17\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> **ignore the friction** on the crate, as if nothing slowed it at all.
- <InlineMath math="40\text{ m}" /> uses <InlineMath math="\dfrac{v^2}{a}" /> for the stopping distance, **forgetting the factor of <InlineMath math="2" />**.

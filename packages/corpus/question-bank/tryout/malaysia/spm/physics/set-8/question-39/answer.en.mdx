# The parent was at rest, so the two products share equal momentum and the light one takes most of the energy

Before the decay the nucleus is at rest, so its momentum is zero, and afterwards the alpha particle and the lead nucleus must have **equal and opposite momentum**. For equal momentum, kinetic energy <InlineMath math="\dfrac{p^2}{2m}" /> is **inversely proportional to mass**, so the heavy lead nucleus takes only <InlineMath math="\dfrac{4}{206}" /> of the alpha particle's energy. The energy released is the **total** of both kinetic energies, not the alpha particle's alone.

The alpha particle leaves at about <InlineMath math="1.6\times10^{7}\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. The lead nucleus recoils with about <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ MeV}" />, so the decay releases about <InlineMath math="5.4\text{ MeV}" /> in all.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0\text{ MeV}" /> lets the lead nucleus **stay still**, but then the momentum after the decay would not be zero.
- <InlineMath math="5.3\text{ MeV}" /> for the lead nucleus **shares the energy equally**, but with equal momentum the heavier particle gets far less energy.
- <InlineMath math="1.1\times10^{7}\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> uses <InlineMath math="E=mv^2" />, **forgetting the <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />** in the kinetic energy.

# Thrust is momentum given to the air each second, and thinner air must be thrown faster

To hover, the thrust must equal the weight. The thrust is the **momentum given to the air each second**: mass per second times speed, <InlineMath math="(\rho Av)v=\rho Av^2" />. The power is the **kinetic energy given to the air each second**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac12(\rho Av)v^2" />. In thinner air, the same thrust needs a larger <InlineMath math="v" />, and because power grows as <InlineMath math="v^3" /> while thrust grows as <InlineMath math="v^2" />, the power **rises** even though less air is moved.

<BlockMath math="P=\tfrac12\rho Av^3=\tfrac12(1.2)(0.20)(7.07)^3\approx42\text{ W}" />

<BlockMath math="\text{plateau: }v=\sqrt{\frac{12}{0.90\times0.20}}\approx8.2\text{ m s}^{-1},\qquad P=\tfrac12(0.90)(0.20)(8.16)^3\approx49\text{ W}" />

At sea level the air leaves at about <InlineMath math="7.1\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, and the drone gives it kinetic energy at about <InlineMath math="42\text{ W}" />. On the plateau it must throw the thinner air down at about <InlineMath math="8.2\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, which takes about <InlineMath math="49\text{ W}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="32\text{ W}" /> **scales the power down with the density**, but the thrust must stay equal to the weight, so the air must move faster.
- <InlineMath math="85\text{ W}" /> uses <InlineMath math="\dot m v^2" /> for the kinetic energy each second, **forgetting the <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />**.
- <InlineMath math="42\text{ W}" /> on the plateau assumes the **same thrust needs the same power**, but thinner air must be thrown faster.

# The roof lifts when the pressure difference times its area beats its weight and its fixings together

Fast air over the roof has **lower pressure** than the still air inside, so the roof is pushed up with a force of the pressure difference times the area. It lifts once that force exceeds **everything** holding it down: its weight **and** the <InlineMath math="30\text{ kN}" /> the fixings supply. The air over the roof is faster than the wind in the open, so the open wind speed is the roof speed **divided by** <InlineMath math="1.5" />.

A pressure difference of <InlineMath math="525\text{ Pa}" /> would lift the roof. That needs air moving at about <InlineMath math="30\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> over the roof, which happens when the wind in the open reaches about <InlineMath math="71\text{ km h}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="150\text{ Pa}" /> counts only the **roof's weight**, forgetting the <InlineMath math="30\text{ kN}" /> the fixings add.
- <InlineMath math="21\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> uses <InlineMath math="\rho v^2" /> for the pressure difference, **forgetting the <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" />**.
- <InlineMath math="107\text{ km h}^{-1}" /> takes the **air speed over the roof** as the wind speed, but the air over the roof moves <InlineMath math="1.5" /> times faster than the wind.

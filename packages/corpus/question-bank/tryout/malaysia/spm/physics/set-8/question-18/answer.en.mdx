# Each colour has its own critical angle, so one beam can be split into light that escapes and light that is trapped

Glass bends violet slightly more than red, so its refractive index for violet is a little larger and its **critical angle**, from <InlineMath math="\sin c=\dfrac1n" />, a little smaller. Light escapes only if its angle of incidence is **below** its own critical angle. The ray's <InlineMath math="41.0°" /> lies **between** the two critical angles, so the colours part company: red leaves, very nearly grazing the face, while violet is totally reflected back into the glass.

The critical angle for red is about <InlineMath math="41.5°" />. At <InlineMath math="41.0°" /> the violet light is beyond its own critical angle, <InlineMath math="40.8°" />, and is totally reflected, while the red light leaves at about <InlineMath math="82°" /> to the normal.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **Violet also leaves** uses one critical angle for all colours, but violet's is smaller and the ray is already past it.
- <InlineMath math="40.8°" /> and **red reflected** swap the colours: the larger refractive index, violet's, gives the smaller critical angle.
- <InlineMath math="26°" /> **divides** <InlineMath math="\sin41.0°" /> by the refractive index, as if the light were entering the glass instead of leaving it.

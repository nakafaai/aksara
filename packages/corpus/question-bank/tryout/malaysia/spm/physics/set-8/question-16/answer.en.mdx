# Two walls, two round trips

Shout down a well and the echo is the trip down plus the trip back up. Each of the first two echoes is a round trip to one wall, so the near wall is <InlineMath math="340\times1.2/2=204\text{ m}" /> away and the far wall <InlineMath math="340\times2.0/2=340\text{ m}" />. **The hiker stands between them, so the canyon is <InlineMath math="204+340=544\text{ m}" /> wide**. Adding the two round trips gives <InlineMath math="1088\text{ m}" />, twice the width, because every metre was walked by the sound both ways.

# The echo that visits both walls

A squash ball hit at one wall can fly across the court, hit the other wall and come back to you. The third echo goes <InlineMath math="204\text{ m}" /> to the near wall, <InlineMath math="544\text{ m}" /> across to the far wall and <InlineMath math="340\text{ m}" /> back, <InlineMath math="1088\text{ m}" /> in all, and starting with the far wall gives the same path. **It arrives <InlineMath math="1088/340=3.2\text{ s}" /> after the clap**, which is also <InlineMath math="1.2+2.0" />. The <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ s}" /> between the first two echoes is only a gap, not an echo.

# Stepping off the middle

Stand halfway between two friends and step towards one of them: you get nearer to that friend and further from the other, both at once. At the middle both echoes travel <InlineMath math="544\text{ m}" /> and arrive together. A step <InlineMath math="x" /> towards one wall shortens that round trip by <InlineMath math="2x" /> and lengthens the other by <InlineMath math="2x" />, so the two paths differ by <InlineMath math="4x" />. **The echoes merge while <InlineMath math="4x/340<0.10\text{ s}" />, so the hiker must stand less than <InlineMath math="8.5\text{ m}" /> from the middle**. Changing only one path gives <InlineMath math="17\text{ m}" />, which counts half of the difference.

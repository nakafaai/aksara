# The gap is the difference of two travel times, so neither time can be ignored

Both sounds cover the same distance, but at very different speeds. The gap the second worker hears is the **difference** between the two travel times, <InlineMath math="\dfrac{d}{340}-\dfrac{d}{5000}" />, not the air time alone. Sound is fastest in the solid, so the rail sound **arrives first**. Warmer air carries sound faster, so on a hot day the air sound arrives sooner and the gap **shrinks**.

The workers are about <InlineMath math="180\text{ m}" /> apart. The sound through the rail arrives first, about <InlineMath math="0.036\text{ s}" /> after the blow, and on the hot day the gap shrinks to about <InlineMath math="0.48\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="170\text{ m}" /> takes the whole <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ s}" /> as the **air travel time**, forgetting that the rail sound takes time too.
- **Through the air first** reverses the speeds: sound travels about fifteen times faster in steel than in air.
- <InlineMath math="0.52\text{ s}" /> lets the gap **grow** on the hot day, but sound is faster in warm air, so the air sound catches up.

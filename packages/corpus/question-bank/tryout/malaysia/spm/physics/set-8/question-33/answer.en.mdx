# The centre bar is pushed both ways

Two friends who pull a child by the arms with equal strength in opposite directions leave the child standing still. Each outer bar carries <InlineMath math="1500\text{ A}" /> against the centre bar's <InlineMath math="3000\text{ A}" />, and opposite currents push apart.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
F&=\left(2.0\times10^{-7}\times\frac{1500}{0.20}\right)\times3000\times2.0=9.0\text{ N}
\end{aligned}" />

Bar A pushes the centre bar towards C, and bar C pushes it towards A with the same <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ N}" />. **The net force on a span of the centre bar is zero**, even though it carries the biggest current. Adding the two pushes to <InlineMath math="18.0\text{ N}" /> ignores their directions, and it would then make the centre bar's supports the weak point at <InlineMath math="22.4\text{ kA}" />.

# An outer bar feels a push and a pull

A magnet on the fridge door can be pushed by one magnet and pulled by another at the same time, and what it feels is the difference. Bar A is pushed away by the centre bar, whose current runs the other way. Bar C, <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ m}" /> away, carries current the same way as A, so it pulls A back towards the middle, only more weakly because it is twice as far and carries half the current.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
F_{\text{B}}&=\left(2.0\times10^{-7}\times\frac{3000}{0.20}\right)\times1500\times2.0=9.0\text{ N}\\
F_{\text{C}}&=\left(2.0\times10^{-7}\times\frac{1500}{0.40}\right)\times1500\times2.0=2.25\text{ N}\\
F&=9.0-2.25=6.75\text{ N}
\end{aligned}" />

**Each outer bar is pushed outwards, away from the centre bar, with <InlineMath math="6.75\text{ N}" /> per span**. Forgetting the far bar leaves <InlineMath math="9.00\text{ N}" />. Letting the far bar push as well, as if every pair of currents repelled, gives <InlineMath math="11.25\text{ N}" />, but like currents attract.

# A fault hits the force twice

Double the side of a square tile and you need four times the paint, because both the length and the width have grown. In a fault every current grows by the same factor, so every field grows by it too, and the force on a bar goes as the current squared. The outer bars carry the only non-zero force, so they set the limit.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{F}{6.75}&=\left(\frac{I}{3000}\right)^2\le\frac{1000}{6.75}=148\\
I&\le3000\times\sqrt{148}=3.65\times10^{4}\text{ A}
\end{aligned}" />

**The centre bar can carry up to about <InlineMath math="36.5\text{ kA}" /> in a fault**, about twelve times its normal current. The options built on <InlineMath math="9.00\text{ N}" /> and <InlineMath math="11.25\text{ N}" /> carry their slips into this step and give <InlineMath math="31.6\text{ kA}" /> and <InlineMath math="28.3\text{ kA}" />.

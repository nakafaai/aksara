# Sand that steals speed

Ride a skateboard and catch a heavy bag thrown straight down to you, and you slow down, because the bag has to be carried along at your speed. The sand is the same. It arrives with no sideways motion, and the wagon floor has to drag it up to speed. **Nothing outside pushes sideways, so the sideways momentum stays at <InlineMath math="2000\times3.0=6000\text{ kg m s}^{-1}" />**, now shared by <InlineMath math="2500\text{ kg}" />, which gives <InlineMath math="v=2.4\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. Keeping <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> because the sand fell straight down forgets that drag.

# A thud, not a bounce

Drop a bag of sand on the floor and it lands with a dull thud and stays put, its motion gone into a little warmth and noise. Here two motions die that way. The sideways one costs <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\times2000\times3.0^2-\tfrac12\times2500\times2.4^2=9000-7200=1800\text{ J}" />. **The downward one costs all the energy the sand gained in its fall, <InlineMath math="mgh=500\times10\times0.80=4000\text{ J}" />**. Together that is <InlineMath math="5.8\text{ kJ}" />. Counting only the sideways loss gives <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ kJ}" />, and counting only the fall gives <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ kJ}" />.

# Can the buffer hold the wagon?

A trampoline can only sag so far before you touch the ground under it. The buffer is like that. **At its full <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ cm}" /> it stores <InlineMath math="\tfrac12kx^2=5760\text{ J}" />, less than the wagon's <InlineMath math="7200\text{ J}" />**, so it cannot stop the wagon. Stopping it would need <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2\times7200/(1.8\times10^{6})}=8.9\text{ cm}" /> of squeeze. The <InlineMath math="1440\text{ J}" /> left over is the wagon's kinetic energy at the stop, so <InlineMath math="v_s=\sqrt{2\times1440/2500}=1.1\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. Answering <InlineMath math="0" /> trusts the buffer without checking how much it can hold.

2000\times3.0&=2500\,v\ \Rightarrow\ v=2.4\text{ m s}^{-1}\\

\tfrac12\times2500\,v_s^2&=7200-5760=1440\ \Rightarrow\ v_s=1.1\text{ m s}^{-1}

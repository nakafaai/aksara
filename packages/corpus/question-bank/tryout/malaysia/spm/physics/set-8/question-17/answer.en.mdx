# When is the image taller than the lamp?

A slide projector makes a huge picture because the slide sits just beyond the lens's focus and the wall is far away. An image is taller than the object when <InlineMath math="v" /> is bigger than <InlineMath math="u" />. The row with <InlineMath math="u=v=24\text{ cm}" /> shows where the two are equal, at <InlineMath math="2f" />. **Nearer than <InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}" /> the image is taller, and farther away it is shorter**. A lamp inside <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" /> gives a virtual image, which no screen can catch. That is the textbook range, between <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}" />. Taking every lamp position the bench allows, up to <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}" />, forgets this taller condition and leads to the <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}" /> option.

# The bench runs out

A camera on a short tripod can't step back far enough for a group photo, however good the lens is. Here the screen side of the bench is only <InlineMath math="100-40=60\text{ cm}" /> long. As the lamp creeps towards <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" />, the sharp image races away to great distances, and past <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}" /> the screen would have to leave the bench.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
v&\le100-40=60\text{ cm}\\
\frac{1}{u}&=\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{v}\le\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{60}=\frac{1}{15}\ \Rightarrow\ u\ge15\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

**The screen shows a taller image only for lamp distances between <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}" />**, with the image as tall as the lamp at <InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}" /> itself. The first row of the table, <InlineMath math="u=15\text{ cm}" /> with the screen at the far end, is the bench's own limit. Quoting <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" /> to <InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}" /> is the textbook answer for an endless bench, not for this one.

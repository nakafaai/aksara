# Light from the focal point leaves the lens parallel, and a plane mirror sends a parallel beam straight back

If the pin is at the **focal point**, the lens turns its light into a **parallel beam**. A plane mirror facing the lens reflects a parallel beam back along itself, still parallel, and the lens focuses it back onto the focal point, right beside the pin. Because the beam between lens and mirror is parallel, **the mirror's distance does not matter**. For the separate object the usual lens equation applies.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> treats the coincidence point like a **concave mirror's centre of curvature**, at twice the focal length, but here the pin sits at the focal point itself.
- **Moving <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> further** assumes the image position depends on the mirror, but the beam between lens and mirror is parallel.
- <InlineMath math="9.4\text{ cm}" /> **adds the reciprocals** in the lens equation instead of subtracting.

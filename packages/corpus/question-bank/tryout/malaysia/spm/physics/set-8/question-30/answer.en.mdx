# Out along one wire, back along the other

A rider who delivers a parcel <InlineMath math="25\text{ m}" /> down the street rides <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" /> in all, because the trip includes the way back. The current reaches the tool along the live conductor and returns along the neutral, so it passes through <InlineMath math="2\times25=50\text{ m}" /> of copper. With <InlineMath math="R=\rho L/A" />, that is <InlineMath math="1.7\times10^{-8}\times50/(1.0\times10^{-6})" /> ohms. **The cable adds <InlineMath math="0.850\,\Omega" /> to the circuit**. Counting one conductor only gives <InlineMath math="0.425\,\Omega" /> and leaves the current with no way home.

# The tool gets less than its label

A sprinkler at the end of a long, thin hose sprays more weakly than one fixed straight to the tap. The rating tells you the tool's resistance, <InlineMath math="R_t=240^2/2000=28.8\,\Omega" />, and that resistance stays put. In series with the cable it draws a smaller current, so the tool itself gets only <InlineMath math="8.094\times28.8=233\text{ V}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
R_t&=\frac{240^2}{2000}=28.8\,\Omega,\qquad I=\frac{240}{28.8+0.85}=8.094\text{ A}\\
P&=I^2R_t=8.094^2\times28.8=1.89\times10^{3}\text{ W}
\end{aligned}" />

**The tool actually takes <InlineMath math="1.89\text{ kW}" />**. Multiplying the current by the socket's <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" /> gives <InlineMath math="1.94\text{ kW}" />, but that also counts the heat made in the cable.

# Choosing thick enough copper

A wider road carries the same traffic with less queueing. The cable and the tool share the socket's voltage in proportion to their resistances, so the cable's share is <InlineMath math="R_c/(R_c+28.8)" />, and that may be at most <InlineMath math="0.02" />. Notice that the cable's own resistance sits in the total too.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{R_c}{R_c+28.8}&\le0.02\ \Rightarrow\ R_c\le\frac{0.02\times28.8}{0.98}=0.588\,\Omega\\
A&\ge\frac{1.7\times10^{-8}\times50}{0.588}=1.45\times10^{-6}\text{ m}^2=1.45\text{ mm}^2
\end{aligned}" />

**Each conductor needs a cross-sectional area of at least <InlineMath math="1.45\text{ mm}^2" />**. Setting <InlineMath math="R_c=0.02\times28.8=0.576\,\Omega" /> gives <InlineMath math="1.48\text{ mm}^2" />. That compares the cable with the tool alone, as if the current stayed at the rated <InlineMath math="8.33\text{ A}" />.

# Current goes out along one conductor and back along the other, and the tool's power falls with the voltage it receives

The current flows to the tool along the live conductor and **back** along the neutral, so the cable puts **<InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" />** of copper in the circuit, not <InlineMath math="25\text{ m}" />. The tool's rating fixes its resistance, <InlineMath math="R=\dfrac{V^2}{P}" />, not its power: with the cable in series the current is a little smaller, and the tool delivers <InlineMath math="I^2R" />, less than its rating. For the cable to lose at most <InlineMath math="2\%" />, its resistance must be at most about <InlineMath math="2\%" /> of the total.

The cable adds <InlineMath math="0.85\,\Omega" />, and the tool delivers only about <InlineMath math="1.89\text{ kW}" />. To lose no more than <InlineMath math="2\%" /> of the supply, each conductor needs an area of at least about <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ mm}^2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.43\,\Omega" /> counts **one conductor only**, but the current must go out and come back.
- <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ kW}" /> assumes the tool **still takes its rated power**, but its resistance is fixed and it now receives less than <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.085\,\Omega" /> converts <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}^2" /> to **<InlineMath math="10^{-5}\text{ m}^2" />**, but a square millimetre is <InlineMath math="10^{-6}\text{ m}^2" />.

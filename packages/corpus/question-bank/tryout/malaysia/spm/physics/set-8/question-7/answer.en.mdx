# Watch from the lorry's cab

Two friends walking side by side on a moving walkway stay side by side, however fast the walkway runs. Seen from the lorry, the car starts at rest and simply speeds up at <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. It must gain <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ m}" /> to reach the lorry, <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}" /> to pass it, <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ m}" /> of clear space, and its own <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m}" />, because its back has to clear too. **That is a gain of <InlineMath math="36\text{ m}" />, so <InlineMath math="\tfrac12\times2.0\times t^2=36" /> and <InlineMath math="t=6.0\text{ s}" />**. Counting only the lorry's <InlineMath math="16\text{ m}" /> gives <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ s}" /> and a car still level with the lorry.

# Back on the ground

The <InlineMath math="36\text{ m}" /> is only what the car gains over the lorry, like counting the steps you take on the walkway and forgetting the walkway carried you too. **Along the road the car travels <InlineMath math="20\times6.0+\tfrac12\times2.0\times6.0^2=120+36=156\text{ m}" />**. Answering <InlineMath math="36\text{ m}" /> leaves out the <InlineMath math="120\text{ m}" /> that both vehicles cover at <InlineMath math="20\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

# Room for the oncoming car

Two people walking towards each other meet sooner than if one of them stood still. In those <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ s}" /> the oncoming car also drives <InlineMath math="20\times6.0=120\text{ m}" /> towards the overtaking car. **So the gap at the start must be at least <InlineMath math="156+120=276\text{ m}" />**. A gap of <InlineMath math="156\text{ m}" /> would be enough only if the other car were parked.

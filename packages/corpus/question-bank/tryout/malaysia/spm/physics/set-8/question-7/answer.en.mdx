# Inside a lift moving at constant speed, the fall looks exactly as it would at rest

When the bolt comes loose it is moving **upwards at <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />**, like everything in the lift. Measured from the lift, bolt and floor start with the same velocity and only the bolt accelerates, so the fall takes **the same time as in a lift at rest**. Measured from the shaft, the bolt is a ball thrown up at <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />: it rises, stops and falls back while the floor rises to meet it.

The bolt hits the floor after about <InlineMath math="0.71\text{ s}" />, the same as if the lift were still. Seen from the shaft it first rises about <InlineMath math="0.46\text{ m}" /> and ends about <InlineMath math="0.33\text{ m}" /> below the point where it came loose.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.45\text{ m}" /> below treats the bolt as **starting from rest in the shaft**, but it keeps the lift's upward speed when it comes loose.
- <InlineMath math="0.46\text{ s}" /> lets the floor **rise to meet a bolt that starts at rest**, the same slip seen from the other side.
- <InlineMath math="0\text{ m}" /> for the rise gets the time and the final position right but **forgets the bolt first moves upwards** before it falls.

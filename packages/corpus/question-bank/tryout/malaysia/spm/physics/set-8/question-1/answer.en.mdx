# A negative zero error is added back, and an uncertainty in the diameter counts twice

The calipers read <InlineMath math="-0.02\text{ cm}" /> when closed, so every reading is <InlineMath math="0.02\text{ cm}" /> **too small**: the correction is to add it. The <InlineMath math="40" /> turns lie side by side, so the coil is <InlineMath math="40" /> diameters long. Resistivity follows from <InlineMath math="R=\dfrac{\rho L}{A}" /> with <InlineMath math="A=\pi r^2" />. Because the area goes as the **square** of the diameter, a percentage uncertainty in the diameter appears **twice** in the resistivity.

The wire is <InlineMath math="0.720\text{ mm}" /> thick and its resistivity is about <InlineMath math="4.9\times10^{-7}" /> ohm metres. The reading of the coil alone gives about <InlineMath math="0.7\%" /> uncertainty in the resistivity.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.710\text{ mm}" /> **subtracts the zero error**, but a negative zero error means the readings are too small, so it must be added.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\times10^{-6}" /> uses the **diameter as the radius** in <InlineMath math="\pi r^2" />, making the area and the resistivity four times too large.
- <InlineMath math="0.35\%" /> is the uncertainty in the **diameter**, but the resistivity depends on its square, which doubles the percentage.

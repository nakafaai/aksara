A radio transmitter T and a receiver R stand on flat ground <InlineMath math="600\text{ km}" /> apart. R picks up a ground wave, which travels straight along the ground, and a sky wave, which is reflected once by a horizontal layer of the upper atmosphere at the point halfway between T and R. During the day the sky wave arrives <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ ms}" /> after the ground wave. Radio waves travel at <InlineMath math="3.0\times10^{8}\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, and the layer reflects a wave only if the wave meets it at an angle of incidence of at least <InlineMath math="40°" />. A wave that meets it more steeply passes through and is lost. Treat the Earth as flat.

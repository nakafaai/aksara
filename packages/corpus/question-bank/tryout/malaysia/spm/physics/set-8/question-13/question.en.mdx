Treat the orbits of the Earth and Mars as circles round the Sun in one plane, of radii <InlineMath math="1.5\times10^{11}\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="2.3\times10^{11}\text{ m}" />. Radio signals travel at <InlineMath math="3.0\times10^{8}\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. When Mars is on the far side of the Sun from the Earth, with the Sun on the line between them, the Sun blocks the direct path, and signals go instead through a relay spacecraft that moves on the Earth's orbit, a quarter of a turn ahead of the Earth.

What is the shortest possible one-way signal delay between the Earth and Mars, what is the one-way delay through the relay when the Sun lies between them, and how long after sending a command from the Earth at that time does the reply from a rover on Mars arrive, if the rover answers at once?

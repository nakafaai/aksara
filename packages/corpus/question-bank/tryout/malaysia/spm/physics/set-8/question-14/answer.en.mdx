# The worst shaking is resonance, and slowing down from above sweeps the driving frequency through it

The ridges strike the wheels at a frequency <InlineMath math="f=\dfrac{v}{d}" />. The violent shaking at one particular speed is **resonance**: there the ridges drive the wheels at their natural frequency. Starting faster, the driving frequency is **above** the natural frequency. As the car brakes, it falls steadily and passes **through** resonance on the way to zero, so the worst shaking comes in the middle of the stop, not at its start or end.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0\text{ s}" /> assumes the **fastest, most frequent** bumping shakes most, but large shaking needs the frequency to match the natural one.
- <InlineMath math="0.080\text{ Hz}" /> **inverts** the relation, using <InlineMath math="\dfrac{d}{v}" />, which is the time between ridges, not the frequency.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ s}" /> is when the car **stops**, where the ridges no longer strike at all.

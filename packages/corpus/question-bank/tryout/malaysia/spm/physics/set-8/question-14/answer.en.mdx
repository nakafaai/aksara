# A loaded bridge swings slower

A diving board bobs more slowly under a heavy diver than under a small child. A bridge does the same. The <InlineMath math="1.08\text{ Hz}" /> belongs to the empty deck, but the group adds <InlineMath math="44\%" /> to the moving mass while it walks, and the frequency falls by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{1.44}=1.2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{1.08}{\sqrt{1.44}}=\frac{1.08}{1.2}=0.90\text{ Hz}
\end{aligned}" />

**With the group on it, the bridge sways at <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ Hz}" />**. Tuning the walk to the engineers' <InlineMath math="1.08\text{ Hz}" /> gives <InlineMath math="1.62\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, a pace that would rock the empty bridge, not the one the group is standing on.

# Left, right, left

On a playground swing you push once per full swing. A walker's body rocks left on a left step and right on a right step, so one full sideways shove takes two steps. To match <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ Hz}" /> the group must step at <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ Hz}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f_{\text{step}}&=2\times0.90=1.8\text{ Hz},\qquad v=0.75\times1.8=1.35\text{ m s}^{-1}\\
N&=0.90\times\frac{54}{1.35}=0.90\times40=36
\end{aligned}" />

**The group drives the sway at <InlineMath math="1.35\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, and the bridge sways <InlineMath math="36" /> times during the <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" /> crossing**. Each sway uses two steps, so <InlineMath math="54\text{ m}" /> of <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ m}" /> steps gives the same <InlineMath math="36" />. Stepping at <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ Hz}" />, as if every step were a full shove, halves the pace to <InlineMath math="0.68\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> and stretches the crossing to <InlineMath math="72" /> sways.

# One runner meets an empty bridge

Every landing on a trampoline presses the mat down, whichever foot lands. Up and down, the push comes at every step. A lone runner hardly adds any mass, so the bounce keeps the empty bridge's <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ Hz}" />, and the runner's feet must land <InlineMath math="2.4" /> times a second. **The runner needs <InlineMath math="1.20\times2.4=2.88\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, about <InlineMath math="2.9\text{ m s}^{-1}" />**. Dividing by the group's factor of <InlineMath math="1.2" /> drops the bounce to <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ Hz}" /> and the pace to <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, yet no group is on the bridge that day.

# A gap spreads a wave strongly only when the gap is about the size of the wavelength

Waves passing through a gap **diffract**: they spread out beyond it. The spreading is strong when the wavelength is **comparable to the gap** and negligible when the wavelength is tiny by comparison. A higher frequency means a **shorter** wavelength, so the whistle spreads less than the voice, even though it may sound louder.

Light's wavelength is only about <InlineMath math="5.6\times10^{-7}" /> of the gap, so it goes almost straight through and the teacher cannot be seen. The whistle's <InlineMath math="3.4\text{ cm}" /> waves spread far less than the voice.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **The whistle spreads more** confuses frequency with spreading: a higher frequency has a shorter wavelength and spreads less.
- <InlineMath math="1.8\times10^{6}" /> is the ratio **turned upside down**, the gap divided by the wavelength.
- <InlineMath math="0.0029\text{ m}" /> uses <InlineMath math="\lambda=\dfrac{f}{v}" /> **instead of <InlineMath math="\dfrac{v}{f}" />**.

# How long is each wave at the gap?

Swing a skipping rope slowly and it makes long loops. Swing it fast and the loops get short. A wave moves one wavelength in one period, so at the gap <InlineMath math="\lambda=vT" /> gives <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}" /> on the calm day and <InlineMath math="90\text{ m}" /> in the storm. How far a wave fans out behind a gap depends on its wavelength compared with the gap's width.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\text{calm}}&=10\times3.0=30\text{ m},\qquad \lambda_{\text{storm}}=10\times9.0=90\text{ m}\\
\frac{30}{60}&=0.50,\qquad \frac{90}{60}=1.5,\qquad \frac{30}{120}=0.25,\qquad \frac{30}{20}=1.5
\end{aligned}" />

**The storm waves, longer than the gap itself, fan out much more widely, so statement I is correct**. Expecting the short, choppy calm-day waves to spread more turns the rule upside down.

# Only the ratio matters

Through a wide stadium exit a crowd keeps walking straight on, while through a narrow turnstile people fan out on the far side. Widening the gap to <InlineMath math="120\text{ m}" /> drops the calm-day ratio from <InlineMath math="0.50" /> to <InlineMath math="0.25" />, so those waves pass through more like a straight beam, and statement II is wrong. A <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> gap does the opposite and lifts the ratio to <InlineMath math="1.5" />, the same as the storm's <InlineMath math="90/60" />. **Equal ratios mean equal spreading, so statement IV is correct**. Rejecting IV because the two waves have different wavelengths misses that only the ratio counts, and that slip leads to I and III.

# The harbour floor changes the wavelength

A marching band that steps off a road onto soft sand slows down, but the drummer keeps the same beat, so the rows bunch closer together. Waves entering the shallower harbour do the same. Their period is fixed by the sea outside, <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ s}" /> for the storm, but their speed drops to <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\lambda&=6.0\times9.0=54\text{ m}\neq90\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Going through the gap alone would not change the wavelength, and that is the trap. **The shallower water shortens the storm waves to <InlineMath math="54\text{ m}" />, so statement III is wrong, and only I and IV are correct**. Choosing I, III and IV applies the gap rule and misses the change of depth written into the question.

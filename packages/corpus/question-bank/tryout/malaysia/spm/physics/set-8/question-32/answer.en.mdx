# The mains voltage is quoted as r.m.s., but the tube strikes on the peak

The <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" /> of the mains is its **root-mean-square** value. An alternating voltage swings up to a **peak** <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" /> times larger, and a transformer scales the whole waveform, peaks included, by its turns ratio. The tube strikes when the **secondary peak** reaches <InlineMath math="9000\text{ V}" />. For the current, the transformer must take in more power than it gives out, so the input is the output **divided by** the efficiency.

The mains peaks at about <InlineMath math="340\text{ V}" />, so the transformer needs a turns ratio of at least <InlineMath math="27" />. While the sign is lit it takes about <InlineMath math="167\text{ W}" /> from the mains, a current of about <InlineMath math="0.69\text{ A}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="38" /> uses the **r.m.s.** <InlineMath math="240\text{ V}" /> as if it were the peak, so it overestimates the turns ratio needed.
- <InlineMath math="0.56\text{ A}" /> **multiplies** by the efficiency, as if the transformer took in less power than it gave out.
- <InlineMath math="170\text{ V}" /> **divides** by <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" /> instead of multiplying: the peak is larger than the r.m.s. value.

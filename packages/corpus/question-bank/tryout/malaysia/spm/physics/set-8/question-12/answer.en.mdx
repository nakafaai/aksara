# The pot warms along with the soup, and at the boil the losses eat into the power

Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of the hob's <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ kW}" /> reaches the pot, <InlineMath math="1600\text{ W}" />. Warming up, that power must heat **the copper pot as well as the soup**. Once boiling, the soup's temperature no longer changes, so the heat that stays in it goes into vaporising water, but the pot is now losing <InlineMath math="600\text{ W}" />, so **only <InlineMath math="1000\text{ W}" />** is left for boiling. The hob's electrical energy counts its full <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ kW}" /> for the whole time.

The soup boils after about <InlineMath math="6.7\text{ min}" />, and boiling away <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ kg}" /> takes about <InlineMath math="19\text{ min}" /> more. Altogether the hob uses about <InlineMath math="0.85\text{ kW h}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.3\text{ min}" /> heats **only the soup**, forgetting that the copper pot has to be warmed too.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ min}" /> boils with the full <InlineMath math="1600\text{ W}" />, **ignoring the <InlineMath math="600\text{ W}" />** the pot loses while at <InlineMath math="100" /> °C.
- <InlineMath math="0.68\text{ kW h}" /> counts only the **<InlineMath math="80\%" /> that reaches the pot**, but the meter records everything the hob draws.

# A boil that stops by itself

Pour water into a hot pan and it sizzles only until the pan has cooled. The nitrogen boils for the same reason, and it stops once the block has fallen to <InlineMath math="-196\text{ °C}" />. The block drops <InlineMath math="27+196=223\text{ °C}" />, and every joule it gives out boils some nitrogen.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m&=\frac{0.20\times900\times(27+196)}{2.0\times10^{5}}=\frac{40140}{2.0\times10^{5}}=0.20\text{ kg}
\end{aligned}" />

**About <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ kg}" /> of nitrogen boils away**. Counting the drop from <InlineMath math="0\text{ °C}" />, only <InlineMath math="196\text{ °C}" />, forgets the first <InlineMath math="27\text{ °C}" /> of cooling and gives <InlineMath math="0.18\text{ kg}" />.

# The first flask has warmth to spare

Before you sit down to share a pizza, you check there are enough slices to go round. Here the slices are joules. To reach <InlineMath math="0\text{ °C}" /> the block needs <InlineMath math="0.20\times900\times196=35280\text{ J}" />. Cooling <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ kg}" /> of water all the way to <InlineMath math="0\text{ °C}" /> would release <InlineMath math="0.50\times4200\times20=42000\text{ J}" />, which is more. So the water never reaches <InlineMath math="0\text{ °C}" />, no ice forms, and one ordinary balance settles it.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
0.50\times4200\times(20-T)&=0.20\times900\times(T+196)\ \Rightarrow\ T=\frac{6720}{2280}=2.9\text{ °C}
\end{aligned}" />

**The first flask ends at <InlineMath math="2.9\text{ °C}" />**, just above freezing.

# The second flask runs out of warmth

With <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ kg}" /> of water the same check fails. Cooling to <InlineMath math="0\text{ °C}" /> releases only <InlineMath math="33600\text{ J}" />, less than the block's <InlineMath math="35280\text{ J}" />. Liquid water cannot cool below <InlineMath math="0\text{ °C}" />, just as a pond cannot get colder than its own ice, so the missing heat has to come from freezing.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
35280-33600&=1680\text{ J}\\
m_{\text{ice}}&=\frac{1680}{3.34\times10^{5}}=5.0\times10^{-3}\text{ kg}
\end{aligned}" />

**The second flask settles at <InlineMath math="0\text{ °C}" /> with about <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ g}" /> of ice**, a thin shell on the block, and almost all of the <InlineMath math="400\text{ g}" /> stays liquid. Putting both flasks through the same balance gives <InlineMath math="-0.90\text{ °C}" />, a liquid colder than its own freezing point, which the freezing step rules out. Freezing the whole <InlineMath math="35280\text{ J}" /> into ice gives <InlineMath math="106\text{ g}" />, because it forgets the <InlineMath math="33600\text{ J}" /> the water gave out first.

A photomultiplier tube detects very faint light. Inside the evacuated tube, electrons leave the cathode, which is at <InlineMath math="0\text{ V}" />, almost at rest. They are pulled to the first of six metal plates called dynodes. The dynodes are held at <InlineMath math="+100\text{ V}" />, <InlineMath math="+200\text{ V}" /> and so on in steps of <InlineMath math="100\text{ V}" /> up to <InlineMath math="+600\text{ V}" />, and after the last dynode the electrons are pulled to a collecting anode at <InlineMath math="+700\text{ V}" />. Every electron that strikes a dynode knocks out <InlineMath math="3" /> electrons, which leave that dynode almost at rest and are pulled on to the next one. The anode only collects electrons. In faint light, <InlineMath math="5.0\times10^{6}" /> electrons leave the cathode each second. The mass of an electron is <InlineMath math="9.11\times10^{-31}\text{ kg}" />, and its charge is <InlineMath math="1.6\times10^{-19}\text{ C}" />.

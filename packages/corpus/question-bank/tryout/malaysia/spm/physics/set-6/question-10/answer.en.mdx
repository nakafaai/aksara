# Work out the input, take the waste, then the coolant’s share sets both flows

At <InlineMath math="25\%" /> efficiency the engine takes in <InlineMath math="80" /> kW, so it wastes <InlineMath math="60" /> kW, and the coolant carries <InlineMath math="0.40 \times 60 = 24" /> kW, **not the whole input**.

Warming by <InlineMath math="12" /> degrees, the water must flow at <InlineMath math="24\,000 \div (4200 \times 12) = 0.48" /> kg/s. The same <InlineMath math="24" /> kW warms the air by <InlineMath math="8.0" /> degrees, so <InlineMath math="3.0" /> kg of air must pass each second, far more mass because air holds **less heat per kilogram**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.63\text{ kg s}^{-1}" /> takes the coolant’s share of the **whole input**, not of the waste.
- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ kg s}^{-1}" /> sends **all** the waste into the coolant instead of forty per cent.
- <InlineMath math="0.12\text{ kg s}^{-1}" /> takes the waste as three quarters of the **output** instead of the input.

# Halve the echo time for the distance, then take two area fractions in turn

The pulse goes there and back, so the Moon is <InlineMath math="3.0 \times 10^8 \times 2.56 \div 2 = 3.84 \times 10^8" /> m away. At the Moon the beam covers <InlineMath math="\pi \times 3500^2 = 3.85 \times 10^7" /> square metres, so the reflector catches <InlineMath math="1.3 \times 10^{11}" /> photons.

Those spread over <InlineMath math="1.77 \times 10^8" /> square metres on the way back, and the <InlineMath math="0.79" /> square metre mirror catches about <InlineMath math="600" /> of them, roughly **one photon in twenty million billion**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="7.68 \times 10^8\text{ m}" /> forgets the pulse makes a **round trip**.
- <InlineMath math="1.3 \times 10^{11}" /> stops at the reflector and forgets the **spreading on the way back**.
- About <InlineMath math="2300" /> uses the mirror’s **diameter as its radius**, making its area four times too big.

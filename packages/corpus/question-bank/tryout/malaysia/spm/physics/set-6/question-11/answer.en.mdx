At constant volume <InlineMath math="p \div T" /> stays fixed in kelvin: <InlineMath math="T = 300 \times 130 \div 100 = 390" /> K, which is <InlineMath math="117^\circ\text{C}" />.

**The bigger lid**: the same <InlineMath math="150" /> N spread over <InlineMath math="1.0 \times 10^{-2}" /> square metres is only <InlineMath math="15" /> kPa of excess, so the air need reach only <InlineMath math="115" /> kPa, at <InlineMath math="300 \times 1.15 = 345" /> K, which is <InlineMath math="72^\circ\text{C}" />. A **bigger lid gives way sooner**.

**Cooling the resealed jar**: the air is shut in at <InlineMath math="100" /> kPa and <InlineMath math="345" /> K, so at <InlineMath math="300" /> K it is at <InlineMath math="100 \times 300 \div 345 = 87" /> kPa. The outside air wins by <InlineMath math="13" /> kPa over <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ m}^2" />, a net inward force of about <InlineMath math="130" /> N, **less** than the clip’s limit because the jar only cooled back from <InlineMath math="72^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="150\text{ N}" /> assumes the inward push matches the clip’s limit, but it is set by how far the jar **cools**.
- <InlineMath math="345^\circ\text{C}" /> forgets to convert **back from kelvin**.
- <InlineMath math="65\text{ N}" /> uses the area of the **smaller lid**.

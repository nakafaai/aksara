# Turn the mercury column into pascals, take off the blood column, and turn back

A column of mercury <InlineMath math="0.120" /> m high presses <InlineMath math="13\,600 \times 9.8 \times 0.120 = 16.0" /> kPa. With the arm raised, the cuff sits at the top of a <InlineMath math="0.40" /> m column of blood, which takes away <InlineMath math="1050 \times 9.8 \times 0.40 = 4.1" /> kPa.

The cuff now feels <InlineMath math="11.9" /> kPa, which a mercury column balances at <InlineMath math="11\,900 \div (13\,600 \times 9.8) = 0.089" /> m, a reading of <InlineMath math="89" /> mmHg. That is why the cuff must be **at heart level**.

**A reading of <InlineMath math="100\text{ mmHg}" />**: the reading must fall by <InlineMath math="20" /> mmHg, which is <InlineMath math="13\,600 \times 9.8 \times 0.020 = 2.67" /> kPa. A column of blood gives that over <InlineMath math="2670 \div (1050 \times 9.8) = 0.26" /> m, so the cuff must be <InlineMath math="0.26" /> m **above** the heart.

**The force on the wall**: <InlineMath math="89" /> mmHg is a mercury column of <InlineMath math="0.089" /> m, so the pressure is <InlineMath math="0.089 \times 13\,600 \times 9.8 = 1.19 \times 10^4" /> Pa. On <InlineMath math="2.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2" /> that gives <InlineMath math="F = pA = 2.4" /> N.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="151\text{ mmHg}" /> adds the blood column, but the raised cuff is **above** the heart, where the pressure is lower.
- <InlineMath math="0.018\text{ N}" /> multiplies the reading in mmHg by the area, without first turning it into **pascals**.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ mmHg}" /> ignores that the cuff reads the pressure **where it sits**.

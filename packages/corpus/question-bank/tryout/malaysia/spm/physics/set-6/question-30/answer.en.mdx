# In series both the electromotive forces and the internal resistances add, in parallel only the resistance is shared

In series the cells give <InlineMath math="4.5" /> V with <InlineMath math="1.5\,\Omega" /> inside, so <InlineMath math="I = 4.5 \div 3.5 = 1.29" /> A and the lamp takes <InlineMath math="1.29^2 \times 2.0 = 3.3" /> W.

In parallel the electromotive force stays <InlineMath math="1.5" /> V but the internal resistance **drops to a third**, <InlineMath math="0.17\,\Omega" />. Then <InlineMath math="I = 1.5 \div 2.17 = 0.69" /> A and the lamp takes <InlineMath math="0.96" /> W.

**The mixed arrangement**: the parallel pair acts as one cell of <InlineMath math="1.5" /> V with <InlineMath math="0.25\,\Omega" /> inside. In series with the third cell that makes <InlineMath math="3.0" /> V and <InlineMath math="0.75\,\Omega" />, so <InlineMath math="I = 3.0 \div 2.75 = 1.09" /> A and <InlineMath math="P = I^2 R = 2.4" /> W, between the other two.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.1\text{ W}" /> and <InlineMath math="4.5\text{ W}" /> leave out the **internal resistance** of the cells.
- <InlineMath math="0.37\text{ W}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ W}" /> add internal resistances in parallel too, but parallel paths **share** the current.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ W}" /> gives the parallel pair a full <InlineMath math="0.50\,\Omega" />, but two equal paths **halve** it.

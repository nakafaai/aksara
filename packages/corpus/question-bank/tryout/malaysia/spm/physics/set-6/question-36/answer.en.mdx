# Find the mass defect, share its energy among the nucleons, then count the nuclei in a gram

Two protons and two neutrons have <InlineMath math="4.03188" /> u, so the **mass defect** is <InlineMath math="0.03037" /> u, worth <InlineMath math="0.03037 \times 931.5 = 28.3" /> MeV, or <InlineMath math="7.1" /> MeV for each of the four nucleons.

A gram holds <InlineMath math="1.0 \times 10^{-3} \div (4.00151 \times 1.66 \times 10^{-27}) = 1.5 \times 10^{23}" /> nuclei, each releasing the **whole** <InlineMath math="28.3" /> MeV, so <InlineMath math="1.5 \times 10^{23} \times 28.3 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-13} = 6.8 \times 10^{11}" /> J.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="28\text{ MeV}" /> is the binding energy of the **whole nucleus**, not of each nucleon.
- <InlineMath math="1.7 \times 10^{11}\text{ J}" /> gives each nucleus only the energy of **one nucleon**.
- <InlineMath math="2.7 \times 10^{12}\text{ J}" /> counts **nucleons** in the gram and gives each the whole nucleus’s energy.

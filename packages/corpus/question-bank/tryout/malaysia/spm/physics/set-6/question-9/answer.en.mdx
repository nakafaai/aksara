# Compare kinetic energy with the depth of the potential well, per kilogram

Per kilogram the surface sits <InlineMath math="GM \div R = 2.82 \times 10^6" /> J deep, so escape needs <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}v^2 = 2.82 \times 10^6" />, <InlineMath math="v = 2.4" /> km/s.

At <InlineMath math="2.0" /> km/s the probe has only <InlineMath math="2.0 \times 10^6" /> J per kilogram, so it stops where <InlineMath math="GM \div r = 0.82 \times 10^6" />, at <InlineMath math="r = 6.0 \times 10^6" /> m, about <InlineMath math="4300" /> km up. Gravity **weakens as it climbs**, so it rises far higher than a constant <InlineMath math="g" /> would allow.

**Passing <InlineMath math="2000\text{ km}" />**: there <InlineMath math="r = 3.74 \times 10^6" /> m and <InlineMath math="GM \div r = 1.31 \times 10^6" /> J per kilogram, so the probe has climbed <InlineMath math="2.82 \times 10^6 - 1.31 \times 10^6 = 1.51 \times 10^6" /> J per kilogram up the well. What is left is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}v^2 = 0.49 \times 10^6" />, so <InlineMath math="v = 1.0" /> km/s, **half** its launch speed.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="1200\text{ km}" /> uses the surface value of <InlineMath math="g" /> all the way up, but the field **falls with distance**.
- <InlineMath math="0.70\text{ km s}^{-1}" /> drops the **half** in the kinetic energy.
- About <InlineMath math="6000\text{ km}" /> is the distance from the Moon’s **centre**, not the height above the surface.

# The tangent of the angle is the acceleration over g, and the tilt flips when the bus brakes

The vertical part of the tension holds the weight and the horizontal part accelerates the mass, so <InlineMath math="a = g\tan 11^\circ = 1.94\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. After <InlineMath math="5.0" /> s the bus moves at <InlineMath math="9.7" /> m/s.

Braking to rest in <InlineMath math="4.0" /> s needs <InlineMath math="2.43\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, so <InlineMath math="\tan\theta = 0.243" /> and <InlineMath math="\theta = 14^\circ" />, now **leaning forwards** because the acceleration points backwards. The distance is the area under the speed graph, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 9.7 \times 9.0 = 44" /> m.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="11^\circ" /> keeps the speeding-up angle, but the braking is **sharper**, over a shorter time.
- Backwards is the direction while speeding up, but braking makes the mass **swing forwards**.
- <InlineMath math="87\text{ m}" /> runs the whole <InlineMath math="9.0" /> s at the **top speed**, but the bus speeds up and slows down.

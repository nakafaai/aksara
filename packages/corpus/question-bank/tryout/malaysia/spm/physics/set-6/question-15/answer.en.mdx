# The delay adds its own phase shift, so the inverted copy is no longer opposite

The hum’s period is <InlineMath math="5.0" /> ms, so a <InlineMath math="1.0" /> ms delay is **a fifth of a period**, a phase shift of <InlineMath math="72^\circ" />. Together with the <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> inversion, the copy is <InlineMath math="108^\circ" /> out of step with the hum.

The two waves add to <InlineMath math="2A\cos 54^\circ = 1.18A" />, so the hum is **louder than before**, about <InlineMath math="1.2" /> times its amplitude. Cancellation needs the delay to be far shorter than the period.

# Why the other options are wrong

- An exact inverted copy cancels only if it arrives **in step**, and the delay moves it out of step.
- A fifth of the amplitude treats the delay fraction as the **size** of the result, but the result comes from the phase difference.
- <InlineMath math="1.6" /> times uses the delay alone and forgets the **inversion**, which adds <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.

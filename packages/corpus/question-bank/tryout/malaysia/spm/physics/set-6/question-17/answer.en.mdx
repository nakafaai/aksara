# Add the powers with their signs, then treat the pair as one converging lens

The powers add with their signs: <InlineMath math="+5.0 - 2.0 = +3.0" /> D, a **converging** pair with <InlineMath math="f = 33.3" /> cm.

With the lamp at <InlineMath math="50" /> cm, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{v} = \tfrac{1}{33.3} - \tfrac{1}{50} = 0.010" />, so the image is real, <InlineMath math="100" /> cm behind the lenses, and <InlineMath math="100 \div 50 = 2" /> times as tall, upside down.

**Refocusing with one lens**: alone the converging lens has <InlineMath math="f = 20" /> cm. With the image held at <InlineMath math="100" /> cm, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{u} = \tfrac{1}{20} - \tfrac{1}{100} = 0.040" />, so the lamp must move to <InlineMath math="25" /> cm, **much closer**, because the stronger lens bends the light more.

**The new image size**: the image is still <InlineMath math="100" /> cm away but the lamp is now <InlineMath math="25" /> cm away, so the image is <InlineMath math="100 \div 25 = 4" /> times as tall, **twice as big** as before.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}" /> ignores that removing a lens **changes the power**.
- <InlineMath math="0.25" /> times divides the lamp’s distance by the image’s, the **wrong way round**.
- <InlineMath math="17\text{ cm}" /> adds the reciprocals when finding the lamp’s distance, but a real image needs them **subtracted**.

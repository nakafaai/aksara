# Count the rows from the truth table, then rewrite the circuit for NAND gates

The heater is on in the **four rows** where <InlineMath math="F = 1" />, and in one more row where <InlineMath math="T = W = 1" /> but <InlineMath math="F = 0" />: <InlineMath math="5" /> of the <InlineMath math="8" /> combinations.

By De Morgan’s rule, <InlineMath math="H = \overline{\overline{T \cdot W} \cdot \overline{F}}" />. One NAND gate makes <InlineMath math="\overline{T \cdot W}" />, a second with both inputs joined makes <InlineMath math="\overline{F}" />, and a third NAND combines them. So **three** gates are enough.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="5" /> gates swaps each AND and OR for its NAND version separately, but the inverters between them **cancel**.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> combinations counts only the frost rows and misses the **cold, shut** row.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> gates leaves out the gate that **inverts** <InlineMath math="F" />.

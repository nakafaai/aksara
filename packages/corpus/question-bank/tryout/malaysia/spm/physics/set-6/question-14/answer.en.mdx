# One loop is half a wavelength, the string’s wave speed stays, and the air keeps the frequency

One loop between two nodes is **half a wavelength**, so the open string carries waves of <InlineMath math="1.30" /> m at <InlineMath math="110 \times 1.30 = 143" /> m/s. Fretted, the wavelength is <InlineMath math="0.98" /> m and the frequency <InlineMath math="143 \div 0.98 = 146" /> Hz.

The sound in the air has the **same frequency** as the string but travels at <InlineMath math="340" /> m/s, so its wavelength is <InlineMath math="340 \div 146 = 2.3" /> m.

**Tuning up instead**: keeping the full <InlineMath math="0.65" /> m means keeping the <InlineMath math="1.30" /> m wavelength, so the wave speed must rise by <InlineMath math="146 \div 110 = 1.33" />. Speed goes as the square root of tension, so the tension must rise by <InlineMath math="1.33^2 = 1.8" />, **the square of the frequency ratio**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="73\text{ Hz}" /> takes the whole string length as a wavelength, but one loop is only **half** a wavelength.
- <InlineMath math="83\text{ Hz}" /> scales the frequency the wrong way, but a shorter string vibrates **faster**.
- <InlineMath math="1.3" /> times scales the tension with the frequency, but the tension goes as the **square** of the wave speed.

# Weigh Jupiter with Io, then use the same GM twice

With Io’s period in seconds, <InlineMath math="1.53 \times 10^5" /> s, the orbit law gives <InlineMath math="GM = 4\pi^2 r^3 \div T^2 = 1.25 \times 10^{17}\text{ m}^3\text{ s}^{-2}" />. At the cloud tops, <InlineMath math="g = GM \div r^2 = 1.25 \times 10^{17} \div 4.9 \times 10^{15} = 26" /> N/kg.

A satellite that stays above one point must go round once every <InlineMath math="9.9" /> hours, <InlineMath math="3.56 \times 10^4" /> s. Then <InlineMath math="r^3 = GM T^2 \div 4\pi^2 = 4.0 \times 10^{24}" />, so <InlineMath math="r = 1.59 \times 10^8" /> m. That is a radius, measured **from the centre**, so the height above the cloud tops is <InlineMath math="1.59 \times 10^8 - 7.0 \times 10^7 = 8.9 \times 10^7" /> m.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.6 \times 10^8\text{ m}" /> is the orbit’s radius, measured from Jupiter’s **centre**, not its height.
- <InlineMath math="6.4\text{ N kg}^{-1}" /> uses <InlineMath math="\pi^2" /> in place of <InlineMath math="4\pi^2" />, losing a **factor of four** in GM.
- <InlineMath math="2.2 \times 10^8\text{ m}" /> uses a **24-hour** day, but the satellite must keep pace with Jupiter’s own spin.

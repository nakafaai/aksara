# Work out the power for each distance, then let the glasses supply what the eye cannot

Focusing distant objects on a retina <InlineMath math="0.020" /> m away needs <InlineMath math="1 \div 0.020 = 50" /> D. At <InlineMath math="25" /> cm the eye needs <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{0.25} + \tfrac{1}{0.020} = 54" /> D, so the young eye can **change its power by** <InlineMath math="4.0" /> D.

The older eye reaches only <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{1.0} + 50 = 51" /> D, a range of <InlineMath math="1.0" /> D. Reading at <InlineMath math="25" /> cm still needs <InlineMath math="54" /> D, so the glasses must add <InlineMath math="54 - 51 = +3.0" /> D.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="54\text{ D}" /> is the eye’s **total** power at <InlineMath math="25" /> cm, not how much it can change.
- <InlineMath math="+1.0\text{ D}" /> gives the older eye’s own range as the glasses, but the glasses must make up the **missing** power.
- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ D}" /> and <InlineMath math="+4.0\text{ D}" /> swap the two people’s ranges, and glasses add to what the eye **can still do**.

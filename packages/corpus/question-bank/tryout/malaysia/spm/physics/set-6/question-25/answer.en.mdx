# Flotation gives the fraction under water, which fixes the total, then repeat for fresh water

A floating body displaces its own weight, so the fraction under the sea is <InlineMath math="920 \div 1030 = 0.893" /> and the fraction showing is <InlineMath math="0.107" />. The <InlineMath math="3000" /> cubic metres showing are that fraction, so the iceberg holds <InlineMath math="3000 \div 0.107 = 2.8 \times 10^4" /> cubic metres.

In fresh water the fraction under is <InlineMath math="920 \div 1000 = 0.92" />, so only <InlineMath math="0.080 \times 2.8 \times 10^4 = 2.2 \times 10^3" /> cubic metres show, **less than in the sea**.

**Melting it down**: in fresh water <InlineMath math="8.0\%" /> always shows, so <InlineMath math="1000" /> cubic metres showing means a total of <InlineMath math="1000 \div 0.080 = 1.25 \times 10^4" /> cubic metres. From <InlineMath math="2.8 \times 10^4" />, about <InlineMath math="1.6 \times 10^4" /> cubic metres must melt, **more than half** the iceberg.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.5 \times 10^4\text{ m}^3" /> starts from a total worked out with the **fresh-water** fraction for the sea.
- <InlineMath math="1.9 \times 10^4\text{ m}^3" /> melts in proportion to the part showing, but in fresh water the iceberg shows a **fixed fraction** of what remains.
- <InlineMath math="2.7 \times 10^2\text{ m}^3" /> takes the fraction under water as the **fraction showing**.

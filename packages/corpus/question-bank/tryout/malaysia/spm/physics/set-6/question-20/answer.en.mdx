# Find the real image of the bulb, then follow the beam through it to the wall

With the bulb <InlineMath math="4.2" /> cm from the mirror, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{v} = \tfrac{1}{4.0} - \tfrac{1}{4.2}" /> gives <InlineMath math="v = 84" /> cm, a **real image** in front of the mirror, where the beam comes to a point.

The beam shrinks from <InlineMath math="16" /> cm to a point over <InlineMath math="0.84" /> m and then spreads at the same rate. On the wall it is <InlineMath math="16 \times (10 - 0.84) \div 0.84 = 174" /> cm wide, about <InlineMath math="1.7" /> m.

**Focusing on the wall**: for an image <InlineMath math="1000" /> cm away, <InlineMath math="1 \div u = 1 \div 4.0 - 1 \div 1000" />, so <InlineMath math="u = 4.016" /> cm, only <InlineMath math="0.16" /> mm beyond the focus. The bulb sits at <InlineMath math="4.20" /> cm, so it must move about <InlineMath math="1.8" /> mm **towards the mirror**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="16\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm}" /> put the bulb at the focus, which makes a **parallel beam**, not a point on the wall.
- <InlineMath math="1.9\text{ m}" /> measures the spreading from the mirror instead of from the **crossing point**.
- Away from the mirror moves the image **closer**, not out to the wall.

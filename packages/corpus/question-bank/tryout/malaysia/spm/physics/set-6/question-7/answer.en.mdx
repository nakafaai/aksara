# Write every drag as a multiple of the weight, using the terminal speed

At terminal speed the drag equals the weight, <InlineMath math="k \times 50^2 = mg" />. At half that speed the drag is a **quarter** of the weight, so the net force is three quarters of the weight and <InlineMath math="a = 7.5\text{ m s}^{-2}" />.

When the parachute opens at <InlineMath math="50" /> m/s the drag jumps to <InlineMath math="25mg" />. The net force is <InlineMath math="25mg - mg = 24mg" /> upwards, a deceleration of <InlineMath math="240\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, which is why parachutes open **gradually**.

**The new terminal speed**: with <InlineMath math="k" /> twenty-five times larger, the drag equals the weight when <InlineMath math="v^2" /> is twenty-five times smaller, so <InlineMath math="v = 50 \div \sqrt{25} = 10" /> m/s, **a fifth** of the speed without the parachute.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> divides the speed by twenty-five, but the drag goes as the **speed squared**.
- <InlineMath math="250\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> leaves out the **weight**, which still pulls down after the parachute opens.
- Still <InlineMath math="50\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> ignores that a larger <InlineMath math="k" /> balances the weight at a **lower speed**.

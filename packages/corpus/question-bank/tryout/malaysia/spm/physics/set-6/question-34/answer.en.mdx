# The force grows with both current and field, and two reversals cancel

Each side feels <InlineMath math="F = NBIL = 50 \times 0.20 \times 0.50 \times 0.040 = 0.20" /> N, and the two sides <InlineMath math="0.030" /> m apart give a turning effect of <InlineMath math="0.0060" /> N m.

Doubling both the current and the field makes each force **four times** as big, so the turning effect becomes <InlineMath math="0.024" /> N m. Reversing the current flips the force, and reversing the field flips it again, so the coil turns **the same way**.

**Part way round**: with its plane at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> to the field, the turning effect is <InlineMath math="0.024 \times \cos 60^\circ = 0.012" /> N m, **half** its greatest value, still turning the same way.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The opposite way counts only one reversal, but reversing **both** puts the force back.
- <InlineMath math="0.012\text{ N m}" /> at first doubles once, but the force grows with the current **and** the field.
- <InlineMath math="0.021\text{ N m}" /> uses the sine of <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, but the turning effect goes as the **cosine**.

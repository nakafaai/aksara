# Conserve momentum for her speed, then add both kinetic energies for the work

Before the throw the total momentum is zero, so <InlineMath math="60v = 2.0 \times 6.0" /> and she drifts back at <InlineMath math="0.20" /> m/s, reaching the station after <InlineMath math="12 \div 0.20 = 60" /> s.

To arrive in <InlineMath math="45" /> s she needs <InlineMath math="0.267" /> m/s, so the wrench must leave at <InlineMath math="8.0" /> m/s. Her arm gives kinetic energy to **both** of them: <InlineMath math="64 + 2.1 = 66" /> J.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="62\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="71\text{ J}" /> count the wrench in her mass, but after the throw it is **not with her**.
- <InlineMath math="64\text{ J}" /> gives energy only to the wrench and forgets **her own** kinetic energy.
- <InlineMath math="37\text{ J}" /> is the work for the **original throw**, not the faster one.

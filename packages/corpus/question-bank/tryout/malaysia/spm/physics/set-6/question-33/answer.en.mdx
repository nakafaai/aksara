# Power and resistance give the induced current, then the voltages give the turns ratio

The heating power in the base is <InlineMath math="I^2R" />, so <InlineMath math="I = \sqrt{2000 \div 0.010} = 447" /> A, about <InlineMath math="450" /> A, driven by an induced voltage of only <InlineMath math="447 \times 0.010 = 4.47" /> V.

For an ideal transformer the voltages are in the ratio of the turns. The base is **one turn**, so the coil has <InlineMath math="230 \div 4.47 = 51" /> turns. On the mains side the current is only <InlineMath math="2000 \div 230 = 8.7" /> A.

**Fewer turns**: with <InlineMath math="41" /> turns the pan’s voltage rises to <InlineMath math="230 \div 41 = 5.6" /> V, and the power goes as the **square** of that voltage: <InlineMath math="5.6^2 \div 0.010 = 3.1" /> kW.

# Why the other options are wrong

- One turn treats the coil like the pan base, but a coil on <InlineMath math="230" /> V must **step the voltage down**.
- <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ kW}" /> lowers the power with the turns, but fewer primary turns give the pan **more** voltage.
- <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ kW}" /> scales the power with the voltage, but power at a fixed resistance goes as the **square** of the voltage.

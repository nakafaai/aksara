# At a steady temperature the heater’s average power equals the heat loss

To keep the temperature steady the heater must replace the <InlineMath math="600" /> W that leaks out, so it runs <InlineMath math="600 \div 1500 = 40\%" /> of the time. Its **rating does not matter**: each day it delivers <InlineMath math="0.60 \times 24 = 14.4" /> kWh, which costs RM <InlineMath math="7.20" />.

Insulation cuts the loss by a third, to <InlineMath math="400" /> W, so the heater saves <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 24 = 4.8" /> kWh a day, and <InlineMath math="4.8 \times 30 \times 0.50 = 72" /> ringgit a month.

**Adding a heat pump**: the insulated room still needs <InlineMath math="400" /> W of heat, but the pump draws only a third of that, so the daily bill falls from RM <InlineMath math="4.80" /> to RM <InlineMath math="1.60" />. That saves a further RM <InlineMath math="3.20 \times 30 = 96" /> a month.

# Why the other options are wrong

- RM <InlineMath math="12" /> and RM <InlineMath math="240" /> run the heater **non-stop**, ignoring the thermostat.
- RM <InlineMath math="7.20" /> and RM <InlineMath math="144" /> leave out the **insulation**.
- RM <InlineMath math="48" /> saves only a third of the bill, but the pump cuts it **to** a third.

# Read the line as the speed equation of free fall, then work back to the release point

The line is <InlineMath math="v^2 = u^2 + 2gh" />, so the gradient gives <InlineMath math="2g = 19.6" />, <InlineMath math="g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, and the **intercept is the square of the speed** at the top gate: <InlineMath math="u = \sqrt{1.96} = 1.40" /> m/s.

Falling from rest to <InlineMath math="1.40" /> m/s takes a drop of <InlineMath math="1.96 \div 19.6 = 0.10" /> m and a time of <InlineMath math="1.40 \div 9.8 = 0.14" /> s.

**Between two gates**: at <InlineMath math="0.40" /> m below the top gate, <InlineMath math="v^2 = 1.96 + 19.6 \times 0.40 = 9.8" />, so <InlineMath math="v = 3.13" /> m/s. Speeding up from <InlineMath math="1.40" /> m/s takes <InlineMath math="(3.13 - 1.40) \div 9.8 = 0.18" /> s, **less** than from rest because the ball arrives already moving.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> divides the intercept by <InlineMath math="g" /> instead of by <InlineMath math="2g" />, which also makes the ball **too fast** at the top gate.
- <InlineMath math="0.32\text{ s}" /> is the time from the **release**, not from the top gate.
- <InlineMath math="0.29\text{ s}" /> treats the ball as starting **from rest** at the top gate.

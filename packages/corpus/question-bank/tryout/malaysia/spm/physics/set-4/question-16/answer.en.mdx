# The three seconds hold the fall and the sound, so solve for the fall time

If the stone falls for <InlineMath math="t" /> seconds, the depth is <InlineMath math="d = 5t^2" />, and the **sound comes back up** the same depth in the remaining <InlineMath math="3.0 - t" /> seconds: <InlineMath math="5t^2 = 340(3.0 - t)" />.

That is <InlineMath math="5t^2 + 340t - 1020 = 0" />, with <InlineMath math="t = 2.88" /> s, so <InlineMath math="d = 5 \times 2.88^2 = 41" /> m. The stone hits the water at <InlineMath math="10 \times 2.88 = 29" /> m/s.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="45\text{ m}" /> lets the stone fall for the full <InlineMath math="3.0" /> s, forgetting the **sound's travel time**.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> with the right depth still uses <InlineMath math="3.0" /> s of falling for the speed.
- <InlineMath math="14\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> is the average speed during the fall, not the speed at the bottom.

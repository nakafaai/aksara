<InlineMath math="N_4" /> then has two <InlineMath math="1" />s and gives <InlineMath math="S = 0" />, while the NOT gate turns <InlineMath math="N_1" />'s <InlineMath math="0" /> into <InlineMath math="C = 1" />. Read as <InlineMath math="CS = 10" />, the circuit shows two: it is a **half adder**, and <InlineMath math="1 + 1 = 2" />.

**The second half adder**: it adds <InlineMath math="S = 0" /> and the third input <InlineMath math="1" />, giving sum <InlineMath math="1" /> and carry <InlineMath math="0" />. The OR gate combines carries <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="0" /> into <InlineMath math="1" />. The outputs read <InlineMath math="11" /> in binary, which is three: <InlineMath math="1 + 1 + 1 = 3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Two is the first half adder alone, before the **third input** is added.
- One drops the first half adder's carry, but the OR gate passes it on to the final carry.
- Eleven reads the binary digits <InlineMath math="11" /> as a decimal number, but in binary they mean three.

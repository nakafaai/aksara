# Balance the energy over the whole fall, then read the force from the stretch

At the lowest point the jumper is momentarily at rest, so the energy lost over the **whole fall**, <InlineMath math="15 + x" /> metres, is stored in the cord: <InlineMath math="600(15 + x) = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 200 \times x^2" />.

That is <InlineMath math="x^2 - 6x - 90 = 0" />, with <InlineMath math="x = 13" /> m, so the lowest point is <InlineMath math="28" /> m below the platform. There the cord pulls <InlineMath math="200 \times 13 = 2.6 \times 10^3" /> N, more than four times the jumper's weight.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="18\text{ m}" /> is where the cord just holds the weight, the resting point, but the jumper arrives there still moving and goes **much lower**.
- <InlineMath math="21\text{ m}" /> sets <InlineMath math="mg = \tfrac{1}{2}kx" />, mixing a force with an energy.
- <InlineMath math="13\text{ m}" /> is only the stretch of the cord and leaves out its <InlineMath math="15" /> m unstretched length.

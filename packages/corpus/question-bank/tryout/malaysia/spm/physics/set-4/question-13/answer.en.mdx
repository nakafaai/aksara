# Turn wavelengths into speeds, then test both crossings

The frequency stays <InlineMath math="12" /> Hz, so the speeds are <InlineMath math="18" /> and <InlineMath math="12" /> cm/s. Going in, <InlineMath math="\sin r = \sin 30^\circ \times 12 \div 18 = 0.33" />, so <InlineMath math="r = 19^\circ" />, bent **towards** the normal.

Coming back out, the ratio turns over: <InlineMath math="\sin r = \sin 45^\circ \times 18 \div 12 = 1.06" />. No angle has a sine above one, so the wave cannot cross and is totally reflected back into the shallow water.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="49^\circ" /> turns the speed ratio upside down, bending the wave **away** from the normal as it slows down.
- <InlineMath math="28^\circ" /> keeps the inward ratio for the way out, but leaving the slow water the wave speeds up and bends away from the normal.
- An unchanged <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> ignores the change of speed, which is what bends the wave.

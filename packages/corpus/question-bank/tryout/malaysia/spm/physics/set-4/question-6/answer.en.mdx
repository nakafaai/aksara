# Accelerate the pair, then let friction stop the block alone

While the string is taut, the net force on both masses is <InlineMath math="10 - 6.0 = 4.0" /> N on <InlineMath math="4.0" /> kg, so <InlineMath math="a = 1.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" />. The hanging mass accelerates downwards, so the tension is **less than its weight**: <InlineMath math="T = 1.0 \times (10 - 1.0) = 9.0" /> N.

After <InlineMath math="0.50" /> m the block moves at <InlineMath math="1.0" /> m/s. With the string slack, friction alone decelerates the <InlineMath math="3.0" /> kg block at <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, a further <InlineMath math="0.25" /> m, so it slides <InlineMath math="0.75" /> m in all.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ N}" /> sets the tension **equal to the weight**, but then the hanging mass could not accelerate.
- <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ m}" /> stops the block when the mass lands, but the block is still moving at <InlineMath math="1.0" /> m/s.
- <InlineMath math="0.83\text{ m}" /> slows <InlineMath math="4.0" /> kg after the landing, but the landed mass no longer moves with the block.

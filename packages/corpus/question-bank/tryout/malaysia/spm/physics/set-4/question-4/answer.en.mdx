# Share the momentum, time the fall, then compare the energies

The split conserves momentum: <InlineMath math="3.0 \times 40 = 2.0v" />, so the larger piece moves off at <InlineMath math="v = 60" /> m/s, still **horizontally**.

It starts with no vertical speed, so it falls <InlineMath math="45" /> m in <InlineMath math="t = \sqrt{2 \times 45 \div 10} = 3.0" /> s and lands <InlineMath math="60 \times 3.0 = 180" /> m ahead. The kinetic energy rises from <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 3.0 \times 40^2 = 2400" /> J to <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times 60^2 = 3600" /> J, so the split releases <InlineMath math="1200" /> J.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3600\text{ J}" /> is the kinetic energy **after** the split, not the energy the split adds.
- <InlineMath math="40\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> forgets that the momentum of the whole shell now rides on two thirds of its mass.
- <InlineMath math="2400\text{ J}" /> is the kinetic energy the shell already had before the split.

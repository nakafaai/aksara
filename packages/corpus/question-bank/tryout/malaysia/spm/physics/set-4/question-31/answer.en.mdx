# Balance the weight to find the charge, then let the doubled force win

The field is <InlineMath math="2000 \div 0.050 = 4.0 \times 10^4" /> V/m. At rest <InlineMath math="qE = mg" />, so <InlineMath math="q = 3.2 \times 10^{-14} \div 4.0 \times 10^4 = 8.0 \times 10^{-19}" /> C, which is <InlineMath math="5" /> electronic charges. The force is upwards, towards the **positive** upper plate, so the drop is negative: <InlineMath math="5" /> extra electrons.

Doubling the voltage doubles the electric force to <InlineMath math="2mg" /> upwards, while the weight stays <InlineMath math="mg" />. The resultant <InlineMath math="mg" /> upwards gives an acceleration of <InlineMath math="g = 10\text{ m s}^{-2}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> uses the whole electric force and forgets the **weight** still pulls down.
- The drop cannot stay at rest, because the doubled electric force no longer matches its weight.
- A drop with electrons missing is positive and would be pushed **down**, away from the positive upper plate.

# Turn the gap into a difference of travel times, then find the P wave's own time

Over a distance <InlineMath math="d" />, the S waves take <InlineMath math="d \div 4.5" /> and the P waves <InlineMath math="d \div 8.0" />. The gap is the **difference of the travel times**: <InlineMath math="d\left(\tfrac{1}{4.5} - \tfrac{1}{8.0}\right) = 20" />, so <InlineMath math="d = 206" /> km.

The P waves needed <InlineMath math="206 \div 8.0 = 25.7" /> s to arrive, so the earthquake happened <InlineMath math="25.7" /> s before the P waves reached the station.

**Tsunami warning**: the tsunami needs <InlineMath math="206\,000 \div 200 = 1030" /> s from the earthquake. The P waves arrived <InlineMath math="25.7" /> s after the earthquake, so the warning is <InlineMath math="1030 - 25.7 = 1004" /> s, about <InlineMath math="16.7" /> minutes.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="70\text{ km}" /> multiplies the gap by the **difference of the speeds**, but the gap comes from the difference of the travel times.
- <InlineMath math="16.4" /> minutes counts from the S waves, but the P waves arrive first and give the earlier warning.
- <InlineMath math="17.2" /> minutes is the tsunami's whole travel time, but the warning only starts when the P waves arrive.

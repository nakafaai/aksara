For a top speed <InlineMath math="v" />, the two slopes cover <InlineMath math="v^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}v^2" /> metres in <InlineMath math="3v" /> seconds, so <InlineMath math="1.5v^2 + v(120 - 3v) = 2000" />, which is **a quadratic**: <InlineMath math="1.5v^2 - 120v + 2000 = 0" />. Its smaller root is <InlineMath math="v = 23.7" /> m/s. The larger root would need more time on the slopes than the whole <InlineMath math="120" /> s.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="21.7\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> scales the speed by <InlineMath math="130 \div 120" />, but **the slopes also lengthen** as the top speed rises.
- <InlineMath math="100\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="16.7\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> treat the whole run as steady speed, with no time spent speeding up or slowing down.
- <InlineMath math="160\text{ s}" /> adds the full <InlineMath math="60" /> s of the slopes to <InlineMath math="2000 \div 20" />, as if the slopes covered no distance.

# Take the two changes in order, then compare the inside with the outside

Cooling at fixed volume follows the **kelvin** temperature: from <InlineMath math="300" /> K to <InlineMath math="250" /> K the pressure falls to <InlineMath math="100 \times 250 \div 300 = 83" /> kPa, and the bottle would be pushed inwards.

Squeezing at fixed temperature follows Boyle's law: <InlineMath math="83 \times 2.0 \div 1.5 = 111" /> kPa. The inside now beats the outside by <InlineMath math="11" /> kPa, so the cap feels <InlineMath math="11 \times 10^3 \times 4.0 \times 10^{-4} = 4.4" /> N outwards.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="83\text{ kPa}" /> stops after the cooling and forgets the bottle is then **squeezed**.
- <InlineMath math="133\text{ kPa}" /> squeezes the air but skips the cooling, which first lowered the pressure.
- <InlineMath math="44\text{ N}" /> converts the cap's area wrongly: <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}^2" /> is <InlineMath math="4.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2" />.

A bar magnet is pushed at a steady speed into a coil connected to a meter, and the induced current is a steady <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mA}" /> while it moves in. The magnet is then pushed in over the same distance at twice the speed. The resistance of the circuit stays the same.

What is the induced current during the faster push, how does the electrical energy produced in the whole push compare with the first push, and how do the current and the energy compare with the first push when the faster push is repeated with a coil of twice as many turns and twice the resistance?

# Pressure for the force, shared volume for the distance

The lever gives <InlineMath math="400" /> N on the master piston, a pressure of <InlineMath math="400 \div 2.0 \times 10^{-4} = 2.0 \times 10^6" /> Pa. By Pascal's principle **every** wheel piston feels that pressure: <InlineMath math="2.0 \times 10^6 \times 6.0 \times 10^{-4} = 1200" /> N each.

The master piston moves <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cm and pushes out <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of fluid, which is shared among four pistons of total area <InlineMath math="24\text{ cm}^2" />. Each moves <InlineMath math="4.0 \div 24 = 0.17" /> cm, about <InlineMath math="1.7" /> mm.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="300\text{ N}" /> shares the force among the four pistons, but each one feels the **full pressure**.
- <InlineMath math="6.7\text{ mm}" /> sends all the fluid to one piston, but it is shared among four.
- <InlineMath math="4800\text{ N}" /> multiplies each piston's force by four again, counting the shared pressure four times.

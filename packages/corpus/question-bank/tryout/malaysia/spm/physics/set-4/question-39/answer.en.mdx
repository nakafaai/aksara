# One fission, every nucleus, the useful share, then the time

One fission releases <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 1.66 \times 10^{-27} \times (3.0 \times 10^8)^2 = 3.0 \times 10^{-11}" /> J. One kilogram holds <InlineMath math="1000 \div 235 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 2.56 \times 10^{24}" /> nuclei, so it releases <InlineMath math="7.7 \times 10^{13}" /> J.

Only <InlineMath math="30\%" />, <InlineMath math="2.3 \times 10^{13}" /> J, becomes **electrical energy**. At <InlineMath math="1.0 \times 10^9" /> W that lasts <InlineMath math="2.3 \times 10^4" /> s, about <InlineMath math="6.4" /> hours.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="21" /> hours turns all the fission energy into electricity, ignoring the <InlineMath math="30\%" /> **efficiency**.
- About <InlineMath math="71" /> hours divides by the efficiency, as if the losses added energy instead of wasting it.
- About <InlineMath math="1500" /> hours counts a kilogram as <InlineMath math="1000" /> moles, forgetting a mole of uranium-235 has a mass of <InlineMath math="235" /> g.

# The water carries the same total weight both ways, but the stone helps when it is under water

With the block just under the surface it displaces <InlineMath math="500" /> g of water, which must carry the block's <InlineMath math="0.60 \times 500 = 300" /> g plus the stone: <InlineMath math="500 - 300 = 200" /> g.

Hanging under water, the stone of volume <InlineMath math="200 \div 3.0 = 67\text{ cm}^3" /> now **displaces water itself**. The total still to be carried is <InlineMath math="500" /> g, so the block needs to displace only <InlineMath math="500 - 67 = 433\text{ cm}^3" />, and <InlineMath math="67\text{ cm}^3" /> of it rises above the water.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Staying just under the surface forgets that the hanging stone now displaces water too, which **lifts** the block.
- <InlineMath math="300\text{ g}" /> sets the stone equal to the block's mass, but the stone only has to make up the gap to <InlineMath math="500" /> g.
- <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" /> treats the hanging stone as if it were gone, but its weight still pulls on the block through the thread.

# Put the sample on the same footing as the living value, then count halvings

Remove the background first: <InlineMath math="16 - 1.0 = 15" /> counts per minute. The living value is **per gram**, so divide by the mass: <InlineMath math="15 \div 4.0 = 3.75" /> counts per minute per gram.

The sample keeps <InlineMath math="3.75 \div 15 = \tfrac{1}{4}" /> of the living activity, which is two halvings. The campfire is <InlineMath math="2 \times 5730 = 11\,460" /> years old.

**The second fire**: <InlineMath math="4.75 - 1.0 = 3.75" /> counts per minute over <InlineMath math="4.0" /> g is <InlineMath math="0.94" /> per gram, a sixteenth of the living value: four half-lives, <InlineMath math="22\,920" /> years. It was lit <InlineMath math="22\,920 - 11\,460 = 11\,460" /> years before the first fire.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="10\,900" /> years forgets to remove the **background**, which matters more and more as the count gets smaller.
- About <InlineMath math="22\,900" /> years counts four half-lives for a quarter, but a quarter is two halvings.
- About <InlineMath math="22\,920" /> years earlier is the second fire's whole age, not how much older it is than the first.

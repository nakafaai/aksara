# Add the three stages, then divide by the net rate of removal

Cooling the water to <InlineMath math="0^\circ\text{C}" /> removes <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 4200 \times 20 = 42\,000" /> J, freezing <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 3.34 \times 10^5 = 167\,000" /> J, and cooling the ice <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 2100 \times 10 = 10\,500" /> J: <InlineMath math="219\,500" /> J in all.

The leak puts back <InlineMath math="10" /> W of the <InlineMath math="50" /> W, so heat leaves at a **net** <InlineMath math="40" /> W. The time is <InlineMath math="219\,500 \div 40 = 5490" /> s, about <InlineMath math="91" /> minutes.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="61" /> minutes adds the leak to the freezer's rate, but the leak works **against** it.
- About <InlineMath math="73" /> minutes uses the full <InlineMath math="50" /> W and forgets the leak.
- About <InlineMath math="87" /> minutes stops at <InlineMath math="0^\circ\text{C}" /> ice and leaves out cooling it to <InlineMath math="-10^\circ\text{C}" />.

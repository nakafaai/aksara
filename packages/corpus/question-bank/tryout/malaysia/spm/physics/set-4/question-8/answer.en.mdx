# Get GM from the surface, the radius from the day, then subtract the Earth

<InlineMath math="GM = gR^2 = 9.8 \times (6.4 \times 10^6)^2 = 4.0 \times 10^{14}" />. With <InlineMath math="T = 86\,400" /> s, <InlineMath math="r^3 = \dfrac{GMT^2}{4\pi^2} = 7.6 \times 10^{22}" />, so <InlineMath math="r = 4.2 \times 10^7" /> m, measured from the **centre** of the Earth.

The height above the surface is <InlineMath math="4.2 \times 10^7 - 6.4 \times 10^6 = 3.6 \times 10^7" /> m. The satellite travels the whole circle of radius <InlineMath math="r" /> in a day: <InlineMath math="v = 2\pi r \div T = 3.1 \times 10^3" /> m/s.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.2 \times 10^7\text{ m}" /> is the orbit's radius from the Earth's centre, not its **height above the surface**.
- <InlineMath math="7.9\text{ km s}^{-1}" /> is the speed of an orbit just above the surface, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{gR}" />, which is far faster than an orbit this high.
- <InlineMath math="2.6\text{ km s}^{-1}" /> uses the height instead of the radius for the circle the satellite travels.

# Find the pressure inside, then the pressure difference across the piston

At constant temperature, <InlineMath math="100 \times 20 = p \times 50" />, so <InlineMath math="p = 40" /> kPa inside. The atmosphere still pushes on the outside, so the force needed comes from the **difference**: <InlineMath math="(100 - 40) \times 10^3 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-4} = 12" /> N.

On the mountain the air starts at <InlineMath math="70" /> kPa and falls to <InlineMath math="28" /> kPa, while the outside presses with only <InlineMath math="70" /> kPa: <InlineMath math="(70 - 28) \times 10^3 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-4} = 8.4" /> N. Both pressures scale by the same factor, so the force does too.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The same <InlineMath math="12\text{ N}" /> on the mountain forgets that **both** the outside and the inside pressures are lower there.
- <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ N}" /> uses only the pressure inside, but the air outside pushes the piston back in.
- <InlineMath math="20\text{ N}" /> uses only the atmosphere outside and forgets the air inside still pushes outwards.

# Time from cathode to screen

<BlockMath math="\tfrac12mv^2=eV\ \Rightarrow\ v=\sqrt{\frac{2(1.6\times10^{-19})(2000)}{9.11\times10^{-31}}}=2.65\times10^7\text{ m s}^{-1}" />

**In the gap the electron speeds up uniformly from rest, so its average speed is only <InlineMath math="v/2" />**. After the anode it keeps the full speed <InlineMath math="v" />.

<BlockMath math="t=\frac{0.020}{v/2}+\frac{0.20}{v}=1.51+7.55=9.1\text{ ns}" />

# Electrons in flight

**The current says how many electrons leave the cathode each second**. Each spends <InlineMath math="9.1\text{ ns}" /> in the tube.

<BlockMath math="N=\frac{I}{e}\,t=\frac{0.32\times10^{-3}}{1.6\times10^{-19}}\times9.1\times10^{-9}=1.8\times10^{7}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.5\times10^{7}" /> **counts only the electrons in the drift to the screen**.
- <InlineMath math="1.7\times10^{7}" /> lets the electrons cross the gap at the full final speed.
- <InlineMath math="2.6\times10^{7}" /> drops the half in the kinetic energy, which lowers the speed and lengthens the time.

III is true because the regular pattern comes from two sources interfering, and one slit alone only spreads the waves out. **IV breaks the condition the formula rests on**: a strong signal beside the centre needs <InlineMath math="a\sin\theta=\lambda" />, and with <InlineMath math="a=1.0\text{ cm}" /> less than <InlineMath math="\lambda=1.5\text{ cm}" /> that would need <InlineMath math="\sin\theta=1.5" />. Only the central strong signal is left, so the <InlineMath math="75\text{ cm}" /> from <InlineMath math="\lambda=ax/D" /> never appears. So I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, but **with one slit there is nothing to interfere with**, so the regular pattern goes.
- I, II and IV only accepts IV, which applies <InlineMath math="\lambda=ax/D" /> where no strong signal beside the centre can exist.
- III and IV only drops the two correct calculations and keeps the false IV.

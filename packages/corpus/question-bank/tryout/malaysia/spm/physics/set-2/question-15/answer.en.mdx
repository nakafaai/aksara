# The highest frozen rate gives the wave frequency

Each turn opens <InlineMath math="4" /> slits, so the flash frequency is <InlineMath math="4\times6.0=24\text{ Hz}" />. **The highest rate that freezes the waves is the one where one wave passes per flash**, so the wave frequency is <InlineMath math="24\text{ Hz}" />.

<BlockMath math="v=f\lambda=24\times1.5=36\text{ cm s}^{-1}" />

# The slower disc lets the waves gain a little each flash

**At <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ rev s}^{-1}" /> the flashes come at <InlineMath math="20\text{ Hz}" />**. Between flashes the waves travel <InlineMath math="36/20=1.8\text{ cm}" />, one wavelength plus <InlineMath math="0.3\text{ cm}" />, so each crest seems to step <InlineMath math="0.3\text{ cm}" /> forward.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **Backward drift would need flashes faster than <InlineMath math="24\text{ Hz}" />, so that each crest falls short of the next position**.
- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ cm s}^{-1}" /> uses the change of <InlineMath math="1\text{ rev s}^{-1}" /> and forgets the <InlineMath math="4" /> slits per turn.
- <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ cm s}^{-1}" /> takes the wave frequency as <InlineMath math="6\text{ Hz}" />, one flash per turn.

# Lift at sea level

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta P&=\tfrac12(1.2)\left(75^2-65^2\right)=840\text{ Pa}\\
F&=840\times20=16\,800\text{ N}\ \Rightarrow\ m=1680\text{ kg}
\end{aligned}" />

# Change the density and the speeds together

The pressure difference goes with <InlineMath math="\rho v^2" />. **Both speeds grow by <InlineMath math="1.2" />, so the difference of their squares grows by <InlineMath math="1.44" />**, while the thinner air multiplies it by <InlineMath math="0.75" />.

<BlockMath math="m=1680\times1.44\times0.75=1814\text{ kg}\approx1810\text{ kg}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2420\text{ kg}" /> **uses the faster speeds but forgets the thinner air**.
- <InlineMath math="1510\text{ kg}" /> scales the lift by <InlineMath math="1.2" /> instead of <InlineMath math="1.2^2" />.
- <InlineMath math="1260\text{ kg}" /> applies the thinner air but forgets the faster take-off.

# The electron: momentum, then kinetic energy

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
p&=\frac{h}{\lambda}=\frac{6.63\times10^{-34}}{1.0\times10^{-10}}=6.63\times10^{-24}\text{ kg m s}^{-1}\\
E_k&=\frac{p^2}{2m}=\frac{(6.63\times10^{-24})^2}{2(9.11\times10^{-31})}=2.41\times10^{-17}\text{ J}\\
V&=\frac{E_k}{e}=151\text{ V}
\end{aligned}" />

# The photon follows a different rule

**A photon's energy is <InlineMath math="hc/\lambda" />, not <InlineMath math="p^2/2m" />**, so the same wavelength does not mean the same energy.

<BlockMath math="E=\frac{hc}{\lambda}=1.99\times10^{-15}\text{ J},\qquad \frac{E}{E_k}=\frac{1.99\times10^{-15}}{2.41\times10^{-17}}=82" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- **A ratio of <InlineMath math="1" /> assumes that equal wavelengths carry equal energies**.
- <InlineMath math="12.4\text{ kV}" /> applies the photon relation to the electron.
- <InlineMath math="302\text{ V}" /> and <InlineMath math="41" /> drop the half in <InlineMath math="p^2/2m" />.

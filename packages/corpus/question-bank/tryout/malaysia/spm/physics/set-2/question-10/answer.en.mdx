# Check whether all the steam can condense

The water and the can absorb heat only until they reach <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
Q_{\text{max}}&=\left(0.30\times4200+0.20\times900\right)(100-20)=1440\times80=115\,200\text{ J}\\
mL&=0.060\times2.26\times10^6=135\,600\text{ J}
\end{aligned}" />

**The steam could release more than the water and the can are able to absorb**, so not all of it condenses and the mixture stops at <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Mass that condenses

<BlockMath math="m=\frac{115\,200}{2.26\times10^6}=0.051\text{ kg}=51\text{ g}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="112^\circ\text{C}" /> **solves the usual heat balance blindly, but water in an open can cannot pass <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="60\text{ g}" /> assumes all the steam condenses, which would release more heat than can be absorbed.
- <InlineMath math="45\text{ g}" /> leaves out the heat taken in by the aluminium can.

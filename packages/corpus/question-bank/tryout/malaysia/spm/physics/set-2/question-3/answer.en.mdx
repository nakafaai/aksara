# Judge each statement on its own

Statement I comes from the drag force. <InlineMath math="\eta=\dfrac{F}{6\pi rv}" /> has units <InlineMath math="\dfrac{\text{kg m s}^{-2}}{\text{m}\cdot\text{m s}^{-1}}=\text{kg m}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}" />, so I is true. Statement II follows the formula, since <InlineMath math="v_t\propto r^2" /> for the same materials, so doubling <InlineMath math="r" /> gives four times the speed.

**Statement III leaves out the upthrust**. At terminal speed the forces balance, so drag plus upthrust equals the weight, and the drag alone is smaller than the weight. That is why <InlineMath math="(\rho-\sigma)" /> appears in the formula. Statement IV is false because the seven SI base quantities are length, mass, time, current, temperature, amount of substance and luminous intensity.

So only I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only keeps III. **The upthrust also holds the sphere up**, so drag is less than the weight.
- II and IV only drops the correct unit in I and accepts <InlineMath math="\eta" /> as a base quantity, but it is a derived quantity.
- I, II and III only again accepts III, which ignores the upthrust.

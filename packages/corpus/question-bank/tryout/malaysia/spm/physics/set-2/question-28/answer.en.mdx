# Size <InlineMath math="R" /> for normal brightness

Each lamp draws <InlineMath math="3.0/6.0=0.50\text{ A}" /> and has resistance <InlineMath math="6.0^2/3.0=12\,\Omega" />. The pair draws <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ A}" /> at <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ V}" />, and **the internal resistance takes its share of the remaining <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ V}" />**.

<BlockMath math="12-6.0=1.0\times(R+1.0)\ \Rightarrow\ R=5.0\,\Omega" />

# One lamp left

**The remaining lamp is now alone in series with <InlineMath math="R" /> and the internal resistance**. The total resistance rises, but the lamp now takes the whole current instead of half of it.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
I&=\frac{12}{12+5.0+1.0}=0.667\text{ A}\\
P&=0.667^2\times12=5.3\text{ W}
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ W}" /> **assumes the remaining lamp carries on unchanged**.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\,\Omega" /> forgets the internal resistance, and its <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ W}" /> carries that slip.
- <InlineMath math="4.0\,\Omega" /> counts the internal resistance twice.

# Energy falls faster than amplitude

The energy of an oscillation goes with the square of its amplitude. **Each oscillation keeps <InlineMath math="0.9" /> of the amplitude but only <InlineMath math="0.9^2=0.81" /> of the energy**.

<BlockMath math="0.81^3=0.53,\qquad 0.81^4=0.43" />

So the energy is below half after <InlineMath math="4" /> oscillations, and **I** is correct. It first drops below half there, not after <InlineMath math="7" />, so **IV** is wrong.

# Amplitude

<BlockMath math="0.9^6=0.53,\qquad 0.9^7=0.48" />

The amplitude is below half after <InlineMath math="7" /> oscillations, so **III** is correct.

# Period

For small swings the period of a simple pendulum depends only on its length and <InlineMath math="g" />, not on its amplitude, so **II** is wrong.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **Options with IV confuse the energy with the amplitude, which halves after <InlineMath math="7" />**.
- I, II and III only assumes that a smaller swing is quicker.

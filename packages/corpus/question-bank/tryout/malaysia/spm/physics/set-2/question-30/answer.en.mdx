# Read one cell

**The <InlineMath math="V" /> intercept is the e.m.f., <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ V}" />, and the <InlineMath math="I" /> intercept is the short-circuit current, so <InlineMath math="r=1.5/3.0=0.50\,\Omega" />**.

# A reversed cell subtracts its e.m.f. but still adds its resistance

**The backwards cell opposes one of the others**, so the net e.m.f. is <InlineMath math="1.5+1.5-1.5=1.5\text{ V}" />. Its internal resistance is still in the path of the current, so all three resistances add.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
I&=\frac{1.5}{2.0+3(0.50)}=0.43\text{ A}\\
V&=0.43\times2.0=0.86\text{ V}
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.29\text{ A}" /> **ignores the reversed cell and uses <InlineMath math="4.5\text{ V}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ A}" /> leaves out the internal resistance of the reversed cell.
- <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ A}" /> ignores every internal resistance.

# Follow the light through lens, mirror and lens again

On the first pass the lens would form an image at <InlineMath math="v" /> with <InlineMath math="\dfrac1{20}=\dfrac1{30}+\dfrac1v" />, so <InlineMath math="v=60\text{ cm}" />. That point lies <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> beyond the mirror, so the light never reaches it. **The mirror folds the converging light back, and it meets <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> in front of the mirror**, which is <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> from the lens.

That point is the lens's focal point. Light diverging from the focal point leaves a convex lens parallel to the axis, so <InlineMath math="v\to\infty" /> and no final image forms.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A real image <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> in front of the lens uses <InlineMath math="u=60\text{ cm}" /> on the way back, the distance to the would-be image, instead of <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" />. **The mirror moves the image to <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> from the lens**.
- A real image <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> behind the lens is where the reflected light meets, before it passes back through the lens.
- A real image <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}" /> in front of the lens simply mirrors the first-pass result, ignoring the mirror's position.

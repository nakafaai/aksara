# Upper part: bring infinity to the far point

**A very distant object must form a virtual image at <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m}" />, so <InlineMath math="f=-2.0\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="P=-0.5\text{ D}" />**.

# Lower part: bring <InlineMath math="25\text{ cm}" /> out to the near point

**The book at <InlineMath math="25\text{ cm}" /> must form a virtual image at <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}" />**.

<BlockMath math="P=\frac1{0.25}+\frac1{-0.50}=+2.0\text{ D}" />

# The lower part also moves the far limit

**Through the lower part, the farthest clear object is the one whose image lands at the far point, <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m}" />**.

<BlockMath math="\frac1u=2.0-\frac1{-2.0}=2.5\ \Rightarrow\ u=0.40\text{ m}=40\text{ cm}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m}" /> **keeps the bare eye's far point, but the converging lens pulls the clear range in**.
- <InlineMath math="+6.0\text{ D}" /> adds <InlineMath math="1/0.50" /> instead of subtracting it, treating the image as real.
- <InlineMath math="+0.5\text{ D}" /> and <InlineMath math="-2.0\text{ D}" /> swap the signs: a far limit needs a diverging lens and a near limit a converging one.

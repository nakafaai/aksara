# Compare the new speed with the escape speed at r

In a circular orbit <InlineMath math="v=\sqrt{GM/r}" />, and the escape speed from the same radius is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2GM/r}=\sqrt2\,v" />. **So <InlineMath math="\sqrt2\,v" /> is exactly the escape speed where the satellite is**, and I is true. Kinetic energy goes as <InlineMath math="v^2" />, so it doubles, and II is true.

III is false because escape speed falls with distance, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2GM/r}" /> is smaller at <InlineMath math="r" /> than at the surface, not larger. IV is false because <InlineMath math="T=2\pi\sqrt{r^3/GM}" /> contains no mass of the satellite.

So only I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts III. **Escape speed is smaller farther out**, since <InlineMath math="r" /> sits in the denominator.
- II and IV only rejects I and accepts IV, but the period does not depend on the satellite's mass.
- I and III only misses II and keeps the false III.

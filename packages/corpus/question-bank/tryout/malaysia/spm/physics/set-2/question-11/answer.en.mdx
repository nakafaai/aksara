# The atmospheric pressure is unknown on a mountain

With the open end up, the thread adds its <InlineMath math="10\text{ cmHg}" /> to the atmosphere <InlineMath math="P" />. With the open end down, it takes <InlineMath math="10\text{ cmHg}" /> away. **Boyle's law for the two positions gives one equation in <InlineMath math="P" />**, so the usual <InlineMath math="76\text{ cmHg}" /> is not needed and is wrong here.

<BlockMath math="(P+10)(15.0)=(P-10)(20.0)\ \Rightarrow\ 15P+150=20P-200\ \Rightarrow\ P=70\text{ cmHg}" />

# Lay the tube flat

**Horizontal, the thread adds nothing, so the gas is at <InlineMath math="70\text{ cmHg}" />**.

<BlockMath math="L=\frac{80\times15.0}{70}=17.1\text{ cm}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="17.5\text{ cm}" /> **averages the two lengths, but length goes with <InlineMath math="1/P" />, not linearly with the thread's effect**.
- <InlineMath math="76\text{ cmHg}" /> assumes sea-level pressure, which contradicts the two measured lengths.
- <InlineMath math="80\text{ cmHg}" /> is the gas pressure with the open end up, not the atmosphere.

<BlockMath math="800\times8.0=1000\times4.0+\rho_Y\times4.0\ \Rightarrow\ \rho_Y=600\text{ kg m}^{-3}" />

# The extra <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}" /> of Y

**When the boundary on the left rises by <InlineMath math="d" />, the water on the right falls by <InlineMath math="d" />**, so the water levels move <InlineMath math="2d" /> apart. That extra water column balances the extra <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}" /> of Y.

<BlockMath math="1000\times2d=600\times2.0\ \Rightarrow\ d=0.60\text{ cm}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ cm}" /> **lets only one arm move, forgetting that the water drops on one side as it rises on the other**.
- <InlineMath math="1600\text{ kg m}^{-3}" /> assumes the water levels are equal under both columns, and its <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ cm}" /> carries that slip.
- <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ cm}" /> balances Y against oil instead of against water.

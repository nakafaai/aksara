# Speed doubles the e.m.f. and the frequency

Twice the speed means twice as many turns per second, so **I** is correct. The rate of change of flux also doubles, so the peak e.m.f. and the current double at every point of a turn.

# Charge per half-turn does not change

The current doubles, but each half-turn lasts half as long. **The charge in a half-turn depends only on the change of flux and the resistance**, <InlineMath math="Q=N\Delta\Phi/R" />, not on the speed. **II** is wrong.

# Energy per revolution and torque

The average power goes with the e.m.f. squared, so it becomes <InlineMath math="4" /> times larger, while each revolution takes half the time.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
E_{\text{rev}}&=P\,t_{\text{rev}}\ \to\ 4\times\tfrac12=2\ \text{times}\\
\tau&=\frac{P}{\omega}\ \to\ \frac{4}{2}=2\ \text{times}
\end{aligned}" />

So **III** and **IV** are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **Any option with II assumes the doubled current flows for the same time**.
- I and IV only misses that the energy per revolution doubles, though the power quadruples.

# Read the sense of the acceleration from the tilt

The spring pulls the mass up and towards the support. The mass sits east of the support, so **the horizontal pull, and with it the acceleration, points west**. The train moves east, so it is slowing down.

# Balance vertically, then use the horizontal part

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
T\cos15^\circ&=mg\ \Rightarrow\ T=\frac{2.0}{0.966}=2.07\text{ N}\\
a&=g\tan15^\circ=2.7\text{ m s}^{-2}\\
x&=\frac{T}{k}=\frac{2.07}{40}=0.052\text{ m}=5.2\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

**The spring force is larger than the weight, so the extension is more than the <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ cm}" /> it would have at rest**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **Speeding up would leave the mass trailing west of the support, the opposite tilt**.
- <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ cm}" /> divides the weight by <InlineMath math="k" />, but the spring must also supply the horizontal force.
- <InlineMath math="2.6\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> uses <InlineMath math="g\sin15^\circ" /> and also takes the wrong sense.

# Fall time relative to the accelerating floor

At <InlineMath math="t=1.0\text{ s}" /> the lift is still accelerating upwards at <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> and moves up at <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. The bolt shares that velocity, so **relative to the floor it starts at rest and closes in at <InlineMath math="10+1.5=11.5\text{ m s}^{-2}" />**.

<BlockMath math="t=\sqrt{\frac{2(2.5)}{11.5}}=0.66\text{ s}" />

The bolt lands at <InlineMath math="t=1.66\text{ s}" />, before the lift stops accelerating at <InlineMath math="t=2.0\text{ s}" />.

# Where the bolt is in the shaft

**In the shaft the bolt starts with <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> upwards and only gravity acts on it**.

<BlockMath math="s=1.5(0.66)-\tfrac12(10)(0.66)^2=0.99-2.17=-1.18\text{ m}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.71\text{ s}" /> **treats the lift as moving at constant velocity, as it does only after <InlineMath math="t=2.0\text{ s}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="2.17\text{ m}" /> forgets that the bolt starts with the lift's upward velocity.
- <InlineMath math="0.77\text{ s}" /> subtracts the lift's acceleration from <InlineMath math="g" /> instead of adding it.

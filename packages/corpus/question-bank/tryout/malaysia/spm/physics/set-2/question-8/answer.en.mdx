# Radius and period of the orbit

**The field strength falls as <InlineMath math="1/r^2" />, so one ninth of the surface value means <InlineMath math="r=3R" />**. Kepler's third law then compares it with the orbit at <InlineMath math="r=R" />.

<BlockMath math="T=85\times3^{3/2}=442\text{ min}=7.4\text{ h}" />

# The ground below turns too

**The point on the equator moves in the same direction**, so the satellite must gain a whole turn on it. Their angular speeds subtract.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{T_{\text{pass}}}=\frac{1}{7.4}-\frac{1}{24}\ \Rightarrow\ T_{\text{pass}}=10.6\text{ h}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="7.4\text{ hours}" /> **is the orbital period, which ignores the spin of the Earth below**.
- <InlineMath math="5.6\text{ hours}" /> adds the angular speeds, as for a satellite orbiting against the spin.
- <InlineMath math="21.5\text{ hours}" /> takes the height, not the radius, as <InlineMath math="3R" />, so the orbit is at <InlineMath math="4R" />.

# The first image is virtual, so its distance is negative

An upright image three times as tall is **virtual**, so <InlineMath math="v = -4.5" /> cm. Then <InlineMath math="1 \div f = 1 \div 1.5 - 1 \div 4.5" />, giving <InlineMath math="f = 2.25" /> cm.

At <InlineMath math="2.5" /> cm the tooth is just **beyond the focus**: <InlineMath math="1 \div v = 1 \div 2.25 - 1 \div 2.5" /> gives <InlineMath math="v = +22.5" /> cm. The image is real and inverted, <InlineMath math="22.5 \div 2.5 = 9" /> times as tall.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.1\text{ cm}" /> takes the first image as **real**, but an upright image from a concave mirror is virtual.
- Virtual and upright keeps the first kind of image, but the tooth is now **beyond the focus**.
- <InlineMath math="0.11" /> times divides the object distance by the image distance, the **wrong way round**.

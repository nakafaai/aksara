# Balance the heat carried off by the vapour against cooling and freezing

Cooling all the water to <InlineMath math="0^\circ\text{C}" /> needs <InlineMath math="4200 \times 0.100 \times 20 = 8400" /> J. If <InlineMath math="x" /> kg evaporates, only the **rest** freezes, so <InlineMath math="2.5 \times 10^6 x = 8400 + 3.34 \times 10^5 (0.100 - x)" />.

That gives <InlineMath math="x = 41\,800 \div 2.834 \times 10^6 = 0.015" /> kg. So about <InlineMath math="15" /> g evaporates and <InlineMath math="85" /> g is left as ice.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="88\text{ g}" /> leaves out the heat needed first to **cool** the water to <InlineMath math="0^\circ\text{C}" />.
- <InlineMath math="83\text{ g}" /> freezes all <InlineMath math="100" /> g, but the water that **evaporates** never freezes.
- No ice stops at <InlineMath math="0^\circ\text{C}" />, but the pump keeps the water **evaporating** until the rest has frozen.

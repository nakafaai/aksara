# Each connection measures a different combination

With the voltmeter across the resistor alone, the ammeter carries the currents of **both**, so the ratio of the readings is the parallel combination, <InlineMath math="2.0 \times 20 \div 22 = 1.8\,\Omega" />.

With the voltmeter across the resistor and ammeter, the <InlineMath math="2.5\,\Omega" /> branch is in parallel with <InlineMath math="20\,\Omega" />, giving <InlineMath math="2.22\,\Omega" />. The battery’s current is <InlineMath math="6.0 \div (2.22 + 0.50) = 2.20" /> A, and the voltmeter reads <InlineMath math="2.20 \times 2.22 = 4.9" /> V.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.0\,\Omega" /> and <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ V}" /> treat the meters as **ideal**, but these meters change the circuit.
- <InlineMath math="3.9\text{ V}" /> is the voltage across the resistor **alone**, but the second voltmeter also spans the ammeter.
- <InlineMath math="2.5\,\Omega" /> is what the **second** connection gives, the resistor plus the ammeter.

# Only the vertical parts of the tension hold the sign up

Each half of the wire runs <InlineMath math="2.0" /> m across and <InlineMath math="0.10" /> m down, so <InlineMath math="\sin\theta \approx 0.050" />. The two halves together lift the <InlineMath math="196" /> N sign: <InlineMath math="2T \times 0.050 = 196" />, so <InlineMath math="T = 2.0" /> kN, ten times the weight.

A **flatter** wire needs more tension. At <InlineMath math="5.0" /> kN, <InlineMath math="\sin\theta = 196 \div 10\,000 = 0.0196" />, so the sag must be at least <InlineMath math="2.0 \times 0.0196 = 0.039" /> m, about <InlineMath math="3.9" /> cm.

**Hung off-centre**: the two parts now slope differently, by <InlineMath math="0.10 \div 1.0" /> and <InlineMath math="0.10 \div 3.0" />, but they pull sideways **equally** on the hanging point. With that horizontal pull <InlineMath math="H" />, the vertical parts give <InlineMath math="H \times (0.10 + 0.033) = 196" />, so <InlineMath math="H = 1.47" /> kN, and the shorter part, barely steeper, carries about <InlineMath math="1.5" /> kN.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="98\text{ N}" /> shares the weight between the parts as if they pulled **straight up**.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ kN}" /> twice keeps the central tension, but off-centre the parts together are **steeper** and need less.
- <InlineMath math="3.9\text{ kN}" /> and <InlineMath math="2.9\text{ kN}" /> let one part hold the whole sign, forgetting that **two** parts pull up.

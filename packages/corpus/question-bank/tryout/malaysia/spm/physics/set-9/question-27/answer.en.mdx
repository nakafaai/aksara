# Lower pressure outside, slightly lower pressure inside

Climbing <InlineMath math="150" /> m leaves a column of air <InlineMath math="1.2 \times 9.8 \times 150 = 1764" /> Pa lighter above the manometer, about <InlineMath math="1.8" /> kPa. The sealed flask keeps its volume, but cooling by <InlineMath math="1.0" /> K lowers its pressure by <InlineMath math="100\,000 \div 300 = 333" /> Pa.

The flask is now <InlineMath math="1764 - 333 = 1431" /> Pa **above** the outside air, so it pushes the water towards the open arm: the open arm stands <InlineMath math="1431 \div 9800 = 0.15" /> m higher.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="18\text{ cm}" /> ignores the **cooling** of the air in the flask.
- The flask arm higher reverses the push, but the flask is at the **higher** pressure.
- <InlineMath math="21\text{ cm}" /> adds the cooling effect, but a cooler flask pushes **less**, not more.

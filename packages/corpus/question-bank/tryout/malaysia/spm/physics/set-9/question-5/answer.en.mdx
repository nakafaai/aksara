# Compare the greatest deceleration friction allows with the lorry’s

Friction can slow the crate by at most <InlineMath math="0.40 \times 10 = 4.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, but the lorry slows at <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, so the crate **slides forward** across the floor. Relative to the lorry it speeds up from rest at <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" />, and <InlineMath math="3.0 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times t^2" /> gives <InlineMath math="t = 1.7" /> s.

The lorry needs <InlineMath math="20 \div 6.0 = 3.3" /> s to stop, so it is **still braking** at the impact, and the crate hits the wall at <InlineMath math="2.0 \times 1.73 = 3.5" /> m/s relative to the lorry.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> leave out the **friction**, which still slows the crate while it slides.
- <InlineMath math="3.3\text{ s}" /> waits for the lorry to stop, but the crate has already crossed the <InlineMath math="3.0" /> m **long before** that.
- <InlineMath math="13\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> is the crate’s speed over the **road**, not relative to the lorry.

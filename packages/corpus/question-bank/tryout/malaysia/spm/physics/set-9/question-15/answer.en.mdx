# Use the gap between the resonances, not the first length alone

Neighbouring loud lengths are **half a wavelength** apart, so <InlineMath math="\lambda = 2 \times (50.0 - 16.5) = 67.0" /> cm and <InlineMath math="v = 512 \times 0.670 = 343" /> m/s: statement I is correct. The air column is driven by the fork, so at <InlineMath math="50.0" /> cm it still vibrates at <InlineMath math="512" /> Hz, in a longer pattern, and II is wrong.

Striking harder raises the amplitude, not the frequency, so the resonant length is unchanged and III is wrong. At <InlineMath math="256" /> Hz the wavelength doubles to <InlineMath math="134" /> cm, and the first resonance comes near a **quarter** of it, about <InlineMath math="33" /> cm, so IV is correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only accepts II, but a driven air column vibrates at the **fork’s** frequency at every resonance.
- II and III only rejects I, but the gap between the two lengths is **half a wavelength** and gives <InlineMath math="343" /> m/s.
- I, II and IV accepts II, but the second loud length is a longer pattern at the **same** <InlineMath math="512" /> Hz.

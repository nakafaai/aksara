# The air and the column share the atmospheric pressure, and Boyle’s law links them

At first the trapped air is at <InlineMath math="76.0 - 72.0 = 4.0" /> cmHg in an <InlineMath math="8.0" /> cm space. With a column of height <InlineMath math="h" />, the air is at <InlineMath math="74.0 - h" /> and fills <InlineMath math="80.0 - h" /> cm, because the tube’s top is **fixed**. Boyle’s law gives <InlineMath math="(74.0 - h)(80.0 - h) = 32" />.

That is <InlineMath math="h^2 - 154h + 5888 = 0" />, so <InlineMath math="h = 70.6" /> cm and the air is at <InlineMath math="3.4" /> cmHg. The column falls only <InlineMath math="1.4" /> cm, **less** than the <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cmHg fall in pressure, because the expanding air pushes down less.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="70.0\text{ cm}" /> lets the column fall the full <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cm, ignoring that the trapped air **expands** and loses pressure.
- <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cmHg}" /> with <InlineMath math="70.6" /> cm keeps the air’s pressure, but a larger space means **lower** pressure.
- <InlineMath math="70.1\text{ cm}" /> keeps the reading a fixed **fraction** of the true pressure, which Boyle’s law does not give.

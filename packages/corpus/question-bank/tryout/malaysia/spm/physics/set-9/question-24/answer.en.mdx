# The atmosphere acts on both sides, so only the water column counts

The atmosphere pushes on the sea and, through the snorkel, on the air in the lungs, so it **cancels**. The chest feels only the extra <InlineMath math="\rho g h" />, and <InlineMath math="1030 \times 9.8 \times h = 5000" /> gives <InlineMath math="h = 0.50" /> m.

There the water pushes on the chest with <InlineMath math="5000 \times 0.080 = 400" /> N more than the air inside pushes out, which is why snorkels are kept **short**.

**The tube sets its own limit**: with its top <InlineMath math="5" /> cm above the water, the <InlineMath math="30" /> cm tube lets the mouth go only <InlineMath math="25" /> cm deep, so the chest reaches only <InlineMath math="45" /> cm. That is **shallower** than the muscles allow, so it decides the depth. There the extra pressure is <InlineMath math="1030 \times 9.8 \times 0.45 = 4.5" /> kPa, and the extra force <InlineMath math="4540 \times 0.080 = 360" /> N.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="400\text{ N}" /> use only the muscles’ limit, but the **tube** stops the swimmer sooner.
- <InlineMath math="400\text{ N}" /> at <InlineMath math="0.45" /> m uses the muscles’ full <InlineMath math="5.0" /> kPa, but at that depth the water adds **less**.
- <InlineMath math="8.4\text{ kN}" /> adds the atmosphere, but the air in the lungs pushes back with the **same** atmospheric pressure.

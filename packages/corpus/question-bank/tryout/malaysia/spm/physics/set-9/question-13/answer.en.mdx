# Treat the two masts as a double slit

The wavelength is <InlineMath math="3.0 \times 10^8 \div 6.0 \times 10^6 = 50" /> m. The two transmitters behave like two slits <InlineMath math="400" /> m apart, and the road is a screen <InlineMath math="10" /> km away, so the **dark fringes**, where the radio fades, are <InlineMath math="x = \lambda D \div a = 50 \times 10\,000 \div 400 = 1250" /> m apart.

At <InlineMath math="25" /> m/s the car passes one fade every <InlineMath math="1250 \div 25 = 50" /> s.

**Masts out of step**: opposite the midpoint the two paths are equal, so waves that **left half a cycle apart** still arrive half a cycle apart and cancel. That point is a fade, and the others follow every <InlineMath math="1.25" /> km on either side.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.63\text{ km}" /> from the midpoint is right for masts **in phase**, where the midpoint is loud.
- <InlineMath math="0.63\text{ km}" /> apart is the distance from a fade to the next **loud** place, half the spacing.
- <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" /> takes one fade per **wavelength** along the road, but the fringes are spread out by <InlineMath math="D \div a" />.

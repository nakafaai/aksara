# Count the spacings, then shrink the wavelength in water

From the first to the eleventh bright fringe there are **ten** spacings, so <InlineMath math="x = 1.20" /> mm. Then <InlineMath math="\lambda = ax \div D = 0.50 \times 10^{-3} \times 1.20 \times 10^{-3} \div 1.0 = 6.0 \times 10^{-7}" /> m, or <InlineMath math="600" /> nm.

In water the frequency is unchanged but the light is <InlineMath math="1.33" /> times slower, so its wavelength, and with it the fringe spacing, is <InlineMath math="1.33" /> times **smaller**: <InlineMath math="1.20 \div 1.33 = 0.90" /> mm.

**Counting in water**: at <InlineMath math="0.90" /> mm apart, <InlineMath math="12.0 \div 0.90 = 13.3" />, so <InlineMath math="13" /> whole spacings fit. Thirteen spacings join **fourteen** fringes, counting the one at the start.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="545\text{ nm}" /> divides by eleven, but eleven fringes have only **ten** gaps.
- <InlineMath math="13" /> counts the gaps, but the fringes are **one more** than the gaps between them.
- <InlineMath math="11" /> keeps the air pattern, ignoring that the fringes **close up** in water.

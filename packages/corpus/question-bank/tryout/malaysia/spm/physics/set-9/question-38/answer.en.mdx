# Turn the ratio into the fraction of uranium left

Every lead-207 atom was once a uranium-235 atom, so for every uranium atom left there were <InlineMath math="1 + 3 = 4" /> at first. A **quarter** remains, which takes <InlineMath math="2" /> half-lives: the rock is <InlineMath math="1.4 \times 10^9" /> years old.

After one more half-life only an **eighth** of the original uranium remains, so there are <InlineMath math="7" /> lead atoms for every uranium atom.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.1 \times 10^9" /> years takes the ratio of <InlineMath math="3" /> as **three half-lives**, but three lead atoms per uranium atom mean only two.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> doubles the ratio, but it is the **original** atoms per uranium atom that double: <InlineMath math="4" /> becomes <InlineMath math="8" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.1 \times 10^9" /> years treats the uranium as a **third** of the original, but the lead and uranium together make up the original atoms.

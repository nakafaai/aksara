# Find the switching resistance, then share the current at the junction

The transistor switches on when <InlineMath math="0.70" /> V lies across the <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ k}\Omega" /> resistor and <InlineMath math="5.3" /> V across the thermistor, so the thermistor is <InlineMath math="1.0 \times 5.3 \div 0.70 = 7.6\text{ k}\Omega" />: about <InlineMath math="40^\circ\text{C}" />.

At <InlineMath math="50^\circ\text{C}" /> the base **holds** the junction at <InlineMath math="0.70" /> V. The thermistor then carries <InlineMath math="5.3 \div 5.0 = 1.06" /> mA, the fixed resistor takes <InlineMath math="0.70" /> mA, and the base gets the **difference**, <InlineMath math="0.36" /> mA. The collector current is <InlineMath math="100 \times 0.36 = 36" /> mA.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="106\text{ mA}" /> sends the whole thermistor current into the base, forgetting the share taken by the **fixed resistor**.
- <InlineMath math="70\text{ mA}" /> takes the fixed resistor’s current as the base current, but the base gets what is **left over**.
- <InlineMath math="0.36\text{ mA}" /> is the base current, not the collector current, which is **100 times** larger.

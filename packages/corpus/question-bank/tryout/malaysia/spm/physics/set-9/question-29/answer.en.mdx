# At top speed, useful power equals force times speed

The useful power is <InlineMath math="0.85 \times 600 \times 200 = 102" /> kW. At top speed it all goes into pushing against the resistance, so on the level <InlineMath math="v = 102\,000 \div 6000 = 17" /> m/s.

On the hill a **share of the weight** also acts down the road: <InlineMath math="12\,000 \times 9.8 \div 50 = 2352" /> N. The total opposing force is <InlineMath math="8352" /> N, so <InlineMath math="v = 102\,000 \div 8352 = 12" /> m/s.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> uses all <InlineMath math="120" /> kW, ignoring the <InlineMath math="85\%" /> **efficiency**.
- <InlineMath math="16\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> takes the share of the weight as <InlineMath math="12\,000 \div 50" />, forgetting to multiply the mass by **g**.
- <InlineMath math="0.83\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> adds the **whole** weight, but on a gentle hill only a fiftieth of it acts along the road.

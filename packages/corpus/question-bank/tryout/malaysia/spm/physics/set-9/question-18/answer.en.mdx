# Follow the light lens by lens, and check where the first image falls

For the first lens <InlineMath math="1 \div v = 1 \div 10 - 1 \div 15" />, so <InlineMath math="v = 30" /> cm: a real, inverted image twice as tall. It lies only <InlineMath math="35 - 30 = 5" /> cm in front of the second lens, **inside** its focal length.

So the second lens acts as a magnifying glass: <InlineMath math="1 \div v = 1 \div 10 - 1 \div 5" /> gives <InlineMath math="v = -10" /> cm, a virtual image <InlineMath math="10" /> cm in front of it, twice the size of its object and **not turned over again**. The final image is inverted and <InlineMath math="2 \times 2 = 4" /> times as tall.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Beyond the second lens and upright treats the second image as **real**, but an object inside the focus gives a virtual one.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> beyond the first lens stops there, but that image is only the **object** for the second lens.
- Upright assumes the second lens inverts again, but a **virtual** image keeps its object’s way up.

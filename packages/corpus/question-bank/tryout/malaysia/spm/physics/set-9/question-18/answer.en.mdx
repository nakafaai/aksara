# Follow the light through one lens at a time

For the first lens <InlineMath math="1 \div v = 1 \div 10 - 1 \div 15" />, so <InlineMath math="v = 30" /> cm: a real, **inverted** image twice as tall. It lies <InlineMath math="50 - 30 = 20" /> cm in front of the second lens.

For the second lens <InlineMath math="1 \div v = 1 \div 10 - 1 \div 20" />, so <InlineMath math="v = 20" /> cm, with magnification <InlineMath math="1" />. Inverting the inverted image makes it **upright**, and the total magnification is <InlineMath math="2 \times 1 = 2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Inverted forgets that the second lens **inverts again**, turning the image back up.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> beyond the first lens stops there, but that image is only the **object** for the second lens.
- Four times as tall doubles at both lenses, but the second lens has its object at <InlineMath math="2f" />, where it gives **the same size**.

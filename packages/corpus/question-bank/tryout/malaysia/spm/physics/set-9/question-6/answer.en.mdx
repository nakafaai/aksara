# Put both stones on the same clock

Measure time from the first drop. The first stone has fallen <InlineMath math="5t^2" />, and the second <InlineMath math="15(t - 1) + 5(t - 1)^2" />. Setting them equal leaves <InlineMath math="5t = 10" />, so they meet at <InlineMath math="t = 2.0" /> s, <InlineMath math="5 \times 2.0^2 = 20" /> m below the bridge.

The first stone lands at <InlineMath math="\sqrt{45 \div 5} = 3.0" /> s. The second solves <InlineMath math="45 = 15\tau + 5\tau^2" />, so <InlineMath math="\tau = 1.85" /> s after its throw, at <InlineMath math="2.85" /> s. It arrives only <InlineMath math="0.15" /> s **earlier**, because the first stone has been speeding up all the time.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ s}" /> earlier keeps the gap of the throw, but the second stone **overtakes** and stays ahead by much less.
- Landing together would need the meeting point at the **water**, but they meet <InlineMath math="25" /> m above it.
- <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ m}" /> is where the first stone is when the second is **thrown**, not where they meet.

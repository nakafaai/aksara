# Find the rise per stop, then check the peak, not the build-up

Each stop gives the discs <InlineMath math="0.80 \times \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1500 \times 30^2 = 5.4 \times 10^5" /> J, a rise of <InlineMath math="5.4 \times 10^5 \div (16 \times 450) = 75" /> K. Between stops they keep <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of it, so each stop leaves a lasting <InlineMath math="45" /> K.

The **hottest** moment is the end of a stop, when its full <InlineMath math="75" /> K sits on top of the build-up: <InlineMath math="30 + 45(k - 1) + 75 \le 500" /> gives <InlineMath math="k \le 9.8" />. The tenth stop would pass the limit, so <InlineMath math="9" /> stops.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="94\text{ K}" /> puts **all** the kinetic energy into the discs, not <InlineMath math="80\%" />.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> stops ignores the **cooling** between stops.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> stops checks only the lasting <InlineMath math="45" /> K, but during a stop the discs are a **further** <InlineMath math="30" /> K hotter.

# Work in the frame of train Q for both cases

Meeting head on, the trains close at <InlineMath math="45" /> m/s and must cover <InlineMath math="120 + 180 = 300" /> m between them, which takes <InlineMath math="300 \div 45 = 6.7" /> s.

Overtaking, P gains on Q at only <InlineMath math="5" /> m/s, and braking cuts that by <InlineMath math="0.10" /> m/s every second. The gain stops after <InlineMath math="50" /> s, when P has moved <InlineMath math="5^2 \div (2 \times 0.10) = 125" /> m along Q. P needs <InlineMath math="300" /> m to clear Q, so it **never gets past** and then drops back.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Fully past after <InlineMath math="60" /> s ignores the **braking**, which ends the gain long before <InlineMath math="300" /> m.
- <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" /> for the meeting uses the **difference** of the speeds, but trains moving towards each other close at the sum.
- <InlineMath math="250\text{ m}" /> uses <InlineMath math="v^2 \div a" />, leaving out the **half** in <InlineMath math="v^2 = 2as" />.

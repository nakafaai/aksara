# Check whether the spring has enough travel before using it

The trolley has <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times 3.0^2 = 9.0" /> J. Stopping it would need <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 800 \times x^2 = 9.0" />, so <InlineMath math="x = 0.15" /> m, but the spring has only <InlineMath math="0.10" /> m of **travel**, so the trolley reaches the stop still moving.

At full compression the spring holds <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 800 \times 0.10^2 = 4.0" /> J, leaving <InlineMath math="5.0" /> J of kinetic energy: <InlineMath math="v = \sqrt{2 \times 5.0 \div 2.0} = 2.2" /> m/s. The spring’s force is greatest at full compression, <InlineMath math="800 \times 0.10 = 80" /> N.

**Leaving the spring**: the bounce keeps the trolley’s <InlineMath math="5.0" /> J, and as the spring stretches back to its natural length it **returns** its <InlineMath math="4.0" /> J. The trolley leaves with all <InlineMath math="9.0" /> J again, at <InlineMath math="3.0" /> m/s.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.2\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> twice forgets that the spring **gives back** the energy it stored.
- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> takes the stored energy as <InlineMath math="kx^2" />, leaving out the **half**.
- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> on leaving lets the spring take its <InlineMath math="4.0" /> J **again** on the way out, but it releases that energy instead.

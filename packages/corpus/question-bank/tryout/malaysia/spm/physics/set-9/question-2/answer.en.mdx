# The offset cancels, but each division is worth more than a kelvin

Between ice and steam the thermometer moves <InlineMath math="98 - 3 = 95" /> divisions for a true <InlineMath math="100" /> K, so each division is <InlineMath math="100 \div 95" /> K. A rise of <InlineMath math="33" /> divisions is a true rise of <InlineMath math="33 \times 100 \div 95 = 34.7" /> K. The <InlineMath math="3^\circ" /> offset **cancels** in the difference.

With a fixed resistance the power goes as the voltage **squared**, so on <InlineMath math="220" /> V the heater gives only <InlineMath math="200 \times (220 \div 240)^2 = 168" /> W. Then <InlineMath math="c = 168 \times 300 \div (0.40 \times 34.7) = 3.6 \times 10^3\text{ J kg}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="33.0\text{ K}" /> takes the readings at face value, but each division is **more than a kelvin**.
- <InlineMath math="4.3 \times 10^3" /> uses the label’s <InlineMath math="200" /> W, but on <InlineMath math="220" /> V the heater gives **less**.
- <InlineMath math="4.0 \times 10^3" /> scales the power with the voltage once, but it goes as the voltage **squared**.

# The furthest the lens can move gives the largest image distance

A distant scene focuses at <InlineMath math="v = f = 50" /> mm. A nearer object needs a **larger** image distance, and the lens can give at most <InlineMath math="v = 54" /> mm. Then <InlineMath math="1 \div u = 1 \div 50 - 1 \div 54" />, so <InlineMath math="u = 675" /> mm, about <InlineMath math="0.68" /> m.

The magnification is <InlineMath math="v \div u = 54 \div 675 = 0.080" />: the image on the sensor is a twelfth of the object’s size.

**With the extension tube**: the lens now sits at least <InlineMath math="60" /> mm from the sensor, so it can **no longer** focus on distant scenes. The farthest sharp object has <InlineMath math="v = 60" /> mm: <InlineMath math="1 \div u = 1 \div 50 - 1 \div 60" />, so <InlineMath math="u = 300" /> mm, or <InlineMath math="0.30" /> m.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Still focusing on distant scenes forgets that the tube keeps the lens **at least** <InlineMath math="60" /> mm from the sensor.
- <InlineMath math="26\text{ mm}" /> and <InlineMath math="27\text{ mm}" /> **add** the reciprocals, but a real image needs <InlineMath math="1 \div u = 1 \div f - 1 \div v" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.23\text{ m}" /> is the **nearest** distance with the tube, at <InlineMath math="v = 64" /> mm, not the farthest.

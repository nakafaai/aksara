# Average the catches, then let the square root halve the uncertainty

The five distances add to <InlineMath math="100.0" /> cm, so the mean is <InlineMath math="20.0" /> cm, and half the range is <InlineMath math="(21.0 - 18.5) \div 2 = 1.25" /> cm, a fraction of <InlineMath math="6.25\%" />. The time is <InlineMath math="t = \sqrt{2 \times 0.200 \div 9.8} = 0.202" /> s.

Because <InlineMath math="t" /> goes as the **square root** of <InlineMath math="d" />, its fractional uncertainty is half that of <InlineMath math="d" />: <InlineMath math="3.1\%" /> of <InlineMath math="0.202" /> s is <InlineMath math="0.006" /> s.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\pm 0.013\text{ s}" /> carries the whole <InlineMath math="6.25\%" /> into the time, forgetting that the **square root halves** it.
- <InlineMath math="0.143\text{ s}" /> leaves the **<InlineMath math="2" />** out of <InlineMath math="2d" />.
- <InlineMath math="\pm 0.025\text{ s}" /> uses the **whole range** and does not halve it for the square root.

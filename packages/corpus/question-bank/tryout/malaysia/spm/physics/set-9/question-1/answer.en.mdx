# Average the catches, then let the square root halve the uncertainty

The five distances add to <InlineMath math="100.0" /> cm, so the mean is <InlineMath math="20.0" /> cm, and half the range is <InlineMath math="(21.0 - 18.5) \div 2 = 1.25" /> cm, a fraction of <InlineMath math="6.25\%" />. The time is <InlineMath math="t = \sqrt{2 \times 0.200 \div 9.8} = 0.202" /> s.

Because <InlineMath math="t" /> goes as the **square root** of <InlineMath math="d" />, its fractional uncertainty is half that of <InlineMath math="d" />: <InlineMath math="3.1\%" /> of <InlineMath math="0.202" /> s is <InlineMath math="0.006" /> s.

**Timing twenty swings**: both presses come about <InlineMath math="0.202" /> s late, so the lateness **cancels** in the interval. Only the scatter is left: each press may be off by <InlineMath math="0.006" /> s, at most <InlineMath math="0.012" /> s for the two, which is <InlineMath math="0.012 \div 20 = 0.0006" /> s in one period.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ s}" /> adds the two delays, but a delay at **both** ends cancels in the interval.
- <InlineMath math="\pm 0.013\text{ s}" /> forgets that the **square root halves** the fractional uncertainty.
- <InlineMath math="0.0003\text{ s}" /> counts the scatter of **one press**, but the interval has a start and a stop.

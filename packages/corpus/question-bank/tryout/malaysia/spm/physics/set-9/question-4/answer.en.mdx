# Split the relative speed with momentum, then follow the boat

Momentum stays zero, so the person moves back at <InlineMath math="u" /> and the boat forwards at <InlineMath math="v" /> with <InlineMath math="60u = 120v" />. Their speeds **relative to each other** add to <InlineMath math="3.0" /> m/s, so <InlineMath math="u = 2.0" /> m/s and <InlineMath math="v = 1.0" /> m/s.

The boat glides <InlineMath math="1.0^2 \div (2 \times 0.25) = 2.0" /> m. The jump gave <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2} \times 60 \times 2.0^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 120 \times 1.0^2 = 120 + 60 = 180" /> J.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.5\text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="405\text{ J}" /> take the <InlineMath math="3.0" /> m/s as the person’s speed over the water, but it is measured **from the boat**.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ J}" /> counts only the person, but the boat’s **motion** also came from the jump.
- <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m}" /> uses <InlineMath math="v^2 \div a" />, leaving out the **half**.

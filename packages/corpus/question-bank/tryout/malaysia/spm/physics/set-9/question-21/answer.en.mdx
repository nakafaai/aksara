# Compare the two pulls, then let friction supply only the difference

Along the slope the block’s weight gives <InlineMath math="5.0 \times 9.8 \times \sin 30^\circ = 24.5" /> N downwards, and the hanging mass pulls back with <InlineMath math="19.6" /> N. On a smooth slope the net <InlineMath math="4.9" /> N accelerates **both** masses: <InlineMath math="a = 4.9 \div 7.0 = 0.70\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> down the slope.

On the rough slope, holding the block still needs only <InlineMath math="4.9" /> N of friction, less than the <InlineMath math="10" /> N available, so the block **stays at rest** with <InlineMath math="4.9" /> N of friction up the slope.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ N}" /> takes friction at its **limit**, but static friction gives only what is needed.
- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> divides by the block alone, but the hanging mass **speeds up too**.
- No friction mistakes rest for balance without friction, but the slope must still supply the <InlineMath math="4.9" /> N **difference**.

# Compare the two pulls, then let friction supply only the difference

Along the slope the block’s weight gives <InlineMath math="5.0 \times 9.8 \times \sin 30^\circ = 24.5" /> N downwards, and the hanging mass pulls back with <InlineMath math="19.6" /> N. On a smooth slope the net <InlineMath math="4.9" /> N accelerates **both** masses: <InlineMath math="a = 4.9 \div 7.0 = 0.70\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> down the slope.

On the rough slope, holding the block still needs only <InlineMath math="4.9" /> N of friction, less than the <InlineMath math="10" /> N available, so the block **stays at rest** with <InlineMath math="4.9" /> N of friction up the slope.

**Dragging the block up**: to start the block moving up, the string must beat the weight’s share **and** friction, which now acts **down** the slope: <InlineMath math="24.5 + 10 = 34.5" /> N. The hanging mass must weigh more than that, so at least <InlineMath math="34.5 \div 9.8 = 3.5" /> kg.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ N}" /> at rest takes friction at its **limit**, but static friction gives only what is needed.
- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ kg}" /> lets friction help the string, but friction always **opposes** the motion that is starting, here down the slope.
- <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ kg}" /> balances only the weight’s share and forgets the **friction** that must also be overcome.

# Find the energy from the mass lost, then share it by momentum

The mass falls from <InlineMath math="11.02160" /> u to <InlineMath math="11.01860" /> u, a loss of <InlineMath math="0.00300" /> u, so <InlineMath math="0.00300 \times 931.5 = 2.79" /> MeV is released.

Nothing was moving before, so the two nuclei fly apart with **equal and opposite** momenta. Since kinetic energy is <InlineMath math="p^2 \div 2m" />, it is shared in inverse proportion to mass: helium gets <InlineMath math="7 \div 11" /> of the energy, <InlineMath math="1.78" /> MeV.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.02\text{ MeV}" /> gives helium the lithium’s share, but the **lighter** nucleus moves faster and carries more.
- <InlineMath math="1.40\text{ MeV}" /> splits the energy **equally**, but equal momenta do not mean equal energies.
- <InlineMath math="2.79\text{ MeV}" /> gives helium everything, forgetting the lithium must **recoil**.

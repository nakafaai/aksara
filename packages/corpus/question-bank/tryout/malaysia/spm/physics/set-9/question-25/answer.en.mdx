# Compare the water displaced before and after

In the boat, the stone displaces water of its own **weight**: <InlineMath math="30 \div 1000 = 0.030\text{ m}^3" />. On the bottom it displaces only its own **volume**, <InlineMath math="30 \div 3000 = 0.010\text{ m}^3" />. The displaced water falls by <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ m}^3" />, so the pool level falls by <InlineMath math="0.020 \div 20 = 1.0" /> mm.

The boat itself now displaces <InlineMath math="0.030\text{ m}^3" /> less, so it rises <InlineMath math="0.030 \div 2.0 = 1.5" /> cm relative to the water around it.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Rising adds the stone’s volume to the pool, forgetting that it **already displaced** three times as much while it floated.
- <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ mm}" /> forgets that the stone on the bottom **still displaces** its own volume.
- Staying the same treats the displaced water as fixed, but a sunk stone displaces its volume, **not its weight**.

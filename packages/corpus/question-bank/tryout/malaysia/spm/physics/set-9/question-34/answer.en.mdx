# The electromotive force follows how fast the flux changes

The electromotive force depends on how **fast** the flux through the coil changes. When the coil’s plane lies along the field, its sides cut across the field lines at the greatest rate, so the electromotive force is greatest then and statement I is correct. Statement III confuses the most flux with the fastest change: at right angles the flux is greatest but momentarily **not changing**, so the electromotive force is zero.

Turning twice as fast gives twice as many cycles each second and twice the rate of cutting, so II is correct. A split-ring commutator swaps the coil’s connections every half turn, so the outside current always flows the **same way**, and IV is correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only leaves out IV, but a commutator does keep the outside current flowing **one way**.
- II, III and IV accepts III, but the most flux through the coil is the moment when it is **changing slowest**.
- III and IV drops I and II, but the electromotive force does peak with the coil **along** the field and does grow with the speed.

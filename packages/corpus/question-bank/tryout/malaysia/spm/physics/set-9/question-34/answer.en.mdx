# Check each statement against what makes the electromotive force

The electromotive force follows how **fast** the flux changes. At half the turning rate the sides cut the field half as fast, so the peak halves to <InlineMath math="6.0" /> V, and there are half as many cycles, <InlineMath math="25" /> Hz: statement I is correct. When the most lines pass through the coil, the flux is **momentarily not changing**, so the electromotive force is zero there, and II is wrong.

An alternating output heats like a steady supply of its r.m.s. value, <InlineMath math="12 \div \sqrt{2} = 8.5" /> V, so III is correct. A commutator only flips the negative half of each cycle, so the output always pushes one way but still **rises and falls** between zero and <InlineMath math="12" /> V, and IV is wrong.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only accepts II, but the most flux is the moment when the flux is changing **slowest**.
- II and IV only accepts both wrong statements and rejects I and III, which follow from the **rate** of cutting and the r.m.s. value.
- I, III and IV accepts IV, but a commutator gives a **pulsing** one-way output, not a steady one.

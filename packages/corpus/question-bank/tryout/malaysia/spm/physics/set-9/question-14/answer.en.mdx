# Refract one way, then check the critical angle the other way

Going into shallow water, <InlineMath math="\sin r = \sin 50^\circ \times 0.30 \div 0.40 = 0.575" />, so <InlineMath math="r = 35^\circ" />: the waves slow down and bend **towards** the normal.

Going the other way, the waves speed up and bend away from the normal. They can just leave at the critical angle, where <InlineMath math="\sin c = 0.30 \div 0.40" />, so <InlineMath math="c = 48.6^\circ" />. At <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> they are beyond it, so they are **totally reflected** back into the shallow region.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="41^\circ" /> uses the deep-to-shallow ratio again, but waves entering **faster** water bend away from the normal.
- Along the boundary happens only **at** the critical angle, <InlineMath math="48.6^\circ" />, not beyond it.
- <InlineMath math="38^\circ" /> scales the **angle** by the speed ratio, but it is the sines that are in that ratio.

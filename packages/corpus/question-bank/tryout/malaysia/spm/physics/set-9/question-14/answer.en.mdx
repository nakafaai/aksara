# Refract one way, then check the critical angle the other way

Going into shallow water, <InlineMath math="\sin r = \sin 50^\circ \times 0.30 \div 0.40 = 0.575" />, so <InlineMath math="r = 35^\circ" />: the waves slow down and bend **towards** the normal.

Going the other way, the waves speed up and bend away from the normal. They can just leave at the critical angle, where <InlineMath math="\sin c = 0.30 \div 0.40" />, so <InlineMath math="c = 48.6^\circ" />. At <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> they are beyond it, so they are **totally reflected** back into the shallow region.

**How slow the deep water could be**: total reflection at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> needs the critical angle to be no more than <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, so <InlineMath math="0.30 \div v \le \sin 60^\circ" />. The deep water must carry the waves at least <InlineMath math="0.30 \div 0.866 = 0.35" /> m/s: any slower and the waves would **escape** into it.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.26\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="\sin 60^\circ" />, giving a speed **slower** than the shallow water, where no total reflection can happen.
- <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> is the present speed, but the waves would still be reflected at **lower** speeds.
- <InlineMath math="38^\circ" /> scales the **angle** by the speed ratio, but it is the sines that are in that ratio.

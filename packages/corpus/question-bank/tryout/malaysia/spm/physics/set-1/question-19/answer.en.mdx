# The image position measured from the bottom

Apparent depth is measured from the top surface, but the top surface rises when the oil is poured. So **turn the apparent depth into the height of the image above the bottom**, before and after.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{before: }&d=\frac{12}{4/3}=9.0\text{ cm},\ h=12-9.0=3.0\text{ cm}\\
\text{after: }&d=\frac{6.0}{1.5}+\frac{12}{4/3}=13\text{ cm},\ h=18-13=5.0\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

The apparent depth does grow from <InlineMath math="9.0" /> to <InlineMath math="13\text{ cm}" />, but the top surface rises <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ cm}" />, more than that growth. So the image of the coin rises by <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}" /> from the bottom of the container.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Rising <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}" /> assumes the image moves by the apparent thickness of the oil layer, <InlineMath math="6.0/1.5" />.
- Falling <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}" /> reads the growth of the apparent depth from <InlineMath math="9" /> to <InlineMath math="13\text{ cm}" /> as a fall, but **the reference surface rises too**.
- Falling <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}" /> has the right size with the wrong direction.

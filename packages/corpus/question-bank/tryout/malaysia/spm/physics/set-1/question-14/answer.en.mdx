# The frequency stays fixed during refraction

The frequency is set by the source, so **the frequency does not change when the waves enter the shallow region**. From the deep region, <InlineMath math="f=\dfrac{v}{\lambda}=\dfrac{20}{2.0}=10\text{ Hz}" />. The wavelength and the direction then follow the change in speed.

Draw the direction of travel at the boundary. The waves slow down in the shallow region, so the ray bends towards the normal and each wavelength gets shorter.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\lambda_2&=\frac{v_2}{f}=\frac{12}{10}=1.2\text{ cm}\\
\sin r&=\frac{v_2}{v_1}\sin i=\frac{12}{20}\sin30^\circ=0.30\ \Rightarrow\ r\approx17^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

The waves slow down, so the wavelength shortens and the direction bends towards the normal, giving an angle smaller than <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />. So in the shallow region the wavelength is <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ cm}" /> and the angle of refraction is about <InlineMath math="17^\circ" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- An angle of <InlineMath math="56^\circ" /> comes from the inverted speed ratio, but waves that slow down **bend towards the normal**.
- <InlineMath math="3.3\text{ cm}" /> inverts the speed ratio, as if the waves got longer as they slowed.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}" /> keeps the wavelength fixed, but it is the frequency that stays fixed.

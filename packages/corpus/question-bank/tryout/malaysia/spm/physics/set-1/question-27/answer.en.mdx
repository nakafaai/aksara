# The direction of the difference and matching units

There are two decisions. First, the gas pushes the water in its arm **down**, so the gas pressure exceeds atmospheric pressure by the pressure of a <InlineMath math="27.2\text{ cm}" /> water column. Second, the water column must be converted to cm Hg before it is added to <InlineMath math="76\text{ cm Hg}" />. Columns giving the same pressure are inversely proportional to their densities.

Change the level difference in the lab. A water column is <InlineMath math="13.6" /> times taller than a mercury column of the same pressure, so <InlineMath math="27.2\text{ cm}" /> of water is only <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm Hg}" />.

So the pressure of the gas is <InlineMath math="78\text{ cm Hg}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="74\text{ cm Hg}" /> subtracts the difference, but a lower water level on the gas side means **the gas pressure is greater**.
- <InlineMath math="103.2\text{ cm Hg}" /> adds centimetres of water to centimetres of mercury without converting.
- <InlineMath math="76\text{ cm Hg}" /> assumes the gas is at atmospheric pressure, but the levels in the two arms differ.

# The angle at the second face

There are two steps. First, find the angle of refraction at the top face with Snell's law. Second, because the side face is perpendicular to the top face, **the normals of the two faces are perpendicular too**, so the angle of incidence at the side is <InlineMath math="90^\circ-r" />. Compare that angle with the critical angle of the glass.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\sin r&=\frac{\sin60^\circ}{1.50}=0.577\ \Rightarrow\ r\approx35.3^\circ\\
i_2&=90^\circ-35.3^\circ\approx54.7^\circ\\
\sin c&=\frac1{1.50}\ \Rightarrow\ c\approx41.8^\circ
\end{aligned}" />

The ray goes from glass towards air with <InlineMath math="i_2\approx55^\circ" />, larger than the critical angle of <InlineMath math="41.8^\circ" />. So total internal reflection occurs at the side face.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="35^\circ" /> is the angle of refraction at the top face, not the angle of incidence at the side face, because **the normals of the two faces are perpendicular**.
- The ray does not leave at <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, because the side of the block is not parallel to the original ray and the angle of incidence is past the critical angle.
- <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> is smaller than the critical angle, so if the angle really were <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />, the ray would leave.

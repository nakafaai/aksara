While it is still fairly bright, <InlineMath math="R_{\text{LDR}}" /> is smaller, so <InlineMath math="V_B" /> has not reached the threshold. For the threshold to be reached at that smaller <InlineMath math="R_{\text{LDR}}" />, **<InlineMath math="R" /> must be made smaller**, because a smaller <InlineMath math="R" /> gives the LDR a larger share of the voltage. So replace <InlineMath math="R" /> with a resistor of smaller resistance.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A larger <InlineMath math="R" /> lowers <InlineMath math="V_B" />, so the bulb **switches on later**, when it is darker.
- Swapping <InlineMath math="R" /> and the LDR makes <InlineMath math="V_B" /> rise in bright light, so the bulb comes on in daylight and goes off in the dark.
- A higher-power bulb sits in the collector circuit and does not change the base voltage that decides when the transistor switches on.

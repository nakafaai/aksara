# Signed area under the graph

Displacement equals the area under the velocity-time graph, with **area below the time axis counted as negative**. Work out each interval by its shape: a triangle where the velocity changes uniformly from or to zero, a rectangle where it is constant.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
s_1&=\tfrac12(4)(12)=24\text{ m}\\
s_2&=(6)(12)=72\text{ m}\\
s_3&=\tfrac12(3)(12)=18\text{ m}\\
s_4&=-\tfrac12(2)(6)=-6\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

The total is <InlineMath math="24+72+18-6=108\text{ m}" />. The car went <InlineMath math="114\text{ m}" /> forwards, then came back <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" />. So its displacement is <InlineMath math="108\text{ m}" /> from the start, in its original direction.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="120\text{ m}" /> is the **distance travelled**, because the <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" /> backwards is added as positive.
- <InlineMath math="102\text{ m}" /> comes from treating the backward motion as a rectangle <InlineMath math="2\times6=12\text{ m}" />, although the speed rises uniformly from zero.
- <InlineMath math="126\text{ m}" /> comes from treating the slowing phase as a rectangle <InlineMath math="3\times12=36\text{ m}" />.

# Turns from voltage, current from power

The turns ratio follows the voltage ratio, <InlineMath math="\dfrac{N_p}{N_s}=\dfrac{V_p}{V_s}" />. For the primary current, remember the transformer is not ideal: **the input power is larger than the output power**, because some energy is lost as heat.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{N_p}{N_s}&=\frac{240}{12}=\frac{20}{1}\\
P_p&=\frac{P_s}{0.80}=\frac{24}{0.80}=30\text{ W}\\
I_p&=\frac{P_p}{V_p}=\frac{30}{240}=0.125\text{ A}
\end{aligned}" />

So the turns ratio is <InlineMath math="20:1" /> and the current in the primary coil is <InlineMath math="0.125\text{ A}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ A}" /> assumes <InlineMath math="100\%" /> efficiency, giving <InlineMath math="24/240" />.
- <InlineMath math="1:20" /> inverts the ratio, but a step-down transformer has **more turns on the primary**.
- <InlineMath math="0.080\text{ A}" /> multiplies the current by the efficiency, but the input power must be larger than the output, not smaller.

# From lens power to focal length

The power of a lens in dioptres is the reciprocal of its focal length in **metres**, <InlineMath math="f=\dfrac1P" />. In normal adjustment, the focal point of the objective coincides with that of the eyepiece, so the length of the telescope is <InlineMath math="f_o+f_e" /> and its magnification is <InlineMath math="\dfrac{f_o}{f_e}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f_o&=\frac1{1.0}=1.0\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}, &\quad f_e&=\frac1{20}=0.05\text{ m}=5\text{ cm}\\
M&=\frac{f_o}{f_e}=\frac{100}{5}=20, &\quad L&=f_o+f_e=105\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

The magnification also equals <InlineMath math="\dfrac{P_e}{P_o}=\dfrac{20}{1.0}=20" />, which agrees. So the magnification of the telescope is <InlineMath math="20" /> and the lenses are <InlineMath math="105\text{ cm}" /> apart.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.05" /> inverts the ratio, but **the long-focus objective** belongs in the numerator.
- <InlineMath math="95\text{ cm}" /> subtracts the focal lengths, but in normal adjustment they are added.
- A magnification of <InlineMath math="21" /> with <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}" /> adds the powers and uses only the objective's focal length.

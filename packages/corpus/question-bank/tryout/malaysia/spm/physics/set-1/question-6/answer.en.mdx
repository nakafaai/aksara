# Recoil speed, then the stopping work

There are two steps. First, the rifle and bullet are at rest before firing, so their total momentum is zero. **The bullet's forward momentum equals the rifle's backward momentum**. Second, the shoulder stops the rifle over a distance, so the work done by the force equals the rifle's kinetic energy.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(0.020)(400)&=(4.0)\,v\ \Rightarrow\ v=2.0\text{ m s}^{-1}\\
E_k&=\tfrac12(4.0)(2.0)^2=8.0\text{ J}\\
F&=\frac{E_k}{s}=\frac{8.0}{0.040}=200\text{ N}
\end{aligned}" />

The distance of <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}" /> must first become <InlineMath math="0.040\text{ m}" />. So the average force from the shoulder is <InlineMath math="200\text{ N}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="400\text{ N}" /> comes from leaving out the half in the kinetic energy.
- <InlineMath math="4.0\times10^{4}\text{ N}" /> uses the bullet's kinetic energy of <InlineMath math="1600\text{ J}" />, but the shoulder stops the **rifle**.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ N}" /> comes from using the distance as <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ m}" /> without converting centimetres.

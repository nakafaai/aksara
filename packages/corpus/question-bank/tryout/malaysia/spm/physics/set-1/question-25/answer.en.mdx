# The block's mass from floating in water

A floating object displaces its own weight of liquid. In water the submerged part is <InlineMath math="500-200=300\text{ cm}^3" />, so the block's mass equals the mass of <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" /> of water. In oil, **the block's mass is unchanged**, but oil is lighter, so the block must displace a larger volume.

Switch the liquid in the lab. The block's weight stays the same, so in the less dense oil it has to push aside more liquid and sinks deeper.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m&=(1.0)(300)=300\text{ g}\\
V_{\text{sub}}&=\frac{300}{0.80}=375\text{ cm}^3\\
V_{\text{above}}&=500-375=125\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

The block sinks deeper in oil, which makes sense because oil is less dense. So the volume of the block above the oil surface is <InlineMath math="125\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" /> assumes the block floats at the same level in any liquid.
- <InlineMath math="260\text{ cm}^3" /> multiplies the submerged volume by <InlineMath math="0.80" />, but **a less dense liquid makes an object sink deeper**, not shallower.
- <InlineMath math="160\text{ cm}^3" /> multiplies the volume above the water by <InlineMath math="0.80" />.

# Two changes at once

The volume and the temperature both change, so use the combined gas equation <InlineMath math="\dfrac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\dfrac{p_2V_2}{T_2}" />. The temperatures must be in **kelvin**, because the gas laws work with absolute temperature: <InlineMath math="T_1=27+273=300\text{ K}" /> and <InlineMath math="T_2=127+273=400\text{ K}" />.

<BlockMath math="p_2=p_1\cdot\frac{V_1}{V_2}\cdot\frac{T_2}{T_1}=150\times2\times\frac{400}{300}=400\text{ kPa}" />

Both changes raise the pressure: the compression doubles it, and the heating adds another third. So the final pressure of the gas is <InlineMath math="400\text{ kPa}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="200\text{ kPa}" /> accounts for the heating only and forgets the compression.
- <InlineMath math="100\text{ kPa}" /> inverts the volume ratio, as if a smaller volume lowered the pressure.
- <InlineMath math="1410\text{ kPa}" /> uses the temperatures in degrees Celsius, but **the gas laws need kelvin**.

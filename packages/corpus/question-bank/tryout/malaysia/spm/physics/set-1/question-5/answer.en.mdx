# Each ball's arrival time

Work out each arrival time from the moment ball A is thrown. For ball A, take upwards as positive: its final displacement is <InlineMath math="-15\text{ m}" />. Ball B only starts falling at <InlineMath math="t=1.0\text{ s}" />, so **its fall time must have one second added**.

Play the lab and follow both clocks. Ball A spends time going up and coming back before it even starts the drop that ball B makes.

The negative root <InlineMath math="t=-1\text{ s}" /> in ball A's equation is rejected. Ball B arrives at <InlineMath math="2.73\text{ s}" /> and ball A at <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ s}" />. So ball B reaches the sea first, about <InlineMath math="0.27\text{ s}" /> earlier.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Ball A first assumes ball A falls straight away, but ball A **rises for one second** before coming down.
- <InlineMath math="1.27\text{ s}" /> compares B's fall time of <InlineMath math="1.73\text{ s}" /> with <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ s}" /> without the one-second delay.
- Arriving together assumes both balls take the same time after passing the cliff edge, but their speeds there differ.

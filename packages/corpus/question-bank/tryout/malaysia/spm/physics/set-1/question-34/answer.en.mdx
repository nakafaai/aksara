# Eddy currents and Lenz's law

As the magnet falls, the magnetic flux through each copper ring around it changes, so induced currents flow round the pipe, called eddy currents. By Lenz's law, **an induced current always opposes the change that causes it**, so these currents brake the magnet.

Drop the magnets in the lab and compare the three pipes. The copper pipe slows the magnet most, the slit copper less, and the plastic pipe not at all.

Now judge each statement. Statement I is correct because there is no induced current in the plastic pipe, so the magnet falls freely, while the copper pipe brakes it. Statement II is correct because that is exactly Lenz's law. Statement III is wrong because work done against the braking force turns some mechanical energy into electrical energy and then heat in the pipe. Statement IV is correct because the slit cuts the path of the currents circling the pipe, so the eddy currents and their braking force drop sharply. So I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only misses statement IV, but the slit breaks **the circular current path** in the pipe wall.
- II and III accepts statement III, but mechanical energy falls as it turns into heat.
- I, III and IV rejects statement II, but that opposition to the motion is exactly what slows the magnet.

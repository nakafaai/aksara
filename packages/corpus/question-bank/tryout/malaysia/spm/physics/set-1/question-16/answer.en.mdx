# Interference first, then the wave equation

Loud sounds occur where the interference is constructive, and the distance between two successive loud sounds is <InlineMath math="x" /> in <InlineMath math="\lambda=\dfrac{ax}{D}" />. Here <InlineMath math="a=1.5\text{ m}" /> is the distance between the loudspeakers and <InlineMath math="D=6.0\text{ m}" /> is the distance to the walking line. **The wavelength is not yet the answer**, because the question asks for the frequency.

Mark <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="D" /> and <InlineMath math="x" /> on the layout before you substitute. Each distance has its own place in <InlineMath math="\lambda=\dfrac{ax}{D}" />, and swapping <InlineMath math="a" /> with <InlineMath math="D" /> is the usual slip.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\lambda&=\frac{ax}{D}=\frac{(1.5)(1.2)}{6.0}=0.30\text{ m}\\
f&=\frac{v}{\lambda}=\frac{330}{0.30}=1100\text{ Hz}
\end{aligned}" />

This frequency lies in the range of human hearing, so it makes sense. So the frequency of the sound is <InlineMath math="1100\text{ Hz}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="275\text{ Hz}" /> uses <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ m}" /> as the wavelength, but **the distance between loud sounds is not the wavelength**.
- <InlineMath math="44\text{ Hz}" /> rearranges the formula upside down, <InlineMath math="\lambda=\dfrac{aD}{x}=7.5\text{ m}" />.
- <InlineMath math="2200\text{ Hz}" /> treats <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ m}" /> as the distance from a loud to a quiet point and halves it.

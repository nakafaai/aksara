# The e.m.f. and internal resistance first

The current in each trial comes from the terminal voltage and the external resistance, <InlineMath math="I=\dfrac VR" />. Then the two pairs of readings give two equations <InlineMath math="V=E-Ir" />. **The new current can only be found once <InlineMath math="E" /> and <InlineMath math="r" /> are known**, because the terminal voltage changes when the external resistor changes.

Change the resistor in the lab. The terminal voltage drops as the current grows, and the two readings fix the e.m.f. and the internal resistance.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ A}" /> divides the e.m.f. by the external resistance only, but **the current also passes through the internal resistance**.
- <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ A}" /> uses the first terminal voltage, <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ V}" />, as if the terminal voltage did not change.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ A}" /> uses the second terminal voltage, <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ V}" />, with the same assumption.

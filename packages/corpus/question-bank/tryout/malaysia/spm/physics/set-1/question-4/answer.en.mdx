# The collision first, then friction

This problem has two stages with different laws. When the bullet embeds, **momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not**, because most of the energy turns into heat and deformation of the wood. After that, friction slows the block to rest.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
(0.020)(300)&=(0.020+1.98)\,v\ \Rightarrow\ v=\frac{6.0}{2.0}=3.0\text{ m s}^{-1}\\
a&=\frac{F}{m}=\frac{4.0}{2.0}=2.0\text{ m s}^{-2}\\
s&=\frac{v^2}{2a}=\frac{3.0^2}{2(2.0)}=2.25\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

The deceleration uses the combined mass of <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ kg}" />, because the bullet moves with the block. So the block slides <InlineMath math="2.25\text{ m}" /> before it stops.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="225\text{ m}" /> comes from turning all of the bullet's kinetic energy, <InlineMath math="900\text{ J}" />, into work against friction. **Kinetic energy is not conserved** in an inelastic collision.
- <InlineMath math="4.50\text{ m}" /> comes from dropping the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> in <InlineMath math="v^2=2as" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.50\text{ m}" /> is the stopping time <InlineMath math="v/a=1.5\text{ s}" /> mistaken for a distance.

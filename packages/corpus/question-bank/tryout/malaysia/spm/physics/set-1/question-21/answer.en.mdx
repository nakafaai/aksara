# The direction of acceleration from the scale reading

The scale reads the normal force <InlineMath math="R" /> pushing the student up. The student's weight is <InlineMath math="mg=600\text{ N}" />, so the net upward force is <InlineMath math="R-mg" />. A reading larger than the weight means **the acceleration points upwards**.

Draw the two forces on the student to scale. The upward arrow is longer, so the net force and the acceleration point up, whichever way the lift is moving.

<BlockMath math="a=\frac{R-mg}{m}=\frac{720-600}{60}=2\text{ m s}^{-2}\ \text{upwards}" />

An upward acceleration does not tell you the direction of motion. The lift may be moving up and speeding up, or moving down and slowing down. Among the options, only moving down while slowing at <InlineMath math="2\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> fits. So the lift could be moving downwards with its speed decreasing at <InlineMath math="2\text{ m s}^{-2}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Moving up while slowing means a downward acceleration, which would make the scale read <InlineMath math="480\text{ N}" />.
- Moving down while speeding up also means **a downward acceleration**, not an upward one.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ m s}^{-2}" /> comes from <InlineMath math="720/60" />, the scale reading divided by the mass without subtracting the weight.

# The neutron count, then the mass defect

First, find <InlineMath math="x" /> from conservation of nucleon number: <InlineMath math="235+1=141+92+x" />, so <InlineMath math="x=3" />. Second, work out the **mass defect**, the mass before the reaction minus the mass after, and convert it to kilograms before using <InlineMath math="E=mc^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta m&=(235.0439+1.0087)-(140.9144+91.9262+3\times1.0087)=0.1859\text{ u}\\
\Delta m&=0.1859\times1.66\times10^{-27}=3.09\times10^{-28}\text{ kg}\\
E&=\Delta m\,c^2=(3.09\times10^{-28})(3.0\times10^{8})^2\approx2.78\times10^{-11}\text{ J}
\end{aligned}" />

Check the proton numbers: <InlineMath math="92=56+36" />, so the equation balances. So about <InlineMath math="2.78\times10^{-11}\text{ J}" /> is released per reaction.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.29\times10^{-10}\text{ J}" /> counts only one neutron on the right, but **three neutrons are produced** by conservation of nucleon number.
- <InlineMath math="2.78\times10^{-8}\text{ J}" /> uses <InlineMath math="1\text{ u}=1.66\times10^{-24}" />, which is in grams, not kilograms.
- <InlineMath math="9.26\times10^{-20}\text{ J}" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="c" /> instead of <InlineMath math="c^2" />.

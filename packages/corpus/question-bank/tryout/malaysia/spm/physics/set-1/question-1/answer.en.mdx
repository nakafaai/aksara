# Precision and accuracy are read separately

**Precision** means the readings are close to one another, while **accuracy** means their average is close to the actual value. Judge each student on both counts. P's readings are accurate but less precise, with a spread of <InlineMath math="0.06\text{ cm}" />. R's readings spread over <InlineMath math="0.29\text{ cm}" />, so they are not precise. S's readings are precise and accurate. Q's readings differ by only <InlineMath math="0.02\text{ cm}" /> from one another, but all of them sit well above <InlineMath math="2.50\text{ cm}" />.

Pick each student in the lab and compare how tight the readings sit with how far their mean lies from the actual length.

The same shift in every reading is the mark of a systematic error, such as a zero error. Q's calliper always reads <InlineMath math="0.12\text{ cm}" /> too large, so its zero error is positive. So Q's readings are precise but not accurate, with a zero error of about <InlineMath math="+0.12\text{ cm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-0.12\text{ cm}" /> flips the sign, but an instrument that **reads too large** has a positive zero error.
- P is accurate, because the average is <InlineMath math="2.50\text{ cm}" />, but the readings are the less precise ones.
- R is not precise, because the readings spread widely, so its error is random, not a zero error.

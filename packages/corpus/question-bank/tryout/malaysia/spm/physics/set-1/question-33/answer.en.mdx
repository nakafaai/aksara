# The size of the current from the balance of forces

The magnetic force must point upwards with a size equal to the weight of the wire, so <InlineMath math="BIl=mg" />. First turn the mass into kilograms and the length into metres.

<BlockMath math="I=\frac{mg}{Bl}=\frac{(0.0060)(10)}{(0.15)(0.20)}=\frac{0.060}{0.030}=2.0\text{ A}" />

For the direction, use Fleming's left-hand rule with the condition that **the force must point up**. The first finger points along the field, north, and the thumb points up, so the second finger, the current, points east. So the current is <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ A}" /> towards the east.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A current towards the west gives a downward force, which **adds to the weight** instead of balancing it.
- <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ A}" /> uses the mass as a force without multiplying by <InlineMath math="g" />.
- <InlineMath math="2000\text{ A}" /> uses the mass of <InlineMath math="6.0" /> in grams without converting it to kilograms.

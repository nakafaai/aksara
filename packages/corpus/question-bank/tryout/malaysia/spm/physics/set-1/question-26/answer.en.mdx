# Constant flow rate, then Bernoulli's principle

Water is incompressible, so the volume passing each second is the same in both sections: <InlineMath math="A_1v_1=A_2v_2" />. Cross-sectional area is proportional to **the square of the diameter**, so half the diameter gives a quarter of the area.

<BlockMath math="v_2=v_1\cdot\frac{A_1}{A_2}=0.50\times\left(\frac{4.0}{2.0}\right)^2=0.50\times4=2.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" />

Put both steps on the pipe. The narrow section carries the same flow at four times the speed, and the faster water has the lower pressure, so its tube holds the lower column.

By Bernoulli's principle, in a horizontal pipe faster water has lower pressure. The pressure in the narrow section is lower, so the water in the tube above it rises less. So the speed in the narrow section is <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> and the water level in the tube above it is lower.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> uses the ratio of diameters, but **area goes with the square of the diameter**.
- A higher level above the narrow section reverses Bernoulli's principle.
- <InlineMath math="0.13\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> inverts the area ratio, as if the water slowed down in the narrow section.

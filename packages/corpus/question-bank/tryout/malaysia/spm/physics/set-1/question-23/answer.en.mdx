# The force on each spring

The key is the force each spring feels. Spring P supports the whole <InlineMath math="20\text{ N}" /> load. **Springs Q and R share the load**, <InlineMath math="10\text{ N}" /> each, so they stretch by the same amount. The extension of the system is the extension of P plus the extension of the pair Q and R.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
x_P&=\frac{20}{200}=0.10\text{ m}=10\text{ cm}\\
x_{QR}&=\frac{10}{200}=0.05\text{ m}=5\text{ cm}\\
x&=10+5=15\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Another way: the parallel pair acts like one spring of <InlineMath math="400\text{ N m}^{-1}" /> in series with P, giving <InlineMath math="\tfrac{20}{400}+\tfrac{20}{200}=0.15\text{ m}" />. So the total extension is <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> assumes Q and R each carry <InlineMath math="20\text{ N}" />, but **the load is split** between the parallel pair.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> treats all three springs as being in series.
- <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> counts spring P only and forgets the pair Q and R.

# The total resistance changes, so the voltage split changes

A bulb's brightness follows its power, <InlineMath math="P=\dfrac{V^2}{R}" />. When <InlineMath math="L_3" /> breaks, only <InlineMath math="L_2" /> is left in the parallel branch, so **the total resistance rises and the current from the battery falls**. Compare the voltage across each bulb before and after.

Break <InlineMath math="L_3" /> in the lab. The parallel part loses a path, so its resistance rises, the total current falls, and the share of the voltage moves toward <InlineMath math="L_2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Both dimmer looks only at the falling total current, but **the voltage across <InlineMath math="L_2" /> actually rises** because <InlineMath math="L_1" /> takes a smaller share of the voltage.
- Both brighter assumes losing one bulb sends more current to the others, but the total current falls.
- No change treats the parallel branch as independent, but it is in series with <InlineMath math="L_1" />.

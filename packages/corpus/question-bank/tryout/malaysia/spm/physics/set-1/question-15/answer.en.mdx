# The sound and the boat share the distance

During the <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ s}" />, the sound travels to the cliff and back, while the boat keeps moving towards the cliff. Let the starting distance be <InlineMath math="d" />. **The echo meets the boat at a point already closer to the cliff**, so the distance of the sound plus the distance of the boat equals twice the starting distance.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
340(4.0)+10(4.0)&=2d\\
1360+40&=2d\ \Rightarrow\ d=700\text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

Check: the sound travels <InlineMath math="700\text{ m}" /> to the cliff, then <InlineMath math="660\text{ m}" /> back to meet the boat, which has moved <InlineMath math="40\text{ m}" /> forward. The total is <InlineMath math="1360\text{ m}" />, exactly how far sound travels in <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ s}" />. So the boat was <InlineMath math="700\text{ m}" /> from the cliff when the horn was sounded.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="680\text{ m}" /> ignores the motion of the boat, as if it were at rest.
- <InlineMath math="660\text{ m}" /> subtracts the boat's distance, but the boat **moves towards the cliff**, which shortens the return path of the sound.
- <InlineMath math="1400\text{ m}" /> is the sum of the two distances without halving it.

# Orbital radius is measured from the Earth's centre

Kepler's third law states <InlineMath math="T^2\propto r^3" />, where <InlineMath math="r" /> is the orbital radius from the **centre of the Earth**, not the height above the surface. So turn the heights into orbital radii first: satellite X is at <InlineMath math="r_X=R+3R=4R" /> and satellite Y at <InlineMath math="r_Y=R+R=2R" />.

<BlockMath math="T_Y=T_X\left(\frac{r_Y}{r_X}\right)^{3/2}=8.0\left(\frac{2R}{4R}\right)^{3/2}=\frac{8.0}{2\sqrt2}\approx2.8\text{ hours}" />

The result makes sense, because the nearer satellite moves faster along a shorter path. So the orbital period of satellite Y is about <InlineMath math="2.8" /> hours.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.5" /> hours comes from using the height ratio <InlineMath math="R:3R" /> as the radius ratio, but **the radius is measured from the centre**.
- <InlineMath math="4.0" /> hours assumes the period is proportional to the radius, but the power is <InlineMath math="3/2" />.
- <InlineMath math="5.7" /> hours assumes the period is proportional to the square root of the radius.

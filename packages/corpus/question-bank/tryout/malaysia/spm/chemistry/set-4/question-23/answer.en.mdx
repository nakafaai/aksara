# One count of electrons, three half-equations

In a series circuit **every electron passes through all three cells**. Silver: <InlineMath math="2.16 \div 108 = 0.020" /> mol, and <InlineMath math="\text{Ag}^+ + e^- \rightarrow \text{Ag}" /> takes one electron per atom, so <InlineMath math="0.020" /> mol of electrons has flowed.

Cell <InlineMath math="2" />: <InlineMath math="\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}" /> takes two electrons per atom, so <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol of copper, <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 64 = 0.64" /> g.

Cell <InlineMath math="3" />: platinum is inert, so at the anode water is oxidised, <InlineMath math="2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{O}_2 + 4\text{H}^+ + 4e^-" />. Each oxygen molecule releases **four electrons**: <InlineMath math="0.020 \div 4 = 0.0050" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="0.0050 \times 24 = 0.12\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> deposits one copper atom per electron, but each copper(II) ion needs two.
- <InlineMath math="0.24\text{ dm}^3" /> of oxygen counts two electrons per oxygen molecule instead of four.
- Hydrogen forms at the **cathode** of cell <InlineMath math="3" />, where hydrogen ions gain electrons, not at the anode.

# Turn the pH back into a hydrogen ion concentration

At <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> ethanoic acid has pH <InlineMath math="2.9" />, so <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] = 10^{-2.9} \approx 1.3 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. If every molecule ionised, it would be <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

The fraction that ionises is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1.3 \times 10^{-3}}{0.10} \times 100\% \approx 1.3\%" />. Hydrochloric acid, by contrast, gives pH <InlineMath math="1.0" /> at the same concentration, because it **ionises completely**. Diluting either acid tenfold raises hydrochloric acid's pH by a full unit but ethanoic acid's by only about half a unit, as the chart shows.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Monoprotic means **one hydrogen ion per molecule at most**, not that every molecule gives one up.
- The pH is a logarithm, not a percentage.
- <InlineMath math="0.013\%" /> slips two decimal places when turning <InlineMath math="0.013" /> into a percentage.

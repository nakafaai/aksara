# Each step removes one gas, so each difference names one

Water condenses on cooling, so the <InlineMath math="110\text{ cm}^3" /> is carbon dioxide and leftover oxygen. Sodium hydroxide **absorbs only the carbon dioxide**: <InlineMath math="110 - 50 = 60\text{ cm}^3" />. The <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> it leaves is unused oxygen, so <InlineMath math="150 - 50 = 100\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen reacted.

Equal volumes hold equal numbers of molecules. For <InlineMath math="\text{C}_x\text{H}_y + (x + \tfrac{y}{4})\,\text{O}_2 \rightarrow x\,\text{CO}_2 + \tfrac{y}{2}\,\text{H}_2\text{O}" />: carbon dioxide gives <InlineMath math="x = 60 \div 20 = 3" />, and oxygen gives <InlineMath math="x + \tfrac{y}{4} = 100 \div 20 = 5" />, so <InlineMath math="y = 8" />. The hydrocarbon is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_8" />, propane.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Propene, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_6" />, would use only <InlineMath math="4.5" /> oxygen per molecule, <InlineMath math="90\text{ cm}^3" />, leaving <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" />.
- At room temperature the water is **a liquid**, and its volume is negligible, so the last gas cannot be water vapour.
- Butane would give <InlineMath math="80\text{ cm}^3" /> of carbon dioxide, not the <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> the alkali removed.

# Heat from the whole mixture, divided by the limiting ion

Heat given out: the mixture is <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="100" /> g, and it warms by <InlineMath math="30.1 - 28.0 = 2.1^\circ\text{C}" />: <InlineMath math="100 \times 4.2 \times 2.1 = 882\text{ J}" />.

Silver ions: <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 0.050 = 0.025" /> mol. Chloride ions: <InlineMath math="0.30 \times 0.050 = 0.015" /> mol. <InlineMath math="\text{Ag}^+ + \text{Cl}^- \rightarrow \text{AgCl}" /> is one to one, so **only <InlineMath math="0.015" /> mol of silver chloride forms**. Per mole: <InlineMath math="882 \div 0.015 = 58\,800\text{ J}" />, and the reaction warms the cup, so <InlineMath math="\Delta H = -58.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-35.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by the silver ions, but the chloride **runs out first**.
- <InlineMath math="-29.4\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> uses only <InlineMath math="50" /> g, but both solutions are warmed.
- A positive sign would mean heat taken in, yet the temperature rises, so heat is given out.

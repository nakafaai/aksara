**Iron**: two per oxide, <InlineMath math="1.6 \times 10^4" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="1.6 \times 10^4 \times 56 = 0.896" /> tonnes in theory. The yield is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.806}{0.896} \times 100\% = 90\%" />.

**Carbon monoxide**: three per oxide, <InlineMath math="2.4 \times 10^4" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="2.4 \times 10^4 \times 24 = 5.76 \times 10^5\text{ dm}^3 = 576\text{ m}^3" />. The slag loss happens after reduction, so it does not change the gas used.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="192\text{ m}^3" /> takes one carbon monoxide per iron(III) oxide, but each oxide gives up three oxygen atoms.
- <InlineMath math="72\%" /> treats all <InlineMath math="1.6" /> tonnes of ore as iron(III) oxide, which predicts <InlineMath math="1.12" /> tonnes of iron.
- <InlineMath math="384\text{ m}^3" /> takes one carbon monoxide per iron atom, two per oxide instead of **three**.

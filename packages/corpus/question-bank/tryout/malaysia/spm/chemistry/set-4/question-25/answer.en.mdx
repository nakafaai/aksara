# Pure oxide first, then two iron atoms per formula unit

Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of the ore is iron(III) oxide: <InlineMath math="0.80 \times 1.6 = 1.28" /> tonnes, or <InlineMath math="1\,280\,000" /> g. With <InlineMath math="M_r = 2(56) + 3(16) = 160" />, that is <InlineMath math="8000" /> mol.

Each formula unit holds **two iron atoms**, so <InlineMath math="16\,000" /> mol of iron forms: <InlineMath math="16\,000 \times 56 = 896\,000" /> g, which is <InlineMath math="0.896" /> tonnes. Carbon monoxide takes oxygen from the oxide and is oxidised to carbon dioxide, so it is the reducing agent.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.12" /> tonnes treats the whole ore as iron(III) oxide, and carbon monoxide gains oxygen, which makes it the reducing agent.
- Iron(III) oxide **loses oxygen**, so it is reduced and acts as the oxidising agent.
- <InlineMath math="0.448" /> tonnes counts one iron atom per formula unit instead of two.

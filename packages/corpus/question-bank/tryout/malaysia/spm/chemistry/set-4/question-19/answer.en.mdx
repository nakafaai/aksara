# The peroxide sets the volume, the catalyst sets the speed

Hydrogen peroxide: <InlineMath math="0.40 \times \tfrac{25.0}{1000} = 0.010" /> mol. **Two molecules give one oxygen molecule**, so <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol of oxygen forms: <InlineMath math="0.0050 \times 24\,000 = 120\text{ cm}^3" />.

A catalyst offers a path with a lower activation energy and comes out of the reaction unchanged, so all <InlineMath math="0.50" /> g can be filtered off, dried and weighed again. More catalyst gives more surface for the reaction, so the gas forms faster, but the amount of peroxide still caps the volume at <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> treats the ratio as one to one, and a catalyst does not lose mass.
- A catalyst is **not used up**, so it is not consumed by the reaction.
- A catalyst changes how fast the gas forms, not how much forms.

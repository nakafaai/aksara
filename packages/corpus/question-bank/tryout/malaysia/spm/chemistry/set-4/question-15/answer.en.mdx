# Narrow the cation with two alkalis, then name the anion

A white precipitate that dissolves in excess sodium hydroxide is an **amphoteric hydroxide**: aluminium, zinc or lead(II). Ammonia separates them, because only zinc hydroxide dissolves in excess ammonia, as the zinc ions form a complex with it.

Silver nitrate after nitric acid gives white silver chloride, so the anion is chloride. Barium chloride gives no white barium sulfate, which rules out sulfate. X is zinc chloride.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Aluminium hydroxide stays as a white precipitate in excess ammonia.
- Lead(II) hydroxide also **does not dissolve** in excess ammonia.
- A sulfate would give a white precipitate with barium chloride, but there was no change.

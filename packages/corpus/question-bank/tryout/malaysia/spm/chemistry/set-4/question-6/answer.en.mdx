# Heat to constant mass, then compare moles

The mass stops changing at <InlineMath math="1.60" /> g, so **all the water has gone**: heating to constant mass is what proves the salt is anhydrous. The water lost is <InlineMath math="2.50 - 1.60 = 0.90" /> g.

Anhydrous salt: <InlineMath math="1.60 \div 160 = 0.010" /> mol. Water: <InlineMath math="0.90 \div 18 = 0.050" /> mol. So <InlineMath math="x = 0.050 \div 0.010 = 5" />. The blue crystals turn into a white powder, because the colour comes from water molecules bound to the copper(II) ions.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.56" /> divides the **masses**, <InlineMath math="0.90 \div 1.60" />, instead of the moles.
- Water leaves on heating, so blue turns white. White turning blue is what happens when water is added back.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> doubles the moles of water.

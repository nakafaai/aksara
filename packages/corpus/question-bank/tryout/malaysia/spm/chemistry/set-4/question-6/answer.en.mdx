# The final mass fixes the salt, each loss counts the water

At <InlineMath math="250^\circ\text{C}" /> the mass stops at <InlineMath math="1.60" /> g of anhydrous salt: <InlineMath math="1.60 \div 160 = 0.010" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{CuSO}_4" />. The total water lost is <InlineMath math="2.50 - 1.60 = 0.90" /> g, or <InlineMath math="0.90 \div 18 = 0.050" /> mol, so **five water molecules per formula unit**: <InlineMath math="x = 5" />.

The first stage removed <InlineMath math="2.50 - 1.78 = 0.72" /> g, <InlineMath math="0.040" /> mol, which is four of the five waters. The solid held at <InlineMath math="130^\circ\text{C}" /> still keeps one, <InlineMath math="\text{CuSO}_4 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O}" />, and only the hotter stage drives off the last, more tightly bound water, leaving the white anhydrous salt.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="x = 4" /> counts only the first stage, so the solid at <InlineMath math="130^\circ\text{C}" /> is **not yet anhydrous**.
- <InlineMath math="\text{CuSO}_4 \cdot 3\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> would weigh <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 214 = 2.14" /> g, not <InlineMath math="1.78" /> g.
- <InlineMath math="0.56" /> is a mass ratio of water to salt, not a ratio of moles.

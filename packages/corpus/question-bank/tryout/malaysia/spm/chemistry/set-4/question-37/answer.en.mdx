# Count the links, because each link gives off one water molecule

In a condensation polymer, every new link joins two monomers and **gives off one small molecule**, here water. One repeat unit holds one diacid and one diamine, and it is tied into the chain by two amide links: one at each end of the diacid.

<InlineMath math="226" /> kg is <InlineMath math="226\,000 \div 226 = 1000" /> mol of repeat units. With two links each, <InlineMath math="2000" /> mol of water leaves: <InlineMath math="2000 \times 18 = 36\,000" /> g, or <InlineMath math="36" /> kg.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="18\text{ kg}" /> counts one link per repeat unit, but each unit carries **two amide links**.
- Nylon-6,6 forms by condensation, which gives off water. Addition polymers form without losing any atoms.
- <InlineMath math="72\text{ kg}" /> counts four molecules per unit, twice the number of links.

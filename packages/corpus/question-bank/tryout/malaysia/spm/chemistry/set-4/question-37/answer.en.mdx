# Count the links, then the mass the polymer should have

Acid: <InlineMath math="292 \div 146 = 2.00" /> kmol. Diamine: <InlineMath math="232 \div 116 = 2.00" /> kmol. They join one to one, so they make <InlineMath math="2.00" /> kmol of repeat units.

Each repeat unit is tied into the chain by **two amide links**, one at each end of the diacid, and each link gives off one water molecule. Water: <InlineMath math="2 \times 2.00 = 4.00" /> kmol, or <InlineMath math="4.00 \times 18 = 72\text{ kg}" />.

Mass is conserved, so the nylon predicted is the monomers less the water: <InlineMath math="292 + 232 - 72 = 452\text{ kg}" />, which is also <InlineMath math="2.00 \times 226" />. The yield is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{407}{452} \times 100\% = 90\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="36\text{ kg}" /> counts one link per unit. The predicted nylon then becomes <InlineMath math="488\text{ kg}" /> and the yield <InlineMath math="83\%" />.
- <InlineMath math="78\%" /> divides the nylon by the monomers, <InlineMath math="407 \div 524" />, forgetting that the water's mass never becomes polymer.
- Nylon-6,6 forms by **condensation**, which gives off water. Addition polymers form without losing any atoms.

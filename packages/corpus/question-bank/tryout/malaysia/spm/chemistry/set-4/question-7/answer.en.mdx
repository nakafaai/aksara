# One formula unit, three ions

<InlineMath math="M_r(\text{CaCl}_2) = 40 + 2(35.5) = 111" />, so <InlineMath math="11.1" /> g is <InlineMath math="0.100" /> mol. Each formula unit splits into **one calcium ion and two chloride ions**: <InlineMath math="\text{CaCl}_2 \rightarrow \text{Ca}^{2+} + 2\text{Cl}^-" />.

That makes <InlineMath math="0.300" /> mol of ions: <InlineMath math="0.300 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 1.81 \times 10^{23}" />. Of these, <InlineMath math="0.200" /> mol are chloride ions: <InlineMath math="1.20 \times 10^{23}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.602 \times 10^{23}" /> counts formula units, not ions.
- <InlineMath math="1.20 \times 10^{23}" /> counts only the chloride ions.
- <InlineMath math="0.602 \times 10^{23}" /> is the number of **calcium** ions, not chloride ions.

# Let the precipitate fix the chloride, then solve for both salts

Silver chloride, <InlineMath math="M_r = 143.5" />: <InlineMath math="43.05 \div 143.5 = 0.30" /> mol. Every chloride ion ends up in the precipitate, so the solution held <InlineMath math="0.30" /> mol of chloride ions.

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> mol be calcium chloride (<InlineMath math="M_r = 111" />) and <InlineMath math="y" /> mol be sodium chloride (<InlineMath math="M_r = 58.5" />). Then **chloride**: <InlineMath math="2x + y = 0.30" />, and **mass**: <InlineMath math="111x + 58.5y = 16.95" />. Putting <InlineMath math="y = 0.30 - 2x" /> into the mass equation gives <InlineMath math="17.55 - 6x = 16.95" />, so <InlineMath math="x = 0.10" /> and <InlineMath math="y = 0.10" />.

All ions: <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{Ca}^{2+}" />, <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{Na}^{+}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.30" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}^{-}" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.50" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 3.01 \times 10^{23}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20 \times 10^{23}" /> counts formula units, <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol, not the ions **they split into**.
- <InlineMath math="1.81 \times 10^{23}" /> counts only the chloride ions and leaves out the metal ions.
- <InlineMath math="2.76 \times 10^{23}" /> treats the whole powder as calcium chloride and never uses the precipitate.

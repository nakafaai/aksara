# Zinc gives, copper takes, and the copper ions run down

Zinc is higher in the electrochemical series, so it is the negative terminal: <InlineMath math="\text{Zn} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-" />. The zinc electrode loses <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 65 = 0.65" /> g. The electrons flow through the wire to the copper, where <InlineMath math="\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}" /> adds <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 64 = 0.64" /> g.

The copper(II) ions started at <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 0.100 = 0.050" /> mol, and <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol has been **taken out of the solution** as copper metal: <InlineMath math="0.040 \div 0.100 = 0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The blue colour fades.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.49\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> subtracts the moles straight from the concentration instead of from the moles in <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ dm}^3" />.
- The salt bridge carries ions such as sulfate and potassium to balance charge. It does not supply copper(II) ions.
- Copper is the **positive terminal**, where copper is deposited, so the copper electrode gains mass and the zinc electrode loses it.

# Stock concentration, then a fortyfold dilution, then pOH

<InlineMath math="4.0 \div 40 = 0.10" /> mol in <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ dm}^3" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

The pipette carries <InlineMath math="0.40 \times \tfrac{25.0}{1000} = 0.010" /> mol into <InlineMath math="1.000\text{ dm}^3" />, a **fortyfold dilution** to <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Sodium hydroxide is a strong alkali, so <InlineMath math="[\text{OH}^-] = 0.010\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, <InlineMath math="\text{pOH} = 2" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{pH} = 14 - 2 = 12" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.016" /> divides grams by cubic centimetres, which is not a concentration in <InlineMath math="\text{mol dm}^{-3}" />.
- pH <InlineMath math="13" /> belongs to <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, a tenfold dilution, but the volume grows fortyfold.
- pH <InlineMath math="2" /> is the pOH. An alkali has a pH **above <InlineMath math="7" />**.

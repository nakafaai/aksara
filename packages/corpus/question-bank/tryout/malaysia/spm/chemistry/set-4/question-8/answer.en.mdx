# Count hydroxide ions against hydrogen ions

Sodium hydroxide: <InlineMath math="4.0 \div 40 = 0.10" /> mol in <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ dm}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> taken holds <InlineMath math="0.40 \times 0.0250 = 0.010" /> mol of hydroxide ions.

Sulfuric acid: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 0.0250 = 0.0050" /> mol, and it is **diprotic**, <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow 2\text{H}^+ + \text{SO}_4^{2-}" />, so it gives <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol of hydrogen ions. They match the hydroxide exactly: <InlineMath math="2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}" />. The mixture holds only sodium sulfate, a neutral salt, so its pH is <InlineMath math="7" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.016\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> divides the mass by the volume, <InlineMath math="4.0 \div 250" />, instead of moles **by cubic decimetres**.
- pH <InlineMath math="13" /> treats the acid as giving one hydrogen ion per molecule, which leaves <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol of hydroxide in <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ dm}^3" />.
- pH <InlineMath math="1" /> would need the acid in excess, but the ions cancel exactly.

# Concentration of the stock, then the volume to dilute

<InlineMath math="4.0 \div 40 = 0.10" /> mol in <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ dm}^3" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.10 \div 0.250 = 0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

The second solution needs <InlineMath math="0.10 \times 0.500 = 0.050" /> mol. The volume of stock that holds it is <InlineMath math="0.050 \div 0.40 = 0.125\text{ dm}^3" />, or <InlineMath math="125\text{ cm}^3" />, then water is added up to <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}^3" />. In one line, <InlineMath math="M_1V_1 = M_2V_2" />: <InlineMath math="0.40 \times V_1 = 0.10 \times 500" />, a **fourfold dilution**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.016" /> divides grams by cubic centimetres, <InlineMath math="4.0 \div 250" />, which **is not a concentration in** <InlineMath math="\text{mol dm}^{-3}" />.
- <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" /> halves the concentration, but it has to fall from <InlineMath math="0.40" /> to <InlineMath math="0.10" />, a quarter.
- <InlineMath math="62.5\text{ cm}^3" /> dilutes eightfold and gives only <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

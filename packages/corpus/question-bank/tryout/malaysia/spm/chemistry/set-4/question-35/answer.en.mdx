# Divide by the molar mass to get heat per gram

A rocket pays for every gram it lifts, so the useful figure is **heat per gram**: the heat of combustion divided by the molar mass.

Methanol gives <InlineMath math="726 \div 32 = 22.7\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" />, ethanol <InlineMath math="1376 \div 46 = 29.9" />, octane <InlineMath math="5470 \div 114 = 48.0" /> and hydrogen <InlineMath math="286 \div 2 = 143" />. Hydrogen wins by far, and it burns to water alone: <InlineMath math="2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Octane has the largest value per mole, but each mole weighs <InlineMath math="114" /> g, so per gram it gives only a third of hydrogen's heat.
- Methanol's light molecules carry oxygen already, so it gives the **least** heat per gram of the four.
- Ethanol gives <InlineMath math="29.9\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" />, far below hydrogen, and it does form carbon dioxide.

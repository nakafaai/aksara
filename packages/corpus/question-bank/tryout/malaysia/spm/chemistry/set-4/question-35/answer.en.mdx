# Count the oxygen the rocket must carry, then divide

Balance each combustion per mole of fuel and add the oxygen's mass (<InlineMath math="\text{O}_2 = 32" />) to the fuel's:

- Methanol: <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \tfrac{3}{2}\text{O}_2" />, mass <InlineMath math="32 + 48 = 80" />, heat <InlineMath math="726 \div 80 = 9.08\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" />.
- Ethanol: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 3\text{O}_2" />, mass <InlineMath math="46 + 96 = 142" />, heat <InlineMath math="1376 \div 142 = 9.69\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" />.
- Octane: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_8\text{H}_{18} + \tfrac{25}{2}\text{O}_2" />, mass <InlineMath math="114 + 400 = 514" />, heat <InlineMath math="5470 \div 514 = 10.6\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" />.
- Hydrogen: <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\text{O}_2" />, mass <InlineMath math="2 + 16 = 18" />, heat <InlineMath math="286 \div 18 = 15.9\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" />.

Hydrogen still wins, but by far less than it seems from the fuel alone: the oxygen is **eight times** the mass of the hydrogen it burns.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="143\text{ kJ}" /> divides by the hydrogen alone and forgets the <InlineMath math="16" /> g of oxygen each <InlineMath math="2" /> g **of hydrogen needs**.
- Octane has the largest heat per mole, but it needs <InlineMath math="400" /> g of oxygen per mole, so per gram of fuel and oxygen it comes second.
- <InlineMath math="8.4\text{ kJ}" /> adds a whole mole of oxygen, <InlineMath math="32" /> g, per mole of hydrogen instead of half a mole.

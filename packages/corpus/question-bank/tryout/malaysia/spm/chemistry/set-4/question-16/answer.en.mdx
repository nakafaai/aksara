# The bend in the graph is where the ions run out together

The precipitate grows until <InlineMath math="10" /> cm³ of potassium iodide and then stops. At that point the iodide ions have **used up every lead(II) ion**: <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{Pb}^{2+}" /> met <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{I}^-" />. The ratio <InlineMath math="1 : 2" /> gives <InlineMath math="\text{PbI}_2" />.

Extra iodide finds no lead(II) ions, so the height stays at <InlineMath math="6.0" /> cm. The most precipitate is <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{PbI}_2" />, with <InlineMath math="M_r = 207 + 2(127) = 461" />: <InlineMath math="0.0050 \times 461 = 2.31" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{PbI}" /> would stop the graph at <InlineMath math="5" /> cm³, but it keeps rising to <InlineMath math="10" /> cm³.
- <InlineMath math="4.61\text{ g}" /> counts <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol of precipitate, the moles of **iodide ions**, not of lead(II) iodide.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Pb}_2\text{I}" /> would need only <InlineMath math="2.5" /> cm³ of iodide, and lead(II) cannot balance one iodide ion twice.

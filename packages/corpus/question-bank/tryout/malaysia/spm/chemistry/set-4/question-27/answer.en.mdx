# Balance to name X, then let its double bond count the bromine

Taking octane from dodecane leaves <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8" />, shared between two molecules, so X is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4" />, ethene. Dodecane, <InlineMath math="M_r = 170" />: <InlineMath math="17.0 \div 170 = 0.10" /> mol, giving <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol of ethene, <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 24 = 4.8\text{ dm}^3" />.

Ethene's **one double bond adds one bromine molecule**: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{Br}_2" />. That is <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" />, <InlineMath math="M_r = 160" />: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 160 = 32" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Ethane, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_6" />, does not balance: the four carbons and eight hydrogens left **over make ethene**.
- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ dm}^3" /> counts one molecule of X per dodecane, but the equation gives two.
- <InlineMath math="16\text{ g}" /> uses <InlineMath math="80" /> for bromine, the mass of an atom, but bromine reacts as <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" />.

# Balance the atoms to name X, then count the moles

The atoms left over after octane are <InlineMath math="12 - 8 = 4" /> carbon and <InlineMath math="26 - 18 = 8" /> hydrogen, shared between two molecules: each X is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4" />, ethene. Cracking a long alkane gives a shorter alkane and an alkene.

Dodecane, <InlineMath math="M_r = 12(12) + 26(1) = 170" />: <InlineMath math="17.0 \div 170 = 0.10" /> mol. **Two molecules of ethene** form from each, so <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 24 = 4.8\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8" /> fits one molecule of X, but the equation gives two.
- Ethene is right, but there are **two moles** of it per mole of dodecane, so <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ dm}^3" /> is half the volume.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_6" /> would need <InlineMath math="12" /> hydrogen atoms for two molecules, but only <InlineMath math="8" /> are left.

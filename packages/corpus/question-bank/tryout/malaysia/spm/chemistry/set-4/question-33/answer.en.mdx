# Heat into the water, per mole of fuel, against the true value

The water took in <InlineMath math="200 \times 4.2 \times 10.0 = 8400" /> J, or <InlineMath math="8.4" /> kJ. The ethanol burned was <InlineMath math="0.46 \div 46 = 0.010" /> mol, so the experiment gives <InlineMath math="\Delta H = -\tfrac{8.4}{0.010} = -840\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

The water received <InlineMath math="\tfrac{840}{1376} = 61\%" /> of the heat the fuel really released, so about **<InlineMath math="39\%" /> escaped**: it warmed the air, the can and the clamp, and some ethanol burned incompletely to soot.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The data-book value assumes all the heat reaches the water, which an open flame under a can never achieves.
- <InlineMath math="-84\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol, ten times the ethanol burned.
- <InlineMath math="61\%" /> is the share the water **did** receive, not the share that escaped.

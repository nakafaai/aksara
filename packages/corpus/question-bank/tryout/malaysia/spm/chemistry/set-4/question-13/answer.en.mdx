# Atoms per cubic centimetre, then electrons per atom

Sodium: <InlineMath math="0.97 \div 23 = 0.042" /> mol of atoms in <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ cm}^3" />, each giving **one** electron, so <InlineMath math="0.042" /> mol of delocalised electrons. Aluminium: <InlineMath math="2.70 \div 27 = 0.100" /> mol of atoms, each giving **three**, so <InlineMath math="0.300" /> mol. Magnesium sits between: <InlineMath math="1.74 \div 24 \times 2 = 0.145" /> mol.

Aluminium packs <InlineMath math="0.300 \div 0.042 \approx 7" /> times as many delocalised electrons into the same volume, around smaller ions of charge <InlineMath math="3+" />. The attraction between the ions and the electron sea is stronger, so more energy is needed to loosen the lattice, and aluminium melts at <InlineMath math="660^\circ\text{C}" /> against sodium's <InlineMath math="98^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> counts aluminium atoms, not the three electrons each one gives.
- Larger atoms with one electron each make a weaker electron sea, which is why sodium melts **lower**.
- <InlineMath math="0.97" /> and <InlineMath math="2.70" /> are masses in grams, not amounts in moles.

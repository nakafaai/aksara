# Count the electrons each atom gives to the sea

Both samples are <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of atoms: <InlineMath math="2.3 \div 23" /> and <InlineMath math="2.7 \div 27" />. Sodium has one valence electron and aluminium has three, so the aluminium gives **three times as many** electrons: <InlineMath math="0.30 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 1.81 \times 10^{23}" />, against <InlineMath math="6.02 \times 10^{22}" /> for sodium.

Aluminium's ions also carry a charge of <InlineMath math="3+" /> and are smaller, so they attract the denser sea of electrons more strongly. Stronger metallic bonding takes more energy to loosen, which is why aluminium melts at <InlineMath math="660^\circ\text{C}" /> against sodium's <InlineMath math="98^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Equal moles of atoms do not mean equal electrons: each aluminium atom gives three, each sodium atom one.
- Larger atoms do not give more electrons. Sodium gives **one per atom**, and fewer atoms fit in a gram.
- <InlineMath math="6.02 \times 10^{22}" /> counts one electron per aluminium atom instead of three.

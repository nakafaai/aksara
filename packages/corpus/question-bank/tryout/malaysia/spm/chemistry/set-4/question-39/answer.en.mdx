# Atom economy reads the equation, yield reads the brewery

**Atom economy** uses only the equation: one glucose, <InlineMath math="180" />, gives two ethanol, <InlineMath math="2 \times 46 = 92" />, so it is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{92}{180} \times 100\% = 51\%" />. The rest leaves as carbon dioxide however well the brewery works.

**Percentage yield** compares the real mass with the predicted one. Glucose: <InlineMath math="900 \div 180 = 5.00" /> kmol, which should give <InlineMath math="10.0" /> kmol of ethanol, <InlineMath math="10.0 \times 46 = 460\text{ kg}" />. The yield is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{368}{460} \times 100\% = 80\%" />.

**Hydration** gives one ethanol per ethene. <InlineMath math="368 \div 46 = 8.00" /> kmol of ethanol needs <InlineMath math="8.00" /> kmol of ethene, <InlineMath math="8.00 \times 28 = 224\text{ kg}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- An atom economy of <InlineMath math="100\%" /> mixes it up with yield. Even a perfect fermentation loses the **carbon dioxide's mass**.
- <InlineMath math="41\%" /> divides the ethanol by the glucose, <InlineMath math="368 \div 900" />, instead of by the ethanol the equation predicts.
- <InlineMath math="448\text{ kg}" /> takes two ethene per ethanol, copying the fermentation ratio into the hydration equation.

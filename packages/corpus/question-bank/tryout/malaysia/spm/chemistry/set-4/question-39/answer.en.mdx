# Wanted product over everything that went in

Hydration: the reactants weigh <InlineMath math="28 + 18 = 46" />, and the product is all ethanol, <InlineMath math="46" />, so the atom economy is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{46}{46} \times 100\% = 100\%" />. **Every atom ends up in the product**.

Fermentation: one glucose, <InlineMath math="180" />, gives two ethanol molecules, <InlineMath math="2 \times 46 = 92" />, so the atom economy is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{92}{180} \times 100\% = 51\%" />. The rest leaves as carbon dioxide. Fermentation still has other strengths: it runs on renewable sugar at body temperature.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Yeast works well, but the carbon dioxide it gives off is a by-product, so nearly half the mass is not ethanol.
- Steam is a reactant in hydration, but all of its atoms **end up in the ethanol**, so nothing is wasted.
- Fermentation makes carbon dioxide as well as ethanol, so it cannot reach <InlineMath math="100\%" />.

# Only the metal above hydrogen reacts

Zinc sits above hydrogen in the reactivity series, so it reacts: <InlineMath math="\text{Zn} + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{ZnCl}_2 + \text{H}_2" />. Copper sits **below hydrogen** and does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid.

The brass holds <InlineMath math="3.0" /> g of zinc: <InlineMath math="3.0 \div 65 = 0.0462" /> mol, which gives <InlineMath math="0.0462" /> mol of hydrogen, or <InlineMath math="0.0462 \times 24 = 1.11\text{ dm}^3" />. The <InlineMath math="7.0" /> g of copper stays behind as a solid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.69\text{ dm}^3" /> treats all <InlineMath math="10.0" /> g as zinc, but copper does not react.
- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ g}" /> is the mass of zinc that dissolves. The solid left is the <InlineMath math="7.0" /> g of copper.
- Each zinc atom gives **one hydrogen molecule**, made of two atoms, so the moles of gas equal the moles of zinc.

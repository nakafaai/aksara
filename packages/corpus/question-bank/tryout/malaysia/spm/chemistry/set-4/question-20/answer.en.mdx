# Hydrogen gives the zinc, the rest is copper that turns black

Only zinc sits above hydrogen, so only zinc reacts: <InlineMath math="\text{Zn} + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{ZnCl}_2 + \text{H}_2" />. Hydrogen: <InlineMath math="0.96 \div 24 = 0.040" /> mol, and **one zinc atom gives one hydrogen molecule**, so the brass held <InlineMath math="0.040 \times 65 = 2.60" /> g of zinc, which is <InlineMath math="26.0\%" />.

The solid left is the copper, <InlineMath math="10.0 - 2.60 = 7.40" /> g, or <InlineMath math="7.40 \div 64 = 0.1156" /> mol. Heating in air turns it into black copper(II) oxide, <InlineMath math="2\text{Cu} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CuO}" />, one <InlineMath math="\text{CuO}" /> for each <InlineMath math="\text{Cu}" />: <InlineMath math="0.1156 \times 80 = 9.25" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.9\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="13.0\%" /> take two zinc atoms per hydrogen molecule, which **halves the zinc**.
- <InlineMath math="9.02\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="27.9\%" /> use <InlineMath math="22.4\text{ dm}^3" />, the molar volume at STP, instead of the room value given.
- <InlineMath math="12.5\text{ g}" /> oxidises all <InlineMath math="10.0" /> g of brass, but the zinc already dissolved in the acid.

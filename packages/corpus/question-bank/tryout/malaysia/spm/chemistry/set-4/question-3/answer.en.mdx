# Turn the average into abundances, then count through the compound

Let boron-10 be a fraction <InlineMath math="x" /> of the atoms. The average mass is <InlineMath math="10.0x + 11.0(1 - x) = 10.8" />, so <InlineMath math="11.0 - x = 10.8" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 0.20" />: boron-10 is <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of the atoms.

Boron trifluoride: <InlineMath math="M_r = 10.8 + 3 \times 19 = 67.8" />, so <InlineMath math="6.78 \div 67.8 = 0.100" /> mol, holding **one boron atom per formula unit**, <InlineMath math="0.100" /> mol of boron. Boron-10: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 0.100 = 0.020" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="0.020 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 1.20 \times 10^{22}" /> atoms.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="80\%" /> belongs to boron-11. The average <InlineMath math="10.8" /> lies nearer <InlineMath math="11.0" />, so the heavier isotope is **the common one**.
- <InlineMath math="6.02 \times 10^{22}" /> counts every boron atom and forgets that only a fifth are boron-10.
- <InlineMath math="50\%" /> would give an average of <InlineMath math="10.5" />, not <InlineMath math="10.8" />.

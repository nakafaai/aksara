# Mean mass first, then the share of one isotope

The relative atomic mass is <InlineMath math="0.199 \times 10 + 0.801 \times 11 = 1.99 + 8.81 = 10.8" />. So <InlineMath math="1.08" /> g of natural boron is <InlineMath math="1.08 \div 10.8 = 0.100" /> mol of atoms.

Every atom is boron-10 with a chance of <InlineMath math="19.9\%" />, so boron-10 makes up <InlineMath math="0.199 \times 0.100 = 0.0199" /> mol: <InlineMath math="0.0199 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 1.20 \times 10^{22}" /> atoms. The **mean mass sets the total**, and the abundance picks out one isotope.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.82 \times 10^{22}" /> uses the abundance of **boron-11**, not boron-10.
- <InlineMath math="10.5" /> is the plain mean of <InlineMath math="10" /> and <InlineMath math="11" />, which ignores that boron-11 is four times as common.
- <InlineMath math="6.02 \times 10^{22}" /> counts every boron atom, both isotopes together.

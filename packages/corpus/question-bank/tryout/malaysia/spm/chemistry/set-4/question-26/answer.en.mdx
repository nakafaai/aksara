# Titre, ratio, scale up, then mass

Dichromate: <InlineMath math="0.0100 \times 0.0150 = 1.50 \times 10^{-4}" /> mol. **Each dichromate oxidises six iron(II) ions**, so the portion held <InlineMath math="9.00 \times 10^{-4}" /> mol of iron(II).

The portion is <InlineMath math="25.0" /> of <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />, a quarter of the tablet, so the tablet held <InlineMath math="3.60 \times 10^{-3}" /> mol, which is <InlineMath math="3.60 \times 10^{-3} \times 278 = 1.00" /> g. Share: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1.00}{1.50} \times 100\% = 66.7\%" />. Orange dichromate(VI) is reduced to green chromium(III).

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="16.7\%" /> forgets that the titrated portion is only a quarter **of the tablet**.
- <InlineMath math="11.1\%" /> takes one iron(II) per dichromate and so misses the factor of six, though it scales up by four.
- <InlineMath math="33.4\%" /> uses three iron(II) per dichromate, and the colour change is backwards: dichromate starts orange.

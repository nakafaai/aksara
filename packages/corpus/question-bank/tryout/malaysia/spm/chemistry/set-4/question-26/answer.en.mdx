# Six iron(II) ions for every dichromate ion

Dichromate used: <InlineMath math="0.0200 \times \tfrac{25.0}{1000} = 0.000500" /> mol. Chromium falls from <InlineMath math="+6" /> to <InlineMath math="+3" /> in both atoms, taking six electrons, and each iron(II) ion gives one, so the equation needs **six iron(II) ions per dichromate ion**: <InlineMath math="0.00300" /> mol.

In <InlineMath math="30.0\text{ cm}^3" /> that is <InlineMath math="0.00300 \div 0.0300 = 0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Orange dichromate(VI) ions turn into green chromium(III) ions as they are reduced.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.0167\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> treats the ratio as one to one.
- The colour runs from orange to green, because orange dichromate(VI) is **reduced to green chromium(III)**.
- <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> multiplies by six twice.

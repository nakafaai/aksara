Both bases take **two acid per formula unit**: <InlineMath math="\text{Mg(OH)}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{MgCl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{CaCO}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2" />. With <InlineMath math="a" /> mol of magnesium hydroxide (<InlineMath math="M_r = 58" />) and <InlineMath math="b" /> mol of calcium carbonate (<InlineMath math="M_r = 100" />): <InlineMath math="a + b = 0.0200" /> and <InlineMath math="58a + 100b = 1.58" />. Then <InlineMath math="2.00 - 42a = 1.58" />, so <InlineMath math="a = 0.0100" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.58\text{ g}" />.

Stomach acid: the tablet neutralises <InlineMath math="0.0400" /> mol, and <InlineMath math="0.0400 \div 0.10 = 0.400\text{ dm}^3 = 400\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.27\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}^3" /> use all the acid added and forget the part the sodium hydroxide **found left over**.
- <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" /> divides the <InlineMath math="0.0200" /> mol of base, not the <InlineMath math="0.0400" /> mol of acid it neutralises.
- <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ g}" /> is the mass of the calcium carbonate, not the magnesium hydroxide.

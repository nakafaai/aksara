# Two hydroxide ions in each formula unit

<InlineMath math="M_r = 24 + 2(16 + 1) = 58" />, so the tablet holds <InlineMath math="0.50 \div 58 = 0.00862" /> mol. Each formula unit carries **two hydroxide ions**, so it neutralises two moles of acid: <InlineMath math="0.0172" /> mol of hydrochloric acid.

At <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, that acid fills <InlineMath math="0.0172 \div 0.10 = 0.172\text{ dm}^3" />, or <InlineMath math="172\text{ cm}^3" />. Magnesium hydroxide is chosen because it is a weak, barely soluble base, so it neutralises the excess acid without making the stomach alkaline.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="86\text{ cm}^3" /> treats the reaction as one to one and misses the **second hydroxide ion**.
- <InlineMath math="345\text{ cm}^3" /> doubles the acid twice.
- <InlineMath math="17.2\text{ cm}^3" /> slips a decimal place when changing <InlineMath math="0.172\text{ dm}^3" /> into cubic centimetres.

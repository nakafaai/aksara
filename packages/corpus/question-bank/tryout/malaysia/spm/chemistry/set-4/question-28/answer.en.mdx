# Share four carbon atoms between the two parents

An ester joins an alcohol and an acid, and **all four carbon atoms** come from those two parts. The acid needs at least one carbon atom, the one in its <InlineMath math="\text{COO}" /> group, and so does the alcohol, so the splits are <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />, <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="2" />, or <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="1" />.

- Methanol with propanoic acid gives methyl propanoate.
- Ethanol with ethanoic acid gives ethyl ethanoate.
- Propan-1-ol with methanoic acid gives propyl methanoate.

All three have the formula <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8\text{O}_2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Two esters misses propyl methanoate, where the acid has a **single carbon atom**.
- Butyl methanoate has five carbon atoms: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_2" />.
- Ethyl propanoate also has five carbon atoms, so it is not an isomer of <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8\text{O}_2" />.

# Share five carbon atoms between the two halves

An ester <InlineMath math="\text{RCOOR'}" /> takes its carbons from the acid, which includes the <InlineMath math="\text{C=O}" /> carbon, and from the alcohol. Five carbons can split as acid and alcohol in four ways, each with an unbranched chain:

The name puts the **alcohol part first** and the acid part second: methyl butanoate, ethyl propanoate, propyl ethanoate and butyl methanoate. Hydrolysis returns each to its parents, so only **ethyl** propanoate gives ethanol, together with propanoic acid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3" /> esters misses one split, usually butyl methanoate, whose acid holds only **the carbonyl carbon**.
- Propyl ethanoate is made from propanol and ethanoic acid, so it gives propanol, not ethanol.
- Pentanoic acid has the same formula but is a carboxylic acid, not an ester.

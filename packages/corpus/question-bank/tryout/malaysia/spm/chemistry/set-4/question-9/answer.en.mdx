# The higher halogen wins, and the moles carry over one to one

Chlorine sits above bromine in Group 17, so its atoms **attract electrons more strongly** and take them from bromide ions: <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{Br}^- \rightarrow 2\text{Cl}^- + \text{Br}_2" />.

<InlineMath math="0.71 \div 71 = 0.010" /> mol of chlorine frees <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol of bromine molecules: <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 160 = 1.60" /> g. Bromine dissolves better in the organic solvent than in water, so it moves into that layer and colours it orange-brown.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A lower halogen cannot displace a higher one: bromine cannot free chlorine.
- <InlineMath math="0.80\text{ g}" /> uses <InlineMath math="80" />, one bromine atom, though bromine forms **diatomic molecules** of mass <InlineMath math="160" />.
- Purple in the organic layer is the colour of iodine, not bromine.

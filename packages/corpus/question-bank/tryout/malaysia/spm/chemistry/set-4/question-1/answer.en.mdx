# A titre comes from two readings, so it carries two uncertainties

The titre is the difference of the readings: <InlineMath math="24.80 - 1.25 = 23.55" /> cm³. Each reading can be off by <InlineMath math="0.05" /> cm³ in either direction, and in the worst case the two errors add, so the titre is uncertain by **±<InlineMath math="<InlineMath math="0.10" />\text{ cm}^3" />**.

As a share of the titre: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.10}{23.55} \times 100\% = 0.42\%" />. A larger titre would make the same ±<InlineMath math="<InlineMath math="0.10" />\text{ cm}^3" /> a smaller percentage, which is why titrations aim for titres above about <InlineMath math="20" /> cm³.

# Why the other options are wrong

- ±<InlineMath math="<InlineMath math="0.05" />\text{ cm}^3" /> counts only one reading, though the titre needs **both** the initial and the final reading.
- <InlineMath math="26.05\text{ cm}^3" /> adds the readings instead of subtracting them.
- <InlineMath math="4.2\%" /> slips a decimal place: <InlineMath math="0.10 \div 23.55" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0042" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.42\%" />.

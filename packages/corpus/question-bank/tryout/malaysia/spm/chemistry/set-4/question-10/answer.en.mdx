# The ion names the element, the reaction names the oxide

An element in Period <InlineMath math="3" /> that forms <InlineMath math="\text{R}^{2-}" /> **gains two electrons** to fill its third shell, so it has six valence electrons: sulfur. A Period <InlineMath math="3" /> element on the right of the table forms an acidic oxide, and pH <InlineMath math="1" /> confirms a strong acid.

Sodium hydroxide: <InlineMath math="8.0 \div 40 = 0.20" /> mol, twice the oxide. That fits <InlineMath math="\text{SO}_3 + 2\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}" />, so the salt is sodium sulfate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Chlorine forms ions of charge <InlineMath math="1-" />, and its oxides are acidic, not basic.
- Silicon does not form a <InlineMath math="2-" /> ion, and its oxide does not dissolve in water.
- An amphoteric oxide reacts with **both acids and alkalis**. Reacting with an alkali alone marks an acidic oxide.

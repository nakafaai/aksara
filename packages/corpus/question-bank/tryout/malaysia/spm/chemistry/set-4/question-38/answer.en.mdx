# Water joins each unit, then yeast splits each glucose

One unit, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5" />, has <InlineMath math="M_r = 72 + 10 + 80 = 162" />, so <InlineMath math="16.2" /> g is <InlineMath math="0.100" /> mol of units. Hydrolysis adds **one water molecule per unit**, giving <InlineMath math="0.100" /> mol of glucose (<InlineMath math="M_r = 180" />): <InlineMath math="0.100 \times 180 = 18.0" /> g. The glucose is heavier than the starch because it gained the water.

Fermentation: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{CO}_2" />. Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> reacts, <InlineMath math="0.080" /> mol, which releases <InlineMath math="2 \times 0.080 = 0.160" /> mol of carbon dioxide: <InlineMath math="0.160 \times 24 = 3.84\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="16.2\text{ g}" /> treats hydrolysis as if no atoms joined. Mass is conserved only when the water is counted too.
- <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" /> ferments all the glucose and ignores the <InlineMath math="80\%" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ dm}^3" /> takes one carbon dioxide per glucose, but each glucose releases **two**.

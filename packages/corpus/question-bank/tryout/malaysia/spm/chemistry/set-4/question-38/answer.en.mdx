# Each released unit gains one water molecule

A starch unit, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5" />, has <InlineMath math="M_r = 162" />, so <InlineMath math="16.2" /> g holds <InlineMath math="0.100" /> mol of units. Hydrolysis breaks each link by **adding a water molecule**, so every unit becomes one glucose molecule, <InlineMath math="M_r = 180" />.

The glucose weighs <InlineMath math="0.100 \times 180 = 18.0" /> g. Mass is still conserved: the extra <InlineMath math="1.8" /> g is the water taken up.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Mass is conserved for everything taking part, and that **includes the water** the starch takes up.
- Each unit gives one glucose molecule, not two.
- Complete hydrolysis breaks every link, so none of the starch is left.

# The acid decides how much salt can form

Magnesium oxide is in excess, so the leftover solid is **filtered off** and the acid sets the amount: <InlineMath math="1.00 \times \tfrac{60.0}{1000} = 0.0600" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4" />. <InlineMath math="\text{MgO} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{MgSO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}" /> is one to one, so <InlineMath math="0.0600" /> mol of magnesium sulfate forms.

Each unit crystallises with seven water molecules, so the crystals weigh <InlineMath math="0.0600 \times 246 = 14.8" /> g in theory and <InlineMath math="0.80 \times 14.8 = 11.8" /> g at <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="7.2\text{ g}" /> weighs anhydrous magnesium sulfate, <InlineMath math="0.0600 \times 120" />, but the crystals carry their **water of crystallisation**.
- <InlineMath math="14.8\text{ g}" /> is the theoretical mass, before the <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield.
- <InlineMath math="5.8\text{ g}" /> applies the yield to the anhydrous mass instead of the crystals.

# Find the limiting reactant, then carry the water of crystallisation

Magnesium oxide, <InlineMath math="M_r = 40" />: <InlineMath math="4.0 \div 40 = 0.10" /> mol. Sulfuric acid: <InlineMath math="1.00 \times 0.0600 = 0.060" /> mol. <InlineMath math="\text{MgO} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{MgSO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}" /> is one to one, so **the acid runs out first** and <InlineMath math="0.040" /> mol of oxide stays solid: <InlineMath math="0.040 \times 40 = 1.6" /> g on the filter paper.

Each mole of acid gives one mole of crystals, <InlineMath math="M_r = 120 + 7 \times 18 = 246" />: <InlineMath math="0.060 \times 246 = 14.8" /> g in theory, and <InlineMath math="14.8 \times 0.80 = 11.8" /> g at <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="14.8\text{ g}" /> is the mass at <InlineMath math="100\%" /> yield.
- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ g}" /> is the oxide that **dissolved**, not the oxide left behind.
- <InlineMath math="5.76\text{ g}" /> uses anhydrous magnesium sulfate, <InlineMath math="M_r = 120" />, and drops the seven waters in each crystal.

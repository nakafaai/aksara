# Balance the charges, then find the share of sulfur

The charges must cancel: <InlineMath math="+3" /> and <InlineMath math="-2" /> meet at <InlineMath math="6" />, so two aluminium ions balance three sulfate ions: <InlineMath math="\text{Al}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3" />. In water it splits into <InlineMath math="2 + 3 = 5" /> ions.

<InlineMath math="M_r = 2(27) + 3[32 + 4(16)] = 54 + 288 = 342" />. The three sulfur atoms weigh <InlineMath math="96" />, so sulfur is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{96}{342} \times 100\% = 28.1\%" /> of the mass.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{AlSO}_4" /> leaves a charge of <InlineMath math="+1" /> unbalanced.
- <InlineMath math="9.4\%" /> counts **one sulfur atom** where the formula has three.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Al}_3(\text{SO}_4)_2" /> gives <InlineMath math="+9" /> against <InlineMath math="-4" />.

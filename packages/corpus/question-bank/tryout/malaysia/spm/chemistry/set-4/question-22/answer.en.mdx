# Two silver atoms land for every copper atom that leaves

<InlineMath math="\text{Cu} + 2\text{Ag}^+ \rightarrow \text{Cu}^{2+} + 2\text{Ag}" />. Each copper atom gives up two electrons, so it frees **two silver atoms**. For every mole of copper, <InlineMath math="64" /> g leaves the wire and <InlineMath math="2 \times 108 = 216" /> g of silver lands on it, a gain of <InlineMath math="152" /> g. A gain of <InlineMath math="0.76" /> g means <InlineMath math="0.76 \div 152 = 0.0050" /> mol of copper reacted.

Silver formed: <InlineMath math="2 \times 0.0050 \times 108 = 1.08" /> g. Copper(II) ions: <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol in <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ dm}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0050 \div 0.250 = 0.020\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, which is why the solution turns blue.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.0050\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> is the amount in moles, not divided by the <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ dm}^3" /> of solution.
- <InlineMath math="0.76\text{ g}" /> is the **net** gain, which already subtracts the copper that dissolved.
- <InlineMath math="1.87\text{ g}" /> takes one silver atom per copper atom, a gain of only <InlineMath math="44" /> g per mole.

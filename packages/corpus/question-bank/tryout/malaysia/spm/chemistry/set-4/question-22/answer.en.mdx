# Each mole swaps <InlineMath math="65\text{ g}" /> of zinc for <InlineMath math="64\text{ g}" /> of copper

<InlineMath math="\text{Zn} + \text{Cu}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + \text{Cu}" />. For every mole that reacts, <InlineMath math="65" /> g of zinc leaves the strip and <InlineMath math="64" /> g of copper lands on it, so the solid **loses <InlineMath math="1\text{ g}" /> per mole**.

A loss of <InlineMath math="0.050" /> g means <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol reacted. So <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 64 = 3.2" /> g of copper formed and <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 65 = 3.25" /> g of zinc dissolved.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Copper does deposit, but each copper atom is lighter than the zinc atom it replaces, so the strip **loses** mass.
- <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ g}" /> is the change in mass, not the mass of copper.
- <InlineMath math="3.25\text{ g}" /> is the zinc that dissolves, not the copper that forms.

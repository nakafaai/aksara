# The strip loses <InlineMath math="1\text{ g}" /> for every mole that reacts

<InlineMath math="\text{Zn} + \text{Cu}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + \text{Cu}" />. For every mole that reacts, <InlineMath math="65" /> g of zinc leaves the strip and <InlineMath math="64" /> g of copper lands on it, so the solid **loses <InlineMath math="1\text{ g}" /> per mole**. A loss of <InlineMath math="0.050" /> g means <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol reacted.

Copper formed: <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 64 = 3.20" /> g. Each zinc atom that dissolves becomes one zinc ion, so <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{Zn}^{2+}" /> is now in <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ dm}^3" />: <InlineMath math="0.050 \div 0.250 = 0.20\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> forgets to convert <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ dm}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ g}" /> is the **net** change in mass, not the copper deposited.
- <InlineMath math="3.25\text{ g}" /> is the zinc that dissolved, not the copper that formed.

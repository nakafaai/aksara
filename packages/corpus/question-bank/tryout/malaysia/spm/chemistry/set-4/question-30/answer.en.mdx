# The mass lost is the carbon dioxide, which counts the ethanol

Only carbon dioxide leaves the flask, so the <InlineMath math="7.04" /> g lost is carbon dioxide: <InlineMath math="7.04 \div 44 = 0.16" /> mol. The equation makes **one ethanol for every carbon dioxide**, so <InlineMath math="0.16" /> mol of ethanol formed: <InlineMath math="0.16 \times 46 = 7.36" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="9.2\text{ g}" /> assumes all the glucose fermented, but that would release <InlineMath math="8.8" /> g of carbon dioxide, not <InlineMath math="7.04" /> g.
- <InlineMath math="3.68\text{ g}" /> treats each carbon dioxide as half an ethanol, but the equation **pairs them one to one**.
- <InlineMath math="14.7\text{ g}" /> doubles the ethanol and would need more glucose than the flask held.

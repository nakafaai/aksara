# One glucose gives two of each product

Glucose, <InlineMath math="M_r = 6(12) + 12(1) + 6(16) = 180" />: <InlineMath math="18.0 \div 180 = 0.10" /> mol. The equation gives **two ethanol and two carbon dioxide molecules** per glucose.

Ethanol: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 46 = 9.2" /> g. Carbon dioxide: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 24 = 4.8\text{ dm}^3" />. As a check, the masses balance: <InlineMath math="9.2 + 0.20 \times 44 = 9.2 + 8.8 = 18.0" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ dm}^3" /> counts one carbon dioxide molecule per glucose, but the **equation gives two**.
- <InlineMath math="4.6\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ dm}^3" /> halve both products.
- <InlineMath math="18.4\text{ g}" /> doubles the ethanol again, which would need more mass than the glucose had.

# Compare moles, then measure the ester against the limiting acid

Ethanoic acid, <InlineMath math="M_r = 60" />: <InlineMath math="6.0 \div 60 = 0.10" /> mol. Ethanol, <InlineMath math="M_r = 46" />: <InlineMath math="6.9 \div 46 = 0.15" /> mol. They react one to one, so **the acid runs out first**, even though the two masses are close.

Ethyl ethanoate, <InlineMath math="M_r = 88" />: <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol could give <InlineMath math="0.10 \times 88 = 8.80" /> g. The yield is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5.28}{8.80} \times 100\% = 60\%" />. Esterification is **reversible**, so the mixture reaches equilibrium before all the acid reacts, and some ester is lost in purification.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40\%" /> takes ethanol as limiting and expects <InlineMath math="13.2" /> g of ester, more than the **acid can make**.
- <InlineMath math="88\%" /> divides the ester's mass by the acid's mass, <InlineMath math="5.28 \div 6.0" />, ignoring that their molar masses differ.
- <InlineMath math="77\%" /> divides the ester's mass by the ethanol's mass, and ethanol is in excess.

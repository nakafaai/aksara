# One hydrogen molecule for each double bond

Ethene, <InlineMath math="M_r = 28" />: <InlineMath math="1.4 \div 28 = 0.050" /> mol. Its single carbon-carbon double bond takes **one hydrogen molecule**: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_6" />.

So <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol of hydrogen is needed, which is <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 24 = 1.2\text{ dm}^3" />, passed over a nickel catalyst at about <InlineMath math="180^\circ\text{C}" />. The product is ethane. The same reaction hardens vegetable oils into margarine.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ dm}^3" /> adds two hydrogen molecules, but ethene has only one double bond.
- Adding hydrogen gives ethane. Ethanol forms when ethene adds **steam**, not hydrogen.
- <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ dm}^3" /> halves the hydrogen and would leave half the ethene unsaturated.

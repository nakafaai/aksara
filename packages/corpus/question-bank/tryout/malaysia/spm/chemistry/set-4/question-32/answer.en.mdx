# One hydrogen molecule for every double bond, not every molecule

The oil is <InlineMath math="8.84 \div 884 = 0.010" /> mol of molecules. Each has **three carbon-carbon double bonds**, so there are <InlineMath math="0.030" /> mol of double bonds, and each takes one hydrogen molecule: <InlineMath math="0.030 \times 24 = 0.72\text{ dm}^3" />, passed over hot nickel.

With no double bonds left, the long chains straighten and pack closely, so the forces between molecules grow stronger. That is why the saturated fat has a **higher melting point** and is solid at room temperature, which is what margarine needs.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.24\text{ dm}^3" /> adds one hydrogen molecule per oil molecule, but each molecule has **three double bonds**.
- Saturating the chains raises the melting point. The oil is liquid and the fat is solid.
- <InlineMath math="2.16\text{ dm}^3" /> counts nine double bonds per molecule instead of three.

# Divide each heat by the moles of copper that actually formed

Copper(II) ions: <InlineMath math="0.200 \times 0.0250 = 0.0050" /> mol in each run, and the solution is <InlineMath math="25" /> g.

**Iron**, in excess, displaces all the copper: <InlineMath math="25 \times 4.2 \times 7.0 = 735\text{ J}" /> for <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol, so <InlineMath math="735 \div 0.0050 = 147\,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}" />.

**Magnesium**: <InlineMath math="0.060 \div 24 = 0.0025" /> mol, half the copper ions, so **magnesium is limiting** and only <InlineMath math="0.0025" /> mol of copper forms. Heat: <InlineMath math="25 \times 4.2 \times 12.5 = 1312.5\text{ J}" />, and <InlineMath math="1312.5 \div 0.0025 = 525\,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}" />. Both reactions warm the solution, so <InlineMath math="\Delta H = -147" /> and <InlineMath math="-525\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. Magnesium, far above copper in the electrochemical series, releases much more heat per mole.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-263\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides magnesium's heat by all <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol of copper ions, but only half **of them reacted**.
- <InlineMath math="-73.5" /> and <InlineMath math="-263" /> divide by twice the moles, counting electrons instead of copper atoms.
- Positive values would mean heat taken in, yet both temperatures rise.

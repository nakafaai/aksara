# Same moles of copper, different heat

Both runs displace the same copper: <InlineMath math="0.200 \times \tfrac{25.0}{1000} = 0.00500" /> mol. With iron: <InlineMath math="25.0 \times 4.2 \times 7.0 = 735" /> J, so <InlineMath math="\Delta H = -\tfrac{0.735}{0.00500} = -147\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. With magnesium: <InlineMath math="25.0 \times 4.2 \times 25.0 = 2625" /> J, so <InlineMath math="\Delta H = -525\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

The farther apart two metals sit in the electrochemical series, the more energy is released when one displaces the other. Magnesium is far above copper, iron only a little above it, so **magnesium releases more heat** per mole of copper.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The moles of copper are the same, but the heat per mole depends on which metal **gives the electrons**.
- <InlineMath math="-52.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by <InlineMath math="0.0500" /> mol instead of <InlineMath math="0.00500" />.
- The mass of the metal atoms does not set the heat. The gap in the electrochemical series does.

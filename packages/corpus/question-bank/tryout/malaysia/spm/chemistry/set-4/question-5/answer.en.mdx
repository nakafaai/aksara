# Run the two stages in order

<InlineMath math="\text{CaCO}_3 \rightarrow \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2" />: <InlineMath math="10.0 \div 100 = 0.10" /> mol of carbon dioxide. Sodium hydroxide: <InlineMath math="6.0 \div 40 = 0.15" /> mol.

**Stage 1** takes two hydroxide per carbon dioxide, so <InlineMath math="0.15" /> mol of hydroxide uses <InlineMath math="0.075" /> mol of carbon dioxide and makes <InlineMath math="0.075" /> mol of sodium carbonate. That leaves <InlineMath math="0.025" /> mol of carbon dioxide.

**Stage 2**: the leftover <InlineMath math="0.025" /> mol turns <InlineMath math="0.025" /> mol of carbonate into <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol of hydrogen carbonate. Left: <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3" />, <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 106 = 5.30" /> g, and <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{NaHCO}_3" />, <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 84 = 4.20" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.6\text{ g}" /> turns all the carbon dioxide into carbonate, which would need <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol of hydroxide.
- <InlineMath math="7.95\text{ g}" /> stops after stage 1 and forgets the <InlineMath math="0.025" /> mol of carbon dioxide still bubbling in.
- <InlineMath math="8.40\text{ g}" /> makes only hydrogen carbonate, which needs one hydroxide per carbon dioxide, but there is **more hydroxide than that**.

# The copper ions run out, so the solid is the copper formed plus the zinc left over

The zinc is <InlineMath math="1.30\div65=0.0200\text{ mol}" /> and the copper(II) ions <InlineMath math="0.100\times0.150=0.0150\text{ mol}" />. **They swap one for one, so the copper ions run out**, giving <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of copper, <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ g}" />, and leaving <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of zinc, <InlineMath math="0.325\text{ g}" />. **Both solids are collected**, <InlineMath math="0.960+0.325=1.29\text{ g}" />. The zinc ions formed are <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Each zinc gives two electrons, <InlineMath math="0.0300\times6.02\times10^{23}=1.81\times10^{22}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> lets all the zinc react, but only <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of copper ions are there.
- <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ g}" /> collects only the copper, but the zinc left over is on the filter too.
- <InlineMath math="9.03\times10^{21}" /> moves one electron per atom, but **each zinc atom gives two**.

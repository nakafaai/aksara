# Find which reactant runs out, then let it set the gas, the metal left and the salt

The magnesium is <InlineMath math="0.480\div24=0.0200\text{ mol}" /> and the acid <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" />. **The magnesium would need <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of acid, so the acid runs out**. It gives <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" />, and uses <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium, so <InlineMath math="0.0075\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.180\text{ g}" />, is left. The magnesium chloride is also <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" />, in <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> lets all the magnesium react, but <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of acid dissolves only <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> of it.
- <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> gives one salt per acid, but **two acid make one magnesium chloride**.
- <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen molecule per acid, but two acid give one hydrogen molecule.

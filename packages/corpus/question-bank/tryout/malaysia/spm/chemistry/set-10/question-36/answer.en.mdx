# Compare the fuels per gram, then let the heat the water needs set the propane and its carbon dioxide

Methane gives <InlineMath math="890\div16=55.6\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" /> and propane <InlineMath math="2220\div44=50.5\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" />, so **methane releases more per gram**, though propane releases more per mole. The water needs <InlineMath math="1000\times4.2\times75=315\text{ kJ}" />, so the propane is <InlineMath math="315\div2220=0.142\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.142\times44=6.24\text{ g}" />. **Each propane gives three carbon dioxide**, <InlineMath math="0.426\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="10.2\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Propane compares the fuels per mole. **Per gram, methane's lighter molecules give more**.
- <InlineMath math="3.41\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per propane, but each propane has three carbon atoms.
- <InlineMath math="2.08\text{ g}" /> heats the water by <InlineMath math="25^\circ\text{C}" /> instead of from <InlineMath math="25^\circ\text{C}" /> to <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" />, a rise of <InlineMath math="75^\circ\text{C}" />.

# The first reaction sets the azide and the sodium, and the sodium then decides the second reaction and its extra nitrogen

The nitrogen needed is <InlineMath math="72.0\div24=3.00\text{ mol}" />, and **two azide give three nitrogen**, so <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol}" /> of sodium azide, <InlineMath math="M_r=65" />, <InlineMath math="130\text{ g}" />. It also makes <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol}" /> of sodium. **Ten sodium take two potassium nitrate**, so <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.400\times101=40.4\text{ g}" />, and they give <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of nitrogen, <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" />. The total nitrogen is <InlineMath math="72.0+4.80=76.8\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="74.0\text{ g}" /> uses <InlineMath math="M_r=37" /> for sodium azide, as if it held one nitrogen atom. **The azide ion is <InlineMath math="\text{N}_3^-" />**, so <InlineMath math="M_r=65" />.
- <InlineMath math="202\text{ g}" /> takes one potassium nitrate per sodium, but the equation uses two for every ten.
- <InlineMath math="72.0\text{ dm}^3" /> stops at the first reaction and misses the nitrogen the second one adds.

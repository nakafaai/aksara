# Turn the atom counts into moles, weight them for the atomic mass and the neutrons, and then let the total boron meet the chlorine

The atoms are <InlineMath math="3.01\times10^{23}\div6.02\times10^{23}=0.500\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="^{10}\text{B}" /> and <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="^{11}\text{B}" />, **a fifth and four fifths of the <InlineMath math="2.50\text{ mol}" />**, so <InlineMath math="A_r=0.20\times10+0.80\times11=10.8" />. With five protons, <InlineMath math="^{10}\text{B}" /> has five neutrons and <InlineMath math="^{11}\text{B}" /> six, <InlineMath math="0.500\times5+2.00\times6=14.5\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="8.73\times10^{24}" />. **Two boron take three chlorine**, <InlineMath math="3.75\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="90.0\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.5" /> averages the two mass numbers as if the isotopes were equally common, but four atoms in five are <InlineMath math="^{11}\text{B}" />.
- <InlineMath math="7.53\times10^{24}" /> gives every atom five neutrons, but **<InlineMath math="^{11}\text{B}" /> has six**.
- <InlineMath math="60.0\text{ dm}^3" /> takes one chlorine per boron, but two boron take three chlorine.

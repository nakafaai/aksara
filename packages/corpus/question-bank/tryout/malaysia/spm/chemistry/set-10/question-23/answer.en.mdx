# The copper counts the electrons, and the anode turns the same electrons into oxygen and hydrogen ions

The copper is <InlineMath math="0.640\div64=0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and **each copper ion takes two electrons**, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. At the anode four electrons come with one oxygen and four hydrogen ions, so the oxygen is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />, and **the hydrogen ions are <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />** in <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, pH <InlineMath math="1" />. The charge is <InlineMath math="0.0200\times96\,500=1930\text{ C}" />, so the time is <InlineMath math="1930\div0.500=3860\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one oxygen per copper, but one oxygen needs four electrons, the electrons of two copper ions.
- pH <InlineMath math="1.3" /> gives one hydrogen ion per copper, but **each oxygen comes with four hydrogen ions**, two per copper.
- <InlineMath math="1930\text{ s}" /> gives each copper ion one electron, but <InlineMath math="\text{Cu}^{2+}" /> takes two.

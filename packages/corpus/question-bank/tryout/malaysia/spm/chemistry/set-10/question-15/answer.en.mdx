# Dilution changes the pH but not the moles of acid, and a weak acid still gives all its hydrogen to magnesium

The acid is <InlineMath math="0.0100\times0.100=0.00100\text{ mol}" />, and in <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ dm}^3" /> that is <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+]=0.00100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, **a hundredfold dilution**, so the pH rises from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="3" />. Diluting adds no acid, so all <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" /> reaches the magnesium, and two acid give one hydrogen, <InlineMath math="5.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ cm}^3" />. **Ethanoic acid ionises only partly, but as magnesium uses its ions more molecules ionise**, so its <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" /> also gives <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ cm}^3" />, only more slowly.

# Why the other options are wrong

- pH <InlineMath math="2" /> counts a tenfold dilution, but **<InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" /> is a hundredfold one**.
- <InlineMath math="24.0\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen molecule per acid, but two acid give one.
- <InlineMath math="0.120\text{ cm}^3" /> uses only <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of the diluted acid, but all <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ dm}^3" /> of it reacts.

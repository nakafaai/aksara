# Each pH unit is a tenfold dilution, and the diluted acid is then split between the burette and the magnesium

The acid is <InlineMath math="0.0100\times0.100=0.00100\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+]=1.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, **a hundredfold dilution**, so the pH rises from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="3" />. The <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> portion holds <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^{-4}\div0.00200=0.0500\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali. **The rest is <InlineMath math="900\text{ cm}^3" />**, <InlineMath math="9.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, and two acid give one hydrogen, <InlineMath math="4.50\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="10.8\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- pH <InlineMath math="2" /> counts a tenfold dilution, but **<InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" /> is a hundredfold one**.
- <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> gives each acid two hydroxide ions, but hydrochloric acid has one acidic hydrogen.
- <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ cm}^3" /> lets all the acid reach the magnesium, forgetting the <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> portion taken for the titration.

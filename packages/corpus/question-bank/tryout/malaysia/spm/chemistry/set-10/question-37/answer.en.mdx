# The repeat unit sets the chain length and the moles, each unit releases two water, and the yield works backwards to the acid fed in

An average chain holds <InlineMath math="22\,600\div226=100" /> repeat units. The batch is <InlineMath math="22\,600\div226=100\text{ mol}" /> of units, and **each unit forms two amide links, each releasing one water**, <InlineMath math="200\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="200\times18=3.60\text{ kg}" />. Each unit holds one acid, <InlineMath math="M_r=146" />, so the nylon contains <InlineMath math="100\times146=14.6\text{ kg}" /> of it. **Only <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of the acid fed in ends up there**, so <InlineMath math="14.6\div0.90=16.2\text{ kg}" /> must be fed in.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.80\text{ kg}" /> releases one water per unit, but **each unit has two amide links**, and each link releases one water.
- <InlineMath math="13.1\text{ kg}" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="90\%" />, but losing acid means more must be fed in, so divide by <InlineMath math="0.90" />.
- <InlineMath math="155" /> divides by the acid's <InlineMath math="M_r" />, but the repeat unit, <InlineMath math="M_r=226" />, is what repeats along the chain.

# The oxide's formula turns the oxygen into moles of X, the atomic mass names it, the electron arrangement places it, and its hydrogen carries on to the copper

The oxygen is <InlineMath math="2.04-1.08=0.96\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" /> of atoms, and **the formula pairs two X with three O**, so X is <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> and <InlineMath math="A_r=1.08\div0.0400=27" />, aluminium. Its electron arrangement <InlineMath math="2.8.3" /> has three shells, so it is in Period <InlineMath math="3" />, and three valence electrons, which **in the modern table puts it in Group <InlineMath math="13" />**. Two aluminium give three hydrogen, <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" />, and each hydrogen reduces one copper(II) oxide, so <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" /> of copper, <InlineMath math="3.84\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Group <InlineMath math="3" /> counts the valence electrons as the group number, but for Groups <InlineMath math="13" /> to <InlineMath math="18" /> **the group number is ten more than the valence electrons**.
- <InlineMath math="2.56\text{ g}" /> gives one hydrogen molecule per atom of X, but two atoms give three molecules.
- <InlineMath math="7.68\text{ g}" /> gives three hydrogen molecules per atom of X, but three molecules come from two atoms.

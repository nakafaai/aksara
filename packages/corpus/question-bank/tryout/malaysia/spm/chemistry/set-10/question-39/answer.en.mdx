# Each calcium ion takes two soap anions into scum, while one carbonate ion is enough to remove it first

The water holds <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of calcium ions. **Each takes two stearate ions**, so <InlineMath math="0.00400\text{ mol}" /> of soap, <InlineMath math="M_r=306" />, <InlineMath math="1.22\text{ g}" />, is wasted, forming <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of scum, <InlineMath math="M_r=606" />, <InlineMath math="1.21\text{ g}" />. Sodium carbonate removes each calcium ion with one carbonate ion, <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="M_r=106" />, <InlineMath math="0.212\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.612\text{ g}" /> gives each calcium ion one stearate ion, but **its charge of two needs two**.
- <InlineMath math="2.42\text{ g}" /> forms one scum per soap anion, but one scum holds two stearate ions.
- <InlineMath math="0.424\text{ g}" /> gives each calcium ion two carbonate ions, but both ions carry a charge of two.

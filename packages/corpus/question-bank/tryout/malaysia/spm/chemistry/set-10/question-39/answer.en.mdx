# Each calcium ion takes two soap anions, so the soap runs out first, and carbonate removes the calcium left

The soap is <InlineMath math="0.918\div306=0.00300\text{ mol}" />, and **each calcium ion takes two stearate ions**, so it removes only <InlineMath math="0.00150\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of calcium. The soap runs out, forming <InlineMath math="0.00150\text{ mol}" /> of scum, <InlineMath math="0.00150\times606=0.909\text{ g}" />, and leaving <InlineMath math="0.000500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> of calcium ions. Each needs one carbonate ion, <InlineMath math="0.000500\times106=0.0530\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.21\text{ g}" /> gives each calcium ion one stearate ion, so the calcium runs out, but **its charge of two takes two stearate ions**.
- <InlineMath math="0.212\text{ g}" /> softens all the calcium the water started with, but the soap already removed <InlineMath math="0.00150\text{ mol}" /> of it.
- <InlineMath math="0.106\text{ g}" /> gives each calcium ion two carbonate ions, but both ions carry a charge of two.

# Three electrons per aluminium set the charge, the same electrons make the oxygen that burns the anode, and the current sets the time

The aluminium is <InlineMath math="2700\div27=100\text{ mol}" />, and **each ion takes three electrons**, <InlineMath math="300\text{ mol}" />, so the charge is <InlineMath math="300\times96\,500=2.90\times10^7\text{ C}" />. At the anode four electrons come with each oxygen molecule, so the same <InlineMath math="300\text{ mol}" /> of electrons free <InlineMath math="75.0\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen. **Each oxygen burns one carbon**, <InlineMath math="75.0\times12=900\text{ g}" />. The time is <InlineMath math="2.90\times10^7\div(1.00\times10^4)=2.90\times10^3\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="9.65\times10^6\text{ C}" /> gives one electron per aluminium, but **<InlineMath math="\text{Al}^{3+}" /> takes three**.
- <InlineMath math="1800\text{ g}" /> frees one oxygen per two electrons, but each oxygen molecule takes four electrons from two oxide ions.
- <InlineMath math="1.93\times10^7\text{ C}" /> gives two electrons per aluminium, the charge of an oxide ion, not of an aluminium ion.

# The yield sets the decane that cracks, each decane gives two ethene and one hexane, and polymerising keeps the ethene's mass

The decane is <InlineMath math="14.2\div142=0.100\text{ mol}" />, and **only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> cracks**, <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" />. Each cracked decane gives two ethene, <InlineMath math="0.160\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="3.84\text{ dm}^3" />, and one hexane, <InlineMath math="0.0800\times86=6.88\text{ g}" />. **Addition polymerisation adds no atoms and loses none**, so the poly(ethene) has the ethene's mass, <InlineMath math="0.160\times28=4.48\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" /> cracks all the decane, but only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of it does.
- <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one ethene per decane, but **each decane gives two**.
- <InlineMath math="8.60\text{ g}" /> of hexane uses all the decane, but only the <InlineMath math="80\%" /> that cracks makes hexane.

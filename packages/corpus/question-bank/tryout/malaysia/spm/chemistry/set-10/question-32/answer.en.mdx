# Halve the acid, then find that the carbonate runs out first and leaves acid for the alkali

The acid is <InlineMath math="1.48\div74=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, so **each half is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />**. With magnesium two acid give one hydrogen, <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. The carbonate is <InlineMath math="0.212\div106=0.00200\text{ mol}" />, and **it needs only <InlineMath math="0.00400\text{ mol}" /> of acid**, so it runs out, giving <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="48.0\text{ cm}^3" />. The <InlineMath math="0.00600\text{ mol}" /> of acid left needs <InlineMath math="0.00600\div0.100=0.0600\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="60.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> of carbon dioxide lets the acid decide, but <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of carbonate runs out first.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen gives one molecule per acid, but two acid give one.
- <InlineMath math="80.0\text{ cm}^3" /> takes one acid per carbonate, but **each carbonate takes two acid**.

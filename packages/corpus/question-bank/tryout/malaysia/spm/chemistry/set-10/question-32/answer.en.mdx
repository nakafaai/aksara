# Halve the acid first, then let two acid make one molecule of each product

The acid is <InlineMath math="1.48\div74=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, so **each half is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />**. With magnesium two acid give one hydrogen and one salt, so the hydrogen is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />, and the magnesium propanoate, <InlineMath math="M_r=170" />, is <InlineMath math="0.00500\times170=0.850\text{ g}" />. With carbonate two acid also give one carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="1.70\text{ g}" /> use all the acid in each test, but **each test gets only half**.
- <InlineMath math="1.70\text{ g}" /> gives one salt per acid, but one magnesium propanoate holds two propanoate ions.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen gives one molecule per acid, but two acid give one.

# The bromine counts the alkene and gives its formula, and the hydrogen then runs out before the alkene

The bromine is <InlineMath math="3.20\div160=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, and **each double bond takes one bromine molecule**, so the alkene is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="M_r=70" />, and <InlineMath math="14n=70" /> gives <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}" />. The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="360\div24\,000=0.0150\text{ mol}" />, **less than the alkene**, so it runs out, making <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of pentane, <InlineMath math="M_r=72" />, <InlineMath math="1.08\text{ g}" />, and leaving <InlineMath math="0.0050\text{ mol}" /> of alkene, <InlineMath math="0.350\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.44\text{ g}" /> hydrogenates all the alkene, but only <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen is there.
- <InlineMath math="1.05\text{ g}" /> weighs the pentane with the alkene's <InlineMath math="M_r=70" />, but adding hydrogen makes it <InlineMath math="72" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{20}" /> gives each alkene two bromine molecules, but **one double bond takes one**.

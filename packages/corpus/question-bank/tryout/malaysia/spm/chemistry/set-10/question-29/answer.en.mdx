# The bromine counts the alkene's double bonds, which gives its molar mass, and the same moles go into both additions

The bromine is <InlineMath math="3.20\div160=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, and **each double bond takes one bromine molecule**, so the alkene is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> and <InlineMath math="M_r=1.40\div0.0200=70" />. From <InlineMath math="14n=70" />, <InlineMath math="n=5" />, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}" />. Addition puts every atom into the product, so the dibromide is <InlineMath math="1.40+3.20=4.60\text{ g}" />. With steam the <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> gains one water each, giving <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{11}\text{OH}" /> with <InlineMath math="M_r=88" />, <InlineMath math="0.0200\times88=1.76\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.00\text{ g}" /> adds one bromine atom, but **addition across the double bond puts on both atoms of <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" />**.
- <InlineMath math="1.40\text{ g}" /> of alcohol forgets that each molecule gains a water molecule, <InlineMath math="M_r=18" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{20}" /> gives each alkene two bromine molecules, but one double bond takes one.

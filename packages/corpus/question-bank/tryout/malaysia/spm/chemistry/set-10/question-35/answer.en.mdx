# The chloride halves in the second run, so it runs out, and both the heat and the precipitate halve

The mixture takes in <InlineMath math="100\times4.2\times3.9=1638\text{ J}" /> from <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of silver chloride, so <InlineMath math="\Delta H=-1638\div0.0250=-65.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. In the second run the silver is still <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> but the chloride is only <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" />, **so the chloride runs out** and <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> precipitates, <InlineMath math="0.0125\times143.5=1.79\text{ g}" />. That releases half the heat into the same <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />, a rise of <InlineMath math="1.95^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.59\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.9^\circ\text{C}" /> let the silver decide, but only <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> of chloride is there to take it.
- <InlineMath math="-32.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by the silver and the chloride together, but **the heat is per mole of precipitate**.
- <InlineMath math="3.9^\circ\text{C}" /> keeps the first rise, but half as much precipitate forms, so half the heat warms the same mass.

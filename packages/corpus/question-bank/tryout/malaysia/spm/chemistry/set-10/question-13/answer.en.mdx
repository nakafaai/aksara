# Two chloride ions per formula unit set the chloride, and the silver is in excess in the mixed volume

The calcium chloride is <InlineMath math="1.11\div111=0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and **each formula unit gives two chloride ions**, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The portion holds <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of chloride and the silver nitrate <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, so **the chloride runs out**, giving <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of silver chloride, <InlineMath math="0.00200\times143.5=0.287\text{ g}" />. The <InlineMath math="0.00300\text{ mol}" /> of silver ions left are in <InlineMath math="25.0+50.0=75.0\text{ cm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> gives one chloride per formula unit, but **<InlineMath math="\text{CaCl}_2" /> releases two**.
- <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> divides by the <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of silver nitrate alone, but the ions now share the whole <InlineMath math="75.0\text{ cm}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.718\text{ g}" /> lets all the silver precipitate, but only <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of chloride is there to take it.

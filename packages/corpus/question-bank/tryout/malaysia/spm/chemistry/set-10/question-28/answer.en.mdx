# Complete oxidation sets the titre to expect, the real titre gives the fraction, and the fraction carries back to the whole flask

The ethanol is <InlineMath math="2.30\div46=0.0500\text{ mol}" />. Complete oxidation would give <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of acid, <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> in each portion, which **would need <InlineMath math="0.00500\div0.100=0.0500\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />**. The real titre is <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="40.0\div50.0=80.0\%" /> was oxidised. The flask holds <InlineMath math="10\times0.0400\times0.100=0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of acid, <InlineMath math="0.0400\times60=2.40\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.84\text{ g}" /> weighs the acid with the <InlineMath math="M_r" /> of ethanol, <InlineMath math="46" />, but ethanoic acid has <InlineMath math="M_r=60" />.
- <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> gives each acid two hydroxide ions, but **ethanoic acid has one acidic hydrogen**.
- <InlineMath math="0.240\text{ g}" /> is the acid in one portion, not in the whole <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />.

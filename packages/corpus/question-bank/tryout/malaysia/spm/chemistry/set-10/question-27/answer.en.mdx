# Read every product and reactant through the same equation, halving the coefficients for one butane

Butane has <InlineMath math="M_r=58" />, so <InlineMath math="2.90\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />. **The equation is written for two butane**, so one butane gives four carbon dioxide and five water and takes <InlineMath math="6.5" /> oxygen. That is <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="8.80\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.325\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen, <InlineMath math="7.80\text{ dm}^3" />, and <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ mol}" /> of water, <InlineMath math="4.50\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.20\text{ g}" /> gives one carbon dioxide per butane, but **each butane has four carbon atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="15.6\text{ dm}^3" /> uses thirteen oxygen per butane, but thirteen is for two butane.
- <InlineMath math="9.00\text{ g}" /> uses ten water per butane, but ten is for two butane.

# Two unknowns need two equations, one from the mass and one from the carbon, and the moles then give the water and the oxygen

With <InlineMath math="p" /> mol of propane and <InlineMath math="b" /> mol of butane, the mass gives <InlineMath math="44p+58b=5.10" />, and **the carbon gives <InlineMath math="3p+4b=15.4\div44=0.350" />**. These give <InlineMath math="p=b=0.0500" />, so the butane is <InlineMath math="0.0500\times58=2.90\text{ g}" />. Propane makes four water and butane five, <InlineMath math="0.200+0.250=0.450\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="8.10\text{ g}" />. Propane takes five oxygen and butane <InlineMath math="6.5" />, <InlineMath math="0.250+0.325=0.575\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="13.8\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.55\text{ g}" /> splits the mass in half, but the carbon dioxide shows equal moles, not equal masses.
- <InlineMath math="9.00\text{ g}" /> gives propane five water, but **<InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_8" /> has eight hydrogen atoms**, four water.
- <InlineMath math="21.6\text{ dm}^3" /> uses thirteen oxygen per butane, but thirteen is for two butane.

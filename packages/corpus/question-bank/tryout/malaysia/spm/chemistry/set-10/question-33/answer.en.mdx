# The heat per mole of water is fixed, and the rise depends on the water formed in each gram of solution

The solution has a mass of <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> and takes in <InlineMath math="100\times4.2\times6.8=2856\text{ J}" />. **One mole of water forms per mole of acid**, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="\Delta H=-2856\div0.0500=-57.1\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. With <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of each, the water formed and the mass both double, so the rise stays <InlineMath math="6.8^\circ\text{C}" />. **With <InlineMath math="30.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali, the alkali runs out**, forming <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of water, <InlineMath math="0.0300\times57\,120=1714\text{ J}" />, into <InlineMath math="80.0\text{ g}" />, a rise of <InlineMath math="1714\div(80.0\times4.2)=5.10^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="13.6^\circ\text{C}" /> for the larger volumes doubles the heat but not the mass it warms.
- <InlineMath math="8.50^\circ\text{C}" /> lets all the acid react, but **only <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of alkali is there**, so only that much water forms.
- <InlineMath math="-28.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by the acid and the alkali together, but the heat of neutralisation is per mole of water.

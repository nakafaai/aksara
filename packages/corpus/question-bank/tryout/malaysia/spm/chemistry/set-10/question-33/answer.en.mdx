# The heat per mole of water is fixed, so the rise depends on the moles of water formed in each gram of solution

The solution has a mass of <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> and takes in <InlineMath math="100\times4.2\times6.8=2856\text{ J}" />. **One mole of water forms per mole of acid**, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="\Delta H=-2856\div0.0500=-57.1\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. With <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of each, the water formed and the mass both double, so **the rise stays <InlineMath math="6.8^\circ\text{C}" />**. With <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> solutions the water formed doubles but the mass stays <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />, so the rise doubles to <InlineMath math="13.6^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="13.6^\circ\text{C}" /> for the larger volumes doubles the heat but not the mass it warms.
- <InlineMath math="6.8^\circ\text{C}" /> for the stronger solutions keeps the heat the same, but twice the water forms in the same <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />.
- <InlineMath math="-28.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by the acid and the alkali together, but **the heat of neutralisation is per mole of water**.

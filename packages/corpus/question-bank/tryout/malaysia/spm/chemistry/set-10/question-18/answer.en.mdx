# Dilution sets the new concentration and so the time, but the acid, not the thiosulfate, limits the sulfur

In Experiment II the thiosulfate is diluted from <InlineMath math="30.0\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />, so it is <InlineMath math="0.150\times30.0\div50.0=0.0900\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. **The time is inversely proportional to the concentration**, so it becomes <InlineMath math="24\times0.150\div0.0900=40\text{ s}" />. In Experiment I the thiosulfate is <InlineMath math="0.00750\text{ mol}" /> and the acid <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />. **Each thiosulfate takes two acid**, so the acid runs out after <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of thiosulfate, giving <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of sulfur, <InlineMath math="0.160\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="14.4\text{ s}" /> shortens the time at the lower concentration, but **fewer collisions each second mean a longer time**.
- <InlineMath math="0.240\text{ g}" /> lets all the thiosulfate react, but <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of acid is enough for only <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of it.
- <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> uses the <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of water as the share of thiosulfate, but the thiosulfate is the <InlineMath math="30.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

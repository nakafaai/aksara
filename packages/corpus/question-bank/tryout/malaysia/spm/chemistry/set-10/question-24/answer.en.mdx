# Split the ore into its oxide and its silica, then follow each into its own reaction and add up the gas

The ore holds <InlineMath math="0.80\times1.60=1.28" /> tonnes of iron(III) oxide, <InlineMath math="1.28\times10^6\div160=8000\text{ mol}" />, and **each oxide gives two iron**, <InlineMath math="16\,000\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="896\text{ kg}" />. The silica is <InlineMath math="0.15\times1.60=0.24" /> tonnes, <InlineMath math="0.24\times10^6\div60=4000\text{ mol}" />, and each silica takes one limestone, <InlineMath math="4000\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="400\text{ kg}" />. **Carbon dioxide comes from both reactions**, three per oxide, <InlineMath math="24\,000\text{ mol}" />, and one per limestone, <InlineMath math="4000\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="28\,000\times24=6.72\times10^5\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1120\text{ kg}" /> treats all the ore as iron(III) oxide, but only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of it is.
- <InlineMath math="448\text{ kg}" /> gives one iron per oxide, but **<InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3" /> holds two iron atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="5.76\times10^5\text{ dm}^3" /> counts only the carbon dioxide from the reduction and misses the limestone's.

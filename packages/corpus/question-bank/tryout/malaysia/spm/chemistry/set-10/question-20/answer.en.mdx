# The conversion limits the ammonia, the ammonia sets the fertiliser, and its hydrogen works back to the methane

The nitrogen is <InlineMath math="2800\div28=100\text{ mol}" />, and **only <InlineMath math="15\%" /> reacts**, <InlineMath math="15.0\text{ mol}" />. Each nitrogen molecule gives two ammonia, <InlineMath math="30.0\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="30.0\times17=510\text{ g}" />, and each ammonia gives one ammonium nitrate, <InlineMath math="30.0\times80=2.40\text{ kg}" />. The ammonia holds three hydrogen atoms each, so it used <InlineMath math="45.0\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen molecules, and **each methane gives four**, <InlineMath math="11.25\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="11.25\times16=180\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="255\text{ g}" /> gives one ammonia per nitrogen molecule, but **each molecule has two nitrogen atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="3.40\text{ kg}" /> converts all the nitrogen, but only <InlineMath math="15\%" /> reacts in one pass.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ g}" /> takes three hydrogen per methane, but the second water in the equation adds a fourth.

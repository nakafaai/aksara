# The conversion limits the ammonia, each nitrogen molecule gives two ammonia, and the fertiliser keeps both nitrogen atoms

The nitrogen is <InlineMath math="2800\div28=100\text{ mol}" />, and **only <InlineMath math="15\%" /> reacts**, <InlineMath math="15.0\text{ mol}" />. Each nitrogen molecule gives two ammonia, <InlineMath math="30.0\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="30.0\times17=510\text{ g}" />. Each ammonia gives one ammonium nitrate, <InlineMath math="30.0\times80=2400\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ kg}" />. **Ammonium nitrate holds two nitrogen atoms**, one in <InlineMath math="\text{NH}_4^+" /> and one in <InlineMath math="\text{NO}_3^-" />, so it is <InlineMath math="28\div80=35.0\%" /> nitrogen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="255\text{ g}" /> gives one ammonia per nitrogen molecule, but **each molecule has two nitrogen atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="3.40\text{ kg}" /> converts all the nitrogen, but only <InlineMath math="15\%" /> reacts in one pass.
- <InlineMath math="17.5\%" /> counts one nitrogen atom, but the nitrate ion carries a second one.

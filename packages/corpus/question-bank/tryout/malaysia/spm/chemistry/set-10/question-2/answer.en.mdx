# Weight the isotopes for the atomic mass, pair them for the molecules, and count atoms, not molecules, at the end

The relative atomic mass is <InlineMath math="0.75\times35+0.25\times37=35.5" />. A molecule of mass <InlineMath math="72" /> has one atom of each isotope, and **that can happen two ways**, <InlineMath math="^{35}\text{Cl}\text{ then }^{37}\text{Cl}" /> or the reverse, so its share is <InlineMath math="2\times0.75\times0.25=0.375" />, <InlineMath math="37.5\%" />. The gas is <InlineMath math="7.10\div71=0.100\text{ mol}" /> of molecules, <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of atoms, and **a quarter of the atoms are <InlineMath math="^{37}\text{Cl}" />**, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0500\times6.02\times10^{23}=3.01\times10^{22}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="36.0" /> averages the two masses as if the isotopes were equally common.
- <InlineMath math="18.75\%" /> counts only one order of the two atoms. **The mixed pair can form two ways**.
- <InlineMath math="1.51\times10^{22}" /> counts molecules, but each molecule holds two atoms.

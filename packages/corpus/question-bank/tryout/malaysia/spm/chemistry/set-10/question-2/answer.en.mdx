# Weight the isotopes for the atomic mass, pair them for the molecules, and then weigh only the heaviest pairs

The relative atomic mass is <InlineMath math="0.75\times35+0.25\times37=35.5" />. A molecule of mass <InlineMath math="72" /> has one atom of each isotope, and **that can happen two ways**, so its share is <InlineMath math="2\times0.75\times0.25=0.375" />, <InlineMath math="37.5\%" />. The gas is <InlineMath math="7.10\div71=0.100\text{ mol}" /> of molecules, and **a molecule of mass <InlineMath math="74" /> needs both atoms to be <InlineMath math="^{37}\text{Cl}" />**, <InlineMath math="0.25\times0.25=0.0625" />, so <InlineMath math="0.00625\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.00625\times74=0.463\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="36.0" /> averages the two masses as if the isotopes were equally common.
- <InlineMath math="18.75\%" /> counts only one order of the two atoms. **The mixed pair can form two ways**.
- <InlineMath math="1.85\text{ g}" /> takes a quarter of the molecules as mass <InlineMath math="74" />, but both atoms must be the heavier isotope, <InlineMath math="6.25\%" />.

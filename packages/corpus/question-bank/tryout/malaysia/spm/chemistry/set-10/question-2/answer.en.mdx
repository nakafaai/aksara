# Weight the isotopes for the atomic mass, pair them for the molecules, and then follow each atom into its own hydrogen chloride

The relative atomic mass is <InlineMath math="0.75\times35+0.25\times37=35.5" />. A molecule of mass <InlineMath math="72" /> has one atom of each isotope, and **that can happen two ways**, so its share is <InlineMath math="2\times0.75\times0.25=0.375" />, <InlineMath math="37.5\%" />. The gas is <InlineMath math="7.10\div71=0.100\text{ mol}" /> of molecules, which give <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen chloride, **one for each chlorine atom**. A quarter of the atoms are <InlineMath math="^{37}\text{Cl}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{H}^{37}\text{Cl}" />, <InlineMath math="M_r=38" />, <InlineMath math="1.90\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="36.0" /> averages the two masses as if the isotopes were equally common.
- <InlineMath math="18.75\%" /> counts only one order of the two atoms. **The mixed pair can form two ways**.
- <InlineMath math="0.950\text{ g}" /> takes a quarter of the chlorine molecules, but each molecule gives two hydrogen chloride, one for each atom.

# The mass lost is oxygen, which counts the chlorate decomposed, and the catalyst stays in the solid to the end

The solid lost <InlineMath math="2.95-2.47=0.48\text{ g}" /> of oxygen, <InlineMath math="0.48\div32=0.0150\text{ mol}" />, and **two chlorate give three oxygen**, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of chlorate had decomposed, half of the <InlineMath math="2.45\div122.5=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="50\%" />. The oxygen is <InlineMath math="0.0150\times24\,000=360\text{ cm}^3" /> in <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" />, <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />. At the end all the chlorate becomes <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of potassium chloride, <InlineMath math="1.49\text{ g}" />, and **the catalyst is still there**, <InlineMath math="1.49+0.50=1.99\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="75\%" /> gives one chlorate per oxygen, but **two chlorate give three oxygen**, so <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen comes from only <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of chlorate.
- <InlineMath math="1.49\text{ g}" /> leaves out the catalyst, but a catalyst comes out of the reaction with its mass unchanged.
- <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides the final volume of oxygen, <InlineMath math="720\text{ cm}^3" />, by <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" />, but only half of it had formed by then.

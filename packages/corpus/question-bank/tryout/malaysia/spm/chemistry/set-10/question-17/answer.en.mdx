# The catalyst is not used up, so the solid left is the potassium chloride plus all the manganese(IV) oxide

The chlorate is <InlineMath math="2.45\div122.5=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, and **two chlorate give three oxygen**, <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="720\text{ cm}^3" />. It leaves <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of potassium chloride, <InlineMath math="0.0200\times74.5=1.49\text{ g}" />, and **a catalyst is not used up**, so all <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ g}" /> of the manganese(IV) oxide is still there, <InlineMath math="1.99\text{ g}" /> of solid. Half the oxygen is <InlineMath math="360\text{ cm}^3" />, reached at <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" />, so the average rate over that time is <InlineMath math="360\div60=6.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one oxygen per chlorate, but **two chlorate give three oxygen**.
- <InlineMath math="1.49\text{ g}" /> leaves out the catalyst, but a catalyst comes out of the reaction with its mass unchanged.
- <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides the whole volume by the time for only half of it.

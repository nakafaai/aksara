# Compare the nitrogen per gram, let the bag supply its share, and then scale the pure urea up to the impure product

Urea is <InlineMath math="28\div60=46.7\%" /> nitrogen, ammonium nitrate <InlineMath math="28\div80=35.0\%" /> and ammonium sulfate <InlineMath math="28\div132=21.2\%" />, so **urea is richest**. The bag gives <InlineMath math="5000\times28\div80=1750\text{ g}" /> of nitrogen, because both the ammonium ion and the nitrate ion carry one nitrogen atom. The rest is <InlineMath math="2800-1750=1050\text{ g}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="1050\times60\div28=2250\text{ g}" /> of pure urea. **The product is only <InlineMath math="98\%" /> urea**, so <InlineMath math="2250\div0.98=2.30\text{ kg}" /> of it is needed.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Ammonium nitrate also has two nitrogen atoms, but its formula is heavier, <InlineMath math="80" /> against <InlineMath math="60" />, so its share is smaller.
- <InlineMath math="0.875\text{ kg}" /> counts only the ammonium ion's nitrogen, but **the nitrate ion carries a second**.
- <InlineMath math="2.20\text{ kg}" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="98\%" />, but an impure product means more of it is needed, so divide by <InlineMath math="0.98" />.

# Compare the nitrogen per gram of each fertiliser, then scale each by the nitrogen atoms it carries

Urea has <InlineMath math="M_r=60" /> with two nitrogen atoms, <InlineMath math="28\div60=46.7\%" />. Ammonium nitrate is <InlineMath math="28\div80=35.0\%" /> and ammonium sulfate <InlineMath math="28\div132=21.2\%" />, so **urea is richest in nitrogen**. The <InlineMath math="2800\text{ g}" /> of nitrogen is <InlineMath math="200\text{ mol}" /> of atoms, and **each formula unit of all three carries two**, so each needs <InlineMath math="100\text{ mol}" />: urea <InlineMath math="100\times60=6.00\text{ kg}" /> and ammonium sulfate <InlineMath math="100\times132=13.2\text{ kg}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Ammonium nitrate also has two nitrogen atoms, but its formula is heavier, <InlineMath math="80" /> against <InlineMath math="60" />, so its share is smaller.
- <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ kg}" /> gives urea one nitrogen atom, but **<InlineMath math="\text{CO(NH}_2)_2" /> has two**.
- <InlineMath math="26.4\text{ kg}" /> gives ammonium sulfate one nitrogen atom, but its two ammonium ions carry two.

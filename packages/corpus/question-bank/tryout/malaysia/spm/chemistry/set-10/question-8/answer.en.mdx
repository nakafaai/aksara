# The alkali picks out the amphoteric oxide, and the acid then has to dissolve both oxides at their own ratios

**Only aluminium oxide is amphoteric**, so the alkali dissolves it and leaves the <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" /> of magnesium oxide. The aluminium oxide is <InlineMath math="2.22-1.20=1.02\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="1.02\div102=0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and it takes <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of alkali, <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" />. In acid the magnesium oxide, <InlineMath math="1.20\div40=0.0300\text{ mol}" />, takes <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" />, and **each aluminium oxide takes six acid**, another <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" />, so the acid is <InlineMath math="0.120\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" /> takes the solid left as aluminium oxide, but the alkali dissolved the aluminium oxide and left the basic magnesium oxide.
- <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> uses one alkali per aluminium oxide, but the equation takes two.
- <InlineMath math="80.0\text{ cm}^3" /> gives aluminium oxide two acid like magnesium oxide, but **its formula has three oxide ions**, so it takes six.

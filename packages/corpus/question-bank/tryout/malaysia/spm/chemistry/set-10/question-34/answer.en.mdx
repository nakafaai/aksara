# The heat per mole of copper is fixed, so the same moles in twice the solution give half the rise

The solution takes in <InlineMath math="50.0\times4.2\times20.0=4200\text{ J}" /> from <InlineMath math="0.0500\times0.400=0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions, so <InlineMath math="\Delta H=-4200\div0.0200=-210\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, negative because heat is given out. The second run also has <InlineMath math="0.100\times0.200=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, so it releases the same <InlineMath math="4200\text{ J}" />, **but into <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> of solution**, a rise of <InlineMath math="4200\div(100\times4.2)=10.0^\circ\text{C}" />. It deposits the same <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper, <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20.0^\circ\text{C}" /> keeps the rise because the moles are the same, but **the same heat now warms twice the mass**.
- <InlineMath math="0.640\text{ g}" /> halves the copper with the concentration, but the volume doubled too, so the moles are the same.
- <InlineMath math="+210\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> gives the sign of a reaction that takes in heat, but the temperature rose.

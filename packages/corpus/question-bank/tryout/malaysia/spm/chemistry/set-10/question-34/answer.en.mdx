# The heat per mole of copper is fixed, and in the second run the zinc runs out first

The solution takes in <InlineMath math="50.0\times4.2\times20.0=4200\text{ J}" /> from <InlineMath math="0.0500\times0.400=0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions, so <InlineMath math="\Delta H=-4200\div0.0200=-210\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, negative because heat is given out. In the second run the copper(II) ions are <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> but the zinc is only <InlineMath math="0.650\div65=0.0100\text{ mol}" />, **so the zinc runs out**, displacing <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of copper, <InlineMath math="0.640\text{ g}" />. It releases <InlineMath math="0.0100\times210\,000=2100\text{ J}" /> into <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />, a rise of <InlineMath math="5.0^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.0^\circ\text{C}" /> lets all the copper(II) ions react, but only <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of zinc is there.
- <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> deposits copper for all the copper(II) ions, but **the zinc is used up after <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="+210\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> gives the sign of a reaction that takes in heat, but the temperature rose.

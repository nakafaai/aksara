# The lithium sets the hydroxide, the carbon dioxide runs out before the hydroxide, and the hydroxide left is titrated

The lithium is <InlineMath math="0.35\div7=0.0500\text{ mol}" />, and **two lithium give one hydrogen**, <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" />, along with <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of lithium hydroxide. The carbon dioxide is <InlineMath math="480\div24\,000=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, and **each takes two hydroxide**, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />, so the carbon dioxide runs out first. It makes <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of lithium carbonate, <InlineMath math="M_r=74" />, <InlineMath math="1.48\text{ g}" />, and leaves <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of hydroxide, which needs <InlineMath math="0.0100\div0.100=0.100\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of acid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen molecule per lithium atom, but **two lithium atoms make one molecule**.
- <InlineMath math="1.85\text{ g}" /> lets the hydroxide run out, but <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of it can take <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide and only <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> is there.
- <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}^3" /> neutralises all the hydroxide made, forgetting the part the carbon dioxide used.

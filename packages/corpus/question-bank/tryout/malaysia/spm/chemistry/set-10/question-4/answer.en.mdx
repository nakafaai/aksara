# The mass lost is carbon dioxide, which names the carbonate, and the oxide then uses up part of a measured excess of acid

The carbon dioxide is <InlineMath math="1.10\div44=0.0250\text{ mol}" />, and **one carbonate gives one carbon dioxide**, so the carbonate has <InlineMath math="M_r=2.10\div0.0250=84" /> and the metal <InlineMath math="A_r=84-60=24" />, magnesium. The gas is <InlineMath math="0.0250\times24\,000=600\text{ cm}^3" />. The oxide is <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" />, and **each oxide takes two acid**, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" /> added. The <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> left needs <InlineMath math="0.0100\div0.500=0.0200\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Calcium takes <InlineMath math="1.00\div0.0250=40" />, the <InlineMath math="M_r" /> of the oxide, as the metal's. **The oxide includes its oxygen**, so the metal is <InlineMath math="40-16=24" />.
- <InlineMath math="70.0\text{ cm}^3" /> reacts the oxide one to one with the acid, but each oxide takes two acid.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the acid added, forgetting the part the oxide used up.

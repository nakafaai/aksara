# Copper is below hydrogen and stays behind, while each aluminium atom gives one and a half hydrogen molecules

The sample holds <InlineMath math="4.75\text{ g}" /> of aluminium, <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ g}" /> of copper and <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ g}" /> of magnesium. **Copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series, so it does not react with the acid**. Aluminium gives <InlineMath math="2\text{Al}+6\text{HCl}\rightarrow2\text{AlCl}_3+3\text{H}_2" />, three hydrogen molecules for every two atoms, and magnesium gives one per atom.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.46\text{ dm}^3" /> lets the copper give hydrogen too. **Copper does not react with dilute acid**.
- <InlineMath math="4.27\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen molecule per aluminium atom, but each atom loses three electrons, enough for one and a half.
- <InlineMath math="6.67\text{ dm}^3" /> treats the whole <InlineMath math="5.00\text{ g}" /> as aluminium.

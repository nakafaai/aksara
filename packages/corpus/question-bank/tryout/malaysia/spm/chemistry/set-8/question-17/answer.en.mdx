# The same pH hides very different concentrations, and the alkali reacts with every acid molecule

pH <InlineMath math="2.0" /> means <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+]=0.010\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. For hydrochloric acid that is the whole acid. **Ethanoic acid gives that many ions from only <InlineMath math="1.0\%" /> of its molecules**, so its concentration is <InlineMath math="0.010\div0.010=1.0\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. As the alkali removes hydrogen ions, more ethanoic acid molecules ionise, so every molecule is neutralised in the end.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Equal volumes treat equal pH as equal acid. **The weak acid holds a hundred times more molecules**.
- This swaps the two acids: the weak acid is the more concentrated one.
- <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> multiplies by ten instead of dividing by the <InlineMath math="1.0\%" /> that is ionised.

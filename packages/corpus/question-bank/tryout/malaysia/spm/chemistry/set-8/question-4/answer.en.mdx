# Three gases share the molar mass, and only the one that burns to water fits

One mole fills <InlineMath math="24\text{ dm}^3" /> and weighs <InlineMath math="24\times1.83=44\text{ g}" />. **Carbon dioxide, dinitrogen monoxide and propane all have a molar mass of <InlineMath math="44" />**, so the mass alone cannot decide. Carbon dioxide does not burn, and dinitrogen monoxide contains no hydrogen to make water. Only propane burns to water: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_8+5\text{O}_2\rightarrow3\text{CO}_2+4\text{H}_2\text{O}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Carbon dioxide has the right molar mass but cannot burn. **The gas must burn and make water**.
- Dinitrogen monoxide has the right molar mass but no hydrogen, so it cannot give a liquid that turns copper(II) sulfate blue.
- <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ g}" /> gives one water molecule per propane molecule, but its eight hydrogen atoms make four.

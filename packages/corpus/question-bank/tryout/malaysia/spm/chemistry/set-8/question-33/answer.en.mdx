# Reversing the reaction flips the sign, and the equation's value is for two moles of water

Forming <InlineMath math="2\text{ mol}" /> of water releases <InlineMath math="572\text{ kJ}" />, so forming one mole releases <InlineMath math="286\text{ kJ}" />. **Splitting water is the reverse reaction, so it absorbs the same energy**, <InlineMath math="+286\text{ kJ}" /> per mole. The <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ mol}" />, which absorbs <InlineMath math="143\text{ kJ}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Splitting water is the reverse of burning hydrogen. **The heat that burning releases, splitting must absorb**.
- <InlineMath math="286\text{ kJ}" /> multiplies <InlineMath math="572" /> by <InlineMath math="0.50" /> without noticing that the value is for two moles of water.
- <InlineMath math="71.5\text{ kJ}" /> halves the value once more than the two moles in the equation allow.

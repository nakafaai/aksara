# Use the hydrate's molar mass, then three sulfate ions per unit, then the tenfold dilution

**The crystals carry nine water molecules, so their molar mass is <InlineMath math="400+162=562" />**. The <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0445\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0890\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ dm}^3" />. Each formula unit gives three sulfate ions, <InlineMath math="0.267\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Diluting <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" /> divides that by ten.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.0890\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> is the salt before dilution. **Each unit gives three sulfate ions, and the solution is then diluted tenfold**.
- <InlineMath math="0.0375\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> uses the anhydrous molar mass, <InlineMath math="400" />, and leaves out the water of crystallisation.
- <InlineMath math="0.00890\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> dilutes correctly but takes one sulfate ion per formula unit.

# The alcohol oxidises to the same acid only if both halves have the same number of carbon atoms

Oxidising an alcohol keeps its carbon chain, so **the alcohol and the acid must both have two carbon atoms**: ethanol and ethanoic acid. With four carbons in all, E is ethyl ethanoate, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5" />. The <InlineMath math="4.40\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, which gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of ethanoic acid directly and another <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> from the ethanol.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.00\text{ g}" /> counts only the acid from hydrolysis. **The oxidised alcohol adds the same amount again**.
- Methyl propanoate gives methanol, which oxidises to methanoic acid, not to propanoic acid.
- Propyl methanoate gives propan-1-ol, which oxidises to propanoic acid, not to methanoic acid.

# The alcohol must oxidise to the acid in the ester, so both halves have two carbons, then count the acid from both and take the portion

**Only an ester whose acid and alcohol have the same carbon chain fits**: ethanol oxidises to ethanoic acid, so E is ethyl ethanoate, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5" />. It is <InlineMath math="4.40\div88=0.0500\text{ mol}" />, and hydrolysis gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of ethanoic acid and <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol, **which oxidises to another <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of the acid**. The <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" /> holds <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> holds <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide, <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> counts only the acid from the hydrolysis. **The alcohol is oxidised to the same acid**, which doubles it.
- Methyl propanoate gives methanol, which oxidises to methanoic acid, not to propanoic acid.
- <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" /> neutralises all the acid in the <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />, but only <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of it is titrated.

# The oxygen in the compound is whatever mass the carbon and hydrogen leave

The carbon dioxide holds <InlineMath math="0.24\text{ g}" /> of carbon and the water <InlineMath math="0.04\text{ g}" /> of hydrogen. **The rest of the <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.32\text{ g}" />, must be oxygen from the compound**, since the burning oxygen ended up in the products too. The moles are <InlineMath math="0.02:0.04:0.02" />, so the empirical formula is <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2\text{O}" />, mass <InlineMath math="30" />, and the molecule holds two of these units.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2\text{O}" /> is the empirical formula. **Its mass is <InlineMath math="30" />, half the molecular mass**.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_8\text{O}" /> also has a molecular mass of <InlineMath math="60" />, but it needs three carbon atoms for every oxygen atom, not two for two.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4" /> leaves out the <InlineMath math="0.32\text{ g}" /> of oxygen that the compound itself contains.

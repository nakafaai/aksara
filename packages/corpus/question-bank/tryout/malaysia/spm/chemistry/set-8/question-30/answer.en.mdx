# The alcohol brings one oxygen atom of its own, so the gas supplies one fewer

The products hold <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide and <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol}" /> of water, so each molecule has three carbon and eight hydrogen atoms: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_7\text{OH}" />. The products contain <InlineMath math="0.600+0.400=1.00\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen atoms. **The alcohol supplied <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of them itself**, so the gas supplied <InlineMath math="0.900\text{ mol}" /> of atoms, <InlineMath math="0.450\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen molecules.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_8" /> has no hydroxyl group, but the compound is an alcohol. **It also brings its own oxygen**.
- <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ g}" /> supplies all the product oxygen from the gas and forgets the alcohol's own oxygen atom.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}" /> would give only <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, not <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mol}" />.

# With the acid every hydrogen carbonate ion gives its carbon dioxide, twice what heating alone frees

The sodium hydrogen carbonate is <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, and the monoprotic acid reacts one to one, so <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of cream of tartar, molar mass <InlineMath math="188" />, is needed. **With the acid, each hydrogen carbonate ion releases one carbon dioxide molecule**, <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />. Heating the soda alone, <InlineMath math="2\text{NaHCO}_3\rightarrow\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3+\text{CO}_2+\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, would free only half of that and leave a soapy taste of sodium carbonate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> is what heating the soda alone gives. **The acid frees the carbon dioxide from every hydrogen carbonate ion**.
- <InlineMath math="9.40\text{ g}" /> uses half as much acid, as if the acid had two acidic hydrogens, but it is monoprotic.
- <InlineMath math="37.6\text{ g}" /> uses two acid units per hydrogen carbonate, twice what the equation needs.

# The impure acid must supply the pure acid needed, and the gas fills more space in the hot oven

The soda is <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of pure cream of tartar, <InlineMath math="18.8\text{ g}" />, is needed. **Only <InlineMath math="95\%" /> of the powder is the acid**, so <InlineMath math="18.8\div0.95=19.8\text{ g}" /> of it must be used. The <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide would fill <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" /> at room conditions, but at <InlineMath math="180^\circ\text{C}" /> one mole fills <InlineMath math="37.2\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="18.8\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" /> treat the acid as pure and the gas as cool. **The powder is impure and the oven is hot**.
- <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" /> uses the molar volume at room conditions, but the dough rises at <InlineMath math="180^\circ\text{C}" />.
- <InlineMath math="17.9\text{ g}" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="0.95" /> instead of dividing, which supplies too little acid.

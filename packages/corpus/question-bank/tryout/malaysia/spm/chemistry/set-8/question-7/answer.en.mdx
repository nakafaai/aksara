# A gemstone carat is a unit of mass, not a fraction, so convert it to grams first

Unlike the carat of gold, which gives purity, **a gemstone carat is a mass of <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ g}" />**, so the diamond weighs <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ g}" />. That is <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of carbon atoms, <InlineMath math="1.51\times10^{22}" />. Each carbon atom becomes one carbon dioxide molecule, <InlineMath math="\text{C}+\text{O}_2\rightarrow\text{CO}_2" />, so the gas is also <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="7.53\times10^{22}" /> takes <InlineMath math="1.50" /> carats as <InlineMath math="1.50\text{ g}" />. **One carat is only <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ g}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide molecule for every two carbon atoms, but each carbon atom makes one.
- <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^{22}" /> counts the mass of one carat, <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ g}" />, but the diamond is <InlineMath math="1.50" /> carats.

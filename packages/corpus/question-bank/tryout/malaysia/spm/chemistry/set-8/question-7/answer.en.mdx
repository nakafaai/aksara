# Two hydroxide units take each carbon dioxide molecule, and the canister keeps the gas but loses the water

The crew breathes out <InlineMath math="21.0\text{ kg}" />, <InlineMath math="477\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide. Each molecule needs two units of lithium hydroxide, <InlineMath math="954\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="22.9\text{ kg}" />. **The canister takes in the carbon dioxide but gives off one water molecule for each**, so each mole adds only <InlineMath math="44-18=26\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="11.5\text{ kg}" /> uses one lithium hydroxide unit per carbon dioxide molecule. **Two are needed**.
- <InlineMath math="21.0\text{ kg}" /> counts all the carbon dioxide as gain and forgets the water that leaves.
- <InlineMath math="45.8\text{ kg}" /> doubles the lithium hydroxide a second time.

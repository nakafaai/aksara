# Only the hydrogen that removes oxygen counts, and the rest only flushes and protects

The oxide is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="\text{CuO}+\text{H}_2\rightarrow\text{Cu}+\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper. **Reduction needs only <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" />**. The rest flushes air out first, so that no explosive mixture forms, and then keeps air away from the hot copper so it is not oxidised again.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="100\%" /> counts all the hydrogen as reducing. **Most of it only flushes and protects the tube**.
- <InlineMath math="1.60\text{ g}" /> is the mass of the oxide, but removing the oxygen leaves less copper.
- <InlineMath math="20\%" /> takes two hydrogen molecules per oxide, but one removes each oxygen atom.

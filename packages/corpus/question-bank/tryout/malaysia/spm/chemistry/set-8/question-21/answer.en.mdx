# The ammonium nitrogen is oxidised and the nitrate nitrogen reduced, both to the same state

The salt holds nitrogen at <InlineMath math="-3" /> in <InlineMath math="\text{NH}_4^+" /> and at <InlineMath math="+5" /> in <InlineMath math="\text{NO}_3^-" />. In <InlineMath math="\text{N}_2\text{O}" /> both are <InlineMath math="+1" />. **So four electrons pass from the ammonium nitrogen to the nitrate nitrogen in each formula unit**, and <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> of salt transfers <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mol}" />. At room conditions the water is liquid, so only the <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> of dinitrogen monoxide is collected.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.80\text{ mol}" /> counts the four electrons twice, once as lost and once as gained. **They are the same four electrons**.
- <InlineMath math="7.20\text{ dm}^3" /> counts the water as a gas, but it condenses at room conditions.
- <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ mol}" /> gives a change of two for each nitrogen, but each one changes by four.

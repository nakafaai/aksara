# Carbonate finds only the acid, but hot alkali reacts with the acid and hydrolyses the ester too

The sample is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> in all, since both compounds have molar mass <InlineMath math="74" />. **Only the acid has an acidic hydrogen, so only it gives carbon dioxide**, two acid molecules per gas molecule: <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" /> of gas means <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of acid, half the sample. Boiling alkali neutralises the acid and also hydrolyses the ester, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_3\text{COOCH}_3+\text{NaOH}\rightarrow\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}+\text{CH}_3\text{OH}" />, so every molecule uses one hydroxide ion.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="25\%" /> takes one acid molecule per carbon dioxide molecule. **Two acid molecules give one**.
- <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> lets only the acid react with the alkali, but boiling alkali also hydrolyses the ester.
- <InlineMath math="100\%" /> lets the ester give carbon dioxide too, but it has no acidic hydrogen.

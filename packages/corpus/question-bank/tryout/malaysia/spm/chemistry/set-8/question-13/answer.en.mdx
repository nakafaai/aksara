# Two sodium ions balance one oxide ion, and each oxide ion becomes two hydroxide ions

X is sodium, which forms <InlineMath math="\text{Na}^+" />, and Y is oxygen, which forms <InlineMath math="\text{O}^{2-}" />, so the compound is <InlineMath math="\text{X}_2\text{Y}" />, sodium oxide. The <InlineMath math="3.10\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />. **In water each oxide ion takes a proton from a water molecule**, <InlineMath math="\text{Na}_2\text{O}+\text{H}_2\text{O}\rightarrow2\text{NaOH}" />, giving <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of hydroxide ions in <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> gives one hydroxide ion per formula unit. **Each formula unit gives two**.
- <InlineMath math="\text{XY}" /> gives sodium a charge of <InlineMath math="+2" />, but Group 1 atoms lose only one electron.
- <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> doubles the hydroxide a second time.

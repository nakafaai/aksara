# Use the molar volume at the vapour's temperature to name the compound, then follow the industrial equation

The vapour is <InlineMath math="0.300\div30.0=0.0100\text{ mol}" />, so the molar mass is <InlineMath math="154" />. **Carbon, with four valence electrons, shares one with each of four chlorine atoms**, and <InlineMath math="\text{CCl}_4" /> has mass <InlineMath math="154" />. It is a simple covalent molecule, which is why it is a non-conducting liquid. Making <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> takes three chlorine molecules each, <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" />, since the other two chlorine atoms leave in <InlineMath math="\text{S}_2\text{Cl}_2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="960\text{ cm}^3" /> uses four chlorine molecules per molecule of product. **The equation from carbon disulfide uses three**.
- <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_3" /> comes from using <InlineMath math="24\text{ dm}^3" />, the molar volume at room conditions, for the vapour at <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" />.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> uses one chlorine molecule per molecule of product.

# Use the molar volume at the vapour's own temperature, then match the formula to the electrons

The vapour is <InlineMath math="0.300\div30.0=0.0100\text{ mol}" />, so the molar mass is <InlineMath math="154" />. **X, with four valence electrons, shares one with each of four Y atoms**, and <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_4" /> has a mass of <InlineMath math="12+4\times35.5=154" />. It is made of simple covalent molecules with no ions, which is why it does not conduct. Its molecules are non-polar, so it does not mix with water.

# Why the other options are wrong

- An ionic compound would be a solid that conducts when molten. **This liquid does not conduct, so it is covalent**.
- <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_3" /> comes from using <InlineMath math="24\text{ dm}^3" />, the molar volume at room conditions, but the vapour is at <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" />.
- The formula and bonding are right, but the non-polar molecules do not dissolve in water.

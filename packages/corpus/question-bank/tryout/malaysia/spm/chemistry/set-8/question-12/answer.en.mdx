# Name the compound from its vapour, balance the equation, then scale the reactants up for the yield

X is carbon and Y chlorine. The vapour is <InlineMath math="300\div30\,000=0.0100\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="M_r=1.54\div0.0100=154" />, which is <InlineMath math="\text{CCl}_4" />, <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_4" />. Balanced, <InlineMath math="\text{CS}_2+3\text{Cl}_2\rightarrow\text{CCl}_4+\text{S}_2\text{Cl}_2" />. **At <InlineMath math="75\%" /> yield the reaction must be set up for more than <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />**: <InlineMath math="0.0100\div0.75=0.0133\text{ mol}" />. That needs three times as much chlorine, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="960\text{ cm}^3" />, and <InlineMath math="0.0133\text{ mol}" /> of carbon disulfide, <InlineMath math="0.0133\times76=1.01\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="720\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="0.760\text{ g}" /> ignore the yield. **Only <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of the expected compound forms**, so more reactants are needed.
- <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_3" /> does not fit <InlineMath math="M_r=154" />, and carbon forms four bonds with chlorine.
- <InlineMath math="540\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="0.570\text{ g}" /> multiply by <InlineMath math="75\%" /> instead of dividing, which is less than even a full yield would need.

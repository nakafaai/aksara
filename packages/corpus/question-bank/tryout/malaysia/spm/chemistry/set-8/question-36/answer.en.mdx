# Count the hydrogen from the magnesium, read the heat for two moles, then pass on only the share that reaches the water

Magnesium is <InlineMath math="2.40\div24=0.100\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{MgCl}_2+\text{H}_2" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen. **The <InlineMath math="572\text{ kJ}" /> is for two moles of hydrogen**, so burning <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> releases <InlineMath math="28.6\text{ kJ}" />, and <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of it, <InlineMath math="21.45\text{ kJ}" />, reaches the water. The water warms by <InlineMath math="21\,450\div(500\times4.2)=10.2^\circ\text{C}" />. The burning uses half as many moles of oxygen as of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20.4^\circ\text{C}" /> uses <InlineMath math="572\text{ kJ}" /> for each mole of hydrogen. **The equation burns two moles**.
- <InlineMath math="13.6^\circ\text{C}" /> gives all the heat to the water, but only <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of it reaches the water.
- <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" /> takes one mole of oxygen for each mole of hydrogen, but the equation needs only half as much.

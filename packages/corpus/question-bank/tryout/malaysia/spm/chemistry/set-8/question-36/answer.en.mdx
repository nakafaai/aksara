# Moles of hydrogen give the energy, half of it drives the car, then divide by the energy per kilometre

The tank holds <InlineMath math="4000\div2=2000\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen molecules, since **each molecule has two atoms, molar mass <InlineMath math="2" />**. They release <InlineMath math="2000\times286=572\,000\text{ kJ}" />, <InlineMath math="572\text{ MJ}" />, and half of that, <InlineMath math="286\text{ MJ}" />, drives the car. At <InlineMath math="0.70\text{ MJ}" /> per kilometre that is <InlineMath math="409\text{ km}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="817\text{ km}" /> uses all the energy. **Only <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of it drives the car**.
- <InlineMath math="204\text{ km}" /> takes the molar mass of hydrogen as <InlineMath math="4" />, or applies the efficiency twice.
- <InlineMath math="817\,000\text{ km}" /> divides kilojoules by megajoules without converting the units.

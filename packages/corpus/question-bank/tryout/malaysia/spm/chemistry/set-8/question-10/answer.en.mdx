# Only the basic oxide reacts with the acid, and the acidic oxide is left for the alkali

Across Period <InlineMath math="3" /> the oxides change from basic to acidic. **Magnesium oxide is basic and dissolves in the acid, but silicon dioxide is acidic and does not**. The acid is <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="\text{MgO}+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{MgCl}_2+\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> uses it on <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium oxide, <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ g}" />. The residue is the <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ g}" /> of silicon dioxide, <InlineMath math="0.00667\text{ mol}" />, which needs twice that amount of hydroxide.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.33\text{ cm}^3" /> uses one hydroxide ion per silicon dioxide unit. **The equation needs two**.
- <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ g}" /> is the magnesium oxide, which dissolved in the acid, not the residue.
- Silicon dioxide is an acidic oxide, so it does not dissolve in the acid and a residue remains.

# An interval's rate uses the change over that interval, and the final volume counts the magnesium

Between <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" /> the volume rises from <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="65\text{ cm}^3" />, so **the average rate is <InlineMath math="25\div30=0.83\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />**. The volume stops at <InlineMath math="88\text{ cm}^3" /> because the acid is in excess and the magnesium is used up. That is <InlineMath math="0.00367\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, and each magnesium atom gives one hydrogen molecule.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.08\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides the total at <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" /> by <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" />. **The interval starts at <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="0.176\text{ g}" /> takes two magnesium atoms per hydrogen molecule, but each atom gives one molecule.
- <InlineMath math="0.59\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> is the average over the whole <InlineMath math="150\text{ s}" />, not the interval asked for.

# The total follows the moles of hydrogen ions, and the start follows their concentration

With zinc in excess, the hydrogen depends on the hydrogen ions available. I holds <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mol}" /> and II also <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mol}" />, so both give <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" />. **Sulfuric acid gives two hydrogen ions per molecule**, so III holds <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> and gives <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" />. The start depends on concentration: II has half the hydrogen ion concentration of I, so it is slower, and III has twice, so it is faster.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" /> for II halves the hydrogen with the concentration. **II has twice the volume, so the same moles of acid**.
- Equal starts ignore concentration: II is more dilute and III more concentrated in hydrogen ions than I.
- <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" /> for III treats sulfuric acid as monoprotic, but it gives two hydrogen ions per molecule.

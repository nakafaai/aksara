# Weigh the litre to find the salt, follow the dilution into the portion, then let each chloride ion take one silver ion

**One cubic decimetre of seawater weighs <InlineMath math="1030\text{ g}" />, not <InlineMath math="1000\text{ g}" />**, so it holds <InlineMath math="0.035\times1030=36.1\text{ g}" /> of salt, <InlineMath math="0.616\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> sample holds <InlineMath math="0.0154\text{ mol}" /> of chloride ions, spread through <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />, **so the <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> portion has only <InlineMath math="20.0\div250" /> of them**, <InlineMath math="0.001232\text{ mol}" />. <InlineMath math="\text{Ag}^++\text{Cl}^-\rightarrow\text{AgCl}" /> is one to one, so the titration needs <InlineMath math="0.001232\text{ mol}" /> of silver ions, <InlineMath math="30.8\text{ cm}^3" />, and forms <InlineMath math="0.001232\times143.5=0.177\text{ g}" /> of silver chloride.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="29.9\text{ cm}^3" /> takes a litre of seawater as <InlineMath math="1000\text{ g}" />. **Its density is <InlineMath math="1.03\text{ g cm}^{-3}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="61.6\text{ cm}^3" /> counts the sodium ions too, but only the chloride ions react with silver ions.
- <InlineMath math="385\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates the whole <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> sample, but only <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of the diluted solution is used.

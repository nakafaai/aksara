# Count the moles each fuel needs for the heat, then the carbon atoms each mole carries

The trip needs <InlineMath math="50\,000\div1370=36.5\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol, <InlineMath math="1.68\text{ kg}" />, or <InlineMath math="50\,000\div5470=9.14\text{ mol}" /> of octane, <InlineMath math="1.04\text{ kg}" />. **Each ethanol molecule gives two carbon dioxide molecules and each octane molecule eight**, so ethanol gives <InlineMath math="73.0\text{ mol}" /> and octane <InlineMath math="73.1\text{ mol}" />. Octane gives about four times the heat per molecule and four times the carbon, so the carbon dioxide is almost the same, about <InlineMath math="3.2\text{ kg}" /> each.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Each fuel's mass is right, so the slip is elsewhere. **Each ethanol molecule has two carbon atoms and gives two carbon dioxide molecules**, about <InlineMath math="3.2\text{ kg}" />, not <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ kg}" />.
- The masses are swapped: ethanol releases less heat per mole, so more of it is needed.
- <InlineMath math="0.84\text{ kg}" /> of ethanol halves the moles needed, which would supply only half the heat.

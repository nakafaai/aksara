# Scale up for the engine's efficiency, turn moles into mass, then divide by the density

**The fuel must release four times the work**, <InlineMath math="12\,500\div0.25=50\,000\text{ kJ}" />. That is <InlineMath math="50\,000\div1370=36.5\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol, <InlineMath math="1.68\text{ kg}" />, or <InlineMath math="50\,000\div5470=9.14\text{ mol}" /> of octane, <InlineMath math="1.04\text{ kg}" />. **The question asks for volumes**, so dividing by the densities gives <InlineMath math="2.13\text{ dm}^3" /> of ethanol and <InlineMath math="1.49\text{ dm}^3" /> of octane. Ethanol has two carbon atoms and octane eight, so each gives about <InlineMath math="73\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="3.2\text{ kg}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.53\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="0.37\text{ dm}^3" /> use <InlineMath math="12\,500\text{ kJ}" /> as the heat. **Only a quarter of the heat becomes work**, so the fuel must release <InlineMath math="50\,000\text{ kJ}" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.33\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="0.73\text{ dm}^3" /> multiply the mass by the density instead of dividing by it.
- <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ kg}" /> for ethanol counts one carbon atom per molecule, but ethanol has two.

# Compare per kilojoule, not per mole, because octane gives four times the heat per molecule

For <InlineMath math="1000\text{ kJ}" /> the car burns <InlineMath math="0.730\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol or <InlineMath math="0.183\text{ mol}" /> of octane. **Octane gives four times the carbon dioxide per molecule but also about four times the heat**, so the two ratios almost cancel. Ethanol makes <InlineMath math="1.46\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide and octane <InlineMath math="1.46\text{ mol}" />, about <InlineMath math="64\text{ g}" /> each.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="88\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="352\text{ g}" /> compare one mole of each fuel. **The question asks per kilojoule**.
- <InlineMath math="32\text{ g}" /> for ethanol gives it one carbon dioxide molecule per molecule, but its two carbon atoms make two.
- <InlineMath math="16\text{ g}" /> for octane divides by eight instead of multiplying, but each octane molecule makes eight carbon dioxide molecules.

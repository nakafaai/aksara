# The acid's first job is to neutralise the ammonia, one acid molecule for each ammonia molecule

Ammonia keeps the rubber particles negatively charged so they repel each other. **Added acid reacts with the ammonia first**, and only the acid added after that neutralises the charges and coagulates the rubber. The latex holds <InlineMath math="0.0034\times10\,000=34\text{ g}" /> of ammonia, <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mol}" />, which uses <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mol}" /> of ethanoic acid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60\text{ g}" /> uses one acid molecule for two ammonia molecules. **The equation pairs them one to one**.
- <InlineMath math="34\text{ g}" /> is the mass of the ammonia, not of the acid that neutralises it.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ g}" /> uses two acid molecules per ammonia molecule, as for a diprotic acid.

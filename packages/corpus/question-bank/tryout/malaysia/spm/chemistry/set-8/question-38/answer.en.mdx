# Neutralise the ammonia first, add the extra acid for coagulation, then turn the total into a volume

The ammonia is <InlineMath math="0.0034\times10\,000=34\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol}" /> of acid, <InlineMath math="120\text{ g}" />, neutralises it and forms <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol}" /> of ammonium ethanoate, <InlineMath math="2.00\times77=154\text{ g}" />. **Coagulation needs another <InlineMath math="0.50\times10=5.0\text{ g}" />**, so the acid totals <InlineMath math="125\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="125\div60=2.08\text{ mol}" />. **The question asks for the volume of solution**, <InlineMath math="2.08\div2.00=1.04\text{ dm}^3" />. The extra acid finds no ammonia left, so it forms no more salt.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ dm}^3" /> covers only the ammonia. **The rubber needs a further <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ g}" /> of acid to coagulate**.
- <InlineMath math="160\text{ g}" /> lets the extra acid form salt too, but all the ammonia is already used.
- <InlineMath math="2.08\text{ dm}^3" /> is the amount of acid in moles, not the volume of the <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> solution.

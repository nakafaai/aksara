# The acid's first job is to neutralise the ammonia, and only the acid after that coagulates the rubber

Ammonia keeps the rubber particles negatively charged so they repel each other. The latex holds <InlineMath math="34\text{ g}" /> of ammonia, <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mol}" />, **which uses <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mol}" /> of acid, <InlineMath math="120\text{ g}" />, before any coagulation**. Coagulation then takes <InlineMath math="0.50\times10.0=5.0\text{ g}" /> more. The neutralisation forms <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mol}" /> of ammonium ethanoate, molar mass <InlineMath math="77" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="120\text{ g}" /> is only the acid that neutralises the ammonia. **Coagulation needs <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ g}" /> more**.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ g}" /> of salt uses the acid's molar mass for ammonium ethanoate, but the salt also carries the ammonia's mass.
- <InlineMath math="65\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="77\text{ g}" /> use one acid molecule for two ammonia molecules.

# Turn the pH into a concentration, then let each carbonate take two hydrogen ions

pH <InlineMath math="4.0" /> means <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+]=10^{-4}\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, so the lake holds <InlineMath math="5.0\times10^9\times10^{-4}=5.0\times10^5\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen ions. **Each carbonate ion neutralises two hydrogen ions**, so <InlineMath math="2.5\times10^5\text{ mol}" /> of limestone is enough, <InlineMath math="2.5\times10^7\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="50" /> tonnes uses one carbonate for each hydrogen ion. **Each carbonate takes two**.
- <InlineMath math="12.5" /> tonnes halves the limestone a second time.
- <InlineMath math="250" /> tonnes reads pH <InlineMath math="4.0" /> as <InlineMath math="10^{-3}\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, ten times too acidic.

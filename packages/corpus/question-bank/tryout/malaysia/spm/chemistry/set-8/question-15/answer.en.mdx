# Turn the pH into hydrogen ions, halve them for the carbonate, then allow for the impurity

pH <InlineMath math="4.0" /> means <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+]=1.0\times10^{-4}\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, so the lake holds <InlineMath math="5.0\times10^5\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen ions. **Each calcium carbonate takes two hydrogen ions**, so <InlineMath math="2.5\times10^5\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="25" /> tonnes of calcium carbonate, is needed. **The limestone is only <InlineMath math="90\%" /> calcium carbonate**, so <InlineMath math="25\div0.90=27.8" /> tonnes must be added. The carbon dioxide is <InlineMath math="2.5\times10^5\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="6.0\times10^6\text{ dm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="6000\text{ m}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="55.6" /> tonnes takes one calcium carbonate per hydrogen ion. **The equation needs two hydrogen ions for each**.
- <InlineMath math="22.5" /> tonnes multiplies by <InlineMath math="90\%" /> instead of dividing, so it supplies too little calcium carbonate.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m}^3" /> divides the cubic decimetres by a million, but one cubic metre is only <InlineMath math="1000\text{ dm}^3" />.

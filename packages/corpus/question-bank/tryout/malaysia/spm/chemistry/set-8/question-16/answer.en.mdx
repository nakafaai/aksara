# The magnesium is in excess, so the hydrogen counts the acid, two acid molecules per hydrogen molecule

The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />. **In <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{MgCl}_2+\text{H}_2" /> two acid molecules give one hydrogen molecule**, so the acid was <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. It ionises completely, so <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+]=0.50" /> and the pH is <InlineMath math="-\log0.50=0.30" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.25\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> takes one acid molecule per hydrogen molecule. **Two are needed**.
- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> doubles the acid a second time.
- <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> is the amount of acid in moles, not its concentration in <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ dm}^3" />.

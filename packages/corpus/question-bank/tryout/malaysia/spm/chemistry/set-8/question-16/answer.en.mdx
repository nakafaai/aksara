# The hydrogen counts the diluted acid at two molecules each, and the dilution factor takes it back to the stock

The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, and **two acid molecules give each hydrogen molecule**, so the <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> held <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Its pH is <InlineMath math="-\log0.50=0.30" />. The stock was diluted from <InlineMath math="25.0" /> to <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />, ten times, so it was <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> takes one acid molecule per hydrogen molecule. **Two are needed**.
- <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> is the diluted acid, not the concentrated acid it came from.
- pH <InlineMath math="-0.70" /> belongs to the concentrated acid, but the question asks for the diluted one.

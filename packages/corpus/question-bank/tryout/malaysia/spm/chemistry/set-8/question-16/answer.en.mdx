# Weigh a litre of the concentrated acid, dilute it twentyfold, then halve the acid for the hydrogen

**A cubic decimetre of the concentrated acid weighs <InlineMath math="1180\text{ g}" />, not <InlineMath math="1000\text{ g}" />**, and <InlineMath math="36.5\%" /> of it is <InlineMath math="430.7\text{ g}" /> of hydrogen chloride, <InlineMath math="11.8\text{ mol}" />, so the acid is <InlineMath math="11.8\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Diluting <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}^3" /> divides it by twenty, giving <InlineMath math="0.590\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{pH}=-\log0.590=0.23" />. The <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> portion holds <InlineMath math="0.0118\text{ mol}" /> of acid. **<InlineMath math="\text{Mg}+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{MgCl}_2+\text{H}_2" /> gives one hydrogen molecule for two acid molecules**, <InlineMath math="0.00590\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="142\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="283\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen molecule for each acid molecule. **Two acid molecules give one hydrogen molecule**.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> and pH <InlineMath math="0.30" /> take a cubic decimetre of the acid as <InlineMath math="1000\text{ g}" />, but its density is <InlineMath math="1.18\text{ g cm}^{-3}" />.
- pH <InlineMath math="-1.07" /> is the pH of the concentrated acid, not of the diluted acid.

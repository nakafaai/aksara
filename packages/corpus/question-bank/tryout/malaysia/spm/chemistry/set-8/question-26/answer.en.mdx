# Turn the charge into electrons, share them between the electrodes, then follow the sodium through the water into the titration

The charge is <InlineMath math="5.00\times965=4825\text{ C}" />, <InlineMath math="4825\div96\,500=0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. **Each sodium ion takes one electron, but each chlorine molecule needs two**, so the cell gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of sodium and <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine, <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" />. <InlineMath math="2\text{Na}+2\text{H}_2\text{O}\rightarrow2\text{NaOH}+\text{H}_2" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" />, and <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide. **Sulfuric acid neutralises two hydroxide ions per molecule**, so <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of it is needed, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> of chlorine gives one chlorine molecule per electron. **Each chlorine molecule needs two electrons**.
- <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of acid takes one sulfuric acid molecule for each sodium hydroxide, but each molecule neutralises two.
- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen gives one hydrogen molecule per sodium atom, but two sodium atoms give one.

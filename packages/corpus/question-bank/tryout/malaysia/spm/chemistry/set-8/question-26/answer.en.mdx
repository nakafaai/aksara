# Each chlorine molecule takes one electron from each of two iron(II) ions

The solution holds <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mol}" /> of iron(II) ions. Each loses one electron, and **each chlorine molecule gains two electrons**, so it oxidises two iron(II) ions. Only <InlineMath math="0.025\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine is needed. Iron(II) solutions are pale green and iron(III) solutions yellowish brown.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> uses one chlorine molecule per iron(II) ion. **One molecule oxidises two ions**.
- This reverses the colours: the solution starts pale green, from iron(II), and ends yellowish brown.
- <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" /> halves the chlorine a second time.

# One electron per sodium atom, two per chlorine molecule, and two sodium atoms per hydrogen molecule

The charge is <InlineMath math="4825\text{ C}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. Each sodium ion takes one, giving <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of sodium. **Two chloride ions give up two electrons to form one chlorine molecule**, so only <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine forms. In water <InlineMath math="2\text{Na}+2\text{H}_2\text{O}\rightarrow2\text{NaOH}+\text{H}_2" />, so the sodium gives <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> of chlorine makes one molecule per electron. **Each molecule needs two**.
- <InlineMath math="2.30\text{ g}" /> gives two sodium atoms per electron, but each sodium ion takes one.
- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen gives one hydrogen molecule per sodium atom, but two atoms make one.

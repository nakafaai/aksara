# Dehydration loses water, and addition polymerisation keeps every gram of ethene

The ethanol is <InlineMath math="1000\text{ mol}" />, and **each molecule loses a water molecule when it becomes ethene**, so the mass falls from <InlineMath math="46" /> to <InlineMath math="28" /> per mole. With an <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield, <InlineMath math="800\text{ mol}" /> of ethene forms, <InlineMath math="22.4\text{ kg}" />. Addition polymerisation joins the molecules without losing any atoms, so the polymer has the same mass as the ethene.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="36.8\text{ kg}" /> applies the yield to the ethanol's mass. **Each molecule loses water, so the mass per mole falls**.
- <InlineMath math="28.0\text{ kg}" /> ignores the <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield of the dehydration.
- <InlineMath math="17.9\text{ kg}" /> applies the yield a second time to the polymerisation, which loses nothing.

# Balance the electrons: each manganate(VII) takes five and each sulfur dioxide gives two

The manganate(VII) is <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^{-5}\text{ mol}" />. **Manganese falls from <InlineMath math="+7" /> to <InlineMath math="+2" />, taking five electrons, while sulfur rises from <InlineMath math="+4" /> to <InlineMath math="+6" />, giving two**, so five sulfur dioxide molecules react with every two manganate(VII) ions. That is <InlineMath math="2.50\times10^{-5}\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ cm}^3" /> of gas in <InlineMath math="10\,000\text{ cm}^3" /> of air, <InlineMath math="60" /> parts per million.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^{-5}\text{ mol}" /> reacts one to one. **The electrons balance at five sulfur dioxide to two manganate(VII)**.
- <InlineMath math="600" /> ppm divides the gas by <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" /> instead of the <InlineMath math="10\,000\text{ cm}^3" /> of air sampled.
- <InlineMath math="4.00\times10^{-6}\text{ mol}" /> inverts the ratio, two sulfur dioxide to five manganate(VII).

# Turn the volume of ethanol into moles with its density, then one oxygen molecule each, drawn from air

The wine holds <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> of ethanol, which **weighs <InlineMath math="60\times0.79=47.4\text{ g}" />, not <InlineMath math="60\text{ g}" />**, <InlineMath math="1.03\text{ mol}" />. Each ethanol molecule becomes one ethanoic acid molecule, <InlineMath math="1.03\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ dm}^3" />. It takes one oxygen molecule, not the three of burning, so <InlineMath math="24.7\text{ dm}^3" /> of oxygen is used, and that is only <InlineMath math="21\%" /> of the air.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="24.7\text{ dm}^3" /> is the oxygen itself. **Air is only <InlineMath math="21\%" /> oxygen**.
- <InlineMath math="2.61\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> takes <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> of ethanol as <InlineMath math="60\text{ g}" /> and forgets its density.
- <InlineMath math="353\text{ dm}^3" /> supplies three oxygen molecules per ethanol molecule, as for burning it to carbon dioxide.

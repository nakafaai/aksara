# Turning ethanol into ethanoic acid takes one oxygen molecule, far less than burning it

The ethanol is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />. **This oxidation only swaps two hydrogen atoms for one oxygen atom on the end carbon**, using one oxygen molecule per ethanol molecule, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />. Burning ethanol completely would need three. Each ethanol molecule becomes one ethanoic acid molecule, <InlineMath math="0.0500\times60=3.00\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ dm}^3" /> burns the ethanol to carbon dioxide and water. **Turning it into the acid needs one oxygen molecule, not three**.
- <InlineMath math="2.30\text{ g}" /> keeps the ethanol's mass, but the acid is heavier, <InlineMath math="60" /> per mole instead of <InlineMath math="46" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ dm}^3" /> uses one oxygen molecule for every two ethanol molecules, half of what the equation shows.

# The first half of the gas makes the precipitate, and the second half dissolves it again

The limewater holds <InlineMath math="0.0010\text{ mol}" /> of calcium hydroxide, and the gas is <InlineMath math="0.0020\text{ mol}" />. The first <InlineMath math="0.0010\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide makes <InlineMath math="0.0010\text{ mol}" /> of calcium carbonate, <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ g}" />. **The remaining <InlineMath math="0.0010\text{ mol}" /> is exactly enough to turn all of it into soluble calcium hydrogen carbonate**, so the solution clears completely.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ g}" /> stops after the first reaction. **The extra carbon dioxide dissolves the precipitate again**.
- <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ g}" /> makes carbonate from all the carbon dioxide, but there is only enough calcium for half of it.
- <InlineMath math="0.05\text{ g}" /> leaves half the precipitate, but the second reaction has enough gas to dissolve all of it.

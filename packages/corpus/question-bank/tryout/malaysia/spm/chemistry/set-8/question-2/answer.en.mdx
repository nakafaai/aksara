# Weight the masses by abundance, then take magnesium-26's share of the atoms

**The relative atomic mass is the abundance-weighted mean, not the plain mean of the three mass numbers**: <InlineMath math="0.79\times24+0.10\times25+0.11\times26=24.32" />. The sample is then <InlineMath math="2.432\div24.32=0.1000\text{ mol}" /> of atoms, <InlineMath math="6.02\times10^{22}" />, and only <InlineMath math="11\%" /> of them are magnesium-26. Burning changes nothing in the nucleus, so the count is the same in the oxide.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="25.00" /> averages the three mass numbers equally. **Magnesium-24 is far more common**.
- <InlineMath math="2.01\times10^{22}" /> gives each isotope a third of the atoms instead of using the abundances.
- <InlineMath math="6.02\times10^{22}" /> counts every magnesium atom, not only the magnesium-26.

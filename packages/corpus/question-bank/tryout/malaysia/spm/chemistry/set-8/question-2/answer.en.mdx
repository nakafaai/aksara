# Weight the masses by abundance, use that mass to count the oxide, then weigh magnesium-26's share of the atoms

**The relative atomic mass is the abundance-weighted mean**: <InlineMath math="0.79\times24+0.10\times25+0.11\times26=24.32" />, so magnesium oxide has <InlineMath math="M_r=40.32" />. The oxide is <InlineMath math="4.032\div40.32=0.1000\text{ mol}" />, and each formula unit holds one magnesium atom, so the ribbon was <InlineMath math="0.1000\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="2.432\text{ g}" />. **The <InlineMath math="11\%" /> counts atoms, not mass**, so magnesium-26 is <InlineMath math="0.0110\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0110\times26=0.286\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.459\text{ g}" /> averages the three mass numbers equally, as if each isotope were a third of the atoms. **Magnesium-24 is far more common**.
- <InlineMath math="0.268\text{ g}" /> takes <InlineMath math="11\%" /> of the ribbon's mass, but the abundance counts atoms, and a magnesium-26 atom is heavier than the average.
- <InlineMath math="4.032\text{ g}" /> takes the mass of the oxide as the magnesium and forgets the oxygen in it.

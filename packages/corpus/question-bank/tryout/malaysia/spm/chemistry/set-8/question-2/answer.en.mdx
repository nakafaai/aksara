# Convert every temperature to kelvin before comparing it with the table

Add <InlineMath math="273" /> to each Celsius temperature: <InlineMath math="27^\circ\text{C}" /> is <InlineMath math="300\text{ K}" />, <InlineMath math="127^\circ\text{C}" /> is <InlineMath math="400\text{ K}" /> and <InlineMath math="-200^\circ\text{C}" /> is <InlineMath math="73\text{ K}" />. **An element is a gas above its boiling point and a liquid between its melting and boiling points**. At <InlineMath math="300\text{ K}" /> only P and S are above their boiling points. R at <InlineMath math="400\text{ K}" /> and S at <InlineMath math="73\text{ K}" /> both lie between the two points, so both are liquids.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Comparing the Celsius numbers directly with the kelvin values makes every element look solid. **Convert to kelvin first**.
- <InlineMath math="73\text{ K}" /> is above S's melting point, <InlineMath math="54\text{ K}" />, so S has melted and is a liquid, not a solid.
- Q boils at <InlineMath math="332\text{ K}" />, so at <InlineMath math="300\text{ K}" /> it is still a liquid.

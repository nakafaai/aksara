# A change of colour is not proof of redox, so check the oxidation numbers

In <InlineMath math="\text{CrO}_4^{2-}" /> chromium is <InlineMath math="x-8=-2" />, so <InlineMath math="+6" />. In <InlineMath math="\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}" /> it is <InlineMath math="2x-14=-2" />, so <InlineMath math="+6" /> again. **No oxidation number changes, so the reaction is not redox**, only an acid-base change that joins two chromate ions. Two chromate ions give one dichromate ion, so <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ mol}" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Chromium reaches <InlineMath math="+3" /> only when dichromate oxidises something and turns green. **Here it stays at <InlineMath math="+6" />**.
- Chromium cannot go above <InlineMath math="+6" />, and the oxidation numbers here do not change at all.
- The reaction is not redox, but two chromate ions join to make one dichromate ion, so the amount halves.

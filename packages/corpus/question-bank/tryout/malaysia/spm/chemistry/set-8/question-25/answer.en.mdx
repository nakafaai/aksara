# Go from the aluminium to the tarnish by the equation, then see which atoms actually leave the spoon

The aluminium is <InlineMath math="0.054\div27=0.00200\text{ mol}" />. **The equation takes three silver sulfide for every two aluminium**, so the tarnish was <InlineMath math="0.00300\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.00300\times248=0.744\text{ g}" />. It becomes <InlineMath math="0.00600\text{ mol}" /> of silver, <InlineMath math="0.648\text{ g}" />, which stays on the spoon. **Only the sulfur leaves the spoon**, <InlineMath math="0.00300\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.096\text{ g}" />, so the spoon becomes <InlineMath math="0.096\text{ g}" /> lighter.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.331\text{ g}" /> inverts the ratio and takes two silver sulfide for every three aluminium. **The equation has three silver sulfide for two aluminium**.
- An increase of <InlineMath math="0.648\text{ g}" /> treats the silver as new metal added to the spoon, but it was already there in the tarnish.
- A decrease of <InlineMath math="0.744\text{ g}" /> removes the whole tarnish, but its silver stays on the spoon.

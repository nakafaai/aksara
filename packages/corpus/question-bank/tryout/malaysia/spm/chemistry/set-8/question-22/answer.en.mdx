# Two electrons make one chlorine molecule, and each chlorine molecule makes one unit of bleach

The charge is <InlineMath math="5.00\times3600=18\,000\text{ C}" />, or <InlineMath math="0.187\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. **At the anode <InlineMath math="2\text{Cl}^-\rightarrow\text{Cl}_2+2e^-" />, so two electrons give one chlorine molecule**, <InlineMath math="0.0933\text{ mol}" />. Each chlorine molecule then gives one <InlineMath math="\text{NaOCl}" />, so the bleach is also <InlineMath math="0.0933\text{ mol}" />, with molar mass <InlineMath math="74.5" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="13.9\text{ g}" /> makes one chlorine molecule per electron. **Each molecule needs two**.
- <InlineMath math="3.47\text{ g}" /> makes one unit of bleach from two chlorine molecules, but the equation uses one.
- <InlineMath math="6.62\text{ g}" /> is the mass of the chlorine, not of the sodium chlorate(I) it becomes.

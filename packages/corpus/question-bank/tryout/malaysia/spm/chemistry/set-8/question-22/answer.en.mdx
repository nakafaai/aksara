# Every electron taken at the anode leaves a hydrogen ion behind, so the solution turns acidic

The charge is <InlineMath math="0.50\times1930=965\text{ C}" />, or <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. Each silver ion takes one electron, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of silver, <InlineMath math="1.08\text{ g}" />, is deposited. **At the anode one hydrogen ion is left in the solution for every electron released**, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen ions build up in <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ dm}^3" />: the solution becomes nitric acid of <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- pH <InlineMath math="7.0" /> assumes the solution stays neutral. **The anode reaction leaves hydrogen ions behind**.
- <InlineMath math="0.54\text{ g}" /> gives each silver ion two electrons, but silver ions carry a charge of <InlineMath math="+1" />.
- pH <InlineMath math="0.70" /> counts two hydrogen ions per electron, but the half-equation gives four for four.

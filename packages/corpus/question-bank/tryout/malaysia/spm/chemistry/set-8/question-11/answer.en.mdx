# Water ionises the covalent molecules into an acid, methylbenzene does not, and then the acid limits the hydrogen

In methylbenzene hydrogen chloride stays as covalent molecules, with no hydrogen ions, so magnesium does not react. **In water each molecule ionises**, giving <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> of hydrogen ions, pH <InlineMath math="1.0" />. The magnesium, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, would need <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of acid, so the <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of acid runs out first and gives <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> lets all the magnesium react. **The acid runs out first**.
- Methylbenzene leaves hydrogen chloride as molecules, so there are no hydrogen ions to react with magnesium.
- pH <InlineMath math="2.0" /> spreads the acid through <InlineMath math="1\text{ dm}^3" />, but the solution is <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />.

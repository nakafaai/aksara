# Water pulls the covalent molecules apart into ions, but methylbenzene leaves them as molecules

In methylbenzene hydrogen chloride stays as covalent molecules, so there are no free ions and no current. **In water each molecule ionises completely**, <InlineMath math="\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{H}^++\text{Cl}^-" />, so the solution conducts and is acidic. The <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ dm}^3" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> of hydrogen ions, which is pH <InlineMath math="1.0" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Hydrogen chloride is covalent as a gas, but water ionises it. **The solution in water conducts and is acidic**.
- Methylbenzene does not ionise hydrogen chloride, so that solution has no free ions and does not conduct.
- pH <InlineMath math="2.0" /> spreads the acid through <InlineMath math="1\text{ dm}^3" />, but the solution is only <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />.

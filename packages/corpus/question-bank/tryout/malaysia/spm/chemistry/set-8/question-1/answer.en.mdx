# Wait for constant mass to find the anhydrous salt, then follow its sulfate into the precipitate

**Heating is finished only when the mass stops changing**, so the last two readings give the anhydrous salt, <InlineMath math="21.35-20.15=1.20\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />. The water lost is <InlineMath math="22.61-21.35=1.26\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0700\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="x=7" />. In solution each <InlineMath math="\text{MgSO}_4" /> gives one sulfate ion, and <InlineMath math="\text{Ba}^{2+}+\text{SO}_4^{2-}\rightarrow\text{BaSO}_4" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of barium sulfate forms, <InlineMath math="0.0100\times233=2.33\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="x=6" /> and <InlineMath math="2.54\text{ g}" /> use the first heating, before all the water had gone. **The mass is still falling after it**.
- <InlineMath math="2.08\text{ g}" /> is the mass of barium chloride, but the precipitate is barium sulfate.
- <InlineMath math="x=14" /> counts the hydrogen atoms in the water instead of the water molecules.

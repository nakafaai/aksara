# Only a constant mass shows that all the water has gone, and the mole ratio then gives x

The mass keeps falling until two readings agree, so **only the matching last readings, <InlineMath math="21.35\text{ g}" />, show that all the water has gone**. The anhydrous salt is <InlineMath math="21.35-20.15=1.20\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{MgSO}_4" />. The water lost is <InlineMath math="22.61-21.35=1.26\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0700\text{ mol}" />, seven water molecules for each formula unit.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The first heating leaves water behind, since the mass falls further. **Constant mass is reached only when two readings agree**.
- Taking the water lost from the first heating mixes two readings: the salt is right, but the water is too small.
- <InlineMath math="x=14" /> divides the water by half the salt, but the salt is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, not <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />.

# Find which reactant runs out in each experiment, because the powder changes only the speed

The chips are <InlineMath math="1.00\div100=0.0100\text{ mol}" />. In I the acid is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="\text{CaCO}_3+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{CaCl}_2+\text{CO}_2+\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> needs two acid for each carbonate, **so the acid runs out after <InlineMath math="0.0050\text{ mol}" /> of carbonate**, leaving <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ g}" /> and giving <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. The average rate in the first minute is <InlineMath math="80\div1=80\text{ cm}^3\text{ min}^{-1}" />. In II the acid is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, **exactly enough for all <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of carbonate**, so it gives <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />. Powdering the carbonate makes II faster but does not change the total.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0\text{ g}" /> lets all the chips react in I. **Two acid molecules are needed for each carbonate, so the acid runs out first**.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> for II treats the powder as the only change, but II also has twice the acid.
- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide for each acid molecule, but two acid molecules give one.

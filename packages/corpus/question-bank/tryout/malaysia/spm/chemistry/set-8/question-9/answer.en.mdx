# Each chlorine molecule frees two bromide ions, and the bromine keeps their whole mass

Chlorine is above bromine in Group 17, so it displaces it: <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2+2\text{Br}^-\rightarrow2\text{Cl}^-+\text{Br}_2" />. The cubic metre holds <InlineMath math="65.0\text{ g}" /> of bromide, <InlineMath math="0.8125\text{ mol}" />. **One chlorine molecule takes two bromide ions**, so only <InlineMath math="0.406\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine is needed. All the bromide ends up as bromine, so its mass stays <InlineMath math="65.0\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="57.7\text{ g}" /> uses one chlorine molecule per bromide ion. **Each molecule takes two**.
- <InlineMath math="130\text{ g}" /> doubles the bromine, but the bromine atoms only come from the <InlineMath math="65.0\text{ g}" /> of bromide.
- <InlineMath math="14.4\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="32.5\text{ g}" /> halve both amounts a second time.

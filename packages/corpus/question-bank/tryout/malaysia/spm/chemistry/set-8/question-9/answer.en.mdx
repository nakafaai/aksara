# Halve the bromide ions to get the chlorine, then double the chlorine to get the electrons the cell must pass

The seawater holds <InlineMath math="65.0\text{ g}" /> of bromide ions, <InlineMath math="65.0\div80=0.8125\text{ mol}" />. **Each chlorine molecule takes two bromide ions**, so <InlineMath math="0.406\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine is needed, and <InlineMath math="0.406\text{ mol}" /> of bromine, <InlineMath math="65.0\text{ g}" />, forms. At the anode <InlineMath math="2\text{Cl}^-\rightarrow\text{Cl}_2+2e^-" />, so the chlorine needs <InlineMath math="0.8125\text{ mol}" /> of electrons, <InlineMath math="0.8125\times96\,500=78\,400\text{ C}" />. At <InlineMath math="500\text{ A}" /> that takes <InlineMath math="157\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="314\text{ s}" /> makes one chlorine molecule for each bromide ion. **Each chlorine molecule displaces two bromide ions**.
- <InlineMath math="130\text{ g}" /> counts one bromine molecule for each bromide ion, but two ions make one molecule.
- <InlineMath math="78.4\text{ s}" /> counts one electron per chlorine molecule, and <InlineMath math="32.5\text{ g}" /> halves the bromine as well.

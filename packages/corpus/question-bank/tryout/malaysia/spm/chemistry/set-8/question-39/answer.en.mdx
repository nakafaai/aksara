# Let the iodine count the ascorbic acid, take it away from the sodium hydroxide, then give the rest to citric acid three at a time

The iodine is <InlineMath math="0.0100\times0.0250=2.50\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, and one iodine takes one ascorbic acid. **The portion is a tenth of the solution**, so the tablet holds <InlineMath math="2.50\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.440\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="88\%" /> of <InlineMath math="500\text{ mg}" />. The sodium hydroxide is <InlineMath math="4.50\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, of which <InlineMath math="2.50\times10^{-4}" /> goes to the ascorbic acid, leaving <InlineMath math="2.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" /> for citric acid. **Each citric acid takes three hydroxide ions**, so the portion holds <InlineMath math="6.67\times10^{-5}\text{ mol}" />, and the tablet ten times that, <InlineMath math="6.67\times10^{-4}\times192=0.128\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="44\%" /> takes two ascorbic acid for each iodine. **The equation is one to one**.
- <InlineMath math="0.384\text{ g}" /> treats citric acid as monoprotic, but each molecule neutralises three hydroxide ions.
- <InlineMath math="0.0128\text{ g}" /> is the citric acid in one portion, but the portion is only a tenth of the tablet.

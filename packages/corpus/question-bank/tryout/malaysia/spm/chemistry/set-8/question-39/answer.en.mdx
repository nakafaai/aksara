# One iodine molecule per ascorbic acid molecule gives the true mass, which is then compared in the same units

The iodine is <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" />, and **each iodine molecule reacts with one ascorbic acid molecule**, so the tablet holds <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" />. With molar mass <InlineMath math="176" /> that is <InlineMath math="0.440\text{ g}" />, or <InlineMath math="440\text{ mg}" />, against <InlineMath math="500\text{ mg}" /> on the label. Some of the vitamin may have been oxidised by air during storage.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="44\%" /> takes two ascorbic acid molecules per iodine molecule. **The equation pairs them one to one**.
- <InlineMath math="176\%" /> takes two iodine molecules per ascorbic acid molecule.
- <InlineMath math="8.8\%" /> compares <InlineMath math="0.440\text{ g}" /> with <InlineMath math="5.00\text{ g}" />, but <InlineMath math="500\text{ mg}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ g}" />.

# Hotter particles collide harder and more often

The cross disappears when the same amount of sulfur has formed, so **rate is proportional to <InlineMath math="\dfrac{1}{\text{time}}" />**.

- Rate at <InlineMath math="30^\circ\text{C}" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{1}{60} = 0.017\text{ s}^{-1}" />.
- Rate at <InlineMath math="50^\circ\text{C}" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{1}{20} = 0.050\text{ s}^{-1}" />, three times as fast.

Heating gives the particles more kinetic energy. They collide more often, and a larger share of collisions have at least the activation energy, so the frequency of effective collisions rises sharply.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The volumes and concentrations are the same in every run. **Temperature changes the energy of particles, not how many there are**.
- Temperature leaves the activation energy unchanged. A catalyst lowers it.
- Every run makes the same amount of sulfur, which is why the cross is a fair end point.

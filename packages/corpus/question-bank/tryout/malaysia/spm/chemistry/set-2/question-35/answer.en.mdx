# Use the heat the water gained, then divide by the moles burnt

The heat of combustion is the heat released when **<InlineMath math="1" /> mol of fuel** burns completely. The experiment can only measure the heat that reached the water.

- Heat gained by water: <InlineMath math="200 \times 4.2 \times 12.0 = 10080\text{ J}" />.
- Moles of ethanol, <InlineMath math="M_r = 46" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.46}{46} = 0.010\text{ mol}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\Delta H = -\dfrac{10080}{0.010} = -1008000\text{ J mol}^{-1} = -1008\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

The answer is smaller than the data book value because heat escapes to the air and the can, and some ethanol burns incompletely.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-1370\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> is the data book value, **not what this experiment measures**.
- <InlineMath math="-504\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by <InlineMath math="0.020" /> mol, doubling the moles of ethanol.
- <InlineMath math="-10.1\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> is the heat in kJ before dividing by the moles of ethanol.

# Compare the heat the fuel gives with the heat the water gets

The data book value tells you how much heat the burnt propan-1-ol released. The temperature rise tells you how much of it the water actually took in. **The percentage is the heat absorbed over the heat released**, so the smaller number goes on top.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{C}_3\text{H}_7\text{OH})&=\frac{0.90}{60}=0.015\text{ mol}\\
\text{heat released}&=0.015\times2016=30.24\text{ kJ}\\
\text{heat absorbed}&=250\times4.2\times24.0=25\,200\text{ J}=25.2\text{ kJ}\\
\text{percentage}&=\frac{25.2}{30.24}\times100\%=83.3\%
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="83.3\%" /> of the heat reaches the water, and the rest is lost to the can and the surrounding air.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="63.9\%" /> uses <InlineMath math="46" />, the molar mass of ethanol. Propan-1-ol is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_7\text{OH}" />, so its molar mass is <InlineMath math="60" />.
- <InlineMath math="120\%" /> divides the heat released by the heat absorbed. **No more than all of the heat can reach the water, so the percentage must be under <InlineMath math="100\%" />**.
- <InlineMath math="1.25\%" /> compares the water's <InlineMath math="25.2\text{ kJ}" /> with the heat of a whole mole, <InlineMath math="2016\text{ kJ}" />, instead of the <InlineMath math="0.015\text{ mol}" /> that burnt.

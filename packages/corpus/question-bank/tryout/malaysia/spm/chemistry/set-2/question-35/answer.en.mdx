# Find what runs out in each experiment, then divide its heat by the mass of solution it warms

In Experiment I the zinc is in excess, so the <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions all react, <InlineMath math="4.20\text{ kJ}" />, warming <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4200}{50\times4.2}=20.0^\circ\text{C}" />. In Experiment II the copper(II) ions are again <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, but the zinc is only <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, so **the zinc runs out**, <InlineMath math="2.10\text{ kJ}" />, and **that heat is spread through <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />**, <InlineMath math="5.00^\circ\text{C}" />. The copper is <InlineMath math="0.0100\times64=0.640\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.0^\circ\text{C}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> let all the copper(II) ions in Experiment II react, but **only <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of zinc is there**.
- <InlineMath math="20.0^\circ\text{C}" /> for Experiment II keeps the first rise, but less heat now warms twice the mass.
- <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> counts copper from the copper(II) ions, but the zinc runs out first.

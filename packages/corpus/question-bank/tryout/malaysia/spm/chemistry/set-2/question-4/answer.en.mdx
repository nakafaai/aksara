# What the heating leaves and releases

Copper(II) nitrate has <InlineMath math="M_r=187.5" />, so <InlineMath math="3.75\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. The equation turns two nitrates into two oxides, four nitrogen dioxide and **one oxygen**, so <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of nitrate leaves <InlineMath math="1.59\text{ g}" /> of oxide and releases <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of nitrogen dioxide plus <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen. Counting the brown gas alone gives <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ dm}^3" /> and loses the oxygen.

# The alkali runs out mid-stream

The alkali holds <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide. Each four of them take four nitrogen dioxide and one oxygen, so it absorbs <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.00625\text{ mol}" /> and then **stops absorbing anything**. Both gases leak through after that, not only the oxygen.

If the alkali took only nitrogen dioxide, <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ dm}^3" /> would escape, and if it took everything, none would.

# Trace each element to its product, then find oxygen by difference

All the carbon ends up in carbon dioxide and all the hydrogen in water. Oxygen also comes from the air, so **the oxygen in P must be found by difference**, never from the products.

- Carbon: <InlineMath math="0.44 \times \dfrac{12}{44} = 0.12\text{ g}" />.
- Hydrogen: <InlineMath math="0.18 \times \dfrac{2}{18} = 0.02\text{ g}" />.
- Oxygen: <InlineMath math="0.30 - 0.12 - 0.02 = 0.16\text{ g}" />.

Moles: C <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.12}{12} = 0.01" />, H <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.02}{1} = 0.02" />, O <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.16}{16} = 0.01" />. The ratio <InlineMath math="1 : 2 : 1" /> gives the empirical formula <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2\text{O}" />, with <InlineMath math="M_r = 30" />. Since <InlineMath math="\dfrac{60}{30} = 2" />, the molecular formula is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{O}_2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2\text{O}" /> is the empirical formula: it **stops one step early** and ignores the relative molecular mass.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2" /> takes one hydrogen atom per water molecule instead of two, which also leaves the mass at <InlineMath math="58" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}_3" /> multiplies the empirical formula by <InlineMath math="3" />, which gives a mass of <InlineMath math="90" />, not <InlineMath math="60" />.

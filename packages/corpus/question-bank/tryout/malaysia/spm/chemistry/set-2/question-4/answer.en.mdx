# The nitrate sets the oxide and both gases, and the alkali runs out, so it absorbs only part of each gas

The nitrate is <InlineMath math="3.75\div187.5=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, which leaves <InlineMath math="0.0200\times79.5=1.59\text{ g}" /> of oxide and gives <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of nitrogen dioxide and <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" />. The alkali is <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" />, **less than the <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> the nitrogen dioxide needs**, so it absorbs <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of nitrogen dioxide and **a quarter as much oxygen**, <InlineMath math="0.00625\text{ mol}" />. Escaping are <InlineMath math="0.0150" /> and <InlineMath math="0.00375\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.01875\times24=0.450\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="0.360\text{ dm}^3" /> leave out the oxygen, but the equation gives one oxygen for every four nitrogen dioxide.
- <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ dm}^3" /> lets all the oxygen escape, but **the alkali takes one oxygen with every four nitrogen dioxide it absorbs**.
- <InlineMath math="0\text{ dm}^3" /> assumes the alkali absorbs everything, but <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of it is less than the <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of nitrogen dioxide.

# Find the formula from the mole ratio of M to O

An empirical formula is **the simplest whole-number ratio of moles of atoms**, so turn each mass into moles before comparing.

Take the first point: <InlineMath math="1.12\text{ g}" /> of M forms <InlineMath math="1.60\text{ g}" /> of oxide.

- Mass of oxygen that combined: <InlineMath math="1.60 - 1.12 = 0.48\text{ g}" />.
- Moles of M: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{1.12}{56} = 0.020\text{ mol}" />.
- Moles of O: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.48}{16} = 0.030\text{ mol}" />.
- Ratio M : O is <InlineMath math="0.020 : 0.030 = 2 : 3" />.

Every other point gives the same ratio, since the oxide mass is always <InlineMath math="\dfrac{1.60}{1.12}" /> times the metal mass. So the empirical formula is <InlineMath math="\text{M}_2\text{O}_3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{MO}" /> needs a ratio of <InlineMath math="1 : 1" />, which would give only <InlineMath math="1.12 + 0.32 = 1.44\text{ g}" /> of oxide.
- <InlineMath math="\text{M}_3\text{O}_4" /> is a ratio of <InlineMath math="3 : 4" />, close but not the measured <InlineMath math="2 : 3" />. It would give <InlineMath math="1.12 + 0.43 = 1.55\text{ g}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{MO}_2" /> comes from **comparing masses instead of moles**: it would give <InlineMath math="1.12 + 0.64 = 1.76\text{ g}" />.

# The water cools, so the dissolving takes heat in

The heat that breaks up the solid comes out of the water, so the temperature falls. **Taking heat in makes the change endothermic, so its sign is positive**. Work out the heat from the temperature fall, then share it over the moles dissolved.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta\theta&=28.0-24.6=3.4^\circ\text{C}\\
Q&=50\times4.2\times3.4=714\text{ J}=0.714\text{ kJ}\\
n(\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3)&=\frac{2.0}{80}=0.025\text{ mol}\\
\Delta H&=+\frac{0.714}{0.025}=+28.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

On the energy level diagram, the dissolved ions sit above the solid and water by that amount, because they now hold the heat the water lost.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-28.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> reads the fall in temperature as heat given out. The water lost heat to the dissolving, so the reaction gained it.
- <InlineMath math="+0.714\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> is the heat for <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ g}" />, not for one mole. **Divide by <InlineMath math="0.025\text{ mol}" /> to get the value per mole**.
- A positive <InlineMath math="\Delta H" /> with the reactants on top contradicts itself. Above the products would mean energy was released.

# Reading the lamp and the thermometer

The lamp lost <InlineMath math="186.42-185.73=0.690\text{ g}" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol, and the water climbed from <InlineMath math="28.0^\circ\text{C}" /> to <InlineMath math="43.0^\circ\text{C}" />. **The water took in <InlineMath math="15.8\text{ kJ}" />**, so this set-up measures <InlineMath math="-1050\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
Q_1&=250\times4.2\times15.0=15\,750\text{ J},\quad n=\tfrac{0.690}{46}=0.0150\text{ mol},\quad \Delta H=-\tfrac{15.75}{0.0150}=-1050\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

Dividing the heat by the <InlineMath math="0.690\text{ g}" /> itself gives <InlineMath math="-22.8" />, a figure per gram that cannot be a molar heat of combustion.

# Scaling up to the second heating

Experiment II asks the water for <InlineMath math="42.0\text{ kJ}" />. **With the same losses, each mole of ethanol still delivers only <InlineMath math="1050\text{ kJ}" /> to the water**, so the lamp burns <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
Q_2&=500\times4.2\times20.0=42\,000\text{ J},\quad m=\tfrac{42.0}{1050}\times46=1.84\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

Using the accepted <InlineMath math="1370\text{ kJ}" /> per mole quietly assumes a lamp with no losses and gives <InlineMath math="1.41\text{ g}" />. Warming only <InlineMath math="250\text{ g}" /> by <InlineMath math="20.0^\circ\text{C}" /> needs half the heat, which is where <InlineMath math="0.920\text{ g}" /> comes from.

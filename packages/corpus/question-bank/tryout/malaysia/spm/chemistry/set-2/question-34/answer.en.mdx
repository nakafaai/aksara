# The water's heat over the moles burned gives the measured value, and the accepted value shows how much heat was lost

The water absorbs <InlineMath math="250\times4.2\times15.0=15\,750\text{ J}" />, <InlineMath math="15.8\text{ kJ}" />. The ethanol is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.690}{46}=0.0150\text{ mol}" />, so **per mole** the experiment gives <InlineMath math="15.75\div0.0150=1050" />, <InlineMath math="\Delta H=-1050\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. Burning <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> really releases <InlineMath math="0.0150\times1370=20.55\text{ kJ}" />, so **only <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1050}{1370}\times100=76.6\%" /> reaches the water**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="130\%" /> divides the accepted value by the measured one, but more heat cannot reach the water than the burning releases.
- <InlineMath math="-22.8" /> gives the heat per gram, but **a heat of combustion is per mole of fuel**.
- <InlineMath math="23.4\%" /> is the share lost to the surroundings, not the share that reaches the water.

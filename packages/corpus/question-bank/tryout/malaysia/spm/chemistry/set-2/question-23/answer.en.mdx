# Dark blue means iron(II) ions, pink means hydroxide ions

Potassium hexacyanoferrate(III) turns dark blue with <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}^{2+}" /> ions, so **dark blue shows the iron is rusting**. Phenolphthalein turns pink with <InlineMath math="\text{OH}^-" /> ions, which form where oxygen gains electrons.

- I is correct: with X, much dark blue forms, so iron gives up electrons faster. X must be less electropositive than iron.
- II is correct: with Y there is no blue, so iron does not lose electrons. Y gives them up instead, so Y is more electropositive.
- III is correct: Y loses electrons, <InlineMath math="\text{Y} \rightarrow \text{Y}^{n+} + ne^-" />, so Y is oxidised.
- IV is wrong: in the first tube iron is the more electropositive metal, so iron is oxidised, not X.

So I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only leaves out III, the oxidation of Y that protects the iron.
- II and IV only accepts IV, which reverses the roles in the first tube.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and accepts IV. **The metal that rusts in place of iron is always the more electropositive one**.

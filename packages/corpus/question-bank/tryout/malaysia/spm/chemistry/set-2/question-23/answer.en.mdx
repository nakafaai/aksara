# The magnesium runs out, each magnesium reduces two silver ions, and a tenth of the silver ions left is titrated

The magnesium is <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> and the silver ions <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />, and **each magnesium reduces two silver ions**, <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" />, so the magnesium runs out. The silver is <InlineMath math="0.0300\times108=3.24\text{ g}" />, and each silver ion takes one electron, <InlineMath math="0.0300\times6.02\times10^{23}=1.81\times10^{22}" />. **The <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of silver ions left** puts <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" /> in a portion, <InlineMath math="0.00100\div0.0200=0.0500\text{ dm}^3=50.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.62\text{ g}" /> frees one silver per magnesium, but **each magnesium gives two electrons and reduces two silver ions**.
- <InlineMath math="9.03\times10^{21}" /> counts one electron per magnesium atom, but each magnesium gives two.
- <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the silver ions put in, but <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of them became silver metal.

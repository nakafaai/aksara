# Two ammonia take one sulfuric acid, so the ammonia runs out, and each sulfuric acid left needs two hydroxide ions

The ammonia is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3.40}{17}=0.200\text{ mol}" /> and the acid <InlineMath math="0.120\text{ mol}" />, and **two ammonia take one acid**, so the ammonia runs out, giving <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of salt, <InlineMath math="0.100\times132=13.2\text{ g}" />. **Each formula unit holds two nitrogen atoms**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{28}{132}\times100=21.2\%" />. The <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of acid left has two acidic hydrogens each, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of alkali, <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="15.8\text{ g}" /> lets the acid decide, but <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of ammonia can take only <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of it.
- <InlineMath math="10.6\%" /> counts one nitrogen atom, but **the formula has two ammonium ions**.
- <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> neutralises each sulfuric acid with one hydroxide, but it has two acidic hydrogens.

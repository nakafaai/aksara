# Hard water ions grab the soap anions

Hard water contains <InlineMath math="\text{Ca}^{2+}" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" /> ions. **Soap anions combine with them to form an insoluble scum**, so the soap is used up before it can lather.

The soap's foam drops from <InlineMath math="6.0" /> to <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ cm}" /> in hard water. Detergent anions, such as alkyl sulfates, form soluble salts with calcium and magnesium ions, so the detergent still lathers at <InlineMath math="5.8\text{ cm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Where the detergent comes from does not explain the result. **What matters is whether its calcium salt dissolves**.
- Soap solutions are slightly alkaline, not acidic.
- Hardness comes from calcium and magnesium ions, not from hydrogen ions.

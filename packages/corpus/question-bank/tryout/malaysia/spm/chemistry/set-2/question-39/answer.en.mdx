# Ammonia takes only part of the acid

The ammonia is <InlineMath math="3.40\div17=0.200\text{ mol}" />, and **two ammonia molecules share one sulfuric acid**, so they use <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.120\text{ mol}" /> and make <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of ammonium sulfate, <InlineMath math="M_r=132" />, or <InlineMath math="13.2\text{ g}" />. A mass of <InlineMath math="15.8\text{ g}" /> turns every sulfuric acid into salt, and that would take <InlineMath math="0.240\text{ mol}" /> of ammonia. In that picture nothing is left to titrate, hence <InlineMath math="0\text{ cm}^3" />.

## Nitrogen in the fertiliser

Each formula unit, <InlineMath math="(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4" />, has **two nitrogen atoms**, <InlineMath math="28" /> out of <InlineMath math="132" />, which is <InlineMath math="21.2\%" />. With one nitrogen the share drops to <InlineMath math="10.6\%" />.

# What the sodium hydroxide meets

After the ammonia, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of sulfuric acid is left. **The alkali reacts with hydrogen ions, not with acid molecules**, and each sulfuric acid brings two, as the chart shows. So the titration needs <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide, <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of the <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> solution. Matching acid and alkali one for one gives <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

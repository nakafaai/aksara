# Count the repeating units, then follow each atom: two carbons to carbon dioxide, one hydrogen to hydrogen chloride, and two to water

Each unit has <InlineMath math="M_r=62.5" />, so there are <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of units. **Each unit holds two carbon atoms**, <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" />. **Its chlorine takes one hydrogen as hydrogen chloride**, <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, so a portion holds <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali. The other two hydrogens make one water, <InlineMath math="0.100\times18=1.80\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per unit, but each unit holds two carbon atoms.
- <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" /> turns all three hydrogen atoms into hydrogen chloride, but **each unit has only one chlorine atom to take a hydrogen**.
- <InlineMath math="2.70\text{ g}" /> turns all three hydrogen atoms into water, but one of them leaves as hydrogen chloride.

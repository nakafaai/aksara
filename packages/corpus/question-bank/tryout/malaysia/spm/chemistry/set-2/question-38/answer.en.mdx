# Carbon and hydrogen from two kinds of unit

Polyethene, <InlineMath math="M_r=28" /> per unit, is also <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of units. **Both units hold two carbon atoms**, so <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of units burn to <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="9.60\text{ dm}^3" />. One carbon dioxide per unit would give <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" />.

The hydrogen needs more care. A polyethene unit has four hydrogen atoms and makes two water molecules. A PVC unit has three, but one leaves with the chlorine, so it makes only one water. That totals <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="5.40\text{ g}" />. Sending all three PVC hydrogens into water gives <InlineMath math="6.30\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="1.80\text{ g}" /> is the PVC's water alone.

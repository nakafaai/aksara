# Find the tube where one reactant runs out, then compare moles there

The precipitate stops growing **as soon as the iodide ions are used up**. Read where that happens, then compare the moles of the two ions at that point.

- The height stops rising at tube <InlineMath math="2" />, so <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of the metal nitrate exactly uses up the iodide.
- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{M}^{x+}" />: <InlineMath math="0.50 \times \dfrac{2.0}{1000} = 0.0010\text{ mol}" />.
- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{I}^-" />: <InlineMath math="0.50 \times \dfrac{6.0}{1000} = 0.0030\text{ mol}" />.
- Ratio <InlineMath math="\text{M} : \text{I} = 1 : 3" />, so the precipitate is <InlineMath math="\text{MI}_3" />, and to balance three <InlineMath math="\text{I}^-" /> the ion must be <InlineMath math="\text{M}^{3+}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{MI}" /> would need <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ cm}^3" />, and the height would rise up to tube <InlineMath math="6" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{MI}_2" /> would need <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ cm}^3" />, **reading the second tube's height as only part of the way**.
- <InlineMath math="\text{M}_2\text{I}_3" /> has a <InlineMath math="2 : 3" /> ratio, which would need <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

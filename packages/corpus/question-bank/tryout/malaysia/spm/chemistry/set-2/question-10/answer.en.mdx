# The back titration finds the ammonium salt, the rest of the mass is sodium chloride, and both salts give chloride to the silver

The alkali added is <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> and the acid shows <InlineMath math="0.00750\text{ mol}" /> left, so **<InlineMath math="0.0175\text{ mol}" /> reacted with the ammonium chloride**, <InlineMath math="0.0175\times53.5=0.936\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="61.5\%" /> of the mixture, and it gave <InlineMath math="0.0175\times24\,000=420\text{ cm}^3" /> of ammonia. **The other <InlineMath math="0.585\text{ g}" /> is sodium chloride**, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />. Both salts carry chloride, <InlineMath math="0.0275\text{ mol}" /> in all, <InlineMath math="0.0275\times143.5=3.95\text{ g}" /> of silver chloride.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="26.4\%" /> takes the alkali the acid titrated as the alkali the ammonium used, but **the acid measures what was left over**.
- <InlineMath math="840\text{ cm}^3" /> gives two ammonia per ammonium, but each ammonium ion gives one.
- <InlineMath math="2.51\text{ g}" /> precipitates only the ammonium salt's chloride, but the sodium chloride's chloride precipitates too.

# The back titration finds the alkali the ammonium used, which fixes its mass, its share of the sample and its ammonia

The alkali added is <InlineMath math="0.0500\times0.500=0.0250\text{ mol}" /> and the acid shows <InlineMath math="0.0150\times0.500=0.00750\text{ mol}" /> left, so **<InlineMath math="0.0175\text{ mol}" /> reacted with the ammonium chloride**. That is <InlineMath math="0.0175\times53.5=0.936\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.936\div1.07=87.5\%" /> of the sample. Each ammonium gives one ammonia, <InlineMath math="0.0175\times24\,000=420\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.00750\text{ mol}" /> takes the alkali the acid titrated as the alkali the ammonium used, but **the acid measures what was left over**.
- <InlineMath math="840\text{ cm}^3" /> gives two ammonia per ammonium, but each ammonium ion gives one.
- <InlineMath math="27.8\%" /> uses the mass of the ammonia, <InlineMath math="0.298\text{ g}" />, instead of the ammonium chloride it came from.

The zinc is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> and the copper(II) ions <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, so **the copper(II) ions run out**. The solid is <InlineMath math="0.640\text{ g}" /> of copper plus <InlineMath math="0.650\text{ g}" /> of zinc left over, <InlineMath math="1.29\text{ g}" />. **Only that <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of zinc reaches the acid**, <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen, using <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of acid. # Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> let all the zinc reach the acid, but **half of it was used by the copper(II) ions**.
- <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> lets the zinc decide, but only <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions is there, so zinc is left over.
- <InlineMath math="0.640\text{ g}" /> weighs only the copper, but the zinc left over is filtered off with it.

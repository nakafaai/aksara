# Copper ions run out before the zinc does

The zinc is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> but the copper(II) ions only <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, so **half the zinc never finds a copper(II) ion**. The filter paper catches <InlineMath math="0.640\text{ g}" /> of copper mixed with <InlineMath math="0.650\text{ g}" /> of unused zinc, <InlineMath math="1.29\text{ g}" /> in all. Pure copper of <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> would need every zinc atom to displace copper, twice the copper(II) ions in the solution.

Watch the total line. Each zinc atom that leaves is replaced by a copper atom only one mass unit lighter, so **the balance barely moves in the first stage** even though half the zinc has reacted.

# The acid picks out the zinc

Copper sits below hydrogen in the reactivity series, so **the acid dissolves only the leftover zinc**. That is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of zinc against <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of acid, plenty, and one hydrogen comes off per zinc, <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />. Making hydrogen from all <InlineMath math="1.30\text{ g}" /> gives <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> and forgets the zinc already spent on copper.

When the fizzing stops, <InlineMath math="0.640\text{ g}" /> of copper is still lying in the acid. A final <InlineMath math="0\text{ g}" /> would mean copper dissolving in dilute hydrochloric acid, which it does not.

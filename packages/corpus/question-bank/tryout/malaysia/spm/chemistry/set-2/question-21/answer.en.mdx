# A bigger voltage means the metals are further apart in the series

The further apart two metals are in the electrochemical series, **the larger the voltage of their cell**. The more electropositive metal gives away electrons and becomes the negative terminal.

Copper is the positive terminal each time, so every metal is more electropositive than copper. Ranked by voltage, the order from most to least electropositive is P, R, S, Q, then copper.

In a cell of P and R, P is higher in the series, so it is the negative terminal. The voltage is roughly the difference of their voltages against copper: <InlineMath math="2.7 - 1.1 = 1.6\text{ V}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Q gives the smallest voltage, so it is **the least electropositive of the four**, just above copper.
- S is below R in the series, so S cannot displace R. Only a more electropositive metal displaces another.
- Copper is the positive terminal against Q too, so Q is more electropositive than copper.

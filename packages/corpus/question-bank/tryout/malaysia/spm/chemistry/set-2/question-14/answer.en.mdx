# A strong acid ionises fully, a weak acid only partly

pH measures the **concentration of hydrogen ions**, not the concentration of the acid. Two acids at the same concentration can give very different amounts of <InlineMath math="\text{H}^+" />.

/>

- Hydrogen chloride ionises completely: <InlineMath math="\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{H}^+ + \text{Cl}^-" />. At <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] = 0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, so the pH is <InlineMath math="1.00" />.
- Ethanoic acid ionises only partly: <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COO}^- + \text{H}^+" />. Its pH of <InlineMath math="2.87" /> means <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] \approx 0.0013\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, about <InlineMath math="1.3\%" /> of the molecules.

The lower pH of hydrochloric acid comes from its higher hydrogen ion concentration.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **Only the hydrogen in the <InlineMath math="\text{COOH}" /> group ionises**, so counting hydrogen atoms in the molecule tells you nothing about strength.
- The chart compares the two acids at the same concentration, so concentration cannot explain the difference.
- Ethanoic acid is covalent, but it does produce some hydrogen ions in water: otherwise its pH would be <InlineMath math="7" />.

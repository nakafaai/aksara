# Find which solution runs out, titrate a tenth of the carbonate it leaves, and compare the crystals with what that copper could give

The copper(II) ions are <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> and the carbonate ions <InlineMath math="0.0240\text{ mol}" />, so **the copper(II) sulfate runs out**, giving <InlineMath math="0.0200\times123.5=2.47\text{ g}" /> of copper(II) carbonate and leaving <InlineMath math="0.00400\text{ mol}" /> of sodium carbonate. A portion holds a tenth, <InlineMath math="0.000400\text{ mol}" />, and **each carbonate takes two acid**, <InlineMath math="0.000800\div0.0100=0.0800\text{ dm}^3=80.0\text{ cm}^3" />. The <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper could give <InlineMath math="0.0200\times249.5=4.99\text{ g}" /> of crystals, so the yield is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4.24}{4.99}\times100=85.0\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.96\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="70.8\%" /> let the sodium carbonate decide, but only <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions is there, so carbonate is left over.
- <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" /> lets each carbonate take one acid, but the equation uses two.
- <InlineMath math="800\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the carbonate left, but **the portion is only a tenth of the filtrate**.

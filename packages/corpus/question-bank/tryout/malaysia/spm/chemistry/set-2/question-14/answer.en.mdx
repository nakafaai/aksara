# Find which solution runs out, then carry the same amount of copper through every step

The copper(II) ions are <InlineMath math="0.0500\times0.400=0.0200\text{ mol}" /> and the carbonate ions <InlineMath math="0.0400\times0.600=0.0240\text{ mol}" />, so **the copper(II) sulfate runs out**. That gives <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) carbonate, <InlineMath math="0.0200\times123.5=2.47\text{ g}" />, then <InlineMath math="0.0200\times79.5=1.59\text{ g}" /> of oxide, and **each copper ends in one crystal unit with five water molecules**, <InlineMath math="0.0200\times249.5=4.99\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.96\text{ g}" /> lets the sodium carbonate decide, but only <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions is there.
- <InlineMath math="3.19\text{ g}" /> weighs the crystals as anhydrous copper(II) sulfate, but **the five water molecules are part of the crystal's mass**.
- <InlineMath math="1.27\text{ g}" /> weighs copper metal, but heating the carbonate leaves copper(II) oxide.

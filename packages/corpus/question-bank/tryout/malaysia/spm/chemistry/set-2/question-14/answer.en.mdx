# Only the leftover carbonate fizzes

The precipitate stays on the filter paper, so the acid sees just the <InlineMath math="0.00400\text{ mol}" /> in the filtrate. **One carbonate ion gives one carbon dioxide**, so the gas is <InlineMath math="96.0\text{ cm}^3" />. The coefficient two belongs to the acid, and dividing by it produces the <InlineMath math="48.0\text{ cm}^3" /> option. Sending all <InlineMath math="0.0240\text{ mol}" /> of carbonate to the acid gives <InlineMath math="576\text{ cm}^3" />, but most of that carbonate already left the solution inside the precipitate.

# Following the copper into the crystals

Heating, dissolving and crystallising lose no copper atom in the calculation, so **each mole of copper(II) carbonate can become one mole of <InlineMath math="\text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O}" />**, <InlineMath math="M_r=249.5" />. The ceiling is <InlineMath math="4.99\text{ g}" />, and the <InlineMath math="4.24\text{ g}" /> collected is <InlineMath math="85.0\%" /> of it.

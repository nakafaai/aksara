# Turn the pH back into a hydrogen ion concentration

pH is <InlineMath math="-\log[\text{H}^+]" />, so <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] = 10^{-\text{pH}}" />. Each ethanoic acid molecule that ionises gives **one hydrogen ion**, so the hydrogen ion concentration is the concentration of ionised molecules.

- <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] = 10^{-2.87} = 0.00135\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.
- Percentage ionised: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.00135}{0.100} \times 100\% = 1.35\%" />.

Hydrochloric acid at the same concentration has pH <InlineMath math="1.00" />, so <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] = 0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />: it is fully ionised. That is the difference between a strong and a weak acid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.87\%" /> **reads the pH itself as a percentage**.
- <InlineMath math="13.5\%" /> divides by <InlineMath math="0.0100" /> instead of <InlineMath math="0.100" />, using the more dilute solution's concentration.
- <InlineMath math="0.135\%" /> drops a factor of <InlineMath math="10" />, dividing by <InlineMath math="1.00" /> instead of <InlineMath math="0.100" />.

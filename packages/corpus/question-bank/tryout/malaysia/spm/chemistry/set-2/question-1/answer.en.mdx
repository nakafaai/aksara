# Average the concordant titres, then use the <InlineMath math="2" /> : <InlineMath math="1" /> ratio

Three linked steps: read the titres, **average only the concordant ones**, then turn moles of alkali into moles of a diprotic acid.

- Titres: <InlineMath math="24.60" /> (rough), <InlineMath math="24.20" />, <InlineMath math="24.25" /> and <InlineMath math="24.30\text{ cm}^3" />. The last three agree within <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ cm}^3" />, so the average titre is <InlineMath math="\dfrac{24.20 + 24.25 + 24.30}{3} = 24.25\text{ cm}^3" />.
- Moles of NaOH: <InlineMath math="0.100 \times \dfrac{25.0}{1000} = 0.00250\text{ mol}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, so moles of acid <InlineMath math="= \dfrac{0.00250}{2} = 0.00125\text{ mol}" />.
- Concentration: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.00125}{24.25/1000} = 0.0515\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.103\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> uses a <InlineMath math="1 : 1" /> ratio, **as if sulfuric acid gave one hydrogen ion**.
- <InlineMath math="0.206\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> applies the <InlineMath math="2" /> the wrong way, doubling the moles of acid.
- <InlineMath math="0.0258\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> halves the moles twice.

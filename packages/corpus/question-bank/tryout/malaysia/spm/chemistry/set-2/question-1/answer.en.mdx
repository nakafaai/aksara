# Chloride in the portion

The salt is <InlineMath math="5.85\div58.5=0.100\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />, so the first flask holds <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. That is not the solution the question asks about. **Diluting <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> divides the concentration by four**, which gives <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> portion of the diluted solution therefore carries <InlineMath math="0.100\times0.0200=0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of chloride ions.

# Silver the chloride cannot catch

The silver nitrate brings <InlineMath math="0.0500\times0.0500=0.00250\text{ mol}" /> of silver ions, more than the chloride. Every chloride ion takes one silver ion, so <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of silver chloride, <InlineMath math="M_r=143.5" />, settles out. The <InlineMath math="0.117\text{ g}" /> in one option is the mass of sodium chloride in the portion, not of the precipitate.

**Only the <InlineMath math="0.000500\text{ mol}" /> of silver ions that found no chloride** meets the thiocyanate. Titrating all <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" /> would give <InlineMath math="62.5\text{ cm}^3" />, as if nothing had precipitated.

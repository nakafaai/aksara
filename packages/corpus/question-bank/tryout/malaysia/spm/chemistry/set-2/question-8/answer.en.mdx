# One metal and one non-metal partner give two kinds of bonding

Write the electron arrangements first: W is <InlineMath math="2.8.2" />, X is <InlineMath math="2.4" /> and Y is <InlineMath math="2.8.7" />. **W is a metal and transfers electrons, X is a non-metal and shares them**.

- I is correct: W loses <InlineMath math="2" /> electrons, one to each of two Y atoms, so <InlineMath math="\text{WY}_2" />. X needs <InlineMath math="4" /> electrons and shares one pair with each of four Y atoms, so <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_4" />.
- II is correct: molten <InlineMath math="\text{WY}_2" /> has free-moving ions, while <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_4" /> is made of neutral molecules.
- III is wrong: a simple covalent compound has weak forces between molecules, so <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_4" /> melts far below the ionic lattice of <InlineMath math="\text{WY}_2" />.
- IV is wrong: the ionic compound dissolves in water, but the simple covalent one does not.

So I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only accepts III, **comparing melting points the wrong way round**.
- II and IV only rejects the formulae and treats both compounds as soluble.
- I, II and IV only accepts IV. The covalent compound dissolves in organic solvents, not in water.

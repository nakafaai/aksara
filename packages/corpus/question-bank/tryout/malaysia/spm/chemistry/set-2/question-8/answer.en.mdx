# The fluorine runs out first, and each fluoride formula unit then gives two hydrogen fluoride for the alkali

The calcium is <InlineMath math="0.800\div40=0.0200\text{ mol}" /> and the fluorine <InlineMath math="360\div24\,000=0.0150\text{ mol}" />. **They react one to one, so the fluorine runs out**, each calcium atom giving two electrons, and <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of calcium fluoride forms, <InlineMath math="M_r=78" />, <InlineMath math="1.17\text{ g}" />, leaving <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of calcium, <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ g}" />. **Each formula unit holds two fluoride ions**, so it gives two hydrogen fluoride, <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="30.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.56\text{ g}" /> lets all the calcium react, but <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of fluorine is enough for only <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of it.
- <InlineMath math="15.0\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen fluoride per formula unit, but **<InlineMath math="\text{CaF}_2" /> holds two fluoride ions**.
- <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ g}" /> reads the fluorine as atoms, so it has the calcium take half as much as it does.

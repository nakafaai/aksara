# Two non-metals share electrons to make a simple molecule

Start from the electron arrangements: X is <InlineMath math="2.4" /> and Y is <InlineMath math="2.8.7" />. Both are non-metals, so **they share electrons instead of transferring them**.

- I is correct: X needs <InlineMath math="4" /> more electrons and each Y needs <InlineMath math="1" />, so one X shares with four Y atoms, giving <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_4" />.
- II is correct: shared electron pairs are covalent bonds.
- III is wrong: the molecules carry no charge, so the molten compound has no ions or free electrons to carry a current.
- IV is correct: like most simple covalent compounds, it is insoluble in water and soluble in organic solvents.

So I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only leaves out IV, a property of simple covalent molecules.
- I and III only accepts III, which only an ionic compound or a metal shows.
- II, III and IV only rejects the formula <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_4" /> and accepts III. **The formula follows from how many electrons each atom needs**.

# Heat released per mole of water formed

The heat of neutralisation is the heat released when **<InlineMath math="1" /> mol of water** forms. Find the heat from the temperature rise, then divide by the moles of water.

- Temperature rise: <InlineMath math="34.8 - 28.0 = 6.8^\circ\text{C}" />.
- Mass of solution: <InlineMath math="50.0 + 50.0 = 100\text{ g}" />, because both solutions warm up.
- Heat released: <InlineMath math="100 \times 4.2 \times 6.8 = 2856\text{ J}" />.
- Moles of water: <InlineMath math="1.0 \times \dfrac{50.0}{1000} = 0.050\text{ mol}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\Delta H = -\dfrac{2856}{0.050} = -57120\text{ J mol}^{-1} \approx -57.1\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-28.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> uses only <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" /> of solution, **as if only one solution warmed up**.
- <InlineMath math="+57.1\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> has the wrong sign. The temperature rose, so heat was released and <InlineMath math="\Delta H" /> is negative.
- <InlineMath math="-2.86\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> is the heat in kJ before dividing by the moles of water.

# Extend the cooling line back to the moment of mixing

The cup starts losing heat as soon as the reaction warms it, so the first reading after mixing is already too low. **Draw the cooling line back to minute <InlineMath math="2.0" />** to get the temperature the mixture would have reached with no heat loss.

The second line runs the cooling back half a minute, at the same <InlineMath math="0.4^\circ\text{C}" /> per half minute.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta\theta&=42.0-28.0=14.0^\circ\text{C}\\
Q&=50\times4.2\times14.0=2940\text{ J}=2.94\text{ kJ}\\
n(\text{H}_2\text{O})&=\frac{25}{1000}\times2.0=0.050\text{ mol}\\
\Delta H&=-\frac{2.94}{0.050}=-58.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

So the heat of neutralisation is <InlineMath math="-58.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-57.1\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> uses the highest reading, <InlineMath math="41.6^\circ\text{C}" />, which the cup had already cooled to by the time it was read.
- <InlineMath math="+58.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> drops the sign. **Neutralisation gives out heat, so its heat change is negative**.
- <InlineMath math="-117.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by <InlineMath math="0.025" />, the volume in cubic decimetres, and forgets to multiply by the concentration, <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Count oxygen atoms, not molecules

The same number of oxygen atoms means the same **moles of oxygen atoms**. For each sample, find its moles of molecules, then multiply by the oxygen atoms in one molecule.

- Carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="M_r = 44" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{4.4}{44} = 0.1" /> mol, with <InlineMath math="2" /> oxygen atoms each, so <InlineMath math="0.2" /> mol of oxygen atoms.
- Sulfuric acid, <InlineMath math="M_r = 98" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{4.9}{98} = 0.05" /> mol, with <InlineMath math="4" /> oxygen atoms each, so <InlineMath math="0.05 \times 4 = 0.2" /> mol.

Both hold <InlineMath math="0.2" /> mol of oxygen atoms.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="9.8\text{ g}" /> of sulfuric acid has the same number of **molecules** as the carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="0.1" /> mol, but twice the oxygen atoms.
- <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ g}" /> of water is <InlineMath math="0.1" /> mol with one oxygen atom each, so <InlineMath math="0.1" /> mol of oxygen atoms.
- <InlineMath math="3.2\text{ g}" /> of sulfur dioxide, <InlineMath math="M_r = 64" />, is <InlineMath math="0.05" /> mol with two oxygen atoms each, so <InlineMath math="0.1" /> mol.

# The total gas and the carbon dioxide give two equations, and each gas then sets its own oxygen and water

The mixture is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> and the carbon dioxide <InlineMath math="0.120\text{ mol}" />. With <InlineMath math="x" /> of methane and <InlineMath math="y" /> of propane, <InlineMath math="x+y=0.0500" /> and <InlineMath math="x+3y=0.120" />, so <InlineMath math="y=0.0350\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.840\text{ dm}^3" />, and <InlineMath math="x=0.0150\text{ mol}" />. The oxygen used is <InlineMath math="2x+5y=0.205\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="4.92\text{ dm}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="6.00-4.92=1.08\text{ dm}^3" /> is left. **The water is <InlineMath math="2x+4y" /> molecules**, <InlineMath math="0.170\times18=3.06\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.12\text{ g}" /> counts each hydrogen atom as a water molecule, but **each water molecule holds two hydrogen atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="3.12\text{ dm}^3" /> subtracts only the oxygen that ends in carbon dioxide, but the water takes oxygen too.
- <InlineMath math="0.360\text{ dm}^3" /> is the methane, but the question asks for the propane.

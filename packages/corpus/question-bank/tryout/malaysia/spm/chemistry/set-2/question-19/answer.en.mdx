# Count atoms, not molecules

The same number of atoms means the same **moles of atoms**. For a molecule, multiply its moles by the number of atoms in one molecule.

- Oxygen: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{3.2}{32} = 0.1" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{O}_2" />, so <InlineMath math="0.1 \times 2 = 0.2" /> mol of atoms.
- <InlineMath math="0.85\text{ g}" /> of ammonia: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.85}{17} = 0.05" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{NH}_3" />, and each molecule has <InlineMath math="4" /> atoms, so <InlineMath math="0.05 \times 4 = 0.2" /> mol of atoms.

Both hold <InlineMath math="0.2" /> mol of atoms, the same number of atoms.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.7\text{ g}" /> of ammonia has the same number of **molecules** as the oxygen, <InlineMath math="0.1" /> mol, but <InlineMath math="0.4" /> mol of atoms.
- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ g}" /> of magnesium is <InlineMath math="0.1" /> mol of atoms, half as many.
- <InlineMath math="3.2\text{ g}" /> of sulfur has the same mass as the oxygen, but <InlineMath math="\dfrac{3.2}{32} = 0.1" /> mol of atoms.

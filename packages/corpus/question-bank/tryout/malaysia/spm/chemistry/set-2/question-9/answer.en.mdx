# The trial titration fixes the acid, the scaled-up mixture keeps the same ratio, and the crystals carry ten waters

The trial alkali is <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" />, and **each acid takes two alkali**, so the acid is <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.625\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The student's <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" /> are twice the trial, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of alkali, exactly neutralised, giving <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of salt. **The crystals carry ten waters**, <InlineMath math="M_r=322" />, so at most <InlineMath math="0.0250\times322=8.05\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="6.44\div8.05=80.0\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.25\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> reacts the acid one to one with the alkali, but **sulfuric acid takes two hydroxide ions**.
- <InlineMath math="125\%" /> divides the most possible by the mass collected, the wrong way round.
- <InlineMath math="16.1\text{ g}" /> gives one salt per alkali, but two alkali make one sodium sulfate.

# Sulfuric acid gives two hydrogen ions for each formula

The end point is the middle of the steep jump, at <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" />. There, **the moles of hydroxide ions equal the moles of hydrogen ions**, and each <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4" /> supplies two.

<InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}" />

- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4" />: <InlineMath math="0.100 \times \dfrac{25.0}{1000} = 0.00250\text{ mol}" />.
- Moles of NaOH: <InlineMath math="2 \times 0.00250 = 0.00500\text{ mol}" />.
- Concentration of NaOH: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.00500}{20.0/1000} = 0.250\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

The starting pH also fits: <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] = 0.200\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> gives a pH of <InlineMath math="0.70" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.125\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> uses a <InlineMath math="1 : 1" /> ratio, **as if sulfuric acid were monoprotic**.
- <InlineMath math="0.0625\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> applies the <InlineMath math="2" /> the wrong way, dividing instead of multiplying.
- <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> doubles twice: once for the ratio and again for the volume.

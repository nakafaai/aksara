# Extend each trend one step down, then check what a trend cannot change

Down Group 1, each atom gains a filled shell, so the radius grows and the valence electron is held more weakly. **Some properties follow that trend, but the pH of a fully ionised alkali does not**.

- I is correct: X is larger than potassium, so it loses its valence electron more easily and reacts more vigorously.
- II is wrong: the melting point falls from <InlineMath math="181" /> to <InlineMath math="98" /> to <InlineMath math="63^\circ\text{C}" />, so X melts below potassium.
- III is wrong: both XOH and KOH are strong alkalis that ionise completely, so at <InlineMath math="0.1\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> both have <InlineMath math="[\text{OH}^-] = 0.1\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> and the same pH of <InlineMath math="13" />.
- IV is correct: X has one more occupied shell than potassium.

So I and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only accepts II, **extending the melting point trend the wrong way**.
- II and III only rejects the two statements that follow from the larger radius.
- I, III and IV only accepts III, treating "more reactive metal" as "stronger alkali", although both alkalis are already fully ionised.

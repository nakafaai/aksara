# Sodium's oxide is basic and sulfur's is acidic, so they partly neutralise each other before the portion is titrated

Sodium oxide has <InlineMath math="M_r=62" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and **each gives two hydroxide**, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide. Sulfur trioxide, <InlineMath math="M_r=80" />, gives <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of sulfuric acid, and **each acid takes two hydroxide**, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />. That leaves <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of hydroxide in <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" /> in the portion, which needs <InlineMath math="0.00100\div0.0100=0.100\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of acid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> gives one hydroxide per sodium oxide, but **<InlineMath math="\text{Na}_2\text{O}" /> has two sodium**, so it gives two.
- <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" /> ignores the sulfuric acid, but sulfur trioxide is an acidic oxide and cancels part of the alkali.
- <InlineMath math="150\text{ cm}^3" /> lets each sulfuric acid take one hydroxide, but it has two acidic hydrogens.

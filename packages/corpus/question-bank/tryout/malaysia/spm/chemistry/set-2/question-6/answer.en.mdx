# A larger atom gives up its one valence electron more easily

Every Group 1 atom reacts by **losing its single valence electron** to form a <InlineMath math="+1" /> ion. The easier that electron leaves, the more reactive the metal.

Down the group, each atom has one more filled shell, so the radius grows from <InlineMath math="152" /> to <InlineMath math="248" /> pm. The valence electron sits further from the nucleus and is shielded by more inner electrons, so the nucleus attracts it more weakly and it is lost more easily.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Melting point does fall down the group, but **it does not cause the reactivity**: both trends come from the weaker hold on the valence electron.
- The radius increases, it does not decrease, as the chart shows.
- Every Group 1 atom has exactly <InlineMath math="1" /> valence electron.

# Same total, different speed

The **total volume** depends only on how much calcium carbonate reacts, and the **rate** depends on how often particles collide effectively. Read the two separately on the graph.

- Both curves end at <InlineMath math="78\text{ cm}^3" />, because the mass of calcium carbonate is the same and the acid is in excess.
- The powder curve is steeper at the start: <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> in the first <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" />, against <InlineMath math="18\text{ cm}^3" /> for the chips.
- Powder exposes far more surface to the acid, so more hydrogen ions hit the solid each second and the frequency of effective collisions rises.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The total volume is the same for both, as the flat ends of the curves show.
- A larger surface area changes how often particles collide, **not the activation energy**. Only a catalyst lowers that.
- The chips react for longer, but they reach the same <InlineMath math="78\text{ cm}^3" />, not more.

Experiment II flips the situation. Half as much ribbon meets acid twice as strong, so the bars in the chart swap places and the magnesium is now the reactant that runs out. It still makes <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, the same <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />.

## The acid left in Experiment II

The ribbon takes <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, so **half the acid is still there** in the same <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> option lets each magnesium atom take a single acid molecule, while the equation asks for two.

# Two speed-ups at once

Doubling the concentration halves the time, and the <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" /> rise halves it again. **The two factors multiply**, so Experiment II runs four times as fast and stops at <InlineMath math="15\text{ s}" />. A volume of <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> in <InlineMath math="15\text{ s}" /> is <InlineMath math="8.00\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />. With only one of the doublings the time stays at <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" /> and the rate comes out as <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />.

# Find what runs out in each run for the volumes, then multiply the two speed-ups for the time

In Experiment I the magnesium is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> and the acid <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, but **each magnesium takes two acid**, so the acid runs out, <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. In Experiment II the magnesium is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> and the acid <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, so **now the magnesium runs out**, again <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. Doubling the concentration doubles the rate and the extra <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" /> doubles it again, **four times as fast**, so <InlineMath math="60\div4=15\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> lets all the magnesium in Experiment I react, but <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of acid dissolves only half of it.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> in Experiment II lets the acid decide, but **only <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium is there**.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" /> counts only the concentration, but the higher temperature doubles the rate as well.

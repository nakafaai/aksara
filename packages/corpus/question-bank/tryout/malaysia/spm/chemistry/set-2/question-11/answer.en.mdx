# Find what runs out in each experiment before reading any volume, then divide by that experiment's own time

In Experiment I the magnesium is <InlineMath math="0.240\div24=0.0100\text{ mol}" /> and the acid <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, but **each magnesium takes two acid**, so the acid runs out, giving <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. In Experiment II the magnesium is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> and the acid <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, more than enough, so **now the magnesium runs out**, again <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. That forms in <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" />, <InlineMath math="120\div30=4.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> lets all the magnesium in Experiment I react, but <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of acid dissolves only half of it.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> in Experiment II lets the acid decide, but **only <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium is there**.
- <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides by the first experiment's <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" />, but Experiment II took <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" />.

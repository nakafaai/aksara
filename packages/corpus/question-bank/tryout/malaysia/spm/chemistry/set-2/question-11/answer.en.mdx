- Calcium carbonate: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{1.00}{100} = 0.010\text{ mol}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ mol}" /> of HCl.
- Experiments I and II have <InlineMath math="1.0 \times \dfrac{50}{1000} = 0.050\text{ mol}" /> of HCl, in excess, so both give <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{CO}_2" />, <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />.
- Experiment III has only <InlineMath math="0.10 \times \dfrac{50}{1000} = 0.0050\text{ mol}" /> of HCl. The acid runs out first and gives <InlineMath math="0.0025\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{CO}_2" />, <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> for all three assumes **the acid is always in excess**, which fails in experiment III.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> in experiment III uses a <InlineMath math="1 : 1" /> ratio of acid to carbon dioxide instead of <InlineMath math="2 : 1" />.
- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> in experiment II treats powder as giving more gas, but the surface area changes only the rate.

# The cathode counts the electrons, the zinc supplies some of them, the copper that dissolved supplies the rest, and the difference leaves the electrolyte

The cathode gains <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of copper, so <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of electrons pass. The zinc is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> and gives <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of them, so **the copper dissolving gives only <InlineMath math="0.180\text{ mol}" />**, <InlineMath math="0.0900\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="5.76\text{ g}" />. The sludge is the rest, <InlineMath math="7.00-5.76-0.650=0.590\text{ g}" />. **The cathode takes <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions but the anode returns <InlineMath math="0.0900\text{ mol}" />**, so <InlineMath math="0.250" /> falls to <InlineMath math="0.240\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.240\div0.500=0.480\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.40\text{ g}" /> makes the copper dissolved equal the copper deposited, but **the zinc also gives electrons at the anode**.
- <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> keeps the copper(II) ions steady, but zinc ions take the place of some of them.
- <InlineMath math="1.24\text{ g}" /> counts the zinc as sludge, but zinc dissolves into the electrolyte.

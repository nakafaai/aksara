# The negative terminal dissolves and the positive terminal grows

Zinc is more electropositive than copper, so **zinc gives away electrons and dissolves**, while copper(II) ions take electrons and deposit as copper.

- Negative terminal: <InlineMath math="\text{Zn} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-" />. <InlineMath math="0.02" /> mol of electrons come from <InlineMath math="0.01" /> mol of Zn, so zinc loses <InlineMath math="0.01 \times 65 = 0.65\text{ g}" />.
- Positive terminal: <InlineMath math="\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}" />. <InlineMath math="0.02" /> mol of electrons deposit <InlineMath math="0.01" /> mol of Cu, so copper gains <InlineMath math="0.01 \times 64 = 0.64\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Zinc gaining mass reverses the cell, **as if copper were the more electropositive metal**.
- <InlineMath math="1.30\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> take one mole of metal per mole of electrons, but each ion carries a charge of <InlineMath math="2" />.
- Copper gaining <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> treats the two half-equations differently. Both need two electrons per atom.

# The copper(II) ions decide how far the cell runs

The cell stops when the copper(II) ions run out, so **their amount fixes the electrons**. Those electrons come from zinc atoms dissolving, and the new zinc ions join the zinc ions that were already in the half-cell.

Run the cell in the lab: zinc wears away at the negative terminal while copper coats the positive terminal, both for the same <InlineMath math="0.030\text{ mol}" /> of electrons.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{Cu}^{2+})&=0.30\times\frac{50.0}{1000}=0.015\text{ mol}\Rightarrow m(\text{Cu})=0.015\times64=0.96\text{ g}\\
n(e^-)&=2\times0.015=0.030\text{ mol}\Rightarrow n(\text{Zn}\text{ dissolved})=0.015\text{ mol}\\
[\text{Zn}^{2+}]&=\frac{0.10\times0.0500+0.015}{0.0500}=\frac{0.020}{0.0500}=0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}
\end{aligned}" />

So the copper electrode gains <InlineMath math="0.96\text{ g}" />, and the zinc ions reach <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.30\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> counts only the new zinc ions, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.015}{0.0500}" />, and forgets the <InlineMath math="0.0050\text{ mol}" /> already in the half-cell.
- <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.70\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> take the <InlineMath math="0.030\text{ mol}" /> of electrons as moles of copper and of zinc. **Each atom gives or takes two electrons**.
- Zinc ions staying at <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> treats the zinc half-cell as a spectator, though every electron in the circuit comes from a zinc atom turning into an ion there.

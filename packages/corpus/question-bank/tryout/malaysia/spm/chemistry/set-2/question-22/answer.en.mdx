# Track the oxidation number of every changing atom

Iron(II) is oxidised only where its oxidation number rises from <InlineMath math="+2" /> to <InlineMath math="+3" />. Then find the atom that is reduced in each of those reactions, and read **the change for one atom**, not for the whole ion.

In II, iron(II) gains electrons and is reduced, while zinc is oxidised. So iron(II) is oxidised in I and III, manganese changes by <InlineMath math="5" /> and each chromium by <InlineMath math="3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Each Cr by <InlineMath math="6" /> counts the six electrons one dichromate ion gains as the change of one chromium atom. **The six electrons are shared by two chromium atoms**.
- Adding II counts any change of iron(II) as oxidation, but in II iron(II) gains electrons and is reduced.
- II only reads <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}^{2+}\rightarrow\text{Fe}" /> as oxidation because the charge disappears, when losing a positive charge means gaining electrons.

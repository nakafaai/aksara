# The copper on the cathode counts the electrons, which count the oxygen and the hydrogen ions, and the alkali must meet both the acid and the copper left

The cathode gains <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of copper, and **each copper(II) ion takes two electrons**, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. **Four electrons per oxygen molecule** give <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />, and leave <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen ions. Copper(II) ions fall to <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. A portion is an eighth: <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen ions take as many hydroxide ions, and <InlineMath math="0.00125\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions **take two each**, <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> in all, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one oxygen molecule per two electrons, but **each oxygen molecule releases four electrons**.
- <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> neutralises only the hydrogen ions, but the copper(II) ions also take hydroxide as they precipitate.
- <InlineMath math="75.0\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> keep all the copper(II) ions, but half of them plated onto the cathode.

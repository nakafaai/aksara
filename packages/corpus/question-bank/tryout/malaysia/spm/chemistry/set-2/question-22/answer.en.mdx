# Oxidation is an increase in oxidation number

An iron(II) ion is oxidised when **its oxidation number rises from <InlineMath math="+2" />**, which means it loses an electron.

In <InlineMath math="2\text{FeCl}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{FeCl}_3" />, iron goes from <InlineMath math="+2" /> in <InlineMath math="\text{FeCl}_2" /> to <InlineMath math="+3" /> in <InlineMath math="\text{FeCl}_3" />. Each <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}^{2+}" /> loses one electron to chlorine: <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{3+} + e^-" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- In the reaction with hydrochloric acid, iron stays at <InlineMath math="+2" />. It is a neutralisation, not a redox reaction.
- With copper(II) sulfate, iron is oxidised, but **it starts as iron metal at <InlineMath math="0" />, not as iron(II)**. Iron(II) is the product.
- With sodium hydroxide, iron stays at <InlineMath math="+3" /> in a precipitation.

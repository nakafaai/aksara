# The alkali needed does not change

Think of the chart in terms of charge. Before, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions carry <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of positive charge. After, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions and <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen ions carry the same <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />, matched by the untouched sulfate. **Each positive charge costs one hydroxide ion**, whether it neutralises a hydrogen ion or helps precipitate copper(II) hydroxide. The <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> portion is an eighth of the solution, so it needs <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of alkali, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

n(e^-)&=2\times\tfrac{0.640}{64}=0.0200\text{ mol},\quad V(\text{O}_2)=\tfrac{0.0200}{4}\times24\,000=120\text{ cm}^3\\

\end{aligned}" />

Counting only the hydrogen ions, or only the copper, gives <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" />. The <InlineMath math="75.0\text{ cm}^3" /> choice adds the new hydrogen ions to all the copper that was there before, as if the cathode had taken none.

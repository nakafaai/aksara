# The carbon dioxide counts the carbon atoms, which with the mass fixes n, and the formula then balances water and oxygen

The carbon dioxide is <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="n\times\tfrac{1.48}{14n+18}=0.0800" />, which gives <InlineMath math="1.48n=1.12n+1.44" />, <InlineMath math="n=4" />, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{OH}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. **Its ten hydrogen atoms make five water molecules**, <InlineMath math="0.100\times18=1.80\text{ g}" />. Balancing, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{OH}+6\text{O}_2\rightarrow4\text{CO}_2+5\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, and **the alcohol's own oxygen atom lowers the oxygen needed**, <InlineMath math="0.120\times24=2.88\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.12\text{ dm}^3" /> ignores the oxygen atom already in the alcohol, so it needs <InlineMath math="6\tfrac{1}{2}" /> oxygen molecules instead of <InlineMath math="6" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.44\text{ g}" /> gives one water per carbon atom, but **the water comes from the ten hydrogen atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ g}" /> gives one water per hydrogen atom, but each water molecule holds two.

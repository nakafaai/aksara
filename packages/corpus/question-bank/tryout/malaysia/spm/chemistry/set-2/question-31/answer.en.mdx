# How long is the chain

One molecule of <InlineMath math="\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+1}\text{OH}" /> burns to <InlineMath math="n" /> molecules of carbon dioxide, and its relative molecular mass is <InlineMath math="14n+18" />. The gas is <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" />, so **a single equation in <InlineMath math="n" /> pins down the alcohol**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n\times\tfrac{1.48}{14n+18}&=\tfrac{1.92}{24}=0.0800\ \Rightarrow\ 0.36n=1.44,\quad n=4,\quad \text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{OH},\ M_r=74
\end{aligned}" />

The alcohol is butanol, and the sample is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. All four options already name <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{OH}" />, so the marks sit in the next two steps.

# Where the oxygen atoms come from

Burning gives <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide and <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of water, which hold <InlineMath math="0.260\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen atoms between them. **Each alcohol molecule walks in with one oxygen atom of its own**, so the gas only has to supply the other <InlineMath math="0.240\text{ mol}" /> of atoms.

Charging all <InlineMath math="0.260\text{ mol}" /> of atoms to the gas gives <InlineMath math="3.12\text{ dm}^3" />, the oxygen that butane, with the same carbon and hydrogen count but no oxygen inside, would need.

# Two hydrogens out, one oxygen in

Acidified dichromate(VI) turns the <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2\text{OH}" /> end into <InlineMath math="\text{COOH}" />: two hydrogen atoms leave in a water molecule and one oxygen atom joins. **So butanoic acid has <InlineMath math="M_r=88" />, which is <InlineMath math="14" /> more than the alcohol**, and every alcohol molecule becomes one acid molecule.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m(\text{C}_3\text{H}_7\text{COOH})&=0.0200\times88=1.76\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

Writing <InlineMath math="1.48\text{ g}" /> for the acid pretends the molecule did not change. Adding the two oxygen atoms but keeping the water inside the product gives <InlineMath math="M_r=106" /> and <InlineMath math="2.12\text{ g}" />.

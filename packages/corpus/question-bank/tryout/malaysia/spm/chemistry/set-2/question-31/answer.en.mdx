# The carbon dioxide with the mass fixes n, the balanced equation gives the oxygen, and each alcohol gives one acid with one acidic hydrogen

The carbon dioxide is <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="n\times\tfrac{1.48}{14n+18}=0.0800" />, <InlineMath math="1.48n=1.12n+1.44" />, <InlineMath math="n=4" />, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{OH}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. Balancing, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{OH}+6\text{O}_2\rightarrow4\text{CO}_2+5\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, and **the alcohol's own oxygen atom lowers the oxygen needed**, <InlineMath math="0.120\times24=2.88\text{ dm}^3" />. The <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of butanoic acid puts <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> in a portion, and **only its <InlineMath math="\text{-COOH}" /> hydrogen is acidic**, <InlineMath math="0.00200\div0.0200=0.100\text{ dm}^3=100\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.12\text{ dm}^3" /> ignores the oxygen atom already in the alcohol, so it needs <InlineMath math="6\tfrac{1}{2}" /> oxygen molecules instead of <InlineMath math="6" />.
- <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the acid, but **the portion is only a tenth of it**.
- <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" /> gives butanoic acid two acidic hydrogens, but only the <InlineMath math="\text{-COOH}" /> hydrogen reacts with alkali.

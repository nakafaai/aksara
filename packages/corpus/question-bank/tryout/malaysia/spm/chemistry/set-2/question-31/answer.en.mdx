# Equal masses are not equal moles

Both molecules have six carbon atoms, but hex-1-ene has two fewer hydrogen atoms, so it is lighter, <InlineMath math="84" /> against <InlineMath math="86" />. **A gram of hex-1-ene holds more molecules, and so more carbon**. That extra carbon shows up twice: as soot when air is short, and as more carbon dioxide when the oxygen is plentiful.

So hex-1-ene gives more carbon dioxide because more of its mass is carbon.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The double bond holds no extra hydrogen. Hex-1-ene has fewer hydrogen atoms, so it forms less water, <InlineMath math="1.29\text{ g}" /> against <InlineMath math="1.47\text{ g}" />.
- Six carbon atoms per molecule would give equal carbon dioxide only for equal moles. **Equal masses of the two fuels contain different numbers of molecules**.
- Hexane's extra atoms are hydrogen, which forms water, not carbon dioxide. Per gram it carries less carbon.

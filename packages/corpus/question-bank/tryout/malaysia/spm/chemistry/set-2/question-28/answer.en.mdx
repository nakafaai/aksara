# Name each compound in turn, then count the bromine

X is propan-1-ol. Over hot porcelain it loses a water molecule, and the double bond forms between the end carbon and its neighbour, so Y is propene. **Bromine adds across that double bond**, one bromine atom to each of its two carbon atoms.

Turn both molecules in the lab: the double bond of propene sits between carbon <InlineMath math="1" /> and carbon <InlineMath math="2" />, exactly where the hydroxyl group and a hydrogen atom left.

So Z is 1,2-dibromopropane, with <InlineMath math="79.2\%" /> bromine by mass.

# Why the other options are wrong

- 1-bromopropane at <InlineMath math="65.0\%" /> treats step 2 as substitution, swapping one hydrogen for one bromine. **An alkene takes the whole bromine molecule by addition**.
- 1,3-dibromopropane puts the bromine on both end carbons, but the two atoms add to the two carbon atoms of the double bond, carbon <InlineMath math="1" /> and carbon <InlineMath math="2" />.
- <InlineMath math="39.6\%" /> counts only one bromine atom, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{80}{202}" />, in a product that holds two.

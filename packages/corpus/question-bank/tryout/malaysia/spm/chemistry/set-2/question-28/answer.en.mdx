# The yield shrinks every product equally

Esterification is reversible, and this flask stops at <InlineMath math="70.0\%" /> of the ceiling: <InlineMath math="0.0350\text{ mol}" /> of ester, <InlineMath math="3.08\text{ g}" />. **Water leaves the reaction molecule for molecule with the ester**, so <InlineMath math="0.0350\text{ mol}" /> of water is <InlineMath math="2.11\times10^{22}" /> molecules. The pair <InlineMath math="4.40\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.01\times10^{22}" /> describes a reaction that went all the way.

# Acid that stays in the flask

Only the acid that met an ethanol molecule is gone, <InlineMath math="0.0350\text{ mol}" /> of it, so <InlineMath math="0.0650\text{ mol}" /> or <InlineMath math="3.90\text{ g}" /> remains. **The ethanol that never reacted did not take any acid with it**. Subtracting acid for all <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol, to get <InlineMath math="3.00\text{ g}" />, quietly undoes the yield you just applied.

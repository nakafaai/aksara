The acid is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{6.00}{60}=0.100\text{ mol}" /> and the ethanol <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2.30}{46}=0.0500\text{ mol}" />, so **the ethanol limits**. At <InlineMath math="70.0\%" />, <InlineMath math="0.0350\text{ mol}" /> reacts, giving <InlineMath math="0.0350\times88=3.08\text{ g}" /> of ester and <InlineMath math="0.0350\times6.02\times10^{23}=2.11\times10^{22}" /> water molecules. **Only <InlineMath math="0.0350\text{ mol}" /> of acid is used**, so <InlineMath math="0.0650\times60=3.90\text{ g}" /> is left.

- <InlineMath math="4.40\text{ g}" /> lets all the ethanol react, but **the yield is only <InlineMath math="70.0\%" />**.
- <InlineMath math="6.16\text{ g}" /> lets the acid limit, but <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol is less than <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of acid.

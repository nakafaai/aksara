# Each ethene takes one hydrogen and leaves one ethane

Ethane does not react. Ethene adds hydrogen, so two volumes of gas become one. **Every cubic centimetre of ethene shrinks the total by one cubic centimetre**, which makes the decrease equal to the ethene.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{C}_2\text{H}_4+\text{H}_2&\rightarrow\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\\
\text{decrease}&=(30+40)-50=20\text{ cm}^3\\
V(\text{C}_2\text{H}_4)&=20\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}^3" /> splits the <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^3" /> decrease between ethene and hydrogen, but one volume of each becomes one volume of ethane, so the loss equals the ethene alone.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}^3" /> treats the whole mixture as ethene. Then <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen would react and only <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> of gas would remain.
- <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> assumes all the hydrogen is used up. **Hydrogen is in excess here**, and <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^3" /> of it is left over.

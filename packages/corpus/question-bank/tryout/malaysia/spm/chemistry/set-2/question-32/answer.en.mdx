# Adding hydrogen saturates the double bond

Ethene, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4" />, becomes ethane, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_6" />, by gaining two hydrogen atoms. This **hydrogenation** needs hydrogen, a nickel catalyst and a temperature of about <InlineMath math="180^\circ\text{C}" />.

<InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni},\ 180^\circ\text{C}} \text{C}_2\text{H}_6" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- Steam with phosphoric acid adds water and gives **ethanol**, not ethane.
- Bromine water adds bromine and gives 1,2-dibromoethane, the test for a double bond.
- Acidified potassium manganate(VII) oxidises ethene to ethane-1,2-diol.

# The ester runs out, so it sets both products, and the alkali it leaves is titrated a tenth at a time

The ester is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4.40}{88}=0.0500\text{ mol}" /> and the alkali <InlineMath math="0.0500\times2.00=0.100\text{ mol}" />, so **the ester runs out**, giving <InlineMath math="0.0500\times82=4.10\text{ g}" /> of sodium ethanoate and <InlineMath math="0.0500\times46=2.30\text{ g}" /> of ethanol. **The alkali left is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />**, a portion holds a tenth, <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="0.00500\div0.100=0.0500\text{ dm}^3=50.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the alkali added, but **the ester has already used half of it**.
- <InlineMath math="8.20\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="4.60\text{ g}" /> let the alkali decide, but only <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of ester is there.
- <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the alkali left, but the portion is only a tenth of it.

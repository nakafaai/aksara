With <InlineMath math="x" /> of ester and <InlineMath math="y" /> of acid, <InlineMath math="x+y=0.0600" /> and <InlineMath math="88x+60y=4.72" />, so <InlineMath math="x=0.0400" />, <InlineMath math="3.52\text{ g}" /> of ester. **Both give sodium ethanoate**, <InlineMath math="0.0600\times82=4.92\text{ g}" />, but **only the ester gives ethanol**, <InlineMath math="0.0400\times46=1.84\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.28\text{ g}" /> counts sodium ethanoate only from the ester, but the ethanoic acid gives it too.
- <InlineMath math="2.76\text{ g}" /> gives ethanol from all the alkali used, but **the acid gives water, not ethanol**.
- <InlineMath math="5.28\text{ g}" /> gives all the alkali used to the ester, but that is more than the whole mixture weighs.

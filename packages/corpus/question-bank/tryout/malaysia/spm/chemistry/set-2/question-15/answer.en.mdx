# Copper sits out the acid

Copper does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid, so every copper atom in the brass ends up in the black oxide. That oxide is <InlineMath math="4.00\div80=0.0500\text{ mol}" /> and carries <InlineMath math="3.20\text{ g}" /> of copper, so copper makes up <InlineMath math="71.1\%" /> of the brass. **The oxide is heavier than the copper inside it**, since a fifth of its mass is oxygen picked up from the air. Reading the <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ g}" /> as copper inflates the share to <InlineMath math="88.9\%" />, and <InlineMath math="28.9\%" /> is the zinc's share, not the copper's.

# The acid is more than the zinc can use

The rest of the brass, <InlineMath math="1.30\text{ g}" />, is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of zinc, and it needs <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of acid. The flask offers <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, and the chart shows where every bit of it ends up.

**The zinc sets the amounts of both products**: <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" />, and <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of zinc chloride, <InlineMath math="M_r=136" />, which is <InlineMath math="2.72\text{ g}" />. Working the salt out from the acid gives <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.40\text{ g}" />, and that much zinc chloride would need <InlineMath math="1.63\text{ g}" /> of zinc, more than the brass contains.

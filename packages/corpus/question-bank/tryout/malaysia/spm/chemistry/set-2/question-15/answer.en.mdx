# The hydrogen counts only the zinc, the rest of the alloy is copper, and the zinc's share of acid sets what the alkali titrates

The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="600\div24\,000=0.0250\text{ mol}" />, and **each zinc gives one hydrogen**, so the zinc is <InlineMath math="0.0250\times65=1.63\text{ g}" />. **Copper does not react with dilute acid**, so it is the other <InlineMath math="3.37\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="67.5\%" />. The zinc took two acid each, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> is left, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="32.5\%" /> is the zinc's share. The question asks for copper, the metal that does not react.
- <InlineMath math="3.25\text{ g}" /> gives two zinc per hydrogen, but **each zinc gives one hydrogen molecule**, and that slip would leave no acid.
- <InlineMath math="75.0\text{ cm}^3" /> lets each zinc take one acid, but it takes two.

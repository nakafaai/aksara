# The hydrogen counts the zinc, the zinc counts the acid used, and the copper left gains one oxygen per atom

The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{600}{24\,000}=0.0250\text{ mol}" />, so the zinc is <InlineMath math="0.0250\times65=1.625\text{ g}" /> and the copper <InlineMath math="3.375\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="67.5\%" />. **Each zinc uses two acid**, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> is left, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali. The copper is <InlineMath math="3.375\div63.5=0.0531\text{ mol}" />, and **each copper gains one oxygen**, <InlineMath math="0.0531\times79.5=4.23\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="32.5\%" /> is the zinc's share, but the question asks for the copper.
- <InlineMath math="75.0\text{ cm}^3" /> lets each zinc take one acid, but **the equation uses two hydrochloric acid per zinc**.
- <InlineMath math="3.38\text{ g}" /> weighs the copper itself, but heating in air adds oxygen to make copper(II) oxide.

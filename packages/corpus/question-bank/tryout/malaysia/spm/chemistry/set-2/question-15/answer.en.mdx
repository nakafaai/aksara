# Foreign atoms jam the sliding layers

A smaller dent means a harder metal. In pure copper, identical atoms sit in **orderly layers that slide over each other** when a force hits them, so the metal dents easily.

The bronze dents average <InlineMath math="2.1\text{ mm}" /> against <InlineMath math="3.2\text{ mm}" /> for copper, so bronze is harder. Tin atoms are larger than copper atoms. Scattered through the copper, they break up the regular arrangement, and the layers can no longer slide past each other easily.

# Why the other options are wrong

- An alloy is held by **metallic bonding**, not covalent bonds between the two metals.
- Bronze still has a sea of delocalised electrons, which is why it conducts electricity.
- Density does not decide hardness. The point is the disrupted layers, not how much mass fills the space.

# Work back from the oxide to the copper, take the zinc by difference, and let the zinc set the hydrogen and the acid left in each portion

The oxide is <InlineMath math="4.00\div80=0.0500\text{ mol}" />, so **the copper is <InlineMath math="0.0500\times64=3.20\text{ g}" />, not the <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ g}" /> of oxide**, <InlineMath math="71.1\%" /> of the brass. The zinc is the rest, <InlineMath math="1.30\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, giving <InlineMath math="0.0200\times24\,000=480\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen. **Each zinc uses two acid**, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> is left. A portion holds a tenth, <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.00100\div0.0500=0.0200\text{ dm}^3=20.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="28.9\%" /> is the zinc's share, but the question asks for the copper.
- <InlineMath math="88.9\%" /> weighs the oxide as if it were the copper, but **the oxide also holds the oxygen gained in air**.
- <InlineMath math="60.0\text{ cm}^3" /> lets each zinc take one acid, but the equation uses two hydrochloric acid per zinc.

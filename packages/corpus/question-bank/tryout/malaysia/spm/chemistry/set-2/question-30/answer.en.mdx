# Only half the ethene meets the steam

The phosphoric acid converts <InlineMath math="50.0\%" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of ethene, so <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol, <InlineMath math="M_r=46" />, is made. **The other half leaves the catalyst unchanged**, and that is the gas the bromine water receives. If the hydration went all the way, you would get <InlineMath math="4.60\text{ g}" /> of ethanol and no ethene at all for the bromine, which is how <InlineMath math="0\text{ cm}^3" /> ends up in one option.

# Bromine adds across the leftover double bonds

One bromine molecule opens each remaining double bond, so <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of bromine is used. **At <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> that means <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of bromine water**. Sending every ethene molecule to the bromine doubles the volume to <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" />, as though the steam had never touched any of it.

The two routes share the ethene evenly, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> to ethanol and <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> to dibromoethane, so between them they account for every ethene molecule the cracking produced.

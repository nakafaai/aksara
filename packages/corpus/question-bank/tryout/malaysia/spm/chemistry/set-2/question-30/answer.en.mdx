# Find the limiting reactant, then apply the yield

The acid and the alcohol react one to one, so the ester can form only from the smaller number of moles. **Propanoic acid runs out first**, which caps the ester at <InlineMath math="0.075\text{ mol}" /> before the yield cuts it down further.

The chart compares the moles of each reactant with the ester at a full yield and at <InlineMath math="80\%" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH})&=\frac{4.6}{46}=0.100\text{ mol}\\
n(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOH})&=\frac{5.55}{74}=0.075\text{ mol}\\
\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOH}+\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}&\rightarrow\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5+\text{H}_2\text{O}\\
m(\text{ester})&=0.075\times0.80\times102=6.12\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

The ester takes its first word from the alcohol, ethyl, and its ending from the acid, propanoate. So you get <InlineMath math="6.12\text{ g}" /> of ethyl propanoate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Propyl ethanoate swaps the two parts of the name. **The alcohol gives the first word and the acid gives the ending**.
- <InlineMath math="8.16\text{ g}" /> works from the ethanol, <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, but only <InlineMath math="0.075\text{ mol}" /> of acid is there to react with it.
- <InlineMath math="7.65\text{ g}" /> is the mass at a full yield and leaves out the <InlineMath math="80\%" />.

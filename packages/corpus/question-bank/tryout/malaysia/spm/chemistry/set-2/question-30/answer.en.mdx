# The alcohol names the first part, the acid the second

An ester is named **alkyl from the alcohol, then -oate from the acid**. Ethanol gives "ethyl" and propanoic acid gives "propanoate".

- Moles of ethanol, <InlineMath math="M_r = 46" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{4.6}{46} = 0.10\text{ mol}" />. The acid is in excess, so ethanol limits the product.
- Ethyl propanoate, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_2" />, has <InlineMath math="M_r = 102" />. At <InlineMath math="100\%" />: <InlineMath math="0.10 \times 102 = 10.2\text{ g}" />.
- At <InlineMath math="80\%" />: <InlineMath math="10.2 \times 0.80 = 8.16\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Propyl ethanoate **reverses the two parts of the name**: it would come from propanol and ethanoic acid.
- <InlineMath math="10.2\text{ g}" /> is the mass at <InlineMath math="100\%" />, before the yield is applied.
- <InlineMath math="7.04\text{ g}" /> uses the <InlineMath math="M_r" /> of ethyl ethanoate, <InlineMath math="88" />, the ester of the wrong acid.

# The flat end of the curve is all the zinc used up

The acid is in excess, so the reaction stops only when **every zinc atom has reacted**. The final volume tells you how much zinc there was.

The equation is <InlineMath math="\text{Zn} + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{ZnCl}_2 + \text{H}_2" />, so <InlineMath math="1" /> mol of Zn gives <InlineMath math="1" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2" />.

- Final volume: <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3 = 0.240\text{ dm}^3" />.
- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.240}{24} = 0.010\text{ mol}" />.
- Moles of Zn: <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol}" />.
- Mass of Zn: <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 65 = 0.65\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.30\text{ g}" /> uses the <InlineMath math="2" /> in front of HCl, as if **each zinc atom gave half a hydrogen molecule**.
- <InlineMath math="0.33\text{ g}" /> halves the moles, which pairs two zinc atoms with one hydrogen molecule.
- <InlineMath math="6.50\text{ g}" /> reads <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> as <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" />, **dividing by <InlineMath math="100" /> instead of <InlineMath math="1000" />**, which gives <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol.

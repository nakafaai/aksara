# Two metals make the gas, so set up two equations

Both metals react <InlineMath math="1 : 1" /> with hydrogen: <InlineMath math="\text{M} + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{MCl}_2 + \text{H}_2" />. **One equation comes from the total mass, the other from the total moles of hydrogen**.

- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.672}{24} = 0.028\text{ mol}" />.
- Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the mass of magnesium in grams: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{x}{24} + \dfrac{1.00 - x}{65} = 0.028" />.
- Multiply by <InlineMath math="1560" />: <InlineMath math="65x + 24(1.00 - x) = 43.68" />, so <InlineMath math="41x = 19.68" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 0.48\text{ g}" />.
- Percentage of magnesium: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.48}{1.00} \times 100\% = 48\%" />.

Check: <InlineMath math="0.020" /> mol from Mg and <InlineMath math="0.008" /> mol from Zn make <InlineMath math="0.028" /> mol.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="52\%" /> is the percentage of **zinc**.
- <InlineMath math="67\%" /> credits all the hydrogen to magnesium, <InlineMath math="0.028 \times 24 = 0.672\text{ g}" />, ignoring the zinc.
- <InlineMath math="34\%" /> takes two moles of hydrogen per mole of metal, halving the magnesium.

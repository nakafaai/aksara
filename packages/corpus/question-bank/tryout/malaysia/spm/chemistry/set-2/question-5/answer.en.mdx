# Only one metal in the mixture makes the gas

Copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series, so **copper does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid**. All the hydrogen comes from the magnesium.

The volume stops rising at <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />, when the magnesium is used up.

- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.240}{24} = 0.010\text{ mol}" />.
- From <InlineMath math="\text{Mg} + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{MgCl}_2 + \text{H}_2" />, moles of Mg <InlineMath math="= 0.010\text{ mol}" />.
- Mass of Mg: <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 24 = 0.24\text{ g}" />.
- Percentage of Mg: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.24}{1.00} \times 100\% = 24\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="76\%" /> is the percentage of **copper**, the metal that made no gas.
- <InlineMath math="48\%" /> takes two moles of magnesium per mole of hydrogen, reading the <InlineMath math="2" /> in front of HCl as if it belonged to Mg.
- <InlineMath math="12\%" /> halves the moles of magnesium, as if each atom gave two molecules of hydrogen.

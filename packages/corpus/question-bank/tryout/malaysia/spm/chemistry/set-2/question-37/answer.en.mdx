# Count the links in the chain, then take their water away

Each amide link forms when a carboxyl group of the acid meets an amino group of the diamine, and that link gives off one water molecule. **A straight chain of <InlineMath math="1000" /> monomers has one link fewer than it has monomers**, just as a row of beads has one gap fewer than it has beads.

So the chain releases <InlineMath math="999" /> water molecules and has a relative molecular mass of <InlineMath math="113\,018" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1000" /> water molecules gives one per monomer. The two ends of the chain keep their groups, so there is one link fewer.
- <InlineMath math="500" /> counts one water molecule per pair of monomers. **Every link between neighbours gives off water, also the link between one pair and the next**.
- <InlineMath math="131\,000" /> counts the water correctly but never takes its mass away from the monomers.

# Weigh the sheet after the sulfur goes in

Vulcanising adds the sulfur to the rubber, so the finished sheet weighs <InlineMath math="13.6+0.640=14.24\text{ g}" />. **The percentage is taken of that heavier sheet**, which gives <InlineMath math="4.49\%" />. Measuring the sulfur against the raw <InlineMath math="13.6\text{ g}" /> gives <InlineMath math="4.71\%" />, a share of a sheet that no longer exists.

# Two sulfur atoms and two units per cross-link

The sulfur is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of atoms, and **every cross-link spends two of them**, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of cross-links form, <InlineMath math="6.02\times10^{21}" /> in number. The option with <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{22}" /> counts sulfur atoms as if each were a cross-link.

Each cross-link has two ends, one on each chain, so <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of isoprene units carry one. The raw rubber has <InlineMath math="13.6\div68=0.200\text{ mol}" /> of units, and one unit in ten is linked. Giving each cross-link a single unit halves this to <InlineMath math="5.00\%" />.

So even light cross-linking, one unit in ten, is already enough to stop the chains sliding past each other. That is why vulcanised rubber springs back instead of staying stretched.

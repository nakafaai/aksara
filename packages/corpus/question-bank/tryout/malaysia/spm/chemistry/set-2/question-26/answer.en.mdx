# The zinc pays for every one of those electrons

The wire carries nothing the zinc did not release, and a zinc atom releases two. So **half as many zinc atoms as electrons dissolve**: <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.650\text{ g}" /> off the electrode. Letting every electron cost a whole zinc atom doubles that to <InlineMath math="1.30\text{ g}" />.

# Two beakers, two volumes

Those <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of new zinc ions join the <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> already in the zinc beaker. Here is the trap the stem set: **the zinc beaker holds <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" />, not <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />**, so <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" /> is spread through <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ dm}^3" /> and gives <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Dividing by the copper beaker's volume doubles it to <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

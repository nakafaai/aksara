# The zinc lost counts the electrons, which add zinc ions on one side and remove copper(II) ions on the other

The zinc lost is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and **each zinc atom gives two electrons**, <InlineMath math="0.0200\times6.02\times10^{23}=1.20\times10^{22}" />. **The zinc ions rise** from <InlineMath math="0.0500" /> to <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The same electrons plate <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of copper, so <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions is left. A portion holds a tenth, <InlineMath math="0.00400\text{ mol}" />, and **each takes two hydroxide ions**, <InlineMath math="0.00800\div0.100=0.0800\text{ dm}^3=80.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> lowers the zinc ions, but **the zinc electrode dissolves into its own solution**.
- <InlineMath math="6.02\times10^{21}" /> counts one electron per zinc atom, but each zinc atom gives two.
- <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydroxide per copper(II) ion, but copper(II) hydroxide needs two.

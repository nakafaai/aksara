# The relative atomic mass is a weighted average

The relative atomic mass is the **average mass of all the atoms**, weighted by how common each isotope is. Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the percentage of boron-10, so boron-11 is <InlineMath math="100-x" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\frac{10x+11(100-x)}{100}&=10.8\\
1100-x&=1080\\
x&=20
\end{aligned}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="0.20\times10+0.80\times11=2+8.8=10.8" />. The average lies close to <InlineMath math="11" />, so the heavier isotope must be the common one. So <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of boron atoms are boron-10.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="80\%" /> is the share of boron-11, the answer for the **other** isotope.
- <InlineMath math="10.8\%" /> uses the relative atomic mass itself as a percentage.
- <InlineMath math="50\%" /> assumes equal amounts, which would give <InlineMath math="10.5" />, not <InlineMath math="10.8" />.

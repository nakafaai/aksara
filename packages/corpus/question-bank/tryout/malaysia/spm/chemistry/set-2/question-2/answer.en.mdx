# Weight the isotopes, let the hydrogen run out, follow each chlorine atom into its own hydrogen chloride, and send the leftover chlorine to the iodide

The relative atomic mass is <InlineMath math="0.75\times35+0.25\times37=35.5" />. The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> and the chlorine <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" />, so **the hydrogen runs out**, making <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen chloride, one per chlorine atom used. **A quarter of those atoms are <InlineMath math="^{37}\text{Cl}" />**, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{H}^{37}\text{Cl}" />, <InlineMath math="3.01\times10^{22}" />. The <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine left frees as much iodine, <InlineMath math="0.0500\times254=12.7\text{ g}" />.

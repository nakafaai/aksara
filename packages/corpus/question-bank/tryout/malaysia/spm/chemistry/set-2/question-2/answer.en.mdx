# Two unknown abundances need two equations

With three isotopes and one abundance given, two abundances are unknown. **One equation comes from the percentages adding up to <InlineMath math="100" />, the other from the weighted average**.

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the percentage of magnesium-25 and <InlineMath math="y" /> the percentage of magnesium-26.

- Percentages: <InlineMath math="x + y = 100 - 79 = 21" />.
- Weighted average: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{79(24) + 25x + 26y}{100} = 24.32" />, so <InlineMath math="25x + 26y = 2432 - 1896 = 536" />.
- Subtract <InlineMath math="25(x + y) = 525" />: <InlineMath math="y = 536 - 525 = 11" />, and <InlineMath math="x = 10" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\%" /> is the abundance of magnesium-25: **the two unknowns were swapped** at the end.
- <InlineMath math="21\%" /> lumps both heavier isotopes together, as if magnesium-26 were the only other isotope.
- <InlineMath math="5.5\%" /> halves the remaining <InlineMath math="11\%" />, as if it had to be shared again.

# Take the yield first, then each glucose gives two ethanol and two carbon dioxide, and each ethanol gives one acid

The glucose is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{9.00}{180}=0.0500\text{ mol}" />, and **only <InlineMath math="80.0\%" /> reacts**, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />. **Each glucose gives two of each product**, <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ dm}^3" />, and <InlineMath math="0.0800\times46=3.68\text{ g}" /> of ethanol. That ethanol gives <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" /> of ethanoic acid, a portion holds a tenth, <InlineMath math="0.00800\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="0.00800\div0.100=0.0800\text{ dm}^3=80.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="4.60\text{ g}" /> let all the glucose react, but **only <InlineMath math="80.0\%" /> of it does**.
- <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="1.84\text{ g}" /> give one of each product per glucose, but the equation gives two.
- <InlineMath math="800\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the acid, but the portion is only a tenth of it.

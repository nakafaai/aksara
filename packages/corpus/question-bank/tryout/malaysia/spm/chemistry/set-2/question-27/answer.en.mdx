# Each extra carbon unit adds the same amount of heat

Members of a homologous series differ by **one <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> unit**. Burning that extra unit releases about the same energy each time, so the heat of combustion rises in equal steps.

The steps are <InlineMath math="1560 - 890 = 670" />, <InlineMath math="2220 - 1560 = 660" /> and <InlineMath math="2880 - 2220 = 660" />. Pentane has one more carbon than butane:

<InlineMath math="2880 + 660 = 3540\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3200\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> adds only about half a step, **as if the series were levelling off**.
- <InlineMath math="4200\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> adds two steps, which is the prediction for hexane.
- <InlineMath math="2950\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> barely moves past butane, ignoring the extra <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> unit.

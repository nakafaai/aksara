# Only part of the glucose ferments

Cut the glucose down to what actually reacts before touching the equation: <InlineMath math="80.0\%" /> of <InlineMath math="9.00\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="7.20\text{ g}" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> at <InlineMath math="M_r=180" />. **Using all <InlineMath math="9.00\text{ g}" /> inflates every later number by a quarter**, and that is how <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="4.60\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> end up side by side.

# One sugar, two of each product

The fermentation equation splits each glucose into **two ethanol and two carbon dioxide**, so both come to <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" />. The gas fills <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ dm}^3" /> and the ethanol, <InlineMath math="M_r=46" />, weighs <InlineMath math="3.68\text{ g}" />. Reading the equation as one of each halves both, to <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="1.84\text{ g}" />.

# The acid keeps every carbon chain

Oxidation only rebuilds the end of the chain, so each ethanol becomes exactly one ethanoic acid and the <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" /> carries straight through.

**One carbonate ion soaks up the hydrogen ions of two acid molecules** before it lets go of its carbon dioxide, so the carbonate releases <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />, or <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ dm}^3" />. A whole carbon dioxide for each acid molecule would bring back <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ dm}^3" />, which treats ethanoic acid as if it had two acidic hydrogens.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6)&=0.800\times\tfrac{9.00}{180}=0.0400\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ n(\text{CO}_2)=n(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH})=0.0800\text{ mol}\\
V_1&=0.0800\times24=1.92\text{ dm}^3,\quad m(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH})=0.0800\times46=3.68\text{ g}\\
n(\text{CH}_3\text{COOH})&=0.0800\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ V_2=\tfrac{0.0800}{2}\times24=0.960\text{ dm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

So the second gas is half the first, even though no carbon chain went missing in between.

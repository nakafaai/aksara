# Extend the trend first, then use the heat

The chart stops at butane, so pentane's heat of combustion has to come from the trend. Each extra <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> unit adds about the same amount, <InlineMath math="2220-1560=660" /> and <InlineMath math="2880-2220=660\text{ kJ}" />, because every alkane burns one more <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> group. Then **the heat absorbed by the water decides the moles of pentane**.

The chart extends the line by one more step, to pentane.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta H(\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12})&\approx-(2880+660)=-3540\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\\
Q&=250\times4.2\times40.0=42\,000\text{ J}=42.0\text{ kJ}\\
n&=\frac{42.0}{3540}=0.0119\text{ mol}\Rightarrow m=0.0119\times72=0.85\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

So about <InlineMath math="0.85\text{ g}" /> of pentane is burnt.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.05\text{ g}" /> uses butane's <InlineMath math="2880\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, stopping at the last value in the chart instead of extending it to pentane.
- <InlineMath math="0.72\text{ g}" /> adds two steps, <InlineMath math="2880+2\times660=4200" />, which is the prediction for hexane.
- <InlineMath math="0.69\text{ g}" /> converts the moles with butane's molar mass, <InlineMath math="58" />. **Pentane is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}" />, so its molar mass is <InlineMath math="72" />**.

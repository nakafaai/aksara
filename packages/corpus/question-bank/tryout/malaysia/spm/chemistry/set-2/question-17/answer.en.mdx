# Use the table to confirm the reaction, then count bromide ions

The table shows chlorine frees bromine but bromine cannot free chlorine, so **chlorine is the more reactive halogen** and the reaction goes:

<InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{Br}^- \rightarrow 2\text{Cl}^- + \text{Br}_2" />

<Mermaid
  title={<>From Reactivity to Mass</>}
  description={<>The table fixes the order, so only chlorine can free bromine, and every two bromide ions give one bromine molecule.</>}
  chart={"flowchart LR\n  T[Chlorine frees bromine, bromine frees no chlorine] --> O[Chlorine is more reactive than bromine]\n  O --> E[Cl2 + 2Br- to 2Cl- + Br2]\n  E --> M[0.0100 mol Br- gives 0.00500 mol Br2, 0.80 g]"}
/>

- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{Br}^-" />: <InlineMath math="0.200 \times \dfrac{50.0}{1000} = 0.0100\text{ mol}" />.
- Two bromide ions give one bromine molecule: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.0100}{2} = 0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" />.
- Mass: <InlineMath math="0.00500 \times 160 = 0.80\text{ g}" />, since <InlineMath math="M_r(\text{Br}_2) = 2 \times 80" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.60\text{ g}" /> takes **one bromine molecule per bromide ion**, forgetting that each <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" /> needs two.
- <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ g}" /> uses <InlineMath math="M_r = 80" />, the mass of a bromine atom, for the molecule <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.36\text{ g}" /> is the mass of chlorine used, <InlineMath math="0.00500 \times 71" />, not of the bromine formed.

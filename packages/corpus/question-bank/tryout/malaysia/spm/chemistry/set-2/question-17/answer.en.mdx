# The hydrogen counts only the metal, but the alkali comes from the metal and the oxide together

The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" />, and **two sodium atoms give one hydrogen molecule**, so the metal is <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.690\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.690}{0.845}\times100=81.7\%" />. The oxide is <InlineMath math="0.155\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" />, and **it adds two hydroxide ions per unit**, so the hydroxide is <InlineMath math="0.0300+0.00500=0.0350\text{ mol}" />. A portion holds <InlineMath math="0.00350\text{ mol}" />, which needs half as much sulfuric acid, <InlineMath math="0.00175\div0.100=0.0175\text{ dm}^3=17.5\text{ cm}^3" />. All the solution gives <InlineMath math="0.0350\times58.5=2.05\text{ g}" /> of chloride.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="15.0\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="1.76\text{ g}" /> count only the hydroxide from the metal, but **the oxide dissolves to sodium hydroxide too**.
- <InlineMath math="35.0\text{ cm}^3" /> lets each sulfuric acid neutralise one hydroxide, but it has two acidic hydrogens.
- <InlineMath math="40.8\%" /> takes one sodium atom per hydrogen molecule, but the equation needs two.

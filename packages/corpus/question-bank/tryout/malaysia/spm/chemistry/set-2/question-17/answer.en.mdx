# Chain the two displacements into one order

A halogen displaces another from its halide **only if it is more reactive**, because it takes the electron the halide ion holds. Each result gives one link of the order.

<Mermaid
  title={<>Ranking the Halogens from Two Results</>}
  description={<>Each displacement shows that the added halogen is more reactive than the one it frees, so the two results chain into one order.</>}
  chart={"flowchart LR\n  P[P: most reactive] -->|displaces| Q[Q]\n  Q -->|displaces| R[R: least reactive]\n  P -.->|so P also displaces R| R"}
/>

- P frees Q from potassium Q, so P is more reactive than Q.
- Q frees R from potassium R, so Q is more reactive than R.
- Together: P is more reactive than Q, which is more reactive than R. So P is also more reactive than R and frees R from potassium R.

<InlineMath math="\text{P}_2 + 2\text{KR} \rightarrow 2\text{KP} + \text{R}_2" />

A halogen reacts by taking electrons, so the most reactive halogen, P, is also the strongest oxidising agent.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Q is **released**, not the one doing the releasing: being freed from potassium Q shows Q is less reactive than P.
- R is the weakest oxidising agent: it takes electrons least readily, which is why nothing could release it from a reaction it lost.
- Q water and potassium P would need Q to take electrons from P ions, but Q is less reactive than P.

# Only a more reactive halogen can take the electron

A halogen reacts by **gaining one electron**. A smaller atom pulls that electron in more strongly, so reactivity falls down Group 17.

Chlorine atoms are smaller than bromine atoms, so chlorine is more reactive and takes electrons from bromide ions:

<InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{KBr} \rightarrow 2\text{KCl} + \text{Br}_2" />

The colourless solution turns orange or brown as bromine forms.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Bromine is less reactive than chlorine, so it **cannot take electrons back from chloride ions**.
- Iodine is the least reactive of the three, so it displaces neither bromide nor chloride.

# Bromine in, bromine out

The bromine water starts with <InlineMath math="0.0750\text{ mol}" /> of bromine. Whatever the alkene leaves goes on to the iodide, and <InlineMath math="6.35\text{ g}" /> of iodine is <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> at <InlineMath math="M_r=254" />, so that much bromine was spare. **The iodine measures the leftover, not the reaction**, which means the alkene took <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />. Plugging the spare <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> into the formula instead makes the alkene twice as heavy, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_8\text{H}_{16}" />.

# Pinning down n

One bromine molecule per double bond means <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of alkene in <InlineMath math="2.80\text{ g}" />, so <InlineMath math="M_r=56" />. Every <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> unit adds <InlineMath math="14" />, so **<InlineMath math="14n=56" /> gives <InlineMath math="n=4" />**, a butene, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8" />.

# What the alkene turns into

Both bromine atoms stay on the product, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8\text{Br}_2" /> with <InlineMath math="M_r=216" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of it weighs <InlineMath math="10.8\text{ g}" />. **That is just the alkene plus the bromine it took**, <InlineMath math="2.80\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="8.00\text{ g}" />. Adding a single bromine atom per molecule stops at <InlineMath math="6.80\text{ g}" />.

On burning, each molecule sends its four carbon atoms into four carbon dioxide molecules, so <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> or <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" />. One carbon dioxide per molecule shrinks that to <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" />.

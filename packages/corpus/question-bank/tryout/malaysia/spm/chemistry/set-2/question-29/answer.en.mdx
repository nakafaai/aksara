# Move the double bond, then branch the chain

Structural isomers have **the same molecular formula but a different arrangement of atoms**. For an alkene, change where the double bond sits and whether the chain branches.

<Mermaid
  title={<>The Alkene Isomers of <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8" /></>}
  description={<>A straight chain of four carbons allows the double bond in two places, and a branched chain allows one more isomer: three in all.</>}
  chart={"flowchart TB\n  S[C4H8 alkenes] --> A[Straight chain]\n  S --> B[Branched chain]\n  A --> A1[But-1-ene: CH2=CH-CH2-CH3]\n  A --> A2[But-2-ene: CH3-CH=CH-CH3]\n  B --> B1[2-methylpropene: CH2=C-CH3 with CH3 on carbon 2]"}
/>

- Straight chain, double bond at the end: but-1-ene.
- Straight chain, double bond in the middle: but-2-ene. Putting it between carbons <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> is just but-1-ene read from the other end.
- Branched chain of three carbons with a methyl group: 2-methylpropene.

That makes <InlineMath math="3" /> structural isomers.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2" /> counts only the straight chains and **misses the branched 2-methylpropene**.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> counts the same molecule twice by numbering the chain from both ends, or adds the ring compound cyclobutane, which is not an alkene.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> adds both rings, cyclobutane and methylcyclopropane, which have no double bond.

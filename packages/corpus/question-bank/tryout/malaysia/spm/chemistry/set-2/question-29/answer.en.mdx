# One bromine molecule per alkene molecule gives the molar mass, the molar mass gives the formula, and the formula gives each product

The bromine is <InlineMath math="0.100\times0.500=0.0500\text{ mol}" />, and **one bromine molecule adds to each alkene molecule**, so the alkene is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> and <InlineMath math="M_r=2.80\div0.0500=56" />. Then <InlineMath math="14n=56" />, <InlineMath math="n=4" />, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8" />. The product <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8\text{Br}_2" /> has <InlineMath math="M_r=216" />, <InlineMath math="0.0500\times216=10.8\text{ g}" />. **Each molecule holds four carbon atoms**, <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4" /> counts bromine atoms, <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, as molecules, but **each double bond takes one <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" /> molecule**.
- <InlineMath math="6.80\text{ g}" /> adds only one bromine atom, but the whole molecule <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" /> adds across the bond.
- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per molecule, but each molecule holds four carbon atoms.

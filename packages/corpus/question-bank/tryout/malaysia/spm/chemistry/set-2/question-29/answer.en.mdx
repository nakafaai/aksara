# Work back from the product's carbon skeleton

Hydrogen adds across the double bond without moving any carbon atom, so P has the same skeleton as 2-methylbutane: a chain of four carbon atoms with a methyl branch on the second one. **Every distinct place for the double bond on that skeleton gives one possible P**.

The skeleton has four carbon-carbon bonds: from the branched carbon to the near end of the chain, from the branched carbon along the chain, at the far end of the chain, and from the branched carbon to its methyl group. The last one gives the same molecule as the first, because both join the branched carbon to an end carbon atom.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2" /> leaves out 3-methylbut-1-ene, whose double bond sits at the far end of the chain, away from the branch.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> counts every alkene with the formula <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}" />, but pent-1-ene and pent-2-ene give pentane. **The product fixes the skeleton before you count**.
- <InlineMath math="4" /> counts the double bond between the branched carbon and its methyl group as a new isomer, but that molecule is 2-methylbut-1-ene again, just drawn with the branch turned.

# The iodine counts the bromine left, the difference is the bromine the alkene took, and one bromine per alkene gives the molar mass

The iodine is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{6.35}{254}=0.0250\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of bromine was left of the <InlineMath math="0.0750\text{ mol}" /> added. **The alkene took the difference**, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, one per molecule, so <InlineMath math="M_r=2.80\div0.0500=56=14n" />, <InlineMath math="n=4" />, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8" />. The product <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8\text{Br}_2" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0500\times216=10.8\text{ g}" />, and **four carbon atoms per molecule** give <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_8\text{H}_{16}" /> takes the iodine as the bromine the alkene used, but **the iodine measures the bromine left over**.
- <InlineMath math="6.80\text{ g}" /> adds only one bromine atom, but the whole molecule <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" /> adds across the bond.
- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per molecule, but each molecule holds four carbon atoms.

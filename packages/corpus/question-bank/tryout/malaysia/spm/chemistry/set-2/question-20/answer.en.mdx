# Choose the method from solubility, then the volume from moles

Lead(II) sulfate is insoluble, so it **must be made by precipitation from two soluble salts**, then filtered, rinsed and dried. The lead(II) ions all end up in the precipitate, so their moles set the volume.

- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{PbSO}_4" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{3.03}{303} = 0.0100\text{ mol}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Pb(NO}_3\text{)}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{PbSO}_4 + 2\text{NaNO}_3" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of lead(II) nitrate is needed.
- Volume: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.0100}{0.500} = 0.0200\text{ dm}^3 = 20.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Titration suits **soluble** salts of sodium, potassium and ammonium. Lead(II) hydroxide is not a soluble alkali.
- <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> gives only <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, half the salt: it halves the moles as if each formula held two lead ions.
- Lead(II) oxide and sulfuric acid form an insoluble coat of lead(II) sulfate that stops the reaction, and an insoluble salt cannot be crystallised from its solution.

# The gas counts only the carbonate, the back-titration counts all the acid used, and the difference belongs to the hydroxide

The carbon dioxide is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, so the carbonate is <InlineMath math="0.00500\times84=0.420\text{ g}" /> and uses <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of acid. A portion has <InlineMath math="0.000500\text{ mol}" /> of acid, so **the whole solution has ten times that**, <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="0.0250-0.00500=0.0200\text{ mol}" /> was used. **The other <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> went to the hydroxide**, two per unit, <InlineMath math="0.00500\times58=0.290\text{ g}" />. Every magnesium ends in one magnesium chloride, <InlineMath math="0.0100\times95=0.950\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.840\text{ g}" /> gives all the acid used to the carbonate, but **the gas shows only <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of carbonate**.
- <InlineMath math="0.580\text{ g}" /> lets each hydroxide unit take one acid, but it takes two.
- <InlineMath math="0.475\text{ g}" /> counts only the chloride from the carbonate, but the hydroxide gives magnesium chloride too.

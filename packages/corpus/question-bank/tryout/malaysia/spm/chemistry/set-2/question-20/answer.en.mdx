# An insoluble salt is made by precipitation

Lead(II) sulfate does not dissolve, so it cannot be crystallised from a solution. **Mix two soluble salts that each carry one of its ions**, and the insoluble salt falls out as a precipitate.

Filtering keeps the precipitate. Rinsing with distilled water removes the soluble sodium nitrate, and drying between filter papers avoids heating the salt.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Lead(II) oxide reacts with sulfuric acid, but the lead(II) sulfate formed is insoluble and **coats the oxide, so the reaction stops**. Nothing is left in the filtrate to crystallise.
- Titration and crystallisation make soluble sodium, potassium and ammonium salts. Lead(II) hydroxide is not a soluble alkali.
- Lead hardly reacts with dilute sulfuric acid, because the insoluble sulfate coats the metal.

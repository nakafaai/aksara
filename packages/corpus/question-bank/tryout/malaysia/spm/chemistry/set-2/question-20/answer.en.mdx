# Find the limiting reactant before the yield

An insoluble salt is made by **precipitation from two soluble salts**, then filtered, rinsed and dried. The yield must be measured against the reactant that runs out first.

<Mermaid
  title={<>Which Reactant Limits the Precipitate</>}
  description={<>The sulfate ions run out first, so they set the mass of lead(II) sulfate, and the yield compares the dry mass with that limit.</>}
  chart={"flowchart LR\n  A[Pb2+: 0.0100 mol] --> C[Mix]\n  B[SO4 2-: 0.00750 mol, runs out first] --> C\n  C --> D[At most 0.00750 mol PbSO4, 2.27 g]\n  D --> E[2.05 g obtained, yield 90.2%]"}
/>

- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{Pb}^{2+}" />: <InlineMath math="0.500 \times \dfrac{20.0}{1000} = 0.0100\text{ mol}" />. Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{SO}_4^{2-}" />: <InlineMath math="0.500 \times \dfrac{15.0}{1000} = 0.00750\text{ mol}" />.
- They react <InlineMath math="1 : 1" />, so sulfate runs out first: at most <InlineMath math="0.00750 \times 303 = 2.27\text{ g}" /> of lead(II) sulfate.
- Percentage yield: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{2.05}{2.27} \times 100\% = 90.2\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Titration makes **soluble** salts of sodium, potassium and ammonium, not an insoluble one.
- <InlineMath math="67.7\%" /> takes the lead(II) ions as limiting, <InlineMath math="\dfrac{2.05}{3.03}" />, although there are more of them than sulfate ions.
- <InlineMath math="111\%" /> divides the expected mass by the actual mass. A yield above <InlineMath math="100\%" /> is impossible.

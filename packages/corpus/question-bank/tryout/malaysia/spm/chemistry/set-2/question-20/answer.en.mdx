# Choose the method from solubility, then the volume from moles

Lead(II) sulfate is insoluble, so it **must be made by precipitation from two soluble salts**, then filtered, rinsed and dried. The lead(II) ions all end up in the precipitate, so their moles set the volume.

/>

- Moles of lead(II) nitrate: <InlineMath math="0.500 \times \dfrac{20.0}{1000} = 0.0100\text{ mol}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Pb(NO}_3\text{)}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{PbSO}_4 + 2\text{NaNO}_3" />, so at most <InlineMath math="0.0100 \times 303 = 3.03\text{ g}" /> of lead(II) sulfate forms.
- Percentage yield: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{2.73}{3.03} \times 100\% = 90.1\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Titration suits **soluble** salts of sodium, potassium and ammonium, so it cannot make lead(II) sulfate.
- <InlineMath math="111\%" /> divides the expected mass by the actual mass. A yield above <InlineMath math="100\%" /> is impossible here.
- Lead(II) oxide and sulfuric acid form an insoluble coat that stops the reaction, and an insoluble salt cannot be crystallised from its solution.

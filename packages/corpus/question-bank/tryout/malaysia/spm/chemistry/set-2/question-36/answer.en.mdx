# Find what runs out in experiment II, then share its heat over the bigger solution

Experiment I tells you the heat for each mole: <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions gives <InlineMath math="2100\text{ J}" />, so the reaction gives <InlineMath math="210\text{ kJ}" /> per mole. In experiment II, **the zinc runs out first**, since only <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol}" /> of it meets <InlineMath math="0.040\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{I}:\ n(\text{Cu}^{2+})&=\frac{50}{1000}\times0.20=0.010\text{ mol},\ Q=50\times4.2\times10.0=2100\text{ J}\\
\text{II}:\ n(\text{Cu}^{2+})&=\frac{100}{1000}\times0.40=0.040\text{ mol},\ n(\text{Zn})=\frac{0.65}{65}=0.010\text{ mol}\\
Q_{\text{II}}&=0.010\times210\,000=2100\text{ J}\\
\Delta\theta_{\text{II}}&=\frac{2100}{100\times4.2}=5.0^\circ\text{C}
\end{aligned}" />

The chart shows the rise each reading of experiment II would give. Only the last one uses both the zinc and the solution that are really there.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20^\circ\text{C}" /> lets all <InlineMath math="0.040\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions react, as if zinc were still in excess. **Here <InlineMath math="0.65\text{ g}" /> of zinc is only <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="40^\circ\text{C}" /> makes the same mistake and also keeps the solution at <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" />, though it is now <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />.
- <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" /> sees that zinc limits the heat to <InlineMath math="2100\text{ J}" />, but spreads it over <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" /> instead of <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />.

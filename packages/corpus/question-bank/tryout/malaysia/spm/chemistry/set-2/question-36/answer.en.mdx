# Twice the heat warms twice the water by the same amount

The temperature rise is <InlineMath math="\Delta\theta = \dfrac{Q}{mc}" />. Doubling the volume of the same solution **doubles the heat and doubles the mass at the same time**.

- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{Cu}^{2+}" />: experiment I has <InlineMath math="0.2 \times \dfrac{50}{1000} = 0.010\text{ mol}" />, experiment II has <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="Q" /> doubles.
- The mass of solution goes from <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" /> to <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />, so <InlineMath math="m" /> doubles.
- <InlineMath math="\Delta\theta = \dfrac{2Q}{2mc} = \dfrac{Q}{mc}" />, so the rise stays at <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20^\circ\text{C}" /> doubles the heat but **forgets the doubled mass of solution**.
- <InlineMath math="5^\circ\text{C}" /> doubles the mass but forgets the doubled heat.
- <InlineMath math="40^\circ\text{C}" /> has no basis: nothing in the experiment changes fourfold.

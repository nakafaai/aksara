# Concentration decides which ion discharges at the anode

At an inert anode, hydroxide ions are discharged before chloride ions, **unless the chloride ions are much more concentrated**. The same current flows through both cells in series.

- Cell I, dilute: hydroxide ions are discharged, <InlineMath math="4\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 4e^-" />, so oxygen forms.
- Cell II, concentrated: chloride ions are discharged, <InlineMath math="2\text{Cl}^- \rightarrow \text{Cl}_2 + 2e^-" />, so chlorine forms.
- In cell II, <InlineMath math="2" /> mol of electrons give <InlineMath math="1" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2" />, so <InlineMath math="0.04" /> mol of electrons give <InlineMath math="0.02" /> mol.
- Volume of chlorine: <InlineMath math="0.02 \times 24 = 0.48\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Chlorine in cell I and oxygen in cell II swaps the two cells, **as if dilution favoured chloride ions**.
- <InlineMath math="0.96\text{ dm}^3" /> takes one mole of chlorine for every mole of electrons, forgetting that each <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2" /> needs two.
- Chlorine at both anodes ignores the concentration effect in the dilute cell.

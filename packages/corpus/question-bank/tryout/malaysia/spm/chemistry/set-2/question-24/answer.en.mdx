# How far the chlorine gets

Start with the gas, because it is the reagent that can run out: <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine, against <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of bromide. One chlorine molecule clears two bromide ions, so **the chlorine is gone after <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of bromide** and only <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of bromine forms, <InlineMath math="0.800\text{ g}" /> at <InlineMath math="M_r=160" />. Turning every bromide into bromine, <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" />, would need half as much chlorine again as was bubbled in.

# Bromine swaps solvent, then partner

Bromine molecules are non-polar, so the shaking pulls **all <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of bromine into the hexane** while the ions stay in the water. There it oxidises iodide and frees one iodine molecule per bromine molecule. Iodine leaves as <InlineMath math="\text{I}_2" /> with <InlineMath math="M_r=254" />, giving <InlineMath math="1.27\text{ g}" />. Weighing it as single atoms halves that to <InlineMath math="0.635\text{ g}" />.

# Every halide in the first water layer

That water layer never met the iodide. It holds the <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of chloride the chlorine turned into and the <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of bromide the chlorine never reached. **Silver nitrate takes both**, white silver chloride and cream silver bromide, so it has <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of halide to cover.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{Cl}_2)&=\tfrac{120}{24\,000}=0.00500\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ m(\text{Br}_2)=0.00500\times160=0.800\text{ g},\quad m(\text{I}_2)=0.00500\times254=1.27\text{ g}\\
n(\text{Cl}^-)+n(\text{Br}^-)&=0.0100+0.00500=0.0150\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ V(\text{AgNO}_3)=\tfrac{0.0150}{0.100}=0.150\text{ dm}^3=150\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

Counting only the leftover bromide gives <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />, as though the chloride made in the first step had disappeared. Notice the halide total is exactly the bromide you started with, because each bromide that left as bromine was replaced by one chloride.

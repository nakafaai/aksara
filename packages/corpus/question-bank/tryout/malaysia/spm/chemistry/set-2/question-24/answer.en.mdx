# The chlorine runs out, so it frees only part of the bromine, and every bromide it removes is replaced by a chloride

The chlorine is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{120}{24\,000}=0.00500\text{ mol}" /> and the bromide <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" />, so **the chlorine runs out**, freeing <InlineMath math="0.00500\times160=0.800\text{ g}" /> of bromine. That bromine frees as much iodine, <InlineMath math="0.00500\times254=1.27\text{ g}" />. The water layer keeps <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of bromide and gains <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of chloride, and **silver nitrate precipitates both**, <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="150\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> counts only the bromide left, but **the chloride formed precipitates with silver too**, so the halide total stays <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.91\text{ g}" /> let the bromide decide, but <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine frees only <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of bromine.
- <InlineMath math="0.635\text{ g}" /> weighs the iodine as atoms, but it forms as <InlineMath math="\text{I}_2" />, <InlineMath math="M_r=254" />.

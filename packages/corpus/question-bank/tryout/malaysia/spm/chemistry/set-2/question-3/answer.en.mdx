# Splitting the isotopes

Let <InlineMath math="x" /> be the fraction of copper-63. The mean mass sits a quarter of the way from <InlineMath math="63" /> to <InlineMath math="65" />, so **three atoms in four are copper-63**, <InlineMath math="75.0\%" />. The <InlineMath math="25.0\%" /> option names the heavier isotope instead.

# Hydrogen limits the reduction

The oxide is <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> but the hydrogen only <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" />, so **only <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" /> of copper forms** and some oxide stays black. Any route that starts the second reaction with <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of copper has skipped this check.

# Silver ions limit the displacement

Now the copper is <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" /> and the silver ions <InlineMath math="0.220\text{ mol}" />. One copper atom takes two silver ions, so the copper would need <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mol}" /> and **the silver ions run out first**. They give <InlineMath math="0.220\text{ mol}" /> of silver and use only <InlineMath math="0.110\text{ mol}" /> of copper.

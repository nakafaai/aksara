# A flat stretch on a heating curve is a change of state

While a substance melts, the heat it absorbs goes into **overcoming the forces between particles**, not into making them move faster, so the temperature stops rising.

- I is correct: from minute <InlineMath math="4" /> to minute <InlineMath math="8" /> the solid is melting, so solid and liquid are both there.
- II is correct: the absorbed heat overcomes the forces of attraction between molecules, so the temperature stays at <InlineMath math="80^\circ\text{C}" />.
- III is wrong: after minute <InlineMath math="8" /> all the naphthalene is liquid, and liquid molecules move about and slide past each other.
- IV is correct: the flat stretch sits at the melting point, <InlineMath math="80^\circ\text{C}" />.

So I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II misses IV, **the melting point read off the flat stretch**.
- II and III accepts III, which describes a solid, not the liquid at minute <InlineMath math="10" />.
- I, III and IV rejects II, the reason the temperature stops rising, and accepts III.

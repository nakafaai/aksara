# A pure substance freezes at one temperature, an impure one over a range

A flat stretch on a cooling curve is freezing at a fixed temperature. **A short dip before it is supercooling**, and a curve that only bends without a flat stretch belongs to an impure sample.

- I is wrong: pure Q dips to <InlineMath math="78^\circ\text{C}" /> at minute <InlineMath math="4" /> before crystals form, then rises to <InlineMath math="80^\circ\text{C}" /> and holds. Its freezing point is <InlineMath math="80^\circ\text{C}" />.
- II is correct: on the plateau, particles releasing heat as they attract each other into the solid replace the heat lost, so the temperature holds.
- III is correct: impure Q never holds a constant temperature. It slopes slowly from about <InlineMath math="76" /> to <InlineMath math="72^\circ\text{C}" />, below <InlineMath math="80^\circ\text{C}" />, so it freezes over a lower range.
- IV is wrong: a constant temperature means the average kinetic energy of the particles stays the same.

So II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only accepts I, **reading the supercooling dip as the freezing point**.
- I, II and IV only accepts I and IV and misses what the impurity does.
- III and IV only accepts IV, which contradicts the constant temperature on the plateau.

# Two oxidation states from one metal

Each half is <InlineMath math="0.560\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of iron. Dilute acid only takes iron to iron(II), while chlorine takes it to iron(III), so **one half carries two chloride ions per iron and the other three**. The flask holds <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of chloride, and a tenth of it needs <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of silver nitrate. Treating both halves as iron(II) chloride gives <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# A precipitate that keeps changing

The second portion holds <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" /> of each iron ion, and each becomes its own hydroxide: iron(II) hydroxide, <InlineMath math="M_r=90" />, and iron(III) hydroxide, <InlineMath math="M_r=107" />. Standing in air then oxidises the green iron(II) hydroxide, and **every iron atom ends as iron(III) hydroxide**.

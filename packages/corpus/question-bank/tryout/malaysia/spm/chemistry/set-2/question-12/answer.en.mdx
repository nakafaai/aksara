# Acid stops iron at iron(II) and chlorine takes it to iron(III), so the chloride, the fresh precipitate and the air each count the two halves differently

Each half is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of iron. The acid half gives iron(II) chloride, two chloride ions per iron, and **chlorine takes the other half to iron(III) chloride, three per iron**, so <InlineMath math="0.0200+0.0300=0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of chloride. A portion is a tenth, <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of silver nitrate. A portion's <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" /> of each iron ion gives <InlineMath math="0.0900\text{ g}" /> of iron(II) hydroxide and <InlineMath math="0.107\text{ g}" /> of iron(III) hydroxide, <InlineMath math="0.197\text{ g}" />, and in air **every iron ends as iron(III) hydroxide**, <InlineMath math="0.00200\times107=0.214\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="0.180\text{ g}" /> treat the chlorine half like the acid half, but **chlorine oxidises iron to iron(III)**, which carries three chloride ions and forms iron(III) hydroxide.
- <InlineMath math="0.214\text{ g}" /> for the fresh precipitate weighs it all as iron(III) hydroxide, but the acid half gives iron(II), which oxidises only later in air.
- <InlineMath math="0.197\text{ g}" /> after standing ignores the air, which turns the iron(II) hydroxide into the heavier iron(III) hydroxide.

# Iron shows two oxidation states, so each half gives a different chloride and a different hydroxide

Each half is <InlineMath math="0.560\div56=0.0100\text{ mol}" />. Dilute acid takes iron only to iron(II), one hydrogen per iron, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />. **Chlorine takes it to iron(III)**, three chlorine for two iron, <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="360\text{ cm}^3" />. The iron(II) gives <InlineMath math="0.0100\times90=0.900\text{ g}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{Fe(OH)}_2" /> and **the iron(III) gives <InlineMath math="\text{Fe(OH)}_3" />**, <InlineMath math="0.0100\times107=1.07\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="1.97\text{ g}" /> in all.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> of chlorine and <InlineMath math="1.80\text{ g}" /> make iron(II) in both halves, but **chlorine oxidises iron to iron(III)**.
- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen dissolves all the iron in acid, but only one half went into the acid.
- <InlineMath math="2.14\text{ g}" /> makes iron(III) hydroxide from both halves, but dilute acid stops at iron(II).

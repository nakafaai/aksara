# Oxides turn from basic to acidic across Period <InlineMath math="3" />

Metal oxides are basic, and non-metal oxides are acidic. Aluminium sits at the change, so its oxide is **amphoteric: it reacts with both acids and alkalis**.

  description={<>From left to right the oxides change from basic, through amphoteric aluminium oxide, to acidic, as the elements change from metals to non-metals.</>}

/>

- I is correct: <InlineMath math="\text{Na}_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{NaOH}" />, an alkali.
- II is correct: aluminium oxide is amphoteric, so it neutralises an acid and also reacts with sodium hydroxide.
- III is wrong: silicon dioxide is acidic, since it reacts with hot concentrated alkalis, but it does not dissolve in water.
- IV is correct: <InlineMath math="\text{SO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{SO}_3" />, an acid.

So I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only misses IV, the acidic oxide of a non-metal.
- III and IV only rejects the basic and amphoteric oxides and accepts III.
- I, II and III only takes silicon dioxide as soluble. **Acidic does not mean soluble**: sand is silicon dioxide.

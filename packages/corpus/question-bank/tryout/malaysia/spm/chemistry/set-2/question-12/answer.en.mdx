# Acid stops iron at iron(II) and chlorine takes it to iron(III), so the fresh precipitate is a mixture, and air turns it all into iron(III) hydroxide

Each half is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of iron. **Chlorine takes iron to iron(III)**, three chlorine atoms per iron, so <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> reacts, <InlineMath math="360\text{ cm}^3" />. The acid half gives <InlineMath math="0.0100\times90=0.900\text{ g}" /> of iron(II) hydroxide and the chlorine half <InlineMath math="0.0100\times107=1.07\text{ g}" /> of iron(III) hydroxide, <InlineMath math="1.97\text{ g}" /> in all. In air **every iron ends as iron(III) hydroxide**, <InlineMath math="0.0200\times107=2.14\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="1.80\text{ g}" /> treat the chlorine half like the acid half, but **chlorine oxidises iron to <InlineMath math="+3" />**, so it takes more chlorine and gives iron(III) hydroxide.
- <InlineMath math="2.14\text{ g}" /> for the fresh precipitate weighs it all as iron(III) hydroxide, but the acid half gives iron(II), which oxidises only later in air.
- <InlineMath math="1.97\text{ g}" /> after standing ignores the air, which turns the iron(II) hydroxide into the heavier iron(III) hydroxide.

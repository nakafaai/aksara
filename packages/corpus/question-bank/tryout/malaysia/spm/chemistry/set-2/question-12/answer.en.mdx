# Across a period, oxides change from basic to acidic

Proton number increases from left to right across Period <InlineMath math="3" />, and **the oxides change from basic, through amphoteric, to acidic** as the elements change from metals to non-metals.

  description={<>An oxide that reacts only with acid is basic and belongs to a metal on the left, one that reacts with both is amphoteric, and one that reacts only with alkali is acidic and belongs to a non-metal on the right.</>}

/>

- Z's oxide reacts only with acid and dissolves to give pH <InlineMath math="13" />: a basic oxide of a metal on the left, like sodium oxide.
- Y's oxide reacts with both acid and alkali: an amphoteric oxide, like aluminium oxide, in the middle.
- X's oxide reacts only with alkali and gives pH <InlineMath math="2" />: an acidic oxide of a non-metal on the right, like sulfur dioxide.

So by increasing proton number the order is Z, Y, X. Of the non-metals of Period <InlineMath math="3" /> whose oxides are acidic and dissolve in water, only sulfur gives a gaseous oxide, sulfur dioxide, so X is sulfur.

# Why the other options are wrong

- X, Y, Z, sodium, is the exact reverse: it **puts the acidic oxide on the left** and names a metal whose oxide is basic.
- Z, Y, X, aluminium, has the right order, but aluminium oxide is amphoteric and insoluble, which describes Y.
- Y, Z, X, phosphorus, breaks the order, and phosphorus(V) oxide is a solid, not a gas.

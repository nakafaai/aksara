# Let the masses name the oxide

The mass lost, <InlineMath math="0.64\text{ g}" />, is the oxygen the carbon monoxide removed. Turning both masses into moles gives four oxygen atoms for every three iron atoms. **Do not assume the common oxide**: the chart shows that <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3" /> would need a ratio of <InlineMath math="1.50" />, and the measured <InlineMath math="1.33" /> matches <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4" />.

Every oxygen atom becomes one carbon dioxide, and each carbon dioxide precipitates one calcium carbonate, so <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> gives <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ g}" />. The <InlineMath math="3.00\text{ g}" /> option counts the iron instead of the oxygen.

# Titrating the iron that the acid made

Dilute sulfuric acid turns all <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of iron into iron(II) ions, and a portion holds a tenth. **One manganate(VII) ion oxidises five iron(II) ions**, so the portion needs only <InlineMath math="0.000600\text{ mol}" /> of it. Pairing them one to one gives <InlineMath math="150\text{ cm}^3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{Fe})&=\tfrac{1.68}{56}=0.0300,\quad n(\text{O})=\tfrac{2.32-1.68}{16}=0.0400\ \Rightarrow\ \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4,\quad m(\text{CaCO}_3)=0.0400\times100=4.00\text{ g}\\
n(\text{MnO}_4^-)&=\tfrac15\times\tfrac{0.0300}{10}=0.000600\text{ mol},\quad V=\tfrac{0.000600}{0.0200}=30.0\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

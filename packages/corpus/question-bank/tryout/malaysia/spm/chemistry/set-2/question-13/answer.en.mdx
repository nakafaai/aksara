# The mass lost is the oxygen, the mole ratio names the oxide, and every oxygen atom carries on as carbon dioxide into the limewater

The oxygen removed is <InlineMath math="2.32-1.68=0.64\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />, and the iron <InlineMath math="1.68\div56=0.0300\text{ mol}" />, **a ratio of three to four**, <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4" />. **Each oxygen atom takes one carbon monoxide**, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="960\text{ cm}^3" />, and leaves as <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, which precipitates <InlineMath math="0.0400\times100=4.00\text{ g}" /> of calcium carbonate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{FeO}" /> rounds three to four as one to one, but **the ratio is exact**, <InlineMath math="0.0300" /> to <InlineMath math="0.0400" />.
- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ g}" /> count oxygen molecules, but each oxygen atom takes its own carbon monoxide.
- <InlineMath math="1.76\text{ g}" /> is the mass of the carbon dioxide, not of the calcium carbonate it precipitates.

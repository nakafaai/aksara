# The reactant that runs out first decides the product

When two reactants are measured out, **the one that is used up first limits how much product forms**. Compare what each reactant needs with what is there.

- Moles of Mg: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{6.0}{24} = 0.25\text{ mol}" />.
- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{O}_2" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{2.4}{24} = 0.10\text{ mol}" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{O}_2" /> reacts with only <InlineMath math="2 \times 0.10 = 0.20" /> mol of Mg, so oxygen runs out and <InlineMath math="0.05" /> mol of Mg is left over.
- Moles of MgO: <InlineMath math="2 \times 0.10 = 0.20\text{ mol}" />.
- Mass of MgO: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times (24 + 16) = 8.0\text{ g}" />. Magnesium left: <InlineMath math="0.25 - 0.20 = 0.05" /> mol, <InlineMath math="0.05 \times 24 = 1.2\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ g}" /> with nothing left assumes **all the magnesium reacts**, but there is not enough oxygen for it.
- <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ g}" /> pairs <InlineMath math="1" /> mol of oxygen with <InlineMath math="1" /> mol of magnesium, ignoring the <InlineMath math="2" /> in the equation, which also changes what is left.
- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ g}" /> left subtracts the moles of oxygen, <InlineMath math="0.10" />, from the moles of magnesium, instead of the <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol that reacted.

# Shared pairs before the water

Phosphorus pentachloride has <InlineMath math="M_r=208.5" />, so the sample is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />. Each molecule holds five phosphorus to chlorine bonds, one shared pair each, so **count bonds, not molecules**: <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of pairs. The option with <InlineMath math="6.02\times10^{21}" /> counts molecules.

# Two acids from one solid

Water turns each <InlineMath math="\text{PCl}_5" /> into one phosphoric acid with three acidic hydrogens and five hydrochloric acid with one each. A portion is a tenth of the flask, and the chart sets what the two reagents see.

The sodium hydroxide meets **eight acidic hydrogens per original molecule**. Leaving out the phosphoric acid gives <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali. The silver nitrate meets only the five chloride ions, and <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> would credit the solid with one chlorine.

n(\text{PCl}_5)&=\tfrac{2.085}{208.5}=0.0100\text{ mol},\quad N(\text{pairs})=5\times0.0100\times6.02\times10^{23}=3.01\times10^{22}\\

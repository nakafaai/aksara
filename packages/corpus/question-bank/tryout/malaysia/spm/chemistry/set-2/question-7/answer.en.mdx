# Ion charge matters most, ion size next, and the count of ions not at all

The attraction between ions grows with **the product of their charges** and with how close small ions can get. Compare pairs that differ in only one of these.

- I is correct: <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{O}^{2-}" /> attract with charges <InlineMath math="2 \times 2" />, against <InlineMath math="1 \times 1" /> for <InlineMath math="\text{Na}^+" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{F}^-" />, so magnesium oxide melts at <InlineMath math="2852" /> against <InlineMath math="993^\circ\text{C}" />.
- II is correct: both compounds hold <InlineMath math="\text{Na}^+" />, and the smaller <InlineMath math="\text{F}^-" /> sits closer to it than <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}^-" />, so the attraction is stronger.
- III is wrong as an explanation: magnesium fluoride melts higher because <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" /> carries a larger charge than <InlineMath math="\text{Na}^+" />. **The number of ions per formula unit does not set the strength of the attraction**.
- IV is wrong: ions in a solid lattice cannot move, so the solids do not conduct.

So I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only accepts III's reason, which counts ions instead of comparing charges.
- II and IV only accepts IV, which holds only for the molten or dissolved compounds.
- I, II and III only adds III, the same counting slip.

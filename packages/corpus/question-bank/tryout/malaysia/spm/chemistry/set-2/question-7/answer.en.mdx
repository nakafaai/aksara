# The formula sets how many of each ion the salt releases, and each ion then meets its own precipitating partner

Aluminium sulfate has <InlineMath math="M_r=342" />, so <InlineMath math="3.42\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />. **Each formula unit releases two aluminium ions and three sulfate ions**, <InlineMath math="0.0200" /> and <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0500\times6.02\times10^{23}=3.01\times10^{22}" /> ions. Each sulfate gives one barium sulfate, <InlineMath math="0.0300\times233=6.99\text{ g}" />. **Each aluminium ion takes three hydroxide ions**, <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="60.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{22}" /> counts two ions per formula unit, but **<InlineMath math="\text{Al}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3" /> releases five**.
- <InlineMath math="2.33\text{ g}" /> gives one sulfate per formula unit, but the formula holds three.
- <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydroxide per aluminium ion, but <InlineMath math="\text{Al(OH)}_3" /> needs three.

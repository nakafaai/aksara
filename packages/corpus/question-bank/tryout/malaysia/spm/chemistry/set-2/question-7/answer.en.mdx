# Each molecule gives five chloride ions and a triprotic acid, so the alkali counts eight hydrogens per molecule and the silver five chlorides

Phosphorus pentachloride has <InlineMath math="M_r=208.5" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, with five <InlineMath math="\text{P–Cl}" /> bonds each, <InlineMath math="0.0500\times6.02\times10^{23}=3.01\times10^{22}" /> shared pairs. It gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrochloric acid and <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of phosphoric acid, and **phosphoric acid has three acidic hydrogens**, so <InlineMath math="0.0500+0.0300=0.0800\text{ mol}" /> in all. **A portion is a tenth**, <InlineMath math="0.00800\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="80.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali, and its <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of chloride takes <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of silver nitrate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.02\times10^{21}" /> counts one shared pair per molecule, but **each of the five chlorine atoms shares its own pair**.
- <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali neutralises only the hydrochloric acid, but the phosphoric acid formed is acidic too.
- <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of silver nitrate gives one chloride per molecule, but each molecule releases five.

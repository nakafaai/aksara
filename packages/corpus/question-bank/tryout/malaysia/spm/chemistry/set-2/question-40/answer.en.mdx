# The converter lets some dioxide through

Only <InlineMath math="0.0900\text{ mol}" /> of sulfur trioxide leaves the catalyst, and the lab below sets the bent dioxide beside the flat trioxide. **Every trioxide molecule becomes one oleum molecule**, <InlineMath math="M_r=178" />, so <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ g}" /> forms. # Dilution doubles the acid

Water splits each oleum into **two sulfuric acid molecules**. One holds sulfur that came from the burner, the other holds the sulfur of the acid that absorbed the trioxide, and the chart tracks both. So <InlineMath math="0.180\text{ mol}" /> of sulfuric acid forms, <InlineMath math="17.6\text{ g}" />. One acid per oleum gives <InlineMath math="8.82\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="19.6\text{ g}" /> doubles the full <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> without the converter's loss.

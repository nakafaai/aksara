# Atom economy compares the wanted product with everything made

Atom economy is <InlineMath math="\dfrac{\text{mass of desired product}}{\text{total mass of reactants}} \times 100\%" />. **Every atom that ends up in a by-product lowers it**.

- Mass of reactant: one glucose, <InlineMath math="M_r = 6(12) + 12(1) + 6(16) = 180" />.
- Mass of desired product: two ethanol, <InlineMath math="2 \times 46 = 92" />.
- Atom economy: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{92}{180} \times 100\% = 51.1\%" />.

The other <InlineMath math="48.9\%" /> leaves as carbon dioxide. Hydration of ethene, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}" />, has no by-product, so its atom economy is <InlineMath math="100\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="100\%" /> belongs to hydration, **not fermentation, which also makes carbon dioxide**.
- <InlineMath math="25.6\%" /> counts only one ethanol molecule, <InlineMath math="\dfrac{46}{180}" />.
- <InlineMath math="48.9\%" /> is the share that becomes carbon dioxide, the waste.

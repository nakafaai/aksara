# Boiling separates molecules, it does not break them

Boiling a simple molecular substance overcomes the **forces between molecules**. The covalent bonds inside each molecule stay intact.

From HCl to HI, the boiling point rises with molecular size, because larger molecules attract each other more strongly. HF breaks the pattern. Fluorine is very electronegative and small, so the hydrogen of one HF molecule is strongly attracted to the fluorine of the next: these hydrogen bonds are much stronger than the forces between HCl molecules, and more heat is needed to separate the molecules.

# Why the other options are wrong

- **Boiling does not break the <InlineMath math="\text{H}-\text{F}" /> covalent bond**, so its strength does not set the boiling point.
- HF has the smallest relative molecular mass of the four, <InlineMath math="20" />, not the largest.
- A boiling liquid turns into molecules of gas, not ions.

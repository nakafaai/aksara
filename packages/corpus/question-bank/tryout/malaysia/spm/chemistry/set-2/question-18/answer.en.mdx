# The charges fix the nitride's formula, the two masses split the magnesium, and a tenth of the acid the ammonia leaves is titrated

Magnesium, <InlineMath math="2.8.2" />, forms <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" /> and nitrogen, <InlineMath math="2.5" />, forms <InlineMath math="\text{N}^{3-}" />, so **the nitride is <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2" />**, <InlineMath math="M_r=100" />. With <InlineMath math="x\text{ mol}" /> of nitride and <InlineMath math="y\text{ mol}" /> of oxide, <InlineMath math="3x+y=0.0300" /> and <InlineMath math="100x+40y=1.10" />, so <InlineMath math="x=0.00500" />, <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ g}" />. **Two nitride ions per unit** give <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of ammonia, <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />. It uses <InlineMath math="0.0100" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of acid, a portion holds a tenth of the <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> left, and <InlineMath math="0.00150\div0.0500=0.0300\text{ dm}^3=30.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ g}" /> is the mass of the oxide, but the question asks for the nitride.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" /> give one ammonia per formula unit, but **each unit holds two nitride ions**.
- <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the acid in the portion as if no ammonia had reacted, but the ammonia used <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of it.

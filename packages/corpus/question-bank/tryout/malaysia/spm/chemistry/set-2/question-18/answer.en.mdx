# Building the nitride from its ions

Magnesium, proton number <InlineMath math="12" />, has two outer electrons and gives both away to become <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" />, as the lab shows with oxygen. Nitrogen, proton number <InlineMath math="7" />, has five outer electrons and needs three more, so it becomes <InlineMath math="\text{N}^{3-}" />. **Three magnesium ions balance the charge of two nitride ions**, so the compound is <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2" />, <InlineMath math="M_r=100" />.

# Splitting the magnesium between two products

Every one of the <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium atoms ends up in one product or the other. All as oxide, the solid would weigh <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" />. All as nitride, <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ g}" />. **The real <InlineMath math="1.10\text{ g}" /> sits exactly halfway**, so half the magnesium went each way: <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of nitride, <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ g}" />, next to <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ g}" /> of oxide. Picking <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ g}" /> weighs the wrong product.

# Following the nitrogen into the acid

Each formula unit holds two nitride ions, so **<InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of nitride frees <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of ammonia**, <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />. One ammonia per formula unit halves this to <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> and leaves acid for <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali. The ammonia uses <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of acid, and the portion carries a tenth of what remains.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
f&=\tfrac{1.10-1.00}{1.20-1.00}=0.500\ \Rightarrow\ n(\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2)=\tfrac{0.500\times0.0300}{3}=0.00500\text{ mol},\quad m=0.500\text{ g}\\
n(\text{NH}_3)&=2\times0.00500=0.0100\text{ mol},\quad V=0.0100\times24\,000=240\text{ cm}^3\\
V(\text{NaOH})&=\tfrac{(0.0250-0.0100)\div10}{0.0500}=0.0300\text{ dm}^3=30.0\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

If the ammonia were forgotten, the portion would still hold <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" /> of acid and need <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

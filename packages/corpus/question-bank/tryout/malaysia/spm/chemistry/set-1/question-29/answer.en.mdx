# Find the limiting reagent first

The acid and the alcohol react <InlineMath math="1:1" />, so **the theoretical yield is set by the substance with fewer moles**, not by the larger mass.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n_{\text{acid}}&=\frac{6.0}{60}=0.10\text{ mol},\quad n_{\text{ethanol}}=\frac{3.45}{46}=0.075\text{ mol}\\
\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}+\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}&\rightleftharpoons\text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5+\text{H}_2\text{O}\\
m_{\text{theoretical}}&=0.075\times88=6.60\text{ g}\\
\%\ \text{yield}&=\frac{4.95}{6.60}\times100\%=75\%
\end{aligned}" />

The chart lines up the moles. Ethanol is the shorter reactant bar, so it sets the theoretical bar of ester, and the actual bar is three quarters of it.

So the percentage yield is <InlineMath math="75\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="56\%" /> takes ethanoic acid as the limiting reagent, but **the ethanol runs out first**.
- <InlineMath math="83\%" /> divides the mass of ester by the mass of acid, but percentage yield compares the actual and theoretical masses of the ester.
- <InlineMath math="62\%" /> uses an <InlineMath math="M_r" /> of <InlineMath math="106" /> that adds the ester and the water, but the water is not part of the product weighed.

# Dilution, then the degree of ionisation

Step one is dilution: the volume goes from <InlineMath math="10" /> to <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" />, a factor of <InlineMath math="100" />, so the concentration falls <InlineMath math="100" /> times to <InlineMath math="0.0010\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Step two is ionisation. Hydrochloric acid is a strong acid that **ionises completely**, so its hydrogen ion concentration equals the acid concentration.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
M&=0.10\times\frac{10}{1000}=0.0010\text{ mol dm}^{-3}\\
\text{pH}(P)&=-\log(0.0010)=3
\end{aligned}" />

Ethanoic acid is a weak acid that ionises only partly, so its hydrogen ion concentration is far below <InlineMath math="0.0010\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> and its pH is above <InlineMath math="3" />. So P has a pH of <InlineMath math="3" />, and the pH of Q is higher than <InlineMath math="3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A pH of <InlineMath math="3" /> for both assumes ethanoic acid ionises completely, but **a weak acid ionises only partly**.
- A pH of <InlineMath math="1" /> is the pH of the hydrochloric acid before dilution.
- A pH of <InlineMath math="4" /> assumes the concentration falls <InlineMath math="1000" /> times, but the volume rises only <InlineMath math="100" /> times.

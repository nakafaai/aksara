# Heat released, then per mole of water

The heat of reaction warms **the whole mixture**, which is <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />, or <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />. The heat of neutralisation is defined per mole of water formed, and each mole of acid with a mole of base forms one mole of water.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
Q&=mc\theta=(100)(4.2)(6.5)=2730\text{ J}\\
n(\text{H}_2\text{O})&=1.0\times\frac{50}{1000}=0.050\text{ mol}\\
\Delta H&=-\frac{2730}{0.050}=-54{,}600\text{ J mol}^{-1}=-54.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

On an energy level diagram, the products sit <InlineMath math="54.6\text{ kJ}" /> below the reactants for every mole of water formed.

The sign is negative because the temperature rises, which means the reaction is exothermic. So the heat of neutralisation is <InlineMath math="-54.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-27.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> uses a mass of only <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" />, but **both solutions** are heated.
- <InlineMath math="+54.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> uses a positive sign, but a reaction that raises the temperature is exothermic.
- <InlineMath math="-109.2\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> uses <InlineMath math="0.025\text{ mol}" /> of water, as if each mole of acid and base gave only half a mole of water.

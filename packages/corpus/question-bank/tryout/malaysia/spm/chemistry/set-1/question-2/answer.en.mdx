# Isotope abundance, then the chance of a pair

Step one is the abundance. Let the fraction of chlorine-35 be <InlineMath math="x" />, so the fraction of chlorine-37 is <InlineMath math="1-x" /> and the average mass must be <InlineMath math="35.5" />. Step two: a molecule of mass <InlineMath math="72" /> has one atom of mass <InlineMath math="35" /> and one of mass <InlineMath math="37" />, and this pair **can form in two orders**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
35x+37(1-x)&=35.5\ \Rightarrow\ x=0.75\\
P(72)&=2(0.75)(0.25)=0.375\\
N&=0.375\times6.02\times10^{23}\approx2.26\times10^{23}
\end{aligned}" />

The chart shows the three kinds of molecule side by side. The bar for mass <InlineMath math="72" /> is twice <InlineMath math="0.75\times0.25" />, because it counts both orders of the pair.

Check: the chance of a molecule of mass <InlineMath math="70" /> is <InlineMath math="0.5625" /> and of mass <InlineMath math="74" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0625" />, so the three add up to <InlineMath math="1" />. So there are about <InlineMath math="2.26\times10^{23}" /> molecules of mass <InlineMath math="72" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.13\times10^{23}" /> uses <InlineMath math="0.75\times0.25" /> without the factor <InlineMath math="2" />, but **the <InlineMath math="35" /> atom can be in either position** of the pair.
- <InlineMath math="3.39\times10^{23}" /> is the number of molecules of mass <InlineMath math="70" />, made of two chlorine-35 atoms.
- <InlineMath math="1.51\times10^{23}" /> uses only the abundance of chlorine-37, <InlineMath math="0.25" />, as if it were the fraction of molecules.

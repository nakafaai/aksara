# Two mole ratios decide it

A Group <InlineMath math="1" /> metal reacts with water in the ratio <InlineMath math="2\text{X}:1\text{H}_2" />, and sulfuric acid is diprotic, so **one mole of acid neutralises two moles of base**.

The diagram chains the two ratios. The first arrow doubles the moles, and the acid step halves them again.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2\text{X}+2\text{H}_2\text{O}&\rightarrow2\text{XOH}+\text{H}_2\\
n_{\text{H}_2}&=\frac{0.240}{24}=0.010\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ n_{\text{X}}=0.020\text{ mol}\\
A_r&=\frac{0.46}{0.020}=23\\
\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4+2\text{XOH}&\rightarrow\text{X}_2\text{SO}_4+2\text{H}_2\text{O}\\
n_{\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4}&=\frac{0.020}{2}=0.010\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ V=\frac{0.010}{0.10}=0.100\text{ dm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

So the <InlineMath math="A_r" /> of X is <InlineMath math="23" /> and <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of sulfuric acid is needed.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="23" /> and <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" /> treats sulfuric acid as monoprotic, but **each molecule releases two hydrogen ions**.
- <InlineMath math="46" /> and <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> sets the moles of X equal to the moles of hydrogen, which halves both the moles of X and of base.
- <InlineMath math="46" /> and <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> makes both mistakes at once, equal moles of X and hydrogen and a monoprotic acid.

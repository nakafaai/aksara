# The mole ratio from the equation

The equation shows that **one dichromate ion oxidises six iron(II) ions**, so the moles of dichromate are one sixth of the moles of iron(II). Find the moles of iron(II) first, then divide by six.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{Fe}^{2+})&=0.12\times\frac{25.0}{1000}=0.0030\text{ mol}\\
n(\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-})&=\frac{0.0030}{6}=0.00050\text{ mol}\\
V&=\frac{0.00050}{0.020}=0.025\text{ dm}^3=25.0\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

The diagram shows what each ion turns into. The electrons that six iron(II) ions give up are exactly what one dichromate ion needs.

For the colour, the orange dichromate ion is reduced to the green <InlineMath math="\text{Cr}^{3+}" /> ion, as the oxidation number of chromium falls from <InlineMath math="+6" /> to <InlineMath math="+3" />. So <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of potassium dichromate(VI) solution is needed, and its colour changes from orange to green.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="150\text{ cm}^3" /> multiplies by six, but **less dichromate is needed**, not more.
- Green to orange reverses the reaction, but the orange dichromate is reduced to green chromium(III) ions.
- <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> uses a <InlineMath math="1:3" /> ratio, as if one dichromate ion gave only one <InlineMath math="\text{Cr}^{3+}" /> ion.

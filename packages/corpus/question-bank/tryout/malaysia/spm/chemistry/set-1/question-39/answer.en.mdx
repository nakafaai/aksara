# Which reactant runs out first

Both amounts are given, so **find the limiting reactant** before working out the soap. One mole of fat needs three moles of sodium hydroxide.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{fat})&=\frac{8.9}{890}=0.010\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{needs }0.030\text{ mol NaOH}\\
n(\text{NaOH})&=1.0\times\frac{20}{1000}=0.020\text{ mol}<0.030
\end{aligned}" />

The chart sets the sodium hydroxide needed beside what is there. The bar for what is there is shorter, so sodium hydroxide runs out first.

  title={<>Sodium hydroxide needed and available</>}
  description={<>Using up all <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol}" /> of fat would need <InlineMath math="0.030\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide, but only <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ mol}" /> is available. So <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ mol}" /> of sodium stearate forms.</>}
  bars="apart"
  categories={["Needed for all the fat", "Available", "Sodium stearate formed"]}
  series={[{ label: "Amount", mark: "bar", values: [(3 * 8.9) / 890, (1.0 * 20) / 1000, (1.0 * 20) / 1000] }]}
  valueLabel="Amount (mol)"
/>

Sodium hydroxide runs out first. Each mole of sodium hydroxide gives one mole of sodium stearate, so <InlineMath math="0.020" /> mol of soap forms.

<BlockMath math="m=0.020\times306=6.12\text{ g}" />

So the maximum mass of sodium stearate is <InlineMath math="6.12\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="9.18\text{ g}" /> works from the fat, but **the sodium hydroxide runs out first**.
- <InlineMath math="2.04\text{ g}" /> divides the moles of sodium hydroxide by three, but sodium hydroxide and soap are in a <InlineMath math="1:1" /> ratio.
- <InlineMath math="3.06\text{ g}" /> uses a <InlineMath math="1:1" /> ratio between fat and soap.

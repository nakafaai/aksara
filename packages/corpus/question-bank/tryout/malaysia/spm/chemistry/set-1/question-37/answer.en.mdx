# Cross-links and the charge on latex

In vulcanisation, sulfur atoms join the rubber chains with **cross-links**, so statement I is correct. The cross-links stop the chains slipping past one another, so the rubber returns to its shape after stretching and is more elastic, and statement II is correct. The cross-links also use up double bonds that oxygen attacks easily and stop the rubber softening when heated, so statement III is correct.

Statement IV is wrong. The rubber particles in latex are coated with a negatively charged protein layer, so they repel one another. Acid neutralises that charge and makes latex coagulate, while ammonia, a base, keeps the negative charge and prevents coagulation. So I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only misses statement III, but the cross-links make the rubber **more resistant to heat and oxidation**.
- I, II and IV accepts statement IV, but ammonia prevents latex from coagulating.
- II, III and IV rejects the correct statement I and accepts the wrong statement IV.

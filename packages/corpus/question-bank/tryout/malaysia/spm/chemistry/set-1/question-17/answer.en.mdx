# Rate and final amount answered separately

The initial rate depends on surface area and concentration. The acid concentration is the same, but powder has **a much larger surface area** than chips, so the initial rate in experiment II is higher.

The final volume of gas depends on the reactant that runs out first. Work out the moles of both reactants in each experiment.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{CaCO}_3)&=\frac{5.0}{100}=0.050\text{ mol}\ \text{(both)}\\
\text{I: }n(\text{HCl})&=0.050\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{HCl limiting},\ n(\text{CO}_2)=0.025\text{ mol}\\
\text{II: }n(\text{HCl})&=0.10\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{both used up},\ n(\text{CO}_2)=0.050\text{ mol}
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- The same final volume assumes calcium carbonate is always limiting, but in experiment I **the acid runs out first**.
- The same initial rate forgets that powder has a larger surface area.
- Half the volume inverts the ratio of the moles of carbon dioxide in the two experiments.

# Electron arrangement sets the ion charges

W has the arrangement <InlineMath math="2.8.2" /> and loses two electrons to form <InlineMath math="\text{W}^{2+}" />. V has the arrangement <InlineMath math="2.5" /> and gains three electrons to form <InlineMath math="\text{V}^{3-}" />. To balance the charges, **the formula is <InlineMath math="\text{W}_3\text{V}_2" />**.

The diagram balances the electrons. Three W atoms give away six electrons, and two V atoms take exactly those six.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n_\text{W}&=\frac{0.72}{24}=0.030\text{ mol}\\
n_{\text{W}_3\text{V}_2}&=\frac{0.030}{3}=0.010\text{ mol},\quad M_r=3(24)+2(14)=100\\
m&=0.010\times100=1.00\text{ g}\\
n_{e^-}&=2\times0.030=0.060\text{ mol}
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ g}" /> of compound forms and <InlineMath math="0.060" /> mol of electrons are transferred.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ g}" /> with <InlineMath math="0.030\text{ mol}" /> counts one electron per W atom, but **each W atom loses two electrons**.
- <InlineMath math="1.14\text{ g}" /> uses the formula WV, but charges of <InlineMath math="2+" /> and <InlineMath math="3-" /> balance only in <InlineMath math="\text{W}_3\text{V}_2" />.
- <InlineMath math="3.00\text{ g}" /> with <InlineMath math="0.18\text{ mol}" /> takes <InlineMath math="0.030" /> mol as the moles of compound, but it is the moles of W atoms.

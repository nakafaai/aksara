# Gas left over and gas formed

At the same temperature and pressure, gas volumes are in the same ratio as moles. Three things must be counted: the oxygen used, the oxygen **left over** because it was in excess, and the carbon dioxide formed. The water is now liquid, so it does not count as gas.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{O}_2\text{ used}&=5\times20=100\text{ cm}^3,\quad\text{O}_2\text{ left}=150-100=50\text{ cm}^3\\
\text{CO}_2\text{ formed}&=3\times20=60\text{ cm}^3\\
\text{gas left}&=50+60=110\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

So the total volume of gas left is <InlineMath math="110\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> counts only the carbon dioxide, but **the excess oxygen is still there**.
- <InlineMath math="190\text{ cm}^3" /> also counts <InlineMath math="80\text{ cm}^3" /> of steam, but the water has condensed to liquid.
- <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> counts only the leftover oxygen and forgets the carbon dioxide.

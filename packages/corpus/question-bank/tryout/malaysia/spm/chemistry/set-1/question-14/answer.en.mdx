# The hydrogen ions left over

The acid and base neutralise each other in a <InlineMath math="1:1" /> ratio, so compare their moles. The pH is set by **the hydrogen ions left over**, and their concentration is worked out in the volume of the mixture.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{H}^+)&=0.10\times0.050=0.0050\text{ mol}\\
n(\text{OH}^-)&=0.080\times0.050=0.0040\text{ mol}\\
[\text{H}^+]&=\frac{0.0050-0.0040}{0.100}=0.010\text{ mol dm}^{-3}\\
\text{pH}&=-\log(0.010)=2.0
\end{aligned}" />

The mixture is still acidic, because the acid is slightly in excess. So the pH of the mixture is <InlineMath math="2.0" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.7" /> divides the leftover hydrogen ions by <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> only, but **the mixture has a volume of <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />**.
- <InlineMath math="1.0" /> is the pH of the original hydrochloric acid, before it was mixed with the base.
- <InlineMath math="7.0" /> assumes the mixture is neutral, but there are more moles of acid than of base.

# The alkane formula first, then arrange the chain in order

Alkanes follow <InlineMath math="\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}" />, so the relative molecular mass is <InlineMath math="14n+2" />.

<BlockMath math="14n+2=86\ \Rightarrow\ n=6\ \Rightarrow\ \text{C}_6\text{H}_{14}" />

Arrange the six carbon atoms by the length of the longest chain, so nothing is missed or counted twice. A six-carbon chain gives hexane. A five-carbon chain with one methyl branch gives 2-methylpentane and 3-methylpentane. A four-carbon chain with two methyl branches gives 2,2-dimethylbutane and 2,3-dimethylbutane. **A branch at the end of the chain, or numbering from the other end, gives no new isomer**. So the alkane has <InlineMath math="5" /> structural isomers.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4" /> usually misses 3-methylpentane or 2,3-dimethylbutane, because **not every branch position was tried**.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> counts one structure twice, for example 4-methylpentane, which is 2-methylpentane numbered from the other end.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> counts only one structure for each chain length.

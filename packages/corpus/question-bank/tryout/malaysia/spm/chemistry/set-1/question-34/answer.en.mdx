# Heat absorbed by the water, then per mole of ethanol

The heat from combustion raises the water temperature by <InlineMath math="43.0-28.0=15.0^\circ\text{C}" />. The heat of combustion is defined **per mole of fuel**, so the heat is divided by the moles of ethanol, not its mass.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
Q&=(200)(4.2)(15.0)=12{,}600\text{ J}=12.6\text{ kJ}\\
n&=\frac{0.46}{46}=0.010\text{ mol}\\
\Delta H&=-\frac{12.6}{0.010}=-1260\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

The sign is negative because combustion releases heat to the water. So the heat of combustion of ethanol is <InlineMath math="-1260\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-12.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> is the total heat, **not yet divided by the moles of ethanol**.
- <InlineMath math="+1260\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> uses a positive sign, but combustion is exothermic.
- <InlineMath math="-27.4\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides the heat by the mass of <InlineMath math="0.46\text{ g}" />, giving a value per gram, not per mole.

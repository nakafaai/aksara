# Find the limiting reactant

Both amounts are given, so first decide **which reactant runs out first**. The hydrogen produced is set by that reactant, not by the one in excess.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{Mg})&=\frac{2.4}{24}=0.10\text{ mol}\\
n(\text{HCl})&=2.0\times\frac{50}{1000}=0.10\text{ mol}
\end{aligned}" />

<InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium needs <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ mol}" /> of HCl, but only <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> is present. So HCl is the limiting reactant and some magnesium is left over.

<BlockMath math="n(\text{H}_2)=\tfrac12\times0.10=0.050\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ V=0.050\times24=1.2\text{ dm}^3" />

So the maximum volume of hydrogen is <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ dm}^3" /> works from the magnesium, but **the magnesium is in excess** and the acid runs out first.
- <InlineMath math="1.12\text{ dm}^3" /> uses a molar volume of <InlineMath math="22.4\text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}" />, but the gas is measured at room conditions.
- <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ dm}^3" /> halves the correct amount of hydrogen a second time.

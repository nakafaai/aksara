# A lower yield means more starting material

Ethene and ethanol are in a <InlineMath math="1:1" /> ratio. But only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of the ethene turns into ethanol, so to get a given amount of ethanol **more ethene is needed** than the theoretical amount.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH})&=\frac{23}{46}=0.50\text{ mol}\\
n(\text{C}_2\text{H}_4)&=\frac{0.50}{0.80}=0.625\text{ mol}\\
V&=0.625\times24=15\text{ dm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

The chart shows where the ethene goes. Only the part that turns into ethanol counts, so the ethene fed in must be more than the ethanol it makes.

Check: <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of <InlineMath math="0.625" /> mol is <InlineMath math="0.50" /> mol of ethanol, exactly <InlineMath math="23\text{ g}" />. So <InlineMath math="15\text{ dm}^3" /> of ethene is needed.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12\text{ dm}^3" /> ignores the percentage yield, as if all the ethene turned into ethanol.
- <InlineMath math="9.6\text{ dm}^3" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="0.80" />, but **a lower yield needs more starting material**, not less.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ dm}^3" /> uses a <InlineMath math="2:1" /> ratio between ethene and ethanol, but the ratio is <InlineMath math="1:1" />.

# Limiting reagent and spectator ions

The precipitate is set by **the reagent with fewer moles**. Chloride ions do not precipitate, so all of them stay in the filtrate, whose volume is now that of both solutions together.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n_{\text{Ba}^{2+}}&=0.20\times0.050=0.010\text{ mol}\\
n_{\text{SO}_4^{2-}}&=0.25\times0.030=0.0075\text{ mol}\ \text{(limiting)}\\
m_{\text{BaSO}_4}&=0.0075\times233\approx1.75\text{ g}\\
n_{\text{Cl}^-}&=2\times0.010=0.020\text{ mol}\\
[\text{Cl}^-]&=\frac{0.020}{0.080}=0.25\text{ mol dm}^{-3}
\end{aligned}" />

So the precipitate is <InlineMath math="1.75\text{ g}" /> and the chloride ion concentration is <InlineMath math="0.25\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.33\text{ g}" /> takes barium as limiting, but **the sulfate ions run out first**.
- <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> divides the chloride by <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> only, but the filtrate has a volume of <InlineMath math="80\text{ cm}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.125\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> forgets that each <InlineMath math="\text{BaCl}_2" /> carries two chloride ions.

# Narrowing down the cation, then the anion

Start with the cation. A white precipitate that dissolves in excess sodium hydroxide points to an amphoteric ion: <InlineMath math="\text{Al}^{3+}" />, <InlineMath math="\text{Zn}^{2+}" /> or <InlineMath math="\text{Pb}^{2+}" />. A white precipitate that **does not dissolve in excess ammonia** rules out <InlineMath math="\text{Zn}^{2+}" />, because the zinc precipitate dissolves in excess ammonia. No yellow precipitate with potassium iodide rules out <InlineMath math="\text{Pb}^{2+}" />. So the cation is <InlineMath math="\text{Al}^{3+}" />.

For the anion, a white precipitate with barium chloride in acid is barium sulfate, so the anion is <InlineMath math="\text{SO}_4^{2-}" />. The hydrochloric acid goes in first to remove carbonate ions, which could also give a white precipitate. So salt X is aluminium sulfate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Zinc sulfate is ruled out because zinc hydroxide **dissolves in excess ammonia**.
- Lead(II) nitrate is ruled out because potassium iodide would give a yellow precipitate, and nitrate gives no precipitate with barium chloride.
- Aluminium chloride has the right cation, but chloride ions give no precipitate with barium chloride.

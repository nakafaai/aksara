# The terminals, then the mass deposited

Magnesium has the more negative <InlineMath math="E^\circ" />, so it is oxidised and becomes the negative terminal, while silver is the positive terminal. At the positive terminal **silver ions gain electrons and deposit** as silver metal, so the silver electrode gets heavier. Each <InlineMath math="\text{Ag}^+" /> ion needs only one electron.

Put both half-cells on one <InlineMath math="E^\circ" /> scale. The lower one, magnesium, is the negative terminal, and the gap between them is the cell voltage.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.58\text{ V}" /> adds the two values as they stand, losing the negative sign of magnesium.
- <InlineMath math="1.08\text{ g}" /> assumes each silver ion needs two electrons, but **the silver ion's charge is only <InlineMath math="+1" />**.
- A loss of mass happens at the magnesium electrode, which dissolves, not at the silver electrode, where metal deposits.

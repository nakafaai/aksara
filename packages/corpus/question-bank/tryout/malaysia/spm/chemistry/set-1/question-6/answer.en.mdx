# Concentration after dilution, then the titration

Dilution does not change the number of moles of solute, so <InlineMath math="M_1V_1=M_2V_2" />. After that, watch the reaction ratio: **one mole of sulfuric acid neutralises two moles of sodium hydroxide**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
M_2&=\frac{(2.0)(25.0)}{500}=0.10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}\\
n(\text{NaOH})&=0.10\times\frac{20.0}{1000}=0.0020\text{ mol}\\
n(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4)&=\tfrac12\times0.0020=0.0010\text{ mol}\\
V&=\frac{0.0010}{0.050}=0.020\text{ dm}^3=20.0\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

So the volume of sulfuric acid needed is <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" /> uses a <InlineMath math="1:1" /> ratio, but **sulfuric acid is diprotic**, so half as much is enough.
- <InlineMath math="400\text{ cm}^3" /> uses the starting concentration of <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> and forgets the dilution.
- <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> inverts the ratio and halves once more.

# Only the alkene reacts with bromine

In the dark, hexane does not react with bromine, because **substitution in alkanes needs ultraviolet light**. All the bromine is used by hex-1-ene through addition across its one double bond, in a <InlineMath math="1:1" /> ratio.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{Br}_2)&=\frac{8.0}{160}=0.050\text{ mol}=n(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12})\\
m(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12})&=0.050\times84=4.2\text{ g}\\
\text{percentage}&=\frac{4.2}{10.0}\times100\%=42\%
\end{aligned}" />

The chart shows the two parts of the mixture and where the bromine goes. All of it goes to the hex-1-ene and none to the hexane.

So the mixture contains <InlineMath math="42\%" /> hex-1-ene by mass.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="58\%" /> is the percentage of hexane, the part of the mixture that does not react.
- <InlineMath math="84\%" /> uses <InlineMath math="80" /> as the molar mass of bromine, but **bromine exists as <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" /> molecules**.
- <InlineMath math="80\%" /> compares the mass of bromine directly with the mass of the mixture, without going through moles.

# The same charge passes through both cells

In a series circuit, **the same number of electrons passes through both cells**. Find the moles of electrons from the silver, then use them for the copper at the cathode and the oxygen at the carbon anode.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{Ag}^++e^-&\rightarrow\text{Ag}:\quad n_{e^-}=\frac{2.16}{108}=0.020\text{ mol}\\
\text{Cu}^{2+}+2e^-&\rightarrow\text{Cu}:\quad m=\frac{0.020}{2}\times64=0.64\text{ g}\\
4\text{OH}^-&\rightarrow\text{O}_2+2\text{H}_2\text{O}+4e^-\\
V_{\text{O}_2}&=\frac{0.020}{4}\times24{,}000=120\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

So the cathode of the second cell gains <InlineMath math="0.64\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen forms.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> assumes each oxygen molecule needs only two electrons, but **forming it releases four electrons**.
- <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> assumes each copper ion needs only one electron, but the ion carries a <InlineMath math="2+" /> charge.
- <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> makes both of those mistakes at once.

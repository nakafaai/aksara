# The oxygen lost decides the formula and the hydrogen

The mass lost is oxygen. **Each oxygen atom is taken by one hydrogen molecule** to form water, so the moles of hydrogen equal the moles of oxygen atoms.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m_\text{O}&=2.32-1.68=0.64\text{ g}\ \Rightarrow\ n_\text{O}=\frac{0.64}{16}=0.040\text{ mol}\\
n_\text{Fe}&=\frac{1.68}{56}=0.030\text{ mol}\\
\text{Fe}:\text{O}&=0.030:0.040=3:4\ \Rightarrow\ \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4\\
\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4+4\text{H}_2&\rightarrow3\text{Fe}+4\text{H}_2\text{O}\\
V_{\text{H}_2}&=0.040\times24=0.96\text{ dm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

So the formula is <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4" /> and <InlineMath math="0.96\text{ dm}^3" /> of hydrogen reacts.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4" /> with <InlineMath math="0.72\text{ dm}^3" /> sets the moles of hydrogen equal to the moles of iron, but **hydrogen reacts with the oxygen**, not with the iron.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_2" /> with <InlineMath math="0.48\text{ dm}^3" /> divides the oxygen mass by <InlineMath math="32" />, but an empirical formula counts oxygen atoms.
- <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_4\text{O}_3" /> inverts the mole ratio of iron to oxygen.

# Halogen reactivity and displacement

A halogen reacts by **gaining one electron**. Down the group there are more shells, the atomic radius grows, and the nucleus attracts an incoming electron less strongly. So the reactivity falls, which makes statements I and II correct.

The diagram puts the halogens in order of reactivity. A halogen displaces only the halogens below it, so follow the arrows for statements III and IV.

A more reactive halogen can displace a less reactive one from a solution of its salt. Chlorine is more reactive than iodine, so chlorine displaces iodine from potassium iodide and the solution turns brown, which makes statement IV correct. Iodine, on the other hand, is less reactive than bromine, so it cannot displace bromine, and statement III is wrong. So I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only misses statement IV, but chlorine **does displace iodine**.
- II, III and IV accepts statement III and rejects statement I, when the reverse is true for both.
- I, II, III and IV accepts statement III, but a less reactive halogen cannot displace a more reactive one.

# From the titration to the molar mass of the crystals

The titration gives the moles of sodium carbonate in one portion. Multiply by <InlineMath math="10" /> because **the portion is only a tenth of the solution**, then find the <InlineMath math="M_r" /> of the crystals and set aside the <InlineMath math="\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3" /> part.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n_{\text{HCl}}&=0.10\times0.0200=0.0020\text{ mol}\\
n_{\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3}\ \text{in portion}&=\frac{0.0020}{2}=0.0010\text{ mol}\\
\text{in }250\text{ cm}^3&=0.0010\times10=0.010\text{ mol}\\
M_r&=\frac{2.86}{0.010}=286\\
106+18x&=286\ \Rightarrow\ x=10
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="x=10" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2" /> uses a <InlineMath math="1:1" /> ratio, which makes the <InlineMath math="M_r" /> <InlineMath math="143" />, but **one mole of carbonate reacts with two moles of acid**.
- <InlineMath math="16" /> divides <InlineMath math="286" /> directly by <InlineMath math="18" /> without subtracting the <InlineMath math="106" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3" />.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> makes both mistakes, a <InlineMath math="1:1" /> ratio and no subtraction of <InlineMath math="106" />.

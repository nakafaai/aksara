# Which metal becomes the anode

The more electropositive metal becomes the anode and is oxidised, while the other metal is where oxygen and water are reduced to <InlineMath math="\text{OH}^-" /> ions. **Magnesium is more electropositive than iron**, so on nail Q it is the magnesium that is sacrificed.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{anode (magnesium): }&\text{Mg}\rightarrow\text{Mg}^{2+}+2e^-\\
\text{cathode (iron): }&\text{O}_2+2\text{H}_2\text{O}+4e^-\rightarrow4\text{OH}^-
\end{aligned}" />

Iron is not oxidised, so there are no <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}^{2+}" /> ions and no dark blue colour. <InlineMath math="\text{OH}^-" /> ions form on the iron surface and turn the phenolphthalein pink. <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" /> ions give no colour with either indicator. So around nail Q there is a pink colour around the iron nail and no dark blue colour.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Dark blue around the iron nail is what you see at **nail P**, because copper is less electropositive, so the iron rusts.
- Dark blue around the magnesium is wrong, because the dark blue colour appears only with <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}^{2+}" /> ions, not <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" />.
- Magnesium does not stop the reactions but takes over as the anode, so <InlineMath math="\text{OH}^-" /> ions still form.

# The new concentration, then the time

First, find the concentration of sodium thiosulfate after dilution. The total volume becomes <InlineMath math="20+30=50\text{ cm}^3" />, the same as in experiment I, so the depth of liquid above mark X is the same too. Second, since <InlineMath math="\tfrac1t" /> is proportional to concentration, **the time is inversely proportional to concentration**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
M_2&=\frac{(0.20)(20)}{50}=0.080\text{ mol dm}^{-3}\\
t_2&=t_1\cdot\frac{M_1}{M_2}=20\times\frac{0.20}{0.080}=50\text{ s}
\end{aligned}" />

A more dilute solution reacts more slowly, so the time should be longer. So mark X disappears after <InlineMath math="50\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ s}" /> treats the time as proportional to concentration, but **a dilute solution reacts more slowly**.
- <InlineMath math="20\text{ s}" /> forgets the dilution.
- <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" /> divides the thiosulfate volume by the water volume only, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{20}{30}" />, instead of the total volume.

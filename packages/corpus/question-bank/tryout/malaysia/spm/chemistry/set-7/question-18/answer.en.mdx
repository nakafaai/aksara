# Read the rate from the graph, make the hydroxide, dissolve only what the water holds, then titrate a quarter of that

In the first <InlineMath math="20\text{ s}" /> the hydrogen reaches <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />, so the average rate is <InlineMath math="120\div20=6.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />. The calcium is <InlineMath math="0.40\div40=0.0100\text{ mol}" />, which forms <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of calcium hydroxide, <InlineMath math="0.74\text{ g}" />. **The water dissolves only <InlineMath math="0.17\text{ g}" />**, so <InlineMath math="0.57\text{ g}" /> stays as solid. The <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of filtrate holds a quarter of the dissolved hydroxide, <InlineMath math="0.0425\div74=5.74\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />. **Each calcium hydroxide takes two hydrochloric acid**, <InlineMath math="1.15\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="23.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.74\text{ g}" /> leaves all the calcium hydroxide as solid. **The water dissolves <InlineMath math="0.17\text{ g}" /> of it**.
- <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> by <InlineMath math="10\text{ s}" />, but the first <InlineMath math="20\text{ s}" /> is asked for.
- <InlineMath math="11.5\text{ cm}^3" /> takes one hydrochloric acid per calcium hydroxide, but each hydroxide neutralises two.

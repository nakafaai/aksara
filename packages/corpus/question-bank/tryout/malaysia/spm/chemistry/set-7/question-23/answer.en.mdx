# The volume drop is the oxygen, and the rust formula sets how much iron that oxygen takes

The gas falls by <InlineMath math="21.0\text{ cm}^3" />, the oxygen in the air, <InlineMath math="8.75\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />. In <InlineMath math="4\text{Fe}+3\text{O}_2\rightarrow2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3" /> **four iron atoms take three oxygen molecules**, so the iron rusted is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4}{3}" /> of the oxygen. **The volume stops falling because the oxygen has run out**, while most of the steel wool is still bright: rusting needs both water and oxygen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.0490\text{ g}" /> rusts one iron atom per oxygen molecule. **The equation uses four for every three**.
- <InlineMath math="0.0980\text{ g}" /> rusts two iron atoms per oxygen molecule.
- The steel wool is in excess. **The oxygen runs out first**, which is why the volume steadies at <InlineMath math="79\%" /> of the air.

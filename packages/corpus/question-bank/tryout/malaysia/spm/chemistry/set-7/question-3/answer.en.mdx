# The arrangement names sulfur, the molecule holds eight atoms, and each atom makes its own dioxide

An ion <InlineMath math="\text{X}^{2-}" /> with eighteen electrons has sixteen protons, so X is sulfur. **Each molecule is a ring of eight atoms**, <InlineMath math="256" /> per mole, so <InlineMath math="1.60\text{ g}" /> is only <InlineMath math="0.00625\text{ mol}" /> of molecules but <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of atoms. Burning gives one sulfur dioxide molecule per sulfur atom.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.01\times10^{22}" /> is the number of sulfur atoms. **Each molecule holds eight**.
- <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ dm}^3" /> makes one dioxide per <InlineMath math="\text{S}_8" /> molecule. **Each of its eight atoms makes one**.
- <InlineMath math="1.51\times10^{22}" /> treats sulfur as <InlineMath math="\text{S}_2" /> molecules.

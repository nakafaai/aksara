# Each formula unit carries two ammonium ions, and the hydroxide runs out before they do

The <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of ammonium sulfate holds <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of ammonium ions, but there are only <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of hydroxide ions. **The alkali runs out first**, so it sets the ammonia, and <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of ammonium ions are left. Two ammonium ions belong to each formula unit, so that is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of ammonium sulfate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> lets every ammonium ion react. **The <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of hydroxide is not enough**.
- <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ dm}^3" /> counts one ammonium ion per formula unit. **Each unit holds two**.
- <InlineMath math="1.32\text{ g}" /> treats each leftover ammonium ion as a whole formula unit, but two ions make one unit.

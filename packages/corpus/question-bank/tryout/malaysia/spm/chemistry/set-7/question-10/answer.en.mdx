# Find X from the oxygen's share of the mass, then let the amphoteric oxide take the base and the acid in their own ratios

In <InlineMath math="\text{X}_2\text{O}_3" /> the <InlineMath math="48\text{ g}" /> of oxygen is <InlineMath math="47.1\%" /> of the mass, so the formula mass is <InlineMath math="48\div0.471=102" /> and X is <InlineMath math="(102-48)\div2=27" />, aluminium. The sample is <InlineMath math="1.02\div102=0.0100\text{ mol}" />. The alkali takes two per oxide, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" />. **Aluminium oxide is amphoteric, so it also dissolves in acid**, <InlineMath math="\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3+6\text{HCl}\rightarrow2\text{AlCl}_3+3\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" /> of acid, <InlineMath math="30.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60.0\text{ cm}^3" /> is the volume of acid at <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. **The acid is <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />**, so half that volume is enough.
- Iron(III) oxide is <InlineMath math="70\%" /> iron, not <InlineMath math="52.9\%" />, and it does not dissolve in sodium hydroxide.
- <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> takes one sodium hydroxide per oxide, but the equation uses two.

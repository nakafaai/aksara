# Each sucrose molecule becomes two sugar molecules, so the ethanol and gas double twice

The sucrose is <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />. **Splitting it first gives <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of simple sugar**, and each sugar molecule then gives two ethanol and two carbon dioxide molecules. So one sucrose molecule ends as four of each, <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol and <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="9.2\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" /> ferment the sucrose as if it were one sugar molecule. **It splits into two first**.
- <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per two ethanol molecules, but the fermentation gives one of each.
- <InlineMath math="36.8\text{ g}" /> doubles once more than the equations allow.

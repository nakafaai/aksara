# Each formula unit of the oxide holds two aluminium atoms, so it costs six electrons

The charge is <InlineMath math="1.50\times1800=2700\text{ C}" />, or <InlineMath math="0.0280\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. Three electrons free one aluminium ion, so <InlineMath math="0.00933\text{ mol}" /> of aluminium is oxidised. **Each <InlineMath math="\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3" /> holds two aluminium ions**, which is six electrons per formula unit and <InlineMath math="0.00466\text{ mol}" /> of oxide.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.951\text{ g}" /> charges three electrons per formula unit. **Each unit has two aluminium atoms and needs six**.
- <InlineMath math="0.252\text{ g}" /> is the aluminium that was oxidised, not the oxide that formed.
- <InlineMath math="1.43\text{ g}" /> charges two electrons per formula unit, as if each oxide ion took the charge.

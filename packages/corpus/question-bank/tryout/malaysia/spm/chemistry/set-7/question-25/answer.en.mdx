# Turn the charge into electrons, three per aluminium atom, build the oxide, then see that the frame gains only the oxygen

The charge is <InlineMath math="1.50\times1800=2700\text{ C}" />, <InlineMath math="2700\div96\,500=0.0280\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. **Each aluminium atom loses three**, so <InlineMath math="9.33\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of aluminium joins the layer, which is <InlineMath math="4.66\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of oxide, <InlineMath math="4.66\times10^{-3}\times102=0.476\text{ g}" />. **That aluminium was already part of the frame**, so the frame gains only the oxygen, <InlineMath math="3\times4.66\times10^{-3}\times16=0.224\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.951\text{ g}" /> gives each aluminium atom only one and a half electrons. **Each loses three**.
- <InlineMath math="0.252\text{ g}" /> is the aluminium in the layer, not the whole oxide.
- An increase of <InlineMath math="0.476\text{ g}" /> adds the whole oxide, but its aluminium was already in the frame.

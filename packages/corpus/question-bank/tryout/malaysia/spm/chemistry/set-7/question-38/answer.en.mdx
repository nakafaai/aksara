# Each unit loses a water molecule as it joins, so more acid goes in than polymer comes out

One kilogram of PLA is <InlineMath math="13.9\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_4\text{O}_2" /> units. **Each unit came from one lactic acid molecule, <InlineMath math="90" /> per mole, that lost a water molecule**, so <InlineMath math="1.25\text{ kg}" /> of acid is needed. Each unit holds three carbon atoms, so composting releases three carbon dioxide molecules per unit. The ester links in PLA can be broken by water and microbes, which is why it is biodegradable.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ kg}" /> of acid ignores the water lost. **Each unit is lighter than the acid it came from**.
- <InlineMath math="0.611\text{ kg}" /> gives one carbon dioxide per unit. **Each unit holds three carbon atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="0.80\text{ kg}" /> scales the wrong way, as if the polymer were heavier than the acid.

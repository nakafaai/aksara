# Count repeating units, add back the water each one lost, then burn each unit by its balanced equation

The repeating unit has <InlineMath math="M_r=72" />, so the batch is <InlineMath math="1000\div72=13.9\text{ mol}" /> of units. **Each unit came from one lactic acid that lost a water molecule**, so the acid needed is <InlineMath math="13.9\times90=1.25\text{ kg}" />. Composting follows <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_4\text{O}_2+3\text{O}_2\rightarrow3\text{CO}_2+2\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, **three carbon dioxide and three oxygen per unit**: <InlineMath math="41.7\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="1.83\text{ kg}" />, and <InlineMath math="41.7\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen, <InlineMath math="1000\text{ dm}^3=1.00\text{ m}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ kg}" /> of lactic acid forgets the water lost. **Each unit is lighter than the acid that made it**.
- <InlineMath math="0.611\text{ kg}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.333\text{ m}^3" /> give one carbon dioxide per unit, but each unit has three carbon atoms.
- <InlineMath math="0.80\text{ kg}" /> subtracts the water instead of adding it back, but the acid is heavier than the polymer it makes.

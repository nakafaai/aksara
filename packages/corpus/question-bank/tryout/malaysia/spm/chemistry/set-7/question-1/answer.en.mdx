# Every mass the student needs is a difference of two readings, so the zero error cancels

The balance adds <InlineMath math="0.04\text{ g}" /> to every reading, so **each difference of two readings is already correct**: the sample is <InlineMath math="2.48\text{ g}" /> and the mass lost on heating is <InlineMath math="0.66\text{ g}" />. Only copper(II) carbonate changes, <InlineMath math="\text{CuCO}_3\rightarrow\text{CuO}+\text{CO}_2" />, so the loss is carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" />, from <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of carbonate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="76.2\%" /> subtracts the zero error from only one reading, which spoils the difference. **The error is in both readings and cancels**.
- <InlineMath math="26.6\%" /> is the carbon dioxide as a share of the sample, not the carbonate.
- <InlineMath math="73.4\%" /> compares the residue with the sample, but the residue is copper(II) oxide and the impurity, not the carbonate.

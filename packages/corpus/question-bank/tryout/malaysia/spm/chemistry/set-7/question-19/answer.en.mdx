# The acid's moles set the gas, and its concentration sets how fast the gas comes

**Both flasks hold the same <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen chloride**, and the marble is in excess, so both give the same <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" />. The second acid is half as concentrated, so its particles collide with the marble less often per second and the reaction starts more slowly, taking longer to finish.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> doubles the gas because the volume doubled. **The moles of acid are the same**.
- <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" /> halves the gas because the concentration halved, but the volume doubled too.
- A more dilute acid reacts more slowly, not faster.

# The density and the mass percentage give the concentration, and the heat of mixing sets the order

One cubic centimetre of the concentrated acid weighs <InlineMath math="1.84\text{ g}" />, of which <InlineMath math="1.80\text{ g}" /> is sulfuric acid, so it is <InlineMath math="18.4\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. **The moles of acid stay the same on dilution**: <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ mol}" /> is needed. Mixing releases a lot of heat, so **the acid goes into the water**, where the large amount of water absorbs it. Water poured onto the acid can boil and spatter.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Adding water to concentrated acid can make the first drops boil and spray acid. **Always add the acid to the water**.
- <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> treats the concentrated acid as <InlineMath math="10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. **Its density and percentage give <InlineMath math="18.4" />**.
- <InlineMath math="6.8\text{ cm}^3" /> halves the acid needed, as if the solution's concentration referred to hydrogen ions.

# The pure acid is only part of the concentrated liquid, and the diluted acid uses two hydroxide ions per molecule

The diluted acid needs <InlineMath math="0.25\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="24.5\text{ g}" /> of pure <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4" />. **That is only <InlineMath math="98\%" /> of the concentrated liquid**, so <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ g}" /> of it is needed, and its density turns that into <InlineMath math="13.6\text{ cm}^3" />. The <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> portion holds <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> of acid, and each molecule neutralises two hydroxide ions, so it needs <InlineMath math="0.025\text{ mol}" /> of alkali.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="31.3\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydroxide ion to each acid molecule. **Sulfuric acid is diprotic**.
- <InlineMath math="13.3\text{ cm}^3" /> treats the concentrated acid as pure and leaves out the <InlineMath math="2\%" /> water it contains.
- <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> takes the mass of concentrated acid as its volume and forgets the density.

# Only a double bond with the same groups on both sides gives a single alcohol

The straight chain can hold the double bond at the end, but-1-ene, or in the middle, but-2-ene, and the branched chain gives methylpropene: three isomers. **Steam can add the hydroxyl group to either carbon of the double bond**. Only in but-2-ene are both carbons alike, so both ways give butan-2-ol. The <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of alkene gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{OH}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- There are three alkene isomers, two straight-chain and one branched. **Methylpropene is the one often missed**.
- But-1-ene has its double bond at the end, so the hydroxyl group can join either the end carbon or the second carbon, giving two alcohols.
- Methylpropene's double bond joins two different carbons, so it can give two alcohols.

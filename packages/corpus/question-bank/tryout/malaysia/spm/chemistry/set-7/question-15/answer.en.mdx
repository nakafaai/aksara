# Sulfuric acid gives two hydrogen ions, and each hydrogen carbonate ion takes only one

The spill holds <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ mol}" /> of sulfuric acid, which is **diprotic, so it gives <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen ions**. A hydrogen carbonate ion takes one hydrogen ion and releases one carbon dioxide molecule, <InlineMath math="\text{HCO}_3^-+\text{H}^+\rightarrow\text{H}_2\text{O}+\text{CO}_2" />, so both the solid and the gas are <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ mol}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="252\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="72\text{ dm}^3" /> treat the acid as giving one hydrogen ion per molecule. **Sulfuric acid gives two**.
- <InlineMath math="72\text{ dm}^3" /> releases one carbon dioxide for every two hydrogen carbonate ions, but each releases one.
- <InlineMath math="318\text{ g}" /> is sodium carbonate, which takes two hydrogen ions per ion. **The spill is treated with the hydrogen carbonate**.

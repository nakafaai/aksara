# The least reactive metal is displaced first, and the magnesium runs out before the copper does

Silver is further below magnesium than copper, so **silver ions are reduced first**. Two silver ions take the electrons of one magnesium atom, so <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium frees all <InlineMath math="0.540\text{ g}" /> of silver. The remaining <InlineMath math="0.00750\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium frees <InlineMath math="0.00750\text{ mol}" /> of copper, <InlineMath math="0.480\text{ g}" />. **That is not all the copper**, so blue copper(II) ions remain.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.64\text{ g}" /> displaces copper first. **Silver ions are the stronger oxidising agent and react first**.
- <InlineMath math="1.18\text{ g}" /> displaces all the silver and all the copper, which would need <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium.
- The mass is right, but <InlineMath math="0.0025\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions are left, so the solution stays blue.

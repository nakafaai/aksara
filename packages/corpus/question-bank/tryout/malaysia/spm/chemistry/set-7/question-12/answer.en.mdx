# The gas holds exactly the mass of P as carbon, and only one form of carbon conducts

The gas that turns limewater chalky is carbon dioxide. <InlineMath math="4.40\text{ g}" /> of it is <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, which holds <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" /> of carbon, **all of the mass of P, so P is pure carbon**. Diamond does not conduct, but graphite does: each carbon atom bonds covalently to three others in a layer, and its fourth outer electron is delocalised along the layer.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A metal burning in oxygen leaves a solid oxide, not just a gas. **The gas holds all of the mass of P as carbon**.
- Diamond is pure carbon too, but all four outer electrons of each atom are in bonds, so it does not conduct.
- Carbon atoms share electrons and do not form ions. The layers of graphite are held only by weak forces.

# Weigh the ice, give that mass back its liquid volume, count its moles for the heat, and let the densest water sink

The ice on one square metre is <InlineMath math="100\times100\times10.0=100\,000\text{ cm}^3" />, and it weighs <InlineMath math="0.917\times100\,000=91\,700\text{ g}" />. **As liquid that water filled only <InlineMath math="91.7\text{ dm}^3" />**, so freezing raised the volume by <InlineMath math="8.3\text{ dm}^3" />. The water is <InlineMath math="91\,700\div18=5094\text{ mol}" />, so freezing released <InlineMath math="5094\times6.0=3.06\times10^4\text{ kJ}" />. **Water is densest at <InlineMath math="4^\circ\text{C}" />**, so water at <InlineMath math="4^\circ\text{C}" /> sinks to the bottom and stays there under the floating ice.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0^\circ\text{C}" /> is the temperature just under the ice. **The densest water, at <InlineMath math="4^\circ\text{C}" />, sinks to the bottom**.
- <InlineMath math="3.33\times10^4\text{ kJ}" /> takes the ice's mass as <InlineMath math="100\text{ kg}" />, but its density is only <InlineMath math="0.917\text{ g cm}^{-3}" />.
- <InlineMath math="30.6\text{ kJ}" /> counts the water in kilograms instead of grams when finding its moles.

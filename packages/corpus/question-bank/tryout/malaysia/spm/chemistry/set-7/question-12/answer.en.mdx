# Work back from the ice to the water it came from, then use the density maximum of water

The ice is <InlineMath math="100\text{ dm}^3" />, with mass <InlineMath math="91.7\text{ kg}" />. That came from <InlineMath math="91.7\text{ dm}^3" /> of liquid water, so the volume grew by <InlineMath math="8.3\text{ dm}^3" />. **Hydrogen bonds hold ice in an open lattice**, so ice is less dense and floats. Liquid water is densest at <InlineMath math="4^\circ\text{C}" />, so that water sinks to the bottom and stays there under the floating ice.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The bottom is not at <InlineMath math="0^\circ\text{C}" />. **Water is densest at <InlineMath math="4^\circ\text{C}" />**, so the coldest water stays at the top and freezes there.
- <InlineMath math="9.1\text{ dm}^3" /> starts from <InlineMath math="100\text{ dm}^3" /> of water, but the <InlineMath math="100\text{ dm}^3" /> is the ice, which came from less water.
- No change in volume would mean ice and water had the same density, but ice is less dense.

# The mass stays the same, so the volume is that mass divided by the new density

The pipe holds <InlineMath math="1000\text{ g}" /> of water. **Freezing keeps the mass but lowers the density**, so the ice takes <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1000}{0.917}=1091\text{ cm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="91\text{ cm}^3" /> more than the pipe. In ice each molecule hydrogen bonds to four neighbours in a fixed, open arrangement that holds them further apart than in the liquid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="83\text{ cm}^3" /> multiplies the volume by the fall in density, <InlineMath math="1-0.917" />. **Volume is mass divided by density**, which gives a larger change.
- The covalent bonds inside water molecules do not change on freezing. **The open lattice of hydrogen bonds causes the expansion**.
- Ice is less dense than water, so the same mass takes more room.

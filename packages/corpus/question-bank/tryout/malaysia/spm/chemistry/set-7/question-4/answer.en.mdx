# Each formula unit holds two copper ions and gives off two gases, but only one stays a gas

The formula unit has a molar mass of <InlineMath math="222" />, so the sample is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />. **It contains two copper ions, so it leaves two copper(II) oxide units**, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. The carbonate part gives one carbon dioxide and the hydroxide part one water molecule, <InlineMath math="\text{CuCO}_3\cdot\text{Cu(OH)}_2\rightarrow2\text{CuO}+\text{CO}_2+\text{H}_2\text{O}" />. **At room conditions the water is liquid**, so only the carbon dioxide counts as gas.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.80\text{ g}" /> leaves one copper(II) oxide per formula unit. **Each unit holds two copper ions**.
- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> counts the water as a gas, but it condenses at room conditions.
- Both values are wrong: the residue holds two copper(II) oxide units per formula unit, and the water condenses.

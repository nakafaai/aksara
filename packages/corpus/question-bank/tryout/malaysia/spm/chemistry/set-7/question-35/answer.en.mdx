# Find the energy stored, scale up a hundredfold for the sunlight, share it over the leaves, and take six carbon dioxide per glucose

The glucose is <InlineMath math="3.60\div180=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, which stores <InlineMath math="0.0200\times2800=56.0\text{ kJ}" />. **Only <InlineMath math="1.0\%" /> of the sunlight is stored**, so <InlineMath math="56.0\div0.010=5600\text{ kJ}" />, <InlineMath math="5.60\text{ MJ}" />, must fall on the leaves. The leaves catch <InlineMath math="2.0\times200=400\text{ kJ}" /> each hour, so they need <InlineMath math="5600\div400=14" /> hours. **Each glucose takes six carbon dioxide**, <InlineMath math="0.120\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="2.88\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="56.0\text{ kJ}" /> is only the energy stored. **The sunlight must be a hundred times more**.
- <InlineMath math="0.48\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per glucose, but the equation takes six.
- <InlineMath math="0.560\text{ MJ}" /> scales up by ten, but <InlineMath math="1.0\%" /> means a factor of a hundred.

# The glucose stores only a hundredth of the light, and six carbon dioxide molecules build each glucose

The <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of glucose stores <InlineMath math="56.0\text{ kJ}" />. **That is only <InlineMath math="1.0\%" /> of the light**, so <InlineMath math="100" /> times as much, <InlineMath math="5.60\text{ MJ}" />, must fall on the leaves. Each glucose molecule is built from six carbon dioxide molecules, <InlineMath math="0.120\text{ mol}" /> in all.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="56.0\text{ kJ}" /> is the energy stored, not the sunlight needed. **Only <InlineMath math="1.0\%" /> of the light is stored**.
- <InlineMath math="0.48\text{ dm}^3" /> takes one carbon dioxide per glucose. **Six carbon atoms need six molecules**.
- <InlineMath math="0.560\text{ MJ}" /> scales by ten instead of a hundred, as if <InlineMath math="10\%" /> were stored.

# Divide the chain by one unit, scale the feed up for the conversion, then crack one hexane for each propene

Each propene unit has <InlineMath math="M_r=42" />, so a chain holds <InlineMath math="84\,000\div42=2000" /> units. The polymer is <InlineMath math="1000\div42=23.8\text{ mol}" /> of units. **Only <InlineMath math="92\%" /> of the feed polymerises**, so <InlineMath math="23.8\div0.92=25.9\text{ mol}" /> of propene must be fed, <InlineMath math="621\text{ dm}^3" />. **Each hexane gives one propene**, so <InlineMath math="25.9\text{ mol}" /> of hexane, <InlineMath math="25.9\times86=2.23\text{ kg}" />, is cracked.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="571\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="2.05\text{ kg}" /> ignore the <InlineMath math="92\%" /> conversion. **More propene must be fed than ends up in the polymer**.
- <InlineMath math="1000" /> units divides by <InlineMath math="84" />, twice the mass of one propene unit.
- <InlineMath math="1.11\text{ kg}" /> makes two propene from each hexane, but the equation gives one propene and one propane.

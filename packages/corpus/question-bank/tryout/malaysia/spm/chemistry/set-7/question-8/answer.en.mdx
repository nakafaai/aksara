# Only the metal gives hydrogen, so the gas counts the metal, the rest is oxide, and both parts use alkali

The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="720\div24\,000=0.0300\text{ mol}" />, and two aluminium give three hydrogen, so the metal is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.54\text{ g}" />. The rest, <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ g}" />, is oxide, <InlineMath math="15.6\%" /> of the foil. **The oxide is amphoteric, so it dissolves in the alkali without giving off gas**. The metal uses <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide, and the <InlineMath math="9.80\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" /> of oxide uses <InlineMath math="1.96\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" />. **Both parts use alkali**, <InlineMath math="0.0220\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="11.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="84.4\%" /> is the share of aluminium metal, not of the oxide. **The hydrogen counts the metal**.
- The oxide gives no hydrogen: all <InlineMath math="720\text{ cm}^3" /> comes from the metal, as the second equation shows.
- <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> leaves the oxide out, but it reacts with the alkali too and uses some of it.

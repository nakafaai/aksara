# Find the heat the water needs, scale it up for the heat that escapes, then turn it into moles of butane

The water needs <InlineMath math="1000\times4.2\times75=315\text{ kJ}" />, warmed through <InlineMath math="75^\circ\text{C}" />, not <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" />. **That is only <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of what the flame gives out**, so the butane must release <InlineMath math="788\text{ kJ}" />, <InlineMath math="0.274\text{ mol}" /> at <InlineMath math="2877\text{ kJ}" /> per mole.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.35\text{ g}" /> assumes all the flame's heat reaches the water. **Only <InlineMath math="40\%" /> does**.
- <InlineMath math="2.54\text{ g}" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="0.40" /> instead of dividing, as if the losses added heat.
- <InlineMath math="21.2\text{ g}" /> heats the water through <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" />. **It starts at <InlineMath math="25^\circ\text{C}" />**.

# The same electrons dissolve magnesium at one strip and deposit silver at the other, with different charges

Magnesium is more reactive, so it gives up electrons and is the negative terminal: <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}\rightarrow\text{Mg}^{2+}+2e^-" />. **Each magnesium atom releases two electrons, but each silver ion takes only one**, so the <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of electrons dissolves <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium yet deposits <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of silver. The silver strip gains far more than the magnesium strip loses.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ g}" /> adds the magnesium lost to the silver gained. **The magnesium strip gets lighter**.
- Silver is the less reactive metal, so it is the positive terminal, where silver ions gain electrons.
- <InlineMath math="4.20\text{ g}" /> deposits one silver atom per two electrons. **A silver ion needs only one**.

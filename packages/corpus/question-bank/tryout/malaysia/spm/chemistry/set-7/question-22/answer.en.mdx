# The metal higher in the series dissolves, each silver ion takes one electron, and the silver comes out of its solution

**Magnesium is higher in the electrochemical series, so it is the negative terminal**: <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}\rightarrow\text{Mg}^{2+}+2e^-" /> dissolves <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" />. At the silver strip <InlineMath math="\text{Ag}^++e^-\rightarrow\text{Ag}" /> deposits <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="10.8\text{ g}" />. The strips gain <InlineMath math="10.8-1.20=9.60\text{ g}" />. **The silver deposited comes out of the solution**, leaving <InlineMath math="0.200-0.100=0.100\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- An increase of <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ g}" /> adds the magnesium's mass instead of taking it away. **The magnesium dissolves**.
- Silver is the positive terminal, where silver ions take electrons from the wire.
- <InlineMath math="4.20\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.750\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> give each silver ion two electrons, but a silver ion takes only one.

# Convert the cube to cubic centimetres before using the density, then count two ions per formula unit

**A cube of edge <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ mm}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ cm}" /> on each side**, so its volume is <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^{-3}\text{ cm}^3" />, not <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}^3" />. Its mass is <InlineMath math="2.16\text{ mg}" />, <InlineMath math="3.69\times10^{-5}\text{ mol}" /> of NaCl. Each formula unit is one sodium ion and one chloride ion held in the lattice by strong attraction between opposite charges.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.22\times10^{19}" /> counts formula units. **Each unit holds two ions**.
- <InlineMath math="4.45\times10^{22}" /> treats the cube as <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}^3" />. **A millimetre is a tenth of a centimetre, so the volume is a thousandth**.
- <InlineMath math="2.22\times10^{22}" /> makes both slips: the wrong volume and one ion per unit.

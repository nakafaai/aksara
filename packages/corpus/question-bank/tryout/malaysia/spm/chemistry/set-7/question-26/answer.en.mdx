# Take only the useful charge, give each copper ion two electrons, then remove that copper from the solution because the anode adds none back

The charge is <InlineMath math="500\times24.0\times3600=4.32\times10^7\text{ C}" />, and **only <InlineMath math="85\%" /> of it deposits copper**, <InlineMath math="3.67\times10^7\text{ C}" />, <InlineMath math="380\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. <InlineMath math="\text{Cu}^{2+}+2e^-\rightarrow\text{Cu}" /> gives <InlineMath math="190\text{ mol}" /> of copper, <InlineMath math="190\times64=12.2\text{ kg}" />. **An inert anode gives no copper back**, so the <InlineMath math="4000\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions falls to <InlineMath math="3810\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.762\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="14.3\text{ kg}" /> uses all the charge. **Only <InlineMath math="85\%" /> of it deposits copper**.
- <InlineMath math="24.4\text{ kg}" /> gives each copper ion one electron, but a copper(II) ion needs two.
- <InlineMath math="0.800\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> treats the anode as copper that dissolves to replace the ions, but this anode is inert.

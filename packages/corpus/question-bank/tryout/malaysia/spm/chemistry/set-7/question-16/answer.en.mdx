# The small ribbon runs out first, so both flasks give the same gas, but at different speeds

Each flask holds <InlineMath math="0.025\text{ mol}" /> of acid, but the ribbon is only <InlineMath math="0.0050\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium, which needs just <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol}" /> of acid. **The magnesium is the limiting reactant**, so both flasks give <InlineMath math="0.0050\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen. **Ethanoic acid is weak**: it keeps only a small fraction of its molecules ionised, so its hydrogen ion concentration is lower and its reaction slower, although it supplies the same total acid as it reacts.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" /> lets all the acid react. **The ribbon runs out after using only <InlineMath math="0.010\text{ mol}" /> of acid**.
- The volumes are wrong, since the magnesium limits them, and a weak acid reacts more slowly at the same concentration.
- Both give <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />, but the weak acid has fewer hydrogen ions at any moment, so it reacts more slowly.

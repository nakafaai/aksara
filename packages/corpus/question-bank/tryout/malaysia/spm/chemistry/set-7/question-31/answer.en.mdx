# Only an alkane whose hydrogen atoms are all alike gives one product, and each substitution frees one hydrogen chloride

**In 2,2-dimethylpropane all twelve hydrogen atoms sit on four identical methyl groups**, so replacing any one of them gives the same product. Pentane gives three different monochloro products and 2-methylbutane four. The <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, which gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{11}\text{Cl}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen chloride. In <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ dm}^3" /> that is <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, pH <InlineMath math="1.0" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Pentane has hydrogen atoms on end and middle carbons, which give different products. **Only one isomer has all its hydrogen atoms alike**.
- pH <InlineMath math="0.70" /> counts two hydrogen chloride molecules per substitution, but one chlorine atom replaces one hydrogen atom and frees one.
- <InlineMath math="3.55\text{ g}" /> is close to the mass of chlorine in the product, not the mass of the product.

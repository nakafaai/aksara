# Only the isomer whose twelve hydrogens are all alike gives one product, then follow its hydrogen chloride into a twentieth of the solution

**In 2,2-dimethylpropane all twelve hydrogen atoms sit on four identical methyl groups**, so swapping any one gives the same product. Pentane has three kinds of hydrogen and gives three products. The alkane is <InlineMath math="3.60\div72=0.0500\text{ mol}" />, giving <InlineMath math="0.0500\times106.5=5.33\text{ g}" /> of product and <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen chloride. The <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> portion holds a twentieth, <InlineMath math="2.50\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" />. **<InlineMath math="\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow2\text{NaCl}+\text{CO}_2+\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> takes two acid per carbonate**, so <InlineMath math="1.25\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Pentane has three kinds of hydrogen atom, so it gives three different monochloro products. **Only 2,2-dimethylpropane gives one**.
- <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> takes one sodium carbonate per acid molecule, but each carbonate neutralises two.
- <InlineMath math="3.55\text{ g}" /> is the mass of chlorine used, not of the chloroalkane formed.

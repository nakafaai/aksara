# A single product needs every hydrogen atom to be in the same kind of position

Chlorine replaces one hydrogen atom. **Only if every hydrogen atom sits in an identical position can each replacement give the same product**. In 2,2-dimethylpropane all twelve hydrogen atoms are on four identical methyl groups around one central carbon, so there is only one monochloro product. The <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of alkane gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{11}\text{Cl}" />, molar mass <InlineMath math="106.5" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Pentane has hydrogen atoms on its end and inner carbons, so **it gives three different monochloro products**.
- <InlineMath math="3.55\text{ g}" /> uses the mass of chlorine taken up rather than the mass of the product.
- 2-Methylbutane has four kinds of hydrogen position, so it gives four different monochloro products.

# The acid runs out first, because each hypochlorite ion needs two of its molecules

The bleach holds <InlineMath math="0.035\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hypochlorite and the cleaner <InlineMath math="0.060\text{ mol}" /> of hydrochloric acid. **The hypochlorite would need <InlineMath math="0.070\text{ mol}" /> of acid**, so the acid is the limiting reactant. Its <InlineMath math="0.060\text{ mol}" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.030\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine, and some bleach is left over.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="840\text{ cm}^3" /> lets all the hypochlorite react. **It needs twice as much acid as there is**.
- <InlineMath math="1440\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one chlorine molecule per acid molecule, but two acid molecules make one.
- <InlineMath math="1680\text{ cm}^3" /> doubles the hypochlorite, as if each formula unit gave two chlorine molecules.

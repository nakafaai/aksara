# Average the densities by volume, with the air adding volume but no mass, then compare masses

Take <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}^3" /> of composite: <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ cm}^3" /> of glass weighs <InlineMath math="1.02\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.55\text{ cm}^3" /> of polyester <InlineMath math="0.66\text{ g}" />. **The <InlineMath math="0.05\text{ cm}^3" /> of air fills space but adds no mass**, so the density is <InlineMath math="1.68\text{ g cm}^{-3}" />. The glass is <InlineMath math="1.02\div1.68=60.7\%" /> of the mass, more than its share of the volume because it is the denser material.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.74\text{ g cm}^{-3}" /> gives the polyester <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of the volume. **Five per cent is air**.
- <InlineMath math="40.0\%" /> is the glass's share of the volume, but the denser glass has a larger share of the mass.
- <InlineMath math="1.61\text{ g cm}^{-3}" /> takes the air out of the glass instead of out of the polyester.

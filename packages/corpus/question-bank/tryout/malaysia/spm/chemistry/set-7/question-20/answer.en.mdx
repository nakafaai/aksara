The bell holds <InlineMath math="1800\text{ g}" /> of copper, <InlineMath math="28.1\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="200\text{ g}" /> of tin, <InlineMath math="1.68\text{ mol}" />. **A tin atom is almost twice as heavy as a copper atom**, so tin is only <InlineMath math="1.68\div29.8=5.64\%" /> of the atoms, not <InlineMath math="10\%" />. # Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.0\%" /> is the share of the mass. **Tin atoms are heavier, so they are a smaller share of the atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="675\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per copper(II) oxide, but two oxide units give one.
- <InlineMath math="1.80\text{ kg}" /> is the mass of the copper, not of the copper(II) oxide that held it.

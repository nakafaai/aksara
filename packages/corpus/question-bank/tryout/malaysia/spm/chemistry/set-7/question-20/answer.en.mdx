# Work in one cubic centimetre: the volume shares give the masses, and the masses give the percentage

One cubic centimetre of composite holds <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ cm}^3" /> of glass, <InlineMath math="1.02\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ cm}^3" /> of polyester, <InlineMath math="0.72\text{ g}" />, so it weighs <InlineMath math="1.74\text{ g}" />. **The glass is the denser material, so its share of the mass is larger than its share of the volume**. Combining the stiff, strong fibres with the light, tough polyester gives a hull that is strong and low in density.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40.0\%" /> is the glass's share of the volume, not of the mass. **The denser glass carries more of the mass**.
- <InlineMath math="1.52\text{ g cm}^{-3}" /> treats the <InlineMath math="40\%" /> as a share of mass, and the percentage repeats that slip.
- <InlineMath math="41.4\%" /> is the polyester's share of the mass, not the glass's.

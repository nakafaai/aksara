# Turn each volume into a mass with its own density, then count the carbon atoms in each fuel

The litre holds <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of ethanol, <InlineMath math="79\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="900\text{ cm}^3" /> of octane, <InlineMath math="630\text{ g}" />. **Each fuel brings its own carbon**: ethanol <InlineMath math="1.72\text{ mol}" /> with two carbon atoms each, and octane <InlineMath math="5.53\text{ mol}" /> with eight. Every carbon atom becomes one carbon dioxide molecule.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.16\text{ kg}" /> burns the whole litre as octane. **A tenth of the volume is ethanol, with its own density and carbon count**.
- <InlineMath math="1.95\text{ kg}" /> counts only the octane and leaves out the carbon dioxide from the ethanol.
- <InlineMath math="1.05\text{ kg}" /> gives one carbon dioxide for every two carbon atoms.

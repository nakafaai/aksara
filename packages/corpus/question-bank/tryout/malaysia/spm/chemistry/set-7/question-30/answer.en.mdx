# Weigh each fuel from its volume and density, burn each by its own equation, then scale the oxygen up to air

The litre holds <InlineMath math="100\times0.79=79\text{ g}" /> of ethanol, <InlineMath math="1.72\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="900\times0.70=630\text{ g}" /> of octane, <InlineMath math="5.53\text{ mol}" />. **Each burns by its own equation**: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}+3\text{O}_2\rightarrow2\text{CO}_2+3\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> and <InlineMath math="2\text{C}_8\text{H}_{18}+25\text{O}_2\rightarrow16\text{CO}_2+18\text{H}_2\text{O}" />. The carbon dioxide is <InlineMath math="3.43+44.2=47.6\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="2.10\text{ kg}" />. The oxygen is <InlineMath math="5.15+69.1=74.2\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1781\text{ dm}^3" />, **and air is only <InlineMath math="21\%" /> oxygen**, so <InlineMath math="1781\div0.21=8.48\text{ m}^3" /> of air is needed.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.16\text{ kg}" /> and <InlineMath math="8.77\text{ m}^3" /> treat the whole litre as octane. **A tenth of it is ethanol**.
- <InlineMath math="1.78\text{ m}^3" /> is the volume of oxygen, but the burning draws it from air that is only <InlineMath math="21\%" /> oxygen.
- <InlineMath math="0.374\text{ m}^3" /> multiplies the oxygen by <InlineMath math="0.21" /> instead of dividing by it.

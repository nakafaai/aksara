# The ester needs one acid and one alcohol molecule, so the ethanol must be split evenly

The ethanol is <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" />. **Every ester molecule uses one ethanoic acid and one ethanol molecule**, and each acid molecule came from an ethanol molecule. So the oxidised part and the remaining part must be equal, <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> each, which gives <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of ester. Any other split leaves one of them in excess.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Oxidising all of it leaves no ethanol for the ester. **The ethanol has to supply both halves**.
- <InlineMath math="4.40\text{ g}" /> halves the amount of ester a second time, but <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of acid with <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of ethanol gives <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of ester.
- Oxidising one third makes only <InlineMath math="0.0667\text{ mol}" /> of acid, which runs out while ethanol is left over.

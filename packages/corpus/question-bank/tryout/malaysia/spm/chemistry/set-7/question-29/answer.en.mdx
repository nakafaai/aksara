# Each acid pairs with each alcohol, and swapping the carbon between the two halves keeps the formula

Two acids and two alcohols make <InlineMath math="2\times2=4" /> esters: methyl methanoate, methyl ethanoate, ethyl methanoate and ethyl ethanoate. **Methyl ethanoate and ethyl methanoate both have the formula <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}_2" />**: one carbon has simply moved from the acid half to the alcohol half, so they are isomers. The other two differ in formula.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2" /> esters pairs each acid only with the alcohol of the same length. **Every acid can pair with every alcohol**.
- Methyl ethanoate and ethyl methanoate have the same atoms arranged differently, so two of the four share a formula.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> esters counts acid with acid or alcohol with alcohol, which do not form esters.

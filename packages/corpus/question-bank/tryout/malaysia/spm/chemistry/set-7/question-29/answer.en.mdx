# Half the ethanol becomes acid, only three quarters of it esterifies, and the quarter left is titrated

The ethanol is <InlineMath math="9.20\div46=0.200\text{ mol}" />. **Oxidising half gives one acid for each alcohol**: <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of each. Only <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of the acid reacts, so <InlineMath math="0.0750\text{ mol}" /> of ester forms, <InlineMath math="0.0750\times88=6.60\text{ g}" />. **The other <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of acid stays unreacted**, and it needs <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide, <InlineMath math="0.0250\div0.500=0.0500\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="8.80\text{ g}" /> lets all the acid esterify. **Only <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of it reacts**, so some acid is left to titrate.
- <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the acid made, but the acid that became ester no longer reacts with the alkali.
- <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> lets each sodium hydroxide neutralise two acid molecules, but ethanoic acid is monoprotic.

# Three oxygen molecules make two ozone, and each ozone gives one oxygen back to the iodide

The discharge turns <InlineMath math="30\%" /> of the oxygen, <InlineMath math="72\text{ cm}^3" />, into ozone. **Three oxygen molecules make two ozone molecules**, so <InlineMath math="48\text{ cm}^3" /> of ozone forms and <InlineMath math="168\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen remains, <InlineMath math="216\text{ cm}^3" /> in all. **Each ozone molecule that reacts with the iodide releases one oxygen molecule**, so the gas leaving is still <InlineMath math="216\text{ cm}^3" />, now all oxygen. The ozone is <InlineMath math="48\div24\,000=0.00200\text{ mol}" />, which frees <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ mol}" /> of iodine, <InlineMath math="0.00200\times254=0.508\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="168\text{ cm}^3" /> removes the ozone without the oxygen it releases. **Each ozone molecule gives back one oxygen molecule**.
- <InlineMath math="0.762\text{ g}" /> counts the <InlineMath math="72\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen used as ozone, but three oxygen molecules make only two ozone.
- <InlineMath math="0.254\text{ g}" /> uses <InlineMath math="127" />, the mass of an iodine atom, but iodine forms <InlineMath math="\text{I}_2" /> molecules of mass <InlineMath math="254" />.

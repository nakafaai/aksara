# Three molecules become two, so the gas shrinks, but no atom is created or lost

The syringe starts with <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen molecules. **Every three that react become two ozone molecules**, so the <InlineMath math="0.0030\text{ mol}" /> that react leave <InlineMath math="0.0020\text{ mol}" /> of ozone beside <InlineMath math="0.0070\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen, <InlineMath math="0.0090\text{ mol}" /> of molecules in all. The atoms only rearrange, so there are still <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of them.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> assumes the number of molecules stays the same. **Three oxygen molecules become two ozone molecules**.
- <InlineMath math="5.42\times10^{21}" /> counts the molecules after the change, not the atoms.
- <InlineMath math="264\text{ cm}^3" /> lets the gas expand, but the reaction turns more molecules into fewer.

# Three oxygen molecules shrink into two ozone molecules, and each ozone molecule hands one oxygen molecule back

Of the <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="72\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen reacts and gives <InlineMath math="48\text{ cm}^3" /> of ozone, so the gas becomes <InlineMath math="168+48=216\text{ cm}^3" />. **The iodide does not simply absorb the ozone**: each ozone molecule gives up one oxygen atom to the iodide and leaves as an oxygen molecule. So <InlineMath math="48\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen comes back out with the <InlineMath math="168\text{ cm}^3" /> that never reacted, <InlineMath math="216\text{ cm}^3" /> in all.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="168\text{ cm}^3" /> lets the iodide absorb the ozone completely. **Each ozone molecule releases one oxygen molecule**.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> ignores the shrinkage: three molecules become two, so the volume falls.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> after the iodide turns the ozone back into oxygen as <InlineMath math="2\text{O}_3\rightarrow3\text{O}_2" />, but one atom of each ozone molecule stays with the iodine.

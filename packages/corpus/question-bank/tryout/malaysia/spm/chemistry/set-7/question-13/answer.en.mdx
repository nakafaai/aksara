# Count molecules, then share each atom's three links between two atoms, and burn every carbon atom to one carbon dioxide

With <InlineMath math="M_r=720" />, the sample is <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="6.02\times10^{20}" /> molecules. **Each link joins two atoms, so a molecule has <InlineMath math="60\times3\div2=90" /> links, not <InlineMath math="180" />**, and the sample has <InlineMath math="90\times6.02\times10^{20}=5.42\times10^{22}" />. **Burning turns every carbon atom into carbon dioxide**, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_{60}+60\text{O}_2\rightarrow60\text{CO}_2" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1.44\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.61\times10^{22}" /> counts carbon atoms, not molecules. **Sixty atoms make one molecule**.
- <InlineMath math="1.08\times10^{23}" /> counts every link twice, once from each of its two atoms.
- <InlineMath math="0.024\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per molecule, but each molecule has sixty carbon atoms.

# Hydrogen removes the four oxygen atoms, but the iron dissolves only to iron(II)

The magnetite is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. **Each formula unit holds four oxygen atoms, and each needs one hydrogen molecule to leave as water**, <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4+4\text{H}_2\rightarrow3\text{Fe}+4\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" /> is used. That gives <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" /> of iron. Dilute acid takes iron only to iron(II), <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{FeCl}_2+\text{H}_2" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen comes back, even though magnetite itself held iron(III).

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.44\text{ dm}^3" /> for the reduction uses one hydrogen molecule per iron atom. **The hydrogen removes oxygen, and there are four oxygen atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="2.16\text{ dm}^3" /> dissolves the iron to iron(III), but dilute hydrochloric acid gives iron(II).
- <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ dm}^3" /> uses one hydrogen molecule per iron(III) ion and leaves oxygen in the solid.

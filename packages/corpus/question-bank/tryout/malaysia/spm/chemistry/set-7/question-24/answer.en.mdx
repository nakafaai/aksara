# The charges must cancel, and every oxide ion needs one hydrogen molecule to leave as water

Four oxide ions carry <InlineMath math="-8" />, so the three iron ions carry <InlineMath math="+8" />. **Only one iron(II) and two iron(III) ions give <InlineMath math="+8" />**. Reduction removes all four oxygen atoms as water, <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4+4\text{H}_2\rightarrow3\text{Fe}+4\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of magnetite needs <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- One iron(III) and two iron(II) ions give <InlineMath math="+7" />. **The four oxide ions need <InlineMath math="+8" />**.
- <InlineMath math="1.44\text{ dm}^3" /> uses three hydrogen molecules, one per iron atom, but the hydrogen removes oxygen, and there are four oxygen atoms.
- <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ dm}^3" /> uses one hydrogen molecule per iron(III) ion and leaves oxygen in the solid.

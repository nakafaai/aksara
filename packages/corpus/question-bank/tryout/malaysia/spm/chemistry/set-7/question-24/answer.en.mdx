# Three copper atoms give six electrons, which reduce only two of the eight nitrate groups

The copper is <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" />, and **the equation makes two nitrogen monoxide molecules for every three copper atoms**, so <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of gas forms. Each copper atom gives two electrons, and each nitrogen that becomes <InlineMath math="\text{NO}" /> takes three, falling from <InlineMath math="+5" /> in nitrate to <InlineMath math="+2" />. The other six nitrate groups stay as nitrate ions. In air, <InlineMath math="\text{NO}" /> is oxidised again to brown <InlineMath math="\text{NO}_2" />.

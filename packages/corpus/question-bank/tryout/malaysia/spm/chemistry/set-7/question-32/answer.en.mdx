# Let the water fix the chain length, then follow the alcohol through its acid into the ester at sixty per cent

The water is <InlineMath math="1.80\div18=0.100\text{ mol}" />. **Each alcohol molecule gives <InlineMath math="n+1" /> water molecules**, so <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1.48}{14n+18}(n+1)=0.100" />, which gives <InlineMath math="n=4" />: butan-1-ol, <InlineMath math="M_r=74" />, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. It oxidises to <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of butanoic acid, and with methanol to methyl butanoate, <InlineMath math="M_r=102" />. **At <InlineMath math="60\%" /> yield** that is <InlineMath math="0.0120\times102=1.22\text{ g}" />. The burned sample gives four carbon dioxide per molecule, <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ dm}^3" />.

- <InlineMath math="2.04\text{ g}" /> ignores the yield. **Only <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of the acid becomes ester**.
- Propan-1-ol, <InlineMath math="M_r=60" />, would give <InlineMath math="1.78\text{ g}" /> of water, not <InlineMath math="1.80\text{ g}" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.06\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of the acid's mass, not of the ester's.

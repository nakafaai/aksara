# Oxidation adds fourteen grams per mole, which names the alcohol, and the ester takes the methyl group

Oxidation swaps two hydrogen atoms for one oxygen atom, <InlineMath math="-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}\rightarrow-\text{COOH}" />, a gain of <InlineMath math="14" /> per mole. **The <InlineMath math="0.28\text{ g}" /> gain means <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />**, so the alcohol's molar mass is <InlineMath math="74" />: butan-1-ol, which gives butanoic acid. With methanol it forms methyl butanoate, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_7\text{COOCH}_3" />, molar mass <InlineMath math="102" />, and <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0120\text{ mol}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.04\text{ g}" /> is the ester at full yield. **Only <InlineMath math="60\%" /> forms**.
- Propan-1-ol, molar mass <InlineMath math="60" />, would gain only <InlineMath math="0.35\text{ g}" /> from <InlineMath math="1.48\text{ g}" />, not <InlineMath math="0.28\text{ g}" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.06\text{ g}" /> uses the acid's molar mass, <InlineMath math="88" />, for the ester and forgets the methyl group it gains.

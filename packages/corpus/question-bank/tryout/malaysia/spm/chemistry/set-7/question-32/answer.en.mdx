# Oxidation swaps two hydrogen atoms for one oxygen atom, so every mole gains fourteen grams

The alcohol group <InlineMath math="-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}" /> becomes <InlineMath math="-\text{COOH}" />. **Each molecule loses two hydrogen atoms and gains one oxygen atom**, a gain of <InlineMath math="16-2=14" /> per mole. The mass rose by <InlineMath math="0.28\text{ g}" />, so there were <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, and the alcohol's molar mass is <InlineMath math="74" />: <InlineMath math="14n+18=74" /> gives <InlineMath math="n=4" />.

\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- Propan-1-ol, molar mass <InlineMath math="60" />, would give a gain of only <InlineMath math="0.35\text{ g}" /> from <InlineMath math="1.48\text{ g}" />. **The data fit four carbon atoms**.
- Ethanol, molar mass <InlineMath math="46" />, would gain far more, <InlineMath math="0.45\text{ g}" />, from the same mass.
- Pentan-1-ol comes from dividing the acid's mass by the moles, which gives the acid's molar mass, <InlineMath math="88" />, not the alcohol's.

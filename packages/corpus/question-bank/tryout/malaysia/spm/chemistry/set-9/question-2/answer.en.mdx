# Use the heavy isotope's mass, count the neutrons each molecule carries, then let each heavy water give one deuterium molecule

**Deuterium has a nucleon number of <InlineMath math="2" />, so heavy water has <InlineMath math="M_r=2(2)+16=20" />, not <InlineMath math="18" />**. The sample is <InlineMath math="3.60\div20=0.180\text{ mol}" />. Each deuterium has one neutron and each oxygen-16 has eight, so a molecule has <InlineMath math="10" />, and the sample has <InlineMath math="1.80\times6.02\times10^{23}=1.08\times10^{24}" />. The equation gives one deuterium molecule per heavy water, <InlineMath math="0.180\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="4.32\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="8.67\times10^{23}" /> counts only the oxygen's neutrons. **Each deuterium atom adds one more**.
- <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{24}" /> and <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" /> use <InlineMath math="M_r=18" />, the mass of ordinary water.
- <InlineMath math="2.16\text{ dm}^3" /> is the oxygen, not the deuterium: two heavy water give two deuterium and one oxygen.

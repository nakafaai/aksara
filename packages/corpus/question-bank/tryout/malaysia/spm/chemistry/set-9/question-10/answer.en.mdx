# Find X from the oxygen's share of the mass, then let each oxide give four acid molecules with three hydrogen ions each

The <InlineMath math="160\text{ g}" /> of oxygen in <InlineMath math="\text{X}_4\text{O}_{10}" /> is <InlineMath math="56.3\%" /> of the mass, so the formula mass is <InlineMath math="160\div0.563=284" /> and X is <InlineMath math="(284-160)\div4=31" />, phosphorus. The sample is <InlineMath math="0.284\div284=0.00100\text{ mol}" />. **Each oxide gives four acid molecules**, <InlineMath math="0.00400\text{ mol}" />, **and each acid molecule gives three hydrogen ions**, so <InlineMath math="0.0120\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide is needed, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />.

V(\text{NaOH})&=\tfrac{3\times4\times0.00100}{0.100}=0.120\text{ dm}^3

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40.0\text{ cm}^3" /> neutralises one hydrogen ion per acid molecule. **All three hydrogen atoms react**.
- <InlineMath math="30.0\text{ cm}^3" /> makes one acid molecule per oxide, but the equation gives four.
- Silicon dioxide, <InlineMath math="\text{SiO}_2" />, does not dissolve in water to give an acid, and <InlineMath math="\text{X}_4\text{O}_{10}" /> with <InlineMath math="43.7\%" /> X points to <InlineMath math="A_r=31" />.

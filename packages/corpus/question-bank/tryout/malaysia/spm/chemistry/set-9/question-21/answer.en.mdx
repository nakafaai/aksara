# Let each manganate(VII) ion count five iron(II) ions, scale the portion up to a litre, and weigh the crystals with their water

In <InlineMath math="\text{MnO}_4^-" /> manganese is <InlineMath math="+7" />, and it ends as <InlineMath math="\text{Mn}^{2+}" />, <InlineMath math="+2" />. The manganate(VII) is <InlineMath math="4.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, **and each ion takes five electrons, so it oxidises five iron(II) ions**: <InlineMath math="2.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> in the portion, <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. **The crystals carry seven water molecules**, <InlineMath math="M_r=278" />, so a litre held <InlineMath math="0.0800\times278=22.2\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.45\text{ g}" /> lets each manganate(VII) oxidise one iron(II) ion. **Each one takes five electrons, so five iron(II) ions**.
- <InlineMath math="12.2\text{ g}" /> weighs anhydrous iron(II) sulfate, but the crystals carry seven water molecules.
- <InlineMath math="+6" /> misreads manganese in <InlineMath math="\text{MnO}_4^-" />: four oxygens at <InlineMath math="-2" /> and a charge of <InlineMath math="-1" /> make it <InlineMath math="+7" />.

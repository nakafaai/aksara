# Turn the charge into electrons, make the chlorine and the alkali from them, then let the chlorine use up the alkali two to one

The charge is <InlineMath math="965\text{ C}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. The concentrated brine gives chlorine at the anode, <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, and at the cathode <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />, **with one hydroxide ion per electron**, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />. **Each chlorine molecule takes two sodium hydroxide**, so the <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine uses all <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, leaving none, and makes <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hypochlorite in <InlineMath math="200\text{ cm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen molecule per electron. **Each hydrogen molecule takes two electrons**.
- <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> makes one sodium hypochlorite per sodium hydroxide, but the equation makes one per chlorine molecule.
- <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> of sodium hydroxide left uses it one to one with chlorine, but each chlorine molecule takes two.

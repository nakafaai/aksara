# Read the carbon dioxide and the leftover oxygen from the two contractions, then match them to the burning equation

**The alkali removes only carbon dioxide**, so <InlineMath math="110-50=60\text{ cm}^3" /> is carbon dioxide and the <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> left is unused oxygen. Equal volumes hold equal moles, so <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrocarbon gives <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> of carbon dioxide: <InlineMath math="x=3" />. **The oxygen used is <InlineMath math="150-50=100\text{ cm}^3" />, not <InlineMath math="150-60" />**, five times the hydrocarbon. In <InlineMath math="\text{C}_x\text{H}_y+\left(x+\tfrac{y}{4}\right)\,\text{O}_2\rightarrow x\,\text{CO}_2+\tfrac{y}{2}\,\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, <InlineMath math="3+\tfrac{y}{4}=5" />, so <InlineMath math="y=8" />: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_8" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_6" /> takes the oxygen used as <InlineMath math="150-60=90\text{ cm}^3" />. **The gas the alkali leaves is the unused oxygen, <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" />**.
- <InlineMath math="110\text{ cm}^3" /> is the gas left after cooling, which still includes the unused oxygen.
- <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> is the oxygen left, not the carbon dioxide that the alkali absorbed.

# Turn the warming into heat, share it over the moles burned, then compare with the data book value to find the loss

The water gains <InlineMath math="200\times4.2\times25.0=21\,000\text{ J}" />, <InlineMath math="21.0\text{ kJ}" />. The ethanol is <InlineMath math="0.920\div46=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, **so per mole the experiment gives <InlineMath math="21.0\div0.0200=1050\text{ kJ}" />**, a heat of combustion of <InlineMath math="-1050\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. The full value is <InlineMath math="1370\text{ kJ}" />, so <InlineMath math="1370-1050=320\text{ kJ}" /> per mole is lost, **<InlineMath math="320\div1370=23.4\%" /> of the heat released**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-1050\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> and <InlineMath math="23.4\%" /> go together. **The loss is a share of all the heat released, <InlineMath math="1370\text{ kJ}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="30.5\%" /> divides the loss by the measured value, but the loss is a share of the heat actually released.
- <InlineMath math="76.6\%" /> is the share that reaches the water, not the share lost.

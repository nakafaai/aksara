# Equal volumes hold equal moles, so the carbon dioxide counts carbon atoms, which splits the mixture, and each gas then takes its own oxygen

Methane gives one carbon dioxide per molecule and ethane two. **If the mixture holds <InlineMath math="x" /> of ethane, the carbon dioxide is <InlineMath math="100+x=160" />**, so <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> of ethane and <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> of methane. <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_4+2\text{O}_2\rightarrow\text{CO}_2+2\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> and <InlineMath math="2\text{C}_2\text{H}_6+7\text{O}_2\rightarrow4\text{CO}_2+6\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, **so ethane takes three and a half volumes of oxygen**: <InlineMath math="2(40)+3.5(60)=290\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="290\text{ cm}^3" /> go together. **Ethane gives two carbon dioxide and takes three and a half oxygen per molecule**.
- <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> of ethane swaps the two gases: the extra <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> of carbon dioxide comes from ethane's second carbon atom.
- <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}^3" /> gives ethane seven oxygen per molecule, but the balanced equation has seven for two molecules.

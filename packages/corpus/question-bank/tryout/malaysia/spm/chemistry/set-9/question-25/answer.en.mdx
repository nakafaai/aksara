# Standardise the thiosulfate on a known amount of iodine, then use it to measure the iodine the vitamin C did not take

Potassium iodate has <InlineMath math="M_r=214" />, so the sample is <InlineMath math="5.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, and **each iodate gives three iodine molecules**, <InlineMath math="1.50\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" />. Each iodine takes two thiosulfate, <InlineMath math="3.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ dm}^3" />, so the thiosulfate is <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. In the tablet run the same <InlineMath math="1.50\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of iodine is added. The back titration uses <InlineMath math="0.0120\times0.100=1.20\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of thiosulfate, so **<InlineMath math="6.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" /> of iodine was left over**. The ascorbic acid took the rest, <InlineMath math="9.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="9.00\times10^{-4}\times176=0.158\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="31.7\%" /> of the tablet.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> reads the thiosulfate as one to one with iodine. **Each iodine takes two thiosulfate ions**.
- <InlineMath math="0.106\text{ g}" /> takes the iodine left over as the iodine the ascorbic acid used. **The back titration measures what was not used**.
- <InlineMath math="0.264\text{ g}" /> lets the ascorbic acid take all the iodine added, ignoring the <InlineMath math="12.0\text{ cm}^3" /> back titration.

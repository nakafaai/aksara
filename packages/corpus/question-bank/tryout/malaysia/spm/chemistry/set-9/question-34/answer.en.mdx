# Find the heat from the warming, divide by the copper(II) ions displaced, see that doubling the solution doubles both heat and mass, and count the zinc used

The solution gains <InlineMath math="50.0\times4.2\times10.0=2100\text{ J}" />. It held <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of copper(II) ions, so the heat of displacement is <InlineMath math="-2.10\div0.0100=-210\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, negative because heat is released. With <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />, twice the heat warms twice the mass, **so the rise is still <InlineMath math="10.0^\circ\text{C}" />**. In the first experiment <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of zinc reacts, <InlineMath math="0.65\text{ g}" />, **so <InlineMath math="0.35\text{ g}" /> of the zinc is left**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20.0^\circ\text{C}" /> doubles the heat but forgets the mass. **Twice the solution absorbs twice the heat**.
- <InlineMath math="+210\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> gives the sign of an endothermic change, but the temperature rose, so heat was released.
- <InlineMath math="-105\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.675\text{ g}" /> count twice the copper(II) ions in the heat and half of them in the zinc used.

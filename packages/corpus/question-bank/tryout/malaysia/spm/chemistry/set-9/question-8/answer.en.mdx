# Two metal atoms give one hydrogen molecule, so double the hydrogen to find the metal, then halve the hydroxide for a diprotic acid

The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="120\div24\,000=0.00500\text{ mol}" />. **<InlineMath math="2\text{M}+2\text{H}_2\text{O}\rightarrow2\text{MOH}+\text{H}_2" /> needs two metal atoms per hydrogen molecule**, so the metal is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> and <InlineMath math="A_r=0.390\div0.0100=39" />: potassium. It forms <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of hydroxide ions. **Sulfuric acid gives two hydrogen ions per molecule**, so <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> is needed, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Rubidium takes one metal atom per hydrogen molecule. **Two metal atoms give each hydrogen molecule**, which points to <InlineMath math="A_r=39" />.
- <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> takes one sulfuric acid per hydroxide ion, but each acid molecule neutralises two.
- Sodium, <InlineMath math="A_r=23" />, would give <InlineMath math="203\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen from <InlineMath math="0.390\text{ g}" />, not <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />.

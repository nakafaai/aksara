# Two metal atoms make one hydrogen molecule, which names the metal, and the hydroxide then splits between a portion and the rest

The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="120\div24\,000=0.00500\text{ mol}" />, and <InlineMath math="2\text{M}+2\text{H}_2\text{O}\rightarrow2\text{MOH}+\text{H}_2" /> means **two metal atoms give one hydrogen molecule**, so the metal is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> and <InlineMath math="A_r=0.390\div0.0100=39" />, potassium. The <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of potassium hydroxide in <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" /> puts <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" /> in each <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> portion. **Each sulfuric acid takes two hydroxide ions**, so the portion needs <InlineMath math="0.000500\text{ mol}" /> of acid, <InlineMath math="0.000500\div0.0500=0.0100\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" />. The whole solution makes <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4" />, <InlineMath math="0.00500\times174=0.870\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Rubidium and <InlineMath math="5.00\text{ cm}^3" /> give one metal atom per hydrogen molecule, which halves the moles and makes <InlineMath math="A_r" /> about <InlineMath math="78" />.
- <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ cm}^3" /> neutralises one hydroxide per sulfuric acid, but **<InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4" /> has two hydrogen ions**.
- <InlineMath math="0.0870\text{ g}" /> is the salt from one portion only, not from the whole <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />.

# The conversion sets the acid and the gas left over, and the acid then sets the fertiliser

The sulfur is <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^6\div32=3.125\times10^4\text{ mol}" />, and each sulfur gives one sulfur dioxide. **Only <InlineMath math="96\%" /> goes on**, <InlineMath math="3.00\times10^4\text{ mol}" /> of sulfur trioxide and so of sulfuric acid, <InlineMath math="3.00\times10^4\times98=2.94\times10^6\text{ g}" />. That is <InlineMath math="98\%" /> of the concentrated acid, so the acid sold is <InlineMath math="2.94\div0.98=3.00" /> tonnes. **The other <InlineMath math="4\%" /> is <InlineMath math="1250\text{ mol}" /> of sulfur dioxide**, which takes <InlineMath math="1250\times56=70.0\text{ kg}" /> of calcium oxide. Each sulfuric acid gives one ammonium sulfate, <InlineMath math="3.00\times10^4\times132=3.96\times10^6\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="3.96" /> tonnes.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.88" /> tonnes multiplies by <InlineMath math="98\%" />. **The pure acid is only part of the concentrated acid**, so divide by <InlineMath math="0.98" />.
- <InlineMath math="3.13" /> tonnes and <InlineMath math="0\text{ kg}" /> convert all the sulfur dioxide, ignoring that only <InlineMath math="96\%" /> of it reacts.
- <InlineMath math="1750\text{ kg}" /> of calcium oxide traps all the sulfur dioxide, but only the unconverted <InlineMath math="4\%" /> reaches the trap.

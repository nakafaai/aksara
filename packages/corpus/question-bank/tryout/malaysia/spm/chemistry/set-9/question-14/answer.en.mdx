# Each pH unit is a tenfold change, so two units mean a hundredfold dilution, and the weak acid releases fewer hydrogen ions

**A fall of one pH unit is a tenfold change in hydrogen ion concentration**, so going from pH <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="3" /> lowers it from <InlineMath math="0.100" /> to <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, a hundredfold dilution. The <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> must become <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" />, **so <InlineMath math="990\text{ cm}^3" /> of water is added**. Ethanoic acid is weak and ionises only partly, so the same dilution gives fewer hydrogen ions and a pH higher than <InlineMath math="3" />.

\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" /> is the final volume. **The acid already fills <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of it**.
- A pH equal to <InlineMath math="3" /> treats ethanoic acid as fully ionised, but it is a weak acid.
- <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^3" /> treats pH as if three times the pH meant three times the volume, but each pH unit is a factor of ten.

# Each pH unit is a tenfold dilution, and the diluted acid then splits between the burette and the magnesium

pH <InlineMath math="1" /> means <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+]=0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> and pH <InlineMath math="2" /> means <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, so **the acid is diluted ten times**, to <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />, by adding <InlineMath math="90.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of water. The <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> portion holds <InlineMath math="2.50\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" /> of acid, which needs <InlineMath math="2.50\times10^{-4}\div0.0500=0.00500\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="5.00\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali. **The rest is <InlineMath math="75.0\text{ cm}^3" />**, <InlineMath math="7.50\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" /> of acid, and <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{MgCl}_2+\text{H}_2" /> halves it, <InlineMath math="3.75\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="9.00\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> is the final volume. **The water added is the difference**, <InlineMath math="100-10.0=90.0\text{ cm}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="90.0\text{ cm}^3" /> use the acid at pH <InlineMath math="1" />, before it was diluted.
- <InlineMath math="18.0\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen molecule per acid, but **two acid give one hydrogen**.

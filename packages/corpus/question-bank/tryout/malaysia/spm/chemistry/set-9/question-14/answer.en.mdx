# Two pH units mean a hundredfold dilution, which changes the concentration but not the moles of acid, and the weak acid releases fewer hydrogen ions

**A fall of one pH unit is a tenfold change in hydrogen ion concentration**, so pH <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="3" /> lowers it from <InlineMath math="0.100" /> to <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, a hundredfold dilution. The <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> becomes <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="990\text{ cm}^3" /> of water is added. **Diluting does not change the moles of acid**: still <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="0.00100\div0.0100=0.100\text{ dm}^3" /> of alkali, <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />. Ethanoic acid is weak and ionises only partly, so its diluted pH is higher than <InlineMath math="3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" /> is the final volume. **The acid already fills <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of it**.
- A pH equal to <InlineMath math="3" /> treats ethanoic acid as fully ionised, but it is a weak acid.
- <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ cm}^3" /> thinks dilution removes acid, but the same <InlineMath math="0.00100\text{ mol}" /> is only spread through more water.

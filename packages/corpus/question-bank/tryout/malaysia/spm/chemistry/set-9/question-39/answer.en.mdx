# Each fat molecule carries three fatty acid parts, so it takes three sodium hydroxide and gives three soap units

The fat is <InlineMath math="8.90\div890=0.0100\text{ mol}" />. **Each molecule has three fatty acid parts**, so it takes <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of sodium hydroxide, <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" />, and gives <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of sodium stearate. The soap has <InlineMath math="M_r=306" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0300\times306=9.18\text{ g}" /> forms.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.06\text{ g}" /> give one soap unit per fat molecule. **Each fat molecule has three fatty acid parts**.
- <InlineMath math="3.06\text{ g}" /> of soap uses the right alkali but makes only one soap unit per fat molecule.
- <InlineMath math="8.90\text{ g}" /> keeps the mass of the fat, but the soap gains sodium and the glycerol leaves.

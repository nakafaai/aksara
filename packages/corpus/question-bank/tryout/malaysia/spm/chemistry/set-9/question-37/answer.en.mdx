# Count repeating units, give each one chlorine atom and so one hydrogen chloride, then scale the ethene up for the yield

Each repeating unit keeps the monomer's formula, <InlineMath math="M_r=62.5" />, so the PVC is <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ mol}" /> of units. **Each unit holds one chlorine atom**, so burning gives <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen chloride, <InlineMath math="384\text{ dm}^3" />, trapped one to one by <InlineMath math="16.0\times40=640\text{ g}" /> of sodium hydroxide. **At <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield more ethene was needed than ended in the polymer**: <InlineMath math="16.0\div0.80=20.0\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="560\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="192\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen chloride per two units. **Every unit carries its own chlorine atom**.
- <InlineMath math="320\text{ g}" /> takes two hydrogen chloride per sodium hydroxide, but the neutralisation is one to one.
- <InlineMath math="358\text{ g}" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="80\%" /> instead of dividing, which is less ethene than even a full yield would need.

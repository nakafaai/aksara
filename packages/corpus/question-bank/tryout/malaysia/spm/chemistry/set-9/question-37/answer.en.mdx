# The repeat unit sets both the chain length and the moles, and the yield works backwards to the ethene

Chloroethene has <InlineMath math="M_r=62.5" />, so an average chain holds <InlineMath math="62\,500\div62.5=1000" /> units. The <InlineMath math="1000\text{ g}" /> of PVC is <InlineMath math="1000\div62.5=16.0\text{ mol}" /> of units, and **an <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield means more ethene went in than chloroethene came out**, <InlineMath math="16.0\div0.80=20.0\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="20.0\times28=560\text{ g}" />. Each unit carries one chlorine atom, so burning gives <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen chloride, <InlineMath math="16.0\times24=384\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="448\text{ g}" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="80\%" />, but **the yield loses material**, so more ethene must go in. Divide by <InlineMath math="0.80" />.
- <InlineMath math="192\text{ dm}^3" /> pairs the chlorine atoms as <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2" />, but each chlorine atom leaves as one hydrogen chloride molecule.
- <InlineMath math="2230" /> divides by the <InlineMath math="M_r" /> of ethene, but the repeat unit of PVC is chloroethene, <InlineMath math="M_r=62.5" />.

# Each double bond takes one hydrogen molecule, so three per oil molecule, and the fat weighs the oil plus that hydrogen

The oil is <InlineMath math="8.78\div878=0.0100\text{ mol}" />. **Each double bond takes one hydrogen molecule, and each oil molecule has three**, so <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="720\text{ cm}^3" />, reacts. **Addition puts the hydrogen into the molecule**, so the fat weighs <InlineMath math="8.78+0.0300\times2=8.84\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> adds one hydrogen molecule per oil molecule. **Each of the three double bonds takes one**.
- <InlineMath math="8.78\text{ g}" /> keeps the mass unchanged, but the hydrogen added stays in the fat.
- <InlineMath math="1440\text{ cm}^3" /> gives two hydrogen molecules to each double bond, but one molecule saturates it.

# The damp crucible adds moisture that leaves on heating, so it spoils both the anhydrous mass and the water lost

The student takes the anhydrous salt as <InlineMath math="16.600-15.090=1.510\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="9.44\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" />, and the water as <InlineMath math="17.590-16.600=0.990\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0550\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="x=5.8" />. **The crucible carried <InlineMath math="15.090-15.000=0.090\text{ g}" /> of moisture, and heating drove it off with the water of crystallisation**. Truly the anhydrous salt is <InlineMath math="16.600-15.000=1.600\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and the water of crystallisation is <InlineMath math="0.990-0.090=0.900\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="x=5" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="x=5" /> for the student treats the error like a zero error that cancels. **The moisture is in the first reading only, then evaporates**.
- <InlineMath math="x=5.5" /> corrects the anhydrous mass but still counts the moisture as water of crystallisation.
- <InlineMath math="x=5.3" /> removes the moisture from the water but still takes the anhydrous salt from the damp reading.

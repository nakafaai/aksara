# Name the ester from its alcohol and acid, let the scarcer reactant set the amount, then take seventy per cent

**The alcohol gives the first part of the name and the acid the second**, so methanol and propanoic acid give methyl propanoate, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOCH}_3" />, <InlineMath math="M_r=88" />. The acid is <InlineMath math="7.40\div74=0.100\text{ mol}" /> and the methanol <InlineMath math="4.80\div32=0.150\text{ mol}" />. **They react one to one, so the acid limits the ester** to <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="8.80\text{ g}" />. At <InlineMath math="70\%" /> yield, <InlineMath math="6.16\text{ g}" /> is obtained.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Propyl methanoate swaps the two parts. **The alcohol named first is methanol, so the ester is methyl propanoate**.
- <InlineMath math="8.80\text{ g}" /> is the expected mass, but only <InlineMath math="70\%" /> of it is obtained.
- <InlineMath math="9.24\text{ g}" /> bases the yield on the methanol, but it is the reactant in excess.

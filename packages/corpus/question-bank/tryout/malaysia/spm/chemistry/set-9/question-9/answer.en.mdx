# Each chlorine molecule takes two iodide ions, so the chlorine runs out, and half the iodide is left in solution

The chlorine is <InlineMath math="60\div24\,000=0.00250\text{ mol}" /> and the iodide <InlineMath math="0.0500\times0.200=0.0100\text{ mol}" />. **Each chlorine molecule takes two iodide ions**, so it uses <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> and frees <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" /> of iodine, <InlineMath math="\text{I}_2" />, <InlineMath math="0.00250\times254=0.635\text{ g}" />. The other <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of iodide stays in the <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.27\text{ g}" /> turns all the iodide into iodine. **The chlorine is enough for only half of it**.
- <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> gives one iodide ion per chlorine molecule, but each molecule takes two.
- <InlineMath math="0.318\text{ g}" /> uses <InlineMath math="127" /> for iodine, but it forms <InlineMath math="\text{I}_2" /> of mass <InlineMath math="254" />.

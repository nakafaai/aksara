# Take eighty per cent of the alcohol into the alkene, then add one hydrogen molecule to each alkene to make the alkane

The alcohol is <InlineMath math="3.70\div74=0.0500\text{ mol}" />. <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{OH}\rightarrow\text{C}_4\text{H}_8+\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> gives one alkene per alcohol, **but only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> is dehydrated**, so <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of but-1-ene, <InlineMath math="0.96\text{ dm}^3" />. Hydrogen adds across the double bond, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8+\text{H}_2\rightarrow\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}" />, **so the product is butane**, <InlineMath math="M_r=58" />: <InlineMath math="0.0400\times58=2.32\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="2.90\text{ g}" /> treat all the alcohol as dehydrated. **Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of it is**.
- <InlineMath math="2.24\text{ g}" /> weighs the but-1-ene, but hydrogen adds two atoms to each molecule, making butane.
- <InlineMath math="1.16\text{ g}" /> makes one butane from two but-1-ene molecules, but each alkene gives its own.

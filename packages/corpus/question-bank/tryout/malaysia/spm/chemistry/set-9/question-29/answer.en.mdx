# Take eighty per cent of the alcohol into the alkene, then add one hydrogen molecule to each alkene to make the alkane

The alcohol is <InlineMath math="3.70\div74=0.0500\text{ mol}" />. Dehydration gives one alkene per alcohol, **but only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> reacts**, so <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of but-1-ene, <InlineMath math="0.96\text{ dm}^3" />. **Each double bond takes one hydrogen molecule**, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.96\text{ dm}^3" />, and the product is butane, <InlineMath math="M_r=58" />: <InlineMath math="0.0400\times58=2.32\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="2.90\text{ g}" /> treat all the alcohol as dehydrated. **Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of it is**.
- <InlineMath math="2.24\text{ g}" /> weighs the but-1-ene, but hydrogen adds two atoms to each molecule, making butane.
- <InlineMath math="0.48\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="1.16\text{ g}" /> give one hydrogen and one butane per two alkene molecules, but each alkene takes its own.

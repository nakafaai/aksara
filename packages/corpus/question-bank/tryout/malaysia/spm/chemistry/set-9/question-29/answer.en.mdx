# Take eighty per cent into the alkene, add hydrogen to make the alkane, then burn every carbon atom into carbon dioxide

The alcohol is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />. **Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> is dehydrated**, so <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of but-1-ene, <InlineMath math="0.96\text{ dm}^3" />. Hydrogen adds across the double bond to give <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of butane, <InlineMath math="M_r=58" />, <InlineMath math="2.32\text{ g}" />. **Each butane has four carbon atoms**, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}+\tfrac{13}{2}\text{O}_2\rightarrow4\text{CO}_2+5\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.160\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="3.84\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="2.90\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" /> treat all the alcohol as dehydrated. **Only <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of it is**.
- <InlineMath math="2.24\text{ g}" /> weighs the but-1-ene, but hydrogen adds two atoms to each molecule, making butane.
- <InlineMath math="1.16\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ dm}^3" /> make one butane from two alkene molecules, but each alkene gives its own.

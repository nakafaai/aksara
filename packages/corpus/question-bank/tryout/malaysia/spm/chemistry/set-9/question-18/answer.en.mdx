# Count back from the oxygen to the peroxide, undo the tenfold dilution, then let the neat stock give ten times the gas

The oxygen is <InlineMath math="240\div24\,000=0.0100\text{ mol}" />. **Two peroxide molecules give one oxygen**, so the portion held <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. **The stock was diluted ten times**, so it is <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Half the oxygen, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />, came in <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />, <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />. Undiluted, <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> holds <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of peroxide and gives <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen, <InlineMath math="2400\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> is the diluted solution. **The stock was ten times more concentrated**.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides the final volume by <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />, but only half had formed by then.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> for the stock forgets that it is ten times stronger than the portion that gave <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />.

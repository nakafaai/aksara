# Two peroxide molecules give one oxygen, half of it comes in the first forty seconds, and the catalyst is not used up

The peroxide is <InlineMath math="0.0500\times0.400=0.0200\text{ mol}" />. **Two peroxide molecules give one oxygen molecule**, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />, forms in all. Half of it, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />, comes in the first <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />, <InlineMath math="120\div40=3.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />. **A catalyst is not used up**, so all <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ g}" /> of manganese(IV) oxide is recovered.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one oxygen molecule per peroxide molecule. **Two peroxide molecules give one**.
- Recovering no solid treats the manganese(IV) oxide as a reactant, but a catalyst is unchanged at the end.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides the final volume by <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />, but only half of it has formed by then.

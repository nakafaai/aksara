# Two peroxide molecules give one oxygen, half comes in the first forty seconds, and the catalyst changes only the speed, never the total

The first experiment has <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of peroxide. **Two peroxide molecules give one oxygen molecule**, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />. Half, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />, comes in <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />, <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />. **The catalyst is not used up and does not change how much oxygen forms**, so the second experiment, with half the peroxide, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, gives half the oxygen, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one oxygen molecule per peroxide molecule. **Two peroxide molecules give one**.
- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> for the second experiment treats the catalyst as setting the amount, but the peroxide does, and there is half as much.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides the final volume by <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />, but only half of it has formed by then.

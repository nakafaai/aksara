# The water cools because the salt takes in heat, so find the heat per mole, then scale it to the heat the cold pack must take

The water loses <InlineMath math="100\times4.2\times6.3=2646\text{ J}" />, taken in by <InlineMath math="8.00\div80=0.100\text{ mol}" /> of salt. **Heat is absorbed, so the heat of solution is positive**: <InlineMath math="+2.646\div0.100=+26.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. The cold pack must take <InlineMath math="250\times4.2\times10.0=10\,500\text{ J}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="10.5\div26.5=0.396\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.396\times80=31.7\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-26.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> gives the sign of a change that releases heat. **The water cooled, so heat was absorbed**.
- <InlineMath math="12.7\text{ g}" /> cools only <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> of water, but the pack must cool <InlineMath math="250\text{ g}" />.
- <InlineMath math="+2.65\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides the heat in kilojoules by ten times the moles, which also makes the mass ten times too big.

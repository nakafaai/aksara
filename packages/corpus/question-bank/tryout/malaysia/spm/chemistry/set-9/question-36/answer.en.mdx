# Find the heat per mole from the small test, then scale it to the pack in both directions

The water loses <InlineMath math="100\times4.2\times6.0=2520\text{ J}" />, and the salt is <InlineMath math="8.00\div80=0.100\text{ mol}" />, so **the heat of solution is <InlineMath math="+25.2\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />**, positive because the solution takes heat in. The pack has <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ mol}" />, which takes in <InlineMath math="12.6\text{ kJ}" /> from <InlineMath math="250\text{ g}" /> of water, a fall of <InlineMath math="12\,600\div(250\times4.2)=12.0^\circ\text{C}" />, so the pack ends at <InlineMath math="30.0-12.0=18.0^\circ\text{C}" />. **Cooling by <InlineMath math="20.0^\circ\text{C}" /> needs <InlineMath math="250\times4.2\times20.0=21.0\text{ kJ}" />**, <InlineMath math="21.0\div25.2=0.833\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="66.7\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-25.2\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> gives the sign of a change that releases heat, but the temperature fell, and the pack would warm to <InlineMath math="42.0^\circ\text{C}" /> instead of cooling.
- <InlineMath math="33.3\text{ g}" /> cools by <InlineMath math="10.0^\circ\text{C}" />, but **the water has to fall from <InlineMath math="30.0^\circ\text{C}" /> to <InlineMath math="10.0^\circ\text{C}" />**, by <InlineMath math="20.0^\circ\text{C}" />.
- <InlineMath math="12.0^\circ\text{C}" /> is the fall, not the final temperature, which is <InlineMath math="30.0-12.0=18.0^\circ\text{C}" />.

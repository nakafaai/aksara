# Read the ions from the electron arrangements, balance their charges, then count two electrons for each magnesium ion discharged

X loses two electrons to form <InlineMath math="\text{X}^{2+}" /> and Y gains one to form <InlineMath math="\text{Y}^-" />, **so the charges balance as <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_2" />**, magnesium chloride, <InlineMath math="M_r=95" />. The sample is <InlineMath math="4.75\div95=0.0500\text{ mol}" />, which gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium, <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" />. **Each <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" /> takes two electrons**, so <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of electrons pass, <InlineMath math="0.100\times96\,500=9650\text{ C}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{XY}" /> pairs the ions one to one. **<InlineMath math="\text{X}^{2+}" /> needs two <InlineMath math="\text{Y}^-" />**.
- <InlineMath math="4825\text{ C}" /> gives each magnesium ion one electron, but <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" /> needs two.
- <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ g}" /> doubles the magnesium, as if each formula unit held two metal ions.

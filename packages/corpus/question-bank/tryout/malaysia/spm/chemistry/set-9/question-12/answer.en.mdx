# Name the ions, turn the charge into electrons, share two electrons to each ion discharged, then burn the metal into its oxide

X is magnesium, which forms <InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" />, and Y is chlorine, which forms <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}^-" />. The charge is <InlineMath math="2.00\times4825=9650\text{ C}" />, <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. **Each magnesium ion takes two electrons**, so <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium, <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" />. **At the anode two chloride ions give one chlorine molecule**, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" />. Burning gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium oxide, <InlineMath math="0.0500\times40=2.00\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ g}" /> gives each magnesium ion one electron. **<InlineMath math="\text{Mg}^{2+}" /> takes two**.
- <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" /> counts chlorine atoms as molecules, but two chloride ions give one molecule.
- An oxide of <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" /> forgets the oxygen that joins the magnesium when it burns.

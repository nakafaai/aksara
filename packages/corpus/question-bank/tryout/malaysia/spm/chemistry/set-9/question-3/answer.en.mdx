# Weigh the liquid, count it as two-atom molecules, let each mole fill the molar volume, then count two atoms per molecule in the balloon

One cubic centimetre weighs <InlineMath math="0.807\text{ g}" />. **Nitrogen is <InlineMath math="\text{N}_2" />, <InlineMath math="M_r=28" />**, so that is <InlineMath math="0.0288\text{ mol}" />, which spreads to <InlineMath math="0.0288\times24\,000=692\text{ cm}^3" /> of gas. The balloon needs <InlineMath math="5000\div692=7.23\text{ cm}^3" /> of the liquid. It holds <InlineMath math="5.00\div24=0.208\text{ mol}" /> of molecules, **and each molecule has two atoms**, <InlineMath math="0.417\times6.02\times10^{23}=2.51\times10^{23}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1384\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="3.61\text{ cm}^3" /> take nitrogen as single atoms, <InlineMath math="M_r=14" />. **Nitrogen gas is made of <InlineMath math="\text{N}_2" /> molecules**.
- <InlineMath math="1.25\times10^{23}" /> counts molecules, but each nitrogen molecule holds two atoms.
- <InlineMath math="857\text{ cm}^3" /> takes the liquid's mass as <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ g}" />, but its density is <InlineMath math="0.807\text{ g cm}^{-3}" />.

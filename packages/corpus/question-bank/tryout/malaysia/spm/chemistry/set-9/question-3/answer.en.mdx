# Weigh the liquid, count its molecules as two-atom molecules, and let each mole spread to the molar volume

One cubic centimetre weighs <InlineMath math="0.807\text{ g}" />. **Nitrogen is <InlineMath math="\text{N}_2" />, <InlineMath math="M_r=28" />**, so that is <InlineMath math="0.0288\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1.74\times10^{22}" /> molecules, which spread to <InlineMath math="0.0288\times24\,000=692\text{ cm}^3" /> of gas. **The same molecules fill almost seven hundred times the space**. The balloon needs <InlineMath math="5000\div692=7.23\text{ cm}^3" /> of the liquid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1384\text{ cm}^3" /> takes nitrogen as single atoms, <InlineMath math="M_r=14" />. **Nitrogen gas is made of <InlineMath math="\text{N}_2" /> molecules**.
- <InlineMath math="2.51\times10^{19}" /> is the number of molecules in a cubic centimetre of the gas, not of the liquid.
- <InlineMath math="857\text{ cm}^3" /> takes the liquid's mass as <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ g}" />, but its density is <InlineMath math="0.807\text{ g cm}^{-3}" />.

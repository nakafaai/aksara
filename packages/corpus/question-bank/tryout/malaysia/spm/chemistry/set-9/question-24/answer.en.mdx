# Acid takes iron only to iron(II), chlorine finishes the job at one molecule per two ions, and the alkali catches the iron as iron(III) hydroxide

The iron is <InlineMath math="0.280\div56=0.00500\text{ mol}" />. **Dilute acid oxidises iron only to iron(II)**, <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{FeCl}_2+\text{H}_2" />, giving <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. **One chlorine molecule oxidises two iron(II) ions**, <InlineMath math="0.00250\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" />. The iron is now iron(III), so the alkali precipitates <InlineMath math="\text{Fe(OH)}_3" />, <InlineMath math="0.00500\times107=0.535\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> of chlorine takes one chlorine molecule per iron(II) ion. **Each chlorine molecule oxidises two**.
- <InlineMath math="180\text{ cm}^3" /> and no chlorine treat the acid as taking iron straight to iron(III), but dilute acid stops at iron(II).
- <InlineMath math="0.450\text{ g}" /> precipitates iron(II) hydroxide, but the chlorine has already oxidised the iron to iron(III).

# Acid takes the iron to iron(II), the chlorine oxidises only two thirds of it, and the alkali precipitates both kinds

The iron is <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" />, and dilute acid takes it only to iron(II), giving <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. The chlorine is <InlineMath math="40\div24\,000=1.67\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" />, **and each molecule oxidises two iron(II) ions**, <InlineMath math="3.33\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="66.7\%" /> of the iron. **The rest stays as iron(II)**, so the precipitate is <InlineMath math="3.33\times10^{-3}\times107=0.357\text{ g}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{Fe(OH)}_3" /> and <InlineMath math="1.67\times10^{-3}\times90=0.150\text{ g}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{Fe(OH)}_2" />, <InlineMath math="0.507\text{ g}" /> in all.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="100\%" /> lets the chlorine oxidise all the iron. **<InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> is enough for only two thirds of it**.
- <InlineMath math="33.3\%" /> oxidises one iron(II) ion per chlorine molecule, but each takes two.
- <InlineMath math="180\text{ cm}^3" /> treats the acid as taking iron straight to iron(III), but dilute acid stops at iron(II).

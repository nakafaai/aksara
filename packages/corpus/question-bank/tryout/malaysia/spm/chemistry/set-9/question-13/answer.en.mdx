# Each atom gives its valence electrons, every two electrons make one hydrogen molecule, and magnesium gives two per atom

The aluminium is <InlineMath math="2.70\div27=0.100\text{ mol}" />. **Each atom gives three valence electrons**, so <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1.81\times10^{23}" /> electrons. In the acid these electrons reduce hydrogen ions, <InlineMath math="2\text{H}^++2e^-\rightarrow\text{H}_2" />, **two electrons per hydrogen molecule**, so <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ dm}^3" />. Magnesium gives two electrons, so one hydrogen per atom: <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium, <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="7.20\text{ g}" /> of magnesium gives each magnesium atom one electron. **Each magnesium atom gives two**, so half as many atoms are needed.
- <InlineMath math="6.02\times10^{22}" /> gives one electron per aluminium atom, but it has three valence electrons.
- <InlineMath math="7.20\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen molecule per electron, but each needs two.

# Each atom gives as many electrons as its outer shell holds, so match the electrons, not the atoms

The aluminium is <InlineMath math="2.70\div27=0.100\text{ mol}" />. **Each aluminium atom gives three valence electrons**, so the sea holds <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.300\times6.02\times10^{23}=1.81\times10^{23}" /> electrons. **Each magnesium atom gives only two**, so the same <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mol}" /> of electrons needs <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium, <InlineMath math="0.150\times24=3.60\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.81\times10^{23}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ g}" /> are correct only together. **Each aluminium atom gives three electrons and each magnesium atom two**.
- <InlineMath math="6.02\times10^{22}" /> gives one electron per atom, but aluminium has three valence electrons.
- <InlineMath math="7.20\text{ g}" /> gives each magnesium atom one electron, but it has two valence electrons.

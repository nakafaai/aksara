# The mass lost is the carbon dioxide, so turn it into moles and volume for the rate, and let the acid set the total

**The balance loses only the carbon dioxide that escapes**, so <InlineMath math="0.88\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.88\div44=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" />, and the average rate is <InlineMath math="480\div50=9.6\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />. The marble is in excess, so the acid sets the total: <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of acid, and **two acid molecules give one carbon dioxide**, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="2.20\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.40\text{ g}" /> gives one carbon dioxide per acid molecule. **Two acid molecules give one**.
- <InlineMath math="17.6\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides the mass in milligrams by the time, which is not a gas volume.
- <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> halves the carbon dioxide, but each <InlineMath math="44\text{ g}" /> lost is one whole mole.

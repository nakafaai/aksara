# Find which reactant runs out in each experiment before reading any rate from the data

The marble is <InlineMath math="2.50\div100=0.0250\text{ mol}" /> and the acid in Experiment I is <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />. **The acid needs only <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of marble, so the acid runs out**, giving <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" />, and leaving <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ g}" /> of marble. Half of that is <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />, reached at <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />, so the average rate is <InlineMath math="240\div40=6.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />. In Experiment II the acid is <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" />, more than the <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> the marble needs. **Now the marble runs out**, so the gas is <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" /> lets the marble decide in Experiment I, but **<InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of acid dissolves only <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of it**, and half of <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" /> is then read at <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" />.
- <InlineMath math="960\text{ cm}^3" /> doubles the gas with the acid, but in Experiment II the marble runs out first.
- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> in Experiment II assumes concentration changes only the rate. **Here the extra acid also dissolves the marble that was left over**.

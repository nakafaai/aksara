# The solid loses the zinc that dissolves but gains the copper that deposits, mole for mole, and each pair moves two electrons

The copper(II) ions are <InlineMath math="0.100\times0.200=0.0200\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of zinc dissolves, <InlineMath math="1.30\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of copper deposits, <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" />. **The solid loses one and gains the other**, so it ends <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ g}" /> lighter. **Each zinc atom gives two electrons**, <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="2.41\times10^{22}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ g}" /> lighter and <InlineMath math="2.41\times10^{22}" /> go together. **Zinc dissolves as copper deposits, and each zinc gives two electrons**.
- An increase of <InlineMath math="1.28\text{ g}" /> counts the copper gained but not the zinc lost.
- <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{22}" /> counts one electron per zinc atom, but <InlineMath math="\text{Zn}^{2+}" /> has lost two.

# Each magnesium atom displaces two silver ions, so the solid gains much more silver than it loses magnesium

The silver ions are <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />. **Each magnesium atom gives two electrons, so it displaces two silver ions**: <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium dissolves, <InlineMath math="0.24\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of silver deposits, <InlineMath math="2.16\text{ g}" />. **The solid loses the magnesium but gains the silver**, <InlineMath math="2.16-0.24=1.92\text{ g}" /> heavier. The electrons equal the silver ions, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{22}" />, and the <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium ions in <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> is <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.41\times10^{22}" /> gives each silver ion two electrons, but a silver ion takes only one. **The electrons equal the silver ions, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="2.16\text{ g}" /> counts the silver gained but not the magnesium lost.
- <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> gives one magnesium ion per silver ion, but each magnesium replaces two.

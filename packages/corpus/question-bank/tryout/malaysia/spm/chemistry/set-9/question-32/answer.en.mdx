# Two acid molecules dissolve each carbonate, so the vinegar runs out first, and each carbonate dissolved gives one carbon dioxide

The acid is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> and the scale <InlineMath math="3.00\div100=0.0300\text{ mol}" />. **Each carbonate needs two acid molecules**, so the acid dissolves only <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="2.50\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ g}" /> of scale is left. Each carbonate dissolved gives one carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="0.0250\times24\,000=600\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="720\text{ cm}^3" /> dissolve all the scale. **The vinegar is enough for only <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" /> of it**.
- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> with no scale left gives one carbon dioxide per acid molecule and ignores that the acid runs out.
- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per acid molecule, but two acid molecules give one.

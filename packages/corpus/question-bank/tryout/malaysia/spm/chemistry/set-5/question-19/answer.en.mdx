# Two doublings from the temperature

From <InlineMath math="30^\circ\text{C}" /> to <InlineMath math="50^\circ\text{C}" /> is two rises of <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" />, so **every part of the metal's surface now reacts <InlineMath math="2\times2=4" /> times as fast**.

# The longer strip brings surface and metal

Twice the length is twice the surface touching the acid, so the strip loses mass <InlineMath math="8" /> times as fast as the first one did. **It also has twice as much metal to get through**, so the time is <InlineMath math="120\times2\div8=30\text{ s}" />. Ignoring the extra surface gives <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" />. Dividing <InlineMath math="120\text{ s}" /> by eight without the extra metal gives <InlineMath math="15\text{ s}" />.

# The gas depends only on the magnesium

<InlineMath math="0.096\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0040\text{ mol}" />, and **each magnesium atom frees one hydrogen molecule whatever the temperature**, so <InlineMath math="96\text{ cm}^3" /> forms. The <InlineMath math="48\text{ cm}^3" /> in two choices is the shorter strip's gas.

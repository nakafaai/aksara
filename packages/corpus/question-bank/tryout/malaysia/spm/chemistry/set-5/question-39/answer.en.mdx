# Three limits for one child

At <InlineMath math="18\text{ kg}" /> the child may have <InlineMath math="270\text{ mg}" /> in one dose and <InlineMath math="1080\text{ mg}" /> in a day, in at most four doses. **All three limits apply at once**, and the smallest one wins.

# What the cold syrup has already used

Each <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ mL}" /> dose of the cold syrup held <InlineMath math="7.5/5.0\times80=120\text{ mg}" />, so the child has taken <InlineMath math="240\text{ mg}" />. **The dose count covers every paracetamol medicine**, so those two doses also use up half of the four, and two remain. Working from the <InlineMath math="1080\text{ mg}" /> day limit alone, as if nothing had been taken, gives the <InlineMath math="45.0\text{ mL}" /> option.

# The tighter limit decides

By the daily limit there is room for <InlineMath math="840\text{ mg}" />, which is <InlineMath math="35.0\text{ mL}" /> of plain syrup, but that would mean a single dose bigger than <InlineMath math="270\text{ mg}" /> or a fifth dose. **Two doses of <InlineMath math="270\text{ mg}" /> allow only <InlineMath math="540\text{ mg}" />**, and at <InlineMath math="120\text{ mg}" /> per <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ mL}" /> that is <InlineMath math="22.5\text{ mL}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
2\times270&=540\text{ mg}<1080-240=840\text{ mg}\ \Rightarrow\ V=\tfrac{540}{120}\times5.0=22.5\text{ mL}\\
n&=\tfrac{0.240+0.540}{151}=\tfrac{0.780}{151}=5.17\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}
\end{aligned}" />

The day ends at <InlineMath math="780\text{ mg}" /> of paracetamol, with <InlineMath math="M_r=151" />. Counting only the plain syrup gives <InlineMath math="3.58\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> and forgets the morning's cold syrup.

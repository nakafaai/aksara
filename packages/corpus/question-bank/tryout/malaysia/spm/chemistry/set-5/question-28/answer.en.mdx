# Only the ethene touches the purple

Ethane is saturated, so it bubbles straight through. **The carbon-carbon double bond in ethene is the only thing the manganate(VII) can attack**, which means the colour change measures the ethene alone.

# Matching electrons across the two half-equations

Each manganate(VII) ion takes five electrons and each ethene molecule gives two, so **five ethene molecules use up two manganate(VII) ions**. The solution held <InlineMath math="0.0500\times0.0200=1.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of manganate(VII), enough for <InlineMath math="2.50\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of ethene, <InlineMath math="60.0\text{ cm}^3" />. Out of <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" />, that makes <InlineMath math="25.0\%" />.

Reading the two equations as one to one gives <InlineMath math="24.0\text{ cm}^3" />, or <InlineMath math="10.0\%" />. The reverse ratio, two ethene for five manganate(VII), shrinks it to <InlineMath math="9.60\text{ cm}^3" />, or <InlineMath math="4.00\%" />.

# The diol, and the manganese left behind

Each ethene becomes one diol molecule, <InlineMath math="M_r=62" />, so <InlineMath math="2.50\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> weighs <InlineMath math="0.155\text{ g}" />. On the other side, **every manganate(VII) ion becomes exactly one manganese(II) ion**, so the colourless solution still holds <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of manganese, the same <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> the purple had. The <InlineMath math="0.00800\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> option runs the two to five ratio a second time, on the manganese.

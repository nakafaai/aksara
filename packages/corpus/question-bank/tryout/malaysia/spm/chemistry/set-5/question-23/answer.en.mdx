# Hydrogen gives electrons away, so its electrode is the negative terminal

Hydrogen is oxidised, so its electrode releases electrons into the wire and **is the negative terminal**. Each hydrogen molecule gives two electrons and each oxygen molecule takes four, so the cell uses twice as much hydrogen as oxygen by volume. The overall change is hydrogen burning to water.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The positive terminal is where oxygen takes electrons. **Hydrogen gives electrons, which makes its terminal negative**.
- Equal volumes ignore that oxygen takes four electrons for every two that hydrogen gives.
- <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ dm}^3" /> gives each hydrogen molecule one electron, but the half-equation shows two.

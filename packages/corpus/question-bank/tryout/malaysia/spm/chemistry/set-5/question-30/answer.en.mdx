# The ester takes some of the alkali, and the acid measures the rest

Each ester molecule takes one hydroxide ion and gives one ethanol molecule. **The alkali was added in excess**, so the titration measures only what the ester did not use.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60.0\text{ cm}^3" /> titrates all the alkali added, as if the ester had used none.
- <InlineMath math="1.38\text{ g}" /> makes ethanol from the sodium hydroxide instead of from the ester. **The ester, the smaller amount, limits the ethanol**.
- The alkali was <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> against <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of ester, so some must be left.

# What each tube catches

**The calcium chloride tube comes first because it takes only water**. Soda lime absorbs water as well as carbon dioxide, so in the other order the two gains could not be told apart. The first tube gained <InlineMath math="1.08\text{ g}" /> of water, <InlineMath math="0.0600\text{ mol}" />, which carries <InlineMath math="0.120\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen atoms. The soda lime gained <InlineMath math="2.20\text{ g}" /> of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of carbon.

# The mass nobody weighed is oxygen

Carbon and hydrogen account for <InlineMath math="0.600+0.120=0.720\text{ g}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.880\text{ g}" />. The other <InlineMath math="0.160\text{ g}" /> **has to be oxygen inside X**, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, giving the ratio <InlineMath math="5:12:1" /> and the formula <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}\text{O}" />, which matches <InlineMath math="M_r=88" />. Carbon and hydrogen alone would suggest <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}" />, whose <InlineMath math="M_r=72" /> does not fit, and pentane would never give hydrogen with sodium. With five carbons in a straight chain and the hydroxyl group at the end, X is pentan-1-ol.

# How much oxygen came from the cylinder

The products hold <InlineMath math="2\times0.0500+0.0600=0.160\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen atoms, but **one in every sixteen was already in the alcohol**. The cylinder supplied the other <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" /> of atoms, which is <InlineMath math="0.0750\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen molecules, <InlineMath math="1.80\text{ dm}^3" />. If the cylinder had supplied all <InlineMath math="0.160\text{ mol}" /> of product oxygen, the answer would be <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ dm}^3" />, and counting the atoms as molecules gives <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ dm}^3" />, twice too much.

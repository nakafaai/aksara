# Equal gas volumes are equal moles, so the oxygen ratio names the alkane

Each volume of alkane needs <InlineMath math="130\div20.0=6.5" /> volumes of oxygen. **For <InlineMath math="\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}" /> the oxygen needed is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3n+1}{2}" />**, which is <InlineMath math="6.5" /> when <InlineMath math="n=4" />: butane, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}" />. Its <InlineMath math="2.90\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, and each molecule's ten hydrogen atoms make five water molecules.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Propane needs only <InlineMath math="5" /> oxygen molecules per molecule. **The ratio <InlineMath math="6.5" /> fits butane**.
- <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ g}" /> gives four water molecules per molecule, as for <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8" />, but butane has ten hydrogen atoms.
- Pentane needs <InlineMath math="8" /> oxygen molecules per molecule, more than the <InlineMath math="6.5" /> measured.

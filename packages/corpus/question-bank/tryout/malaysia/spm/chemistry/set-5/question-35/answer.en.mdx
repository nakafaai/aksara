# A weak alkali pays for its own ionisation out of the heat

The same <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of water forms as with sodium hydroxide, but ammonia is only partly ionised. **As the acid removes hydroxide ions, more ammonia must ionise, and that step absorbs energy**, so less heat warms the solution.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-57.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> holds only when both acid and alkali are fully ionised from the start.
- <InlineMath math="-26.0\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, adding the acid and the alkali, though they make only <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of water together.
- The same amount of water forms. **The difference is energy absorbed, not water missing**.

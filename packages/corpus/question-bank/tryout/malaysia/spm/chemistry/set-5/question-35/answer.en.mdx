# Count the ammonia, not the acid

The <InlineMath math="0.680\text{ g}" /> of gas is <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of ammonia, while the acid brings <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />. **The ammonia runs out first**, so only <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of neutralisation happens and the extra acid just sits there. Dividing the heat by the acid's <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> gives <InlineMath math="-42.0\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, as if every acid molecule had found a partner.

# Heat into the whole mixture

Both solutions end up at the same temperature, so **the <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> of the mixture is what absorbed the heat**, <InlineMath math="2100\text{ J}" /> for a <InlineMath math="5.0^\circ\text{C}" /> rise. Spread over <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> that is <InlineMath math="-52.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. Counting only the <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" /> of acid halves the heat and leads to <InlineMath math="-26.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

# The missing heat ionised the ammonia

Ammonia is a weak alkali: in its solution only a few molecules have turned into ammonium and hydroxide ions. As the acid removes hydroxide ions, more ammonia reacts with water to replace them, and **that ionisation takes in heat**. It costs <InlineMath math="57.3-52.5=4.80\text{ kJ}" /> for every mole of ammonia.

# Swapping in a strong alkali

**Sodium hydroxide is fully ionised already**, so the same <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> would give the full <InlineMath math="57.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> into the same <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />. The alkali still runs out before the acid, which is why <InlineMath math="6.82^\circ\text{C}" />, worked from the acid's moles, is too high.

n(\text{NH}_3)&=\tfrac{0.680}{17}=0.0400\text{ mol}<n(\text{HCl})=0.0500\text{ mol}\\
\Delta H&=-\tfrac{100\times4.2\times5.0}{0.0400}=-52\,500\text{ J mol}^{-1}=-52.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\\
57.3-52.5&=4.80\text{ kJ mol}^{-1},\quad \Delta T(\text{NaOH})=\tfrac{0.0400\times57\,300}{100\times4.2}=5.46^\circ\text{C}
\end{aligned}" />

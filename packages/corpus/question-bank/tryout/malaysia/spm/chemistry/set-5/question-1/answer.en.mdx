# The volume's uncertainty, not the mass's, decides what you can claim

The metal's volume is the rise in level, <InlineMath math="31.0-25.0=6.0\text{ cm}^3" />. Each reading can be off by <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ cm}^3" />, so **the rise carries both errors**, <InlineMath math="\pm 1.0\text{ cm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="16.7\%" /> of it. The balance adds only <InlineMath math="0.02\%" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{54.32}{6.0}=9.05\text{ g cm}^{-3}\\
\frac{1.0}{6.0}\times100\%&=16.7\%\\
9.05\pm16.7\%&\Rightarrow 7.54\text{ to }10.56\text{ g cm}^{-3}
\end{aligned}" />

So the cylinder cannot tell the three metals apart. A density bottle or a burette would.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="8.3\%" /> counts one reading of the cylinder, though the rise needs two.
- Copper alone ignores the uncertainty. **Any density from <InlineMath math="7.54" /> to <InlineMath math="10.56\text{ g cm}^{-3}" /> fits the data**.
- <InlineMath math="1.75\text{ g cm}^{-3}" /> divides by the final reading, <InlineMath math="31.0\text{ cm}^3" />, which includes the water.

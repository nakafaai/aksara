# Read the silica off the composition, then match each glass to its property

Silica is <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of the mass. Boron oxide makes the glass **expand very little on heating**, so it survives a flame or boiling water without cracking. In photochromic glass, light gives silver ions electrons, and the tiny silver atoms that form darken the lens. In dim light the change reverses.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Borosilicate glass is chosen for beakers because it expands less than soda-lime glass, not more.
- <InlineMath math="360\text{ kg}" /> uses the silica share of soda-lime glass, about <InlineMath math="72\%" />, not the <InlineMath math="80\%" /> in the chart.
- Silver ions gain electrons to become silver atoms. **Gaining electrons is reduction**.

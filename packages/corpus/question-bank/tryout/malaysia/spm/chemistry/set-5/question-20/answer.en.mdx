# Both carbonates lose their carbon dioxide in the furnace, not just the sodium carbonate

The glass holds <InlineMath math="150\text{ g}" /> of sodium oxide, <InlineMath math="2.42\text{ mol}" />, and each mole comes from one mole of sodium carbonate, <InlineMath math="256\text{ g}" />. **The limestone breaks down too**: its <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> of calcium oxide, <InlineMath math="1.79\text{ mol}" />, also releases one carbon dioxide per formula unit. Together they give <InlineMath math="4.21\text{ mol}" /> of carbon dioxide.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="150\text{ g}" /> is the sodium oxide itself. **The furnace is fed sodium carbonate, which is heavier**.
- <InlineMath math="106\text{ g}" /> counts only the sodium carbonate's carbon dioxide and forgets the limestone.
- <InlineMath math="513\text{ g}" /> takes two sodium carbonate units per sodium oxide, but one unit already holds two sodium atoms.

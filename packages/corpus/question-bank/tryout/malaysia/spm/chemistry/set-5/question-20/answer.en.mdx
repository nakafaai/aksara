# Each oxide's share of the glass gives its moles, and each equation turns them into a raw material

The chart gives <InlineMath math="65\text{ kg}" /> of boron oxide and <InlineMath math="20\text{ kg}" /> of sodium oxide. **Two boric acid molecules make one boron oxide unit**, so the boric acid moles double. Each sodium oxide unit comes from one sodium carbonate, which releases one carbon dioxide. The volume in cubic decimetres is large enough to give in cubic metres. Boron oxide is what lets the glass expand very little on heating, so beakers survive a flame.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="57.6\text{ kg}" /> takes one boric acid per boron oxide unit. **The equation needs two**.
- <InlineMath math="15.5\text{ m}^3" /> gives each sodium oxide two carbon dioxide molecules, but one sodium carbonate releases one.
- <InlineMath math="7.74\text{ dm}^3" /> works in grams as if the batch were <InlineMath math="500\text{ g}" />. **The <InlineMath math="20\text{ kg}" /> of sodium oxide is <InlineMath math="20\,000\text{ g}" />**.

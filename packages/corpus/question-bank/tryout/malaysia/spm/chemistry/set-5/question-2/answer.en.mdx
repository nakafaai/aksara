# The solid's slope gives the heater's power, and both trends fix when melting starts and ends

The power is unknown, so get it from the part of the graph where only the solid warms: <InlineMath math="20^\circ\text{C}" /> a minute in <InlineMath math="12.8\text{ g}" /> of solid. **The flat stretch does not start or end on a grid point**. The solid's line reaches <InlineMath math="80^\circ\text{C}" /> half a minute after the reading at <InlineMath math="2\text{ min}" />, and the liquid's line leaves <InlineMath math="80^\circ\text{C}" /> three quarters of a minute before the reading at <InlineMath math="9\text{ min}" />. During melting the heat pulls molecules apart against their attraction, so their kinetic energy, and the temperature, stay constant.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="16.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> counts only the grid points at <InlineMath math="80^\circ\text{C}" />, from <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="8\text{ min}" />, and misses the melting that happens before and after them.
- A constant temperature means the average kinetic energy is constant. **The heat raises the potential energy of the molecules as they separate**.
- <InlineMath math="23.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> times the whole gap from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="9\text{ min}" />, which includes warming the solid and the liquid.

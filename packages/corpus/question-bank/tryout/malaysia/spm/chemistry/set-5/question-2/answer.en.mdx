# The flat part of the curve is where melting happens

The temperature holds at <InlineMath math="80^\circ\text{C}" /> from minute <InlineMath math="3.2" /> to <InlineMath math="4.8" />. **During those <InlineMath math="96\text{ s}" /> every joule goes into separating the molecules**, not into speeding them up, so the temperature, a measure of their average kinetic energy, stops rising.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t&=(4.8-3.2)\times60=96\text{ s}\\
E&=40\times96=3840\text{ J}=3.84\text{ kJ}\\
n&=\frac{25.6}{128}=0.200\text{ mol}\\
\frac{3.84}{0.200}&=19.2\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

So one mole needs <InlineMath math="19.2\text{ kJ}" /> to melt.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A rising kinetic energy would raise the temperature, which the plateau shows does not happen.
- <InlineMath math="3.84\text{ kJ}" /> melts the sample, <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" />. **Dividing by the moles gives the value per mole**.
- <InlineMath math="38.4\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> reads the start of the plateau, minute <InlineMath math="3.2" />, as its length.

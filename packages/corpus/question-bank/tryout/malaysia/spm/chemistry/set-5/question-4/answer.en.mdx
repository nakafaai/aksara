# Only the carbide makes gas

Calcium oxide reacts with water too, but quietly, with no gas. **Every bubble of ethyne comes from one calcium carbide unit**, so <InlineMath math="\tfrac{480}{24\,000}=0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of ethyne means <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of carbide, <InlineMath math="0.0200\times64=1.28\text{ g}" /> of the <InlineMath math="1.84\text{ g}" />. That is <InlineMath math="69.6\%" />. Dividing the ethyne's own mass, <InlineMath math="0.520\text{ g}" />, by the sample gives <InlineMath math="28.3\%" />, which measures the gas, not the solid it came from. The remaining <InlineMath math="30.4\%" /> is the oxide, the other share of the sample.

# Both solids end as calcium hydroxide

The <InlineMath math="0.560\text{ g}" /> left is calcium oxide, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and it slakes to calcium hydroxide just as the carbide does. **Each calcium atom, whichever solid it started in, ends in one <InlineMath math="\text{Ca(OH)}_2" />**.

# Breaking bonds costs energy, forming bonds releases it, and the difference is the enthalpy change

Breaking one <InlineMath math="\text{H–H}" /> and one <InlineMath math="\text{Cl–Cl}" /> bond takes in energy, and forming two <InlineMath math="\text{H–Cl}" /> bonds gives out more, so the reaction is exothermic. **That <InlineMath math="-184\text{ kJ}" /> belongs to two moles of hydrogen chloride**, so one mole is <InlineMath math="-92\text{ kJ}" />. One mole of chlorine reacts in that equation, so <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine releases a tenth of <InlineMath math="184\text{ kJ}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="+184" /> subtracts the wrong way. **More energy is released by forming bonds than is taken in by breaking them**.
- <InlineMath math="9.2\text{ kJ}" /> uses the value per mole of hydrogen chloride, though <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine makes <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ mol}" /> of it.
- <InlineMath math="-184\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> gives the value for the whole equation, which forms two moles of hydrogen chloride.

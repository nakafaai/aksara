# Each cup gives one enthalpy change

Both solids weigh in at <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and the acid outnumbers what either needs. **The <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ g}" /> of solution is what warms up**, not the solid dropped into it, and a rise in temperature means heat left the reaction, so both signs are negative.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta H_1&=-\tfrac{50.0\times4.2\times22.0}{0.0100}=-462\,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}=-462\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\\
\Delta H_2&=-\tfrac{50.0\times4.2\times7.0}{0.0100}=-147\,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}=-147\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

# Building the cycle

Magnesium never burns in either cup, so the target reaction has to be assembled. Send magnesium through the acid (<InlineMath math="\Delta H_1" />), burn the hydrogen it gives off into water (<InlineMath math="-286\text{ kJ}" />), then run Experiment II backwards so that magnesium chloride and water turn into magnesium oxide and acid. **Running a reaction backwards flips the sign of its <InlineMath math="\Delta H" />**, so that leg is <InlineMath math="+147\text{ kJ}" />, and the acid and magnesium chloride cancel out.

Leave the hydrogen unburnt and the cycle stops at <InlineMath math="-315\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, with no oxygen ever joining the magnesium. Add <InlineMath math="\Delta H_2" /> without flipping it and you reach <InlineMath math="-895\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, which treats dissolving the oxide as a second burst of heat.

# From one mole to a six gram strip

The strip is <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ mol}" /> of magnesium, and **each mole that burns releases <InlineMath math="601\text{ kJ}" />**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta H_f(\text{MgO})&=\Delta H_1+(-286)-\Delta H_2=-462-286+147=-601\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\\
Q&=\tfrac{6.00}{24}\times601=150\text{ kJ}
\end{aligned}" />

Dividing <InlineMath math="6.00\text{ g}" /> by <InlineMath math="40" />, the molar mass of the oxide, gives <InlineMath math="90.2\text{ kJ}" />, but the stem weighs the metal, not the ash.

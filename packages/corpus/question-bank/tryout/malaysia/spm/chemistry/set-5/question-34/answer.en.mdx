# The water runs out first, so it sets the heat, and only part of that heat reaches the soup

The equation uses the two in a one to one ratio. **There is less water than calcium oxide, so the water decides how much reacts**, and a quarter of the calcium oxide is left over. Of the heat from <InlineMath math="0.750\text{ mol}" />, seven tenths warms the soup.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="45.5^\circ\text{C}" /> lets all <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ mol}" /> of calcium oxide react. **Only <InlineMath math="0.750\text{ mol}" /> of water is there to react with it**.
- <InlineMath math="48.8^\circ\text{C}" /> uses the water correctly but puts all the heat into the soup instead of <InlineMath math="70\%" />.
- <InlineMath math="65.0^\circ\text{C}" /> lets all the calcium oxide react and loses no heat.

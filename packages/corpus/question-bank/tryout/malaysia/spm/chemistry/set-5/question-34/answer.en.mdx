# The pouch runs out first

The can holds <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ mol}" /> of calcium oxide but only <InlineMath math="0.750\text{ mol}" /> of water, and they react one to one. **The water is the limit**, so a quarter of the oxide stays behind as dry powder. Working from the oxide's <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ mol}" /> puts <InlineMath math="65.0\text{ kJ}" /> into play and, after the loss, the <InlineMath math="45.5^\circ\text{C}" /> option.

# Seven tenths reaches the soup

The reaction releases <InlineMath math="0.750\times65=48.75\text{ kJ}" />, but **only <InlineMath math="70\%" /> of it ends up in the soup**: <InlineMath math="34.1\text{ kJ}" />. Skip that cut and the soup seems to rise by <InlineMath math="48.8^\circ\text{C}" />.

# Joules into degrees

Now divide by what actually warms up. **Only the <InlineMath math="250\text{ g}" /> of soup goes in the denominator**, with the soup's own <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ J g}^{-1}\,^\circ\text{C}^{-1}" /> rather than water's value.

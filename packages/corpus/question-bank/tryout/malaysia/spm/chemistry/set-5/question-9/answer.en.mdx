# The chloride's formula names the element, and the vapour's molar mass shows two units joined

The chlorine gained is <InlineMath math="1.065\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of atoms. Only <InlineMath math="n=3" /> gives a Period <InlineMath math="3" /> element, aluminium. **The vapour's molar mass is twice that of <InlineMath math="\text{AlCl}_3" />**, so each particle is a dimer in which two chlorine atoms bridge the aluminium atoms with dative bonds. Aluminium oxide is amphoteric.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{AlCl}_3" /> is the empirical formula. **The vapour's molar mass is <InlineMath math="267" />, twice <InlineMath math="133.5" />**.
- An oxide that reacts only with acids is basic, like magnesium oxide. **Aluminium oxide also dissolves in sodium hydroxide**.
- An oxide that reacts only with alkalis is acidic, like the oxides of phosphorus and sulfur, which lie further right in Period <InlineMath math="3" />.

# Try each charge until the atomic mass lands on a Period <InlineMath math="3" /> element

The chlorine gained is <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of atoms. **Each possible formula gives a different atomic mass for E**, and only one lands on a real Period <InlineMath math="3" /> element.

Aluminium's chloride is covalent enough to fume and hydrolyse, and its oxide is amphoteric.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Aluminium oxide dissolves in sodium hydroxide too, which is what amphoteric means.
- Phosphorus would need an atomic mass of <InlineMath math="31" />, which no formula here gives.
- Magnesium forms <InlineMath math="\text{MgCl}_2" />, which would make E's atomic mass <InlineMath math="18" />. **The masses fix the charge, not a guess**.

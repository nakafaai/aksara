# Which element E is

Think of a ring of keys. You try each one until one turns the lock. Here the keys are the possible valencies. The chlorine in the chloride weighs <InlineMath math="1.335-0.27=1.065\text{ g}" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine atoms. If E has valency <InlineMath math="v" />, then <InlineMath math="0.27\text{ g}" /> of E is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.0300}{v}\text{ mol}" />, so its relative atomic mass must be <InlineMath math="9v" />. **Only valency three fits a real Period <InlineMath math="3" /> atom: aluminium, <InlineMath math="27" />**.

# Weighing the vapour

Now count the particles in the vapour and see how heavy one mole of them is. The vapour is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.190}{38}=0.00500\text{ mol}" />, so one mole weighs <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1.335}{0.00500}=267\text{ g}" />. That is exactly twice <InlineMath math="\text{AlCl}_3" />. **So two <InlineMath math="\text{AlCl}_3" /> units hold hands and fly around as one <InlineMath math="\text{Al}_2\text{Cl}_6" /> molecule**. Had the vapour been plain <InlineMath math="\text{AlCl}_3" />, the <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of aluminium would have filled <InlineMath math="380\text{ cm}^3" />, twice what was measured.

# Splitting doubles the count

Snap five chocolate bars in half and you hold ten pieces. The same thing happens at <InlineMath math="800^\circ\text{C}" />. **Each <InlineMath math="\text{Al}_2\text{Cl}_6" /> breaks into two <InlineMath math="\text{AlCl}_3" />, so the number of gas particles doubles**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{Al}_2\text{Cl}_6)&=\tfrac{0.190}{38}=0.00500\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ n(\text{AlCl}_3)=2\times0.00500=0.0100\text{ mol}\\
V_{800}&=0.0100\times88=0.880\text{ dm}^3=880\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

Scaling the old <InlineMath math="190\text{ cm}^3" /> by <InlineMath math="\tfrac{88}{38}" /> alone keeps the old number of particles. That stops at <InlineMath math="440\text{ cm}^3" />, half the real volume.

# How aluminium oxide behaves

Aluminium oxide is like a kid who plays happily on both teams. Aluminium sits right where Period <InlineMath math="3" /> changes from metals to non-metals. **Its oxide reacts with hydrochloric acid and with sodium hydroxide solution too, so it is amphoteric**. Treating it like sodium oxide or magnesium oxide, which only react with acids, misses the alkali side.

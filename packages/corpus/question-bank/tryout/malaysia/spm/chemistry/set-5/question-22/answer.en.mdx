# Follow the electrons from the aluminium to the oxygen, and the oxygen to the carbon

Every aluminium ion takes three electrons at the cathode, and the anode gives up the same number of electrons as oxide ions turn into oxygen. **Each oxygen molecule then burns one carbon atom to carbon dioxide**, so the anodes must be replaced regularly.

Pure aluminium oxide melts above <InlineMath math="2000^\circ\text{C}" />. Dissolved in cryolite it can be electrolysed near <InlineMath math="950^\circ\text{C}" />, which saves a great deal of energy.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="720\text{ kg}" /> makes carbon monoxide, one oxygen atom for each carbon. **The question says carbon dioxide**.
- Cryolite does not make a solid conduct. It dissolves the oxide so that its ions are free to move in a melt.
- <InlineMath math="480\text{ kg}" /> pairs one carbon with each aluminium atom instead of following the electrons.

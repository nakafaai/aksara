# The purple beaker sets the budget

Beaker Q holds <InlineMath math="0.0200\times0.0200=4.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" /> of manganate(VII) ions, and its half-equation shows **each one needs five electrons to become a pale manganese(II) ion**. When the purple has just gone, <InlineMath math="2.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of electrons have crossed the wire. The iodide in P could have supplied <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, five times more, so the manganate(VII) is what stops the cell.

Give each manganate(VII) ion a single electron and the whole budget shrinks to <InlineMath math="4.00\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" />, which is where <InlineMath math="0.0508\text{ g}" /> of iodine and <InlineMath math="0.192\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> of iodide come from.

# Iodide pays in pairs

Over in P, **two iodide ions hand over two electrons and join into one iodine molecule**. So <InlineMath math="2.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of electrons means the same amount of iodide used but only <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of iodine, <InlineMath math="M_r=254" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(e^-)&=5\times(0.0200\times0.0200)=2.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}\\
m(\text{I}_2)&=\tfrac{2.00\times10^{-3}}{2}\times254=0.254\text{ g},\quad c(\text{I}^-)=\tfrac{0.0100-2.00\times10^{-3}}{0.0500}=0.160\text{ mol dm}^{-3}
\end{aligned}" />

If every electron made a whole iodine molecule, the iodine would double to <InlineMath math="0.508\text{ g}" /> and the iodide would drain to <InlineMath math="0.120\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The brown colour in P is real, but there is only half that much behind it.

# Which way the wire carries them

The electrons are released in P, so the carbon rod in P is the negative terminal, and **they travel through the galvanometer to the rod in Q**, where the manganate(VII) ions collect them. The salt bridge only moves ions so both beakers stay neutral. An arrow from Q to P would describe iodine turning back into iodide.

# The two isomers use different amounts of dichromate, so two equations fix the mixture

Both alcohols have a molar mass of <InlineMath math="60" />, so the mixture is <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" />. **Propan-1-ol is oxidised to an acid and needs twice the dichromate per mole that propan-2-ol needs**, since propan-2-ol stops at the ketone propanone. Solving the two equations gives <InlineMath math="0.0090\text{ mol}" /> of propan-1-ol. Only propanoic acid is acidic, and propanone is neutral.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.36\text{ g}" /> is the mass of propan-2-ol. **The isomer that needs more dichromate is the larger share here**.
- Propanone is a ketone, which has no acidic hydrogen and leaves litmus unchanged.
- <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ g}" /> treats the whole mixture as propan-1-ol, which would need <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of the dichromate, not <InlineMath math="80.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

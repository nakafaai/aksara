# Reading the burette twice

With phenolphthalein the reading runs from <InlineMath math="0.50" /> to <InlineMath math="22.50\text{ cm}^3" />, a titre of <InlineMath math="22.00\text{ cm}^3" />. Methyl orange carries it on to <InlineMath math="25.50" />, so **the second titre is only <InlineMath math="3.00\text{ cm}^3" />**, measured from where the first stopped, not from zero.

# The second titre counts the carbonate

By the time phenolphthalein turns colourless, every carbonate ion has become one hydrogencarbonate ion. **Methyl orange then needs exactly one more hydrogen ion for each of them**, so <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mmol}" /> of acid in the second stage means <InlineMath math="0.300\text{ mmol}" /> of carbonate, <InlineMath math="0.0120\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Halving the second titre, as if both steps of the carbonate happened after the first colour change, gives <InlineMath math="0.00600" />.

# Taking the carbonate out of the first titre

The first <InlineMath math="2.20\text{ mmol}" /> of acid did two jobs at once. It neutralised the hydroxide and **gave each carbonate ion its first hydrogen ion**, so the hydroxide is <InlineMath math="2.20-0.300=1.90\text{ mmol}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0760\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Calling the whole first titre hydroxide gives <InlineMath math="0.0880" />, and taking the carbonate off twice gives <InlineMath math="0.0640" />.

# Back to the gas the bottle swallowed

**Each carbonate ion was made from one carbon dioxide molecule**, so every <InlineMath math="\text{dm}^3" /> of the solution took in <InlineMath math="0.0120\text{ mol}" /> of the gas, <InlineMath math="288\text{ cm}^3" /> at room conditions.

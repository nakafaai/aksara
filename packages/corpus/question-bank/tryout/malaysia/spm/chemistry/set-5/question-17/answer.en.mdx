# The rise in temperature gives the enthalpy change, and the warm liquid shrinks as it cools

The water warms, so dissolving releases heat and **the enthalpy change is negative**. A liquid contracts as it cools, so a flask filled to the mark while warm holds less than <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" /> once cold. The same <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> in a smaller volume is more concentrated.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A temperature rise means heat is released, so the sign is negative, not positive.
- A less concentrated solution would need the volume to grow. **Cooling makes the liquid shrink**.
- <InlineMath math="-4.45\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ mol}" /> instead of <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />, and the flask's volume does depend on temperature.

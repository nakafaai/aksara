# Find the ratio of atoms first, then remember chlorine comes in pairs

The gain in mass is the chlorine that joined. **Dividing each mass by its atomic mass gives the ratio of atoms**, and a liquid that boils at <InlineMath math="114^\circ\text{C}" /> is made of small molecules, not ions.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{SnCl}_2" /> would need only <InlineMath math="1.42\text{ g}" /> of chlorine, and an ionic chloride would be a high-melting solid.
- <InlineMath math="1.92\text{ dm}^3" /> counts chlorine atoms as gas particles. **Chlorine gas is <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2" />, two atoms a molecule**.
- A liquid of covalent molecules has no ions to carry charge, so it cannot conduct.

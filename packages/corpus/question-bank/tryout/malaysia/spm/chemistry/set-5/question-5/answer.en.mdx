# What the tube really used

Picture a jar of fifty sweets. If ten are still in the jar at the end, you ate forty, not fifty. The chlorine works the same way. You put in <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> is still there, so **only <InlineMath math="0.960\text{ dm}^3" /> of chlorine actually reacted**. That is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.960}{24}=0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2" />. Putting the full <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> onto the tin's mass gives <InlineMath math="5.93\text{ g}" />, but the leftover chlorine never joined in.

# Chlorine comes in pairs

Chlorine molecules are like socks sold in packs of two. Each <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2" /> pack holds two chlorine atoms. So **<InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of molecules hands over <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" /> of chlorine atoms**. The tin is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2.38}{119}=0.0200\text{ mol}" />. Now compare: <InlineMath math="0.0800" /> to <InlineMath math="0.0200" /> is four chlorine atoms on every tin atom, so the chloride is <InlineMath math="\text{SnCl}_4" />.

If you count the packs as single socks, you get only two chlorines per tin. Then you write <InlineMath math="\text{SnCl}_2" />, and <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of it weighs <InlineMath math="3.80\text{ g}" />.

# Weigh it, then look at how it behaves

A good habit is to weigh the product two ways. Both should agree.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m(\text{SnCl}_4)&=0.0200\times(119+4\times35.5)=5.22\text{ g}\\
m(\text{Sn})+m(\text{Cl}_2)&=2.38+0.0400\times71=5.22\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

Both give <InlineMath math="5.22\text{ g}" />. Now the bonding. This liquid boils at <InlineMath math="114^\circ\text{C}" />, only a bit hotter than water. **Its particles hold on to each other only loosely, so they are small covalent molecules**. A liquid of molecules has no ions moving around, so it cannot carry a current. Table salt is ionic, and it does not even melt until <InlineMath math="801^\circ\text{C}" />. That is why calling <InlineMath math="\text{SnCl}_4" /> ionic does not fit.

# Every bond joins two atoms, so halve the count per atom

Each carbon in diamond makes four bonds, but **every bond is shared by the two atoms it joins**, so diamond holds two bonds per atom. Graphite's three bonds per atom give one and a half.

Graphite's fourth electron is delocalised along the layer and forms no bond between two particular atoms, which is why graphite conducts.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{24}" /> counts every bond twice, once from each atom at its ends.
- Equal counts ignore that a graphite atom makes three bonds, not four.
- <InlineMath math="3.01\times10^{23}" /> halves the count twice. **Halve once, for the two ends of each bond**.

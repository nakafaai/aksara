# What stays on the filter paper

Stir sugar and sand into tea. The sugar disappears, the sand sits at the bottom. Here carbon disulfide plays the tea. **Small molecules like <InlineMath math="\text{S}_8" /> dissolve in an organic solvent, but a giant covalent lattice like diamond cannot**. So the <InlineMath math="6.00\text{ g}" /> on the paper is diamond. The sulfur that dissolved is what is missing, <InlineMath math="12.40-6.00=6.40\text{ g}" />.

Mix up which solid stays and you put <InlineMath math="6.00\text{ g}" /> of sulfur on the paper and <InlineMath math="6.40\text{ g}" /> of diamond in the solution. Your bond counts then come out as <InlineMath math="1.13\times10^{23}" /> for the paper and <InlineMath math="6.42\times10^{23}" /> for the solution.

# Each bond has two ends

A handshake needs two hands. If you count hands, you count every handshake twice. Bonds are the same. A carbon in diamond reaches out to four neighbours, but each bond is shared with the neighbour. **So diamond has two bonds per carbon atom**. In an <InlineMath math="\text{S}_8" /> ring, eight friends stand in a circle holding hands, and that makes eight handshakes, one bond per sulfur atom.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{C}:\ \tfrac{6.00}{12}\times2&=1.00\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ 6.02\times10^{23}\\
\text{S}_8:\ \tfrac{6.40}{256}\times8&=0.200\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ 1.20\times10^{23}
\end{aligned}" />

Counting four bonds per carbon and two per sulfur counts each bond from both ends. Both answers double, to <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{24}" /> and <InlineMath math="2.41\times10^{23}" />.

# Dissolving keeps the rings whole

Tip a box of bead bracelets into water. The bracelets drift apart, but each bracelet stays closed. Dissolving sulfur works like that. **The solvent only pulls whole <InlineMath math="\text{S}_8" /> rings away from each other, so it beats the weak forces between molecules and breaks <InlineMath math="0" /> covalent bonds**. If the rings had opened, the answer would be <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{23}" />, but then letting the solvent evaporate could not give you rhombic sulfur back, and it does.

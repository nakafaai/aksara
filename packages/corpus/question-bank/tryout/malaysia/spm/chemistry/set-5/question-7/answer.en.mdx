# The cheapest bag is not the cheapest nitrogen

Each formula's nitrogen share sets how much of the bag is useful. **Two nitrogen atoms in each formula unit, but very different masses**, so the light urea carries the most. Dividing each price by its nitrogen gives the cost of one kilogram of nitrogen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.6\text{ kg}" /> picks the cheapest bag, but its nitrogen costs the most per kilogram.
- <InlineMath math="8.75\text{ kg}" /> counts only the ammonium nitrogen. **The nitrate ion carries a nitrogen atom too**.
- <InlineMath math="11.7\text{ kg}" /> counts one nitrogen in urea, though <InlineMath math="\text{CO(NH}_2)_2" /> has two.

# Concentration decides the anode, and sodium never leaves the solution

At the anode, chloride ions are so concentrated that **chlorine is discharged instead of oxygen**. At the cathode, sodium ions stay in solution because water is far easier to reduce, so hydrogen forms and leaves hydroxide ions behind. Each gas takes two electrons per molecule.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Oxygen forms from dilute sodium chloride solution. **A concentrated solution discharges chloride ions**.
- Sodium metal forms only from molten sodium chloride. In water, water is reduced instead.
- <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ dm}^3" /> gives each molecule one electron, but each <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2" /> or <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2" /> needs two.

# The chloride runs out a third of the way through, and the anode switches to oxygen

The solution holds only <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of chloride ions, enough for <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> of the electrons. **Once the chloride is used up, hydroxide ions are discharged at the anode instead**, and each oxygen molecule takes four electrons, not two. At the cathode hydrogen forms the whole time. The sodium ions stay with the hydroxide ions left behind.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.6\text{ dm}^3" /> at the anode treats all the electrons as making chlorine, but only <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of chloride is present.
- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ dm}^3" /> stops the anode gas when the chloride runs out. **The current keeps flowing and discharges hydroxide ions**.
- The hydrogen ions removed at the cathode outnumber the hydroxide ions removed at the anode, so <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of hydroxide ions stays in solution with the sodium ions.

# The electrons magnesium gives fix the charge on each metal ion, and the mass fixes the metal

The magnesium is <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" />, and **each magnesium atom gives two electrons**, <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> in all. These reduce <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of metal ions, so each ion takes three electrons and <InlineMath math="n=3" />. The metal's molar mass is <InlineMath math="1.97\div0.0100=197" />, which is gold.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="n=2" /> swaps one metal ion for each magnesium atom. **Count the electrons, not the atoms**.
- Platinum's relative atomic mass is <InlineMath math="195" />, but the data give <InlineMath math="197" />.
- Platinum often forms <InlineMath math="\text{Pt}^{4+}" />, but the electron count gives a charge of <InlineMath math="+3" /> and the mass gives <InlineMath math="197" />.

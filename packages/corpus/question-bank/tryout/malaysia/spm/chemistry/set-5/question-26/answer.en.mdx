# The hydrogen measures the acid

Zinc reacts with both the gold(III) ions and the acid, so the gas is the one clean measurement. The <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> collected comes to <InlineMath math="5.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen, and **each hydrogen molecule took two hydrogen ions**, so the waste held <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of acid, <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> in <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />. One hydrogen ion per molecule gives <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# The rest of the zinc buys gold

Of the <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1.30}{65}=0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of zinc, <InlineMath math="5.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> went to the acid. The other <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> handed its electrons to gold(III) ions, and since **three zinc atoms reduce two gold ions**, that recovers <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of gold, <InlineMath math="1.97\text{ g}" />.

Had no hydrogen formed and all the zinc gone to the gold, there would be <InlineMath math="2.63\text{ g}" />. Giving each dissolved zinc atom its own gold atom gives <InlineMath math="3.94\text{ g}" />, but a zinc atom carries only two electrons and a gold ion needs three.

# Every zinc atom ends up as an ion

Whichever partner it met, **each zinc atom that dissolved is now a zinc ion in the solution**: <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> in <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ dm}^3" />, <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

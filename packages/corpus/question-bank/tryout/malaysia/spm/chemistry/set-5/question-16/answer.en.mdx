# The tests name copper(II) nitrate, the residue counts its water, and the water does not stay a gas

The brown ring shows nitrate, and the deep blue solution in excess ammonia shows copper(II). The black residue is copper(II) oxide, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, so <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of Y weighs <InlineMath math="2.42\text{ g}" />. The extra <InlineMath math="54" /> in its molar mass is three water molecules. **At room conditions the water of crystallisation condenses**, so only nitrogen dioxide and oxygen stay as gas.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> counts only the nitrogen dioxide. **The nitrate also releases one oxygen molecule for every two copper ions**.
- <InlineMath math="1320\text{ cm}^3" /> also counts the <InlineMath math="0.0300\text{ mol}" /> of water, which is a liquid at room conditions.
- Anhydrous copper(II) nitrate would weigh only <InlineMath math="1.88\text{ g}" /> for <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of copper, not <InlineMath math="2.42\text{ g}" />.

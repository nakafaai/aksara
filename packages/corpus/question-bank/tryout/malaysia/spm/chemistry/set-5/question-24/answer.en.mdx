# Average the oxidation number over the sulfur atoms, then use the two-to-one ratio

The oxygen atoms and the charge fix the total for the sulfur atoms, and **dividing by the number of sulfur atoms gives an average**, which need not be a whole number. Iodine is reduced from <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="-1" />, and two thiosulfate ions react with each iodine molecule.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.080\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> reacts one thiosulfate ion with each iodine molecule.
- <InlineMath math="+4" /> to <InlineMath math="+5" /> gives all the sulfur's charge to one atom. **Share the total over the two or four sulfur atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> halves the iodine twice, as if four thiosulfate ions took each molecule.

# Hydrogen sulfide runs out first, and its sulfur is the part that is oxidised

Each gas is <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />. **Hydrogen sulfide is needed twice as fast**, so it runs out when only <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of sulfur dioxide has reacted. That gives <InlineMath math="0.0150\text{ mol}" /> of sulfur. In hydrogen sulfide sulfur rises from <InlineMath math="-2" /> to <InlineMath math="0" />, so it is oxidised, while sulfur from sulfur dioxide falls from <InlineMath math="+4" /> to <InlineMath math="0" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.96\text{ g}" /> lets all the sulfur dioxide react. **The hydrogen sulfide runs out first**.
- One third is the sulfur from sulfur dioxide, which is reduced, not oxidised.
- <InlineMath math="0.32\text{ g}" /> counts only the sulfur from hydrogen sulfide and leaves out the sulfur dioxide's share.

# Boron is two electrons short, and nitrogen has a spare pair

In <InlineMath math="\text{BF}_3" /> boron has only six electrons around it, and the nitrogen of ammonia has one lone pair. **Nitrogen gives both electrons of that pair to boron, a dative bond**, so the pair becomes a bonding pair and nitrogen keeps no lone pair.

That is <InlineMath math="3+1+3=7" /> bonding pairs. Each fluorine keeps three lone pairs, <InlineMath math="3\times3=9" />, and nitrogen and boron keep none.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6" /> bonding pairs leaves out the <InlineMath math="\text{N–B}" /> bond, which is a real covalent bond once formed.
- <InlineMath math="10" /> lone pairs still counts nitrogen's pair. **It is now shared, so it is no longer a lone pair**.
- Boron has no lone pair to give. The donor in a dative bond is the atom with the spare pair, nitrogen.

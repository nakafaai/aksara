# Going back means climbing from the products to the same peak

The catalyst lowers the peak to <InlineMath math="58\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> above the reactants, and the products lie <InlineMath math="98\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> below them. **The reverse reaction starts at the products, so it climbs the whole <InlineMath math="58+98" />**. The heat released depends only on how much hydrogen peroxide decomposes, not on the catalyst.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="17\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> is how much the catalyst lowers the peak, not the reverse activation energy.
- <InlineMath math="9.8\text{ kJ}" /> halves the heat, as if <InlineMath math="98\text{ kJ}" /> belonged to two moles. **The value is per mole of hydrogen peroxide**.
- <InlineMath math="173\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> uses the peak without the catalyst, though the question asks about the catalysed reaction.

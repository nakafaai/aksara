# Each formula unit gives two hydroxide ions, and a hundredfold dilution drops the pH by two

Barium hydroxide releases **two hydroxide ions per formula unit**, so the hydroxide concentration is twice the solution's. Diluting <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" /> makes it a hundred times weaker, which moves pOH up by two.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12.4" /> uses one hydroxide ion per formula unit.
- <InlineMath math="11.7" /> treats the dilution as tenfold. **From <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> to <InlineMath math="1000\text{ cm}^3" /> is a hundredfold**.
- <InlineMath math="1.3" /> and <InlineMath math="3.3" /> are the pOH values, which an alkali's pH is not.

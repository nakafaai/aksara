# Each magnesium atom ends in one of two products, so set up two equations

If every atom became the oxide, the product would weigh <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ g}" />, and if every atom became the nitride, <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ g}" />. **The measured mass sits between, and where it sits tells the split**. Let <InlineMath math="x" /> moles of magnesium form the oxide and <InlineMath math="y" /> moles form the nitride.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.3\%" /> treats the missing <InlineMath math="0.04\text{ g}" /> as the nitride, but the nitride's own mass is not the shortfall.
- <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ g}" /> forgets that one formula unit of nitride holds three magnesium atoms.
- <InlineMath math="6.7\%" /> compares formula units of nitride, <InlineMath math="0.0020\text{ mol}" />, with magnesium atoms. **Count magnesium atoms on both sides**.

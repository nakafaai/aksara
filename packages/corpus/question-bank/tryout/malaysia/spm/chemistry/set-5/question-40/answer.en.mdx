# One cube, measured twice

The edge is <InlineMath math="1.0\times10^{-8}\text{ m}" /> or <InlineMath math="1.0\times10^{-6}\text{ cm}" />. **Keep each quantity in the unit its data use**: the density wants cubic centimetres, the surface wants square metres. One cube then weighs <InlineMath math="1.93\times10^{-17}\text{ g}" /> and offers <InlineMath math="6.0\times10^{-16}\text{ m}^2" /> over its six faces. Mixing the two units shrinks the answer to <InlineMath math="9.65\times10^{-7}\text{ g}" />.

# How many cubes cover the bed

Thirty square metres needs <InlineMath math="5.0\times10^{16}" /> cubes, and **the gold mass is just that count times one cube's mass**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m_1&=19.3\times(1.0\times10^{-6})^3=1.93\times10^{-17}\text{ g},\quad A_1=6\times(1.0\times10^{-8})^2=6.0\times10^{-16}\text{ m}^2\\
N&=\tfrac{30.0}{6.0\times10^{-16}}=5.0\times10^{16}\ \Rightarrow\ m=5.0\times10^{16}\times1.93\times10^{-17}=0.965\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

# Atoms on the skin, atoms inside

The <InlineMath math="0.965\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="2.95\times10^{21}" /> atoms, while the surface holds <InlineMath math="3.61\times10^{20}" /> of them. **All six faces of every cube count**, which is why about one atom in eight can do chemistry. Counting a single face per cube gives <InlineMath math="2.04\%" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\tfrac{30.0/(8.30\times10^{-20})}{(0.965/197)\times6.02\times10^{23}}\times100=\tfrac{3.61\times10^{20}}{2.95\times10^{21}}\times100=12.3\%
\end{aligned}" />

# Bigger cubes hide more gold

A cube's surface grows with the edge squared but its mass with the edge cubed, so **surface per gram falls in step with the edge**. A hundred times the edge needs a hundred times the gold, <InlineMath math="96.5\text{ g}" />, and only <InlineMath math="0.123\%" /> of those atoms would sit at the surface. Squaring the hundred gives <InlineMath math="9.65\text{ kg}" />, which tracks surface, not mass.

# Cutting a cube a million times along each edge multiplies its surface a million times

Each edge holds a million nanocubes, so **the count goes as the cube of that, <InlineMath math="10^{18}" />**. Each nanocube is tiny, but together they expose a million times the original <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}^2" />, about the floor area of a large house. That huge surface is why nanogold works as a catalyst.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ m}^2" /> slips a factor of a hundred, the step between square centimetres and square metres.
- <InlineMath math="10^{6}" /> counts nanocubes along one edge only. **A cube divides in three directions**.
- <InlineMath math="6.0\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^2" /> is the original surface. Cutting exposes new faces, so the total grows.

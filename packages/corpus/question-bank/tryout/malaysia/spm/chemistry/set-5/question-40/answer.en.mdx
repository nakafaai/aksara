# Surface per gram grows as the cube gets smaller, in step with one over the edge

Each cube's surface grows with the edge squared, but its mass grows with the edge cubed. **So the surface per gram is proportional to one over the edge**. Work one cube in consistent units: the edge of <InlineMath math="10\text{ nm}" /> is <InlineMath math="10^{-8}\text{ m}" /> for the area and <InlineMath math="10^{-6}\text{ cm}" /> for the volume, since the density is per cubic centimetre.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10\,000" /> times squares the edge ratio. **The mass for a given area grows with the edge, not its square**, since area per gram goes as one over the edge.
- <InlineMath math="9.65\times10^{-7}\text{ g}" /> puts the volume in cubic metres but uses a density per cubic centimetre, a factor of <InlineMath math="10^{6}" />.
- <InlineMath math="5.79\text{ g}" /> counts one face of each cube instead of all six.

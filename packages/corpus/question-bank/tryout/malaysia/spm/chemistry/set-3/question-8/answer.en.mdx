# The mass gained is chlorine, counted first as atoms and then as molecules

The solid gained <InlineMath math="1.585 - 0.520 = 1.065" /> g, and **all of that is chlorine**: <InlineMath math="1.065 \div 35.5 = 0.030" /> mol of chlorine atoms. The metal is <InlineMath math="0.520 \div 52 = 0.010" /> mol, so the ratio is <InlineMath math="1 : 3" /> and the chloride is <InlineMath math="\text{MCl}_3" />, with M at <InlineMath math="+3" />.

Chlorine gas is <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2" />, so <InlineMath math="0.030" /> mol of atoms came from **<InlineMath math="0.015" /> mol of molecules**: <InlineMath math="0.015 \times 24 = 0.36\text{ dm}^3" />.

M stays at <InlineMath math="+3" />, so the precipitate is <InlineMath math="\text{M(OH)}_3" />, with <InlineMath math="M_r = 52 + 3 \times 17 = 103" />: <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 103 = 1.03" /> g. M, with a violet chloride and relative atomic mass <InlineMath math="52" />, is chromium.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{MCl}_2" /> does not fit: two chlorine atoms per metal atom would add only <InlineMath math="0.71" /> g, not <InlineMath math="1.065" /> g.
- <InlineMath math="0.72\text{ dm}^3" /> counts chlorine **atoms** as if they were molecules of gas.
- <InlineMath math="0.86\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="\text{M(OH)}_2" />, which would need M at <InlineMath math="+2" />, but it stays at <InlineMath math="+3" />.

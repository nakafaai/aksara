# Heat per mole, and why the rise does not change

Heat released: <InlineMath math="50 \times 4.2 \times 10.0 = 2100" /> J, or <InlineMath math="2.10" /> kJ. The copper(II) ions are limiting: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times \tfrac{50}{1000} = 0.010" /> mol. So <InlineMath math="\Delta H = -\tfrac{2.10}{0.010} = -210\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />.

With <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />, twice as many moles react and twice as much heat is released, but that heat now warms **twice the mass of solution**. The two factors cancel, so the rise stays <InlineMath math="10.0^\circ\text{C}" />. Only a change in concentration would change the rise.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Doubling the heat would double the rise only if the mass stayed the same, but **the mass doubles too**.
- <InlineMath math="-105\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> and a halved rise both treat the larger volume as if it diluted the solution, but the concentration is unchanged.
- <InlineMath math="-21.0\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol, mistaking the concentration for the amount.

# The pellets give out heat twice

Sodium hydroxide: <InlineMath math="2.0 \div 40 = 0.050" /> mol. The acid holds <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of hydrogen ions, which is more than enough, so **all <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol** dissolves and is then neutralised.

Dissolving: <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 44.5 = 2.225" /> kJ. Neutralising: <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 57.3 = 2.865" /> kJ.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.8^\circ\text{C}" /> counts only the neutralisation and **forgets** that the solid gives out heat as it dissolves.
- <InlineMath math="5.3^\circ\text{C}" /> counts only the dissolving.
- <InlineMath math="18.9^\circ\text{C}" /> neutralises all <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of acid, but only <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol of hydroxide is there to react.

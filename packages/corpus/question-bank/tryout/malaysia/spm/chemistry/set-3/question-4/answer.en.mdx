# The final volume measures only the pure part

The impurities make no gas, so **the carbon dioxide counts only the calcium carbonate**. Work back from the final volume to its mass, then compare with the sample.

<InlineMath math="\text{CaCO}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2" />

- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{CO}_2" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{0.960}{24} = 0.040\text{ mol}" />.
- Moles of <InlineMath math="\text{CaCO}_3" /> <InlineMath math="= 0.040\text{ mol}" />, mass <InlineMath math="0.040 \times 100 = 4.0\text{ g}" />.
- Purity: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{4.0}{5.0} \times 100\% = 80\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40\%" /> uses the <InlineMath math="2" /> in front of HCl, **halving the calcium carbonate**.
- <InlineMath math="96\%" /> reads <InlineMath math="960\text{ cm}^3" /> as a percentage of something, with no mole step at all.
- <InlineMath math="20\%" /> is the percentage of impurities, <InlineMath math="\dfrac{1.0}{5.0}" />.

# Match each pattern of conduction to a structure

Conduction needs **charged particles that can move**. Read each substance for which particles it has and when they move.

- P conducts only when its ions are free, molten or dissolved, and melts at <InlineMath math="801^\circ\text{C}" />: an ionic compound, sodium chloride.
- Q never conducts and melts at <InlineMath math="1710^\circ\text{C}" />: a giant covalent structure, silicon dioxide.
- R is a simple molecule that melts at <InlineMath math="-114^\circ\text{C}" /> and **forms ions only in water**: hydrogen chloride.
- S conducts as a solid through delocalised electrons and melts at <InlineMath math="1085^\circ\text{C}" />: a metal, copper.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Copper conducts as a solid, so it cannot be Q, and silicon dioxide never conducts, so it **cannot be S**.
- Glucose dissolves as molecules, not ions, so its solution does not conduct.
- Copper conducts as a solid, unlike P, and sodium chloride does not conduct as a solid, unlike S.

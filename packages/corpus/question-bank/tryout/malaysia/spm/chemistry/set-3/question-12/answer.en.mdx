# Read the structure from both clues together

A substance that never conducts and melts at <InlineMath math="1710^\circ\text{C}" /> has **no free ions or electrons** and is held together by bonds that are very hard to break. That is a giant covalent structure, like silicon dioxide.

Q melts only when a huge number of strong covalent bonds break, so its melting point is the highest of the four. P is ionic: its ions are fixed in the solid and free when molten or dissolved. S is a metal with delocalised electrons. R is a simple molecule that only forms ions when it reacts with water.

# Why the other options are wrong

- R is covalent: it does not conduct as a pure liquid. Its molecules **ionise in water**, as hydrogen chloride does, so only the solution conducts.
- S conducts as a solid, which ions cannot do. Its current is carried by delocalised electrons.
- P is ionic, but Q melts higher than P at <InlineMath math="1710^\circ\text{C}" /> against <InlineMath math="801^\circ\text{C}" />.

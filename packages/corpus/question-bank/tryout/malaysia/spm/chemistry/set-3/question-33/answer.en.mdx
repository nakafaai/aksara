Heat released: <InlineMath math="100 \times 4.2 \times 13.0 = 5460" /> J, or <InlineMath math="5.46" /> kJ. Each solution holds <InlineMath math="2.0 \times \tfrac{50}{1000} = 0.10" /> mol, so <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of water forms. The heat of neutralisation is <InlineMath math="-\tfrac{5.46}{0.10} = -54.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, negative because **heat is given out**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="-27.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> divides by <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol, adding the acid and the alkali, but they react together to make only <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of water.
- <InlineMath math="-5.46\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> is the heat in kilojoules, **not yet divided** by the moles of water.
- A positive value would mean the mixture cooled down, but its temperature rose.

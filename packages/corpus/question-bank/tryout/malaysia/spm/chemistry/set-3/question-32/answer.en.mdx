# Equal masses of alkenes hold equal masses of carbon

Every alkene is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}" />, so its carbon fraction is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12n}{14n} = 85.7\%" /> for every <InlineMath math="n" />: the **flat line** on the chart. Any <InlineMath math="5.6" /> g of alkene holds <InlineMath math="0.857 \times 5.6 = 4.8" /> g of carbon, which is <InlineMath math="0.40" /> mol.

Each carbon atom ends up in one carbon dioxide molecule, so each sample gives <InlineMath math="0.40" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="0.40 \times 44 = 17.6" /> g. Check with molecules: ethene, <InlineMath math="5.6 \div 28 = 0.20" /> mol <InlineMath math="\times 2 = 0.40" />. Propene, <InlineMath math="5.6 \div 42 = 0.133" /> mol <InlineMath math="\times 3 = 0.40" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Propene has more carbon atoms per molecule, but **fewer molecules** in the same mass. The two effects cancel exactly.
- Ethene has more molecules, but fewer carbon atoms in each, so again the totals match.
- <InlineMath math="8.8\text{ g}" /> counts <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol of carbon, the moles of ethene molecules, without the two carbon atoms in each.

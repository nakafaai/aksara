# A fixed ratio of atoms gives a fixed percentage

Every alkene is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}" />, so it holds **two hydrogen atoms for every carbon atom**, whatever its length. Its carbon percentage is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12n}{14n} \times 100\% = 85.7\%" /> for every <InlineMath math="n" />.

An alkane <InlineMath math="\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}" /> always has two more hydrogen atoms, so its percentage stays below <InlineMath math="85.7\%" />. Per carbon atom an alkene has less hydrogen to burn, so more carbon is left unburnt as soot. That is why ethene burns with a sootier flame than ethane.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The alkene line is **flat at <InlineMath math="85.7\%" />**, so the percentage does not rise.
- The alkane percentage rises from <InlineMath math="75.0\%" /> towards <InlineMath math="85.7\%" />, so it does not fall.
- Methane has the lowest percentage, <InlineMath math="75.0\%" />, of all the alkanes.

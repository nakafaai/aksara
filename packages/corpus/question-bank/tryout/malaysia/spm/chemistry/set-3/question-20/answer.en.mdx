# A smaller dent means a harder metal

The same weight falls the same height, so the ball bearing hits both blocks with the same energy. The mean dent is <InlineMath math="3.0" /> mm on copper and <InlineMath math="2.1" /> mm on bronze, so bronze **resists the dent better** and is harder.

In pure copper, identical atoms sit in orderly layers that slide over each other when a force acts. Bronze has tin atoms of a different size among them. These atoms **disturb the layers**, so the layers slide less easily and the metal deforms less.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A smaller dent shows that the metal gave way less, which means it is harder, not softer.
- Copper and tin are both metals, so the atoms are held by metallic bonding, not covalent bonds.
- Identical atoms in pure copper make the layers slide more easily, which is why pure copper is the softer metal here.

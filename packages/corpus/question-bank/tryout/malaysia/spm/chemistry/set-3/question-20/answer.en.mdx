The same weight falls the same height, so the smaller mean dent, <InlineMath math="2.1" /> mm against <InlineMath math="3.0" /> mm, shows that **bronze is harder**.

In <InlineMath math="100" /> g of bronze there are <InlineMath math="90 \div 64 = 1.41" /> mol of copper and <InlineMath math="10 \div 119 = 0.084" /> mol of tin, a ratio of <InlineMath math="1.41 \div 0.084 \approx 17" />. Each tin atom, larger than the copper atoms around it, sits among about seventeen of them. These scattered atoms **disturb the orderly layers**, so the layers cannot slide easily when a force acts.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A smaller dent means a harder metal. The ratio of <InlineMath math="9" /> also comes from the **masses**, <InlineMath math="90 \div 10" />, not from the moles.
- Copper and tin are both metals, held by metallic bonding, not covalent bonds. The ratio of <InlineMath math="9" /> is a mass ratio, not an atom ratio.
- Identical atoms let the layers slide more easily, which is why pure copper is the softer metal here.

# Name from both parents, mass from the limiting reactant

An ester takes the **alkyl part from the alcohol** and the **acid part from the carboxylic acid**: ethanol and propanoic acid give ethyl propanoate, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5" />.

At <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield, <InlineMath math="8.16\text{ g}" /> is obtained.</>}

Ethanol, <InlineMath math="M_r = 46" />, is the limiting reactant: <InlineMath math="4.6 \div 46 = 0.10" /> mol. The ester has <InlineMath math="M_r = 5(12) + 10(1) + 2(16) = 102" />, so the theoretical mass is <InlineMath math="10.2" /> g and the real mass is <InlineMath math="0.80 \times 10.2 = 8.16" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Methyl propanoate would need methanol, not ethanol.
- <InlineMath math="10.2\text{ g}" /> is the theoretical mass, before the <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield is applied.
- Propyl ethanoate **swaps the two parts**: it would come from propanol and ethanoic acid. It has the same mass, but not the same name.

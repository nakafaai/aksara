# The bromine changes the iron before the hydroxide arrives

Iron(II): <InlineMath math="0.0250 \times 0.20 = 0.0050" /> mol. **One bromine molecule oxidises two iron(II) ions**, so <InlineMath math="0.0025" /> mol of bromine is needed: <InlineMath math="0.0025 \div 0.050 = 0.050\text{ dm}^3 = 50.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

At the end point every iron ion is now iron(III). The hydroxide gives <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}^{3+} + 3\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{Fe(OH)}_3" />, a **brown** precipitate with <InlineMath math="M_r = 56 + 3 \times 17 = 107" />: <InlineMath math="0.0050 \times 107 = 0.535" /> g.

**Bromide**: each bromine molecule becomes **two bromide ions**, so <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol of bromide sits in <InlineMath math="25.0 + 50.0 = 75.0\text{ cm}^3" />: <InlineMath math="0.0050 \div 0.0750 = 0.067\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> simply matches the volume of the iron(II) solution, and green iron(II) hydroxide ignores that the bromine has **oxidised** all the iron.
- <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> uses one bromine molecule per iron ion.
- <InlineMath math="0.033\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> gives one bromide ion per bromine molecule instead of two.

# Split the reaction into two half-equations

Iron goes from <InlineMath math="+2" /> to <InlineMath math="+3" />: <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{3+} + e^-" />. Each ion **loses one electron**, so iron(II) is oxidised, and the green colour gives way to the yellowish brown of iron(III).

Bromine goes from <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="-1" /> in both atoms: <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2 + 2e^- \rightarrow 2\text{Br}^-" />. One mole of bromine therefore **accepts two moles of electrons**, which two moles of iron(II) supply.

# Why the other options are wrong

- An iron(II) ion becomes iron(III) by **losing** an electron, not gaining one.
- Bromine takes electrons, so it is the oxidising agent. Iron(II) is the reducing agent.
- A bromine molecule has two atoms, and each gains one electron, so one mole accepts two moles of electrons.

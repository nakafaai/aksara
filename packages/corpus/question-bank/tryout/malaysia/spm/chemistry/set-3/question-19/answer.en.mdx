# Every gram the flask loses is carbon dioxide

With cotton wool in the neck, the only thing that leaves is **carbon dioxide**. In the first <InlineMath math="20\text{ s}" /> the flask loses <InlineMath math="0.44" /> g: <InlineMath math="0.44 \div 44 = 0.010" /> mol, which is <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 24\,000 = 240\text{ cm}^3" />. Average rate: <InlineMath math="240 \div 20 = 12\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />.

The loss stops at <InlineMath math="0.88" /> g, so the reaction is over: <InlineMath math="0.88 \div 44 = 0.020" /> mol of carbon dioxide. One mole of carbonate gives one mole of carbon dioxide, so <InlineMath math="0.020" /> mol of magnesium carbonate, <InlineMath math="0.020 \times 84 = 1.68" /> g, was added.

**Acid**: each carbonate takes **two** nitric acid molecules, so <InlineMath math="0.020" /> mol needs <InlineMath math="0.040" /> mol of acid: <InlineMath math="0.040 \div 2.0 = 0.020\text{ dm}^3 = 20\text{ cm}^3" /> at the least. The acid was in excess, so the real volume was larger.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.84\text{ g}" /> works from the first <InlineMath math="20\text{ s}" /> only, but the carbonate keeps reacting until the loss **stops at** <InlineMath math="0.88" /> g.
- <InlineMath math="0.022" /> is the rate in grams per second, <InlineMath math="0.44 \div 20" />, written with the wrong unit.
- <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}^3" /> takes one acid molecule per carbonate, but the equation needs two.

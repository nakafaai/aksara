# Find the limiting reactant before judging any rate

The marble is <InlineMath math="2.0 \div 100 = 0.020" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{CaCO}_3" />, which needs <InlineMath math="0.040" /> mol of acid. Every experiment supplies <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol of acid, so the **marble is limiting in all four**. Each gives <InlineMath math="0.020" /> mol of carbon dioxide, which is <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> at room conditions.

Experiment III spreads the same acid through twice the volume, so its concentration is half. Particles collide less often, and the same <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> takes longer to collect.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Powder only raises the rate. The amount of marble, and so the final volume, **stays the same**.
- A higher temperature makes the reaction faster, but it cannot make more gas from the same marble.
- Experiment III has the same moles of acid as Experiment I, and the marble is limiting anyway, so the volume does not halve.

# Find the limiting reactant before you apply the yield

Aluminium: <InlineMath math="5.4 \div 27 = 0.20" /> mol. Iron(III) oxide: <InlineMath math="24.0 \div 160 = 0.15" /> mol. Two moles of aluminium need one mole of oxide, so <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol of aluminium needs only <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of oxide: **aluminium runs out first**.

Iron formed: <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol, <InlineMath math="11.2" /> g, of which <InlineMath math="80\%" /> is collected: <InlineMath math="0.80 \times 11.2 = 8.96" /> g. Oxide left: <InlineMath math="0.15 - 0.10 = 0.05" /> mol, <InlineMath math="0.05 \times 160 = 8.0" /> g. The yield cuts the iron collected, not the oxide that reacts.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="13.4\text{ g}" /> works from the oxide, <InlineMath math="0.15" /> mol making <InlineMath math="0.30" /> mol of iron, though the oxide is **in excess**.
- <InlineMath math="11.2\text{ g}" /> with no oxide left ignores both the excess oxide and the <InlineMath math="80\%" /> yield.
- <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ g}" /> left over takes one mole of oxide per mole of aluminium.

# Gas volumes react in the same ratio as moles

At the same temperature and pressure, **volumes of gases react in the ratio of their coefficients**. Two traps remain: leftover oxygen and the water, which is liquid at room temperature.

- Oxygen used: <InlineMath math="20 \times 5 = 100\text{ cm}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="150 - 100 = 50\text{ cm}^3" /> is left.
- Carbon dioxide formed: <InlineMath math="20 \times 3 = 60\text{ cm}^3" />.
- Water condenses to a liquid, so it adds no gas volume.
- Total gas: <InlineMath math="50 + 60 = 110\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> counts the carbon dioxide only and **forgets the unused oxygen**.
- <InlineMath math="150\text{ cm}^3" /> keeps the starting oxygen, as if nothing reacted.
- <InlineMath math="190\text{ cm}^3" /> counts <InlineMath math="80\text{ cm}^3" /> of steam, but water is a liquid at room temperature.

# Elastic means it springs back, not that it stretches far

An elastic material **returns to its original length** when the load is removed. The vulcanised strip goes back to <InlineMath math="10" /> cm, while the unvulcanised strip stays at <InlineMath math="18" /> cm, stretched for good.

In vulcanised rubber, sulfur atoms form cross-links between neighbouring polymer chains. Under a load the chains straighten, but the cross-links stop them sliding apart and **pull them back** when the load goes. Without cross-links, the chains slide past each other and stay in their new places.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Stretching further and staying stretched shows the unvulcanised strip is less elastic, not more.
- Vulcanisation uses only some of the double bonds to form cross-links. The strip stretches less because the **cross-links hold the chains**.
- Sulfur cross-links make sliding harder, not easier.

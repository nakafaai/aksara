# A latex particle stays apart while its charge holds

Each rubber particle in latex is wrapped in a protein membrane with a **negative charge**, so the particles repel each other and stay suspended. Hydrogen ions neutralise that charge, the particles collide and join, and the latex coagulates.

Ethanoic acid supplies hydrogen ions at once, so that sample coagulates immediately. In the untreated sample, bacteria feed on the latex and **slowly produce lactic acid**. The pH drifts down from <InlineMath math="7.0" /> and the latex coagulates near <InlineMath math="5.8" />, after about <InlineMath math="12" /> hours. Ammonia keeps the pH at <InlineMath math="10.5" />, so the charge stays and the latex stays liquid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Hydroxide ions **keep the particles charged**. That is why ammonia is added to keep latex liquid.
- The untreated sample takes about <InlineMath math="12" /> hours, and the falling pH points to acid, not oxidation.
- Acid neutralises the charge on the membrane. The rubber's double bonds stay as they are.

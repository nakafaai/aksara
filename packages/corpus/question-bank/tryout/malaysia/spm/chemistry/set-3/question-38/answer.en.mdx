# Empirical formula, molecular formula, then the chain

Atom ratio: <InlineMath math="24.0 \div 12 = 2.0" /> carbon to <InlineMath math="76.0 \div 19 = 4.0" /> fluorine, which is <InlineMath math="1 : 2" />. The empirical formula <InlineMath math="\text{CF}_2" /> has mass <InlineMath math="50" />, but the monomer has <InlineMath math="100" />, so it is **two of them**: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{F}_4" />, tetrafluoroethene.

Each chain: <InlineMath math="50\,000 \div 100 = 500" /> monomer units. An addition polymer has **the same composition as its monomer**, so <InlineMath math="76.0\%" /> of the polymer is fluorine: <InlineMath math="0.760 \times 1000 = 760" /> g.

**Fluorine per chain**: each unit carries four fluorine atoms, so a chain of <InlineMath math="500" /> units has <InlineMath math="500 \times 4 = 2000" />. Check: <InlineMath math="2000 \times 19 = 38\,000" />, which is <InlineMath math="76\%" /> of <InlineMath math="50\,000" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{CF}_2" /> is only the empirical formula, and <InlineMath math="1000" /> units divides by its mass, <InlineMath math="50" />, instead of the monomer's.
- <InlineMath math="1000" /> fluorine atoms gives each unit two, but <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{F}_4" /> carries **four**.
- <InlineMath math="380\text{ g}" /> halves the fluorine, but polymerising joins monomers without losing atoms.

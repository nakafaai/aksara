# The plateau counts the peroxide, the concentration sets the speed

The curve levels off at <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />: <InlineMath math="0.120 \div 24 = 0.0050" /> mol of oxygen. **Two moles of peroxide give one mole of oxygen**, so there were <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol of peroxide in <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ dm}^3" />: <InlineMath math="0.010 \div 0.0500 = 0.20\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

The repeat uses another <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of the same solution, so it holds the same <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol and still gives <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. Diluting it to <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> halves the concentration, so particles collide less often and the oxygen comes **more slowly**.

**Early rate**: by <InlineMath math="20\text{ s}" /> the chart shows <InlineMath math="72\text{ cm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.072 \div 24 = 0.0030" /> mol of oxygen from <InlineMath math="0.0060" /> mol of peroxide. Average rate: <InlineMath math="0.0060 \div 20 = 3.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol s}^{-1}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> takes one mole of peroxide per mole of oxygen instead of two.
- <InlineMath math="1.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol s}^{-1}" /> is the rate for **oxygen**, not for the peroxide, which is used twice as fast.
- <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> treats dilution as removing peroxide, but the repeat holds the same <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol.

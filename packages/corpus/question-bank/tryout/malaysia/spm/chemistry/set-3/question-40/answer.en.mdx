# Convert to the substance the limit counts, then scale to a kilogram

Each sodium benzoate unit carries **one benzoate group**, the same as one benzoic acid molecule. <InlineMath math="0.150 \div 144 = 0.00104" /> mol of sodium benzoate counts as <InlineMath math="0.00104 \times 122 = 0.127" /> g of benzoic acid.

The can holds <InlineMath math="0.330" /> kg, so the level is <InlineMath math="127 \div 0.330 \approx 385" /> mg per kg. That is well below <InlineMath math="1000" /> mg per kg, so the drink is within the limit. The preservative slows the growth of yeasts and moulds, which matters in a sweet, acidic drink.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ g}" /> counts the sodium benzoate itself, while the limit is **counted as benzoic acid**.
- <InlineMath math="1270\text{ mg}" /> per kg divides by <InlineMath math="0.100" /> kg instead of the can's <InlineMath math="0.330" /> kg.
- <InlineMath math="1500\text{ mg}" /> per kg makes both mistakes: it skips the conversion and uses the wrong mass of drink.

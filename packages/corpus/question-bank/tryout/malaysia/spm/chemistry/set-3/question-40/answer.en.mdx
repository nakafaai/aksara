# Dose from body mass, volume from the label

One dose is <InlineMath math="15 \times 20 = 300" /> mg. The syrup holds <InlineMath math="120" /> mg in <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ cm}^3" />, so <InlineMath math="300" /> mg is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{300}{120} \times 5.0 = 12.5\text{ cm}^3" />. With **at most four doses** a day, the limit is <InlineMath math="4 \times 12.5 = 50\text{ cm}^3" />.

Paracetamol is an **analgesic**: it relieves pain and lowers a fever, but it does not kill bacteria. Staying within four doses matters, because too much paracetamol damages the liver.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ cm}^3" /> gives only <InlineMath math="120" /> mg, the amount in one spoonful, not the <InlineMath math="300" /> mg the child's mass needs.
- An antibiotic such as penicillin kills bacteria. Paracetamol **only eases the symptoms**.
- <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ cm}^3" /> halves the label's spoonful again and gives just <InlineMath math="60" /> mg.

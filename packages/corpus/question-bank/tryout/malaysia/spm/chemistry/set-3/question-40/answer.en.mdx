# Count the preservative as benzoic acid, then compare twice

The limit counts **benzoic acid**, so convert the salt: <InlineMath math="0.150 \times \tfrac{122}{144} = 0.127" /> g per can, which is <InlineMath math="127" /> mg. The can holds <InlineMath math="0.330" /> kg of drink: <InlineMath math="127 \div 0.330 = 385" /> mg per kg.

That is under <InlineMath math="1000" /> mg per kg, so the drink is within the limit. The adult may take <InlineMath math="5 \times 60 = 300" /> mg of benzoic acid a day, so <InlineMath math="300 \div 127 = 2.4" /> cans reach it.

**Child**: <InlineMath math="5 \times 20 = 100" /> mg a day, but one can holds <InlineMath math="127" /> mg of benzoic acid, so **one can is already over** the child's intake, even though the drink is legal.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="455" /> mg and <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cans both use the mass of the **salt**, and with <InlineMath math="150" /> mg against <InlineMath math="100" /> mg the child would still be over, not under.
- <InlineMath math="2.0" /> cans with <InlineMath math="385" /> mg converts for the limit but uses the salt's <InlineMath math="150" /> mg for the daily intake.
- <InlineMath math="127" /> mg is the amount in one can, not per kilogram of drink, and that same <InlineMath math="127" /> mg is more than the child's <InlineMath math="100" /> mg.

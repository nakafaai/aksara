# Weight each isotope by how common it is

The relative atomic mass is the **abundance-weighted mean** of the isotope masses: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{75.8 \times 35 + 24.2 \times 37}{100} = \tfrac{2653 + 895.4}{100} = 35.5" />. It sits close to <InlineMath math="35" /> because that isotope is about three times as common.

The ion <InlineMath math="^{37}\text{X}^-" /> has nucleon number <InlineMath math="37" /> and proton number <InlineMath math="17" />, so it has <InlineMath math="37 - 17 = 20" /> neutrons. A charge of <InlineMath math="1-" /> means **one extra electron**: <InlineMath math="17 + 1 = 18" /> electrons.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="36.0" /> is the plain average of <InlineMath math="35" /> and <InlineMath math="37" />, which ignores the abundances. The ion also has <InlineMath math="20" /> neutrons, not <InlineMath math="18" />.
- A negative ion has more electrons than protons, so <InlineMath math="^{37}\text{X}^-" /> has <InlineMath math="18" /> electrons, not <InlineMath math="17" />.
- <InlineMath math="36.5" /> weights the heavier isotope too much, and <InlineMath math="17" /> electrons describes the neutral atom.

# An ion's charge tells you how many electrons it gained

A charge of <InlineMath math="2-" /> means the atom **gained two electrons**. Protons never change, so find them from the neutral atom.

<Mermaid
  title={<>From the Ion Back to the Atom</>}
  description={<>The ion has two extra electrons, so the atom has two fewer: count protons from the atom, then add neutrons.</>}
  chart={"flowchart LR\n  I[Y2- ion: 18 electrons, 20 neutrons] -->|remove the 2 gained electrons| A[Y atom: 16 electrons, so 16 protons]\n  A --> N[Nucleon number: 16 + 20 = 36]\n  A --> E[Atom arrangement: 2.8.6]\n  I --> F[Ion arrangement: 2.8.8]"}
/>

- Electrons in the atom: <InlineMath math="18 - 2 = 16" />, so Y has <InlineMath math="16" /> protons.
- Nucleon number: <InlineMath math="16 + 20 = 36" />.
- Atom of Y with <InlineMath math="16" /> electrons: <InlineMath math="2.8.6" />. The ion, with <InlineMath math="18" />, is <InlineMath math="2.8.8" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="38" /> takes the <InlineMath math="18" /> electrons of the ion as the number of protons.
- <InlineMath math="36" /> with <InlineMath math="2.8.8" /> gets the nucleon number right but **gives the arrangement of the ion, not the atom**.
- <InlineMath math="34" /> and <InlineMath math="2.8.4" /> subtract the two electrons twice.

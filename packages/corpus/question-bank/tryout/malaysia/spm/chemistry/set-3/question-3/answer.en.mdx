# Weight the isotopes, then pair them like two coin tosses

The relative atomic mass is the abundance-weighted mean: <InlineMath math="0.758 \times 35 + 0.242 \times 37 = 35.5" />.

Each X₂ molecule takes **two atoms at random**, so the share of each pairing is a product of the abundances:

- Mass <InlineMath math="70" />, both atoms of mass <InlineMath math="35" />: <InlineMath math="0.758^2 = 0.575" />.
- Mass <InlineMath math="72" />, one of each, in either order: <InlineMath math="2 \times 0.758 \times 0.242 = 0.367" />.
- Mass <InlineMath math="74" />, both of mass <InlineMath math="37" />: <InlineMath math="0.242^2 = 0.059" />.

Dividing by the smallest, <InlineMath math="0.575 : 0.367 : 0.059 \approx 9.8 : 6.2 : 1" />, close to <InlineMath math="9 : 6 : 1" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="36.0" /> ignores the abundances, and equal peaks would need the two isotopes to be **equally common**.
- A molecule can hold one atom of each isotope, so a peak at <InlineMath math="72" /> appears, and it is the second largest.
- The <InlineMath math="9" /> : <InlineMath math="6" /> : <InlineMath math="1" /> ratio is right, but <InlineMath math="36.5" /> weights the heavier isotope too much.

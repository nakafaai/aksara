# The flame must release more heat than the water receives

Heat the water needs: <InlineMath math="Q = 2000 \times 4.2 \times 75 = 630\,000" /> J, or <InlineMath math="630" /> kJ. The rise is <InlineMath math="100 - 25 = 75^\circ\text{C}" />.

Only <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of the flame's heat arrives, so the methane must release <InlineMath math="630 \div 0.60 = 1050" /> kJ. **Divide by the efficiency**, because the flame has to supply more than the water receives.

Methane: <InlineMath math="1050 \div 890 = 1.18" /> mol, which is <InlineMath math="1.18 \times 24 = 28.3\text{ dm}^3" />.

**Carbon dioxide**: <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}" /> gives **one carbon dioxide per methane**: <InlineMath math="1.18 \times 44 = 51.9" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10.2\text{ dm}^3" /> **multiplies** by <InlineMath math="0.60" />, which would make the flame supply less heat than the water takes in.
- <InlineMath math="17.0\text{ dm}^3" /> assumes every joule reaches the water.
- <InlineMath math="37.8\text{ dm}^3" /> heats the water through <InlineMath math="100^\circ\text{C}" /> instead of the <InlineMath math="75^\circ\text{C}" /> rise.

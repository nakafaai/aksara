# Each extra CH₂ adds the same amount

The steps are <InlineMath math="1367 - 726 = 641" />, <InlineMath math="2021 - 1367 = 654" /> and <InlineMath math="2676 - 2021 = 655\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. Each member of the series has **one more <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> group**, which burns to one more carbon dioxide and one more water molecule, so the heat grows by about <InlineMath math="650\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> each time.

Pentanol: <InlineMath math="2676 + 655 \approx 3330\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. In a school experiment, part of the heat warms the air, the can and the clamp instead of the water, and some alcohol burns incompletely. The water records **less heat than was released**, so the measured value is smaller.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Pentanol has one more carbon and two more hydrogen atoms than butanol, so it releases more heat, not the same.
- Heat lost to the can and the air is missing from the water's reading, so the value comes out **smaller**, not larger.
- The steps stay close to <InlineMath math="650\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, so the trend is a straight line, not one that grows faster.

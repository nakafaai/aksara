# Count the ions, neutralise, then dilute

The acid gives **two hydrogen ions** per molecule: <InlineMath math="2 \times 0.10 \times \tfrac{50}{1000} = 0.010" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{H}^+" />. The alkali gives <InlineMath math="0.10 \times \tfrac{50}{1000} = 0.0050" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{OH}^-" />. They neutralise one to one, leaving <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol of hydrogen ions.

Those ions now sit in <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />: <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] = 0.0050 \div 0.100 = 0.050" /> mol dm⁻³, so <InlineMath math="\text{pH} = -\log 0.050 = 1.3" />. On the chart, this mixture is the point at <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali.

# Why the other options are wrong

- pH <InlineMath math="7.0" /> assumes equal volumes of equal concentration neutralise exactly, but sulfuric acid supplies **twice the hydrogen ions**.
- pH <InlineMath math="1.0" /> leaves the excess ions in <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> and forgets that the volume doubled.
- pH <InlineMath math="0.7" /> is the acid before any alkali is added.

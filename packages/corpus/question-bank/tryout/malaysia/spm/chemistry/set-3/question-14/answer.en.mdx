# Dilute first, then count both hydrogen ions

The flask holds the same moles of acid in **twenty times the volume**, because <InlineMath math="500 \div 25.0 = 20" />. The acid concentration falls to <InlineMath math="0.10 \div 20 = 0.0050" /> mol dm⁻³.

Each <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4" /> gives **two hydrogen ions**, so <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] = 2 \times 0.0050 = 0.010" /> mol dm⁻³ and <InlineMath math="\text{pH} = -\log 0.010 = 2.0" />.

The pH rises by about <InlineMath math="0.3" /> every time the volume doubles, and by <InlineMath math="1" /> for every tenfold dilution.

# Why the other options are wrong

- pH <InlineMath math="2.3" /> comes from <InlineMath math="-\log 0.0050" />: it forgets that each acid molecule releases **two hydrogen ions**.
- pH <InlineMath math="0.7" /> is the acid before dilution, with <InlineMath math="[\text{H}^+] = 0.20" /> mol dm⁻³.
- pH <InlineMath math="1.0" /> treats the dilution as tenfold, but the volume grows twentyfold.

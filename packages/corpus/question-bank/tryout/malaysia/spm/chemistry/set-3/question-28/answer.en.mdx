# Two alcohol molecules make one hydrogen molecule

Hydrogen: <InlineMath math="0.120 \div 24 = 0.0050" /> mol. The equation makes **one hydrogen molecule from two alcohol molecules**, so there were <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol of alcohol, and <InlineMath math="M_r = 0.74 \div 0.010 = 74" />.

For <InlineMath math="\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+1}\text{OH}" />, <InlineMath math="M_r = 12n + 2n + 1 + 17 = 14n + 18" />. So <InlineMath math="14n + 18 = 74" /> and <InlineMath math="n = 4" />: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{OH}" />.

Four carbon atoms can form a **straight chain or a branched chain**, and the hydroxyl group can sit at the end or inside each one: butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol. That makes four.

**Sodium**: the equation uses **one sodium atom per alcohol molecule**, so <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol of sodium reacts, <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 23 = 0.23" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_7\text{OH}" /> has <InlineMath math="M_r = 60" />, which does not fit the <InlineMath math="74" /> the data give.
- Two isomers count only the **straight chain** and miss the two branched alcohols.
- <InlineMath math="0.12\text{ g}" /> takes one sodium atom per hydrogen molecule, but two sodium atoms give one hydrogen molecule.

# Shorten the main chain one carbon at a time

- **Six-carbon chain**: hexane.
- **Five-carbon chain with one branch**: the branch on carbon <InlineMath math="2" /> or carbon <InlineMath math="3" /> gives 2-methylpentane and 3-methylpentane.
- **Four-carbon chain with two branches**: both on carbon <InlineMath math="2" /> gives 2,2-dimethylbutane, one on each middle carbon gives 2,3-dimethylbutane.

A three-carbon chain cannot hold the remaining three carbons without making a longer chain, so there are **five isomers**.

All five have the same mass. The more branched a molecule, the more compact it is, so it **touches its neighbours over a smaller surface** and boils lower. 2,2-dimethylbutane, with both branches on one carbon, is the most compact.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Four isomers misses one of the five, usually 3-methylpentane or 2,3-dimethylbutane.
- Hexane, the straight chain, has the largest contact surface and boils **highest**, at <InlineMath math="69^\circ\text{C}" />.
- A sixth isomer such as 4-methylpentane is 2-methylpentane numbered from the wrong end, and 3-methylpentane boils at <InlineMath math="63^\circ\text{C}" />, not lowest.

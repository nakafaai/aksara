# Shorten the chain one carbon at a time

Five carbon atoms can form a straight chain (pentane), a four-carbon chain with one branch (2-methylbutane), or a three-carbon chain with two branches on the middle carbon (2,2-dimethylpropane). Any other drawing is one of these three turned around, so there are **three structural isomers**.

All three have the same mass, so the difference comes from shape. Branching makes a molecule more compact, so it **touches its neighbours over a smaller surface** and the attractive forces between molecules are weaker. The most branched isomer boils lowest.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Two isomers misses 2,2-dimethylpropane, the isomer with two branches.
- Pentane is the straight chain, with the largest contact surface, so it boils **highest**, not lowest.
- A fourth isomer, such as 3-methylbutane, is 2-methylbutane numbered from the wrong end.

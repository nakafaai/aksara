# The more electropositive metal is the one that corrodes

When two metals touch in a moist medium, the **more electropositive metal gives up electrons**. Iron is above copper, so iron loses electrons to copper faster than it would alone: <InlineMath math="\text{Fe} \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{2+} + 2e^-" />. The jelly around that nail turns the deepest blue.

Zinc and magnesium are above iron, so they give electrons instead and iron is protected. The electrons reduce water and oxygen to hydroxide ions, <InlineMath math="\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 4e^- \rightarrow 4\text{OH}^-" />, which turn the jelly pink.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Zinc is more electropositive than iron, so it **corrodes in place of iron**. This is sacrificial protection.
- Magnesium protects iron, so the jelly around that nail turns pink, not dark blue.
- Pink shows hydroxide ions. Iron(II) ions give the dark blue colour.

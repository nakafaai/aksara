# The wool gains only the oxygen it takes from the air

Oxygen: <InlineMath math="0.20 \times 1.20 = 0.24\text{ dm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.24 \div 24 = 0.010" /> mol. It all reacts, so the gas left is the rest of the air: <InlineMath math="1.20 - 0.24 = 0.96\text{ dm}^3" />.

**Four iron atoms take three oxygen molecules**: iron rusted <InlineMath math="0.010 \times \tfrac{4}{3} = 0.0133" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="0.0133 \times 56 = 0.75" /> g.

The iron was already in the wool, so the wool only gains the oxygen: <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 32 = 0.32" /> g. The rust itself weighs <InlineMath math="0.75 + 0.32 = 1.07" /> g, but most of that mass was there before.

**Fraction rusted**: <InlineMath math="0.75 \div 2.00 \times 100\% = 37\%" />, so most of the wool is still iron when the oxygen runs out.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="56\%" /> rusts <InlineMath math="1.12" /> g of iron, two iron atoms per oxygen molecule, but the ratio is **four to three**.
- <InlineMath math="0.24\text{ dm}^3" /> is the oxygen that reacted, not the gas left in the flask.
- <InlineMath math="1.07\text{ g}" /> is the mass of the rust, which counts the iron that was already there.

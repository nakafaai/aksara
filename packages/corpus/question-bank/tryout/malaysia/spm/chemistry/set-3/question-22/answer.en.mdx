# Count electrons, not grams

Zinc is more electropositive than iron, so it gives up electrons in place of the iron. Zinc lost: <InlineMath math="650 \div 65 = 10" /> mol, and each atom gives **two electrons**: <InlineMath math="20" /> mol of electrons.

Each iron atom that corrodes would also give two electrons, so <InlineMath math="20" /> mol of electrons stands in for <InlineMath math="10" /> mol of iron: <InlineMath math="10 \times 56 = 560" /> g.

Magnesium: <InlineMath math="650 \div 24 = 27.1" /> mol, giving <InlineMath math="54.2" /> mol of electrons. That is <InlineMath math="54.2 \div 20 = 2.7" /> times as many, because magnesium atoms are **lighter**, so the same mass holds more of them.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="10" /> mol counts zinc atoms, not the **two electrons** each one gives.
- <InlineMath math="1120\text{ g}" /> takes one electron per iron atom, but iron(II) ions need two.
- Lighter atoms mean **more atoms** in the same mass, and each magnesium atom also gives two electrons, so magnesium supplies more, not fewer.

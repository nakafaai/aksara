# Order from the tests, mass from the equation

A metal coats only when it **gives electrons to the ions of a metal below it**. Q is displaced by both P and R, so Q is lowest. R leaves P nitrate unchanged, so P is above R. The order is P, R, Q.

Both ions carry <InlineMath math="2+" />, so <InlineMath math="\text{P} + \text{Q}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{P}^{2+} + \text{Q}" /> is one to one. <InlineMath math="2.4 \div 24 = 0.10" /> mol of P frees <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of Q, which is <InlineMath math="0.10 \times 64 = 6.4" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- R above P would mean R displaces P, but R in P nitrate shows **no change**.
- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ g}" /> assumes the mass of metal stays the same. The moles stay the same, and Q atoms are heavier.
- Q below R is right, but R does not displace P, so P must be above R. <InlineMath math="3.2\text{ g}" /> also halves the moles, as if two P atoms freed one Q.

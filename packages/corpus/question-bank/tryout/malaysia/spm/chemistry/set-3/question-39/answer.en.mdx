# The head of the ion decides what happens in hard water

Both soap and detergent anions have a **hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail** that dissolves in grease and a **hydrophilic ionic head** that dissolves in water. The difference lies in the head.

The carboxylate head of soap combines with calcium and magnesium ions to form an insoluble scum, so the soap is used up and little foam forms. The sulfonate head of detergent forms salts that **stay dissolved**, so detergent still foams and cleans in hard water.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Detergent gives the same foam in both waters, so its anions do not form scum.
- The tail is hydrophobic, so it dissolves in grease, not in water. Soap foams far less in hard water.
- Both have an ionic head, but only the soap's head forms an insoluble scum with calcium and magnesium ions.

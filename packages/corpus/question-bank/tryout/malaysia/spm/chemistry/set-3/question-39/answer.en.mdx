# Three soap ions from every fat molecule

The fat is <InlineMath math="8.90 \div 890 = 0.0100" /> mol. The equation gives **three sodium stearate units for every fat molecule**, so <InlineMath math="0.0300" /> mol of soap forms: <InlineMath math="0.0300 \times 306 = 9.18" /> g.

In hard water, the carboxylate head of each stearate ion combines with calcium and magnesium ions to form an **insoluble scum**. The soap is used up making scum, so little foam forms, while a detergent's sulfonate head stays dissolved and keeps foaming.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.06\text{ g}" /> counts only one soap unit per fat molecule, and soap lathers poorly in hard water.
- <InlineMath math="9.18\text{ g}" /> is right, but soap lathers **worse** than a detergent in hard water, as the chart shows.
- <InlineMath math="27.5\text{ g}" /> multiplies by three twice: once for the moles and again for the mass.

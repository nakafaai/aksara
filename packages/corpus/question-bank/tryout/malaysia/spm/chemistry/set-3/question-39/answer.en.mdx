# Work back from the product, and divide by the yield

Aspirin needed: <InlineMath math="1000 \times 300\text{ mg} = 300\,000\text{ mg} = 300" /> g, which is <InlineMath math="300 \div 180 = 1.667" /> mol.

At <InlineMath math="75\%" /> yield, each mole of salicylic acid gives only <InlineMath math="0.75" /> mol of aspirin, so the lab must **start with more**: <InlineMath math="1.667 \div 0.75 = 2.222" /> mol.

Salicylic acid: <InlineMath math="2.222 \times 138 = 307" /> g.

**Anhydride**: <InlineMath math="20\%" /> more moles than the salicylic acid, <InlineMath math="2.222 \times 1.20 = 2.667" /> mol, which is <InlineMath math="2.667 \times 102 = 272" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="230\text{ g}" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="0.75" />, which would make **less** aspirin than the tablets need.
- <InlineMath math="227\text{ g}" /> of anhydride matches the salicylic acid mole for mole and leaves out the <InlineMath math="20\%" /> excess.
- <InlineMath math="300\text{ g}" /> is the mass of aspirin, not of salicylic acid.

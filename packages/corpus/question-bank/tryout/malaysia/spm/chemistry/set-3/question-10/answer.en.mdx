Potassium is more reactive, because its outer electron sits farther from the nucleus and is lost more easily. But each potassium atom is heavier, so <InlineMath math="0.23" /> g holds only <InlineMath math="0.23 \div 39 = 0.0059" /> mol and gives about <InlineMath math="71\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen: **faster, yet less gas**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> forgets that **two sodium atoms** make one hydrogen molecule.
- Sodium hydroxide is a strong alkali, so the solution is strongly alkaline, not acidic.
- Equal masses hold fewer potassium atoms than sodium atoms, so potassium gives less hydrogen, not more.

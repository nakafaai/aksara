# Larger atoms, stronger forces between them

Noble gases exist as single atoms. Boiling one overcomes the **weak attractive forces between atoms**, which grow as atoms get larger and hold more electrons.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Every noble gas except helium has <InlineMath math="8" /> valence electrons, so radon does not have fewer.
- The trend runs the other way, and **reactivity does not decide a boiling point**.
- A stable octet explains why noble gases are unreactive, not why their boiling points differ.

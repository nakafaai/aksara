# Two trends run in opposite directions down Group 17

Down the group the molecules get larger, so the forces between them get stronger and the **melting point rises**: from <InlineMath math="-220" /> to <InlineMath math="114^\circ\text{C}" />. Astatine continues the trend, so it is a solid at room temperature, darker still than the purple-black iodine.

At the same time, each atom adds a shell. The outer shell sits farther from the nucleus, so the atom **attracts an extra electron less strongly**, and reactivity falls down the group. Astatine reacts with hot iron wool more slowly than iodine does.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The melting point rises down the group, so astatine is a solid, and reactivity falls, so it is less reactive than iodine.
- Only a **more reactive** halogen displaces a less reactive one. Astatine sits below iodine, so it cannot displace it.
- Larger molecules mean stronger forces between them and a higher melting point, not a lower one.

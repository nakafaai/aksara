# Use the titration for the ratio, then scale up to the batch

Acid in the titration: <InlineMath math="0.0200 \times 1.25 = 0.0250" /> mol, which neutralised <InlineMath math="0.0250" /> mol of sodium hydroxide in <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" />. So the alkali is <InlineMath math="0.0250 \div 0.0250 = 1.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

The batch is **four times the titration**: <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of alkali holds <InlineMath math="0.100" /> mol, and <InlineMath math="80.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of acid holds exactly <InlineMath math="0.100" /> mol too, so neither is left over.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.56\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> divides the volumes the wrong way round, as if the alkali were **more concentrated** than the acid.
- <InlineMath math="100\%" /> takes the crystals as the full <InlineMath math="5.85" /> g, but only <InlineMath math="5.20" /> g was collected.
- <InlineMath math="1.25\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> copies the acid's concentration, though equal moles came from unequal volumes.

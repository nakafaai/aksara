Mass: <InlineMath math="0.200 \times \tfrac{250}{1000} = 0.0500" /> mol of <InlineMath math="\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3" />, with <InlineMath math="M_r = 2(23) + 12 + 3(16) = 106" />, is <InlineMath math="0.0500 \times 106 = 5.30" /> g.

Dilution: the same moles in **four times the volume**, so <InlineMath math="0.200 \div 4 = 0.0500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. The flask for titration holds <InlineMath math="0.0500 \times \tfrac{25.0}{1000} = 0.00125" /> mol of carbonate, which needs **two moles of acid per mole**: <InlineMath math="0.00250" /> mol. At <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, that is <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ dm}^3" />, or <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12.5\text{ cm}^3" /> treats the reaction as one to one and misses the second **mole of acid**.
- <InlineMath math="21.2\text{ g}" /> is the mass for a whole cubic decimetre of solution, not for <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="2.65\text{ g}" /> halves the mass, and <InlineMath math="12.5\text{ cm}^3" /> also misses the two to one ratio.

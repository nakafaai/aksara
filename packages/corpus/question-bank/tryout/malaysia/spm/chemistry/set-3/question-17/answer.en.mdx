# Two steps: weigh for the flask, then dilute the portion

The relative formula mass of <InlineMath math="\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3" /> is <InlineMath math="2(23) + 12 + 3(16) = 106" />. The flask needs <InlineMath math="0.200 \times \tfrac{250}{1000} = 0.0500" /> mol, which is <InlineMath math="0.0500 \times 106 = 5.30" /> g.

The pipette carries <InlineMath math="0.200 \times \tfrac{25.0}{1000} = 0.00500" /> mol into the second flask. Spread through <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />, that is <InlineMath math="0.00500 \div 0.100 = 0.050" /> mol dm⁻³. In one line, <InlineMath math="M_1V_1 = M_2V_2" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.200 \times 25.0 = M_2 \times 100" />, the **same moles in four times the volume**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.80\text{ mol}" /> dm⁻³ multiplies by the dilution factor instead of dividing: a diluted solution cannot be **more concentrated** than its stock.
- <InlineMath math="21.2\text{ g}" /> is the mass for a whole cubic decimetre, not for <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="2.65\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.025\text{ mol}" /> dm⁻³ halve both results, as if the stock solution were <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> dm⁻³.

# Make the ethene, then let the bromine run out

Hot porcelain dehydrates ethanol: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}" />. Ethanol: <InlineMath math="2.3 \div 46 = 0.050" /> mol, and at <InlineMath math="75\%" />, <InlineMath math="0.0375" /> mol of ethene forms, <InlineMath math="0.0375 \times 24 = 0.90\text{ dm}^3" />.

The bromine water holds only <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 0.10 = 0.0050" /> mol of bromine, and **one bromine molecule adds to one ethene molecule**.

So all the bromine reacts and the solution is decolourised, but <InlineMath math="0.0375 - 0.0050 = 0.0325" /> mol of ethene is left over: <InlineMath math="0.0325 \times 24 = 0.78\text{ dm}^3" /> passes through.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ dm}^3" /> ignores the <InlineMath math="75\%" /> yield.
- The bromine water **runs out** after <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol, so it cannot absorb all <InlineMath math="0.0375" /> mol of ethene.
- <InlineMath math="0.66\text{ dm}^3" /> takes two ethene molecules per bromine molecule, but each bromine molecule adds across one double bond.

# One to one, twice, with the yield in between

Hot porcelain removes water from ethanol: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}" />. Ethanol is <InlineMath math="2.3 \div 46 = 0.050" /> mol, so <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol of ethene could form, and at **<InlineMath math="75\%" /> yield** <InlineMath math="0.0375" /> mol is collected: <InlineMath math="0.0375 \times 24 = 0.90" /> dm³.

Ethene adds **one bromine molecule across its double bond**: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{Br}_2" />. So <InlineMath math="0.0375" /> mol of bromine reacts, and with <InlineMath math="M_r(\text{Br}_2) = 160" /> that is <InlineMath math="0.0375 \times 160 = 6.0" /> g.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ g}" /> ignore the <InlineMath math="75\%" /> yield.
- Dehydration gives ethene, not ethane, and ethane does not react with bromine water.
- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ g}" /> uses <InlineMath math="80" />, the mass of one bromine atom, though a **bromine molecule** has two atoms.

# Products give carbon and hydrogen, the difference gives oxygen

All the carbon ends up in carbon dioxide: <InlineMath math="1.32 \div 44 = 0.030" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="0.36" /> g. All the hydrogen ends up in water: <InlineMath math="0.54 \div 18 = 0.030" /> mol of water, so <InlineMath math="0.060" /> mol of hydrogen atoms, or <InlineMath math="0.06" /> g. The oxygen came from the oxygen gas too, so find Z's own oxygen **by difference**: <InlineMath math="0.90 - 0.36 - 0.06 = 0.48" /> g, which is <InlineMath math="0.030" /> mol.

The ratio <InlineMath math="0.030 : 0.060 : 0.030 = 1 : 2 : 1" /> gives the empirical formula <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2\text{O}" />, mass <InlineMath math="30" />. Since <InlineMath math="180 \div 30 = 6" />, the molecular formula is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2\text{O}" /> is only the **empirical formula**, with mass <InlineMath math="30" />, not <InlineMath math="180" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}_3" /> has mass <InlineMath math="90" />, half of <InlineMath math="180" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{O}_2" /> has mass <InlineMath math="60" />.

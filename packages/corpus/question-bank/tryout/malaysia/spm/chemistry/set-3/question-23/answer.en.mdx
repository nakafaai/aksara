# Three electrons out of each aluminium, four into each oxygen

Aluminium: <InlineMath math="0.90 \div 27 = 0.0333" /> mol. Each atom becomes <InlineMath math="\text{Al}^{3+}" /> and gives **three electrons**: <InlineMath math="0.0333 \times 3 = 0.10" /> mol of electrons through the device.

Every electron that leaves the aluminium arrives at the oxygen, and **each oxygen molecule takes four**: <InlineMath math="0.10 \div 4 = 0.025" /> mol of oxygen, or <InlineMath math="0.025 \times 24 = 0.60\text{ dm}^3" />.

**Running time**: <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of electrons at <InlineMath math="0.30" /> mol per hour lasts <InlineMath math="0.10 \div 0.30 = \tfrac{1}{3}" /> hour, which is <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ dm}^3" /> and about <InlineMath math="7" /> minutes count aluminium atoms, not the **three electrons** each gives.
- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> gives each oxygen molecule two electrons instead of four.
- <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" /> gives each oxygen molecule one electron.

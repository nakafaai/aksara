# Voltage is the distance between two metals in the series

Each voltage shows how far a metal sits **above copper** in the electrochemical series: magnesium <InlineMath math="2.7" /> V, zinc <InlineMath math="1.1" /> V. Both are above copper, so the gap between them is <InlineMath math="2.7 - 1.1 = 1.6" /> V.

Magnesium is the more electropositive metal, so it **gives up electrons**: <InlineMath math="\text{Mg} \rightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+} + 2e^-" />. The electrons travel through the wire to zinc, where zinc ions take them: <InlineMath math="\text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Zn}" />. Magnesium atoms leave as ions, so the magnesium electrode loses mass, while zinc metal builds up on the zinc electrode.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.8" /> V adds the two voltages, as if the metals sat on **opposite sides** of copper, and the electrons flow the other way.
- Zinc is the positive terminal here: it receives electrons and gains mass, so it does not lose any.
- <InlineMath math="1.9" /> V uses iron's <InlineMath math="0.8" /> V in place of zinc's <InlineMath math="1.1" /> V.

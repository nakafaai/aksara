# Same formula, different structure, different forces between molecules

Molar mass: <InlineMath math="0.23" /> g fills <InlineMath math="0.120 \div 24 = 0.0050" /> mol, so <InlineMath math="M_r = 0.23 \div 0.0050 = 46" />. Ratio of atoms: <InlineMath math="52.2 \div 12 = 4.35" />, <InlineMath math="13.0 \div 1 = 13.0" />, <InlineMath math="34.8 \div 16 = 2.18" />, which is <InlineMath math="2 : 6 : 1" />. The empirical formula <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}" /> already has mass <InlineMath math="46" />, so it is the molecular formula.

Two structures fit: **ethanol**, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}" />, and **dimethyl ether**, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_3\text{OCH}_3" />.

Boiling separates molecules, it does not break covalent bonds. Ethanol has an <InlineMath math="\text{O–H}" /> group, so its molecules hold each other by hydrogen bonds. Dimethyl ether has no hydrogen on oxygen, so only weak forces act between its molecules. That is why ethanol boils at <InlineMath math="78^\circ\text{C}" />.

**Burning**: both compounds are <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}" />, so they share one equation, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O} + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}" />. <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol needs <InlineMath math="0.015" /> mol of oxygen, <InlineMath math="0.015 \times 24\,000 = 360\text{ cm}^3" />, whichever isomer it is.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The two molecules have **the same mass**, so mass cannot explain the difference in boiling point.
- Isomers have the same formula, so the same mass needs **the same oxygen** to burn.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> takes one oxygen molecule per molecule of compound, but the equation needs three.

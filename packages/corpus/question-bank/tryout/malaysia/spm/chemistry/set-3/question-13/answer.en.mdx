# Hydrogen bonds outweigh molecular mass

Boiling separates molecules and **leaves the covalent bonds inside them intact**, so the pattern comes from the forces between molecules.

A hydrogen bond forms only when hydrogen is bonded to a small, very electronegative atom: nitrogen, oxygen or fluorine. Water, hydrogen fluoride and ammonia qualify, so they boil far higher than their small masses suggest. Sulfur is larger and less electronegative, so <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{S}" /> has no hydrogen bonds. With almost twice the mass of water, it still boils <InlineMath math="160^\circ\text{C}" /> lower.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2\text{S}" /> is heavier, but it boils at <InlineMath math="-60^\circ\text{C}" />, far below water. Mass alone does not decide the boiling point.
- Boiling does **not break covalent bonds**. Methane boils lowest because only weak forces hold its molecules together.
- Nitrogen is more electronegative than carbon, and ammonia boils above methane, not below it.

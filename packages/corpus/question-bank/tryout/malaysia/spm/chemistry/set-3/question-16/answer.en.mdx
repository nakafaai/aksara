Nitric acid: <InlineMath math="0.0220 \times 0.200 = 0.00440" /> mol, which reacts one to one with ammonia. So <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of the flask held <InlineMath math="0.00440" /> mol, and the flask is <InlineMath math="0.00440 \div 0.0250 = 0.176\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

The flask is **not the cleaner**. <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> became <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" />, a dilution of <InlineMath math="250 \div 10.0 = 25" /> times, so the cleaner is <InlineMath math="0.176 \times 25 = 4.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. With <InlineMath math="M_r(\text{NH}_3) = 17" />, that is <InlineMath math="4.40 \times 17 = 74.8\text{ g dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.176\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> is the diluted solution in the flask, **not the cleaner** in the bottle.
- <InlineMath math="0.0440\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> takes the moles in the whole flask, <InlineMath math="0.0440" /> mol, as if they were a concentration.
- <InlineMath math="61.6\text{ g dm}^{-3}" /> multiplies by the mass of nitrogen alone, <InlineMath math="14" />, not by the molar mass of ammonia.

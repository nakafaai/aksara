# Same moles of acid, different hydrogen ion concentration

Both flasks hold <InlineMath math="0.10 \times \tfrac{50}{1000} = 0.0050" /> mol of a **monoprotic acid**. With excess magnesium, each acid reacts completely and gives <InlineMath math="0.0025" /> mol of hydrogen, which is <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> at room conditions.

Hydrochloric acid ionises completely, so its hydrogen ion concentration starts at <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol dm⁻³. Ethanoic acid **ionises only partly**, so far fewer hydrogen ions meet the magnesium at any moment. As they are used up, more ethanoic acid molecules ionise, until all of the acid has reacted.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A strong acid reacts faster, but the final volume depends only on the moles of acid, which are equal.
- Gradual ionisation keeps the **hydrogen ion concentration low**, which makes ethanoic acid slower, not faster.
- Ethanoic acid does have the higher pH, but both flasks contain <InlineMath math="0.0050" /> mol of acid. Its pH is higher because it ionises only partly.

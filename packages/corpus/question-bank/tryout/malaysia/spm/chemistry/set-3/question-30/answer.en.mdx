# Name the product, then carry the moles through two reactions

An excess of acidified potassium manganate(VII) under reflux **oxidises ethanol all the way** to ethanoic acid, so X is ethanoic acid.

Ethanol: <InlineMath math="4.6 \div 46 = 0.10" /> mol, which gives <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of acid in theory and <InlineMath math="0.75 \times 0.10 = 0.075" /> mol in practice: <InlineMath math="0.075 \times 60 = 4.5" /> g. In <InlineMath math="2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{CaCO}_3 \rightarrow (\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Ca} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2" />, **two moles of acid give one mole of gas**: <InlineMath math="0.0375" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="0.0375 \times 24 = 0.90\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Ethene comes from dehydrating ethanol, not from oxidising it, and it does not react with calcium carbonate.
- <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ dm}^3" /> treats the acid and the gas as one to one, but ethanoic acid gives **one hydrogen ion per molecule**, and two are needed per carbon dioxide.
- Ethyl ethanoate needs ethanoic acid to react with more ethanol and concentrated sulfuric acid, which is a separate step.

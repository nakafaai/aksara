# An oxidising agent turns ethanol into a carboxylic acid

Acidified potassium manganate(VII) is an oxidising agent. Under reflux it **oxidises ethanol all the way** to ethanoic acid, and the manganate(VII) ions are reduced to colourless manganese(II) ions.

Ethanoic acid is a weak acid, so it reacts with calcium carbonate: <InlineMath math="2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{CaCO}_3 \rightarrow (\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Ca} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2" />. The carbon dioxide turns limewater chalky.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Ethene comes from **dehydrating** ethanol over hot porous pot, not from oxidising it.
- Ethyl ethanoate forms when ethanoic acid reacts with more ethanol and a little concentrated sulfuric acid, not in this oxidation.
- Ethanol, not X, gave the electrons. X is the product of the oxidation, so it cannot be the reducing agent that decolourised the solution.

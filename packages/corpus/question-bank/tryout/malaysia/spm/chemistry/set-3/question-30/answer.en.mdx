# Make the acid, then check which reactant runs out

Acidified manganate(VII) oxidises ethanol to **ethanoic acid**, and the purple colour fades as it is reduced. Ethanol: <InlineMath math="4.6 \div 46 = 0.10" /> mol. At <InlineMath math="75\%" />, <InlineMath math="0.075" /> mol of acid forms, <InlineMath math="0.075 \times 60 = 4.5" /> g.

<InlineMath math="2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{CaCO}_3 \rightarrow (\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Ca} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2" />. The carbonate is <InlineMath math="1.0 \div 100 = 0.010" /> mol, and it needs only <InlineMath math="0.020" /> mol of acid.

The acid is in excess, so the **carbonate runs out first**: <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol of carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="0.010 \times 24 = 0.24\text{ dm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Ethene forms when ethanol is dehydrated, not oxidised, and it does not react with calcium carbonate.
- <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ dm}^3" /> works from all the acid, but the carbonate **runs out** after <InlineMath math="0.010" /> mol.
- Ethyl ethanoate needs an acid and an alcohol heated together, but here the oxidising agent turns the ethanol into acid.

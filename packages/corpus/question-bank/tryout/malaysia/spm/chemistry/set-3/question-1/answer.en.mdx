# Weigh by difference, scale down to the aliquot, then double

The solid in the flask is what **left the bottle**: <InlineMath math="18.462 - 15.812 = 2.650" /> g, which is <InlineMath math="2.650 \div 106 = 0.02500" /> mol. In <InlineMath math="250.0\text{ cm}^3" /> that is <InlineMath math="0.1000\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, so each <InlineMath math="25.00\text{ cm}^3" /> portion holds <InlineMath math="0.002500" /> mol, a tenth of the total.

The equation needs **two moles of acid for each mole of carbonate**, so each titration uses <InlineMath math="0.005000" /> mol of hydrochloric acid.

The rough titre and run <InlineMath math="2" /> are left out. Runs <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" /> agree within <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ cm}^3" />, and their mean is <InlineMath math="(24.65 + 24.60 + 24.70) \div 3 = 24.65\text{ cm}^3" />. So the acid is <InlineMath math="0.005000 \div 0.02465 = 0.203\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.101\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> uses one mole of acid per mole of carbonate, but the carbonate ion takes **two** hydrogen ions.
- <InlineMath math="2.03\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> puts all <InlineMath math="0.02500" /> mol into one titration, though only a tenth of the solution goes into the flask.
- <InlineMath math="1.21\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> takes the second reading, <InlineMath math="15.812" /> g, as the mass of solid instead of the difference.

# The lone pair left on X makes the extra bond

X is <InlineMath math="2.5" /> and needs three more electrons. With one X per molecule it bonds three Y atoms: <InlineMath math="\text{XY}_3" />, ammonia. X uses three of its five valence electrons, so **one lone pair** is left on it. The gas is <InlineMath math="1.20 \div 24 = 0.050" /> mol.

Hydrogen chloride gives a hydrogen ion with no electrons of its own. The lone pair on nitrogen **bonds to it as a dative bond**: <InlineMath math="\text{NH}_3 + \text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}" />. One dative bond per ammonia molecule: <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="3.01 \times 10^{22}" />.

Solid: <InlineMath math="M_r(\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}) = 14 + 4 + 35.5 = 53.5" />, so <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 53.5 = 2.68" /> g. Once formed, the dative bond is an <InlineMath math="\text{N–H}" /> bond like the other three, so each ammonium ion has **four** <InlineMath math="\text{X–Y}" /> bonds: <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 4 = 0.20" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="1.20 \times 10^{23}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="9.03 \times 10^{22}" /> counts only the three bonds ammonia started with and leaves out the new dative <InlineMath math="\text{N–H}" /> bond.
- <InlineMath math="1.34\text{ g}" /> makes one ammonium chloride from two ammonia molecules, but the reaction is one to one.
- The solid is ionic between ammonium and chloride ions, but inside the ammonium ion the new bond is a **dative covalent** bond.

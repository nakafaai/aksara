# Same shell, more protons, stronger pull

Across Period <InlineMath math="3" />, every element has its valence electrons in the **third shell**, but each has one more proton. The growing nuclear charge pulls the same shell inward.

The radius falls from <InlineMath math="186" /> to <InlineMath math="99" /> pm. Because chlorine's nucleus holds its valence shell most strongly and closest, chlorine attracts a shared pair of electrons most strongly: it is the most electronegative of the seven.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The number of shells stays at **three** across Period <InlineMath math="3" />, so it cannot explain the trend.
- Sodium has the largest radius and the weakest hold on its valence electron, so it is the least electronegative.
- Electron repulsion does not drive the trend, and argon, a noble gas, is not on the chart and does not take part in bonding.

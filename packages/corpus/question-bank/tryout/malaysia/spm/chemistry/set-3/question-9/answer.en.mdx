The oxide gained <InlineMath math="2.04 - 1.08 = 0.96" /> g of oxygen, which is <InlineMath math="0.96 \div 16 = 0.060" /> mol of oxygen atoms. The formula is **not given**, so try each Period <InlineMath math="3" /> oxide and see which needs exactly <InlineMath math="1.08" /> g of R.

For <InlineMath math="\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3" />, <InlineMath math="0.060" /> mol of oxygen goes with <InlineMath math="0.040" /> mol of aluminium, and <InlineMath math="0.040 \times 27 = 1.08" /> g. It fits, and no other oxide does. So R is aluminium, and its oxide is **amphoteric**, so it dissolves in the alkali.

The oxide is <InlineMath math="0.040 \div 2 = 0.020" /> mol, which is <InlineMath math="2.04 \div 102" /> as well. **One oxide takes two sodium hydroxide**: <InlineMath math="0.040" /> mol, which is <InlineMath math="0.040 \div 2.0 = 0.020\text{ dm}^3 = 20\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Magnesium would need <InlineMath math="1.44" /> g for this much oxygen, **not** <InlineMath math="1.08" /> g, and magnesium oxide does not dissolve in the alkali.
- <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}^3" /> takes one sodium hydroxide per formula unit of oxide, but the equation needs two.
- Silicon dioxide would need only <InlineMath math="0.84" /> g of silicon, so its oxide does not fit the masses.

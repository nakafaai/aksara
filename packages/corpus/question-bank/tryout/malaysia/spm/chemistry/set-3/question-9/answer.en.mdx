# Oxides turn from basic to acidic across the period

Across Period <InlineMath math="3" /> the elements change from metals to non-metals, and their oxides change from **basic, through amphoteric, to acidic**. Each clue pins down one element.

- An oxide that reacts with both an acid and an alkali is amphoteric: aluminium oxide, so Q is aluminium.
- The smallest radius on the chart, <InlineMath math="99" /> pm, belongs to chlorine, so R is chlorine. Its oxides are acidic and give an acidic solution in water.
- An oxide that dissolves to give pH <InlineMath math="13" /> is a soluble basic oxide: sodium oxide forms sodium hydroxide, so T is sodium.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Magnesium oxide is basic. It reacts with acids but **not with sodium hydroxide**.
- Sulfur's radius, <InlineMath math="104" /> pm, is larger than chlorine's, so sulfur is not the smallest.
- Magnesium oxide is only slightly soluble and gives a weakly alkaline solution, about pH <InlineMath math="10" />, not pH <InlineMath math="13" />.

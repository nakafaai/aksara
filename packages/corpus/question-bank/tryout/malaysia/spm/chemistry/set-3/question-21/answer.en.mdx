# Oxidation numbers for the change, moles for the volume

In <InlineMath math="\text{KMnO}_4" />, potassium is <InlineMath math="+1" /> and each oxygen is <InlineMath math="-2" />, so manganese is <InlineMath math="+7" />. In <InlineMath math="\text{MnCl}_2" /> it is <InlineMath math="+2" />: manganese **gains electrons and is reduced**.

<InlineMath math="3.16 \div 158 = 0.020" /> mol of potassium manganate(VII) reacts. The equation gives **five chlorine molecules for every two** manganate(VII) units, so <InlineMath math="0.020 \times \tfrac{5}{2} = 0.050" /> mol of chlorine forms, which is <InlineMath math="0.050 \times 24 = 1.2" /> dm³.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A fall from <InlineMath math="+7" /> to <InlineMath math="+2" /> is a reduction, not an oxidation.
- <InlineMath math="0.48\text{ dm}^3" /> treats the ratio as one to one and ignores the <InlineMath math="2" /> : <InlineMath math="5" /> in the equation.
- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ dm}^3" /> takes five chlorine molecules for **each** manganate(VII) unit instead of for every two.

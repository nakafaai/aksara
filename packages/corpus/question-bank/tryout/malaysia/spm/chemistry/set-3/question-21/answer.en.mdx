# Carry the chlorine from the first equation into the second

In <InlineMath math="\text{KMnO}_4" /> manganese is <InlineMath math="+7" />, and in <InlineMath math="\text{MnCl}_2" /> it is <InlineMath math="+2" />. Its oxidation number falls, so manganese is **reduced**, and the manganate(VII) is the oxidising agent that turns chloride ions into chlorine.

Manganate(VII): <InlineMath math="3.16 \div 158 = 0.020" /> mol. **Two give five chlorine molecules**: <InlineMath math="0.020 \times \tfrac{5}{2} = 0.050" /> mol of chlorine.

On the iron, **three chlorine molecules make two iron(III) chloride**: <InlineMath math="0.050 \times \tfrac{2}{3} = 0.0333" /> mol, or <InlineMath math="0.0333 \times 162.5 = 5.42" /> g.

**In solution**: each formula unit releases **three chloride ions**, so <InlineMath math="0.0333 \times 3 = 0.10" /> mol of chloride ions in <InlineMath math="0.250\text{ dm}^3" />: <InlineMath math="0.10 \div 0.250 = 0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A fall in oxidation number is **reduction**, not oxidation.
- <InlineMath math="8.13\text{ g}" /> makes one iron(III) chloride from each chlorine molecule, but each formula unit needs one and a half.
- <InlineMath math="0.13\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> is the concentration of iron(III) chloride, not of the **three chloride ions** each unit gives.

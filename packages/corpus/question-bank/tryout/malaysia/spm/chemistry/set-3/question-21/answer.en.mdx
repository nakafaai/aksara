# Track each element's oxidation number

In <InlineMath math="\text{KMnO}_4" />, potassium is <InlineMath math="+1" /> and each oxygen is <InlineMath math="-2" />, so manganese is <InlineMath math="+7" />. In <InlineMath math="\text{MnCl}_2" /> it is <InlineMath math="+2" />. A fall in oxidation number means manganese **gains electrons and is reduced**.

Some chlorine goes from <InlineMath math="-1" /> in <InlineMath math="\text{HCl}" /> to <InlineMath math="0" /> in <InlineMath math="\text{Cl}_2" />, so it loses electrons and is oxidised. The substance that gives the electrons, hydrochloric acid, is the **reducing agent**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A fall from <InlineMath math="+7" /> to <InlineMath math="+2" /> is a reduction, and potassium manganate(VII) takes electrons, so it is the oxidising agent.
- Chlorine rises from <InlineMath math="-1" /> to <InlineMath math="0" />, which is **oxidation**, not reduction.
- Potassium is <InlineMath math="+1" /> on both sides. Changing partners does not change its oxidation number.

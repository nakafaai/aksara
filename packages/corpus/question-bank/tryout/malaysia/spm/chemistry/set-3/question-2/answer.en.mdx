# Both gases travel for the same time, so distance follows speed

The gases start together and meet at the same instant, so **each covers a distance in proportion to its speed**. The rule in the question gives the ratio of speeds.

  title={<>Where the Two Gases Meet</>}

  chart={"flowchart LR\n  A[Ammonia end, Mr 17] -->|faster, 59 cm| R[White ring: NH4Cl]\n  H[Hydrogen chloride end, Mr 36.5] -->|slower, 41 cm| R"}
/>

- Speed ratio: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{v_{\text{NH}_3}}{v_{\text{HCl}}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{36.5}{17}} = \sqrt{2.147} = 1.465" />.
- Split the <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}" /> tube in the ratio <InlineMath math="1.465 : 1" />: ammonia covers <InlineMath math="100 \times \dfrac{1.465}{2.465} = 59\text{ cm}" />.

So the ring forms about <InlineMath math="59\text{ cm}" /> from the ammonia end, nearer the hydrogen chloride.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="41\text{ cm}" /> is the distance from the **hydrogen chloride** end: the two distances were swapped.
- <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}" /> assumes both gases move at the same speed.
- <InlineMath math="68\text{ cm}" /> uses the ratio of the masses, <InlineMath math="\dfrac{36.5}{53.5}" />, instead of their square roots.

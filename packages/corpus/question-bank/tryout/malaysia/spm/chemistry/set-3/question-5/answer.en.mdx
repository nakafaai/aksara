# Moles of molecules, then atoms per molecule

Find the moles of molecules, then multiply by the **atoms in one molecule**:

- Nitrogen, <InlineMath math="\text{N}_2" />: <InlineMath math="2.8 \div 28 = 0.10" /> mol, <InlineMath math="\times 2 = 0.20" /> mol of atoms.
- Ammonia, <InlineMath math="\text{NH}_3" />: <InlineMath math="3.4 \div 17 = 0.20" /> mol, <InlineMath math="\times 4 = 0.80" /> mol of atoms.
- Ethanol, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}" />: <InlineMath math="4.6 \div 46 = 0.10" /> mol, <InlineMath math="\times 9 = 0.90" /> mol of atoms.
- Methane, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_4" />: <InlineMath math="1.6 \div 16 = 0.10" /> mol, <InlineMath math="\times 5 = 0.50" /> mol of atoms.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Nitrogen has the fewest atoms: two per molecule and only <InlineMath math="0.10" /> mol of molecules.
- Ammonia has <InlineMath math="0.80" /> mol of atoms, **close but below** ethanol's <InlineMath math="0.90" />.
- Methane gives <InlineMath math="0.50" /> mol of atoms. Its count of <InlineMath math="3.01 \times 10^{23}" /> is right, but it is not the largest.

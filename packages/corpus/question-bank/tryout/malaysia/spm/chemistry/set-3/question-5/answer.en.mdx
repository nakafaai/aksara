# Moles of molecules, then atoms per molecule, then particles

Three steps, each with its own trap: **mass to moles of molecules, molecules to hydrogen atoms, moles to particles**.

- Moles of water, <InlineMath math="M_r = 18" />: <InlineMath math="\dfrac{4.5}{18} = 0.25\text{ mol}" />.
- Each molecule has <InlineMath math="2" /> hydrogen atoms: <InlineMath math="0.25 \times 2 = 0.50\text{ mol}" /> of H atoms.
- Number of H atoms: <InlineMath math="0.50 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 3.01 \times 10^{23}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.51 \times 10^{23}" /> is the number of **water molecules**, before counting two hydrogen atoms in each.
- <InlineMath math="6.02 \times 10^{23}" /> takes the sample as one mole.
- <InlineMath math="4.52 \times 10^{23}" /> counts all three atoms of each molecule, oxygen included.

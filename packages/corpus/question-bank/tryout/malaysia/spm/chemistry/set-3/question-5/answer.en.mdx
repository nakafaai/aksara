# Three molecules become two, so the gas shrinks

Oxygen in: <InlineMath math="6.0 \div 24 = 0.25" /> mol. A fifth of it reacts: <InlineMath math="0.050" /> mol, and <InlineMath math="0.20" /> mol is left. **Three oxygen molecules make two ozone molecules**, so <InlineMath math="0.050 \times \tfrac{2}{3} = 0.0333" /> mol of ozone forms.

Gas out: <InlineMath math="0.20 + 0.0333 = 0.2333" /> mol, which is <InlineMath math="0.2333 \times 24 = 5.6\text{ dm}^3" />. Ozone molecules: <InlineMath math="0.0333 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 2.01 \times 10^{22}" />. The mass of gas is still <InlineMath math="8.0" /> g, and only the number of molecules falls.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ dm}^3" /> keeps the volume unchanged, but **fewer molecules** take up less space at the same conditions.
- <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ dm}^3" /> is the oxygen left, without the ozone that formed.
- <InlineMath math="3.01 \times 10^{22}" /> makes one ozone molecule from each oxygen molecule that reacts.

# The acid is used up and water forms, so the concentration falls on two counts

The equation passes two electrons for every two sulfuric acid molecules used, **one acid molecule per electron**. It also makes one water molecule per electron, <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ cm}^3" /> in all, which dilutes what is left. At the negative plate each lead atom gains a sulfate ion as it becomes insoluble lead(II) sulfate, so the plate gets heavier, not lighter.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> ignores the water formed. **The <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ mol}" /> is spread through <InlineMath math="509\text{ cm}^3" />**.
- <InlineMath math="4.50\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> uses one acid molecule per two electrons. **The equation uses two acid molecules per two electrons**.
- The lead does not dissolve. **Lead(II) sulfate is insoluble and stays on the plate**, so the plate gains the sulfate.

# The tests narrow each liquid to a pair, and the numbers pick one of each pair

P is oxidised by dichromate but is not acidic, so it is an alcohol. **The carbon dioxide from burning it shows two carbon atoms per molecule**, which fits ethanol and not propan-1-ol. Q is an acid, and its titration gives a molar mass of <InlineMath math="74" />, propanoic acid rather than ethanoic acid at <InlineMath math="60" />. R adds bromine across a double bond, so it is the alkene.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.460\text{ g}" /> of propan-1-ol would give <InlineMath math="1.01\text{ g}" /> of carbon dioxide, not <InlineMath math="0.880\text{ g}" />.
- <InlineMath math="0.600\text{ g}" /> of ethanoic acid would need <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of the alkali. **A molar mass of <InlineMath math="74" /> is propanoic acid**.
- Hexane has no double bond, so it does not decolourise bromine water in the dark.

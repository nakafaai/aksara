# Both ions reach the argon arrangement, so the nucleus decides their size

Potassium gives one electron and sulfur takes two, so the formula is <InlineMath math="\text{K}_2\text{S}" />, three ions per formula unit. Transferring electrons never creates or destroys any, so a formula unit still holds <InlineMath math="2(19)+16=54" /> electrons. **Both ions have the same <InlineMath math="18" /> electrons, but the sulfide ion has only <InlineMath math="16" /> protons pulling on them**, against <InlineMath math="19" /> in the potassium ion, so its electrons sit further out.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{22}" /> counts formula units, not ions. **Each unit holds three ions**.
- <InlineMath math="6.26\times10^{23}" /> takes away the transferred electrons, but they stay in the compound.
- The potassium ion has three more protons than the sulfide ion for the same electrons, so it pulls them in more tightly and is smaller.

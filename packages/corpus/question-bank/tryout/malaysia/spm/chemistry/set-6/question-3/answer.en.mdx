# Both salts have three ions per unit and nearly the same molar mass, and the four ions share one electron count

Potassium sulfide is <InlineMath math="\text{K}_2\text{S}" />, <InlineMath math="110" />, and calcium chloride <InlineMath math="\text{CaCl}_2" />, <InlineMath math="111" />. Equal moles give a mean of <InlineMath math="110.5" />, so the mixture is <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of formula units, each with three ions. **All four ions have the arrangement <InlineMath math="2.8.8" />**, so the one with most protons pulls those eighteen electrons in hardest and is smallest.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The order is reversed. **More protons for the same eighteen electrons make an ion smaller**, so the calcium ion is the smallest.
- <InlineMath math="2.41\times10^{22}" /> counts two ions per formula unit. **Each unit of either salt holds three**.
- <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{22}" /> counts formula units, not ions.

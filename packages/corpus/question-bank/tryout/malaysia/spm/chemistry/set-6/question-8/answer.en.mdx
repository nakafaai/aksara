# Each absorber removes one gas, so each drop in volume names that gas

Hot copper takes only the oxygen, <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ cm}^3" />. **Copper does not react with carbon dioxide, so that drop comes only at the alkali**, <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="4.0\%" /> of the breath. Each oxygen molecule turns two copper atoms into two units of copper(II) oxide.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.0213\text{ g}" /> is the mass of oxygen the copper gains, not the mass of the oxide it forms.
- <InlineMath math="0.0533\text{ g}" /> makes one copper(II) oxide per oxygen molecule. **Each molecule holds two oxygen atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="20.0\%" /> adds the oxygen to the carbon dioxide, but the copper removed the oxygen before the alkali.

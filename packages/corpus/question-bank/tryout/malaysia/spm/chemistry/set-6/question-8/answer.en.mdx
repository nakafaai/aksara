# Each absorber takes one gas, so each drop names its gas, and the last drop is the nitrogen

Copper takes the oxygen, <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ cm}^3" />, and the alkali the carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ cm}^3" />. **What reaches the magnesium is not all nitrogen**: <InlineMath math="0.9\text{ cm}^3" /> of noble gas stays behind, so the nitrogen is <InlineMath math="79.1\text{ cm}^3" />. Each oxygen molecule turns two copper atoms into copper(II) oxide.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="80.0\%" /> treats the gas left after the alkali as pure nitrogen. **The magnesium leaves <InlineMath math="0.9\text{ cm}^3" /> that is not nitrogen**.
- <InlineMath math="0.0533\text{ g}" /> makes one copper(II) oxide per oxygen molecule. **Each molecule holds two oxygen atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="84.0\%" /> counts everything after the copper as nitrogen, including the carbon dioxide and the noble gases.

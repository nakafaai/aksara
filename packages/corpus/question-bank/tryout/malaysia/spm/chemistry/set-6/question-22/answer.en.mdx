# The electrolysis splits water, so the acid stays and the water shrinks

Hydrogen ions are discharged at the cathode, two electrons per molecule, and hydroxide ions from water at the anode, four electrons per oxygen molecule. **Overall only water is used up**, <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ mol}" /> or <InlineMath math="3.6\text{ cm}^3" />, while the <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of sulfuric acid stays, now in <InlineMath math="196.4\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ dm}^3" /> of oxygen gives each oxygen molecule two electrons. **It takes four**.
- <InlineMath math="0.500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> leaves the concentration unchanged. **The acid stays, but water is removed**, so it rises a little.
- The gases are swapped. Hydrogen forms at the cathode, twice as much as the oxygen.

# The electrolysis splits water, so the acid's ions stay and the solvent shrinks

Hydrogen ions are discharged at the cathode and hydroxide ions from water at the anode. **The oxygen half-equation needs four electrons, the hydrogen one two**, so the gases form in a two to one ratio. Overall only water is used up, <InlineMath math="3.6\text{ g}" /> of it, while the moles of sulfuric acid stay the same in a little less water.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ dm}^3" /> of oxygen gives each oxygen molecule two electrons. **It takes four**.
- The hydrogen ions discharged at the cathode are replaced by those made at the anode, so the acid stays, but water is removed. **Less water means a higher concentration**.
- The gases are swapped. Hydrogen forms at the cathode, and twice as much of it as oxygen.

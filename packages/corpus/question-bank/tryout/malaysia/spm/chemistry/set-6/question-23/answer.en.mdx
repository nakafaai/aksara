# The cathode counts the electrons, and the zinc spends some of them without returning copper

Every two electrons deposit one copper atom at the cathode, so <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of metal atoms dissolved at the anode, copper and zinc together. **The mass of those atoms, <InlineMath math="6.41\text{ g}" />, then splits them into <InlineMath math="0.0900\text{ mol}" /> of copper and <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of zinc**. More copper ions leave the solution than enter it, so the blue colour fades slightly as zinc ions replace them.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="96.2\%" /> divides the cathode's gain by the anode's loss. **The cathode copper partly comes from the solution**, because the zinc's electrons deposit copper too.
- The solution loses <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of copper ions at the cathode but gains only <InlineMath math="0.0900\text{ mol}" /> from the anode, so the blue colour cannot stay the same.
- <InlineMath math="96.2\%" /> is the ratio of the two electrode masses, not the copper in the anode.

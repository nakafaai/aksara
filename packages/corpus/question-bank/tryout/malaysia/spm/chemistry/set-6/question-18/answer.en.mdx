# The mass lost is the carbon dioxide, and the acid runs out before the carbonate

Only carbon dioxide leaves the flask, so the mass lost in <InlineMath math="60\text{ s}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.62\text{ g}" /> of it, which converts to a volume through its moles. **The flask stops losing mass at <InlineMath math="0.88\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of gas, while the carbonate could give <InlineMath math="0.0250\text{ mol}" />**. So the acid ran out first, and the extra carbonate stays in the flask.

- <InlineMath math="0\text{ g}" /> assumes the carbonate runs out. **The final loss of <InlineMath math="0.88\text{ g}" /> is less than the <InlineMath math="1.10\text{ g}" /> all the carbonate would give**.

# Diluting first matches the concentrations, so only the zinc's surface area differs

Mixing <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> acid with the same volume of water gives <InlineMath math="50.0\text{ cm}^3" /> at <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, the same acid as in experiment I. **So the only change is powder for granules**, and the larger surface area makes the start faster. The zinc, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, needs only <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mol}" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mol}" /> of acid, so it is limiting in both and gives <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> halves the hydrogen for the halved volume of acid. **The acid holds the same <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mol}" /> and the zinc is limiting**.
- The concentrations do match, but powder has a larger surface area than granules, so the start is faster.
- <InlineMath math="480\text{ cm}^3" /> lets the acid set the hydrogen, but the zinc runs out first.

# Oxidation changes only the end carbon, so the branch stays and the name keeps it

The dichromate turns the <InlineMath math="\text{–CH}_2\text{OH}" /> group into <InlineMath math="\text{–COOH}" /> and leaves the rest of the carbon skeleton alone. **The methyl branch stays on carbon <InlineMath math="2" />**, so the acid is 2-methylpropanoic acid, an isomer of butanoic acid. Only <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of the alcohol becomes acid, and each acid molecule neutralises one hydroxide ion.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Butanoic acid has a straight chain of four carbon atoms. **Oxidation does not move the branch**, so the acid keeps it.
- <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> takes the yield as <InlineMath math="100\%" />. **Only three quarters of the alcohol becomes acid**.
- <InlineMath math="37.5\text{ cm}^3" /> lets each acid molecule neutralise two hydroxide ions, but the acid has one <InlineMath math="\text{–COOH}" /> group.

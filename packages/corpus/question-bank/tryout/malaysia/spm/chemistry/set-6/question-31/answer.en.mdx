# Oxidation keeps the branch, the yield trims the acid, and two acid molecules release one carbon dioxide

The dichromate turns <InlineMath math="\text{–CH}_2\text{OH}" /> into <InlineMath math="\text{–COOH}" /> and leaves the methyl branch on carbon <InlineMath math="2" />, so the acid is 2-methylpropanoic acid. Only <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of the <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of alcohol becomes acid. **A carbonate ion needs two hydrogen ions to release one carbon dioxide molecule**, so the gas is half the acid.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Butanoic acid has a straight chain. **Oxidation does not move the branch**, so the acid keeps it.
- <InlineMath math="600\text{ cm}^3" /> takes the yield as <InlineMath math="100\%" />. **Only three quarters of the alcohol becomes acid**.
- <InlineMath math="900\text{ cm}^3" /> releases one carbon dioxide per acid molecule. **Two acid molecules share one carbonate ion**.

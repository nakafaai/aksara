# Name the acid, trim it by the yield, then check whether the carbonate is enough

Oxidation turns <InlineMath math="\text{–CH}_2\text{OH}" /> into <InlineMath math="\text{–COOH}" /> and keeps the methyl branch, so the acid is 2-methylpropanoic acid, <InlineMath math="37.5\text{ mmol}" /> after the yield. **The carbonate is only <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ mmol}" />, and each carbonate ion takes two acid molecules**, so it can use just <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ mmol}" /> of acid. The carbonate runs out, and it sets the carbon dioxide at one molecule per carbonate ion.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Butanoic acid has a straight chain. **Oxidation does not move the branch**.
- <InlineMath math="450\text{ cm}^3" /> lets all the acid react. **The carbonate runs out first**.
- <InlineMath math="900\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per acid molecule, and the carbonate could not supply that much anyway.

# Moles of glucose set both the energy and the oxygen, six oxygen molecules to each glucose

Glucose has a molar mass of <InlineMath math="180" />, so <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />. **The <InlineMath math="2800\text{ kJ}" /> is per mole of glucose, and each glucose needs six oxygen molecules**. Respiration releases the energy that photosynthesis stored, the same reaction run backwards.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> takes one oxygen molecule per glucose. **The equation needs six**.
- <InlineMath math="25\,200\text{ kJ}" /> multiplies the grams by <InlineMath math="2800" />, as if the heat were per gram instead of per mole.
- <InlineMath math="280\text{ kJ}" /> and <InlineMath math="14.4\text{ dm}^3" /> use <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of glucose, from a molar mass of <InlineMath math="90" />.

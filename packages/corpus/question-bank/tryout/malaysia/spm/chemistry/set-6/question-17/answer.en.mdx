# Dilution spreads the same hydrogen ions through more water, and the pH can only approach neutral

Diluting a hundred times lowers the hydrogen ion concentration a hundred times, so the pH rises by two. **The moles of hydrogen ions from the acid do not change**, which also means the same moles of alkali neutralise it. However far it is diluted, an acid never becomes alkaline: the water's own ions take over and the pH only approaches <InlineMath math="7" />.

- Statement III is false. **A diluted acid cannot become alkaline**, so its pH stays just below <InlineMath math="7" />.
- Statement IV is false. The diluted solution holds the same <InlineMath math="1.00\times10^{-3}\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen ions, so it needs the same volume of alkali.

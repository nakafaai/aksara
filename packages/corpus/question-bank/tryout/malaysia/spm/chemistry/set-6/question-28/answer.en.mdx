# Each double bond adds one bromine molecule, so the mole ratio counts the double bonds

Two moles of bromine add to every mole of X. **One bromine molecule adds across each carbon to carbon double bond**, so X has two of them, as in buta-1,3-diene. Both double bonds become single bonds, and four bromine atoms join the chain, giving <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_6\text{Br}_4" /> with a molar mass of <InlineMath math="374" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.14\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_6\text{Br}_2" />, one bromine molecule per X. **The bromine used is twice the X**.
- Two double bonds take two bromine molecules, so the product is <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_6\text{Br}_4" />, not <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_6\text{Br}_2" />.
- One double bond would take only <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of bromine, half of what X decolourised.

# Equal moles of carbon dioxide and water mean two hydrogen atoms per carbon, and the bromine counts the molecules

The carbon dioxide and the water are both <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" />, so **there are two hydrogen atoms for every carbon atom**, <InlineMath math="\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}" />, like an alkene. Each molecule adds one bromine molecule, so the <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> of bromine means <InlineMath math="0.0125\text{ mol}" /> of hydrocarbon, with a molar mass of <InlineMath math="56" />: <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_8" />. Burning it needs six oxygen molecules per molecule.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}" /> would give more water than carbon dioxide. **Equal amounts mean <InlineMath math="\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_2\text{H}_4" /> has the right ratio, but <InlineMath math="0.70\text{ g}" /> of it would take <InlineMath math="0.025\text{ mol}" /> of bromine, twice what was used.
- <InlineMath math="1.20\text{ dm}^3" /> supplies oxygen only for the carbon dioxide and forgets the oxygen that ends up in the water.

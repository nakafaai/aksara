# The oxygen depends on the moles of peroxide, not on the volume of liquid, and heat destroys the enzyme's shape

The first run holds <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of peroxide, and **every two peroxide molecules give one oxygen molecule**. The second run has the same volume of liquid but only half the peroxide, so it gives half the oxygen, more slowly. Catalase is a protein: above its best temperature its active site changes shape, and the reaction falls back to its slow rate without a catalyst.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one oxygen molecule per peroxide molecule. **The equation gives one for every two**.
- The second run's total volume is the same, but **it holds half the peroxide**, so it can make only half the oxygen.
- A catalyst is not used up. **The enzyme is still present at <InlineMath math="65^\circ\text{C}" /> but has lost its shape**.

# Two peroxide molecules give one oxygen molecule, and an enzyme stops working once heat changes its shape

The peroxide holds <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" />, and **every two molecules give only one oxygen molecule**. Half of the <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> in <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" /> is <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" />. Catalase is a protein. Above its best temperature, heat changes the shape of its active site so the peroxide no longer fits, and the reaction falls back to its slow rate without a catalyst.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ cm}^3" /> gives one oxygen molecule per peroxide molecule. **The equation gives one for every two**.
- <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> divides the whole <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> by <InlineMath math="40\text{ s}" />, though only half of it forms in that time.
- A catalyst is not used up. **The enzyme is still there at <InlineMath math="65^\circ\text{C}" /> but has lost its shape**.

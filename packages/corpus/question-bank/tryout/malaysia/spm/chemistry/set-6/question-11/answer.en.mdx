# Subliming a molecular solid breaks no covalent bonds, while melting a giant lattice breaks four per silicon

Carbon dioxide is made of separate molecules. **When it sublimes only the forces between molecules are overcome**, so its bond energies are a distraction. In silicon dioxide each silicon atom bonds to four oxygen atoms and each oxygen to two silicon atoms, so counting from silicon gives four bonds per formula unit without counting any twice.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="220\text{ kJ}" /> breaks the double bonds in carbon dioxide. **Subliming leaves every molecule whole**.
- <InlineMath math="93.2\text{ kJ}" /> counts two bonds per silicon, as in <InlineMath math="\text{O=Si=O}" />. **The lattice has four single bonds on each silicon**.
- <InlineMath math="373\text{ kJ}" /> counts each bond from both its silicon and its oxygen, so every bond twice.

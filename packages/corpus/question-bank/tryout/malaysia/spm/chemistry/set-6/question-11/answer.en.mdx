# Subliming a molecular solid breaks no covalent bonds, but melting a giant lattice does

Carbon dioxide is made of separate molecules. **When it sublimes, only the weak forces between molecules are overcome**, and every carbon to oxygen bond survives. Silicon dioxide is one giant covalent lattice in which each silicon atom bonds to four oxygen atoms and each oxygen to two silicon atoms. Count the bonds from the silicon side so that none is counted twice.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.27\text{ mol}" /> breaks both double bonds in every carbon dioxide molecule. **Subliming leaves the molecules whole**.
- <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ mol}" /> draws silicon dioxide like carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="\text{O=Si=O}" />. **In the lattice each silicon atom has four single bonds**.
- <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> counts one bond per formula unit instead of four.

# Judge each statement on its own, and count iodine as two-atom molecules

Iodine and hexane are both non-polar molecular substances, so they mix easily. In potassium iodide solution, **an iodine molecule joins an iodide ion to form the brown triiodide ion**, which is why it dissolves far better than in water. Statement III gives the number of atoms, not molecules. Statement IV mixes up the bond inside a molecule with the forces between molecules.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Statement III doubles the count. **Each <InlineMath math="\text{I}_2" /> molecule holds two atoms**, so <InlineMath math="2.41\times10^{21}" /> is the number of atoms.
- Statement IV is false. **Subliming separates whole molecules**, and the bond inside each <InlineMath math="\text{I}_2" /> survives.
- Statement III is false, so no option that includes it can be right.

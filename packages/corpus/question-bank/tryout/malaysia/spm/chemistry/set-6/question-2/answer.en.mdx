# Volume grows in three directions, so spacing grows by the cube root

Nitrogen is <InlineMath math="\text{N}_2" />, so a mole weighs <InlineMath math="28\text{ g}" />. The same molecules spread through about <InlineMath math="690" /> times the volume. **That volume grows along all three directions at once**, so the distance between neighbouring molecules grows only by the cube root of <InlineMath math="690" />, about <InlineMath math="9" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="690" /> times treats the change in volume as the change in spacing, but spacing is one dimension and volume three.
- <InlineMath math="24.0\text{ dm}^3" /> assumes a whole mole of gas, though the sample is only <InlineMath math="0.0289\text{ mol}" />.
- <InlineMath math="1.39\text{ dm}^3" /> divides by <InlineMath math="14" />. **Nitrogen gas is diatomic, so its molar mass is <InlineMath math="28" />**.

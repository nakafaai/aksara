# Coating to volume, volume to moles, moles to charge, then allow for the charge that is wasted

The layer is area times thickness, with the thickness in centimetres, <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ cm}" />, to match the density: <InlineMath math="1.008\text{ g}" /> of silver. **Each silver ion takes one electron**, so <InlineMath math="901\text{ C}" /> must deposit on the spoon. **That is only <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of what passes**, so the supply must deliver <InlineMath math="1001\text{ C}" />. The spoon is the cathode, where silver ions gain electrons.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60.0" /> minutes ignores the wasted charge. **Only <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of the current deposits silver**.
- <InlineMath math="54.0" /> minutes multiplies by <InlineMath math="0.90" /> instead of dividing, as if the waste made plating faster.
- At the anode metal dissolves. **The object being plated is always the cathode**.

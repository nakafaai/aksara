# Turn the coating into a volume, the volume into moles, and the moles into charge

The layer is a thin slab: area times thickness, with the thickness in centimetres, <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ cm}" />, to match the density. **Each silver ion needs only one electron**, so the moles of electrons equal the moles of silver. The spoon must be the cathode, where silver ions gain electrons and deposit, while the silver anode dissolves to replace them.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="120" /> minutes gives each silver ion two electrons. **Silver ions carry a single positive charge**.
- <InlineMath math="6.0" /> minutes converts the thickness wrongly, <InlineMath math="0.0200\text{ mm}" /> as <InlineMath math="0.000200\text{ cm}" />, which makes the layer ten times too thin.
- At the anode metal dissolves. **The object to be plated is always the cathode**.

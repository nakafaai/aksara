# A disc has two faces to coat, and part of the charge is wasted

**The <InlineMath math="24.0\text{ cm}^2" /> is for each face, so <InlineMath math="48.0\text{ cm}^2" /> is plated**. With the thickness in centimetres, <InlineMath math="0.00200\text{ cm}" />, the silver is <InlineMath math="1.008\text{ g}" />, which needs <InlineMath math="901\text{ C}" /> at one electron per silver ion. Only <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of the charge deposits silver, so <InlineMath math="1001\text{ C}" /> must pass. The disc is the cathode, where silver ions gain electrons.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="33.3" /> minutes plates only one face of the disc.
- <InlineMath math="60.0" /> minutes ignores the wasted charge. **Only <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of the current deposits silver**.
- At the anode metal dissolves. **The object being plated is always the cathode**.

# The gas shrinks as it reacts, so the fifteen per cent is of a smaller total than four moles

If <InlineMath math="x\text{ mol}" /> of nitrogen reacts, the gas holds <InlineMath math="2x" /> of ammonia in a total of <InlineMath math="4-2x" />, **because four molecules become two**. Setting <InlineMath math="2x" /> to <InlineMath math="15\%" /> of that shrinking total gives <InlineMath math="x=0.261" />. Each mole of nitrogen that reacts releases <InlineMath math="92\text{ kJ}" />. Going backwards the reaction starts from the products, so its barrier adds the drop to the forward peak.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="13.8\text{ kJ}" /> reads the <InlineMath math="15\%" /> as the share of nitrogen that reacts. **It is the share of ammonia in the final gas**.
- <InlineMath math="138\text{ kJ}" /> subtracts the drop from the barrier. **The reverse reaction must climb the drop too**.
- <InlineMath math="27.6\text{ kJ}" /> takes <InlineMath math="15\%" /> of the starting <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ mol}" />. **The total falls as the gas reacts**.

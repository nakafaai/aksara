# The enthalpy change belongs to two moles of ammonia, and the reverse climb starts from the products

The value <InlineMath math="-92\text{ kJ}" /> is for the equation as written, which makes **two** moles of ammonia, so one mole releases <InlineMath math="46\text{ kJ}" />. **Going backwards, the reaction starts from the products, <InlineMath math="92\text{ kJ}" /> below the reactants**, so it must climb that drop as well as the forward barrier.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="9.20\text{ kJ}" /> treats <InlineMath math="92\text{ kJ}" /> as the heat for one mole of ammonia. **It is for two**.
- <InlineMath math="138\text{ kJ}" /> subtracts the drop from the barrier. **The reverse reaction must climb the drop too**.
- <InlineMath math="230\text{ kJ}" /> is the forward barrier, measured from the reactants, not from the products.

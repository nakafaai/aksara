# Each octane molecule carries eight carbon atoms into eight carbon dioxide molecules

The petrol is <InlineMath math="45.6\text{ g}" /> of the <InlineMath math="200\text{ g}" />. As octane it is <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol}" />, and **every carbon atom ends up in its own carbon dioxide molecule**, so the gas is eight times the octane. The fractions separate because longer chains have stronger forces between molecules and boil at higher temperatures.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="9.6\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one carbon dioxide per octane molecule. **Each molecule has eight carbon atoms**.
- <InlineMath math="45.6\%" /> divides the petrol's mass by <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> instead of the <InlineMath math="200\text{ g}" /> of crude oil.
- <InlineMath math="86.4\text{ dm}^3" /> counts nine products per octane, taking the water's moles for the carbon dioxide.

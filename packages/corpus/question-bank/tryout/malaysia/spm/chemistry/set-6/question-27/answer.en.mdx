The petrol is <InlineMath math="45.6\text{ g}" /> of <InlineMath math="200\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ mol}" /> of octane. # Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="120\text{ dm}^3" /> is the oxygen alone. **The question asks for air**, which is mostly nitrogen.
- <InlineMath math="45.6\%" /> divides the petrol's mass by <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> instead of the <InlineMath math="200\text{ g}" /> of crude oil.
- <InlineMath math="1140\text{ dm}^3" /> uses <InlineMath math="25" /> oxygen molecules per octane molecule. **The equation's <InlineMath math="25" /> is for two**.

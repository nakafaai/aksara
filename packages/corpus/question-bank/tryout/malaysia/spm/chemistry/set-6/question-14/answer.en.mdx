# Each oxide ion takes two hydrogen ions, just as each acid molecule gives two

The acid is <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ mol}" /> and gives <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen ions. **One oxide ion takes two hydrogen ions to make water**, so <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ mol}" /> of calcium oxide is enough, as the equation shows, and it gives <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ mol}" /> of calcium sulfate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="112\text{ g}" /> gives one oxide ion to each hydrogen ion. **Each oxide ion takes two**.
- <InlineMath math="28\text{ g}" /> halves the acid's amount, as if only half a mole of acid were spilt.
- <InlineMath math="74\text{ g}" /> is the mass of slaked lime, calcium hydroxide, not of quicklime.

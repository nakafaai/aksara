# The titration measures the diluted solution, so multiply back by the dilution factor

The burette reading gives the concentration of the diluted solution, <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. **The vinegar was diluted ten times, so the original is ten times as concentrated**. Ethanoic acid is a weak acid, and with a strong alkali the end point lies above <InlineMath math="\text{pH }7" />, where phenolphthalein changes colour.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ g dm}^{-3}" /> gives the concentration of the diluted solution. **The vinegar is ten times stronger**.
- Methyl orange changes colour in acid, well before the end point of a weak acid with a strong alkali.
- <InlineMath math="96.0\text{ g dm}^{-3}" /> treats ethanoic acid as needing half as much alkali. **Each molecule gives only one hydrogen ion**.

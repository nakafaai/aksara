# The flask runs out of oxygen first, so only part of the aluminium becomes oxide

The aluminium is <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> and the oxygen <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" />. **Burning all the aluminium would need <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" /> of oxygen**, so the oxygen limits the reaction and a third of the aluminium is left as metal. The oxide is <InlineMath math="0.0667\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3" />, five ions per unit, and recovering its aluminium gives each aluminium ion three electrons, counted once.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.01\times10^{23}" /> and <InlineMath math="5.79\times10^4\text{ C}" /> burn all the aluminium. **There is not enough oxygen for that**.
- <InlineMath math="1.29\times10^4\text{ C}" /> gives each aluminium ion one electron. **Its charge is <InlineMath math="3+" />**.
- <InlineMath math="7.72\times10^4\text{ C}" /> adds the anode's electrons to the cathode's, though each electron is carried once.

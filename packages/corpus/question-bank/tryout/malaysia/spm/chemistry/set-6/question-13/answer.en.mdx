# Count five ions per formula unit, and three electrons for each aluminium ion recovered

The <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> of aluminium makes <InlineMath math="0.100\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3" />, each unit with two aluminium ions and three oxide ions. **Getting the metal back reverses the electron transfer**: each aluminium ion takes three electrons at the cathode, and those are the same electrons the oxide ions give up at the anode, so they are counted once.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.20\times10^{23}" /> counts only two ions per unit. **Each unit has five**.
- <InlineMath math="1.93\times10^4\text{ C}" /> gives each aluminium ion one electron. **Its charge is <InlineMath math="3+" />**.
- <InlineMath math="1.16\times10^5\text{ C}" /> adds the anode's electrons to the cathode's, though the current carries each electron only once.

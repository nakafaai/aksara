# The carbon dioxide counts the carbon atoms, and the gas before the alkali is that carbon dioxide plus the unused oxygen

Equal gas volumes hold equal numbers of molecules. **Each methane molecule gives one carbon dioxide and each propane three**, so the total and the carbon dioxide give <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> of methane and <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" /> of propane. They use <InlineMath math="80\text{ cm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen, leaving <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />. The water is liquid after cooling, so the gas is carbon dioxide and spare oxygen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> of propane swaps the gases. **With <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> of propane the carbon dioxide would be <InlineMath math="180\text{ cm}^3" />**.
- <InlineMath math="660\text{ cm}^3" /> also counts the <InlineMath math="320\text{ cm}^3" /> of steam, which condenses at room conditions.
- <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" /> is the gas after the alkali, not before it.

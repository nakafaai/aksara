# Gas volumes count molecules, so the carbon dioxide counts the carbon atoms in the mixture

At the same conditions equal volumes hold equal numbers of molecules. **Each methane molecule gives one carbon dioxide and each propane molecule three**, so two equations, one for the total volume and one for the carbon dioxide, fix the mixture. Then each gas needs its own amount of oxygen: two volumes for methane and five for propane.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> of propane swaps the two gases. **With <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}^3" /> of propane the carbon dioxide would be only <InlineMath math="180\text{ cm}^3" />**.
- <InlineMath math="300\text{ cm}^3" /> counts only the oxygen for the propane and forgets the <InlineMath math="80\text{ cm}^3" /> that the methane uses.
- <InlineMath math="500\text{ cm}^3" /> gives every molecule five volumes of oxygen, as if the methane were propane.

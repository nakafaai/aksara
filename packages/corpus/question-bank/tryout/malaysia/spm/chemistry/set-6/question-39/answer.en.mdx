# Scale the label to the tube, then carry the same moles of fluorine into each compound

Parts per million by mass means milligrams per kilogram, so the tube holds <InlineMath math="145\text{ mg}" /> of fluorine, <InlineMath math="7.63\text{ mmol}" />. **Both compounds carry one fluorine atom per formula unit**, so each tube needs <InlineMath math="7.63\text{ mmol}" /> of its compound. The compounds weigh very differently, <InlineMath math="144" /> and <InlineMath math="42" /> per mole, so the masses differ though the fluoride is the same.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.145\text{ g}" /> is the fluorine alone. **Each compound weighs much more than its fluorine**.
- <InlineMath math="11.0\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="3.21\text{ g}" /> apply the label to a whole kilogram instead of the <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> tube.
- <InlineMath math="0.145\text{ g}" /> of sodium fluoride counts only its fluorine, leaving out the sodium.

# Scale the label to the tube, then carry the fluorine into the compound that holds it

Parts per million by mass means milligrams per kilogram, so a tenth of a kilogram holds <InlineMath math="145\text{ mg}" /> of fluorine. **The compound weighs far more than its fluorine**: each unit of molar mass <InlineMath math="144" /> carries one fluorine atom of <InlineMath math="19" />. Fluoride ions replace hydroxide ions in the enamel, and the fluorapatite formed dissolves less in the acids that bacteria make.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.145\text{ g}" /> is the fluorine alone. **The question asks for the whole compound**, which is about seven and a half times heavier.
- <InlineMath math="11.0\text{ g}" /> applies the label to a whole kilogram instead of the <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" /> tube.
- Fluoride is not an antacid. **It hardens the enamel against acid** rather than neutralising the acid.

# Find the fluorine the tube needs, take away what the phosphate supplies, then turn the rest into sodium fluoride

Parts per million by mass means milligrams per kilogram, so the tube needs <InlineMath math="145\text{ mg}" /> of fluorine. **The phosphate supplies only its fluorine share**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{19}{144}" /> of <InlineMath math="0.760\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="100\text{ mg}" />. The other <InlineMath math="44.7\text{ mg}" /> of fluorine comes from sodium fluoride, which is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{19}{42}" /> fluorine by mass.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.0447\text{ g}" /> is the fluorine still needed, not the sodium fluoride that carries it. **Sodium fluoride is less than half fluorine**.
- <InlineMath math="0.321\text{ g}" /> supplies all the fluorine from sodium fluoride and ignores the phosphate already added.
- <InlineMath math="2.98\text{ g}" /> reads the label as <InlineMath math="1450\text{ mg}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ g}" />. **Parts per million are milligrams per kilogram**.

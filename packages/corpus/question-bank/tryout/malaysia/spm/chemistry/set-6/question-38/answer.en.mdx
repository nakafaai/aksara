# Every repeating unit carries one chlorine atom, however long the chains are

The chain length does not matter: **each repeating unit, <InlineMath math="62.5\text{ g mol}^{-1}" />, holds one chlorine atom**. So <InlineMath math="1000\text{ g}" /> holds <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ mol}" /> of units and gives <InlineMath math="16.0\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen chloride. Each calcium oxide takes two hydrogen chloride molecules, so the scrubber needs <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ mol}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="896\text{ g}" /> gives one calcium oxide to each hydrogen chloride. **Each oxide takes two**.
- <InlineMath math="0.584\text{ g}" /> counts one chlorine per chain, but every repeating unit in the chain carries one.
- <InlineMath math="1168\text{ g}" /> takes two hydrogen chloride molecules per unit, but each unit has only one chlorine atom.

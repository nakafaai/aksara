# Count the carbon in the repeating unit, and judge disposal by how the polymer is built

The chain length does not matter: every <InlineMath math="\text{C}_8\text{H}_8" /> unit carries eight carbon atoms, and each becomes one carbon dioxide molecule, so statement I is right. **Polystyrene is a chain of strong carbon to carbon bonds that microbes cannot break**, so it lasts for centuries in landfill. With too little oxygen the carbon burns only partly.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Statement II is false. **Polystyrene is not biodegradable**, and statement IV is false too.
- Statement IV is false. Polystyrene is an addition polymer, formed when the double bonds of styrene open with nothing lost.
- Statement II is false. Polystyrene stays in landfill for hundreds of years.

# The chain length does not matter, because every repeating unit carries the same atoms

The <InlineMath math="4.16\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="0.0400\text{ mol}" /> of <InlineMath math="\text{C}_8\text{H}_8" /> units, however long the chains. **Count atoms, not units**: <InlineMath math="0.320\text{ mol}" /> of carbon and <InlineMath math="0.320\text{ mol}" /> of hydrogen. A quarter of the carbon, <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" />, becomes carbon monoxide, and every two hydrogen atoms make one water molecule.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="5.76\text{ g}" /> makes one water molecule per hydrogen atom. **Each water molecule holds two**.
- <InlineMath math="5.76\text{ dm}^3" /> is the carbon dioxide, three quarters of the carbon, not the carbon monoxide.
- <InlineMath math="0.240\text{ dm}^3" /> takes a quarter of the units instead of a quarter of the carbon atoms.

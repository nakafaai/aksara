# The heat in the equation belongs to two aluminium atoms, and a high barrier explains the fuse

The <InlineMath math="850\text{ kJ}" /> is released when **two** moles of aluminium react, so <InlineMath math="0.200\text{ mol}" /> is one tenth of the equation. Each aluminium atom frees one iron atom. The reaction is strongly exothermic, but the solids do not react at room temperature, because the activation energy is high, so a very hot magnesium flame starts it.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="170\text{ kJ}" /> gives <InlineMath math="850\text{ kJ}" /> to each mole of aluminium. **It is for two moles**.
- <InlineMath math="5.6\text{ g}" /> forms one iron atom per two aluminium atoms. **The equation gives two iron for two aluminium**.
- A negative <InlineMath math="\Delta H" /> means the reaction releases heat overall. **The fuse supplies only the energy to start it**.

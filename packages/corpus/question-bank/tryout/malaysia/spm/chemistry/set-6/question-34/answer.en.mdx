# Find which solid runs out, then scale the heat to the equation and explain the fuse with the barrier

The aluminium is enough for <InlineMath math="0.100" /> lots of the equation but the oxide for only <InlineMath math="0.0750" />. **The iron(III) oxide runs out first, so it sets both the heat and the iron**, and some aluminium is left over. Each oxide unit gives two iron atoms. The reaction is strongly exothermic, but the solids do not react at room temperature because the activation energy is high, so a very hot magnesium flame starts it.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="85.0\text{ kJ}" /> and <InlineMath math="11.2\text{ g}" /> let all the aluminium react. **The oxide runs out first**.
- <InlineMath math="4.20\text{ g}" /> gives one iron atom per oxide unit. **Each <InlineMath math="\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3" /> holds two**.
- A negative <InlineMath math="\Delta H" /> means the reaction releases heat overall. **The fuse supplies only the energy to start it**.

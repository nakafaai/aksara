# One peroxide molecule oxidises two iron(II) ions, and its oxygen is reduced from minus one

The equation pairs **one hydrogen peroxide with two iron(II) ions**, so the peroxide is half the iron. In hydrogen peroxide each oxygen has an oxidation number of <InlineMath math="-1" />, because the two oxygen atoms share a bond with each other. In water it is <InlineMath math="-2" />, so the oxygen is reduced while the iron is oxidised from <InlineMath math="+2" /> to <InlineMath math="+3" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12.0\%" /> takes one peroxide per iron(II) ion. **The equation uses two iron(II) ions for each peroxide**.
- The peroxide is the oxidising agent here, so its oxygen gains electrons and its oxidation number falls, not rises.
- Oxygen in hydrogen peroxide is <InlineMath math="-1" />, not <InlineMath math="-2" />. **The oxygen to oxygen bond shares its electrons equally**.

# Halve the iron for the peroxide, scale up by the dilution, and weigh the sample through its density

One peroxide molecule oxidises two iron(II) ions, so the portion holds <InlineMath math="1.50\text{ mmol}" />. **The portion is a tenth of the diluted solution**, which holds all the peroxide from the sample, <InlineMath math="15.0\text{ mmol}" /> or <InlineMath math="0.510\text{ g}" />. The sample weighs <InlineMath math="5.00\times1.02=5.10\text{ g}" />. In hydrogen peroxide oxygen is <InlineMath math="-1" />, and in water <InlineMath math="-2" />, so the oxygen is reduced.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="20.0\%" /> takes one peroxide per iron(II) ion. **The equation uses two iron(II) ions for each peroxide**.
- The peroxide is the oxidising agent, so its oxygen gains electrons and its oxidation number falls.
- <InlineMath math="1.00\%" /> forgets that the titrated portion is only a tenth of the diluted solution.

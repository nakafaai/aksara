# Remove the escaped iodine first, then find which reactant runs out

Only <InlineMath math="90.0\%" /> of the iodine stays to react, <InlineMath math="0.0900\text{ mol}" />. **Compare each amount with its number in the equation**: the iodine allows <InlineMath math="0.0300" /> lots of the reaction and the aluminium <InlineMath math="0.0500" />, so iodine runs out first. Two aluminium iodide units form for every three iodine molecules, and the same number of aluminium atoms is used.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="27.2\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ g}" /> let all the iodine react. **A tenth of it escapes before it can**.
- <InlineMath math="40.8\text{ g}" /> treats aluminium as the limiting reactant, but the iodine it needs, <InlineMath math="0.150\text{ mol}" />, is not there.
- <InlineMath math="0.27\text{ g}" /> uses up one aluminium atom per iodine molecule instead of two for every three.

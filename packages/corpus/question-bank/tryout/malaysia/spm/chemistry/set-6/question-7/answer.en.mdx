# Multiply the two yields, then follow the acid through both dilutions

The second yield applies to the ethanol that formed, so the acid is <InlineMath math="0.100\times0.90\times0.80=0.0720\text{ mol}" />. **The titrated portion is two dilutions away from the flask**: <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" /> is a twenty-fifth, and <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> of <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> is a quarter of that. Ethanoic acid reacts with sodium hydroxide one to one.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n(\text{CH}_3\text{COOH})&=\tfrac{2.40}{24}\times0.90\times0.80=0.0720\text{ mol}\\
n(\text{portion})&=0.0720\times\tfrac{10.0}{250}\times\tfrac{25.0}{100}=7.20\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}\\
V&=\tfrac{7.20\times10^{-4}}{0.100}=7.20\times10^{-3}\text{ dm}^3=7.20\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="9.00\text{ cm}^3" /> uses only the first yield. **The oxidation keeps only <InlineMath math="80\%" />**.
- <InlineMath math="72.0\text{ cm}^3" /> forgets the second dilution, as if <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> came straight from the <InlineMath math="250\text{ cm}^3" /> flask.
- <InlineMath math="8.00\text{ cm}^3" /> uses only the second yield.

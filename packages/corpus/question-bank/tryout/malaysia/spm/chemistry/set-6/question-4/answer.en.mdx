# The percentages give the ratio, the gas volume gives the size, and the equation gives the oxygen

The mass percentages fix only the ratio of atoms, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" />. **The gas volume gives the moles, and the moles give the molar mass**, so the molecule holds five <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> units. Burning it needs oxygen for both elements: five molecules for the carbon dioxide and two and a half for the water. Gases at the same conditions react in the ratio of their moles, so the volumes follow the equation directly.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}" /> would contain <InlineMath math="83.3\%" /> carbon and have a molar mass of <InlineMath math="72" />.
- <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> is only the empirical formula. **The molar mass of <InlineMath math="70" /> is five of these units**.
- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> supplies oxygen for the carbon only. **The ten hydrogen atoms also need oxygen to form water**.

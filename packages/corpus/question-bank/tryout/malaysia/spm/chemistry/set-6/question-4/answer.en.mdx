# Weigh the carbon and hydrogen first, and the missing mass tells you whether there is oxygen

The carbon dioxide holds <InlineMath math="0.72\text{ g}" /> of carbon and the water <InlineMath math="0.12\text{ g}" /> of hydrogen. **Together they make the whole <InlineMath math="0.84\text{ g}" />, so X contains no oxygen**, and its ratio is <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" />. The gas volume gives a molar mass of <InlineMath math="84" />, six <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> units. Burning it needs oxygen for both elements: six molecules for the carbon and three for the hydrogen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_6\text{H}_{14}" /> would have a molar mass of <InlineMath math="86" /> and give more water than measured.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_8\text{O}" /> also has a molar mass of <InlineMath math="84" />, but **the carbon and hydrogen already account for all <InlineMath math="0.84\text{ g}" />**, leaving no mass for oxygen.
- <InlineMath math="1440\text{ cm}^3" /> supplies oxygen for the carbon only. **The hydrogen needs oxygen too**.

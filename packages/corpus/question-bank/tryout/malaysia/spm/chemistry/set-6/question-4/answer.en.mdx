# Weigh the carbon and hydrogen first, and the missing mass tells you whether there is oxygen

The carbon dioxide holds <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ g}" /> of carbon and the water <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ g}" /> of hydrogen. **Together they make the whole <InlineMath math="0.70\text{ g}" />, so X contains no oxygen**, and its ratio is <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" />. The gas volume gives a molar mass of <InlineMath math="70" />, five <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_2" /> units. Burning it needs oxygen for both elements: five molecules for the carbon and two and a half for the hydrogen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}" /> would have a molar mass of <InlineMath math="72" /> and give more water than measured.
- <InlineMath math="\text{C}_4\text{H}_6\text{O}" /> also has a molar mass of <InlineMath math="70" />, but **the carbon and hydrogen already account for all <InlineMath math="0.70\text{ g}" />**, leaving no mass for oxygen.
- <InlineMath math="1200\text{ cm}^3" /> supplies oxygen for the carbon only. **The hydrogen needs oxygen too**.

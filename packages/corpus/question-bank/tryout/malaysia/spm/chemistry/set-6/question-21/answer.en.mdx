# A solid ionic compound does not conduct, so charge flows only after the salt melts

In solid lead(II) bromide the ions are fixed in the lattice, so **no current flows until the salt melts**, whatever the supply is set to. Current flows for <InlineMath math="41\text{ min }20\text{ s}" /> minus <InlineMath math="9\text{ min }10\text{ s}" />, <InlineMath math="1930\text{ s}" />, carrying <InlineMath math="965\text{ C}" />. Lead ions take two electrons each at the cathode, and bromide ions lose them at the anode, forming <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.33\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.03\text{ g}" /> count the whole <InlineMath math="2480\text{ s}" />. **The solid salt conducts no current**.
- Lead ions are positive, so they go to the negative cathode.
- <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ g}" /> weighs bromine atoms at <InlineMath math="80" /> but counts them as molecules.

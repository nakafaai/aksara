# Turn the time into seconds, the charge into electrons, and give two electrons to each product particle

The current flows for <InlineMath math="1930\text{ s}" />, carrying <InlineMath math="965\text{ C}" />, <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of electrons. Melting frees the ions to move: lead ions go to the cathode and gain electrons, bromide ions to the anode and lose them. **Both half-equations use two electrons per product particle**, and bromine forms as <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" />, <InlineMath math="160" /> per mole.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.07\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.60\text{ g}" /> use one electron per lead ion and per bromine molecule. **Each takes two**.
- Lead ions are positive, so they go to the negative cathode, not the anode.
- <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ g}" /> weighs bromine atoms at <InlineMath math="80" /> but counts them as molecules.

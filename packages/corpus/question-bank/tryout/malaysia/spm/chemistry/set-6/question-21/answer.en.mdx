# Each lead ion takes two electrons, and each bromine molecule releases two

Melting frees the ions to move. Positive lead ions go to the cathode and gain electrons, and bromide ions go to the anode and lose them. **Both half-equations use two electrons per product particle**, so the <InlineMath math="0.0100\text{ mol}" /> of electrons makes <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of lead and <InlineMath math="0.00500\text{ mol}" /> of bromine molecules, each of mass <InlineMath math="160" /> per mole.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.07\text{ g}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.60\text{ g}" /> use one electron per lead ion and per bromine molecule. **Each takes two**.
- Lead ions are positive, so they are attracted to the negative cathode, not the anode.
- <InlineMath math="0.400\text{ g}" /> weighs bromine atoms at <InlineMath math="80" /> but counts them as molecules. **Bromine forms <InlineMath math="\text{Br}_2" />**.

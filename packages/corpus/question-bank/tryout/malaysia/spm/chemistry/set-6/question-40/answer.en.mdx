# Scale the portion up to the flask, then share the flask among four tablets

Aspirin has one acidic hydrogen, so **each mole of sodium hydroxide counts one mole of aspirin**. The portion is a tenth of the flask, and the flask holds four tablets, so one tablet is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{10}{4}" /> times the portion. Ethanol is used because aspirin dissolves poorly in water, and phenolphthalein suits a weak acid with a strong alkali. The label goes underneath in the percentage.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="108\text{ mg}" /> is the aspirin in the <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ cm}^3" /> portion, which holds less than half a tablet.
- <InlineMath math="1080\text{ mg}" /> is the aspirin in all four tablets. **Divide by four for one tablet**.
- <InlineMath math="111\%" /> divides the label by the measured mass. **The percentage of the label puts the label on the bottom**.

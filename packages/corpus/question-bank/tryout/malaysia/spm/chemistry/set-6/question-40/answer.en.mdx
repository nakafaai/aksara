# A monoprotic acid reacts one to one, and the label is the reference for the percentage

Aspirin has one acidic hydrogen, so **each mole of sodium hydroxide counts one mole of aspirin**. Aspirin dissolves poorly in water, which is why ethanol is used, and as a weak acid with a strong alkali its end point suits phenolphthalein. The measured mass is then compared with the labelled <InlineMath math="300\text{ mg}" />, so the label goes underneath.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="135\text{ mg}" /> gives each aspirin two hydrogen ions to neutralise. **Aspirin is monoprotic**.
- <InlineMath math="540\text{ mg}" /> lets one hydroxide ion count two aspirin molecules, which would put more aspirin in the tablet than its label.
- <InlineMath math="111\%" /> divides the label by the measured mass. **The percentage of the label puts the label on the bottom**.

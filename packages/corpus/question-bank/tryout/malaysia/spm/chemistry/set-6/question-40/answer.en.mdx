# Use the standardised concentration, then scale the portion up to the flask and share it among four tablets

**The label on the alkali is not its true concentration**: standardisation gives <InlineMath math="0.0950\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, so the portion holds <InlineMath math="5.70\times10^{-4}\text{ mol}" /> of aspirin, one hydroxide ion per aspirin. The portion is a tenth of the flask and the flask holds four tablets, so one tablet holds <InlineMath math="\tfrac{10}{4}" /> times the portion. Phenolphthalein suits a weak acid with a strong alkali, and the label goes underneath in the percentage.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="270\text{ mg}" /> uses the alkali's label. **Standardisation shows it is weaker**, so less aspirin reacted.
- <InlineMath math="103\text{ mg}" /> is the aspirin in the portion, which holds less than half a tablet.
- <InlineMath math="1026\text{ mg}" /> is the aspirin in all four tablets. **Divide by four for one tablet**.

# Test each gas against both conditions, then count argon as single atoms

At the same temperature and pressure a gas's density follows its molar mass, so only a gas heavier than <InlineMath math="29" /> sinks through air. **Helium and nitrogen are both lighter than air, and nitrogen also reacts with hot aluminium**. Argon, with a full octet <InlineMath math="2.8.8" />, is unreactive and heavier than air. It is monatomic, so its molar mass is <InlineMath math="40" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Nitrogen is slightly lighter than air and **forms aluminium nitride with the hot metal**, so it fails both conditions.
- Helium is far lighter than air, so it rises away from the weld instead of covering it.
- <InlineMath math="960\text{ dm}^3" /> treats argon as <InlineMath math="\text{Ar}_2" />. **Noble gases are monatomic**.

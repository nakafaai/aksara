# Argon is monatomic, and at the same conditions a gas's density follows its molar mass

**Argon exists as single atoms**, so its molar mass is <InlineMath math="40" />, not <InlineMath math="80" />. Its arrangement <InlineMath math="2.8.8" /> is a full octet, so it neither gains, loses nor shares electrons and cannot react with the hot metal. At the same temperature and pressure, equal volumes hold equal moles, so a gas's density is proportional to its molar mass. Argon is denser than air and sinks over the weld, pushing the oxygen away.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="960\text{ dm}^3" /> treats argon as diatomic, <InlineMath math="\text{Ar}_2" />. **Noble gases are monatomic**.
- <InlineMath math="0.73" /> divides air's molar mass by argon's. **Argon is the heavier gas**, so it sinks.
- Argon atoms already have a full octet, so they form no bonds with each other or with the metal.

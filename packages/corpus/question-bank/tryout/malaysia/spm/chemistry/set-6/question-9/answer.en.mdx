# Test each gas against both conditions, count argon as single atoms, then divide by the flow

At the same conditions a gas's density follows its molar mass. **Helium and nitrogen are lighter than air, and nitrogen also reacts with hot aluminium**, so only argon passes both tests. Argon is monatomic, so <InlineMath math="3.20\text{ kg}" /> is <InlineMath math="80.0\text{ mol}" />, <InlineMath math="1920\text{ dm}^3" />, which lasts <InlineMath math="240" /> minutes at the flow rate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Nitrogen is slightly lighter than air and **forms aluminium nitride with the hot metal**, so it fails both conditions.
- Helium is far lighter than air, so it rises away from the weld.
- <InlineMath math="2.0" /> hours treats argon as <InlineMath math="\text{Ar}_2" />. **Noble gases are monatomic**.

# The mass lost is water and carbon dioxide together, and the residue is all sodium carbonate

**Two moles of sodium hydrogen carbonate lose one water and one carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="62\text{ g}" /> in all**, so the loss gives <InlineMath math="0.0800\text{ mol}" /> of it. After heating, the residue of <InlineMath math="7.52\text{ g}" /> is only sodium carbonate, from both sources. Each carbonate ion takes two hydrogen ions to become carbon dioxide and water.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="33.6\%" /> gives each sodium hydrogen carbonate the whole <InlineMath math="62\text{ g}" /> of loss, but that loss belongs to two of them.
- <InlineMath math="35.5\text{ cm}^3" /> reacts one hydrogen ion with each carbonate ion. **Full neutralisation to carbon dioxide needs two**.
- <InlineMath math="94.7\%" /> counts the loss as carbon dioxide alone, though the water leaves too.

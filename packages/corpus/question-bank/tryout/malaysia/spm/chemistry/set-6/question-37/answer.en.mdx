# Each monomer becomes one repeating unit, the feed covers the gas that does not react, and two raw molecules make each monomer

Addition polymerisation keeps every atom, so each repeating unit has the monomer's molar mass, <InlineMath math="100" />, and the polymer holds <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ mol}" /> of units. **Only <InlineMath math="95\%" /> of the feed reacts**, so <InlineMath math="10.5\text{ mol}" /> must be fed in. **The raw material gives one monomer for every two of its molecules**, so twice as many moles of chlorodifluoromethane, <InlineMath math="86.5" /> per mole, are needed.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="1.73\text{ kg}" /> assume all the monomer polymerises. **Only <InlineMath math="95\%" /> does**.
- <InlineMath math="0.911\text{ kg}" /> makes one monomer from each raw molecule. **The equation needs two**.
- <InlineMath math="228\text{ dm}^3" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="0.95" /> instead of dividing. **The feed must exceed what reacts**.

# Each monomer becomes one repeating unit, and the feed must cover the gas that does not react

Addition polymerisation keeps every atom, so each repeating unit, <InlineMath math="\text{–CF}_2\text{–CF}_2\text{–}" />, has the monomer's molar mass of <InlineMath math="100" />. **Only <InlineMath math="95\%" /> of the gas reacts, so the feed must be larger than the <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ mol}" /> that ends up in the polymer**. The carbon to fluorine bond is one of the strongest single bonds, and the fluorine sheath leaves nothing for food or water to stick to.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ dm}^3" /> assumes all the gas polymerises. **Only <InlineMath math="95\%" /> does**.
- <InlineMath math="228\text{ dm}^3" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="0.95" /> instead of dividing. **The feed must be larger than the amount that reacts**.
- PTFE is an addition polymer, and the double bond opens with no small molecule lost.

# The feed must cover the monomer that never polymerises, and every monomer made brings two hydrogen chloride molecules

Each repeating unit has the monomer's molar mass, <InlineMath math="100" />, so the polymer holds <InlineMath math="10.0\text{ mol}" /> of units. **Only <InlineMath math="95\%" /> of the feed reacts**, so <InlineMath math="10.5\text{ mol}" /> of monomer must be made and fed. **The hydrogen chloride comes with all the monomer made, not just the part that polymerises**: two per monomer, <InlineMath math="21.1\text{ mol}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="240\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="2.00\text{ dm}^3" /> assume all the monomer polymerises. **Only <InlineMath math="95\%" /> does**.
- <InlineMath math="1.05\text{ dm}^3" /> gives one hydrogen chloride per monomer. **The equation gives two**.
- <InlineMath math="228\text{ dm}^3" /> multiplies by <InlineMath math="0.95" /> instead of dividing. **The feed must exceed what reacts**.

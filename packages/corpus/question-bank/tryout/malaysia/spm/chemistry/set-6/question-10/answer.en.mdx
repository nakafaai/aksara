# Both metals give hydrogen, two atoms per molecule, so the hydrogen splits the mass between them

The hydrogen is <InlineMath math="0.01667\text{ mol}" />, and **every two metal atoms, of either kind, give one hydrogen molecule**, so the alloy holds <InlineMath math="0.0333\text{ mol}" /> of atoms. Writing the sodium as <InlineMath math="x\text{ g}" /> and the potassium as <InlineMath math="(1.00-x)\text{ g}" /> gives <InlineMath math="x=0.431" />. Potassium's outer electron is further from its nucleus, so it reacts more vigorously.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.431\text{ g}" /> is the sodium, and sodium is the less reactive metal. **Potassium is the rest of the gram**.
- <InlineMath math="0.431\text{ g}" /> is the mass of sodium, not potassium.
- The mass is right, but potassium, lower in Group 1, reacts more vigorously than sodium.

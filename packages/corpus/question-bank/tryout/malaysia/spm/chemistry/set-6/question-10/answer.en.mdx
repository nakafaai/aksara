# Iron shows two oxidation states, and the oxidising agent decides which one forms

Iron is a transition element with more than one oxidation state. **Chlorine is a strong oxidising agent and takes iron to <InlineMath math="+3" />, but hydrogen ions take it only to <InlineMath math="+2" />**. The same <InlineMath math="0.0500\text{ mol}" /> of iron therefore gives two different chlorides. In the acid each iron atom gives two electrons to two hydrogen ions, which make one hydrogen molecule.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Both samples giving <InlineMath math="6.35\text{ g}" /> assumes chlorine also stops at <InlineMath math="+2" />. **Chlorine oxidises iron fully to <InlineMath math="+3" />**.
- Both giving <InlineMath math="8.13\text{ g}" /> assumes the acid also forms iron(III) chloride, which would give <InlineMath math="1.80\text{ dm}^3" /> of hydrogen.
- <InlineMath math="2.40\text{ dm}^3" /> counts each hydrogen atom as a molecule. **Two hydrogen ions make one <InlineMath math="\text{H}_2" />**.

# The carat number is a fraction of twenty-four, and the copper share applies only to the rest

Eighteen carat means **eighteen parts in twenty-four**, <InlineMath math="75\%" /> gold, so <InlineMath math="3.00\text{ g}" /> of the ring is gold. The other <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ g}" /> is copper and silver, and copper is three quarters of that. **Copper atoms are much lighter than gold atoms**, so less than a fifth of the mass as copper still gives almost as many atoms as the gold. The different atom sizes stop the layers sliding, which hardens the ring.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.35\times10^{21}" /> copper atoms use the <InlineMath math="25\%" /> share, which is the silver.
- <InlineMath math="2.20\times10^{20}" /> reads eighteen carat as <InlineMath math="18\%" /> gold. **It means eighteen twenty-fourths**.
- <InlineMath math="1.22\times10^{22}" /> gold atoms count the whole <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ g}" /> as gold.

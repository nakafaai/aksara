# The carat number is a fraction of twenty-four, and foreign atoms stop the layers sliding

Eighteen carat means **eighteen parts in twenty-four**, <InlineMath math="75\%" /> gold, so the ring holds <InlineMath math="3.00\text{ g}" /> of gold. In pure gold the identical atoms sit in neat layers that slide over each other when a force acts. Copper and silver atoms have different sizes, so they distort the layers and make sliding harder.

# Why the other options are wrong

- An alloy is held by metallic bonding. **No ions form between the metal atoms**, so ionic bonds do not explain the hardness.
- <InlineMath math="2.20\times10^{20}" /> reads eighteen carat as <InlineMath math="18\%" /> of <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ g}" /> of gold. **It means eighteen twenty-fourths**.
- <InlineMath math="1.22\times10^{22}" /> counts the whole <InlineMath math="4.00\text{ g}" /> as gold, though a quarter of the ring is copper and silver.

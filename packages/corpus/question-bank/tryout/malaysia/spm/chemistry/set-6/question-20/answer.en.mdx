# Four hydroxide groups leave as only two water molecules, and their spare oxygen stays behind

The clay's molar mass is <InlineMath math="258" />, so <InlineMath math="5160\text{ g}" /> is <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ mol}" />. **Four hydroxide groups hold four hydrogen atoms, enough for only two water molecules**, and the other two oxygen atoms stay in the ceramic. Each mole loses <InlineMath math="36\text{ g}" /> and leaves <InlineMath math="222\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="3.72\text{ kg}" /> drives off four water molecules per formula unit. **There is hydrogen for only two**.
- <InlineMath math="3.80\text{ kg}" /> removes the whole mass of the four hydroxide groups, oxygen included, but half of that oxygen stays.
- <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ kg}" /> drives off only one water molecule per formula unit.

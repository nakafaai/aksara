# Use the feedback loop to see where the fault starts

TSH and thyroxine are linked in a **negative feedback** loop. Reading both levels together shows which gland is at fault.

<Mermaid
  title={<>Feedback Control of Thyroxine</>}
  description={<>The pituitary gland secretes TSH, which makes the thyroid gland secrete thyroxine from iodine in the diet. A high thyroxine level slows the secretion of TSH, and a low level speeds it up.</>}
  chart={"flowchart LR\n  H[Hypothalamus] --> PI[Pituitary gland]\n  PI -- TSH --> TH[Thyroid gland]\n  I[Iodine in diet] --> TH\n  TH -- thyroxine --> B[Body cells]\n  TH -. high thyroxine slows TSH .-> PI"}
/>

- P: thyroxine is low, so the feedback releases the pituitary, and TSH climbs to <InlineMath math="40" />. The pituitary works, but the thyroid cannot make thyroxine. TSH keeps stimulating the thyroid, which swells into a goitre. This fits a **lack of iodine**.
- Q: thyroxine is low, yet TSH is also low at <InlineMath math="0.2" />. A healthy pituitary would raise TSH, so the fault is in the **pituitary gland**. With little TSH, the thyroid stays small.
- R: thyroxine is high at <InlineMath math="250" /> and TSH is pushed down. The thyroid is overactive on its own, so the feedback has switched TSH off.
- S: both values are in the normal range.

So Q has the faulty pituitary gland, and P most likely lacks iodine.

# Why the other options are wrong

- P and Q swaps the two patients and reads high TSH as a faulty pituitary, **but high TSH is the pituitary responding normally**.
- R and P takes the low TSH of R as pituitary failure, **although R's thyroxine is high and is what switches TSH off**.
- Q and R picks the swollen gland of R as iodine lack, **but without iodine thyroxine would be low, not high**.

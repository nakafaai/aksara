# Separate slow enzymes from destroyed enzymes

The first row shows how fast the enzyme works at that temperature. The second row shows how much of the enzyme is **still intact** afterwards, because cooling restores every molecule that was only slowed down.

- I: at <InlineMath math="5^\circ\text{C}" /> the activity is only <InlineMath math="10\%" />, but after warming to <InlineMath math="35^\circ\text{C}" /> it is back to <InlineMath math="100\%" />. The molecules were slowed by low kinetic energy, not denatured. Statement I is correct.
- II: after <InlineMath math="50^\circ\text{C}" /> only <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of the activity returns, so <InlineMath math="100\%-60\%=40\%" /> of the molecules have lost the shape of their **active site** for good. Statement II is correct.
- III: each <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes leaves <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of the molecules that were still active, so after <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes <InlineMath math="0.6\times0.6=0.36" />, about <InlineMath math="36\%" />, remains. Statement III is correct.
- IV: after <InlineMath math="20^\circ\text{C}" /> all <InlineMath math="100\%" /> returns, so no molecules are denatured. The low value of <InlineMath math="45\%" /> only means slower collisions. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, usually after subtracting <InlineMath math="40\%" /> twice to leave <InlineMath math="20\%" />, **but each step removes** <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of what is still active.
- I, II and IV only reads the low activity at <InlineMath math="20^\circ\text{C}" /> as damage, **but full activity returns after cooling**.
- II and IV only rejects I and so treats every low reading as denaturation, ignoring the **second row of results**.

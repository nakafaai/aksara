# Treat the trunk as a store that empties and refills

Transpiration pulls water out of the leaves, and the **transpiration pull** is passed down the continuous water column in the xylem. When water leaves the top faster than it enters at the roots, the column is stretched and the trunk shrinks a little.

- I: from hour <InlineMath math="3" /> to hour <InlineMath math="9" /> the trunk shrinks while transpiration is high. Water is being pulled out faster than the roots replace it, so the xylem is under tension and its walls are drawn inwards. Statement I is correct.
- II: root pressure pushes water up only slowly and is strongest at night. A high, fast flow at midday under tension is driven by **transpiration pull**. Statement II is wrong.
- III: after hour <InlineMath math="9" /> transpiration falls, the roots keep absorbing water, and the trunk refills to <InlineMath math="0" /> by hour <InlineMath math="18" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the pull begins where water evaporates from the leaves, then travels down the column, so the base of the trunk peaks two hours after the upper branches. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects IV, missing that the **two-hour lag** shows the pull spreading down from the leaves.
- I, II and III only credits root pressure at midday, **but root pressure cannot cause a stretched, shrinking trunk**.
- III and IV only rejects I, **although the shrinking trunk is the clearest sign of tension**.

# Split the uptake into a passive part and an active part

Cyanide removes ATP, so whatever uptake survives it is **passive**. The difference between the two lines is the part that needs energy.

<BlockMath math="\text{at }40\text{ mM}:\quad \text{active}=12-4=8,\qquad \text{passive}=4,\qquad \tfrac{8}{4}=2" />

- I: simple diffusion would keep rising with concentration. The line without cyanide levels off because every **carrier protein** is already busy. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="4" /> units per hour still enter with cyanide, and that uptake also levels off, so it uses carriers too. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the active part is <InlineMath math="8" /> and the passive part is <InlineMath math="4" />, so the active part is twice the passive part. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the passive uptake levels off, which marks **facilitated diffusion** through carriers down the gradient. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects IV, as if passive uptake could not use carriers, **but a levelling line shows carriers are full**.
- I, II and III only accepts II, treating every carrier as an ATP pump, **although uptake continues with cyanide**.
- III and IV only rejects I, reading the flat line as cells that are already full of X instead of **saturated carriers**.

The table gives **heights**, not rates, so first subtract each height from the next one. Week <InlineMath math="n" /> runs from the end of week <InlineMath math="n-1" /> to the end of week <InlineMath math="n" />.

The largest gain is <InlineMath math="58-38=20\text{ cm}" /> in week <InlineMath math="5" />, so the absolute growth rate is highest in week <InlineMath math="5" />. That is the steepest part of the S-shaped growth curve.

Now divide each gain by the height at the start of its week:

<BlockMath math="\text{week }1: \tfrac{2}{2}=1.0,\quad \text{week }2: \tfrac{5}{4}=1.25,\quad \text{week }3: \tfrac{11}{9}\approx1.22,\quad \text{week }4: \tfrac{18}{20}=0.9,\quad \text{week }5: \tfrac{20}{38}\approx0.53" />

A small young plant grows a lot **for its size**, because almost every cell is still dividing. Later the plant gains more centimetres, but each centimetre is a smaller share of a bigger plant.

So the absolute growth rate is highest in week <InlineMath math="5" /> and the relative growth rate is highest in week <InlineMath math="2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Week <InlineMath math="8" /> for both reads the **tallest height** as the fastest growth, but the plant gains only <InlineMath math="2\text{ cm}" /> that week.
- Week <InlineMath math="5" /> for both assumes the two rates peak together, **but the relative rate falls as the plant gets bigger**.
- Week <InlineMath math="4" /> and week <InlineMath math="1" /> names each gain after the week it **starts from**, so the gain from the end of week <InlineMath math="4" /> to the end of week <InlineMath math="5" /> is wrongly called week <InlineMath math="4" />.

# Count the splits, then ask what the meristem has not yet built

The shoots are split at weeks <InlineMath math="4" />, <InlineMath math="8" />, <InlineMath math="12" />, <InlineMath math="16" /> and <InlineMath math="20" />, so the number is **multiplied by <InlineMath math="5" /> five times**. The meristem at the tip is made of young, undivided cells that have not yet formed the vascular tissue the virus travels in.

<BlockMath math="1\times5^5=5\times5\times5\times5\times5=3125" />

- I: five splits of <InlineMath math="5" /> give <InlineMath math="5^5=3125" /> shoots. Statement I is correct.
- II: the virus moves in xylem and phloem. The cells at the very tip have not yet become vascular tissue, so a tiny tip can be cut out before the virus reaches it. Statement II is correct.
- III: every new cell comes from mitosis, which copies the chromosomes exactly, so the plants form a clone of the parent. Statement III is correct.
- IV: meristem cells have the same chromosome number as every other body cell. They can form any tissue because they have **not yet differentiated**. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although mitosis copies the parent's chromosomes exactly**.
- II, III and IV only rejects the count, often after **multiplying by <InlineMath math="5" /> only twice**, <InlineMath math="5\times5=25" />, instead of <InlineMath math="5^5" />, and accepts IV.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and gives meristem cells **extra chromosomes they do not have**.

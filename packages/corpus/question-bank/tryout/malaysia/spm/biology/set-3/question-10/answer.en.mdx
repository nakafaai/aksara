# Compare the pressures on the two sides of each valve

The **bicuspid valve** lies between the atrium and the ventricle, so it is open only while the atrium is above the ventricle. The **semilunar valve** lies between the ventricle and the aorta, so it is open only while the ventricle is above the aorta.

At <InlineMath math="0.2\text{ s}" /> the ventricle is contracting but has not yet beaten the aorta, and at <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ s}" /> it is relaxing but is still above the atrium. In both moments the blood in the ventricle has **no way out**.

So both valves are closed at <InlineMath math="0.2\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ s}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.3\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ s}" /> are the highest pressures, **but that is when the semilunar valve is open** and blood leaves for the aorta.
- <InlineMath math="0.1\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ s}" /> are moments when the atrium is above the ventricle, **so the bicuspid valve is open**.
- <InlineMath math="0.2\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ s}" /> checks only the semilunar valve at <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ s}" /> and forgets that the **bicuspid valve has opened again**.

# Compare mitochondria per unit volume, not per cell

A big cell can hold many mitochondria and still be poorly supplied with them. What shows how much ATP each part of the cell can get is the **number of mitochondria per unit volume**.

<BlockMath math="\text{P}:\ \tfrac{1600}{8000}\times1000=200,\quad \text{Q}:\ \tfrac{300}{5000}\times1000=60,\quad \text{R}:\ \tfrac{900}{1000}\times1000=900,\quad \text{S}:\ \tfrac{150}{500}\times1000=300" />

- Flight muscle contracts for long periods and needs a lot of ATP from aerobic respiration, so it has the **densest mitochondria**: R, with <InlineMath math="900" /> per <InlineMath math="1000\,\mu\text{m}^3" />.
- An adipose cell is mostly a fat droplet that stores energy and does little work, so it has the **sparsest mitochondria**: Q, with <InlineMath math="60" /> per <InlineMath math="1000\,\mu\text{m}^3" />.

So the flight muscle cell is R and the adipose cell is Q.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Flight muscle P, adipose tissue S ranks the cells by the **raw count**, so the biggest cell looks the most active and the smallest looks the least active.
- Flight muscle P, adipose tissue Q uses the density for adipose tissue but the **raw count** for the muscle.
- Flight muscle R, adipose tissue S uses the density for the muscle but picks S for its small count, **although S is five times as packed as Q**.

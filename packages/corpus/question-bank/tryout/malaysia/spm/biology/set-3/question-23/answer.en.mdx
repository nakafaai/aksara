# Decide which leaf is the sun leaf, then count stomata per leaf

Bright light reaches deep into a leaf, so a sun leaf can use a **thicker palisade** with more chloroplasts. In dim light, a leaf does better by spreading wide and packing more chlorophyll into each chloroplast to catch the little light there is.

- Leaf X is twice as thick with two palisade layers, so X is the sun leaf. Statement I is correct.
- Leaf Y is thin and wide, with more chlorophyll per chloroplast, so Y is the shade leaf. Statement II is wrong.

For the stomata, density alone is not the total. Convert each area to square millimetres, using <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}^2=100\text{ mm}^2" />:

<BlockMath math="\text{X}: 300\times(20\times100)=600\,000,\qquad \text{Y}: 150\times(45\times100)=675\,000" />

Leaf Y has **more stomata in total** because its area more than makes up for the lower density. Statement III is correct, and statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and IV only compares stomatal density and stops there, **without multiplying by the leaf area**.
- II and III only reads extra chlorophyll as a sign of strong light, **but packing more chlorophyll helps a leaf in dim light**.
- II and IV only makes both slips, the wrong sun leaf and the **density taken as a total**.

# Read the glucose curve and the insulin curve side by side

In a healthy person, a rise in blood glucose triggers the **beta cells** to release insulin, which brings glucose back down. Comparing the two people shows where the control fails.

- I: P's insulin jumps from <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="45" /> units after the drink, but Q's stays between <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> units. Q's beta cells secrete very little insulin. Statement I is correct.

Statement II is correct.
- III: glucagon raises blood glucose. P's glucose falls while P's insulin is high, so the fall is caused by **insulin**. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: Q's problem is a lack of insulin, not cells that ignore it, so injected insulin would help cells take in glucose and the liver store it as glycogen. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, as if Q's cells could not respond to insulin, **but the data show the insulin itself is missing**.
- I, II and III only credits glucagon for the fall in P, **although glucagon raises blood glucose**.
- II and IV only rejects I, missing that **Q's insulin barely changes** after the drink.

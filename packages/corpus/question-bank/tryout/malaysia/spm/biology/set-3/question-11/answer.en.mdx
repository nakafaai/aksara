# Ask who made the antibodies and what is left on day sixty

Killed bacteria act as an antigen, so group 1 makes its own antibodies and **memory cells**. Serum carries ready-made antibodies from other mice, so group 2 gets antibodies but no memory cells.

- I: group 1 has only <InlineMath math="10" /> units on day <InlineMath math="59" />, yet every mouse survives. Its memory cells recognise bacterium B and start a **secondary response** that is faster and larger than the first one. Statement I is correct.
- II: group 2's antibodies fall from <InlineMath math="60" /> units to <InlineMath math="0" /> before day <InlineMath math="59" />, and only <InlineMath math="10\%" /> survive, close to the <InlineMath math="5\%" /> of the control group. That is passive immunity, and it lasted less than <InlineMath math="60" /> days. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="10" /> units on day <InlineMath math="59" /> is a low level. Survival comes from the memory cells, not from these leftover antibodies. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the antibodies came in the serum. The group 2 mice never met the antigen, so their own lymphocytes made none of them. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, as if serum made the mice produce antibodies, **but serum only delivers them**.
- I, II and III only credits the low antibody level on day <InlineMath math="59" /> and overlooks the **memory cells**.
- II and IV only rejects I, missing that group 1 survives with almost no antibody left, which only a **secondary response** explains.

# Ask which force moves the arm in each phase

A muscle can only **pull** on a bone through its tendon. It never pushes. When the arm moves the other way, either the opposing muscle pulls or the load does the work.

- I: in phase 2 the triceps shows only <InlineMath math="8\%" />, so it is not the triceps that straightens the elbow. The weight of the dumbbell pulls the forearm down. Statement I is correct.
- II: the biceps still shows <InlineMath math="40\%" /> while the elbow straightens. It is contracting as it lengthens, acting as a brake so the dumbbell comes down slowly. Statement II is correct.
- III: the triceps shows <InlineMath math="75\%" /> and straightens the elbow, but it does so by **pulling** on the ulna behind the elbow joint, not by pushing the forearm. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: in phase 1 the triceps shows only <InlineMath math="10\%" />. It relaxes so the biceps can bend the elbow. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts III, **but no muscle can push a bone**.
- I and IV only accepts IV, which would make the triceps fight the biceps, **although its activity is only** <InlineMath math="10\%" />.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and accepts both wrong statements, **as if every movement needed an active muscle on that side**.

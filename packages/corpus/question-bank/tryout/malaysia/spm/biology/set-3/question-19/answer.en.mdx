# Balance the energy over a whole week, not over one day

Fat changes only when **intake and use do not match**. The surplus stays every day, even on walking days, so the walks must first cancel it before any fat is burned.

<BlockMath math="\text{first }37\text{ days}:\quad (10\,200-9600)\times37=600\times37=22\,200\text{ kJ},\qquad \frac{22\,200}{37\,000}=0.6\text{ kg}" />

Now take one whole week of walking:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{surplus}=600\times7=4200\text{ kJ},\qquad \text{walks}=1400\times4=5600\text{ kJ},\qquad \text{net}=4200-5600=-1400\text{ kJ per week}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{weeks}=\frac{22\,200}{1400}\approx15.9\approx16" />
</MathContainer>

So she gains <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ kg}" /> of fat and needs about <InlineMath math="16" /> weeks of walking to lose it.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="4" /> weeks divides by the <InlineMath math="5600\text{ kJ}" /> the walks use and forgets that the **daily surplus continues**.
- About <InlineMath math="7" /> weeks counts the surplus only on the walking days, <InlineMath math="4\times(1400-600)=3200\text{ kJ}" />, and **leaves out the three days without a walk**.
- Never losing it looks only at eating and resting, **but the walks add** <InlineMath math="5600\text{ kJ}" /> of use each week.

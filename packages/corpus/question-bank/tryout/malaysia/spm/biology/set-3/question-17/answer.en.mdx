# Divide the flow by the total area to get the speed

Every second the same <InlineMath math="90\text{ cm}^3" /> of blood passes each level, so where the blood spreads over a larger total area it must **move more slowly**.

<BlockMath math="\text{speed}=\frac{\text{volume per second}}{\text{total area}}:\qquad \text{aorta}: \frac{90}{3}=30\text{ cm/s},\qquad \text{capillaries}: \frac{90}{4500}=0.02\text{ cm/s}=0.2\text{ mm/s}" />

Now convert the speed into a time for a capillary <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" /> long:

<BlockMath math="\text{time}=\frac{1\text{ mm}}{0.2\text{ mm/s}}=5\text{ s}" />

Five seconds is long enough for oxygen, glucose and carbon dioxide to diffuse between the blood and the tissue fluid.

So blood takes <InlineMath math="5" /> seconds, because it moves slowest where the total cross-sectional area is largest.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="50" /> seconds divides <InlineMath math="1" /> by <InlineMath math="0.02" /> without converting **millimetres to centimetres**.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> seconds because of the thin wall has the right time, **but a thin wall speeds up diffusion, not the blood**.
- <InlineMath math="0.2" /> seconds takes the speed in millimetres per second as the time, **without dividing the length by it**.

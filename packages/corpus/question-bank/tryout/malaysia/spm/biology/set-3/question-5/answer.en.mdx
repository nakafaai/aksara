# Read the slope for the rate and the plateau for the substrate

The **slope** of each line is the rate. The level where a line flattens shows how much substrate there was, because each milligram of substrate gives one milligram of product here.

- I: X and Y hold the same <InlineMath math="12\text{ mg}" /> of substrate. Twice the enzyme doubles the rate from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="4\text{ mg}" /> per minute, so the time falls from <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="3" /> minutes. Statement I is correct.
- II: X and Z both rise by <InlineMath math="2\text{ mg}" /> per minute. One unit of enzyme is already working at its limit with <InlineMath math="12\text{ mg}" /> of substrate, so extra substrate does not speed it up. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the plateau equals the substrate, so Z, with <InlineMath math="24\text{ mg}" /> of substrate, will reach <InlineMath math="24\text{ mg}" /> of product at minute <InlineMath math="12" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: Y stops because its substrate is **used up**, at the same <InlineMath math="12\text{ mg}" /> as X. A denatured enzyme would stop below that level. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only reads the higher final amount in Z as a faster start, **but Z's slope matches X's slope**.
- I and IV only blames denaturation for the plateau, **although Y stops exactly when the substrate runs out**.
- III and IV only compares the final amounts of X and Y and misses that Y **gets there twice as fast**.

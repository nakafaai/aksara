# Use the usual lag to spot the year that breaks the pattern

In a **predator and prey** relationship, the predator follows the prey about one step behind, because more food takes time to become more young. A year where the predator does not follow the prey points to another cause.

- I: rats peak at <InlineMath math="280" /> in year <InlineMath math="3" /> and owls at <InlineMath math="14" /> pairs in year <InlineMath math="4" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: from year <InlineMath math="3" /> to year <InlineMath math="5" /> the owls rise to their peak while the rats fall from <InlineMath math="280" /> to <InlineMath math="130" />. More owls eat more rats. Statement II is correct.
- III: rats fall until year <InlineMath math="6" /> and owls follow one year later, falling until year <InlineMath math="7" />. Rats rise again in year <InlineMath math="7" />, so with the usual lag the owls should **rise in year** <InlineMath math="8" />. Instead they fall to <InlineMath math="4" /> pairs. Owls that eat slowly poisoned rats from the neighbouring estate take in the poison themselves, which fits this break. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the poison in year <InlineMath math="6" /> is itself another factor, and food or disease could also act. The data show a link, not a single cause. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only misses the **broken lag in year** <InlineMath math="8" />, when the owls should have followed the rising rats.
- I, II and IV only accepts IV, **although the question itself names rat poison as another factor**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **although the peaks are one year apart**, and accepts the single-cause claim.

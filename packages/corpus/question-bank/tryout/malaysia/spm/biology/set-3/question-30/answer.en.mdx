# Look for the lag between predator and prey

In a **predator and prey** relationship, the predator responds to the prey with a delay, because it takes time for more food to become more young. So the predator line follows the prey line about one step behind.

- I: rats peak at <InlineMath math="280" /> in year <InlineMath math="3" />, and owls peak at <InlineMath math="14" /> pairs in year <InlineMath math="4" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: from year <InlineMath math="3" /> to year <InlineMath math="5" /> the owls rise to their peak while the rats fall from <InlineMath math="280" /> to <InlineMath math="130" />. More owls eat more rats, so the rise helps explain the fall. Statement II is correct.
- III: the data show a link but cannot rule out food, disease or rat poison. Only one other factor needs to exist for "the only factor" to fail. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: rats drop to <InlineMath math="90" /> in year <InlineMath math="6" />, and the owls fall from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="7" /> pairs. With fewer rats, fewer owl chicks can be fed. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, missing that the owls follow the rats **downwards as well as upwards**.
- I, II and III only accepts III, **but a correlation between two populations cannot exclude every other factor**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **although the peaks are one year apart**.

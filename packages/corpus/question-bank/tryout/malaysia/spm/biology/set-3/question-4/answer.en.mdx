# Read each solution from two Benedict's results and the iodine test

A precipitate before the acid means a **reducing sugar** is already there. Extra precipitate after the acid means something was split into reducing sugars by the acid: a non-reducing sugar, or starch.

- P: no precipitate before, but blue-black with iodine. The acid breaks **starch** into glucose, so P holds starch, not a non-reducing sugar.
- Q: no precipitate before, <InlineMath math="48\text{ mg}" /> after, and no starch. Q holds a non-reducing sugar only.
- R: the same <InlineMath math="30\text{ mg}" /> before and after. R holds a reducing sugar only.
- S: <InlineMath math="20\text{ mg}" /> before, so a reducing sugar is present. The extra precipitate after the acid comes from a non-reducing sugar, because S has no starch.

<BlockMath math="56-20=36\text{ mg}" />

So S holds both kinds of sugar, and the non-reducing sugar alone gives <InlineMath math="36\text{ mg}" /> of precipitate.

# Why the other options are wrong

- S, <InlineMath math="56\text{ mg}" /> picks the right solution but forgets to **subtract the reducing sugar** that was already there.
- P, <InlineMath math="40\text{ mg}" /> treats starch as a non-reducing sugar, **but the blue-black iodine result marks starch**.
- Q, <InlineMath math="48\text{ mg}" /> has a non-reducing sugar only, **because it gives no precipitate before the acid**.

- I: the lower side of the root is inhibited by <InlineMath math="80\%" />, so it grows less than the upper side and the root curves **downwards**. Statement I is correct.
- II: the lower side of the shoot grows <InlineMath math="80\%" /> faster, so it lengthens more and the shoot curves **upwards**. Statement II is correct.
- III: root growth is fastest at <InlineMath math="10^{-10}" />, where the shoot shows no change, and shoot growth is fastest at <InlineMath math="10^{-5}" />, where the root is almost stopped. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the upper side of the root is inhibited less, by <InlineMath math="30\%" /> instead of <InlineMath math="80\%" />, so it grows faster than the lower side. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV because both sides of the root are inhibited, **but the upper side is inhibited less and so grows faster**.
- I, II and III only assumes one best concentration for both organs, **although the root and the shoot peak at very different concentrations**.
- II and IV only rejects I, **which follows straight from the upper side growing faster**.

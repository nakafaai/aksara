# Follow each stream in its own direction

Blood picks up oxygen as it moves, so its saturation **rises in the direction it flows**. In the chart the blood is richest at point <InlineMath math="1" /> and poorest at point <InlineMath math="5" />, so it enters at point <InlineMath math="5" /> and leaves at point <InlineMath math="1" />.

- I: water enters at point <InlineMath math="1" /> and blood enters at point <InlineMath math="5" />. They flow in **opposite directions**, a countercurrent. Statement I is correct.
- II: blood leaves at point <InlineMath math="1" /> with <InlineMath math="90\%" />, and water leaves at point <InlineMath math="5" /> with <InlineMath math="20\%" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: in a parallel flow, the fresh water meets the poorest blood at the start, and the two quickly reach the same saturation. After that no gradient is left, so the blood leaves somewhere between <InlineMath math="10\%" /> and <InlineMath math="100\%" />, about halfway, and far below <InlineMath math="90\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: at point <InlineMath math="5" />, water at <InlineMath math="20\%" /> still sits next to blood at <InlineMath math="10\%" />, so a **gradient of** <InlineMath math="10" /> percentage points still drives diffusion. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, missing that a parallel flow **loses its gradient** halfway along the plate.
- I, II and IV only accepts IV, judging the water alone instead of **comparing it with the blood beside it**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and accepts IV, reading the two falling lines as two streams moving the **same way**.

# Follow the corpus luteum through both cycles

After ovulation, the empty follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, which secretes progesterone to keep the endometrium thick. What happens to the corpus luteum decides whether progesterone falls or keeps rising.

- I: an LH surge triggers ovulation about a day later, so ovulation happened at about day <InlineMath math="14" />. Progesterone starts rising only after day <InlineMath math="14" /> in both cycles, which fits a new corpus luteum. Statement I is correct.
- II: in cycle A no embryo forms, so the corpus luteum breaks down after about ten days and its progesterone falls. FSH stays low while progesterone is high, so a rise in FSH cannot be the cause. Statement II is wrong.
- III: in cycle B, an embryo has implanted and secretes **hCG**. hCG keeps the corpus luteum alive, progesterone keeps rising, and the endometrium stays in place. Statement III is correct.
- IV: high progesterone inhibits FSH and LH, so it **prevents** ovulation instead of causing it. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only blames FSH for the fall in cycle A, **but the fall comes from the corpus luteum breaking down**.
- I, III and IV only accepts a second ovulation, **although high progesterone blocks the LH surge**.
- III and IV only rejects the ovulation that the LH surge on day <InlineMath math="13" /> clearly marks.

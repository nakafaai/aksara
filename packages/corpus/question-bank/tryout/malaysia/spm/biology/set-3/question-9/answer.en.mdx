# Ask what the colour change actually measures

The pink colour lasts while any sodium carbonate is left. It disappears only when enough **fatty acid** has been released to neutralise all of it. So the time measures how long it takes to release a fixed amount of acid, and that amount depends on how much sodium carbonate there is.

- I: lipase splits fat into fatty acids and glycerol, and the acids neutralise the sodium carbonate before the indicator turns colourless. Statement I is correct.
- II: W and X differ only in bile, and W is <InlineMath math="3" /> times as fast. Bile emulsifies fat into small droplets, giving lipase more surface. Statement II is correct.
- III: U has twice the sodium carbonate, so it needs twice the fatty acid. It takes twice as long as W, which means fatty acid is released at **the same rate**. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: in tube Z the lipase was boiled, not the bile. Z shows that boiled lipase is denatured. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only reads U's longer time as a slower enzyme, **but U simply needs twice as much acid**.
- I, II and IV only misreads tube Z, **where the boiled substance is lipase**.
- II and III only rejects what the colour change measures and so misjudges tube U.

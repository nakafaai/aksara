# Change one thing at a time and compare the tubes

Each pair of tubes differs in **one added substance**, so each comparison tests one idea. A shorter time means a faster reaction, so the rate is compared as <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{\text{time}}" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{\text{rate in W}}{\text{rate in X}}=\frac{1/4}{1/12}=\frac{12}{4}=3" />

- I: lipase hydrolyses fat into **fatty acids** and glycerol. The acids neutralise the sodium carbonate, so the indicator turns colourless. Statement I is correct.
- II: W and X differ only in bile, and W is <InlineMath math="3" /> times as fast. Bile emulsifies the fat into small droplets, which gives lipase more surface to act on. Statement II is correct.
- III: tube Y holds bile without lipase and stays pink, so bile alone releases no fatty acids. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: in tube Z the lipase was boiled, not the bile. Z shows that boiled lipase is denatured. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only misreads tube Z, **where the boiled substance is lipase**.
- I, II and III only accepts III, treating emulsification as digestion, **but tube Y stays pink**.
- II and IV only rejects the reason for the colour change and accepts the misreading of tube Z.

# Follow the label from each source to the sinks

The radioactive carbon is fixed into sugar in the leaf, the **source**. Wherever the radioactivity turns up later is a **sink** that received that sugar.

- I: the upper leaf sends <InlineMath math="70\%" /> upwards to the shoot tip, and the lower leaf sends <InlineMath math="80\%" /> downwards to the roots. Sugar moves both ways along the stem. Statement I is correct.
- II: with equal exports, the fruits get <InlineMath math="25\%" /> of one half and <InlineMath math="15\%" /> of the other, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{25+15}{200}=\tfrac{40}{200}=20\%" /> of the total. Each leaf mostly feeds the sinks nearest to it, so the fruits in between get only a modest share. Statement II is correct.
- III: xylem carries water only upwards, from the roots to the leaves. Sugar reaching the roots must travel down in the **phloem**. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: a ring of bark removes the phloem but leaves the xylem. Below the lower leaf, the ring cuts the phloem path to the roots, so most of the radioactivity can no longer reach them. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, missing that the bark carries the **phloem** that takes sugar down.
- I, II and III only accepts transport in the xylem, **although sugar reaches the roots, against the direction of xylem flow**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **although the two leaves send their sugar in opposite directions**.

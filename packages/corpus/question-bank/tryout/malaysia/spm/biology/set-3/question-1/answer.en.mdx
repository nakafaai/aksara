# Leave out the anomalous reading before you average

Readings repeated under the same conditions should agree closely. At pH <InlineMath math="7" />, <InlineMath math="31\text{ cm}^3" /> is far from <InlineMath math="20" /> and <InlineMath math="21\text{ cm}^3" />, so it is the **anomalous reading** and stays out of the mean.

- I: the student changes the pH and measures the oxygen, so the volume of oxygen is the **responding variable**. Statement I is correct.
- II: the temperature stays at <InlineMath math="30^\circ\text{C}" /> for every pH, so it is a fixed variable. Statement II is wrong.
- III: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{20.5}{12}\approx1.7" />, so statement III is correct. Keeping the anomaly gives <InlineMath math="\tfrac{24}{12}=2.0" />, which is the trap.
- IV: <InlineMath math="9\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen is still collected at pH <InlineMath math="9" />, so catalase there is less active, not fully denatured. Statement IV is wrong.

So only statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and IV only keeps <InlineMath math="31\text{ cm}^3" /> in the mean, so the ratio becomes <InlineMath math="2.0" />, and then reads a lower volume as a **dead enzyme**.
- I, II and III only treats the temperature as manipulated, **but it is held at** <InlineMath math="30^\circ\text{C}" /> throughout.
- I, III and IV only reads the small volume at pH <InlineMath math="9" /> as complete denaturation, **although oxygen is still released**.

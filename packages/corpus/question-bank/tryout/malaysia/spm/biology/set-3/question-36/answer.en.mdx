# Read the goats and their food together

A population can grow only as long as its food keeps up. Here the goats **eat their own food supply**, so the plant cover sets how many goats the island can carry, and the goats change that number.

- I: the goats climb to <InlineMath math="280" /> while plant cover falls from <InlineMath math="90\%" /> to <InlineMath math="20\%" />. With so little food left, the population crashes. The island then supports only about <InlineMath math="155" /> goats, far below the peak, because the grazing damaged the plants. Statement I is correct.
- II: the largest yearly gain is <InlineMath math="200-120=80" /> goats, between years <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="4" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: from year <InlineMath math="9" /> the numbers hold between <InlineMath math="150" /> and <InlineMath math="160" />, a new **carrying capacity**. Plant cover recovers only to about <InlineMath math="21\%" />, so the island feeds fewer goats than at the start. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the island has no predators. The fall follows the loss of plant cover, so food shortage is the cause. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, often after picking the largest population instead of the **largest yearly gain**.
- II, III and IV only blames predators, **although the island has none**, and rejects the overshoot.
- I, II and IV only accepts predators and rejects the lower carrying capacity that the steady numbers show.

# Read the zones in space as stages in time

On a shore that is building outwards, the zones from the sea inland show the **stages of succession** that each spot passes through over time. The seaward zone is the youngest stage, and the landward zone is the oldest.

- I: *Avicennia* and *Sonneratia* are the pioneers. Their dense pneumatophores slow the water so mud settles, which raises the soil from <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}" /> towards the <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ m}" /> where *Rhizophora* grows. Statement I is correct.
- II: as mud builds up, pioneers colonise the new mud further out, and each zone follows behind them. The whole pattern creeps **towards the sea**. Statement II is correct.
- III: *Bruguiera* grows in zone <InlineMath math="3" />, the highest soil, flooded only <InlineMath math="8" /> times a month. It is the landward species, not the one that copes with the most flooding. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: each zone raises and firms the soil, making it less flooded and less salty. Those new conditions suit later species, which then outcompete the trees that changed the soil. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, missing that the pioneers **prepare the ground** for the species that replace them.
- I, II and III only accepts III, **but the table puts Bruguiera inland, on soil that is rarely flooded**.
- II, III and IV only rejects the **mud-trapping role** of the pioneer roots and accepts the misplaced Bruguiera.

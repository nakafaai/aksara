# Count only the oxygen above the resting level after the run

After the run, part of the uptake would happen anyway at rest. Only the **extra uptake above** <InlineMath math="0.3\text{ L}" /> per minute repays the debt.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\text{debt}&=(2.1-0.3)+(1.5-0.3)+(1.0-0.3)+(0.7-0.3)+(0.5-0.3)+(0.3-0.3)\\&=1.8+1.2+0.7+0.4+0.2+0=4.3\text{ L}\end{aligned}" />

During the run, the muscles got less oxygen than the <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ L}" /> per minute they needed, so part of their glucose was respired anaerobically into **lactic acid**. After the run, the extra oxygen lets the liver oxidise the lactic acid to carbon dioxide and water or turn it back into glucose.

So she takes in <InlineMath math="4.3\text{ L}" /> of extra oxygen, mainly to remove the lactic acid made during the run.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.1\text{ L}" /> adds the whole uptake of minutes <InlineMath math="5" /> to <InlineMath math="10" /> and forgets that **resting uptake is not part of the debt**.
- <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ L}" /> is the shortfall during the run, <InlineMath math="1.2+0.6+0.3+0.3" />, **not the extra oxygen taken in afterwards**.
- <InlineMath math="4.3\text{ L}" /> to replace exhaled oxygen has the right volume, but the debt pays for **removing lactic acid**, not for breathing out carbon dioxide.

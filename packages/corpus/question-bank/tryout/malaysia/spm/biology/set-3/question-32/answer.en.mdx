# List the gametes of each parent, then fill a grid

Because the genes are on different chromosomes, they **assort independently**. <InlineMath math="RrYy" /> makes four kinds of gamete in equal numbers, <InlineMath math="RY" />, <InlineMath math="Ry" />, <InlineMath math="rY" /> and <InlineMath math="ry" />, while <InlineMath math="Rryy" /> makes only two, <InlineMath math="Ry" /> and <InlineMath math="ry" />.

| Gametes | <InlineMath math="Ry" /> | <InlineMath math="ry" /> |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="RY" /> | <InlineMath math="RRYy" /> round yellow | <InlineMath math="RrYy" /> round yellow |
| <InlineMath math="Ry" /> | <InlineMath math="RRyy" /> round green | <InlineMath math="Rryy" /> round green |
| <InlineMath math="rY" /> | <InlineMath math="RrYy" /> round yellow | <InlineMath math="rrYy" /> wrinkled yellow |
| <InlineMath math="ry" /> | <InlineMath math="Rryy" /> round green | <InlineMath math="rryy" /> wrinkled green |

The grid has <InlineMath math="4\times2=8" /> equally likely boxes. Three are round yellow: one <InlineMath math="RRYy" /> and two <InlineMath math="RrYy" />.

Among the <InlineMath math="150" /> round yellow seeds, only the <InlineMath math="RRYy" /> seeds are **homozygous for shape**, which is one box in three.

So <InlineMath math="50" /> of the round yellow seeds are expected to be homozygous for seed shape.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="150" /> is every round yellow seed, **including the heterozygous** <InlineMath math="RrYy" /> ones.
- <InlineMath math="100" /> counts every <InlineMath math="RR" /> seed, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}\times400" />, **without keeping only the yellow ones**.
- <InlineMath math="25" /> uses <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{16}" /> from a cross of two <InlineMath math="RrYy" /> plants, **but one parent here is** <InlineMath math="Rryy" />.

# Work out whether W is a carrier before looking at her child

A boy gets his only X chromosome from his **mother**. W's brother has haemophilia, so their mother passed him <InlineMath math="X^h" /> and must be a carrier, <InlineMath math="X^HX^h" />. Their father is <InlineMath math="X^HY" />.

  title={<>What Is Known About the Family</>}

/>

W got <InlineMath math="X^H" /> from her father and one of her mother's two X chromosomes, so:

<BlockMath math="P(\text{W is } X^HX^h)=\tfrac{1}{2}" />

If W is a carrier, her child with an <InlineMath math="X^HY" /> husband falls into one of four equally likely boxes:

| Gametes | <InlineMath math="X^H" /> from the husband | <InlineMath math="Y" /> from the husband |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="X^H" /> from W | <InlineMath math="X^HX^H" /> girl, normal | <InlineMath math="X^HY" /> boy, normal |
| <InlineMath math="X^h" /> from W | <InlineMath math="X^HX^h" /> girl, carrier | <InlineMath math="X^hY" /> boy, haemophilia |

Only one box in four is a boy with haemophilia, so:

<BlockMath math="P(\text{boy with haemophilia})=\tfrac{1}{2}\times\tfrac{1}{4}=\tfrac{1}{8}" />

So the probability that their first child is a boy with haemophilia is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{8}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" /> treats W as a certain carrier and skips the **one-half chance** that she is not.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" /> is the chance that a son of a carrier is affected, **but the child's sex is not known**, and W may not be a carrier.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{16}" /> halves the answer once more for the child being a boy, **although the grid already counts the sex**.

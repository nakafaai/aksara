# Ask which way heat flows at each air temperature

Heat flows from hotter to cooler. While the air is cooler than the skin, the skin loses heat by radiation. Once the air is **hotter than the skin**, radiation brings heat in, and only the evaporation of sweat can remove it.

- I: at <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" /> the metabolic rate rises to <InlineMath math="130\text{ W}" /> while the core stays at <InlineMath math="37.0^\circ\text{C}" />. Shivering, the rapid contraction of skeletal muscles, releases this extra heat. Statement I is correct.
- II: skin blood flow triples from <InlineMath math="10" /> to <InlineMath math="30" /> units. **Vasodilation** brings warm blood close to the surface, and the air at <InlineMath math="35^\circ\text{C}" /> or less is not hotter than the skin, so radiation still removes heat. Statement II is correct.
- III: at <InlineMath math="40^\circ\text{C}" /> the air is hotter than the skin at <InlineMath math="36^\circ\text{C}" />, so radiation now adds heat. Sweat rate doubles to <InlineMath math="600\text{ g}" /> per hour because evaporation is the only route left. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the skin temperature changes from <InlineMath math="28" /> to <InlineMath math="36^\circ\text{C}" />. It is the core that the hypothalamus keeps constant, by changing skin blood flow, sweating and shivering. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, missing that air **hotter than the skin** reverses radiation.
- I, II and IV only accepts IV, **although the skin temperature varies by** <InlineMath math="8^\circ\text{C}" />.
- II, III and IV only rejects shivering as a source of heat and accepts the constant skin.

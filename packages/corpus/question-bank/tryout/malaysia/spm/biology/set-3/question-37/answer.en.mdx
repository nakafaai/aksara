# Match the label in the oxygen released to its source

A **heavy isotope** acts as a tag. Whichever source the released oxygen matches in its share of <InlineMath math="^{18}\text{O}" /> is where that oxygen came from.

- I: in experiment <InlineMath math="1" /> the oxygen released matches the water at <InlineMath math="0.85\%" />, and in experiment <InlineMath math="2" /> it matches the water at <InlineMath math="0.20\%" />, while the hydrogencarbonate is different both times. Statement I is correct.
- II: the oxygen comes from water, and in the light reaction chlorophyll absorbs light energy that splits water into hydrogen and oxygen. This **photolysis of water** releases the oxygen gas. Statement II is correct.
- III: in experiment <InlineMath math="3" /> the water and the hydrogencarbonate hold the same share, so the oxygen released would match either source. It cannot tell them apart. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: in experiment <InlineMath math="2" /> the oxygen released holds <InlineMath math="0.20\%" />, the share in the water, not the <InlineMath math="0.68\%" /> in the hydrogencarbonate. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts III, **but when both sources carry the same label, the match tells nothing**.
- II and IV only reads experiment <InlineMath math="2" /> backwards, **although its oxygen matches the water**.
- I and IV only accepts a conclusion and then contradicts it in experiment <InlineMath math="2" />.

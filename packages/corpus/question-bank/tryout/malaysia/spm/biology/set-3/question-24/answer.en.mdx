# Turn every time into a rate, then look for the factor that holds the rate back

A shorter time to float means **faster photosynthesis**, so compare rates, using rate <InlineMath math="=\tfrac{100}{\text{time}}" />. A factor is limiting when raising it raises the rate.

<BlockMath math="\text{low light}: \tfrac{100}{20}=5,\qquad \text{high light}: \tfrac{100}{10}=10\ \text{at }0.2\%,\quad \tfrac{100}{5}=20\ \text{at }0.5\%\text{ and }1.0\%" />

- I: with no hydrogencarbonate, no carbon dioxide reaches the chloroplasts, photosynthesis stops, and no oxygen collects to lift the discs. Statement I is correct.
- II: in low light, the rate stays at <InlineMath math="5" /> whether the solution is <InlineMath math="0.2\%" />, <InlineMath math="0.5\%" /> or <InlineMath math="1.0\%" />. Extra carbon dioxide does nothing, so **light is the limiting factor**. Statement II is correct.
- III: at <InlineMath math="0.2\%" /> the low-light rate is <InlineMath math="5" /> and the high-light rate is <InlineMath math="10" />, so low light gives **half**, not twice. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: in high light, going from <InlineMath math="0.2\%" /> to <InlineMath math="0.5\%" /> doubles the rate from <InlineMath math="10" /> to <InlineMath math="20" />, so carbon dioxide limits it at <InlineMath math="0.2\%" />. Going on to <InlineMath math="1.0\%" /> changes nothing, so carbon dioxide no longer limits it at <InlineMath math="0.5\%" />. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, missing that the limiting factor **changes from carbon dioxide to something else** between <InlineMath math="0.2\%" /> and <InlineMath math="0.5\%" />.
- I, II and III only compares the times directly, so the longer <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes looks like twice as much photosynthesis, **when it means half the rate**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and accepts the inverted comparison of times.

# Count shared taxa from the top of the hierarchy down

The more taxa two organisms share, the more **closely related** they are. Start from the kingdom and stop at the first taxon where they differ.

- I: the tiger and the leopard share the kingdom, phylum, class, order Carnivora, family Felidae and genus *Panthera*, and differ only in species. Statement I is correct.
- II: the cat and the tiger share the kingdom, phylum, class, order and family, which is five taxa, and split at the genus. Statement II is correct.
- III: in binomial naming the genus starts with a capital letter, the species name is all **lower case**, and the whole name is printed in italics: *Panthera tigris*. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the table puts the tapir in Perissodactyla and the mousedeer in Artiodactyla. Having hooves does not decide the order. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only groups animals by one visible feature, **but the table places the tapir and the mousedeer in different orders**.
- II and III only rejects I and accepts a capital letter for the species name, **which binomial naming never uses**.
- I and III only accepts the wrong spelling and misses the **five taxa** shared by the cat and the tiger.

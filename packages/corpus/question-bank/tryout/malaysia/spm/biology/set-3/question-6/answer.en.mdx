# Use the steady rise to find the length of the cycle

Without spindle fibres, a cell that reaches metaphase **cannot leave it**. So the rise in the percentage measures how fast cells arrive at mitosis.

<BlockMath math="\text{rate of arrival}=\frac{44-4}{8}=5\%\text{ per hour},\qquad \text{cycle}=\frac{100\%}{5\%\text{ per hour}}=20\text{ hours}" />

Every cell passes through mitosis once in each cycle, so all <InlineMath math="100\%" /> of the cells arrive within one cycle.

- I: one cycle lasts <InlineMath math="20" /> hours. Statement I is correct.
- II: spindle fibres pull the **sister chromatids** apart in anaphase. Without them, the cells stop at metaphase. Statement II is correct.
- III: the DNA was copied in phase S, so each of the <InlineMath math="46" /> chromosomes has two chromatids, giving <InlineMath math="92" /> chromatids. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the cells still reach metaphase with copied chromosomes, so replication was not stopped. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- II and III only rejects I, often after dividing <InlineMath math="8" /> hours by <InlineMath math="0.44" />, **which counts the cells already in metaphase at hour** <InlineMath math="0" />.
- I and II only rejects III by counting the chromatids as chromosomes, **but chromatids stay joined until anaphase**.
- II, III and IV only accepts IV, **although the cells reach metaphase with copied DNA**.

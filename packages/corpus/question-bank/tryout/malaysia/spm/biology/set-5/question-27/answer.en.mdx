# Read each drop as a share of what was left, then ask why the leaf sheds water

The intact leaf goes from <InlineMath math="4" /> to <InlineMath math="1" />, then <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="0.25" />: each <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes it **keeps a quarter**, so it loses three quarters. A drip tip gives water a point to gather and run off, so the leaf dries far faster.

- I: each <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes the intact leaf keeps a quarter of its water and loses **three quarters**, not one quarter. Statement I is wrong.
- II: at <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes the cut leaf holds <InlineMath math="2.2" /> against <InlineMath math="0.25" />, about <InlineMath math="9" /> times as much. Statement II is correct.
- III: drip tips shed water quickly. They suit the constant rain of a rainforest, not dry places where a leaf must keep water. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: a film of water lets fungi, mosses and algae settle and grow on the leaf, blocking light and spreading disease. Shedding water fast limits this. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only accepts I, **reading the quarter that stays as the quarter that is lost**.
- II, III and IV only accepts III, **turning a rain-shedding adaptation into one for dry places**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and accepts both wrong statements about **the rate and the habitat**.

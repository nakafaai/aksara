# Separate the water from the solute

Urine flow times urine concentration gives the **solute excreted per minute**. If that product stays fixed while flow rises, the kidneys are losing extra water, not extra solute.

- I: with less ADH, the walls of the collecting ducts let less water back into the blood, so more stays in the urine. Statement I is correct.
- II: of <InlineMath math="125\text{ mL}" /> filtered each minute, <InlineMath math="124\text{ mL}" /> is reabsorbed before drinking and <InlineMath math="117\text{ mL}" /> at <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes, about <InlineMath math="99\%" /> and <InlineMath math="94\%" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: flow times concentration stays near <InlineMath math="800" /> at every time, so the same solute leaves in more water. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the body is losing water faster than solute, so the blood becomes **more concentrated** and its osmotic pressure rises. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, often after dividing the urine flow by <InlineMath math="125" /> and calling that **the share reabsorbed**.
- II and IV only rejects the ADH mechanism and the **constant solute output**.
- I, II and IV only lets the blood become more dilute, **although it is losing water**.

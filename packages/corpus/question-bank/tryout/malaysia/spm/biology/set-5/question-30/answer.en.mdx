# Compare production with production, not with the mass present

A small standing crop can feed a large one if it **reproduces fast**. Phytoplankton are eaten almost as quickly as they grow, so little is present at any moment, yet over a year they produce far more than the zooplankton.

<BlockMath math="\text{transfer}: \frac{73}{730}=10\%" />

- I: zooplankton outweigh phytoplankton at any one time, so the biomass pyramid is inverted. Energy flowing to each level per year can never exceed the energy reaching the level below. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="4" /> g of phytoplankton add <InlineMath math="2" /> g a day, so the whole standing crop is replaced every <InlineMath math="2" /> days. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="3\%" /> comes from dividing the zooplankton present, <InlineMath math="21" /> g, by the phytoplankton production, <InlineMath math="730" /> g. Production should be compared with production, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{73}{730}=10\%" />. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: phytoplankton capture enough energy to produce <InlineMath math="730" /> g a year, ten times the zooplankton's <InlineMath math="73" /> g. The zooplankton receive less, not more. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts <InlineMath math="3\%" />, which divides **mass present by production** and mixes two different measures.
- II and IV only rejects I and **lets the energy pyramid invert too**.
- I, III and IV only rejects the **fast turnover** that explains the whole pattern.

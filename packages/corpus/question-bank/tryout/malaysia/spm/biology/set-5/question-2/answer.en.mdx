# Turn every image length into a real size, then compare it with the limit

The scale bar converts image lengths to real ones. Whether a light microscope shows a detail depends on its **resolution**, the smallest gap it can separate, not on how much it magnifies.

<BlockMath math="\text{magnification}=\frac{20\text{ mm}}{1\text{ µm}}=\frac{20\,000\text{ µm}}{1\text{ µm}}=20\,000" />

- I: <InlineMath math="36\text{ mm}" /> on the image is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{36}{20}=1.8\text{ µm}" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="20\text{ mm}" /> is <InlineMath math="20\,000\text{ µm}" />, so the magnification is <InlineMath math="20\,000" />, not <InlineMath math="2000" />. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the cristae are only <InlineMath math="0.03\text{ µm}" /> apart, well below the <InlineMath math="0.2\text{ µm}" /> limit. The mitochondrion shows as a dot, but its inside cannot be resolved. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: a ribosome is <InlineMath math="0.025\text{ µm}" /> across. More magnification only enlarges the blur, because detail closer than the limit is lost. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only accepts <InlineMath math="2000" />, from treating <InlineMath math="20\text{ mm}" /> as **<InlineMath math="2000\text{ µm}" /> instead of <InlineMath math="20" /> <InlineMath math="000\text{ µm}" />**.
- II and III only rejects both correct statements and **mixes up the size of the mitochondrion with the gap between its cristae**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III, **although the cristae are far closer than <InlineMath math="0.2\text{ µm}" />**.

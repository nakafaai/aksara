# Build oxygen use from three factors and multiply them

Oxygen used per minute is the blood pumped per minute times the oxygen **taken out of each litre**. Blood pumped per minute is heart rate times stroke volume, and heart rate comes from the gap between beats on the ECG.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{rest}: \frac{20}{25}=0.8\text{ s per beat}\Rightarrow\frac{60}{0.8}=75\text{ beats/min},\qquad \text{exercise}: \frac{12}{25}=0.48\text{ s}\Rightarrow125\text{ beats/min}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{rest}: 75\times0.070=5.25\text{ L/min},\ \ 5.25\times(200-150)\approx263\text{ mL/min}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{exercise}: 125\times0.100=12.5\text{ L/min},\ \ 12.5\times(200-80)=1500\text{ mL/min}" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{1500}{263}\approx5.7" />
</MathContainer>

Each factor rises a little, but because they multiply, oxygen use rises about <InlineMath math="5.7" /> times.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="1.7" /> times counts only the **faster heart rate**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{125}{75}" />.
- About <InlineMath math="2.4" /> times counts only the **rise in blood pumped**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12.5}{5.25}" />, or only the extra oxygen taken from each litre, and misses the other factor.
- About <InlineMath math="4.0" /> times multiplies heart rate by oxygen taken per litre, <InlineMath math="1.67\times2.4" />, and **leaves out the larger stroke volume**.

# Time the first arrival, then trace the route through the phloem

The leaf turns the radioactive carbon dioxide into sugar, which enters the sieve tubes and is **carried to the stylet in the phloem**. The first radioactivity marks the fastest sugar, so the speed comes from the first arrival time.

- I: radioactivity first appears after <InlineMath math="40" /> minutes over <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}" />, so the sugar moves at about <InlineMath math="75\text{ cm}" /> per hour. Statement I is correct.
- II: sap flows out on its own for hours. The sieve tube contents must be under positive pressure, unlike xylem, which is under tension. Statement II is correct.
- III: the label was fed to a leaf <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}" /> **above** the stylet as carbon dioxide, so it travelled down from the leaf as sugar in the phloem. Xylem carries water and ions up from the roots, and the stylet sits in a sieve tube. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: each aphid drains sugar from the sieve tubes it taps. Many aphids between the leaf and the roots take sugar out before it gets there. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **although aphids drain the same sugar on its way down**.
- II, III and IV only rejects the speed and sends the label **up the xylem from the roots**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II, **although sap flows out without the aphid sucking**.

# Scale the flow by the fourth power, not by the area

Area grows with the **square** of the diameter, but flow grows with the **fourth power**, because water near the wall is slowed by friction and a wide vessel has more fast-moving water in its centre.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{80}{20}=4:\quad \text{area}\times4^2=16,\qquad \text{flow}\times4^4=256" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{40}{20}=2:\quad \text{flow}\times2^4=16" />
</MathContainer>

- I: the <InlineMath math="80\text{ µm}" /> vessel has <InlineMath math="16" /> times the area, but <InlineMath math="256" /> times the flow, so it matches <InlineMath math="256" /> narrow vessels, not <InlineMath math="16" />. Statement I is wrong.
- II: one blocked <InlineMath math="80\text{ µm}" /> vessel loses <InlineMath math="256" /> units of flow, the same as <InlineMath math="256" /> blocked <InlineMath math="20\text{ µm}" /> vessels. Wide vessels carry most of the water, so each bubble in one costs a lot. Statement II is correct.
- III: water in the xylem is pulled up under tension. Rings and spirals of lignin hold the walls apart, as hoops hold a hose open. Statement III is correct.
- IV: doubling the width multiplies the flow by <InlineMath math="2^4=16" />, not by <InlineMath math="2" />. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts I, **scaling flow by area**, which gives <InlineMath math="16" /> instead of <InlineMath math="256" />.
- III and IV only rejects II and doubles the flow for **double the width**.
- I and II only accepts I and rejects III, **the lignin that keeps vessels open under tension**.

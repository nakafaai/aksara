# Treat the muscle as a mixture of two fibre types

Each fibre type keeps its own share of force, so the whole muscle keeps a **weighted average** of <InlineMath math="95\%" /> and <InlineMath math="20\%" />, weighted by how many fibres of each type it has. Let <InlineMath math="s" /> be the share of slow fibres.

<BlockMath math="0.95s+0.20(1-s)=0.65\ \Rightarrow\ 0.75s=0.45\ \Rightarrow\ s=0.60" />

Z's sample is <InlineMath math="60\%" /> slow fibres. The fatigue resistance comes from the slow fibres' mitochondria, myoglobin and capillaries, which keep **aerobic respiration** going, while fast fibres run down as lactic acid builds up.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="65\%" /> takes the force kept as the share of slow fibres, forgetting that the **fast fibres still keep <InlineMath math="20\%" />**.
- <InlineMath math="68\%" /> divides <InlineMath math="65" /> by <InlineMath math="95" />, as if the fast fibres **gave no force at all**.
- <InlineMath math="40\%" /> is the share of **fast fibres**, the right working with the two types swapped.

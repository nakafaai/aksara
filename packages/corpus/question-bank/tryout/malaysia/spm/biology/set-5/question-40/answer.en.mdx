- I: soil deepens by <InlineMath math="0.12\text{ cm}" /> a year from year <InlineMath math="50" /> to <InlineMath math="100" /> and <InlineMath math="0.22\text{ cm}" /> a year from year <InlineMath math="100" /> to <InlineMath math="200" />, so it is faster later. Statement I is wrong.
- II: lichens grow on bare rock, release acids that weather it and add their dead remains, starting the first thin soil that mosses need. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="25" /> species are added in <InlineMath math="50" /> years from year <InlineMath math="50" /> to <InlineMath math="100" />, <InlineMath math="0.5" /> a year, faster than in any other interval. Statement III is correct.
- IV: each stage deepens the soil and changes light and moisture, so new species that compete better take over, until a stable forest forms. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II, III and IV are correct.

- I and II only accepts I, **comparing totals of <InlineMath math="6" /> and <InlineMath math="22\text{ cm}" /> without dividing by the years**, and then rejects III.
- I, III and IV only accepts I and rejects II, **the pioneer role of the lichens**.
- II and IV only rejects III, **although <InlineMath math="0.5" /> species a year is the fastest rate**.

# Check each pollen grain's allele against both alleles of the style

The test is made **pollen grain by pollen grain**. A grain is stopped if its single allele matches either of the two alleles in the style. Each plant makes its two kinds of pollen in equal numbers.

- I: none of the <InlineMath math="S_1S_2" /> pollen passes, half of the <InlineMath math="S_1S_3" /> pollen and half of the <InlineMath math="S_2S_3" /> pollen pass, because only their <InlineMath math="S_3" /> grains match neither allele. On average one grain in three gets through. Statement I is correct.
- II: an <InlineMath math="S_1S_3" /> plant makes <InlineMath math="S_1" /> and <InlineMath math="S_3" /> pollen in equal numbers. The <InlineMath math="S_1" /> tubes stop, because <InlineMath math="S_1" /> matches the style, and the <InlineMath math="S_3" /> tubes grow on. Statement II is correct.
- III: every pollen grain from the plant itself carries <InlineMath math="S_1" /> or <InlineMath math="S_2" />, and both match the style, so no tube reaches an ovule and **no seeds form**. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: blocking self-fertilisation forces cross-fertilisation, so each seed combines alleles from two different plants. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- II and IV only rejects I, often after **counting all three pollen sources as half compatible**, which gives one half.
- I, II and III only gives the self-pollinated plant seeds, **although every one of its own tubes is stopped**.
- III and IV only rejects the one-third share and the half of the <InlineMath math="S_1S_3" /> pollen **that gets through**.

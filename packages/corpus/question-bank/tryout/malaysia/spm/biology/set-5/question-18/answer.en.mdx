# Weigh the ATP cost of losing oxygen, then see how rice avoids it

Without oxygen, a root cell gets only <InlineMath math="2" /> ATP from each glucose, so it burns through its sugar and builds up **ethanol**, which is toxic. A root that can bring oxygen down from the leaves avoids most of this.

- I: each glucose gives <InlineMath math="30" /> ATP with oxygen and <InlineMath math="2" /> without, so for the same ATP the anaerobic cell needs <InlineMath math="15" /> glucose for every one used aerobically. Statement I is correct.
- II: rice roots are <InlineMath math="30\%" /> air space, ten times wheat's <InlineMath math="3\%" />. Oxygen diffuses down these spaces from the shoot, so less anaerobic respiration takes place and ethanol stays at <InlineMath math="4" /> instead of <InlineMath math="40" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: the ethanol pathway also releases carbon dioxide, which escapes, so plants cannot rebuild glucose from ethanol as the liver does from lactic acid. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: plant cells respire anaerobically to ethanol and carbon dioxide, not to lactic acid. Rice survives because of its air spaces. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only treats ethanol like lactic acid, **although no oxygen debt is repaid in plants**.
- II and IV only rejects the ATP calculation and gives rice **an animal's lactic acid pathway**.
- I and IV only rejects the **air spaces** that actually explain rice's survival.

# Treat the two genes separately, then multiply

The genes are on different chromosomes, so they are inherited **independently**. Work out the chance for each trait among all children, then multiply.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="X^GX^g\times X^GY:\quad X^GX^G,\ X^GX^g,\ X^GY,\ X^gY\ \Rightarrow\ P(\text{son with G6PD deficiency})=\tfrac{1}{4}" />
<BlockMath math="Tt\times Tt:\quad TT,\ Tt,\ Tt,\ tt\ \Rightarrow\ P(\text{no thalassaemia major})=\tfrac{3}{4}" />
<BlockMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}\times\tfrac{3}{4}=\tfrac{3}{16}" />
</MathContainer>

The question asks about the **next child**, who could be a son or a daughter, so the chance of a son with G6PD deficiency already includes the <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" /> chance of a son. The answer is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{16}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{8}" /> uses <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" /> for G6PD deficiency, the chance **among sons only**, not among all children.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{16}" /> is the chance of a son with G6PD deficiency who **also** has thalassaemia major, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}\times\tfrac{1}{4}" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" /> is the chance of a son with G6PD deficiency and **ignores the thalassaemia gene** altogether.

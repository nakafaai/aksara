# Compare how each signal travels and how long it stays

A nerve impulse runs along a neurone to one target in a fraction of a second and stops when stimulation stops. A hormone must be **carried by the blood** to reach its targets, reaches every organ with matching receptors, and acts until it is broken down.

- II: adrenaline binds receptors on the heart muscle cells themselves. It does not need the brain to turn it into impulses. Statement II is wrong.
- III: five halvings leave <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{32}" /> of the peak, about <InlineMath math="3\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the blood carries adrenaline to the liver and the bronchioles as well as the heart, so it raises blood glucose and widens the airways together. The nerve reaches only the heart. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects IV, the **many targets** that a hormone in the blood reaches at once.
- I, II and III only routes adrenaline through the brain, **although it acts on the heart cells directly**.
- II and IV only rejects I and III, often after taking ten minutes as **five times smaller instead of five halvings**.

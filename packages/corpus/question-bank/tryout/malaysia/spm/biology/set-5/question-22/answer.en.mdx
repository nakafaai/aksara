# Compare the area of the pores with the water they let out

Water vapour leaving a small pore **spreads out sideways** as it diffuses away, so each pore draws vapour from a much wider zone of air than its own area. Many small, well-spaced pores therefore lose almost as much as an open surface.

- II: <InlineMath math="2\%" /> of the area gives <InlineMath math="70\%" /> of the loss, so each unit of pore area loses vapour <InlineMath math="35" /> times as fast as open water. Statement II is correct.
- III: the data show <InlineMath math="70\%" />, not <InlineMath math="2\%" />. Loss is not limited by pore area alone, because vapour spreads out from the edge of each pore. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: carbon dioxide enters and water vapour leaves through the same pores, so closing stomata cuts **both**. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only caps the loss at the pore area, **although the leaf loses <InlineMath math="70\%" />**.
- II and IV only rejects the <InlineMath math="2\%" /> and lets **water loss carry on through closed pores**.
- I and IV only rejects the factor of <InlineMath math="35" /> and **separates carbon dioxide uptake from water loss**.

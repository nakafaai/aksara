# Follow the food store into the root and back out again

A biennial **stores food in its first year** and spends it on reproduction in its second. Add root and shoot at each time to see the whole plant, because one part can grow while the other shrinks.

- I: the root peaks at <InlineMath math="150" /> g and falls to <InlineMath math="20" /> g, a loss of <InlineMath math="130" /> g, about <InlineMath math="87\%" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: as the root shrinks from month <InlineMath math="12" /> to month <InlineMath math="20" />, the shoot grows a flowering stalk, rising to <InlineMath math="110" /> g. The stored food is broken down and moved up to build it. Statement II is correct.
- III: the whole plant is heaviest at month <InlineMath math="8" />, with <InlineMath math="210" /> g. By month <InlineMath math="12" /> the leaves have died back, so the total is only <InlineMath math="170" /> g. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the carrot stores food in its root in the first year and uses it to flower, set seed and die in the second. That is a biennial life cycle. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the biennial cycle the whole graph shows**.
- II, III and IV only takes the **root's peak as the whole plant's peak**, and rejects the <InlineMath math="87\%" /> loss.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **missing where the root's food goes**.

# Count the moles of nitrate, not the change in concentration

The volume of solution changed, so a fall in concentration does not show the amount removed. Count the **moles before and after**, then compare the nitrate taken with the water taken.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{start}: 2.0\times10=20\text{ mmol},\qquad \text{end}: 1.5\times4=6\text{ mmol},\qquad \text{taken up}: 20-6=14\text{ mmol}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{nitrate per litre of water taken}: \frac{14}{0.5}=28\text{ mmol/L}>10\text{ mmol/L}" />
</MathContainer>

The roots took in nitrate at <InlineMath math="28\text{ mmol}" /> for every litre of water, nearly three times the concentration of the solution. If nitrate simply came in with the water, the solution would keep its concentration. Instead it fell, so the root cells took up nitrate selectively and against its concentration gradient, using ATP. The plant took up <InlineMath math="14\text{ mmol}" /> by active transport.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="12" /> mmol by active transport multiplies the fall in concentration, <InlineMath math="6" />, by <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ L}" />, **ignoring the water that left**.
- <InlineMath math="14" /> mmol by diffusion with the water has the right amount, but water carrying nitrate **would leave the concentration unchanged**, not lower it.
- <InlineMath math="6" /> mmol by diffusion with the water is the nitrate **still left in the solution**, not what was taken up.

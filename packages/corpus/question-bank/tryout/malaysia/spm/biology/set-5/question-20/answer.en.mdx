# Read each row in the right direction

A trial compares the treated group with the placebo group row by row. The order matters: a **larger** number of days means a **slower** recovery.

- I: the antibiotic group took <InlineMath math="6.1" /> days and the placebo group <InlineMath math="6.0" />, so the antibiotic did not shorten recovery at all. That fits, because antibiotics act on bacteria and the infection is viral. Statement I is wrong.
- II: <InlineMath math="18" /> of every <InlineMath math="100" /> on the antibiotic had side effects against <InlineMath math="6" /> on the placebo, so about <InlineMath math="12" /> in every <InlineMath math="100" /> had side effects caused by the drug. Statement II is correct.
- III: a few throat bacteria were already resistant. The antibiotic removed their susceptible neighbours, so the resistant ones multiplied, from <InlineMath math="12\%" /> to <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of patients. Statement III is correct.
- IV: mutations arise at random, whether or not an antibiotic is present. The antibiotic only **selects** the resistant bacteria that already exist. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only reads <InlineMath math="6.1" /> against <InlineMath math="6.0" /> backwards, **as if more days meant faster recovery**.
- III and IV only rejects II and lets the drug **cause the mutations it selects**.
- I and II only makes the slip in I and **rejects the selection that explains the rise to <InlineMath math="40\%" />**.

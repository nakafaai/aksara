# Read the shift in the curve and link it to the mitochondria

Lactic acid builds up when muscles make pyruvate faster than their mitochondria can **break it down with oxygen**. More mitochondria let a runner go faster before that happens, so the whole curve shifts to the right.

- I: before training lactate reaches <InlineMath math="4\text{ mmol/L}" /> at <InlineMath math="12\text{ km/h}" />, and after training only at <InlineMath math="14\text{ km/h}" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: at <InlineMath math="14\text{ km/h}" /> lactate falls from <InlineMath math="8" /> to <InlineMath math="4\text{ mmol/L}" />, so less glucose ends as lactic acid and more is respired **aerobically**. Statement II is correct.
- III: pyruvate is broken down with oxygen inside mitochondria. With <InlineMath math="40\%" /> more of them, more pyruvate is oxidised and less is turned into lactic acid. Statement III is correct.
- IV: at <InlineMath math="16\text{ km/h}" /> lactate falls from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="8\text{ mmol/L}" />, a third lower, not half. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **the mitochondria that explain why the curve shifts**.
- II and III only rejects I, although the <InlineMath math="4\text{ mmol/L}" /> point **moves to a faster speed**.
- I, II and IV only takes a fall from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="8" /> as half, **when it is only a third**.

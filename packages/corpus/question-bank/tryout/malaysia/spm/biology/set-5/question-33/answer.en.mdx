# Let the albino young reveal both parents' hidden allele

An albino has genotype <InlineMath math="cc" />, so **each parent must carry** <InlineMath math="c" />. That fixes both genotypes, and the rest follows from the <InlineMath math="2:1:1" /> ratio.

- I: the albino young need <InlineMath math="c" /> from each parent, so the male is <InlineMath math="Cc" /> and the female <InlineMath math="c^{ch}c" />. This gives full, full, chinchilla and albino in a <InlineMath math="2:1:1" /> ratio, as observed. Statement I is correct.
- II: the full-colour young are <InlineMath math="Cc^{ch}" /> and <InlineMath math="Cc" /> in equal numbers, so only **half** carry <InlineMath math="c" />. Statement II is wrong.
- III: every chinchilla young is <InlineMath math="c^{ch}c" />. Crossed with <InlineMath math="c^hc" />, one young in four is <InlineMath math="cc" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: a rabbit is diploid, so it carries **two** alleles of each gene, however many exist in the population. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, but **half the full-colour young are** <InlineMath math="Cc^{ch}" />.
- II and IV only rejects the genotypes and **gives a diploid rabbit four alleles**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **although each rabbit holds just two copies**.

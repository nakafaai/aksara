# Balance the magnesium before and after, then spread it over the leaf area

The solution has no magnesium, so the plant's **total cannot rise**. Any gain in the young leaves must come out of the old leaves. Whether a leaf stays green depends on magnesium **per unit area**, not on the total.

- I: the old leaves lose <InlineMath math="12-6=6\text{ µmol}" /> and the young leaves gain <InlineMath math="8-2=6\text{ µmol}" />, while the total stays at <InlineMath math="14" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: the total is <InlineMath math="14\text{ µmol}" /> on both days, so the plant took up nothing, as expected from a solution with no magnesium. The <InlineMath math="6\text{ µmol}" /> gained by the young leaves came from the old ones. Statement II is wrong.
- III: old leaves hold <InlineMath math="0.1" /> and young leaves <InlineMath math="0.2\text{ µmol}" /> per square centimetre. With half the magnesium, the old leaves can hold at most half as much chlorophyll, so they yellow first. Statement III is correct.
- IV: iron cannot be moved out of a leaf once it is there. Without iron, the **young leaves** yellow first, the opposite pattern. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only takes the young leaves' gain as **uptake from a solution that has no magnesium**.
- III and IV only rejects the transfer in I and gives iron **the mobility of magnesium**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **although iron stays where it is and harms young leaves first**.

# Apply the diffusion rule one factor at a time

The rate depends on **area times difference divided by thickness**. Change one factor and the rate changes by the same factor, or by its inverse for thickness.

<BlockMath math="\frac{30}{70}\approx0.43=43\%,\qquad \text{twice the thickness}: 0.43\times\tfrac{1}{2}\approx21\%" />

- I: the area falls from <InlineMath math="70" /> to <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}^2" />, so the rate falls to <InlineMath math="\tfrac{30}{70}\approx43\%" /> with everything else equal. Statement I is correct.
- II: in exercise the heart pumps faster, so each portion of blood spends less time in the lung capillaries. A healthy lung has spare surface and still loads <InlineMath math="96\%" />, but the reduced surface cannot keep up and saturation falls to <InlineMath math="80\%" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: thickness is on the bottom of the rule, so doubling it halves the rate again, from about <InlineMath math="43\%" /> to about <InlineMath math="21\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: emphysema destroys the **alveolar walls**, the exchange surface itself. The airways are not the main problem. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although thickness divides the rate**, so doubling it halves the rate.
- II and III only rejects I, often after taking the loss, <InlineMath math="40\text{ m}^2" />, as the new share instead of **what remains**.
- I, II and IV only blames the trachea, **while the damage is to the alveoli**.

# Ask which bacteria each soil favours

Waterlogged soil has almost no oxygen. **Denitrifying bacteria** thrive there, using nitrate in place of oxygen and releasing nitrogen gas. Drained soil has oxygen, which **nitrifying bacteria** need to turn ammonium into nitrate.

- I: the gas above the waterlogged plot fills with nitrogen gas, so the lost nitrate leaves as gas instead of staying as ammonium. Statement I is wrong.
- II: nitrate halves every <InlineMath math="4" /> days, three times in <InlineMath math="12" /> days, leaving <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{8}" /> of the start. Statement II is correct.
- III: nitrogen-fixing bacteria turn nitrogen gas into ammonium, not nitrate into ammonium. Nitrate rises in the drained plot because nitrifying bacteria turn ammonium into nitrate. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: in waterlogged soil, denitrifying bacteria would convert added nitrate to nitrogen gas, so much of it would escape. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only accepts I, **although the nitrogen leaves as gas**, and rejects IV.
- II, III and IV only credits the rise in nitrate to **nitrogen fixation instead of nitrification**.
- I, III and IV only rejects the **three halvings** in II and accepts both wrong mechanisms.

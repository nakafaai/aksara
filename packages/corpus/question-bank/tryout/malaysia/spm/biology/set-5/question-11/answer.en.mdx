# Work out each tube's dilution, then compare the last tubes that clump

Each tube halves the serum again, so moving **three tubes further** means the serum can be diluted <InlineMath math="2^3" /> times more and still work. That tells us how much more antibody it holds.

- I: tube <InlineMath math="6" /> holds serum diluted <InlineMath math="320" /> times, so the titre is one part in <InlineMath math="320" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: the second serum still works when diluted <InlineMath math="8" /> times further, so it holds about <InlineMath math="8" /> times as much antibody. Statement II is correct.
- III: the antibody rose during the illness because the patient's own plasma cells made it, which is **active** immunity. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: an antibody has two antigen-binding sites. Bound to two bacteria at once, antibodies link many bacteria into clumps. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and IV only rejects II, often after taking three more tubes as **three times as much antibody**, instead of <InlineMath math="2\times2\times2=8" /> times.
- II and III only rejects the titre and the **two binding sites**, and calls the response passive.
- I, II and III only accepts III, **although the patient's own cells made the antibody**.

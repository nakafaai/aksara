# Follow the pressure through one loop and through two

In a **single circulation**, blood passes through two capillary beds, the gills and then the body, on one push from the heart. In a **double circulation**, it returns to the heart after the lungs and gets a second, separate push for the body.

- I: pressure falls from <InlineMath math="30" /> to <InlineMath math="10\text{ mmHg}" /> across the gills, a loss of <InlineMath math="20" /> out of <InlineMath math="30" />, two thirds. Statement I is correct.
- II: the fish's body arteries receive blood at <InlineMath math="10\text{ mmHg}" />, after the gills, not at <InlineMath math="30" />. The mammal's receive it at <InlineMath math="120" />, so the ratio is <InlineMath math="12" />, not <InlineMath math="4" />. Statement II is wrong.
- III: lung capillaries have very thin walls. A low pressure of <InlineMath math="25\text{ mmHg}" /> moves blood through them without forcing much fluid into the alveoli. Statement III is correct.
- IV: blood reaches the fish heart from the body, already short of oxygen, and goes straight from the heart to the gills. The heart never holds oxygenated blood. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only compares the mammal's <InlineMath math="120" /> with the fish heart's <InlineMath math="30" />, **instead of with the <InlineMath math="10" /> that reaches the fish's body**.
- III and IV only rejects I, **although <InlineMath math="20" /> of <InlineMath math="30\text{ mmHg}" /> is lost in the gills**.
- I, II and III only makes the same slip in II and **rejects the fish heart's deoxygenated blood**.

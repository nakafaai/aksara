# Match each place in the cell to its pH on the curve

The enzyme works best at pH <InlineMath math="4.8" />, inside the lysosome, and poorly at pH <InlineMath math="7.2" />, in the cytosol. Keeping the lysosome acidic **costs ATP**, because protons must be pumped in against their gradient.

<BlockMath math="\frac{10\%}{100\%}=\frac{1}{10}" />

- I: at pH <InlineMath math="7.2" /> the enzyme has <InlineMath math="10\%" /> of its activity at pH <InlineMath math="4.8" />, a tenth. This protects the cell when a little enzyme leaks out. Statement I is correct.
- II: the pumps need ATP. Without it, protons leak back out and the inside drifts towards the cytosol's pH. Statement II is correct.
- III: the enzymes are **less** active at pH <InlineMath math="7.2" />, not more. A dying cell digests itself because its lysosome membranes break and release large amounts of enzyme, and the pH of a dying cell also falls. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: at pH <InlineMath math="7.2" /> the enzyme drops to <InlineMath math="10\%" />, so the digestion of worn-out organelles inside the lysosome slows to about a tenth. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the same tenth as in I** applied inside the lysosome.
- II, III and IV only claims the enzymes speed up at pH <InlineMath math="7.2" />, **although the curve falls to <InlineMath math="10\%" />**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **forgetting that the proton pumps run on ATP**.

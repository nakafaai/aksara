# Follow the gene in pollen, then ask what spraying selects

Wild plants can only gain the gene if crop **pollen** reaches their stigmas, so gene flow depends on how far pollen travels. Once the gene is in wild plants, the herbicide acts as a **selection pressure** in its favour.

- I: the wild seeds can only get the gene through crop pollen, and the share falls from <InlineMath math="4\%" /> to <InlineMath math="0.01\%" /> over <InlineMath math="400\text{ m}" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="0.1\%" /> at <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> against <InlineMath math="4\%" /> at the edge is one fortieth. Statement II is correct.
- III: spraying kills wild plants without the gene, so those carrying it survive, set seed and become more common. Statement III is correct.
- IV: with no pollen from the crop, the gene has no way into the wild plants' seeds, so the gene flow would almost stop. Statement IV is correct.

So all four statements are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only rejects IV, although **the gene can only move in pollen**.
- I and III only rejects II, often after misreading the fall from <InlineMath math="4\%" /> to <InlineMath math="0.1\%" /> as **a quarter**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **the pollen route behind all the other statements**.

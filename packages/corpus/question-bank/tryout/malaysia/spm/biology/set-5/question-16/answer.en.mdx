# Picture the acid moving inwards, and work out the average rate

Acid reaches the **surface of the bolus first** and its centre last. The centre therefore stays near neutral for a while, and amylase there slows down only as the pH falls.

- I: over the first <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes amylase averages about <InlineMath math="72\%" /> of its rate in the mouth, well above half. Statement I is wrong.
- II: from <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes the pH is <InlineMath math="3" /> or lower, and activity is <InlineMath math="5\%" /> and then <InlineMath math="0" />. Strong acid changes the shape of the active site, so starch no longer fits. Statement II is correct.
- III: a smaller bolus has a shorter distance from surface to centre, so acid reaches the centre **sooner** and amylase stops earlier. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the surface of the bolus becomes acidic first, which suits pepsin, while the centre is still close to neutral, which suits amylase. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only accepts I, perhaps by averaging only the <InlineMath math="20" /> minute value, <InlineMath math="30\%" />, **instead of the whole interval**.
- II, III and IV only accepts III, **although acid soaks through a small bolus faster**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and accepts both wrong statements about **the average rate and the bolus size**.

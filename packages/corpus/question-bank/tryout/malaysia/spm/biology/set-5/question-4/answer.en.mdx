# Compare fatty acids that differ in one feature at a time

To see what a double bond does, compare acids with the **same chain length**. To see what chain length does, compare acids with **no double bonds**. Each comparison isolates one feature.

- I: the three <InlineMath math="18" />-carbon acids melt at <InlineMath math="70" />, <InlineMath math="13" /> and <InlineMath math="-5^\circ\text{C}" /> with <InlineMath math="0" />, <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="2" /> double bonds. Each double bond puts a **kink** in the chain, so the chains pack less tightly and melt more easily. Statement I is correct.
- II: a membrane must stay fluid to work. In very cold water, saturated lipids would stiffen, so cold-water fish use about twice as many unsaturated fatty acids, which stay fluid at low temperatures. Statement II is correct.
- III: stearic acid melts at <InlineMath math="70^\circ\text{C}" />, far above <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" />, so a fat rich in it is solid at body temperature. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the saturated acids are palmitic, <InlineMath math="16" /> carbons, at <InlineMath math="63^\circ\text{C}" /> and stearic, <InlineMath math="18" /> carbons, at <InlineMath math="70^\circ\text{C}" />. The shorter chain melts at a lower temperature. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **although the 16-carbon acid melts** <InlineMath math="7^\circ\text{C}" /> lower than the 18-carbon one.
- I, II and III only makes stearic acid liquid at <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" />, **when it melts only at** <InlineMath math="70^\circ\text{C}" />.
- II, III and IV only rejects the clear fall in melting point with each double bond.

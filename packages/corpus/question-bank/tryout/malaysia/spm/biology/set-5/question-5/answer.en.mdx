# Watch what happens to each inhibitor's effect as substrate floods in

An inhibitor that **shares the active site** with the substrate loses ground when substrate is plentiful, because substrate molecules win most of the encounters. An inhibitor that binds **elsewhere** changes the enzyme's shape, so extra substrate cannot undo it.

- I: at <InlineMath math="1\text{ mM}" />, X halves the rate, but at <InlineMath math="40\text{ mM}" /> it lowers it by only about <InlineMath math="9\%" />. Its effect fades as substrate rises, as expected for a **competitive** inhibitor. Statement I is correct.
- II: Y levels off near <InlineMath math="45" />, about half of <InlineMath math="91" />, and even <InlineMath math="40\text{ mM}" /> substrate cannot lift it. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="91-83=8" />, and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{8}{91}\approx9\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: if Y used the active site, extra substrate would crowd it out as it does with X. Y's effect stays, so Y binds somewhere else and changes the enzyme's shape. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, often after dividing <InlineMath math="8" /> by <InlineMath math="83" /> instead of by the **uninhibited rate**.
- II and III only rejects I, missing that X's effect **shrinks from half to a tenth**.
- I, II and IV only places Y on the active site, **although substrate cannot push it aside**.

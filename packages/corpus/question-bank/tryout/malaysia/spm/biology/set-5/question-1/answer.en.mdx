# Judge a result by its size, its sample and how well its repeats agree

A difference between two means is only convincing if it is **larger than the spread** you would get by chance. A tiny sample and widely scattered repeats both make chance a likely explanation.

- I: with only <InlineMath math="5" /> seeds, each seed is <InlineMath math="20" /> percentage points, so a single seed can swing P's result. P's <InlineMath math="40" />-point difference is the least reliable. Statement I is correct.
- II: Q and R both get <InlineMath math="60\%" /> against <InlineMath math="72\%" />, but Q's dishes stay within a few seeds of each other while R's range from <InlineMath math="15" /> to <InlineMath math="48" />. Q's difference stands out from its spread, R's does not. Statement II is correct.
- III: Q's treated dishes give <InlineMath math="108" /> out of <InlineMath math="150" /> seeds, <InlineMath math="72\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: R's scattered results are **inconclusive**. They cannot show an effect, but they cannot prove there is none either. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, often after averaging the three counts as <InlineMath math="36" /> and forgetting that each dish held <InlineMath math="50" /> seeds.
- II and III only trusts P's large difference, **although one seed changes it by twenty points**.
- I, II and IV only turns inconclusive results into proof, **which scattered data can never give**.

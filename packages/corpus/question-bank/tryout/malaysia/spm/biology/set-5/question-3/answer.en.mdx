# Square the change in distance to get the change in time

With <InlineMath math="\text{time}\propto\text{distance}^2" />, a distance <InlineMath math="k" /> times longer takes **<InlineMath math="k^2" /> times as long**. Compare every distance with the <InlineMath math="0.1\text{ mm}" /> that takes <InlineMath math="5" /> seconds.

<BlockMath math="1\text{ mm}: \left(\tfrac{1}{0.1}\right)^2\times5=100\times5=500\text{ s},\qquad 1\text{ cm}: \left(\tfrac{10}{0.1}\right)^2\times5=10\,000\times5=50\,000\text{ s}\approx14\text{ h}" />

- I: <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" /> is <InlineMath math="10" /> times farther, so it takes <InlineMath math="10^2=100" /> times as long, <InlineMath math="500" /> seconds. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> is <InlineMath math="100" /> times farther, so it takes <InlineMath math="10\,000" /> times as long, <InlineMath math="50\,000" /> seconds or nearly <InlineMath math="14" /> hours. Cells that far from the surface would starve, so large animals need a transport system. Statement II is correct.
- III: doubling the distance makes the time <InlineMath math="2^2=4" /> times longer, not twice as long. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the centre of a cell <InlineMath math="0.1\text{ mm}" /> across is only <InlineMath math="0.05\text{ mm}" /> in, half the distance, so it takes <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2\times5=1.25" /> seconds. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only halves the time for half the distance, **but half the distance takes a quarter of the time**.
- I, III and IV only treats time as proportional to distance, **forgetting the square**.
- II and III only rejects I, although it uses the same square rule as II, and accepts the doubling.

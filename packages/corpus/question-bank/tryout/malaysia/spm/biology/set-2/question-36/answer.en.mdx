# Where the yellowing starts tells you how mobile the element is

The pattern of yellowing depends on whether the plant can **move the element** out of old leaves when the supply runs out.

  title={<>Where the Missing Element Shows First</>}

The yellowing starts in the oldest leaves while the youngest stay green. So the plant is stripping the element from old leaves to feed new ones, which only works for a mobile element. Magnesium is part of chlorophyll and nitrogen is part of chlorophyll and proteins, and both are mobile, so a lack of either yellows the old leaves first.

So the missing element could be magnesium or nitrogen.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Calcium is **immobile**, so a lack of it damages the youngest leaves and growing tips first.
- Iron or calcium names two immobile elements, which would yellow the young leaves, not the old ones.
- Iron is immobile too, and its lack shows as yellow young leaves with green veins.

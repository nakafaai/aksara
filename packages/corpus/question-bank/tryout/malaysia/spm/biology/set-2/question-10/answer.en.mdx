# Name each blood group, then check it against the patient's antibody

A red blood cell clumps with anti-A serum when it carries antigen A, and with anti-B serum when it carries antigen B. So the table gives the groups:

- W carries antigen A only: group A.
- X carries antigens A and B: group AB.
- Y carries neither antigen: group O.
- Z carries antigen B only: group B.

The patient has group B, so the plasma contains **anti-A** antibody. Donor red blood cells are safe only if they carry **no antigen A**, otherwise the patient's anti-A clumps them. W and X carry antigen A, so they are unsafe. Y and Z carry no antigen A, so they are safe. So the patient can receive blood from Y and Z only.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Y only accepts the universal donor but rejects Z, although group B cells carry no antigen A.
- X and Z accepts X, wrongly treating group AB as a universal donor. Group AB is the universal **recipient**.
- W, Y and Z accepts W, whose antigen A is clumped by the patient's anti-A.

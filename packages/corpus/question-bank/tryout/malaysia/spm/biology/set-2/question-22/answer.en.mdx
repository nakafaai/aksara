# Shrink the field of view first, then find the density

The stomata were counted at <InlineMath math="\times400" />, so the field that matters is the one at <InlineMath math="\times400" />. A magnification **four times larger** shows a field four times narrower: <InlineMath math="\frac{1.6}{4}=0.4\text{ mm}" /> across.

<BlockMath math="r=\frac{0.4}{2}=0.2\text{ mm},\qquad \text{area}=\pi\times0.2^2\approx0.1257\text{ mm}^2,\qquad \text{density}=\frac{30}{0.1257}\approx239\text{ per mm}^2" />

So the lower surface carries about <InlineMath math="239" /> stomata per <InlineMath math="\text{mm}^2" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="15" /> uses the field of view at <InlineMath math="\times100" />, but **the stomata were counted at** <InlineMath math="\times400" />.
- About <InlineMath math="60" /> uses the diameter <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ mm}" /> as the radius.
- <InlineMath math="750" /> drops <InlineMath math="\pi" /> from the area.

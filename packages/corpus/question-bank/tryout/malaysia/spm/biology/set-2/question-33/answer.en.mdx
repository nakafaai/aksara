# Each pair holds one cause fixed

Identical twins share **all their genes**, so any difference between K's twins comes from the environment. Non-identical twins raised together share **the same environment** but only half their genes, so L's differences lean on genes.

- Blood group: K's twins match although they grew up apart, while L's twins differ although they grew up together. Blood group follows the genes alone.
- Body mass: K's identical twins differ by <InlineMath math="8\text{ kg}" />, more than L's <InlineMath math="3\text{ kg}" />. With the same genes, that gap is the environment's work, so body mass is strongly affected by it.
- Height: K differs less than L, so genes matter a lot for height, and K's <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}" /> shows the environment adds a little.

So the data best support that blood group is controlled by genes alone, while body mass is strongly affected by the environment.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Height by the environment alone fails, because the twins with identical genes differ least in height.
- Blood group is **discontinuous** variation: a person has one of a few distinct groups, with nothing in between.
- Mutations are rare and random, while the steady differences in mass and height between identical twins raised apart point to their different homes.

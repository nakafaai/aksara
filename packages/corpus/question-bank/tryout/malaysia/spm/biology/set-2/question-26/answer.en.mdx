# One pollen tube per ovule, two gametes per tube

Each pollen tube enters **one** ovule, so every ovule needs its own pollen grain: <InlineMath math="30" /> ovules need at least <InlineMath math="30" /> pollen grains.

In double fertilisation, both male gametes of a tube are used in that one ovule. One fuses with the egg to form the diploid zygote, and the other fuses with the two polar nuclei to form the triploid endosperm nucleus. So each ovule uses <InlineMath math="2" /> male gametes:

<BlockMath math="30\times2=60\text{ male gametes}." />

So at least <InlineMath math="30" /> pollen grains are needed, and <InlineMath math="60" /> male gametes take part.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="15" /> pollen grains assumes the two gametes of one tube fertilise **two different ovules**, but both work inside the same ovule.
- <InlineMath math="30" /> male gametes forgets the second gamete that forms the endosperm.
- <InlineMath math="60" /> pollen grains counts one pollen grain per gamete, but each grain brings two.

# Compare the outward push with the inward pull at each end

The net pressure is blood pressure minus osmotic pressure. A positive result pushes fluid **out**. A negative one draws fluid **in**.

- Statement I has the right size but the wrong direction: at the venous end fluid is drawn **back in**. It is wrong.
- Statement II matches the arterial end, so it is correct.
- With the osmotic pressure at <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ kPa}" />, the arterial end pushes out <InlineMath math="4.3-2.0=2.3\text{ kPa}" /> and the venous end draws in only <InlineMath math="2.0-1.6=0.4\text{ kPa}" />. More fluid leaves and less returns, so tissue fluid builds up as swelling (oedema), as in kwashiorkor. Statement III is correct.
- Tissue fluid that does not return enters the **lymphatic capillaries** and flows back as lymph, not through the veins. Statement IV is wrong.

So only II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II accepts I, reading the <InlineMath math="1.7\text{ kPa}" /> at the venous end as outward when it is inward.
- I, III and IV accepts both I and IV, **and rejects the correct arterial pressure in II**.
- III and IV accepts IV, but the leftover tissue fluid returns through the lymphatic system.

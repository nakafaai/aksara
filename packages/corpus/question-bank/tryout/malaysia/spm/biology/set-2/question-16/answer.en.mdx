# Use the anaerobic energy value, then the equation

The energy comes from anaerobic respiration only, so the <InlineMath math="150\text{ kJ}" /> per mole applies, not <InlineMath math="2898\text{ kJ}" />:

/>

<BlockMath math="\text{glucose used}=\frac{75\text{ kJ}}{150\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}=0.5\text{ mol}." />

In muscle, anaerobic respiration splits **one** glucose molecule into **two** lactic acid molecules:

<BlockMath math="\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\rightarrow2\,\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}_3+\text{energy}" />

So <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ mol}" /> of glucose forms <InlineMath math="2\times0.5=1\text{ mol}" /> of lactic acid. The same <InlineMath math="75\text{ kJ}" /> from aerobic respiration would need only <InlineMath math="\frac{75}{2898}\approx0.026\text{ mol}" />, about twenty times less glucose, which is why sprinting uses up glucose so fast.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ mol}" /> of lactic acid forgets that each glucose molecule gives **two** lactic acid molecules.
- <InlineMath math="0.026\text{ mol}" /> uses the aerobic value, but the question says the energy comes from anaerobic respiration alone.
- <InlineMath math="1\text{ mol}" /> of glucose divides <InlineMath math="150" /> by <InlineMath math="75" /> the wrong way round, or gives each lactic acid molecule the whole <InlineMath math="75\text{ kJ}" />.

# Count condensations to build, hydrolyses to cut

Each peptide bond forms by condensation, releasing one water molecule, and breaking one by hydrolysis **uses** one. A chain of <InlineMath math="n" /> amino acids has <InlineMath math="n-1" /> peptide bonds.

Check: the two final chains have <InlineMath math="20+29=49" /> bonds and the piece removed has <InlineMath math="34" />, so <InlineMath math="49+34+2=85" />. So <InlineMath math="85" /> water molecules are released and <InlineMath math="2" /> are used.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="86" /> released gives one water molecule per amino acid instead of one per bond.
- <InlineMath math="49" /> counts only the bonds of the final chains and **forgets the bonds of the piece that was removed**.
- <InlineMath math="35" /> used gives a water molecule to every amino acid of the piece, but only the two bonds joining it are cut.

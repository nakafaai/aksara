# Follow the arrows out of the snake one step at a time

Removing a predator helps its **direct prey** first, because nothing else stands between them. Effects further along the web depend on two pulls at once, so they are not certain.

- Snakes eat rats and frogs, so with fewer snakes both rats and frogs survive better and their numbers rise. This follows straight from the web.
- Grasshoppers now face more frogs, so they tend to fall, not rise.
- Rice is eaten by more rats but by fewer grasshoppers. Those two effects pull in opposite directions, so the rice yield cannot be predicted from the web alone.

So the change that is certain to happen first is that the numbers of rats and frogs rise.

# Why the other options are wrong

- A rise in grasshoppers ignores that **more frogs now eat them**.
- A rise in the rice yield ignores that more rats eat more rice, so the outcome is uncertain.
- A fall in frogs gets the direction wrong, because the frogs have lost a predator.

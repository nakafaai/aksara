# Follow acetylcholine through the junction with each drug

Normally the impulse releases acetylcholine, it binds to the receptors and starts an impulse in the muscle, and the muscle contracts. Acetylcholinesterase then breaks the acetylcholine down, so the stimulation **stops** and the muscle relaxes.

- With P, release and binding still happen, so the muscle contracts. But the acetylcholine is **not broken down**, so it keeps binding and the muscle keeps being stimulated. The muscle stays contracted.
- With Q, acetylcholine is released but its receptors are blocked, so **no impulse starts** in the muscle. The muscle cannot contract.

So with P the muscle stays contracted, and with Q the muscle cannot contract.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The first option swaps the two drugs.
- "No acetylcholine is released" with P is wrong, **because P acts on the enzyme in the gap, not on the release from the neurone**.
- "Contracts and relaxes normally" with P ignores that relaxing needs acetylcholine to be removed, and it gives Q the effect of P.

# Net loss, then the water the plant can spare

The plant loses water through its leaves and gains it through its roots at the same time, so what matters is the **net loss**:

<BlockMath math="35-20=15\text{ g per hour}." />

It wilts at <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of <InlineMath math="300\text{ g}" />, which is <InlineMath math="270\text{ g}" />, so it can spare <InlineMath math="300-270=30\text{ g}" />:

<BlockMath math="\text{time}=\frac{30\text{ g}}{15\text{ g per hour}}=2\text{ hours}." />

So the plant starts to wilt after <InlineMath math="2" /> hours, at <InlineMath math="3\text{ pm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="0.9" /> hour uses the transpiration rate alone, <InlineMath math="\frac{30}{35}" />, and **forgets the water the roots take in**.
- <InlineMath math="1.5" /> hours divides by the uptake rate, <InlineMath math="\frac{30}{20}" />, which is not the rate of loss.
- "Never" ignores that the plant loses more each hour than it takes in, so its water keeps falling.

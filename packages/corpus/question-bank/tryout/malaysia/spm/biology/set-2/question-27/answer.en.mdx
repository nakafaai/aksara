Each adaptation answers one measurement in the table.

- The mud holds almost no oxygen, so roots buried in it cannot respire. **Pneumatophores** grow upwards out of the mud, and their lenticels take in air at low tide.
- The water is very salty, so salt that enters with water would build up in the tissues. **Salt glands** on the leaves secrete the excess salt.
- Tides would wash away small seeds before they could root. A **viviparous seedling** germinates on the tree and drops as a heavy, pointed seedling that sticks into the mud and roots quickly.

So pneumatophores deal with the low oxygen, salt glands with the salty water, and viviparous seedlings with the tides.

# Why the other options are wrong

- The first option swaps pneumatophores and salt glands, but **only a breathing root fixes a lack of oxygen**.
- The second option gives pneumatophores the tides and vivipary the oxygen problem, which matches neither structure.
- The last option swaps salt glands and vivipary.

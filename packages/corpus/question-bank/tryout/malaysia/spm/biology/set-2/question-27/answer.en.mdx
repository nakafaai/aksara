The mud holds almost no oxygen, so roots buried in it cannot **respire**. Pneumatophores grow up out of the mud, and their lenticels take in air at low tide, so statement I is correct. Statement IV is wrong: roots never photosynthesise, and the problem is the lack of oxygen, not too much carbon dioxide.

Water moves by osmosis from a dilute solution to a more concentrated one. Keeping the root cell sap more concentrated than seawater lets water keep entering, so statement II is correct. A seedling that has already germinated roots quickly when it drops, before the tide can wash it away, so statement III is correct. So I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III misses II, the **concentrated cell sap that keeps water moving in**.
- I, II and IV accepts IV, but roots do not photosynthesise.
- II, III and IV rejects I and accepts IV.

# Follow the peak of radioactivity in time

Only the first <InlineMath math="3" /> minutes of protein is radioactive, so that one batch can be tracked. Wherever the batch is, the percentage **peaks** there, then falls as the batch moves on.

- The rough endoplasmic reticulum peaks first, at <InlineMath math="82\%" /> after <InlineMath math="3" /> minutes. Its ribosomes make the protein, and the protein enters its channels.
- The Golgi apparatus peaks next, at <InlineMath math="61\%" /> after <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes, where the protein is modified and packed.
- The secretory vesicles peak last, at <InlineMath math="75\%" /> after <InlineMath math="90" /> minutes, before they fuse with the plasma membrane.
- The smooth endoplasmic reticulum stays near <InlineMath math="3\%" /> throughout, so the batch never passes through it. It makes lipids, not secreted proteins.

So the route is rough endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, secretory vesicle, which makes statement I correct. At <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes the Golgi apparatus holds <InlineMath math="61\%" />, the largest share, and the Golgi apparatus modifies and packs proteins, so statement II is correct. Statement III is wrong, because a steady <InlineMath math="3\%" /> never peaks, so the batch never passes through the smooth endoplasmic reticulum. Statement IV is correct: if vesicles cannot leave the rough endoplasmic reticulum, the batch stays there, so its radioactivity stays high and none reaches the secretory vesicles. So I, II and IV are correct.

- I, II and III accepts III, but the smooth endoplasmic reticulum never shows a peak.

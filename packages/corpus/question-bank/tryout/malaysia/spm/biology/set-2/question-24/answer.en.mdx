# Follow sugar in the phloem and water in the xylem

The ring cuts **only the phloem**. Phloem carries sucrose from the leaves down to the roots and to other sinks. The xylem, which carries water up, is untouched.

- Sucrose flowing down from the leaves cannot cross the gap, so it piles up just above the ring and the tissue there swells. Statement I is expected.
- The xylem still carries water, so the leaves do not wilt. Statement II is wrong.
- Below the ring, the roots receive no new sucrose. They live on stored food for a while, then run out and die if the ring never heals. Statement III is expected.
- All the sugar the leaves make now stays above the ring, so more of it goes to the fruits, which grow larger. Farmers use this, called girdling, on purpose. Statement IV is expected.

So I, III and IV are expected.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II accepts II, but **the xylem is untouched**, so water still reaches the leaves.
- II and III accepts the wilting and misses the swelling above the ring.
- I and III misses IV, the extra sugar that the fruits above the ring receive.

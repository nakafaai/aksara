# Turn every time into a rate first

A shorter time means a faster reaction, so compare **rates**, not times. With rate <InlineMath math="=\frac{1}{\text{time}}" />:

<BlockMath math="20^\circ\text{C}:\ \tfrac{1}{8}=0.125,\quad 30^\circ\text{C}:\ \tfrac{1}{4}=0.25,\quad 40^\circ\text{C}:\ \tfrac{1}{2}=0.5,\quad 50^\circ\text{C}:\ \tfrac{1}{2.5}=0.4\ \text{min}^{-1}" />

- I: <InlineMath math="\frac{0.5}{0.125}=4" />, so statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="0.125\to0.25\to0.5" /> doubles at each step of <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" />, so statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="\frac{0.4}{0.5}=0.8=80\%" />, so statement III is correct. Comparing the times instead gives <InlineMath math="\frac{2.5}{2}=125\%" />, which is the trap.
- IV: at <InlineMath math="60^\circ\text{C}" /> the heat breaks the bonds that hold the enzyme's shape, so the active site **changes shape** and starch can no longer fit into it. The enzyme is denatured. Starch is not held in the site. Statement IV is wrong.

So I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II rejects III, usually after comparing times, which makes <InlineMath math="50^\circ\text{C}" /> look <InlineMath math="125\%" /> as fast.
- I, III and IV accepts IV, **but a denatured active site cannot bind the substrate at all**.
- II and IV rejects the correct ratio in I and accepts the wrong reason in IV.

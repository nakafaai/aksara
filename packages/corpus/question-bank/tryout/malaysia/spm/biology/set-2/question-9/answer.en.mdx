# Heat gained by the water, then share it per gram

The energy from the burning nut is measured as the heat gained by the water. Use the **rise** in temperature, not the final temperature:

<Mermaid
  title={<>Where the Cashew's Energy Goes</>}
  description={<>Part of the energy heats the water by <InlineMath math="2520" /> joules. The rest escapes to the air and the glass, or stays in unburnt soot, which is why the measured value is low.</>}
  chart={"flowchart LR\n  N[Cashew nut 0.50 g] -->|measured| W[Water: 20 g × 4.2 × 30 = 2520 J]\n  N -->|lost| A[Air and glass]\n  N -->|lost| S[Unburnt soot]\n  W --> R[2520 J ÷ 0.50 g = 5.04 kJ per g]"}
/>

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta T&=58-28=30^\circ\text{C}\\
\text{heat}&=m\,c\,\Delta T=20\times4.2\times30=2520\text{ J}
\end{aligned}" />

That heat came from <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ g}" /> of nut, so the energy value per gram is

<BlockMath math="\frac{2520\text{ J}}{0.50\text{ g}}=5040\text{ J g}^{-1}=5.04\text{ kJ g}^{-1}." />

The value is far below <InlineMath math="24\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" /> because much of the heat goes into the air and the glass instead of the water, and some of the nut is left as unburnt soot. So the experiment gives <InlineMath math="5.04\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" />, low because heat escapes and burning is incomplete.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.52\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" /> is the energy of the whole <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ g}" /> nut, not of one gram.
- <InlineMath math="9.74\text{ kJ g}^{-1}" /> uses the final temperature <InlineMath math="58^\circ\text{C}" /> instead of the rise of <InlineMath math="30^\circ\text{C}" />.
- The last option has the right number but the wrong reason: digestion releases **the same total energy** from food as burning. Enzymes only release it in small steps.

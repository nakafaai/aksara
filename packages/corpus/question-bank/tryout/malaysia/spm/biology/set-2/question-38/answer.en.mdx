# Follow the two groups day by day

The antibiotic only removes the bacteria it can kill, so the two groups change **differently**. Track each one, then compare.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{killable}&: 1\,000\,000\xrightarrow{\times0.01}10\,000\xrightarrow{\times0.01}100\\
\text{resistant}&: 100\to100\to100
\end{aligned}" />

After <InlineMath math="2" /> days, <InlineMath math="100" /> of the <InlineMath math="200" /> survivors are resistant, so <InlineMath math="\frac{100}{200}=50\%" />.

Stopping early leaves a population in which half the bacteria are resistant, and they can multiply into an infection the antibiotic cannot treat. So <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of the bacteria still alive are resistant.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.01\%" /> is the share **before** treatment, <InlineMath math="\frac{100}{1\,000\,100}" />.
- <InlineMath math="1\%" /> is roughly the share after one day, <InlineMath math="\frac{100}{10\,100}" />.
- <InlineMath math="99\%" /> assumes the killable bacteria are all gone, but <InlineMath math="100" /> of them are still alive after two days.

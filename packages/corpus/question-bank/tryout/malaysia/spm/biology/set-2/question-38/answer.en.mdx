# Ask what the antibiotic attacks

An antibiotic works on structures that **only bacteria have**, such as the cell wall and bacterial ribosomes. Check each statement against that.

- A virus has no cell wall and makes nothing by itself, so the antibiotic has nothing to attack. Statement I is correct.
- A virus copies itself only by using the machinery inside a host cell. Statement II is correct.
- Stopping early kills the weakest bacteria and leaves the toughest ones to multiply, so resistant bacteria become more common. Statement III is correct.
- The virus uses the host's ribosomes, not its own, so the antibiotic cannot block its proteins and does not cure influenza. Statement IV is wrong.

So I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II misses III, the way an unfinished course **selects resistant bacteria**.
- II and IV accepts IV and rejects I, which mixes up what an antibiotic can reach.
- I, III and IV accepts IV, but antibiotics do not cure a viral infection.

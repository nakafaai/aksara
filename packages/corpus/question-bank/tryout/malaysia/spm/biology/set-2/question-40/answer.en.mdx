# Growth shows where cells lengthen, not where they divide

Each segment started at <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" />, so its growth is its new length minus <InlineMath math="1" />. Cells **divide** in the meristem near the tip, which adds little length at once. They **lengthen** just behind it, which is where the marks spread most. Further back, the cells have stopped growing and are specialising.

- The segments from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="4\text{ mm}" /> grew by <InlineMath math="3.0" /> and <InlineMath math="5.5\text{ mm}" />, far more than any other, so that is the zone of elongation.
- The segments from <InlineMath math="5" /> to <InlineMath math="7\text{ mm}" /> did not grow at all, so their cells have finished lengthening and are differentiating, for example into root hairs and xylem.
- The tip segments grew only <InlineMath math="0.1" /> and <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ mm}" />, which fits a meristem that divides but hardly lengthens.

So the zone of elongation is from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="4\text{ mm}" />, and the zone of differentiation from <InlineMath math="5" /> to <InlineMath math="7\text{ mm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Elongation at <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" /> mistakes the root tip, which **barely grew**, for the fastest-lengthening region.
- Elongation at <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="2\text{ mm}" /> picks the meristem side, and differentiation at <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="4\text{ mm}" /> is where growth peaked.
- The third option swaps the two zones.

# Compare the glucose filtered with the most that can be reabsorbed

The glucose filtered each minute is the filtrate volume times its concentration. Glucose appears in the urine as soon as this load is **more** than the <InlineMath math="375\text{ mg}" /> that the tubules can take back.

The threshold is the concentration at which the filtered load just equals the maximum:

<BlockMath math="125\times c=375\quad\Rightarrow\quad c=\frac{375}{125}=3.0\text{ mg cm}^{-3}." />

At <InlineMath math="4.2\text{ mg cm}^{-3}" />, the filtered load is above the maximum, so the tubules reabsorb their full <InlineMath math="375\text{ mg}" /> and the rest passes into the urine:

<BlockMath math="125\times4.2=525\text{ mg},\qquad 525-375=150\text{ mg per minute}." />

So glucose first appears in the urine above <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ mg cm}^{-3}" />, and at <InlineMath math="4.2\text{ mg cm}^{-3}" /> the urine receives <InlineMath math="150\text{ mg}" /> of glucose per minute.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="525\text{ mg}" /> is the glucose **filtered**, and it forgets that <InlineMath math="375\text{ mg}" /> of it is still reabsorbed.
- <InlineMath math="4.2\text{ mg cm}^{-3}" /> and <InlineMath math="0\text{ mg}" /> assumes the tubules always reabsorb all glucose, which fails once the load passes their maximum.
- <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ mg}" /> subtracts the concentrations, <InlineMath math="4.2-3.0" />, but never multiplies by the <InlineMath math="125\text{ cm}^3" /> filtered each minute.

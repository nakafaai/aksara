# Subtract the dead air from every breath

Total air per minute is the same for both people: <InlineMath math="500\times12=250\times24=6000\text{ cm}^3" />. But the <InlineMath math="150\text{ cm}^3" /> that stays in the airways is lost **in every breath**, so it must be subtracted per breath before multiplying by the number of breaths.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{P}&:\ (500-150)\times12=350\times12=4200\text{ cm}^3\text{ per minute}\\
\text{Q}&:\ (250-150)\times24=100\times24=2400\text{ cm}^3\text{ per minute}
\end{aligned}" />

<InlineMath math="4200-2400=1800" />, so P delivers <InlineMath math="1800\text{ cm}^3" /> more fresh air to the alveoli per minute. Fast, shallow breathing wastes more air in the airways because the loss is paid twice as often.

# Why the other options are wrong

- "Neither" **compares only the total air per minute** and forgets the <InlineMath math="150\text{ cm}^3" /> lost in every breath.
- Q by <InlineMath math="1800\text{ cm}^3" /> has the right difference but the wrong person. The faster breather loses more to the airways.
- P by <InlineMath math="3600\text{ cm}^3" /> subtracts the dead air for Q but not for P, comparing <InlineMath math="6000" /> with <InlineMath math="2400" />.

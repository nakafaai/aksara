- In X, potassium hydroxide absorbs every bit of carbon dioxide, so the liquid moves only because oxygen is taken in. **Oxygen taken in** <InlineMath math="=30" /> units.
- In Y, carbon dioxide stays in the air. The liquid moves by oxygen taken in **minus** carbon dioxide released, so <InlineMath math="30-\text{CO}_2=9" />, which gives **carbon dioxide released** <InlineMath math="=21" /> units.

<BlockMath math="\text{RQ}=\frac{21}{30}=0.70" />

An RQ of about <InlineMath math="0.7" /> is the value for **lipid**, because lipid molecules hold little oxygen, so more oxygen is needed per carbon dioxide released. Carbohydrate gives <InlineMath math="1.0" />, and protein gives about <InlineMath math="0.9" />. So the RQ is <InlineMath math="0.70" />, and the seeds respire mainly lipid, as oily seeds do.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.30" /> uses the <InlineMath math="9\text{ mm}" /> of Y as the carbon dioxide volume, but that movement is the **difference** between oxygen in and carbon dioxide out.
- <InlineMath math="1.00" /> would need Y not to move at all, because then the carbon dioxide released would equal the oxygen taken in.
- <InlineMath math="1.43" /> inverts the ratio, oxygen over carbon dioxide.

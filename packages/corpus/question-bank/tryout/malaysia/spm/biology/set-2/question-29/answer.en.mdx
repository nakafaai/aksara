- Together, X levels off at <InlineMath math="160" /> instead of <InlineMath math="200" />, and Y dies out instead of reaching <InlineMath math="120" />. **Both do worse together**, which is the mark of interspecific competition for the shared bacteria. Statement I is supported.
- X survives the contest and Y is pushed out, so X gets the limited food more efficiently. Statement II is supported.
- Nothing shows X eating Y. Both species eat bacteria, and X also does worse with Y present, which predation would not cause. Statement III is not supported.
- Alone, each curve rises and then levels off although nothing else lives in the tube. The limit comes from members of the same species sharing a fixed food supply, which is intraspecific competition. Statement IV is supported.

So I, II and IV are supported.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II misses IV, the levelling off of each population grown alone.
- I, II and III accepts predation, but **X loses numbers too** when Y is present.
- II, III and IV rejects I, the competition that the two-species tube shows.

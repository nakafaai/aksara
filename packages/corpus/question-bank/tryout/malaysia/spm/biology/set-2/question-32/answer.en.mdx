# Let the first child reveal the parents' genotypes

Group O is <InlineMath math="I^{O}I^{O}" /> (also written <InlineMath math="ii" />), so the first child received <InlineMath math="I^{O}" /> from **each** parent. A group A father carrying <InlineMath math="I^{O}" /> must be <InlineMath math="I^{A}I^{O}" />, and the mother must be <InlineMath math="I^{B}I^{O}" />.

<Mermaid
  title={<>The Cross <InlineMath math="I^{A}I^{O}\times I^{B}I^{O}" /></>}
  description={<>Each parent passes one of two alleles with equal chance, so the four children AB, A, B and O each have a chance of one in four.</>}
  chart={"flowchart TD\n  F[Father A: IA IO] --> AB[IA IB: AB, 1/4]\n  F --> AO[IA IO: A, 1/4]\n  M[Mother B: IB IO] --> AB\n  M --> BO[IB IO: B, 1/4]\n  F --> OO[IO IO: O, 1/4]\n  M --> AO\n  M --> OO\n  F --> BO"}
/>

Each parent passes either allele with a chance of one half, so <InlineMath math="P(I^{A}I^{B})=\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{4}" />. Sex is decided independently, with <InlineMath math="P(\text{girl})=\frac{1}{2}" />:

<BlockMath math="P(\text{girl with AB})=\frac{1}{4}\times\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{8}." />

The first child does not change the next one's chances. It only proved the genotypes. So the probability is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{8}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{4}" /> is the chance of group AB alone and **forgets the chance of a girl**.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}" /> is only the chance of a girl.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{32}" /> ignores what the first child proves and treats each parent as heterozygous with a chance of one half.

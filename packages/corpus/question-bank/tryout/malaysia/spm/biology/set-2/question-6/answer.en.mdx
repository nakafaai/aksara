- In phase S of interphase the DNA **replicates**, so it rises from <InlineMath math="12\text{ pg}" /> to <InlineMath math="24\text{ pg}" />. Each chromosome now has two chromatids.
- Meiosis I separates the **homologous chromosomes** into two cells, so each cell gets half: <InlineMath math="12\text{ pg}" />. The chromosomes still have two chromatids each, and nothing replicates before meiosis II.
- A cell at metaphase II is in this stage, so it holds <InlineMath math="12\text{ pg}" />.
- Meiosis II separates the **sister chromatids**, so each sperm gets half again: <InlineMath math="6\text{ pg}" />.

So a cell at metaphase II has <InlineMath math="12\text{ pg}" /> of DNA and a sperm has <InlineMath math="6\text{ pg}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6" /> and <InlineMath math="3\text{ pg}" /> **forgets the replication in phase S** and halves the original <InlineMath math="12\text{ pg}" /> twice.
- <InlineMath math="24" /> and <InlineMath math="12\text{ pg}" /> treats metaphase II as if meiosis I had not yet divided the DNA.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> and <InlineMath math="12\text{ pg}" /> forgets that meiosis II separates the chromatids and halves the DNA again.

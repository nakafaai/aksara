# Compare the energy caught as ATP with the energy in the glucose

Each mole of ATP holds <InlineMath math="30.5\text{ kJ}" />, so the **share stored** is the ATP made times <InlineMath math="30.5" />, divided by the <InlineMath math="2870\text{ kJ}" /> in glucose. Whatever is not caught as ATP is either lost as heat or left inside the products.

<BlockMath math="\text{aerobic}: \frac{30\times30.5}{2870}=\frac{915}{2870}\approx32\%,\qquad \text{anaerobic}: \frac{2\times30.5}{2870}=\frac{61}{2870}\approx2\%" />

- I: <InlineMath math="915" /> of <InlineMath math="2870\text{ kJ}" /> is about <InlineMath math="32\%" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="61" /> of <InlineMath math="2870\text{ kJ}" /> is about <InlineMath math="2\%" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: lactic acid is still a rich fuel, so most of the energy of glucose stays locked in it. The liver later turns it back into glucose when oxygen is available. Statement III is correct.
- IV: carbon dioxide is a waste product with little energy in it. The <InlineMath math="68\%" /> not caught as ATP is released as **heat**, which keeps the body warm. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, as if **anaerobic respiration released all the energy** and lost it as heat.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and accepts IV, **sending the unused energy into carbon dioxide instead of heat**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and accepts IV, **although <InlineMath math="2" /> ATP hold only <InlineMath math="61\text{ kJ}" />**.

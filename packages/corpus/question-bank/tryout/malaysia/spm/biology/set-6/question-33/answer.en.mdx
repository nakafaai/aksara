# Follow the heterozygotes, since only they produce new homozygotes

A homozygote that selfs gives only its own kind. An <InlineMath math="Aa" /> plant that selfs gives <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}AA" />, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}Aa" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}aa" />, so **each generation halves the heterozygotes** and shares the lost half equally between the two homozygotes.

<BlockMath math="Aa\text{ after four generations}: \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^4=\tfrac{1}{16},\qquad aa: \frac{1-\tfrac{1}{16}}{2}=\frac{15}{32}" />

After four generations only <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{16}" /> of the plants are heterozygous, and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{15}{32}" />, almost half, are <InlineMath math="aa" /> and show the recessive phenotype. This is why selfing quickly produces true-breeding lines.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{16}" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" /> keeps the recessive share at **the first generation's <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{4}" />**, although every generation adds more <InlineMath math="aa" />.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{8}" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{7}{16}" /> is the result after **three generations**, not four.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" /> treats every generation as a fresh <InlineMath math="Aa\times Aa" /> **cross**, ignoring that the homozygotes breed true.

# Count halvings of light, then count how many halvings reach <InlineMath math="1\%" />

Each layer of water absorbs **the same fraction** of the light reaching it, so light halves over every equal step of depth. Reaching <InlineMath math="1\%" /> means dividing the light by <InlineMath math="100" />, which takes about <InlineMath math="6.64" /> halvings.

<BlockMath math="6\text{ m}=3\text{ halvings}: \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^3=12.5\%,\qquad 6.64\times2\approx13\text{ m},\qquad 6.64\times1\approx6.6\text{ m}" />

- I: <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" /> is three halvings of <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" />, leaving <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{8}" />, which is <InlineMath math="12.5\%" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: dividing by <InlineMath math="100" /> takes <InlineMath math="6.64" /> halvings, and at <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> each that is about <InlineMath math="13\text{ m}" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: if each halving takes only <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" />, the same <InlineMath math="6.64" /> halvings are reached at about <InlineMath math="6.6\text{ m}" />, half the depth. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the first <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> remove <InlineMath math="50" /> percentage points of light, but the step from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="14\text{ m}" /> removes less than <InlineMath math="1" />. Light falls by the same **fraction**, not the same amount. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although halving the step halves the depth**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **has light fall by equal amounts**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **missing the <InlineMath math="6.64" /> halvings to reach <InlineMath math="1\%" />**.

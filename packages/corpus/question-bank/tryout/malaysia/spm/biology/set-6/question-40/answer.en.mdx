# Follow the sewage downstream and watch who uses the oxygen

Sewage feeds **decomposing bacteria**, which multiply and use dissolved oxygen as they break the organic matter down. The oxygen dips where they are most active and returns once the food runs out.

The bacteria multiply on the sewage during that time, so oxygen use peaks some way downstream, not at the outfall. Statement II is correct.
- III: mayfly nymphs vanish exactly where oxygen is low and return at <InlineMath math="20\text{ km}" /> as oxygen recovers, while the sewage is still in the water. They need oxygen-rich water, so the lack of oxygen, not poison, keeps them away. Sludge worms, which tolerate low oxygen, are abundant there instead. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: <InlineMath math="2" /> out of <InlineMath math="9\text{ mg/L}" /> is about <InlineMath math="22\%" />, more than a fifth, not less than a tenth. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

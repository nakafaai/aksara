# Match each ripening time to the ethylene in that box

Ethylene is a gas, so it builds up only where it cannot escape. Each box changes **one thing about the gas**, and the ripening time follows the ethylene level, not the mere presence of the apple.

- I: with the apple in a closed box ethylene reaches <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ ppm}" /> and ripening takes only <InlineMath math="4" /> days. Statement I is correct.
- II: in the open box the apple's ethylene drifts away, reaching only <InlineMath math="0.2\text{ ppm}" />. Slow ripening there fits ethylene being the cause, and the absorber result confirms it. Statement II is wrong.
- III: from <InlineMath math="0.1" /> to <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ ppm}" /> the ethylene rises tenfold and ripening falls from <InlineMath math="8" /> to <InlineMath math="4" /> days. One more tenfold rise, to <InlineMath math="10\text{ ppm}" />, would halve it again to about <InlineMath math="2" /> days. Statement III is correct.
- IV: ethylene speeds the changes of ripening, including starch turning to sugar and the fruit softening. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **ignoring that the gas escapes from an open box**.
- III and IV only rejects I and **has ethylene slow the ripening it speeds up**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **although ethylene hastens starch breakdown**.

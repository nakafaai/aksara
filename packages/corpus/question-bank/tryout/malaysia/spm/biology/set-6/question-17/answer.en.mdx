# Multiply each share by the output it is a share of

A share of <InlineMath math="5\text{ L}" /> and a share of <InlineMath math="25\text{ L}" /> are shares of different totals. Turn each into **litres per minute** before comparing rest with exercise.

<BlockMath math="\text{gut}: 1.25\to0.75,\qquad \text{kidneys}: 0.20\times5=1\to0.02\times25=0.5" />
</MathContainer>

- I: the share rises about four times, but the flow rises from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="21\text{ L}" /> per minute, about <InlineMath math="21" /> times, because the total output is also five times larger. Statement I is wrong.
- II: <InlineMath math="15\%" /> of <InlineMath math="5" /> and <InlineMath math="3\%" /> of <InlineMath math="25" /> are both <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ L}" /> per minute, so the brain's supply is protected. Statement II is correct.
- III: gut flow falls from <InlineMath math="1.25" /> to <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ L}" /> per minute even though the heart pumps more, so its arterioles must narrow and divert blood to the muscles. Statement III is correct.
- IV: kidney flow falls from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ L}" /> per minute, a half. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts I, **comparing shares instead of litres**, and rejects IV.
- I and IV only rejects II and III, **although the brain keeps <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ L}" /> and the gut loses flow**.
- II and IV only rejects III, **the arteriole narrowing that cuts gut flow**.

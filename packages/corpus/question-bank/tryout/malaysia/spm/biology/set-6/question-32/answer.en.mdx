# Work out the daughter's genotype first, then her children

A father passes his only X chromosome to **every daughter**. So every daughter of the affected man carries <InlineMath math="X^R" />, and since her mother is unaffected, she is <InlineMath math="X^RX^r" />.

<BlockMath math="X^RY\times X^rX^r\ \Rightarrow\ \text{every daughter }X^RX^r" />
<BlockMath math="X^RX^r\times X^rY:\quad X^RX^r,\ X^rX^r,\ X^RY,\ X^rY\ \Rightarrow\ P(X^RY)=\tfrac{1}{4}" />

The daughter passes <InlineMath math="X^R" /> to half her children, and half her children are sons, so an affected son has a chance of <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}\times\tfrac{1}{2}=\tfrac{1}{4}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" /> is the chance that **a son** is affected, not the chance that the first child is an affected son.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> assumes sons cannot be affected, mixing up the **father-to-daughter** rule with the mother, who passes an X to sons too.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{8}" /> halves again, as if the daughter were **affected only half the time**, although every daughter of the man carries <InlineMath math="X^R" />.

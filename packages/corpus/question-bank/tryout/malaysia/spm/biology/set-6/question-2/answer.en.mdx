# Turn each percentage into a volume before comparing cells

A percentage compares an organelle with **its own cell**. To compare two cells of different sizes, multiply each share by the cell's volume first.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{rough ER}: P\ 0.25\times400=100,\qquad R\ 0.05\times500=25,\qquad \frac{100}{25}=4" />
<BlockMath math="\text{mitochondria}: Q\ 0.35\times2000=700,\qquad P\ 0.05\times400=20,\qquad \frac{700}{20}=35" />
</MathContainer>

- I: a quarter of cell P is rough ER, where ribosomes make proteins for export, such as antibodies. Statement I is correct.
- II: cell R has <InlineMath math="12\%" /> lysosomes, far more than the others. Lysosomes hold the enzymes that digest engulfed bacteria. Statement II is correct.
- III: cell P has <InlineMath math="100" /> and cell R <InlineMath math="25\text{ µm}^3" /> of rough ER, four times as much. Statement III is correct.
- IV: comparing <InlineMath math="35\%" /> with <InlineMath math="5\%" /> gives <InlineMath math="7" />, but cell Q is five times larger. In volume it has <InlineMath math="700" /> against <InlineMath math="20\text{ µm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="35" /> times. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although <InlineMath math="100" /> against <InlineMath math="25" /> is four times**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **compares percentages instead of volumes** in IV.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **missing that lysosomes mark a phagocyte**.

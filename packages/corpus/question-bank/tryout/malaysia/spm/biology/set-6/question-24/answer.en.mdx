# Follow the sugar from source to sink, and see what happens when the sink goes

Leaves are **sources** and fruits are **sinks**. Sucrose moves in the phloem from source to sink, so removing a large sink slows export, and the sugar backs up in the leaf.

- I: export falls from <InlineMath math="15" /> to <InlineMath math="4" />, a cut of about <InlineMath math="73\%" />. Only about a quarter of the export **remains**. Statement I is wrong.
- II: starch goes from <InlineMath math="0.5" /> to <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mg}" /> per square centimetre, four times, and photosynthesis from <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="12" />, a fall of <InlineMath math="\tfrac{8}{20}=40\%" />. Sugar the leaf cannot export piles up and slows it down. Statement II is correct.
- III: the fruits were taking most of the sucrose, and without them export falls sharply, so they acted as sinks drawing sucrose through the phloem. Statement III is correct.
- IV: export falls to <InlineMath math="4" /> and photosynthesis falls, so the sucrose is not simply sent elsewhere. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts I, **reading the quarter that remains as the quarter that is lost**.
- III and IV only rejects II and **claims photosynthesis is unchanged**, although it falls from <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="12" />.
- I and II only accepts I and rejects III, **the role of the fruits as sinks**.

# Follow the heat into the sweat, and ask what the body would do without it

Only **evaporation** carries heat away, because breaking the hydrogen bonds that hold liquid water together takes energy from the skin. The heat capacity of the body then shows how dangerous it would be to keep that heat.

- I: <InlineMath math="2400\text{ kJ}" /> at <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ kJ}" /> per gram needs <InlineMath math="1000\text{ g}" />, about a litre of sweat. Statement I is correct.
- II: the body would warm by about <InlineMath math="9.5\,^\circ\text{C}" />, enough to be fatal, but not <InlineMath math="40" />. The figure <InlineMath math="40" /> comes from dividing <InlineMath math="2400" /> by <InlineMath math="60" /> and **leaving out the <InlineMath math="4.2\text{ kJ}" />** per kilogram per degree. Statement II is wrong.
- III: hydrogen bonds hold water molecules together, so water takes much heat to warm and even more to turn into vapour. Statement III is correct.
- IV: dripping sweat leaves as liquid and takes almost no heat with it. Only evaporation absorbs the <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ kJ}" /> per gram. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts <InlineMath math="40\,^\circ\text{C}" />, **dividing by the mass without the heat capacity**.
- III and IV only rejects the sweat calculation and **lets dripping sweat cool the body**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **although cooling comes from evaporation alone**.

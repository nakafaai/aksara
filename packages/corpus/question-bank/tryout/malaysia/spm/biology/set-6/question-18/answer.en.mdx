# Take the difference across the liver, then times the flow

What the liver takes or gives is the **difference between blood in and blood out**, multiplied by the blood flow. The concentration entering is not what the liver keeps.

- I: the liver keeps the difference, <InlineMath math="12-7=5\text{ mmol}" /> per minute. The <InlineMath math="12" /> is what arrives, not what is removed. Statement I is wrong.
- II: <InlineMath math="5\text{ mmol}" /> per minute for <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes is <InlineMath math="150\text{ mmol}" />, or <InlineMath math="27\text{ g}" />, which insulin makes the liver store as glycogen. Statement II is correct.
- III: after the fast, blood leaves richer than it entered, so the liver is adding glucose. Glucagon triggers the breakdown of glycogen to supply it. Statement III is correct.
- IV: insulin lowers blood glucose by making the liver **store** it. Release is triggered by glucagon. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts I, **taking the glucose that arrives as the glucose removed**.
- III and IV only rejects II and **swaps the roles of insulin and glucagon**.
- I and II only accepts I and rejects III, **missing the release after the fast**.

# Read the plateau as a count of carriers

Each carrier protein can move only so many molecules per second. Once the glucose concentration is high enough to keep **every carrier busy**, adding more glucose cannot speed uptake up. The height of the plateau therefore measures how many carriers are in the membrane.

- I: uptake rises steeply at low glucose, then stops rising at <InlineMath math="12" />, because all carriers are working at full speed. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12}{4}=\tfrac{24}{8}=\tfrac{33}{11}=\tfrac{36}{12}=3" /> at every concentration. Three times as many carriers, each working as before, would triple uptake at every concentration, not just at the plateau. Statement II is correct.
- III: the rate is already the same at <InlineMath math="20" /> and <InlineMath math="40\text{ mM}" />. With the carriers saturated, more glucose changes nothing. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: uptake continues when ATP production is blocked, so it needs no energy from the cell. It is **facilitated diffusion**, which also levels off when its carriers are full. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts III, **although the curve is already flat from <InlineMath math="20" /> mM**.
- II and IV only rejects I and takes a plateau as proof of **active transport, which needs ATP**.
- I and IV only rejects II and accepts IV, **ignoring the blocked ATP**.

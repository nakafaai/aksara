# Remove the seeds, then put back only their hormone

If growth stops when the achenes go and returns when **auxin alone** is added, the achenes must work by supplying auxin. The strip shows how far that auxin reaches.

- I: without achenes the fruit stays at <InlineMath math="1\text{ g}" />, against <InlineMath math="20\text{ g}" /> with them, so the achenes, through their developing seeds, cause the swelling. Statement I is correct.
- II: paste gives <InlineMath math="17\text{ g}" /> against <InlineMath math="20\text{ g}" /> with achenes, so growth is restored only to <InlineMath math="85\%" />, not fully. The achenes may supply more than the single dose of auxin in the paste, such as a steady supply or other hormones. Statement II is wrong.
- III: flesh swells only under the remaining strip, so the auxin does not spread far from the achenes that make it. Statement III is correct.
- IV: mass is proportional to the fertilised achenes, so <InlineMath math="120" /> of <InlineMath math="200" /> gives <InlineMath math="60\%" /> of <InlineMath math="20\text{ g}" />, which is <InlineMath math="12\text{ g}" />. Fewer seeds supply less auxin, so less of the receptacle swells. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **although <InlineMath math="17\text{ g}" /> falls short of <InlineMath math="20\text{ g}" />**, and rejects IV.
- II and IV only rejects I and III, **the experiment's main finding and the strip result**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts II, **treating <InlineMath math="85\%" /> of the mass as full growth**.

# Multiply the levels of folding, then remove one level

Each level folds the surface made by the level before, so the three factors **multiply**. Flattening the villi removes their factor of <InlineMath math="10" /> and leaves a tenth of the area.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\pi\times0.025\times6\approx0.47\text{ m}^2,\qquad 0.47\times3\times10\times20=0.47\times600\approx280\text{ m}^2" />
<BlockMath math="\text{villi flattened}: \frac{280}{10}=28\text{ m}^2,\qquad \frac{280-28}{280}=90\%\text{ lost}" />
</MathContainer>

The healthy intestine absorbs through about <InlineMath math="280\text{ m}^2" />, roughly the floor of a tennis court. With flat villi only <InlineMath math="28\text{ m}^2" /> remain, so about <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of the surface is lost, which explains the poor absorption in coeliac disease.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="280\\text{ m}^2" /> with <InlineMath math="10\%" /> lost confuses **falling to a tenth** with **falling by a tenth**.
- About <InlineMath math="16\\text{ m}^2" /> with <InlineMath math="30\%" /> lost **adds the factors**, <InlineMath math="3+10+20=33" />, instead of multiplying them.
- About <InlineMath math="94\\text{ m}^2" /> **leaves out the circular folds**, multiplying only by <InlineMath math="10\times20=200" />.

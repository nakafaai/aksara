# Turn every percentage into a number of cells

A percentage only shares out the white cells of **that sample**. When the total changes, compare actual numbers of cells per cubic millimetre.

<BlockMath math="\text{Y}: 9000\times0.35=3150,\quad 9000\times0.60=5400,\qquad \text{normal}: 4200,\ 2100" />

- I: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{12\,750}{4200}\approx3" />, so X has about three times the normal neutrophils. Statement I is correct.
- II: Y has <InlineMath math="3150" /> neutrophils against a normal <InlineMath math="4200" />, fewer, even though its total white count is higher. Statement II is correct.
- III: a big rise in phagocytes, as in X, is typical of bacteria. A rise in lymphocytes, as in Y, is typical of a virus, which is fought by antibodies and by killing infected cells. Statement III is correct.
- IV: X's lymphocyte **percentage** falls to a third, from <InlineMath math="30" /> to <InlineMath math="10" />, but the number falls only from <InlineMath math="2100" /> to <InlineMath math="1500" />, about <InlineMath math="71\%" /> of normal. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, **reading Y's high total as more neutrophils**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and accepts IV, **comparing percentages instead of counts** in both.
- I, II, III and IV accepts IV, **although 1500 is not a third of 2100**.

# Split the air per minute into breaths per minute and size of each breath

Air breathed per minute is the **breathing rate times the volume of each breath**, so dividing one by the other gives the rate. Comparing how much each factor grows shows which drives the rise.

<BlockMath math="\text{rate}: \frac{6}{0.50}=12,\quad \frac{9}{0.75}=12,\quad \frac{15}{1.00}=15,\quad \frac{30}{1.50}=20\ \text{breaths/min}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{from }0\%\text{ to }6\%: \text{rate}\times\frac{20}{12}\approx1.7,\qquad \text{depth}\times\frac{1.50}{0.50}=3,\qquad 1.7\times3=5=\frac{30}{6}" />
</MathContainer>

- I: the rate goes from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="20" /> breaths per minute, about <InlineMath math="1.7" /> times, not double. Statement I is wrong.
- II: each breath grows from <InlineMath math="0.50" /> to <InlineMath math="1.50\text{ L}" />, three times as deep. Statement II is correct.
- III: carbon dioxide forms carbonic acid in the blood. Chemoreceptors detect the fall in pH, and the medulla oblongata raises the rate and depth of breathing. Statement III is correct.
- IV: depth rises <InlineMath math="3" /> times and rate only about <InlineMath math="1.7" /> times, so deeper breaths contribute more. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts I, often after **dividing <InlineMath math="30\text{ L}" /> by <InlineMath math="6\text{ L}" /> and calling it the rate**, and rejects IV.
- I and IV only rejects II and III, **although both follow from the table and the carbon dioxide reflex**.
- II and IV only rejects III, **the chemoreceptor route that explains the response**.

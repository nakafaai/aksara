# Compare the benefit of the bacteria in each soil

The rhizobia supply fixed nitrogen. That help matters only where **nitrogen is what limits growth**, so compare with and without bacteria within each soil.

- I: plants go from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="10\text{ g}" />, which is five times heavier, not four. The difference of <InlineMath math="8\text{ g}" /> is four times the starting mass, but the plants become five times as heavy. Statement I is wrong.
- II: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{60}{2}=30" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{300}{10}=30\text{ mg}" /> of nitrogen per gram, the same in both. Each plant built as much tissue as its nitrogen allowed, and in rich soil the bacteria add nothing. Statement II is correct.
- III: the bacteria turn nitrogen gas into ammonium for the plant, and the plant supplies them with sugars and a home in the nodules. Both gain, which is mutualism. Statement III is correct.
- IV: both groups take the same <InlineMath math="60\text{ mg}" /> from the soil, so the extra <InlineMath math="240\text{ mg}" /> in plants with rhizobia came from fixation, which is <InlineMath math="80\%" /> of their <InlineMath math="300\text{ mg}" />. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts four times, **taking the gain of <InlineMath math="8\text{ g}" /> as the factor**, and rejects IV.
- I and IV only rejects II and III, **the equal nitrogen per gram and the mutualism itself**.
- II and IV only rejects III, **although both partners benefit**.

# Follow the calcium to wherever the water goes

Calcium rides in the xylem with the water drawn up by transpiration, and it **barely moves in the phloem**. So each organ receives calcium in step with the water it loses. Leaves lose most, fruit least.

- I: leaf calcium doubles when transpiration rises from <InlineMath math="200" /> to <InlineMath math="500\text{ g}" /> a day, so calcium travels with the water in the xylem. Statement I is correct.
- II: in dry air the leaves gain calcium, but the fruit drop from <InlineMath math="1.2" /> to <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ mg}" /> per gram, half as much. The leaves draw the xylem stream away from the fruit. Statement II is wrong.
- III: <InlineMath math="30\%" /> against <InlineMath math="5\%" /> is six times as common. Statement III is correct.
- IV: since calcium cannot be moved on through the phloem, fruit depend on their own transpiration, which is small, so they run short. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **although fruit calcium halves**, and rejects IV.
- II and IV only rejects I and III, **the xylem route and the sixfold rise in rot**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts II, **missing that the leaves take calcium away from the fruit**.

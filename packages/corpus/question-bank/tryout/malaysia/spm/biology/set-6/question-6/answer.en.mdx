# Double the choices for every pair, then multiply the two parents

Each pair can line up two ways, independently of the others, so <InlineMath math="n" /> pairs give <InlineMath math="2^n" /> kinds of gamete. Any gamete from one parent can meet any gamete from the other, so the **two numbers multiply**.

<BlockMath math="2^4=16,\qquad 16\times16=256,\qquad 2^{23}=8\,388\,608" />

- I: four pairs give <InlineMath math="2^4=16" /> kinds of gamete. Statement I is correct.
- II: each of <InlineMath math="16" /> eggs can meet each of <InlineMath math="16" /> sperm, so there are <InlineMath math="16\times16=256" /> combinations, not <InlineMath math="16+16=32" />. Statement II is wrong.
- III: <InlineMath math="2^{23}" /> is about <InlineMath math="8.4" /> million. Statement III is correct.
- IV: crossing over swaps pieces between homologous chromosomes, making new combinations of alleles on each chromosome, so the true number of gametes is far larger. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only adds the two parents' gametes in II, **although combinations multiply**, and rejects IV.
- II and IV only rejects I and III, **the doubling with each pair**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts <InlineMath math="32" />, **which adds where it should multiply**.

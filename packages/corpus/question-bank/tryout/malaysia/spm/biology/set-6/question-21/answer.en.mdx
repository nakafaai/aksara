# Add the growth of every meristem working at the same time

An apical meristem adds length only at the tip. **Intercalary meristems** sit along the stem, one at the base of each internode, so their growth adds up.

- I: forty internodes each adding <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ cm}" /> give <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}" />, about <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}" /> a day. Statement I is correct.
- II: a single meristem adding <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ cm}" /> a day needs <InlineMath math="40" /> days to add <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: removing the tip removes the apical meristem, but the intercalary meristems lower down keep lengthening their internodes. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: grasses keep meristems at the base of their leaves, so grazing removes old tips while the growing zones survive. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the same base-of-leaf meristems that let grass regrow**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and accepts III, **as if all growth came from the tip**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III, **although the intercalary meristems are left behind**.

# Count the ions per ATP, then per second

Each ATP moves **five ions**, three out and two in. Multiply by the ATP used per second to get the totals, and compare the charges going each way.

- I: three positive ions leave and two enter, so each cycle carries one positive charge out, leaving the inside more negative. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="5" /> ions per ATP times <InlineMath math="2\times10^6" /> ATP is <InlineMath math="10^7" /> ions a second. The value <InlineMath math="5\times10^6" /> forgets to multiply by the <InlineMath math="2" /> million. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the pump needs ATP from respiration. Without it, sodium leaks back in and potassium leaks out, and the gradients slowly disappear. Statement III is correct.
- IV: sodium is pumped out to where it is already more concentrated, and potassium in to where it is more concentrated. Both move **against** their gradients, which is why ATP is needed. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **counting the ions for one ATP times a million instead of two million**.
- III and IV only rejects I and **lets the pump work downhill**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **although the pump spends ATP to move ions uphill**.

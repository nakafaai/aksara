# Count the halvings for each enzyme in its own time units

Heat denatures enzyme molecules one by one, so the active share **halves at a steady rate**. Count how many halving times fit into the heating time.

- I: five halvings leave <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{32}" />, about <InlineMath math="3\%" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="6" /> hours is **two** halving times of <InlineMath math="3" /> hours, so a quarter is left, not half. Statement II is wrong.
- III: an enzyme's shape is held by bonds within the folded chain. More of these bonds make the active site harder to shake out of shape. Statement III is correct.
- IV: surviving heat says nothing about speed at <InlineMath math="37\,^\circ\text{C}" />. An enzyme adapted to <InlineMath math="80\,^\circ\text{C}" /> is usually stiff and slow at body temperature. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **counting <InlineMath math="6" /> hours as one halving instead of two**.
- III and IV only rejects the **five halvings** in I and accepts IV.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **mixing heat stability with speed at <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" />**.

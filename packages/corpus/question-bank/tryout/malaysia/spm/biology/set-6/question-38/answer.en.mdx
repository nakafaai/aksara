# Compare the times to market size, then ask how the gene is kept on the farm

The modified fish grow faster because their extra gene **never switches off in winter**. Farms rely on sterility so that the gene cannot reach wild fish.

- I: the modified fish take <InlineMath math="18" /> months and ordinary fish <InlineMath math="36" />, so the time is halved, not cut to a third. Statement I is wrong.
- II: ordinary salmon slow their growth in cold months as growth hormone falls. The added switch keeps the extra gene active, so growth continues all year. Statement II is correct.
- III: in meiosis homologous chromosomes pair up. With three of each, they cannot be shared evenly, so the fish make no working eggs. Statement III is correct.
- IV: escaped fertile fish could mate with wild salmon and pass the gene on, which is why farms use sterile fish. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts a third, **although <InlineMath math="18" /> is half of <InlineMath math="36" />**, and rejects IV.
- I and IV only rejects II and III, **the reason for faster growth and the reason for sterility**.
- II and IV only rejects III, **although uneven pairing is why triploid fish are sterile**.

# Divide by mass before comparing animals of different sizes

Total oxygen use grows with size, but each kilogram of a small animal works much harder. Divide by body mass to see the **rate per kilogram**.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{mouse}: \frac{0.06}{0.02}=3,\qquad \text{human}: \frac{17.5}{70}=0.25,\qquad \text{elephant}: \frac{280}{4000}=0.07\ \text{L per kg per hour}" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{3}{0.25}=12,\qquad \frac{0.07}{3}\approx\frac{1}{43}" />
</MathContainer>

- I: <InlineMath math="3" /> against <InlineMath math="0.25" /> litres per kilogram per hour is <InlineMath math="12" /> times. Statement I is correct.
- II: the elephant uses <InlineMath math="280" /> litres an hour, sixteen times the human's <InlineMath math="17.5" />. It uses less only **per kilogram**. Statement II is wrong.
- III: a small body has much more surface for each unit of volume, so it loses heat quickly and its cells must respire faster to replace it. Statement III is correct.
- IV: <InlineMath math="0.07" /> against <InlineMath math="3" /> is about <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{43}" />, roughly a fortieth. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **confusing total use with use per kilogram**, and rejects IV.
- II and IV only rejects I and III, **the per-kilogram comparison and the heat-loss reason**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts II, **although <InlineMath math="280\text{ L}" /> is far more than <InlineMath math="17.5\text{ L}" />**.

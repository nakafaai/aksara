# Compare each zone with its own threshold

Each antibiotic has its own threshold, because antibiotics **spread through agar at different speeds**. A zone means something only when it is set against the value for that antibiotic.

- I: area grows with the square of the diameter, so the whole <InlineMath math="24\text{ mm}" /> zone has <InlineMath math="16" /> times the area of the <InlineMath math="6\text{ mm}" /> disc. Taking away the disc leaves <InlineMath math="15" /> times its area of clear agar. Penicillin, by contrast, clears nothing beyond its disc, so the bacteria resist it. Statement I is correct.
- II: tetracycline clears <InlineMath math="24\text{ mm}" />, comfortably above its threshold of <InlineMath math="19" />, while streptomycin only just reaches its threshold. Statement II is correct.
- III: area depends on the square of the diameter, so doubling the diameter gives **four** times the area. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: a small molecule may spread far through agar without being more effective in the body. That is why each antibiotic has its own threshold. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts III, **scaling area with the diameter instead of its square**.
- II and IV only rejects I, **missing that the disc's own area must be taken away**, and ranks antibiotics by zone width alone.
- I and IV only rejects II and **ignores the different thresholds**.

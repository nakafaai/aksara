# Find the factor in shortest supply at each time

The rate is set by **whichever factor is scarcest**. At noon light is plentiful but carbon dioxide inside the leaf is low. At dusk carbon dioxide has built up again, but light is weak.

- I: from 9:00 to noon light rises to <InlineMath math="2000" />, yet the stomata close from <InlineMath math="90" /> to <InlineMath math="30" />, carbon dioxide inside falls to <InlineMath math="180\text{ ppm}" /> and photosynthesis drops from <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="8" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: at 9:00 the rate is <InlineMath math="20" /> with an opening of <InlineMath math="90" />, about <InlineMath math="0.22" /> per unit, and at noon <InlineMath math="8" /> with an opening of <InlineMath math="30" />, about <InlineMath math="0.27" /> per unit. Each unit of open pore does slightly more, because the bright light drives photosynthesis hard, yet the total still falls. Statement II is correct.
- III: at noon the air is hottest and driest, so open stomata would lose water fastest. Closing them protects the plant from wilting. Statement III is correct.
- IV: at 18:00 carbon dioxide is back at <InlineMath math="350\text{ ppm}" /> but light has fallen to <InlineMath math="200" />, so light now limits the rate. Statement IV is correct.

So all four statements are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only rejects IV, **although carbon dioxide has recovered and only light is low**.
- I and III only rejects II and IV, **the rate per unit of opening and the light limit at dusk**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **the stomatal closure that explains the noon dip**.

# Undo the square to turn each distance into a time

The ruler speeds up as it falls, so distance grows with the **square of the time**. To get the time, divide the distance by <InlineMath math="5" /> and take the square root.

<BlockMath math="t=\sqrt{\frac{d}{5}}:\quad \sqrt{\frac{0.20}{5}}=0.20\text{ s},\qquad \sqrt{\frac{0.32}{5}}\approx0.253\text{ s},\qquad \sqrt{\frac{0.15}{5}}\approx0.173\text{ s}" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{0.32}{0.20}=1.6,\qquad \frac{0.253}{0.200}\approx1.26" />

- I: <InlineMath math="\sqrt{0.04}=0.2" /> seconds. Statement I is correct.
- II: the distance grows by <InlineMath math="60\%" />, but the time only by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{1.6}\approx1.26" />, about <InlineMath math="26\%" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: seeing the ruler and deciding to close the fingers involves the eyes, the cerebral cortex and motor neurones. It is a voluntary action, which is why distraction slows it. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: because distance goes with the square of time, doubling the distance multiplies the time by <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2}\approx1.4" />, not <InlineMath math="2" />. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only calls the catch a reflex, **although talking on the phone slows it**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **scales time in step with distance**.
- I and IV only rejects II, **treating distance and time as proportional**.

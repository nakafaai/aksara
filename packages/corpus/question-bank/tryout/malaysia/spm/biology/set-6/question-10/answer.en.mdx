# Find the plasma from the dilution, then scale up to whole blood

A known amount of dye spread through an unknown volume gives a measured concentration, so **volume equals amount divided by concentration**. Plasma is only the part of blood that is not cells, so divide by the plasma share to get whole blood.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{plasma}: \frac{10\text{ mg}}{4\text{ mg/L}}=2.5\text{ L},\qquad \text{blood}: \frac{2.5}{1-0.45}=\frac{2.5}{0.55}\approx4.5\text{ L}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{anaemia}: \frac{2.5}{1-0.30}=\frac{2.5}{0.70}\approx3.6\text{ L}" />
</MathContainer>

- I: <InlineMath math="10\text{ mg}" /> at <InlineMath math="4\text{ mg}" /> per litre fills <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ L}" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: plasma is <InlineMath math="55\%" /> of blood, so blood is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2.5}{0.55}\approx4.5\text{ L}" />. The value <InlineMath math="5.6\text{ L}" /> comes from dividing by the **cell share**, <InlineMath math="0.45" />. Statement II is wrong.
- III: dye lost from the plasma lowers the concentration measured, and a lower concentration gives a larger calculated volume. Statement III is correct.
- IV: with only <InlineMath math="30\%" /> cells, plasma is <InlineMath math="70\%" /> of the blood, so <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2.5}{0.70}\approx3.6\text{ L}" />. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts <InlineMath math="5.6\text{ L}" />, **dividing by the cell share instead of the plasma share**, and rejects IV.
- II and IV only rejects I and III, **the dilution step and the effect of leaked dye**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts II, **which uses the wrong share of the blood**.

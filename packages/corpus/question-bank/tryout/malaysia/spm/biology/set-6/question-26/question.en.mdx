The fleshy part of a strawberry is the swollen receptacle of the flower. The small "seeds" on its surface are achenes, the true fruits, each holding one seed formed after fertilisation. Young strawberries are treated in three ways, and their mass is measured after three weeks. On a fourth fruit, all achenes are removed except one vertical strip, and only the flesh under that strip swells. In untreated fruits, the final mass is proportional to the number of achenes holding a fertilised seed, and a fully pollinated fruit has <InlineMath math="200" /> of them.

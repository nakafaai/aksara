# Read each pH step as a factor of ten

The pH scale is **logarithmic**: one unit is a factor of <InlineMath math="10" /> in hydrogen ions, two units a factor of <InlineMath math="100" />, and half a unit a factor of about <InlineMath math="3" />.

<BlockMath math="6.5-4.5=2\ \Rightarrow\ 10^2=100,\qquad 6.5-6.0=0.5\ \Rightarrow\ 10^{0.5}\approx3.2" />

- I: a fall of <InlineMath math="2" /> pH units means <InlineMath math="10\times10=100" /> times more hydrogen ions. Statement I is correct.
- II: burning coal releases sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, which react with water in clouds to form sulfuric and nitric acids. Statement II is correct.
- III: limestone neutralises acid already in the water. It does nothing to the gases in the air, so the rain stays acidic and liming must be repeated. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: pH <InlineMath math="6.0" /> is half a unit below <InlineMath math="6.5" />, so the lake still holds about <InlineMath math="3.2" /> times as many hydrogen ions as in <InlineMath math="1970" />. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **treating half a pH unit as almost no change**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **puts the limestone to work in the air**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **missing where the acid comes from**.

# Work back from the vitamin A needed to the rice that supplies it

Multiply the vitamin A needed by the conversion factor to get the **beta-carotene needed**, then divide by the beta-carotene in each gram of rice.

- I: <InlineMath math="4800\text{ µg}" /> of beta-carotene at <InlineMath math="30\text{ µg}" /> per gram needs <InlineMath math="160\text{ g}" /> of rice. Statement I is correct.
- II: if half the beta-carotene is lost, each gram holds only <InlineMath math="15\text{ µg}" />, so the child needs **more** rice, <InlineMath math="320\text{ g}" />, not less. Statement II is wrong.
- III: with <InlineMath math="20\%" /> lost, each gram keeps <InlineMath math="0.8\times30=24\text{ µg}" />, so <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4800}{24}=200\text{ g}" /> is needed. Statement III is correct.
- IV: DNA in food is broken down by digestion like any other food. It does not enter the child's cells or change the child's genes. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts <InlineMath math="80\text{ g}" />, **halving the rice when the carotene halves**, which is the wrong direction.
- III and IV only rejects I and **lets eaten genes enter the child's cells**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **although digestion breaks DNA down**.

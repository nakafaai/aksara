# Separate growth in size from growth in mass

A hard exoskeleton fixes the body's outer size, so **length** can only jump when the old skeleton is shed and the new, soft one expands. **Mass**, made of new tissue inside, can build up all the time.

- I: length stays fixed within each stage and jumps only at a moult, when the new exoskeleton is still soft and can be stretched before it hardens. Statement I is correct.
- II: within each stage the dry mass roughly doubles, for example <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="45\text{ mg}" />, while the length stays at <InlineMath math="12\text{ mm}" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: every length grows by <InlineMath math="1.5" /> at each moult, and so do the width and depth if the shape is kept, so volume grows by <InlineMath math="1.5^3\approx3.4" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: a child has an internal skeleton that grows continuously, giving a smooth curve without steps. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although a length factor of <InlineMath math="1.5" /> cubed is about <InlineMath math="3.4" />**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **gives a child the stepwise growth of an exoskeleton**.
- I and IV only rejects II, **although the mass doubles within each stage**.

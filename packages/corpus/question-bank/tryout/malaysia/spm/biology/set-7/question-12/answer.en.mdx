# Find where the saturation jumps, then use the mixing in the right ventricle to compare the two circulations

Start by walking along the table in the order blood flows. The saturation stays at <InlineMath math="70\%" /> from the venae cavae into the right atrium, then **jumps to** <InlineMath math="84\%" /> **in the right ventricle**. Oxygen-rich blood must be entering the right ventricle, and the only extra way in is the hole. On the left side, the saturation stays at <InlineMath math="98\%" /> all the way from the pulmonary vein to the aorta, so nothing joins it there.

- III: the rise in the right ventricle, and no fall in the left ventricle, show that blood goes **from the left ventricle into the right ventricle**. The pressures agree: the left ventricle reaches <InlineMath math="14.0\text{ kPa}" /> and the right only <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ kPa}" />, and blood flows from high pressure to low. Statement III is wrong.
- I: the right ventricle mixes two streams. Call the blood flow returning from the body <InlineMath math="Q_s" /> (it arrives at <InlineMath math="70\%" />) and the flow through the hole <InlineMath math="S" /> (it arrives at <InlineMath math="98\%" />). The mixture leaves at <InlineMath math="84\%" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
70Q_s + 98S &= 84(Q_s + S) \\
14S &= 14Q_s \\
S &= Q_s
\end{aligned}" />

Everything the right ventricle pumps goes to the lungs, so the flow through the lungs is

<BlockMath math="Q_p = Q_s + S = 2Q_s" />

It's tempting to say that in a double circulation the lungs and the body must carry the same flow. That's only true when the septum is whole. Here **the extra blood goes round the lungs a second time** without visiting the body. Statement I is correct.

- II: all the blood from the lungs returns through the left atrium to the left ventricle, so the left ventricle pumps <InlineMath math="Q_p" />. The aorta carries only the part that goes to the body, <InlineMath math="Q_s" />.

<BlockMath math="Q_s = 3.0\text{ dm}^3/\text{min} \;\Rightarrow\; Q_p = 2 \times 3.0 = 6.0\text{ dm}^3/\text{min}" />

So the left ventricle pumps <InlineMath math="6.0\text{ dm}^3" /> a minute: <InlineMath math="3.0" /> into the aorta and <InlineMath math="3.0" /> back through the hole. Statement II is correct.

- IV: the aorta and the pulmonary vein both hold blood at <InlineMath math="98\%" />, and the haemoglobin concentration is the same everywhere, so **each** <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> **carries the same oxygen**. No blood from the right side reaches the left side, because the hole only lets blood go from left to right. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and IV only accepts the mixing on the wrong side, **but the left side stays at** <InlineMath math="98\%" /> from the pulmonary vein to the aorta.
- II and III only lets blood cross from right to left, **although the saturation rises in the right ventricle** and its pressure is the lower one.
- I, II and III only gets both flows right but still sends blood from right to left, **against the higher pressure in the left ventricle**.

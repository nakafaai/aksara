# Only the water the vacuum pushes in measures the air, and only the mesophyll holds it

Start with the mass gains. Soaking only wets the surfaces. The vacuum then fills the air spaces, and the surface film stays the same, so **the air spaces of leaf P hold the gain from the second weighing to the third**:

<BlockMath math="840-480=360\text{ mg of water}=360\text{ mm}^3\text{ of air space}" />

Next, find the thickness of the mesophyll. The air spaces sit in the mesophyll only, so take away both epidermis layers:

<BlockMath math="400-2\times20=360\ \mu\text{m}=0.36\text{ mm}" />

Now work out the volume of the mesophyll in all twenty squares:

<BlockMath math="20\times(10\times10)\text{ mm}^2\times0.36\text{ mm}=720\text{ mm}^3" />

The share of that volume that is air is

<BlockMath math="\frac{360}{720}\times100\%=50\%" />

Work out the other two leaves the same way:

<BlockMath math="\text{Q: }\frac{420-335}{20\times100\times(0.200-0.030)}=\frac{85}{340}=25\%,\qquad \text{R: }\frac{1205-1153}{20\times100\times(0.600-0.080)}=\frac{52}{1040}=5\%" />

Last, match each leaf to its habitat. Leaf P has thin epidermis layers and a mesophyll that is half air. Those big air spaces are aerenchyma: they keep a floating leaf, like a water lily's, up on the water and store air for its cells. Leaf R is the opposite, with thick epidermis layers and a tightly packed mesophyll, the pattern of a xerophyte that holds on to its water. So <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of P's mesophyll is air space, and P is the floating hydrophyte.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="56\%" />, P takes the gain from the fresh mass, <InlineMath math="840-440=400\text{ mg}" />, and gets <InlineMath math="\tfrac{400}{720}" />. **But <InlineMath math="40\text{ mg}" /> of that is the surface film from soaking**, which isn't inside the leaf at all.
- <InlineMath math="50\%" />, R has the right share but picks the thick, fleshy leaf as the water plant. **A thick leaf with thick epidermis and only <InlineMath math="5\%" /> air space stores water against drought**, while a floating leaf needs big air spaces.
- <InlineMath math="45\%" />, P divides by the whole leaf, <InlineMath math="20\times100\times0.40=800\text{ mm}^3" />. **The epidermis layers have no air spaces**, so the share has to be taken of the mesophyll alone.

# Turn each flower's life into calendar days, then check the two ends of the spike

Flower <InlineMath math="k" /> opens on day <InlineMath math="k" />, so on day <InlineMath math="d" /> it's on day <InlineMath math="d-k+1" /> of its life. That gives each flower two calendar windows:

<BlockMath math="\text{flower }k\text{ releases pollen on days }k\text{ and }k+1,\qquad \text{its stigma is receptive on days }k+2\text{ and }k+3" />

- I: flower <InlineMath math="10" /> withers on its fifth day, day <InlineMath math="14" />, so the spike has open flowers from day <InlineMath math="1" /> to day <InlineMath math="13" />, which is <InlineMath math="13" /> days. Pollen is released from day <InlineMath math="1" /> (flower <InlineMath math="1" />) to day <InlineMath math="11" /> (flower <InlineMath math="10" />). A stigma is receptive from day <InlineMath math="3" /> (flower <InlineMath math="1" />) to day <InlineMath math="13" /> (flower <InlineMath math="10" />). Both happen on days <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="11" />, which is <InlineMath math="11-3+1=9" /> days. Statement I is correct.
- II: flower <InlineMath math="1" /> releases pollen on days <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="2" />, before any stigma on the spike is receptive. Flower <InlineMath math="10" /> is receptive on days <InlineMath math="12" /> and <InlineMath math="13" />, after the last pollen on the spike, on day <InlineMath math="11" />. **The bees' route doesn't matter here**, because the right flowers simply aren't open at the same time. Statement II is correct.
- III: moving down, a bee picks up pollen at the top and then reaches the receptive flowers lower down. Flower <InlineMath math="k" /> gets pollen from its own spike if a flower above it is releasing pollen on day <InlineMath math="k+2" />, namely flower <InlineMath math="k+1" /> or <InlineMath math="k+2" />. That works up to flower <InlineMath math="9" />, but flower <InlineMath math="10" /> has no flower above it, and II already showed it can't get pollen from its own spike. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: with a new flower every <InlineMath math="3" /> days, flowers open on days <InlineMath math="1, 4, 7, \ldots" />. On day <InlineMath math="4" />, flower <InlineMath math="1" /> is receptive and flower <InlineMath math="2" /> releases pollen. On day <InlineMath math="5" />, flower <InlineMath math="1" /> has withered. On day <InlineMath math="6" />, flower <InlineMath math="2" /> is receptive but flower <InlineMath math="3" /> hasn't opened. The overlap comes back only on day <InlineMath math="7" />, then day <InlineMath math="10" />, never two days running. Statement IV is correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, perhaps by counting days <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> together. **On day <InlineMath math="5" /> flower <InlineMath math="1" /> has already withered**, so the overlap lasts one day each time.
- I, II, III and IV forgets the top of the spike. **Flower <InlineMath math="10" /> has no flower above it**, so even bees moving downwards can bring it pollen only from other plants.
- II and IV only miscounts the days in I, for example taking the overlap as days <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="12" />. **The last pollen is released on day <InlineMath math="11" />**, the second day of flower <InlineMath math="10" />.

A Weddell seal breathes out before it dives, so it carries very little air in its lungs. At the start of a dive, its heart slows and the blood flow to its skeletal muscles stops. The muscles can then use only the oxygen held by myoglobin inside the muscles, while the oxygen in the blood supplies the brain, the heart and the other organs that still receive blood. On a long dive, the seal opens the blood flow to its muscles again from minute <InlineMath math="16" />. From then on, the muscles take the oxygen they need from the blood, and none of it refills the myoglobin.

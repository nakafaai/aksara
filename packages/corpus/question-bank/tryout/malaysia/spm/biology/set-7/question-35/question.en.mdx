Sickle-cell anaemia is caused by a recessive allele on an autosome. The sickle-cell allele, <InlineMath math="\text{Hb}^S" />, differs from the normal allele, <InlineMath math="\text{Hb}^A" />, in only one base. For members of a family, a section of the gene <InlineMath math="500" /> base pairs (bp) long is copied by the polymerase chain reaction (PCR), and the copies are cut with a restriction enzyme.

- In both alleles, the enzyme has a cutting site <InlineMath math="200\text{ bp}" /> from the left end of the copy.
- In <InlineMath math="\text{Hb}^A" />, the enzyme has a second cutting site <InlineMath math="380\text{ bp}" /> from the left end. The one-base change in <InlineMath math="\text{Hb}^S" /> destroys this second site.

The fragments are separated by gel electrophoresis, and the size of each band is found by comparing it with fragments of known size. The mother's blood sample was spoiled, so her DNA could not be tested.

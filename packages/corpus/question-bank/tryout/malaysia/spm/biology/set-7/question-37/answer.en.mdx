# A baby is at risk only after an earlier Rh-positive birth

Two things decide the risk: the baby must be **Rh-positive**, and the mother must **already have anti-D**, which happens only after she has given birth to an Rh-positive baby. So start with the father's genotype, then follow the order of the births.

The father is Rh-positive, but his mother is <InlineMath math="dd" /> and gave him a <InlineMath math="d" />. So he is <InlineMath math="Dd" />, and with the <InlineMath math="dd" /> mother each child is:

<BlockMath math="P(\text{Rh-positive})=P(Dd)=\tfrac{1}{2},\qquad P(\text{Rh-negative})=P(dd)=\tfrac{1}{2}" />

/>

**The second child.** The first child is never at risk, because the mother has no anti-D yet. The second child is at risk only if the first was Rh-positive and the second is Rh-positive too:

<BlockMath math="P(\text{second at risk})=\tfrac{1}{2}\times\tfrac{1}{2}=\tfrac{1}{4}" />

**Three children.** A child is at risk when it is Rh-positive and some earlier child was Rh-positive. So **no child is at risk** exactly when at most one of the three is Rh-positive. List all <InlineMath math="2^3=8" /> equally likely orders, writing <InlineMath math="+" /> for Rh-positive:

| Order of the three children | Children at risk |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="-\;-\;-" /> | none |
| <InlineMath math="+\;-\;-" />, <InlineMath math="-\;+\;-" />, <InlineMath math="-\;-\;+" /> | none |
| <InlineMath math="+\;+\;-" /> | the second |
| <InlineMath math="+\;-\;+" />, <InlineMath math="-\;+\;+" /> | the third |
| <InlineMath math="+\;+\;+" /> | the second and the third |

<BlockMath math="P(\text{at least one at risk})=1-\tfrac{1+3}{8}=\tfrac{4}{8}=\tfrac{1}{2}" />

So the probability that the second child is at risk is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" />, and the probability that at least one of the first three children is at risk is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{7}{8}" /> treats **every Rh-positive baby as at risk**, forgetting that the mother makes anti-D only after an earlier Rh-positive birth.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5}{8}" /> adds the chance for the second child, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" />, and for the third, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{8}" />, **counting the order** <InlineMath math="+\;+\;+" /> **twice**.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{8}" /> works out only the chance that **the third child** is at risk, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}\times\tfrac{3}{4}" />, and leaves out the second child.

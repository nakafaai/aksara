# Membrane goes with area, fluid goes with volume

Every secretory vesicle that fuses adds its whole surface to the plasma membrane. Endocytosis must take the **same area** back, but the fluid each vesicle carries depends on its **volume**. Area grows with <InlineMath math="d^2" /> and volume with <InlineMath math="d^3" />, so the two don't scale alike.

## Membrane added by exocytosis

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{one secretory vesicle} &: \ 3.14\times1.0^2=3.14\,\mu\text{m}^2 \\
\text{per minute} &: \ 10\times3.14=31.4\,\mu\text{m}^2 \\
\text{in 10 minutes} &: \ 10\times31.4=314\,\mu\text{m}^2,\quad \tfrac{314}{785}=0.40=40\%
\end{aligned}" />

## Coated vesicles needed to take it back

One coated vesicle has **one hundredth of the area**, because its diameter is one tenth and area goes with the square.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{one coated vesicle} &: \ 3.14\times0.10^2=0.0314\,\mu\text{m}^2 \\
\text{per minute} &: \ \tfrac{31.4}{0.0314}=1000 \\
\text{if } d=0.05\,\mu\text{m} &: \ 3.14\times0.05^2=0.00785\,\mu\text{m}^2,\quad \tfrac{31.4}{0.00785}=4000
\end{aligned}" />

## Fluid moved each minute

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{released by secretion} &: \ 10\times\tfrac{3.14\times1.0^3}{6}\approx5.23\,\mu\text{m}^3 \\
\text{taken in by coated vesicles} &: \ 1000\times\tfrac{3.14\times0.10^3}{6}\approx0.523\,\mu\text{m}^3
\end{aligned}" />

The coated vesicles bring back the same membrane but only **one tenth of the volume**.

## Judging each statement

- I: the membrane gains <InlineMath math="314\,\mu\text{m}^2" />, which is <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of <InlineMath math="785\,\mu\text{m}^2" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="1000" /> coated vesicles a minute take back the <InlineMath math="31.4\,\mu\text{m}^2" /> added. Statement II is correct.
- III: **equal area does not mean equal volume**. The coated vesicles take in <InlineMath math="0.523\,\mu\text{m}^3" /> a minute, a tenth of the <InlineMath math="5.23\,\mu\text{m}^3" /> released. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: halving the diameter quarters the area, so the cell needs <InlineMath math="4000" /> vesicles a minute, not <InlineMath math="2000" />. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only assumes that vesicles carrying the same membrane area also carry the same volume, but **volume shrinks faster than area** as vesicles get smaller.
- I and IV only scales the number of vesicles with the diameter instead of its square. That gives <InlineMath math="10\times10=100" /> coated vesicles a minute and twice as many at half the size, **forgetting that area goes with** <InlineMath math="d^2" />.
- I, II and IV only gets the ratio of areas right for statement II, then **halves the diameter but only doubles the count** in statement IV.

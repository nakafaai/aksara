# Turn the times into pressures, then let the beat interval set the speed for everything after

The cuff falls by <InlineMath math="3\text{ mmHg}" /> each second, so **every time on the stopwatch is a cuff pressure**. The gap between taps is the time between heartbeats, and it decides how fast the cuff may fall.

## The three pressures

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{systolic} &= 180-3\times14=138\text{ mmHg} \\
\text{diastolic} &= 180-3\times30=90\text{ mmHg} \\
\text{mean arterial pressure} &= 90+\tfrac{138-90}{3}=90+16=106\text{ mmHg}
\end{aligned}" />

## The beat interval and the fastest accurate speed

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{time between beats} &: \ \tfrac{30.0-14.0}{20}=0.8\text{ s} \\
\text{fastest accurate fall} &: \ \tfrac{2\text{ mmHg}}{0.8\text{ s}}=2.5\text{ mmHg per second}
\end{aligned}" />

The nurse's <InlineMath math="3\text{ mmHg}" /> per second drops <InlineMath math="3\times0.8=2.4\text{ mmHg}" /> between beats, **which is too fast for an accurate reading**.

## Using that speed

The automatic monitor finds the largest pulse when the cuff reaches the mean arterial pressure. In the repeat, the taps run from the systolic to the diastolic pressure, <InlineMath math="2\text{ mmHg}" /> apart, with **a tap at both ends**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{largest pulse} &: \ \tfrac{180-106}{2.5}=\tfrac{74}{2.5}=29.6\text{ s} \\
\text{taps in the repeat} &: \ \tfrac{138-90}{2}=24\text{ gaps}\ \Rightarrow\ 24+1=25\text{ taps}
\end{aligned}" />

## Judging each statement

- I: the systolic pressure is <InlineMath math="138" />, below <InlineMath math="140" />, but the diastolic pressure is <InlineMath math="90" />, which meets the limit. Statement I is correct.
- II: the largest pulse comes at <InlineMath math="29.6\text{ s}" />, not <InlineMath math="26.4\text{ s}" />, because the mean arterial pressure is <InlineMath math="106\text{ mmHg}" />. Statement II is wrong.
- III: one beat every <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ s}" /> allows at most <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ mmHg}" /> per second, and at that speed the taps fall at <InlineMath math="138" />, <InlineMath math="136" />, and so on down to <InlineMath math="90" />: <InlineMath math="25" /> taps. Statement III is correct.
- IV: with the diastolic pressure at <InlineMath math="84" /> and the systolic at <InlineMath math="138" />, the reading is no longer high. But the mean falls from <InlineMath math="106" /> to <InlineMath math="102" />, **only <InlineMath math="4\text{ mmHg}" />**. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only takes the plain average, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{138+90}{2}=114" />, so the largest pulse seems to come at <InlineMath math="\tfrac{180-114}{2.5}=26.4\text{ s}" />. **The mean adds one third of the difference to the diastolic pressure**, because the heart spends longer in diastole.
- I, III and IV only lets the mean fall by the full <InlineMath math="6\text{ mmHg}" />, **forgetting that a lower diastolic pressure widens the gap, and a third of that is added back**.
- I only divides the <InlineMath math="48\text{ mmHg}" /> between the systolic and diastolic pressures by <InlineMath math="2\text{ mmHg}" /> and counts <InlineMath math="24" /> taps. **That counts the gaps**, and with a tap at both ends there is one more tap than gaps, just as the nurse's <InlineMath math="21" /> taps had <InlineMath math="20" /> gaps.

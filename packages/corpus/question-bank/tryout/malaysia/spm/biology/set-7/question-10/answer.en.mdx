# Equal oxygen from the gas breathed in does not mean equal energy, because the RQ changes the yield

Each reading covers <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" />. The fall during one breath in is the tidal volume, and **pulmonary ventilation is the tidal volume times the number of breaths**. The slow fall of the level at the end of each breath out is the oxygen taken up, because the soda lime removes the carbon dioxide breathed back.

## Ventilation and oxygen uptake

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{at rest} &: \ 6\times0.5=3\text{ dm}^3,\quad \text{oxygen }6.00-5.85=0.15\text{ dm}^3\quad(\text{per }30\text{ s}) \\
\text{after exercise} &: \ 10\times1.5=15\text{ dm}^3,\quad \text{oxygen }6.00-5.25=0.75\text{ dm}^3\quad(\text{per }30\text{ s}) \\
\text{oxygen per dm}^3\text{ breathed in} &: \ \tfrac{0.15}{3}=0.05,\qquad \tfrac{0.75}{15}=0.05
\end{aligned}" />

## Energy from the oxygen

Now bring in the second table. The RQ picks **how much energy each <InlineMath math="\text{dm}^3" /> of oxygen gives**: <InlineMath math="20.1\text{ kJ}" /> at rest and <InlineMath math="21.1\text{ kJ}" /> just after exercise.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{after exercise, per minute} &: \ 2\times0.75=1.50\text{ dm}^3\text{ oxygen},\quad 1.50\times21.1=31.65\text{ kJ} \\
\text{per dm}^3\text{ breathed in, at rest} &: \ 0.05\times20.1=1.005\text{ kJ} \\
\text{per dm}^3\text{ breathed in, after exercise} &: \ 0.05\times21.1=1.055\text{ kJ}
\end{aligned}" />

## How long the chamber allows full breaths

A full breath in takes <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ dm}^3" /> below the level at the end of a breath out. So **the end of a breath in stays at <InlineMath math="1.00\text{ dm}^3" /> or more only while the end of a breath out stays at <InlineMath math="1.00+1.5=2.50\text{ dm}^3" /> or more**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{fall allowed} &: \ 6.00-2.50=3.50\text{ dm}^3 \\
\text{time} &: \ \tfrac{3.50}{0.75}\times30\text{ s}=140\text{ s}
\end{aligned}" />

## Judging each statement

- I: the ventilation rises from <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="15\text{ dm}^3" /> per <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" />, five times, and each <InlineMath math="\text{dm}^3" /> gives up <InlineMath math="0.05\text{ dm}^3" /> of oxygen in both conditions. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="1.50\text{ dm}^3" /> of oxygen per minute at <InlineMath math="21.1\text{ kJ}" /> each gives <InlineMath math="31.65\text{ kJ}" /> per minute. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="1.005\text{ kJ}" /> holds only at rest. Just after exercise **the same oxygen gives <InlineMath math="1.055\text{ kJ}" />**, because the RQ is higher. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: full breaths last <InlineMath math="140\text{ s}" />, not <InlineMath math="280\text{ s}" />. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only sees the same <InlineMath math="0.05\text{ dm}^3" /> of oxygen per <InlineMath math="\text{dm}^3" /> and assumes the same energy, **forgetting that the energy from each <InlineMath math="\text{dm}^3" /> of oxygen depends on the RQ**.
- I and IV only reads the fall of <InlineMath math="0.75\text{ dm}^3" /> in <InlineMath math="30\text{ s}" /> **as if it were per minute**. Then the energy is only <InlineMath math="0.75\times21.1\approx15.8\text{ kJ}" /> per minute, and the chamber seems to last <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3.50}{0.75}" /> minutes, <InlineMath math="280\text{ s}" />.
- II only compares the oxygen taken up per breath, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.15}{6}=0.025" /> against <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.75}{10}=0.075\text{ dm}^3" />, and so rejects statement I. Statement I asks about **each <InlineMath math="\text{dm}^3" /> breathed in**, and the deeper breaths hold three times the gas.

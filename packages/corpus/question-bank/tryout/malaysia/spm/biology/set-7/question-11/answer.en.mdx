# Follow the water through a full colon, because half of every extra drink is lost too

The target is the **net water the body gains from the gut**: water eaten and drunk minus water lost in the faeces. Before the infection that's

<BlockMath math="2.0 - 0.1 = 1.9\text{ dm}^3 \text{ a day}" />

The digestive juices don't count as a gain. The body secretes them and takes them back, but only if the gut absorbs them again. So first find out how much fluid really runs through the gut.

<BlockMath math="2.0 + 1.5 + 2.0 + 0.5 + 1.5 + 1.5 = 9.0\text{ dm}^3 \text{ a day}" />

The damaged small intestine absorbs only half of this, so **half reaches the colon**:

<BlockMath math="0.50 \times 9.0 = 4.5\text{ dm}^3" />

The colon would like to absorb all but <InlineMath math="0.1\text{ dm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="4.4\text{ dm}^3" />. But it can absorb **at most** <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ dm}^3" />, so it's working at its limit.

<BlockMath math="\text{water in faeces} = 4.5 - 4.0 = 0.5\text{ dm}^3" />

Now the net gain is <InlineMath math="2.0 - 0.5 = 1.5\text{ dm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ dm}^3" /> short of the <InlineMath math="1.9\text{ dm}^3" /> before.

It's tempting to drink just <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ dm}^3" /> more. But **the extra drink goes through the same damaged gut**. Of an extra volume <InlineMath math="V" />, the small intestine absorbs only half. The other half reaches a colon that is already absorbing its maximum, so all of that half ends up in the faeces.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{faeces} &= 0.5 + 0.50V \\
\text{net gain} &= (2.0 + V) - (0.5 + 0.50V) = 1.5 + 0.50V \\
1.5 + 0.50V &= 1.9 \\
V &= \frac{0.4}{0.50} = 0.8\text{ dm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

Check: with <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ dm}^3" /> extra, the person takes in <InlineMath math="2.8\text{ dm}^3" /> and loses <InlineMath math="0.9\text{ dm}^3" />, so the net gain is <InlineMath math="2.8 - 0.9 = 1.9\text{ dm}^3" />, the same as before.

So the person must drink an extra <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ dm}^3" /> of water a day.

# Why the other options are wrong

- No extra water counts only the <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ dm}^3" /> of food and drink as the gut's fluid, so the colon seems to get just <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ dm}^3" />, **but the** <InlineMath math="7.0\text{ dm}^3" /> **of digestive juices also pass through the gut**.
- <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ dm}^3" /> replaces the shortfall one for one, **forgetting that half of the extra drink is also lost** through the full colon.
- <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ dm}^3" /> treats all <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ dm}^3" /> of faecal water as the shortfall, **but** <InlineMath math="0.1\text{ dm}^3" /> **was lost before the infection too**.

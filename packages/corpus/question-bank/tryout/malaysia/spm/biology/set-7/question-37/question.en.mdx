The Rhesus (Rh) blood group is controlled by one gene. The allele for Rh-positive, <InlineMath math="D" />, is dominant over the allele for Rh-negative, <InlineMath math="d" />. An Rh-negative woman marries an Rh-positive man whose mother is Rh-negative. The woman has never received a blood transfusion.

Assume that:

- the woman makes anti-D antibodies only after she gives birth to an Rh-positive baby, and she never receives an anti-D injection;
- once she has anti-D antibodies, every Rh-positive baby she carries after that is at risk of haemolytic disease of the newborn, and an Rh-negative baby never is.

The couple have three children, born one at a time. What is the probability that their second child is at risk of haemolytic disease of the newborn, and what is the probability that at least one of these three children is at risk, counting the first, the second and the third child?

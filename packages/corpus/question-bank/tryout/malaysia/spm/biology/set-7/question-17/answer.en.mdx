# The luteal phase keeps its length, so a cycle changes only through its follicular phase

Ovulation is when the large follicle disappears and a corpus luteum takes its place. The days before it are the follicular phase, and the days after it up to the next menstruation are the luteal phase. **The corpus luteum lives for a fixed time**, so a faster or slower follicle shifts the whole cycle.

## Timing this cycle

The follicle is last seen on day <InlineMath math="20" />, at <InlineMath math="24\text{ mm}" />, right at the LH peak, and is gone on day <InlineMath math="21" />. By the rule in the question, **day <InlineMath math="20" /> is the day of ovulation**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{follicular phase} &: \ \text{days }1\text{ to }20=20\text{ days} \\
\text{luteal phase} &: \ \text{days }21\text{ to }35=15\text{ days}
\end{aligned}" />

A <InlineMath math="28" />-day cycle that ovulates on day <InlineMath math="14" /> has a follicular phase of <InlineMath math="14" /> days and a luteal phase of <InlineMath math="28-14=14" /> days.

## Predicting the next cycle

The follicle must **grow from <InlineMath math="10" /> to <InlineMath math="24\text{ mm}" />** before it ovulates.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{growth time} &: \ \tfrac{24-10}{1.75}=\tfrac{14}{1.75}=8\text{ days}\ \Rightarrow\ 24\text{ mm on day }9+8=17 \\
\text{ovulation} &: \ \text{day }17 \\
\text{luteal phase of }15\text{ days} &: \ \text{days }18\text{ to }32\ \Rightarrow\ \text{menstruation starts on day }33
\end{aligned}" />

## Judging each statement

- I: the follicle reaches <InlineMath math="24\text{ mm}" /> on day <InlineMath math="17" />, so day <InlineMath math="17" /> is the day of ovulation, and **the <InlineMath math="15" />-day luteal phase** ends on day <InlineMath math="32" />. Menstruation starts on day <InlineMath math="33" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: the endometrium thickens from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="11\text{ mm}" /> in the follicular phase, <InlineMath math="9\text{ mm}" />, but only from <InlineMath math="11" /> to <InlineMath math="13\text{ mm}" /> in the luteal phase, <InlineMath math="2\text{ mm}" />. Progesterone keeps the lining ready rather than building most of it. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the follicular phase is <InlineMath math="20-14=6" /> days longer, and the luteal phase <InlineMath math="15-14=1" /> day longer. Statement III is correct.
- IV: oestrogen peaks at <InlineMath math="260" /> on day <InlineMath math="19" />, just before the LH peak, while progesterone is still below <InlineMath math="1" />. **The LH surge follows the high oestrogen**. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only links the thickening to whichever hormone is highest. The table shows **most of the thickening happens before ovulation**, under rising oestrogen.
- I, III and IV only puts the LH surge down to progesterone, which rises only after ovulation. **Oestrogen, peaking on day <InlineMath math="19" />, sets off the LH peak**.
- III only expects the next cycle to last <InlineMath math="35" /> days again, so it rejects statement I. **The faster follicle brings ovulation forward**, and the luteal phase then runs its usual <InlineMath math="15" /> days.

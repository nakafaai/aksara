# Turn the carbon into dry mass before you balance the pot

The chamber gives carbon, but the statements are about dry mass, so first find how the two are linked. The pot with tomato only shows it, because there all the net carbon ends up in the tomato:

<BlockMath math="\frac{20.0\text{ g dry mass}}{8.0\text{ g carbon}}=2.5\text{ g of dry mass for every gram of carbon}" />

The dry matter of both plants has the same share of carbon, so this factor works for the broomrape too.

- I: the broomrape doesn't change the tomato's photosynthesis or respiration, so the tomato in the second pot still makes <InlineMath math="20.0\text{ g}" /> of dry mass. It keeps only <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ g}" />, so the broomrape took <InlineMath math="20.0-8.0=12\text{ g}" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: now balance the whole second pot. Its net carbon becomes dry mass shared by the two plants:

<BlockMath math="6.4\times2.5=16\text{ g},\qquad 16-8.0=8\text{ g gained by the broomrape}" />

Statement II is correct.

- III: the broomrape took <InlineMath math="12\text{ g}" /> and kept <InlineMath math="8\text{ g}" />, so **it burnt <InlineMath math="4\text{ g}" /> of dry mass in respiration**. The carbon data say the same thing: the second pot takes up <InlineMath math="8.0-6.4=1.6\text{ g}" /> less carbon, which is the carbon the broomrape breathed out, and <InlineMath math="1.6\times2.5=4\text{ g}" /> of dry mass. The <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ g}" /> is carbon, not dry mass. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the plants in the first pot gained <InlineMath math="20\text{ g}" />, and those in the second pot <InlineMath math="8+8=16\text{ g}" />. The pot lost only <InlineMath math="4\text{ g}" />, the part the broomrape respired. The other <InlineMath math="8\text{ g}" /> it took just moved from the tomato into the broomrape. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only treats what the host lost as what the pot lost. **But <InlineMath math="8\text{ g}" /> of the <InlineMath math="12\text{ g}" /> is still in the pot**, now as broomrape, so the pot is down by only <InlineMath math="4\text{ g}" />.
- I, II and III only reads the <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ g}" /> of carbon as <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ g}" /> of dry mass. **Every gram of carbon comes with <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ g}" /> of dry mass**, so the broomrape respired <InlineMath math="4\text{ g}" />.
- I only uses the <InlineMath math="6.4\text{ g}" /> of carbon as if it were dry mass, which is less than the tomato's own <InlineMath math="8.0\text{ g}" /> and leaves nothing for the broomrape. **The carbon has to be multiplied by <InlineMath math="2.5" /> first**, giving <InlineMath math="16\text{ g}" /> for the pot.

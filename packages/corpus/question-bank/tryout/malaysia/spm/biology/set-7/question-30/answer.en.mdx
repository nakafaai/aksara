# Put every flow in tonnes of carbon before you add or compare them

The table is in carbon, but the diesel saving is in carbon dioxide. So the first job is to **convert the <InlineMath math="11" /> tonnes of carbon dioxide into carbon**:

<BlockMath math="11\times\frac{12}{44}=3\text{ t of carbon saved each year}" />

- I: new plant material is photosynthesis minus the plants' own respiration, <InlineMath math="34-18=16\text{ t}" /> for the oil palm and <InlineMath math="30-18=12\text{ t}" /> for the forest. Now take away everything else that leaves:

<BlockMath math="\text{forest: }12-11-0-0=+1\text{ t},\qquad \text{oil palm: }16-12-12-4=-12\text{ t}" />

The oil palm grows faster, yet its hectare loses carbon. Statement I is correct.

- II: the oil palm hectare loses <InlineMath math="12\text{ t}" /> of carbon a year, and <InlineMath math="12\times\tfrac{44}{12}=44\text{ t}" /> of carbon dioxide. Statement II is correct.
- III: all <InlineMath math="12\text{ t}" /> lost by the hectare reach the air, the harvested carbon included. The biodiesel keeps <InlineMath math="3\text{ t}" /> of fossil carbon in the ground, so the hectare still adds

<BlockMath math="12-3=9\text{ t of carbon}=33\text{ t of carbon dioxide a year}" />

Taking <InlineMath math="12-11=1" /> mixes carbon with carbon dioxide. Statement III is wrong.

- IV: on mineral soil the peat flow is gone, so the hectare's carbon stays level, <InlineMath math="16-12-4=0" />, and the only thing that builds up is the fossil carbon the biodiesel keeps in the ground, <InlineMath math="3\text{ t}" /> a year:

<BlockMath math="\frac{200\text{ t}}{3\text{ t per year}}\approx67\text{ years},\qquad 2\times25=50\text{ years},\qquad 3\times25=75\text{ years}" />

The third rotation runs from year <InlineMath math="50" /> to year <InlineMath math="75" />, and year <InlineMath math="67" /> falls inside it. Statement IV is correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only subtracts <InlineMath math="11" /> tonnes of carbon dioxide from <InlineMath math="12" /> tonnes of carbon. **The diesel saving is only <InlineMath math="3\text{ t}" /> of carbon**, so the hectare still adds <InlineMath math="9\text{ t}" /> a year, and on mineral soil the payback takes about <InlineMath math="67" /> years, not <InlineMath math="\tfrac{200}{11}\approx18" />.
- I and IV only converts the wrong way, <InlineMath math="12\times\tfrac{12}{44}\approx3.3\text{ t}" />. **Carbon dioxide is heavier than the carbon it holds**, so <InlineMath math="12\text{ t}" /> of carbon makes <InlineMath math="44\text{ t}" /> of carbon dioxide.
- I and II only adds up the saving in carbon dioxide over two rotations, <InlineMath math="50\times11=550" />, and compares it with <InlineMath math="200" /> tonnes of carbon. **Both sides must be in carbon**: <InlineMath math="50\times3=150\text{ t}" />, still short of <InlineMath math="200\text{ t}" />.

# Take the microorganisms' heat out of flask P before turning any heat into glucose

Flask P warms for two reasons: the peas respire, and so do the microorganisms on them. Flask Q, with dead peas and no microorganisms, shows **what happens with neither**, and flask R, with dead peas and live microorganisms, shows **the microorganisms alone**.

## Splitting the daily rises

**The rise in R minus the rise in Q is the microorganisms' share**, and what is left in P belongs to the peas.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{flask P each day} &: \ 2,\ 3,\ 4\ ^\circ\text{C}\ \ (\text{total }9) \\
\text{microorganisms, R}-\text{Q} &: \ 0,\ 1,\ 2\ ^\circ\text{C}\ \ (\text{total }3) \\
\text{peas} &: \ 2,\ 2,\ 2\ ^\circ\text{C}\ \ (\text{total }6)
\end{aligned}" />

## Turning the heat of the peas into glucose

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{heat from the peas} &: \ 250\times3.2\times6=4800\text{ J}=4.8\text{ kJ} \\
\text{glucose} &: \ \tfrac{4.8}{16}=0.30\text{ g} \\
\text{flask P alone} &: \ 250\times3.2\times9=7200\text{ J}\ \Rightarrow\ \tfrac{7.2}{16}=0.45\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

## Judging each statement

- I: the peas account for <InlineMath math="6^\circ\text{C}" />, which is <InlineMath math="4.8\text{ kJ}" /> and <InlineMath math="0.30\text{ g}" /> of glucose. Statement I is correct.
- II: the microorganisms give <InlineMath math="3" /> of the <InlineMath math="9^\circ\text{C}" />, one third, and their daily share goes <InlineMath math="0" />, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{3}" />, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: flask P alone gives <InlineMath math="0.45\text{ g}" /> against the true <InlineMath math="0.30\text{ g}" />, an extra <InlineMath math="0.15\text{ g}" />. As a share of the true value, **that is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.15}{0.30}=50\%" /> too high**, not <InlineMath math="33\%" />. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the peas warm the flask by <InlineMath math="2^\circ\text{C}" /> on both day <InlineMath math="1" /> and day <InlineMath math="3" />, so their rate stays the same. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only reads flask P's daily rises, <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="4^\circ\text{C}" />, as the peas' own heat, **forgetting to take each day's microorganism share out**.
- I, II and III only divides the extra <InlineMath math="0.15\text{ g}" /> by the wrong value, <InlineMath math="0.45" />, which gives <InlineMath math="33\%" />. **An overestimate is a share of the true value**, <InlineMath math="0.30\text{ g}" />.
- II and IV only turns all of flask P's <InlineMath math="9^\circ\text{C}" /> into glucose, <InlineMath math="0.45\text{ g}" />, and reads its daily rises as the peas' heat, **ignoring the microorganisms that flask R measures**.

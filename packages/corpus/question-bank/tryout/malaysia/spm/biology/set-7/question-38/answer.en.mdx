# Keep a running total of the nitrogen you put in, counting only the additions made before each reading

Nitrification runs in two steps, and **each step has its own bacteria**. Lay out the path first, because it explains the order of the peaks.

Each reading is taken just before that day's dose, so on day <InlineMath math="d" /> only the doses of days <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="d-1" /> are in the water:

<BlockMath math="\text{added before the reading on day }d=2\times(d-1)\text{ mg per dm}^3" />

That gives <InlineMath math="8" />, <InlineMath math="18" />, <InlineMath math="28" />, <InlineMath math="38" />, <InlineMath math="48" /> and <InlineMath math="58" /> on days <InlineMath math="5" /> to <InlineMath math="30" />, and every column of the table adds up to exactly that. No nitrogen is gained or lost.

- I: ammonium peaks on day <InlineMath math="10" /> and nitrite on day <InlineMath math="15" />, five days later. Between the readings on day <InlineMath math="15" /> and day <InlineMath math="20" />, the doses of days <InlineMath math="15" /> to <InlineMath math="19" /> go in, <InlineMath math="5\times2=10\text{ mg}" />. Nitrate is made only from nitrite, and the totals show none is lost, so the rise in nitrate is the nitrogen turned from nitrite into nitrate:

<BlockMath math="29-7=22\text{ mg},\qquad \frac{22}{10}=2.2" />

The fall in nitrite, <InlineMath math="15-8=7" />, is much smaller, because *Nitrosomonas* kept making new nitrite from ammonium while *Nitrobacter*, now plentiful, used it up. Statement I is correct.

- II: on day <InlineMath math="30" />, <InlineMath math="29" /> doses are in the water, <InlineMath math="58\text{ mg}" />, and <InlineMath math="57" /> of them are nitrate:

<BlockMath math="\frac{57}{58}\times100\%\approx98\%" />

- III: between the readings on day <InlineMath math="25" /> and day <InlineMath math="30" />, the doses of days <InlineMath math="25" /> to <InlineMath math="29" /> go in, <InlineMath math="5\times2=10\text{ mg}" />. # Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only divides by <InlineMath math="30\times2=60" /> and gets <InlineMath math="95\%" />. **The day-<InlineMath math="30" /> dose goes in after the reading**, so only <InlineMath math="58\text{ mg}" /> had been added.
- II and III only rejects I by taking the fall in nitrite, <InlineMath math="15-8=7\text{ mg}" />, as the nitrite turned into nitrate. **Nitrite was being made from ammonium at the same time**, so the conversion is the rise in nitrate, <InlineMath math="22\text{ mg}" />.
- I, II, III and IV compares the nitrate with one ammonium reading. **The right comparison is with the <InlineMath math="58\text{ mg}" /> added**, and the nitrogen found matches it exactly.

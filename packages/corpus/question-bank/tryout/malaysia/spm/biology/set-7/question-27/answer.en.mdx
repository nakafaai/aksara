# Find which runs out first for each plant, the water or the month

Each plant stops for one of two reasons: its <InlineMath math="15\text{ kg}" /> of water runs out, or the <InlineMath math="30" /> days end. So first find **the water each plant spends per gram of carbon dioxide**, then check which limit each plant hits.

<BlockMath math="\text{cactus: }\frac{75}{1.5}=50\text{ g},\qquad \text{shrub: }\frac{2000}{4.0}=500\text{ g},\qquad \frac{50}{500}=\frac{1}{10}" />

Statement I is correct. The cactus opens its stomata in the cool, humid night, when water vapour diffuses out of the leaf more slowly, while carbon dioxide still diffuses in.

Now check the limits. The water lasts each plant:

<BlockMath math="\text{shrub: }\frac{15\,000}{2000}=7.5\text{ days},\qquad \text{cactus: }\frac{15\,000}{75}=200\text{ days}" />

The shrub runs dry after <InlineMath math="7.5" /> days, but the cactus still has water when the month ends. So **only the shrub is limited by water**, and the cactus is limited by the <InlineMath math="30" /> days:

<BlockMath math="\text{shrub: }7.5\times4.0=30\text{ g},\qquad \text{cactus: }30\times1.5=45\text{ g of carbon dioxide}" />

- II: the cactus fixes <InlineMath math="\tfrac{45}{30}=1.5" /> times as much, not ten times. Ten times would need the cactus to spend all <InlineMath math="15\text{ kg}" />, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{15\,000}{50}=300\text{ g}" />, but it uses only <InlineMath math="30\times75=2250\text{ g}" /> of water in the month. Statement II is wrong.
- III: convert each mass of carbon dioxide into glucose:

<BlockMath math="\text{cactus: }45\times\frac{180}{264}\approx30.7\text{ g},\qquad \text{shrub: }30\times\frac{180}{264}\approx20.5\text{ g},\qquad \frac{30.7}{20.5}=1.5" />

Statement III is correct.

- IV: with <InlineMath math="30\text{ kg}" />, the shrub's water lasts <InlineMath math="\tfrac{30\,000}{2000}=15" /> days, so it fixes <InlineMath math="15\times4.0=60\text{ g}" />. The cactus was never short of water, so it still fixes <InlineMath math="45\text{ g}" />. Now the shrub fixes more. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only treats the cactus as limited by water too, so it fixes <InlineMath math="300\text{ g}" /> and makes about <InlineMath math="205\text{ g}" /> of glucose. **At <InlineMath math="75\text{ g}" /> a day, the cactus uses only <InlineMath math="2250\text{ g}" /> of water in <InlineMath math="30" /> days**, so the month, not the water, stops it at <InlineMath math="45\text{ g}" />.
- III and IV only compares the daily water losses, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{75}{2000}" />, **without dividing each by the carbon dioxide fixed**, so it misses the <InlineMath math="50\text{ g}" /> against <InlineMath math="500\text{ g}" /> per gram.
- I and III only doubles both plants' totals when the water doubles, giving the cactus <InlineMath math="90\text{ g}" /> against <InlineMath math="60\text{ g}" />. **More water helps only the plant that ran out of it**, so the cactus stays at <InlineMath math="45\text{ g}" />.

# Bone laid down far from the centre resists bending more than its amount suggests

The area of compact bone is the outer circle minus the marrow cavity. Since <InlineMath math="\tfrac{\pi}{4}" /> appears in every area, **compare <InlineMath math="D^2-d^2" /> only**. Resistance to bending is that area times the outer diameter <InlineMath math="D" />.

## Areas and resistance to bending

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{left} &: \ 25^2-15^2=625-225=400,\qquad 400\times25=10\,000 \\
\text{right, career} &: \ 27^2-15^2=729-225=504,\qquad 504\times27=13\,608 \\
\text{right, ten years later} &: \ 27^2-18^2=729-324=405,\qquad 405\times27=10\,935
\end{aligned}" />

## Comparing them

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{career, against left} &: \ \tfrac{504}{400}=1.26,\qquad \tfrac{13\,608}{10\,000}\approx1.36 \\
\text{ten years later, against left} &: \ \tfrac{405}{400}\approx1.01,\qquad \tfrac{10\,935}{10\,000}\approx1.09 \\
\text{right arm's own loss} &: \ \tfrac{504-405}{504}=\tfrac{99}{504}\approx0.196,\qquad \tfrac{13\,608-10\,935}{13\,608}\approx0.196
\end{aligned}" />

**The outer diameter of the right humerus stays at <InlineMath math="27\text{ mm}" />**, so its resistance to bending falls in exactly the same proportion as its area.

## Judging each statement

- I: <InlineMath math="26\%" /> more bone and about <InlineMath math="36\%" /> more resistance to bending. Statement I is correct.
- II: about <InlineMath math="1\%" /> more bone, yet about <InlineMath math="9\%" /> more resistance, because the right tube is still wider. Statement II is correct.
- III: the right humerus loses <InlineMath math="99" /> of its own <InlineMath math="504" /> units, <InlineMath math="19.6\%" />, **not <InlineMath math="25\%" />**. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: both fall by the same <InlineMath math="\tfrac{99}{504}\approx19.6\%" />, because **the outer diameter does not change**. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only divides the loss of <InlineMath math="99" /> by the left humerus's <InlineMath math="400" />, giving <InlineMath math="24.75\%" />, close to <InlineMath math="25\%" />. **The loss is a share of the right humerus's own <InlineMath math="504" />**.
- I, II and IV only compares the drop in the advantage over the left arm, <InlineMath math="36-9=27" /> points of resistance against <InlineMath math="26-1=25" /> points of bone. **The statement asks about the right humerus's own percentage loss**, which is the same for both.
- I only judges the right humerus after ten years by its area alone and so sees no advantage left, **forgetting that resistance to bending also grows with the outer diameter**.

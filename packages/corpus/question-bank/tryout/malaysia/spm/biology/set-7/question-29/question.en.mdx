In an oil palm estate, caterpillars of the bagworm moth, *Metisa plana*, eat the leaves. A small wasp is a parasitoid of the bagworm. It lays one egg inside a caterpillar, and the wasp larva feeds inside the caterpillar and kills it before it can become an adult moth. One adult wasp comes out of each parasitised caterpillar.

The estate releases wasps once. In generation <InlineMath math="1" />, there are <InlineMath math="200" /> caterpillars per palm, and <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of them are parasitised. No more wasps are released after that, and assume that:

- each caterpillar that is not parasitised gives rise to exactly <InlineMath math="5" /> caterpillars in the next generation, and a parasitised caterpillar gives rise to none;
- each wasp that comes out of a generation parasitises <InlineMath math="4" /> different caterpillars of the next generation, one egg in each;
- if the wasps have more eggs than there are caterpillars, every caterpillar is parasitised and the extra eggs are wasted.

Which of the following statements are correct?

I. Generation <InlineMath math="3" /> has <InlineMath math="1400" /> caterpillars per palm.

II. The number of caterpillars per palm falls for the first time from generation <InlineMath math="3" /> to generation <InlineMath math="4" />.

III. More wasps come out of generation <InlineMath math="4" /> than out of generation <InlineMath math="3" />.

IV. Generation <InlineMath math="5" /> has no caterpillars.

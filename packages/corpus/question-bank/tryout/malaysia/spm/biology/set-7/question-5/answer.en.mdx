# Sort every base into DNA or RNA with the bases only one of them has

The extract holds two nucleic acids, and three of the bases are shared. The way in is through what belongs to one acid only: **deoxyribose and thymine are found only in DNA**, ribose and uracil only in RNA.

## The DNA

Each nucleotide has one sugar, so the DNA has <InlineMath math="1000\text{ nmol}" /> of nucleotides, that is <InlineMath math="500\text{ nmol}" /> of base pairs. In a double strand **every adenine pairs with a thymine** and every guanine with a cytosine.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{A}=\text{T} &= 280\text{ nmol} \\
\text{G}=\text{C} &= \tfrac{1000-280-280}{2}=220\text{ nmol} \\
\text{A and T pairs} &: \ \tfrac{280}{500}=56\%\ \text{of the base pairs}
\end{aligned}" />

## The RNA

The RNA has <InlineMath math="3000\text{ nmol}" /> of nucleotides. Adenine and guanine were measured for the whole mixture, so **take away the DNA's share first**. A single strand has no pairing rule, so cytosine is whatever is left.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{A} &= 1000-280=720\text{ nmol} \\
\text{G} &= 1260-220=1040\text{ nmol} \\
\text{C} &= 3000-720-1040-750=490\text{ nmol}
\end{aligned}" />

## Judging each statement

- I: the DNA has <InlineMath math="220\text{ nmol}" /> of guanine and the RNA the other <InlineMath math="1040\text{ nmol}" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: the RNA has <InlineMath math="720\text{ nmol}" /> of adenine, **less than its** <InlineMath math="750\text{ nmol}" /> **of uracil**. Statement II is wrong.
- III: <InlineMath math="280" /> of the <InlineMath math="500\text{ nmol}" /> of base pairs are adenine and thymine pairs, which is <InlineMath math="56\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the RNA has <InlineMath math="490\text{ nmol}" /> of cytosine. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only gives all <InlineMath math="1000\text{ nmol}" /> of adenine to the RNA. That makes adenine beat uracil and leaves only <InlineMath math="3000-1000-1040-750=210\text{ nmol}" /> of cytosine, **forgetting that the DNA holds** <InlineMath math="280\text{ nmol}" /> **of the adenine**.
- I and IV only divides the <InlineMath math="280" /> adenine and thymine pairs by the <InlineMath math="1000\text{ nmol}" /> of nucleotides, giving <InlineMath math="28\%" />, **mixing up nucleotides and base pairs**.
- I, II, III and IV works out the cytosine correctly but judges statement II by setting the total adenine beside the uracil, **without separating the DNA's adenine**.

# Time the cycle from one prophase to the next, then follow the DNA through it

The cells were released at **the start of the S phase**, not at the start of a cycle. So the first <InlineMath math="10" /> hours cover only S and <InlineMath math="\text{G}_2" />, and one full cycle is the gap between two prophases.

## The length of each phase

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{S}+\text{G}_2 &= 10\ \Rightarrow\ \text{G}_2=10-7=3\text{ h} \\
\text{mitosis and cytokinesis} &= 11-10=1\text{ h} \\
\text{one cycle} &= 32-10=22\text{ h} \\
\text{G}_1 &= 22-7-3-1=11\text{ h}
\end{aligned}" />

## The timetable after the first division

The daughter cells start <InlineMath math="\text{G}_1" /> at <InlineMath math="11" /> hours, so **each later phase starts where the one before ends**.

<BlockMath math="\text{G}_1:\ 11\text{ to }22\text{ h},\qquad \text{S}:\ 22\text{ to }29\text{ h},\qquad \text{G}_2:\ 29\text{ to }32\text{ h}" />

## DNA in the culture

Call the DNA of one cell at time <InlineMath math="0" /> one unit. **A cell doubles its DNA in S and shares it between two cells at cytokinesis**.

- At <InlineMath math="9" /> hours, the single cell is in <InlineMath math="\text{G}_2" /> and holds <InlineMath math="2" /> units.
- From <InlineMath math="11" /> hours, there are <InlineMath math="2" /> cells with <InlineMath math="1" /> unit each.
- At <InlineMath math="30" /> hours, both cells have finished the second S phase and are in <InlineMath math="\text{G}_2" />, so the culture holds <InlineMath math="2\times2=4" /> units for each cell present at time <InlineMath math="0" />.

## Judging each statement

- I: <InlineMath math="\text{G}_1" /> lasts <InlineMath math="22-7-3-1=11" /> hours. Statement I is correct.
- II: at <InlineMath math="30" /> hours the culture holds <InlineMath math="4" /> units against <InlineMath math="1" /> unit at time <InlineMath math="0" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: at <InlineMath math="15" /> hours the two daughter cells are in <InlineMath math="\text{G}_1" />. They go on until they reach the start of S at <InlineMath math="22" /> hours and are held there, **before copying their DNA**. Each holds <InlineMath math="1" /> unit, half of the <InlineMath math="2" /> units at <InlineMath math="9" /> hours, and there are <InlineMath math="2" /> cells for every cell at time <InlineMath math="0" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the <InlineMath math="32" /> hours start in the middle of a cycle, so they **include an extra S and <InlineMath math="\text{G}_2" />**. One cycle lasts <InlineMath math="22" /> hours. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- III and IV only takes the cycle as the <InlineMath math="32" /> hours from release to the second prophase, **forgetting that the release was at the start of S**. That stretches <InlineMath math="\text{G}_1" /> to <InlineMath math="21" /> hours, puts the second S at <InlineMath math="32" /> hours, and leaves only <InlineMath math="2" /> units of DNA at <InlineMath math="30" /> hours.
- II and III only works out <InlineMath math="\text{G}_1" /> as <InlineMath math="22-7-3=12" /> hours, **leaving out the hour of mitosis and cytokinesis**. Statements II and III still hold, but statement I seems wrong.
- I and II only thinks a cell held at the start of S has already copied its DNA, as at <InlineMath math="9" /> hours. **The chemical stops the cell before any DNA is copied**, so it holds half that amount.

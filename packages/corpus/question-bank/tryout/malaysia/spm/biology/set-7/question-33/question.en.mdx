In cats, a gene on the X chromosome controls orange fur. The allele <InlineMath math="X^{O}" /> gives orange fur and the allele <InlineMath math="X^{o}" /> gives black fur. The Y chromosome carries no copy of this gene. A female with the genotype <InlineMath math="X^{O}X^{o}" /> has patches of orange and black fur, called tortoiseshell, because in each patch of skin one of her two X chromosomes is switched off at random. A cat with one Y chromosome is male, whether it has one X chromosome or two.

- Cross <InlineMath math="1" />: a tortoiseshell female is mated with an orange male.
- Cross <InlineMath math="2" />: a black female is mated with an orange male, and one of their kittens is a tortoiseshell male. Every cell of this kitten has the sex chromosomes XXY.

Assume that no new mutation occurs.

Which of the following statements are correct?

I. In cross <InlineMath math="1" />, half of the female kittens are expected to be tortoiseshell, and none of the male kittens.

II. The tortoiseshell male of cross <InlineMath math="2" /> came from a sperm that carried both an X chromosome and a Y chromosome, so the error happened in meiosis I in the father.

III. In cross <InlineMath math="1" />, the probability that the first two kittens are both tortoiseshell is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{4}" />.

IV. If a female kitten from cross <InlineMath math="1" />, chosen at random, is later mated with a black male, the probability that their first kitten is orange is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{8}" />.

# Clotting time goes down as active enzyme goes up

The milk clots once a fixed amount of its protein has been changed. More active rennin does that sooner, so **the clotting time is inversely proportional to the concentration of active rennin**. Results P and Q check this: a fifth of the rennin takes five times as long. Their times, <InlineMath math="60" /> to <InlineMath math="300\text{ s}" />, also cover every other time in the table.

<BlockMath math="\frac{0.020}{0.100}=\frac{1}{5},\qquad \frac{300}{60}=5" />

## Active rennin in each extract

So concentration times clotting time stays at <InlineMath math="0.100\times60=6" /> for every solution. Use it to find **how much active rennin** each extract holds.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{R} &: \ \tfrac{6}{90}\approx0.067\% \\
\text{S} &: \ \tfrac{6}{270}\approx0.022\%,\quad \tfrac{0.022}{0.067}=\tfrac{1}{3}\ \text{still active, so } \tfrac{2}{3}\ \text{destroyed}
\end{aligned}" />

## The mixture

Mixing equal volumes averages the **concentrations**, not the times.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{concentration} &= \tfrac{0.100+0.067}{2}\approx0.083\% \\
\text{time} &= \tfrac{6}{0.083}=72\text{ s}
\end{aligned}" />

## Judging each statement

- I: R takes longer, so it holds **less** active rennin, about two thirds of P, not <InlineMath math="1.5" /> times as much. Statement I is wrong.
- II: S takes three times as long as R, so S holds a third of R's active rennin. **The other two thirds lost its activity** during the heating. Statement II is correct.
- III: the mixture clots the milk in <InlineMath math="72\text{ s}" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: a fifth of the concentration gives five times the time. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and IV only treats the clotting time as if it grew with the enzyme, **although a longer time means less active enzyme**. That makes R stronger than P and heating seem to raise the activity.
- II and IV only averages the two clotting times, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{60+90}{2}=75\text{ s}" />, **instead of averaging the concentrations**.
- III and IV only reads the third that is still active as the share destroyed, **mixing up what is left with what is lost**.

# Place every site first, then trace the pin through each damaged path

A pin under the right foot sends impulses in through the right dorsal root. In the lower spinal cord they take three paths: **out through the right ventral root to pull the right leg away, across the cord and out through the left ventral root to straighten the left leg, and up the cord to the brain to be felt**. None of the reflex paths needs the brain.

## Placing the five sites

- R has lost the right knee-jerk and the feeling in the right knee but moves the right leg at will, so **the right dorsal root** is cut.
- P has lost the right knee-jerk but still feels the tap, and cannot move the right leg, so **the right ventral root** is cut.
- Q keeps both knee-jerks and the feeling but cannot move the right leg at will, so the damage is in **the left motor area**.
- That leaves two sites for S. S feels nothing in the right knee and moves neither leg, yet keeps the right knee-jerk, which fits **the cord cut in the chest**. The cut alone would leave the left knee-jerk working, so its absence needs **the left ventral root** as well.

## Judging each statement

- I: S's right dorsal root, right ventral root and lower cord are intact, so the right leg is pulled away. The crossed path must leave through the left ventral root, which is cut, so the left leg does not straighten. Statement I is correct.
- II: R's impulses from the right sole never enter the cord, so there is no pain, no pulling away and no straightening. Statement II is correct.
- III: the reflex that pulls Q's right leg away runs through the spinal cord only. Q feels the pain, and **the right leg is still pulled away**, even though Q cannot move it at will. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: P feels the pain, and the right leg is not pulled away, but the crossed path leaves through P's intact left ventral root, so the left leg straightens. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- II only reads S as the cord cut alone. Then the crossed path below the cut works, and S's left leg should straighten, **ignoring the missing left knee-jerk that needs a second site**.
- II and III only thinks a leg can be pulled away only through the brain. That breaks S's withdrawal in I and Q's in III, **although the withdrawal reflex is a spinal reflex**.
- I, II and IV only forgets the crossed path to the other leg, so it sees no movement at all for P, **missing that the left leg straightens through the left ventral root**.

# Get the gamete shares from the test cross before you self the plant

The colourless shrunken parent is <InlineMath math="ccss" />, so it only ever gives <InlineMath math="cs" /> gametes. Each seed therefore shows exactly **which gamete came from the heterozygous plant**. Start with the total:

<BlockMath math="380+26+22+372=800\text{ seeds}" />

If the genes assorted independently, the four kinds of gamete would be equally common, and each class would hold <InlineMath math="\tfrac{800}{4}=200" /> seeds.

Two classes are huge and two are tiny, so the alleles mostly travel together: the genes are **linked**. The big classes show the chromosomes the plant already had, and the small classes are the new combinations made by crossing over. Turn the counts into gamete shares:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{new combinations} &= \tfrac{26+22}{800}\times100\% = 6\% \\
Cs\text{ and }cS &= \tfrac{6\%}{2} = 3\%\text{ each} \\
CS\text{ and }cs &= \tfrac{100\%-6\%}{2} = 47\%\text{ each}
\end{aligned}" />

A colourless shrunken seed from selfing needs a <InlineMath math="cs" /> pollen grain **and** a <InlineMath math="cs" /> egg cell, and the question says both are made the same way:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
P(ccss) &= 0.47\times0.47 = 0.2209 \\
\text{expected seeds} &= 0.2209\times2000 \approx 442
\end{aligned}" />

- I: the commonest seeds show coloured with full and colourless with shrunken, so the plant has <InlineMath math="C" /> with <InlineMath math="S" /> on one chromosome and <InlineMath math="c" /> with <InlineMath math="s" /> on the other. The arrangement in the statement is the one made by **crossing over**, which shows up in only <InlineMath math="6\%" /> of the gametes. Statement I is wrong.
- II: the genes are linked, and <InlineMath math="6\%" /> of the gametes carry a new combination. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="0.47^2\times2000\approx442" />, which is about <InlineMath math="440" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the <InlineMath math="ccss" /> plant has the **same alleles on both homologues**. Swapping pieces between them still gives <InlineMath math="cs" /> every time, so crossing over there makes nothing new. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- II only rejects III by using <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{16}\times2000=125" />, **a ratio that holds only for genes on different chromosomes**.
- II, III and IV only accepts IV, forgetting that crossing over in a plant that is **homozygous for both genes** can't change its gametes.
- I and II only reads the arrangement from the **rarest** classes. That also makes <InlineMath math="cs" /> a rare gamete of <InlineMath math="3\%" />, giving only about <InlineMath math="0.03^2\times2000\approx2" /> colourless shrunken seeds, so III gets rejected too.

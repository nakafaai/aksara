Each leg is served by its own spinal nerve. Sensory neurones from the leg, including the knee and the sole of the foot, enter the lower spinal cord through the dorsal root of that nerve, and motor neurones to the leg leave through its ventral root. Inside the lower spinal cord, the knee-jerk reflex links the sensory and motor neurones of the same leg. When the sole of one foot steps on a sharp pin, that leg is pulled away, and relay neurones that cross the spinal cord make the other leg straighten to take the weight. Other neurones carry impulses up the spinal cord to the brain, so a touch is felt, and down from the brain, so a leg can be moved at will.

Four patients, P, Q, R and S, have damage at five sites in all. Three patients each have damage at one site, and one patient has damage at two sites. Each site is damaged in only one patient.

- the dorsal root of the right spinal nerve
- the ventral root of the right spinal nerve
- the ventral root of the left spinal nerve
- the spinal cord, cut right across in the chest region, above the nerves to both legs
- the motor area of the left cerebrum that controls the right leg

Assume that the spinal reflexes below a cut in the spinal cord still work.

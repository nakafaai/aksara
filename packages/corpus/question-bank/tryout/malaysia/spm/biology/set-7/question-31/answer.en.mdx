# Put the leak, the burning and the grid saving on one carbon dioxide scale

The cover stops most of the methane, but it doesn't make the carbon disappear. The burned methane comes back as carbon dioxide, a tenth of the methane still escapes, and the electricity saves some carbon dioxide at the power stations. So put everything on one scale, tonnes of carbon dioxide, before and after.

First split the <InlineMath math="800" /> tonnes of methane a year:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{escaping} &= 0.10\times800 = 80\text{ t} \\
\text{burned} &= 800-80 = 720\text{ t}
\end{aligned}" />

Now convert each part with the rules in the question. Escaping methane counts <InlineMath math="28" /> times its mass. Burned methane becomes carbon dioxide in the ratio <InlineMath math="44:16" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{before covering} &= 800\times28 = 22\,400\text{ t} \\
\text{escaping after covering} &= 80\times28 = 2240\text{ t} \\
\text{from burning} &= 720\times\tfrac{44}{16} = 720\times2.75 = 1980\text{ t} \\
\text{after covering} &= 2240+1980 = 4220\text{ t}
\end{aligned}" />

Then carry the burned methane on to the grid. Only the <InlineMath math="720" /> tonnes that are burned make electricity:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{electricity} &= 720\times5.5 = 3960\text{ MWh} \\
\text{saved at the power stations} &= 3960\times0.75 = 2970\text{ t} \\
\text{after covering, with the grid} &= 4220-2970 = 1250\text{ t}
\end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{without the grid: }\frac{22\,400-4220}{22\,400}\approx81\%,\qquad \text{with the grid: }\frac{22\,400-1250}{22\,400}\approx94\%" />
</MathContainer>

- I: leaving out the grid, the cut is about <InlineMath math="81\%" />, not <InlineMath math="90\%" />. The <InlineMath math="90\%" /> is only the share of methane captured, and it leaves out the <InlineMath math="1980" /> tonnes of **carbon dioxide made by burning**. Statement I is wrong.
- II: the escaping methane is worth <InlineMath math="2240" /> tonnes, and the burning gives <InlineMath math="1980" /> tonnes. So the small leak **still outweighs** all the carbon dioxide from the engine. Statement II is correct.
- III: counting the grid, the ponds now cause <InlineMath math="1250" /> tonnes a year instead of <InlineMath math="22\,400" />, a cut of about <InlineMath math="94\%" />, more than <InlineMath math="90\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: methane is made by **anaerobic** bacteria, which work only where oxygen is missing. With air pumped in, aerobic decomposers break the organic matter down to carbon dioxide instead, so less methane forms. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- III only rejects II by converting with <InlineMath math="\tfrac{44}{12}" />, the ratio for carbon instead of for methane, which gives <InlineMath math="720\times\tfrac{44}{12}=2640" /> tonnes, **more than the** <InlineMath math="2240" /> **tonnes of the leak**. With that slip, III still holds, at about <InlineMath math="91\%" />.
- I, II and III only accepts I, reading the <InlineMath math="90\%" /> captured as the cut and **forgetting the carbon dioxide from burning**.
- II, III and IV only accepts IV, giving the methane to bacteria that need oxygen, **although methane comes from anaerobic breakdown**.

# In the long run only the solute that cannot cross decides

A solute that crosses the membrane keeps entering until it is equally concentrated on both sides, and then it no longer pulls water either way. So once it has spread, **only the solute that cannot cross is compared with the cell sap**, which is equivalent to <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> sucrose.

## Why every cell plasmolyses first

At the start, glycerol and urea are outside only, so each <InlineMath math="0.80\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> solution is hypertonic to the sap. Water leaves by osmosis within minutes, **faster than the slow solute enters**, so all the cells plasmolyse. As glycerol or urea creeps in, the sap's own solutes make the cell hypertonic again, water returns and the cell becomes turgid.

## Comparing urea and glycerol

The time to recover is **inversely proportional to the rate of entry**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{\text{rate of urea}}{\text{rate of glycerol}}=\frac{90\text{ min}}{15\text{ min}}=6" />

## Judging each statement

- I: urea enters slowly, so water leaves first. Statement I is correct.
- II: the glycerol spreads into the cells, leaving only <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> sucrose outside, which is below the sap. The cells **become turgid again**. Statement II is wrong.
- III: <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes against <InlineMath math="90" /> minutes means urea enters six times as fast. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the <InlineMath math="0.60\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> sucrose stays outside and is above the sap, so the cells stay plasmolysed once the urea has spread. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only expects the urea in the last mixture to rescue the cells, as it did on its own, **forgetting that the sucrose alone is above the sap**.
- I and IV only takes the shorter time as the slower solute, so it calls glycerol six times as fast, **though a faster solute needs less time** to make the cells turgid.
- I, II, III and IV adds glycerol to sucrose as if both stayed outside, **but glycerol crosses the membrane**, so only the <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> of sucrose counts in the end.

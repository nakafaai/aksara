# From the count per quadrat to the density

**The area of one quadrat is the side squared**, so <InlineMath math="0.5\times0.5=0.25\text{ m}^2" />, not <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}^2" />. Divide the number of plants by the total area of all the quadrats to get the density, then multiply by the area of the field.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{area sampled}&=10\times0.25=2.5\text{ m}^2\\
\text{density}&=\frac{45}{2.5}=18\text{ plants per m}^2\\
\text{population}&=18\times200=3600\\
\text{frequency}&=\frac{7}{10}\times100\%=70\%
\end{aligned}" />

Frequency counts the quadrats that contain the plant, not the number of plants. So the estimated population is <InlineMath math="3600" /> plants and the frequency is <InlineMath math="70\%" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="900" /> multiplies the mean per quadrat, <InlineMath math="4.5" />, by <InlineMath math="200" />, but **one quadrat covers only <InlineMath math="0.25\text{ m}^2" />**.
- <InlineMath math="45\%" /> uses the number of plants as the frequency, but frequency counts the quadrats that contain the plant.
- <InlineMath math="1800" /> uses <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}^2" /> as the quadrat area, but <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ m}" /> is the length of its side.

# Fixed volume, double the length

The coelomic fluid cannot be compressed, so **the volume <InlineMath math="\pi r^2L" /> stays the same**. If the length doubles, <InlineMath math="r^2" /> must halve, so the radius is multiplied by <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\pi r^2L&=\pi r'^2(2L)\ \Rightarrow\ r'^2=\tfrac12r^2\\
r'&=\frac{r}{\sqrt2}\approx0.71r\\
\text{decrease}&\approx1-0.71=0.29=29\%
\end{aligned}" />

A longer, thinner segment forms when the circular muscles contract and the longitudinal muscles relax. So the radius falls by about <InlineMath math="29\%" /> because the circular muscles contract.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="50\%" /> assumes the radius is inversely proportional to the length, but **the length is inversely proportional to <InlineMath math="r^2" />**.
- Contracting longitudinal muscles make the segment shorter and thicker, not longer.
- <InlineMath math="75\%" /> assumes the cross-sectional area falls to a quarter, but it only halves.

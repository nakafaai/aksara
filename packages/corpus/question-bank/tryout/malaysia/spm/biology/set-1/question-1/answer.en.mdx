# Compare the rise in light with the rise in rate

Each time the distance halves, the light intensity becomes <InlineMath math="2^2=4" /> times larger, because intensity is inversely proportional to **the square of the distance**. Compare that rise with the rise in bubbles at each step.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
40\to20\text{ cm}:&\ \text{light}\times4,\ \text{rate}\ \frac{18}{5}=3.6\ \text{times}\\
20\to10\text{ cm}:&\ \text{light}\times4,\ \text{rate}\ \frac{30}{18}\approx1.7\ \text{times}\\
10\text{ cm}+\text{CO}_2:&\ \text{rate}\ \frac{48}{30}=1.6\ \text{times}
\end{aligned}" />

In the first step the rate nearly keeps pace with the light, so light is limiting. In the second step the light rises four times but the rate does not even double, so another factor starts to limit. When carbon dioxide is added at <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" />, the rate rises <InlineMath math="1.6" /> times, so that factor is carbon dioxide. The water bath keeps the temperature fixed. So light limits from <InlineMath math="40" /> to <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" />, and carbon dioxide is the main limiting factor at <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Direct proportion at every distance does not fit, because in the second step **the rate rises only <InlineMath math="1.7" /> times** while the light rises four times.
- Carbon dioxide does not limit at every distance, because from <InlineMath math="40" /> to <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}" /> the rate nearly keeps pace with the light.
- The water bath keeps the temperature fixed, so temperature is not the limiting factor at any distance in this experiment.

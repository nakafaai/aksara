# Cell size, then the new field of view

A cell's actual size does not depend on the magnification. Eight cells fill a diameter of <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mm}" />, so one cell is <InlineMath math="0.40/8=0.050\text{ mm}" /> long. When the magnification is cut by four times, **the field of view becomes four times wider**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{cell length}&=\frac{0.40}{8}=0.050\text{ mm}=50\,\mu\text{m}\\
\text{new diameter}&=0.40\times\frac{400}{100}=1.6\text{ mm}\\
\text{number of cells}&=\frac{1.6}{0.050}=32
\end{aligned}" />

So each cell is <InlineMath math="50\,\mu\text{m}" /> long, and <InlineMath math="32" /> cells can lie across the new field of view.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="128" /> cells uses the area of the field, which grows sixteen times, but the question asks about cells **lying along the diameter**.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> cells reverses the change, as if a lower magnification narrowed the field of view.
- <InlineMath math="20\,\mu\text{m}" /> converts the unit wrongly, because <InlineMath math="0.050\text{ mm}" /> equals <InlineMath math="50\,\mu\text{m}" />.

# Given

Bean seeds germinate and the **dry mass of <InlineMath math="10" /> seedlings** is recorded every <InlineMath math="3" /> days: <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ g}" /> (day <InlineMath math="0" />), <InlineMath math="1.7\text{ g}" /> (day <InlineMath math="3" />), <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ g}" /> (day <InlineMath math="6" />), <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ g}" /> (day <InlineMath math="9" />), and <InlineMath math="2.2\text{ g}" /> (day <InlineMath math="12" />). First leaves emerge on day <InlineMath math="4" />.

# Asked

In which day interval does photosynthesis first exceed respiration, and what is the mean gain in dry mass per seedling per day from day <InlineMath math="9" /> to day <InlineMath math="12" />?

# Solution

Picture a seed's dry mass like a packed lunchbox. Early on, the seedling burns stored food faster than it makes new food, so dry mass drops until its lowest point on day <InlineMath math="6" />. **The moment the curve turns upwards between day <InlineMath math="6" /> and day <InlineMath math="9" />, photosynthesis has overtaken respiration**.

> Seedling dry mass falls while respiration outpaces photosynthesis, and turns upwards as soon as the rate of photosynthesis surpasses respiration.

Even though leaves appear on day <InlineMath math="4" />, they remain too small for photosynthesis to exceed respiration until after day <InlineMath math="6" />. The green segment marks that first rise from day <InlineMath math="6" /> to day <InlineMath math="9" />.

## Mean daily gain calculation

Divide the <InlineMath math="3" />-day mass gain of <InlineMath math="10" /> seedlings by **both the number of seedlings and the number of days**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta m_{9 \to 12} &= 2.2 - 1.6 = 0.6\text{ g for } 10\text{ seedlings} \\
\text{mean gain per seedling per day} &= \frac{0.6}{10 \times 3} = \frac{0.6}{30} = 0.020\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

Quick check: multiplying <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ g}" /> by <InlineMath math="10" /> seedlings and <InlineMath math="3" /> days restores <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ g}" />.

## Why other options fail

- Day <InlineMath math="9" /> to day <InlineMath math="12" /> gives the steepest climb, but **the first positive gain already happens from day <InlineMath math="6" />**.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ g}" /> forgets to divide by <InlineMath math="10" /> seedlings even though the table weighs <InlineMath math="10" /> seedlings together.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="0.060\text{ g}" /> gives the gain per seedling over all <InlineMath math="3" /> days rather than per single day.

# Answer

Photosynthesis first exceeds respiration from day <InlineMath math="6" /> to day <InlineMath math="9" />, and the mean gain in dry mass per seedling per day is <InlineMath math="0.020\text{ g}" />.

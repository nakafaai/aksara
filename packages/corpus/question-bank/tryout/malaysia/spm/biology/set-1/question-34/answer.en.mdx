# Separate the two genes, then multiply

The genes are on different chromosomes, so **their probabilities are found separately and multiplied**. <InlineMath math="Tt\times Tt" /> gives <InlineMath math="\tfrac34" /> tall, and <InlineMath math="Rr\times rr" /> gives <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> wrinkled.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{tall wrinkled}&=\tfrac34\times\tfrac12\times160=60\\
TT\ \text{among the tall}&=\frac{1/4}{3/4}=\tfrac13\\
\text{true-breeding}\ (TTrr)&=\tfrac13\times60=20
\end{aligned}" />

Every wrinkled seed is already <InlineMath math="rr" />, so only the height gene decides true breeding. So the answer is <InlineMath math="60" /> and <InlineMath math="20" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="40" /> is the number of <InlineMath math="Ttrr" /> plants, but **heterozygotes do not breed true**.
- <InlineMath math="15" /> takes <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> of <InlineMath math="60" />, but among the tall plants only <InlineMath math="\tfrac13" /> are <InlineMath math="TT" />.
- <InlineMath math="30" /> and <InlineMath math="10" /> use <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> for tall, but <InlineMath math="Tt\times Tt" /> gives <InlineMath math="\tfrac34" /> tall.

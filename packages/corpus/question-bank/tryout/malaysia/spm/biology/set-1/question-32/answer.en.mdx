# Given

Plants store <InlineMath math="20{,}000\text{ kJ m}^{-2}" /> per year. **Energy transfer efficiencies** are <InlineMath math="10\%" /> to primary, <InlineMath math="15\%" /> to secondary, and <InlineMath math="20\%" /> to tertiary consumers.

Each tertiary consumer needs <InlineMath math="1200\text{ kJ}" /> per year, and <InlineMath math="1" /> hectare equals <InlineMath math="10{,}000\text{ m}^2" />.

# Asked

Find the maximum number of tertiary consumers that <InlineMath math="1" /> hectare of this ecosystem can support.

# Solution

Think of energy climbing a food chain like water carried down a line of leaky buckets. Only a small fraction survives each handoff, so we multiply all three percentages before scaling up from one square metre to a full hectare.

> Across consecutive trophic levels, transfer efficiencies multiply together before scaling from unit area to total habitat area.

Calculate the **annual energy reaching tertiary consumers** per square metre, multiply by <InlineMath math="10{,}000\text{ m}^2" />, and divide by one animal's annual requirement.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
E_{\text{tertiary}} &= 20{,}000 \times 0.10 \times 0.15 \times 0.20 = 60\text{ kJ m}^{-2} \\
E_{\text{hectare}} &= 60 \times 10{,}000 = 600{,}000\text{ kJ} \\
N &= \frac{600{,}000}{1200} = 500
\end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="5" /> converts <InlineMath math="1" /> hectare as <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}^2" />, forgetting that **<InlineMath math="1" /> hectare is <InlineMath math="100 \times 100 = 10{,}000\text{ m}^2" />**.
- <InlineMath math="2500" /> stops at secondary consumer energy (<InlineMath math="300\text{ kJ m}^{-2}" />) and omits the final <InlineMath math="20\%" /> transfer.
- <InlineMath math="250" /> mistakenly applies <InlineMath math="10\%" /> instead of <InlineMath math="20\%" /> at the last step.

# Answer

One hectare of the ecosystem can support at most <InlineMath math="500" /> tertiary consumers.

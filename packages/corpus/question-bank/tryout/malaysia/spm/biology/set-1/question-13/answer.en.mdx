# Cardiac output is stroke volume times heart rate

Cardiac output is the stroke volume multiplied by the heart rate. **The resting cardiac output does not change**, so the stroke volume rises when the heart rate falls.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{resting output}&=72\times70=5040\text{ cm}^3\text{ per minute}\\
\text{new stroke volume}&=\frac{5040}{56}=90\text{ cm}^3\\
\text{maximum output}&=180\times120=21{,}600\text{ cm}^3\text{ per minute}\\
\frac{21{,}600}{5040}&\approx4.3
\end{aligned}" />

So the stroke volume is <InlineMath math="90\text{ cm}^3" /> and the maximum cardiac output is about <InlineMath math="4.3" /> times the resting output.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="54\text{ cm}^3" /> multiplies <InlineMath math="70" /> by <InlineMath math="\tfrac{56}{72}" />, but **the stroke volume must rise** to keep the output the same.
- <InlineMath math="3.2" /> times compares only the heart rates, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{180}{56}" />, without the rise in stroke volume.
- <InlineMath math="2.5" /> times uses the old stroke volume of <InlineMath math="70\text{ cm}^3" /> during exercise, but it is <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}^3" />.

# Given

A culture starts with <InlineMath math="100" /> bacterial cells. They spend **<InlineMath math="40" /> minutes in the lag phase** without dividing, then **double every <InlineMath math="20" /> minutes** with no cell death.

The total elapsed time is <InlineMath math="2" /> hours (<InlineMath math="120" /> minutes).

# Asked

Find how many bacterial cells are present <InlineMath math="2" /> hours after inoculation.

# Solution

Think of bacteria warming up in the locker room for the first <InlineMath math="40" /> minutes before the doubling race starts. Only the remaining <InlineMath math="80" /> minutes produce divisions, giving four crisp doublings.

> Exponential doubling starts only after the lag phase ends, so subtract the lag duration before dividing by the generation time.

Convert <InlineMath math="2" /> hours into minutes, find the **number of doubling intervals** after the lag phase, and apply <InlineMath math="N = N_0 \times 2^k" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t_{\text{growth}} &= 120 - 40 = 80\text{ minutes} \\
k &= \frac{80}{20} = 4\text{ doublings} \\
N &= 100 \times 2^4 = 100 \times 16 = 1600\text{ cells}
\end{aligned}" />

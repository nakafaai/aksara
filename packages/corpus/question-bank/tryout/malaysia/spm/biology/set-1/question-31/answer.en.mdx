# Given

The marsh covers <InlineMath math="2.5" /> hectares. Initially, <InlineMath math="60" /> snails are marked and released, then **<InlineMath math="20\%" /> of the marks are lost** before recapture.

In the second sample, <InlineMath math="50" /> snails are caught and <InlineMath math="10" /> of them still carry marks.

# Asked

Find the estimated population density of the snails per hectare.

# Solution

Imagine painting bright stars on <InlineMath math="60" /> snails before letting them crawl back into the marsh. If rain washes off <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of the stars, only <InlineMath math="48" /> snails can still be recognized as marked when you look again.

> Capture-recapture only works with marks that actually remain visible, so lost marks must be removed before estimating population size.

First find the **recognizably marked snails**, then compute total population <InlineMath math="N" /> and divide by the marsh area to get density.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
M_{\text{eff}} &= 0.80 \times 60 = 48 \\
N &= \frac{48 \times 50}{10} = 240\text{ snails} \\
\text{density} &= \frac{240}{2.5} = 96\text{ snails ha}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

Notice the quick check: <InlineMath math="10" /> out of <InlineMath math="50" /> recaptured snails is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{5}" />, so the whole marsh holds <InlineMath math="5 \times 48 = 240" /> snails, giving <InlineMath math="96" /> per hectare.

## Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="120" /> keeps all <InlineMath math="60" /> initial marks, **forgetting that <InlineMath math="12" /> snails lost their marks** and look unmarked in the second capture.
- <InlineMath math="600" /> multiplies <InlineMath math="240" /> by <InlineMath math="2.5" /> instead of dividing population by area.
- <InlineMath math="24" /> divides the <InlineMath math="60" /> initially marked snails by <InlineMath math="2.5" />, skipping the population estimate completely.

# Answer

The estimated population density of the snails is **<InlineMath math="96" />** per hectare.

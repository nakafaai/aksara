# The point of no change in mass

An isotonic solution causes no net osmosis, so **the tissue's mass does not change**. The mass rises at <InlineMath math="0.2\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> and falls at <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, so the zero lies between them. Since the change is linear, find it by proportion.

Slide the concentration and watch the strips and the cell: the mass change crosses zero between two tubes.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{change from }0.2\text{ to }0.4&=+6\%\to-2\%,\ \text{a difference of }8\%\\
x&=0.2+0.2\times\frac{6}{8}=0.2+0.15=0.35\text{ mol dm}^{-3}\\
\text{at }0.5:&\ \frac{-2+(-10)}{2}=-6\%\ \Rightarrow\ 5.0\times0.94=4.70\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

The zero is nearer <InlineMath math="0.4" /> because <InlineMath math="-2\%" /> is closer to zero than <InlineMath math="+6\%" />. The <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> solution is hypertonic, so the strip loses water. So the isotonic solution is <InlineMath math="0.35\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> and the strip becomes <InlineMath math="4.70\text{ g}" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.30\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> just takes the midpoint, but **the zero lies nearer <InlineMath math="0.4" />**.
- <InlineMath math="5.30\text{ g}" /> adds <InlineMath math="6\%" />, but a hypertonic solution makes the strip lose mass.
- <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> still makes the mass fall by <InlineMath math="2\%" />, and <InlineMath math="4.90\text{ g}" /> uses the change at <InlineMath math="0.4" />, not at <InlineMath math="0.5" />.

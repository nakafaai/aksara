# Follow all four sperm

Meiosis I is normal, so both secondary spermatocytes carry <InlineMath math="23" /> chromosomes. The normal cell gives two sperm with <InlineMath math="23" /> chromosomes. In the faulty cell, **both chromatids of chromosome <InlineMath math="21" /> go into one sperm**, so one sperm carries <InlineMath math="24" /> and the other <InlineMath math="22" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{sperm}&:\ 23,\ 23,\ 24,\ 22\\
24+23&=47\ \text{chromosomes (three copies of chromosome 21)}
\end{aligned}" />

Only the sperm with <InlineMath math="24" /> chromosomes gives Down syndrome. So the fraction is <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> and the child has <InlineMath math="47" /> chromosomes.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> uses the pattern of non-disjunction in meiosis I, but **here meiosis I is normal** and only one cell fails.
- <InlineMath math="45" /> comes from the sperm with <InlineMath math="22" /> chromosomes, which gives monosomy, not Down syndrome.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> and <InlineMath math="45" /> combines both of those mistakes.

# Given

Plant roots absorb potassium ions against a concentration gradient at <InlineMath math="20" /> units in aerated solution, <InlineMath math="12" /> units in nitrogen-bubbled solution, and **<InlineMath math="2" /> units with cyanide** blocking all respiration.

# Asked

What percentage of active uptake in air is still maintained by anaerobic respiration in nitrogen gas?

# Solution

Picture comparing two water pumps when <InlineMath math="2" /> buckets still trickle in passively even after pulling the power plug (cyanide). Before comparing how hard the two pumps work, **those <InlineMath math="2" /> passive units must be subtracted from both measurements**.

> True active uptake is found by subtracting the respiration-independent passive baseline measured under cyanide from each total uptake rate.

Slide the passive baseline <InlineMath math="p" /> below from <InlineMath math="0" /> (uncorrected) to <InlineMath math="2" /> units to watch the active percentage shift from the <InlineMath math="60\%" /> trap down to <InlineMath math="56\%" />.

The orange marker at <InlineMath math="p = 0" /> shows the <InlineMath math="60\%" /> mistake when cyanide is ignored. At <InlineMath math="p = 2" />, the green marker drops to <InlineMath math="55.6\%" /> (about <InlineMath math="56\%" />).

## Subtracting passive uptake

Remove **the <InlineMath math="2" /> respiration-free units** from both air and nitrogen readings, then take their percentage ratio.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{active in air} &= 20 - 2 = 18\text{ units} \\
\text{active in nitrogen} &= 12 - 2 = 10\text{ units} \\
\text{percentage} &= \frac{10}{18} \times 100\% \approx 55.6\% \approx 56\%
\end{aligned}" />

Without oxygen, anaerobic respiration still generates enough ATP to sustain over half of the active transport.

## Why other options fail

- Choosing <InlineMath math="60\%" /> divides <InlineMath math="12" /> by <InlineMath math="20" />, forgetting that **both totals still contain <InlineMath math="2" /> passive units**.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="50\%" /> divides active nitrogen uptake (<InlineMath math="10" />) by total air uptake (<InlineMath math="20" />).
- Choosing <InlineMath math="67\%" /> divides total nitrogen uptake (<InlineMath math="12" />) by active air uptake (<InlineMath math="18" />).

# Answer

About <InlineMath math="56\%" /> of active uptake in air is maintained by anaerobic respiration in nitrogen gas.

# Given

Per <InlineMath math="\text{mm}^2" />, plant Q has <InlineMath math="220" /> upper and <InlineMath math="0" /> lower stomata with a **thin waxy cuticle**. Plant R has <InlineMath math="0" /> upper and <InlineMath math="90" /> sunken lower stomata with a **very thick cuticle**.

Plant S has <InlineMath math="40" /> upper and <InlineMath math="180" /> lower ordinary stomata with a thin cuticle. Every sunken stoma loses half as much water as an ordinary open stoma.

# Asked

Find how many times more water per <InlineMath math="\text{mm}^2" /> leaf S loses than leaf R, and identify which plant is the xerophyte.

# Solution

Think of water escaping a leaf like air leaking from tiny pinholes in a balloon. A pinhole tucked deep inside a sheltered pit leaks only half as fast as an exposed pinhole on the surface.

> Total transpiration compares effective open stomata across both leaf surfaces, where each sunken stoma counts as half an ordinary stoma.

Add the upper and lower stomata for leaf S, then halve leaf R's sunken stomata to find each leaf's **effective stomatal count**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
S &= 40 + 180 = 220\text{ effective stomata mm}^{-2} \\
R &= 90 \times \frac{1}{2} = 45\text{ effective stomata mm}^{-2} \\
\frac{S}{R} &= \frac{220}{45} = \frac{44}{9} \approx 4.9
\end{aligned}" />

Because **plant R combines sunken lower stomata with a very thick cuticle**, it conserves water best in dry habitats, making R the xerophyte.

## Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="2.4" /> times divides <InlineMath math="220" /> by <InlineMath math="90" />, **ignoring the half-loss rule** for sunken stomata.
- Selecting S as the xerophyte reverses the adaptation, because S has a thin cuticle and the highest water loss.
- <InlineMath math="2.0" /> times compares only <InlineMath math="180" /> against <InlineMath math="90" />, leaving out S's <InlineMath math="40" /> upper stomata and R's sunken factor.

# Answer

Leaf S loses about **<InlineMath math="4.9" />** times more water per **<InlineMath math="\text{mm}^2" />** than leaf R, and plant R is the xerophyte.

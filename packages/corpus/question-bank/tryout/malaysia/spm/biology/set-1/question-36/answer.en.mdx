# Given

Albinism is caused by an **autosomal recessive allele** (<InlineMath math="a" />). Both parents are not albino, yet they have an albino son (<InlineMath math="aa" />).

They also have a daughter who is **not albino**.

# Asked

Find the probability that the non-albino daughter carries the albinism allele (<InlineMath math="Aa" />).

# Solution

Imagine four equally likely cards placed face down: <InlineMath math="AA" />, <InlineMath math="Aa" />, <InlineMath math="Aa" />, and <InlineMath math="aa" />. The instant we see the daughter is not albino, we remove the <InlineMath math="aa" /> card, leaving only <InlineMath math="3" /> cards, and <InlineMath math="2" /> of them are carriers.

> Conditional probability removes impossible genotypes first, shrinking the sample space from four equally likely outcomes to only those matching the observed phenotype.

Because the son is <InlineMath math="aa" /> while both parents are unaffected, **both parents must be heterozygotes (<InlineMath math="Aa" />)**. List the four outcomes of <InlineMath math="Aa \times Aa" />, then remove <InlineMath math="aa" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
Aa \times Aa &\to AA,\ Aa,\ Aa,\ aa \\
\text{remaining non-albino} &: AA,\ Aa,\ Aa \\
P(Aa \mid \text{not albino}) &= \frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}" />

Follow the reduced sample space in the diagram below to see why <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{3}" />, not <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}" />, is the exact carrier probability.

<Mermaid
  title={<>Reduced Sample Space for a Non-Albino Offspring</>}
  description={
    <>
      Crossing <InlineMath math="Aa \times Aa" /> creates four equally likely
      outcomes. Ruling out <InlineMath math="aa" /> leaves three possibilities,
      two of which are <InlineMath math="Aa" />.
    </>
  }
  footer={
    <>
      Removing <InlineMath math="aa" /> leaves <InlineMath math="AA, Aa, Aa" />, so <InlineMath math="2" /> of <InlineMath math="3" /> are carriers.
    </>
  }
  chart={`flowchart TD
    C["Aa x Aa: 4 equal outcomes"] --> K1["AA: non-albino, non-carrier"]
    C --> K2["Aa: non-albino, carrier"]
    C --> K3["Aa: non-albino, carrier"]
    C --> OUT["aa: albino (ruled out)"]`}
/>

Check that the remaining probabilities sum to <InlineMath math="1" />: <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{3}" /> for <InlineMath math="AA" /> plus <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{3}" /> for <InlineMath math="Aa" /> gives <InlineMath math="1" />.

## Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}" /> counts <InlineMath math="2" /> out of all <InlineMath math="4" /> outcomes, forgetting that **<InlineMath math="aa" /> is already ruled out** because the daughter is not albino.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{4}" /> is the unconditional chance of an albino child (<InlineMath math="aa" />), not a carrier.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{4}" /> is the unconditional chance of any non-albino child (<InlineMath math="AA" /> or <InlineMath math="Aa" />), which includes non-carriers.

# Answer

The probability that the daughter carries the albinism allele is **<InlineMath math="\frac{2}{3}" />**.

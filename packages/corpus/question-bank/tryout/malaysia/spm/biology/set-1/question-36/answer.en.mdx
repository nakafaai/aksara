# The parents' genotypes, then the condition on the daughter

The albino son is <InlineMath math="aa" />, so each parent gave one <InlineMath math="a" /> allele. Since neither parent is albino, **both must be <InlineMath math="Aa" />**. The cross <InlineMath math="Aa\times Aa" /> gives <InlineMath math="AA" />, <InlineMath math="Aa" />, <InlineMath math="Aa" />, <InlineMath math="aa" /> with equal chances.

The daughter is known not to be albino, so <InlineMath math="aa" /> is ruled out. Three equally likely possibilities remain: <InlineMath math="AA" />, <InlineMath math="Aa" />, <InlineMath math="Aa" />.

<BlockMath math="P(Aa\mid\text{not albino})=\frac{2}{3}" />

So the probability that the daughter carries the albinism allele is <InlineMath math="\tfrac23" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> is the chance of <InlineMath math="Aa" /> among all possible children, but **<InlineMath math="aa" /> is already ruled out** because the daughter is not albino.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> is the chance of a child being albino, not of being a carrier.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac34" /> is the chance of a child not being albino, not of being a carrier.

# Given

All three animals have a backbone, so every path starts at step <InlineMath math="1a" />. The **bat** has skin-covered wings, fur, and feeds its young on milk.

The **turtle** has dry scaly skin, and the **frog** has moist skin without scales.

# Asked

Find the first step where the dichotomous key separates the turtle from the frog, and the first step where it separates the turtle from the bat.

# Solution

A dichotomous key works like a hallway with labeled doors that split into two choices every time. You never jump ahead using outside guesses like flying, only the exact feature written at each door.

> Two organisms stay on the same branch until they reach the first paired statement where their stated traits differ.

Trace all three animals step by step through the key to pinpoint each **branching split**.

\end{aligned}" />

Because **neither the turtle nor the frog has fur or produces milk**, they stay together through step <InlineMath math="3b" /> and split only at step <InlineMath math="4" />. Meanwhile, the bat takes <InlineMath math="3a" /> while the turtle takes <InlineMath math="3b" />, so they split at step <InlineMath math="3" />.

## Why the other options are wrong

- Step <InlineMath math="2" /> for the bat assumes wings mean feathers, yet **a bat's wings are covered in skin, not feathers**, so it passes <InlineMath math="2b" /> to step <InlineMath math="3" />.
- Step <InlineMath math="3" /> for the turtle and frog forgets that both lack fur and milk, so both continue together to step <InlineMath math="4" />.
- Pairing step <InlineMath math="3" /> and step <InlineMath math="2" /> commits both mistakes at once.

# Answer

The key first separates the turtle from the frog at step **<InlineMath math="4" />**, and first separates the turtle from the bat at step **<InlineMath math="3" />**.

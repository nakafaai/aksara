# Given

At full saturation (<InlineMath math="100\%" />), every <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of blood carries **<InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen**. Haemoglobin saturation is <InlineMath math="98\%" /> leaving the lungs and drops to <InlineMath math="25\%" /> leaving an active muscle.

During exercise, blood flows through the muscle at <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ dm}^3\text{ min}^{-1}" /> (<InlineMath math="2000\text{ cm}^3\text{ min}^{-1}" />).

# Asked

What volume of oxygen is released to the muscle every minute?

# Solution

Picture each <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of blood as a delivery van that holds <InlineMath math="20" /> boxes of oxygen when full. It leaves the lungs <InlineMath math="98\%" /> loaded and leaves the muscle <InlineMath math="25\%" /> loaded, so **each van drops off only the difference, <InlineMath math="73\%" /> of its capacity**.

> Oxygen delivered to tissue equals the drop in saturation between blood entering and leaving the muscle, not the full oxygen load from the lungs.

The lower the venous saturation drops, the steeper the delivery line rises. At <InlineMath math="a = 25\%" /> and <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ dm}^3" />, the green marker lands at <InlineMath math="292\text{ cm}^3" />.

## Minute delivery calculation

Compute oxygen released per <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of blood, then multiply by the **<InlineMath math="20" /> lots of <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />** passing each minute.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{per } 100\text{ cm}^3 &= 20 \times (0.98 - 0.25) = 20 \times 0.73 = 14.6\text{ cm}^3 \\
\text{per minute} &= 14.6 \times \frac{2000}{100} = 14.6 \times 20 = 292\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

Quick check: total oxygen arriving is <InlineMath math="392\text{ cm}^3" />, leftover venous oxygen is <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />, and their difference is <InlineMath math="292\text{ cm}^3" />.

## Why other options fail

- Choosing <InlineMath math="392\text{ cm}^3" /> counts all oxygen carried from the lungs, forgetting that **<InlineMath math="25\%" /> still leaves in venous blood**.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> gives the oxygen remaining in blood leaving the muscle rather than the oxygen released.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="14.6\text{ cm}^3" /> is for <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of blood only and skips scaling to <InlineMath math="2000\text{ cm}^3" /> per minute.

# Answer

A volume of **<InlineMath math="292\text{ cm}^3" />** of oxygen is released to the muscle every minute.

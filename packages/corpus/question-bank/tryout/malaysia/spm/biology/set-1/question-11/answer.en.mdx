# Saturation difference, then the blood volume

The oxygen released to the muscle is the oxygen carried when blood leaves the lungs **minus the oxygen still left** when it leaves the muscle. Work it out per <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of blood, then multiply by the blood volume per minute, <InlineMath math="2000\text{ cm}^3" />, which is <InlineMath math="20" /> lots of <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{per }100\text{ cm}^3&=20\times(0.98-0.25)=14.6\text{ cm}^3\\
\text{per minute}&=14.6\times\frac{2000}{100}=292\text{ cm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

So the muscle receives about <InlineMath math="292\text{ cm}^3" /> of oxygen every minute.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="392\text{ cm}^3" /> is all the oxygen carried from the lungs, but **some oxygen returns with the blood** at <InlineMath math="25\%" /> saturation.
- <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> is the oxygen still left in the blood leaving the muscle.
- <InlineMath math="14.6\text{ cm}^3" /> is for <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of blood only, not yet multiplied by the flow per minute.

# Share of cells equals share of time

Because the number of cells in each stage is proportional to its duration, **the fraction of cells in a stage equals the fraction of the cycle time** that stage takes. Then multiply by the length of one cycle.

Look at a real root tip squash first: count how few cells are caught in each stage of mitosis.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{fraction in metaphase}&=\frac{20}{400}=0.05\\
\text{metaphase duration}&=0.05\times20=1.0\text{ hour}\\
\text{cells reaching metaphase}&=\frac{400}{20}=20\text{ per hour}\\
\text{after }3\text{ hours}&=20+3\times20=80
\end{aligned}" />

Check: all of mitosis holds <InlineMath math="100" /> cells, which is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{100}{400}\times20=5.0" /> hours, and metaphase is a small part of it. Colchicine holds cells in metaphase, so **the cells already there stay and new cells keep arriving**. So metaphase normally lasts <InlineMath math="1.0" /> hour and after <InlineMath math="3" /> hours there are <InlineMath math="80" /> cells in metaphase.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="60" /> cells counts only the new arrivals, but **the <InlineMath math="20" /> cells already in metaphase are held too**.
- <InlineMath math="20" /> cells assumes the number stays as normal, but colchicine stops cells leaving metaphase.
- <InlineMath math="5.0" /> hours is the length of the whole of mitosis, not metaphase alone.

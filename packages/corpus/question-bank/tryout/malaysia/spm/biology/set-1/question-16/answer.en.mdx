# The mass of urea is fixed, the volume of water shrinks

Urea is filtered at the same concentration as in plasma and is not reabsorbed. So **all the urea in the filtrate ends up in the urine**. Only the volume of water changes, because most of the water is reabsorbed and the urea becomes concentrated.

Watch the urea grains: none leave the tubule, so they bunch up as the water is taken back.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{urea per day}&=180\text{ dm}^3\times0.3\text{ g dm}^{-3}=54\text{ g}\\
\text{urine volume}&=\frac{54\text{ g}}{18\text{ g dm}^{-3}}=3.0\text{ dm}^3
\end{aligned}" />

A concentration of <InlineMath math="0.03\text{ g}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> is <InlineMath math="0.3\text{ g dm}^{-3}" />, and <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ g}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> is <InlineMath math="18\text{ g dm}^{-3}" />. The urea becomes <InlineMath math="60" /> times more concentrated, so the volume is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{180}{60}=3.0\text{ dm}^3" />. So <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ dm}^3" /> of urine is produced a day.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.30\text{ dm}^3" /> converts the unit wrongly, using <InlineMath math="180\text{ g dm}^{-3}" /> for urine, but **<InlineMath math="1.8\text{ g}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> is <InlineMath math="18\text{ g dm}^{-3}" />**.
- <InlineMath math="60\text{ dm}^3" /> is the concentration factor of urea, not the volume of urine.
- <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ dm}^3" /> uses the urea concentration in urine as a volume.

# From the BMI target to the number of days

First find the mass to lose with <InlineMath math="\text{BMI}=\dfrac{m}{h^2}" />, using **the height squared**. Then turn that mass into energy and divide by the daily deficit.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
m_{\text{target}}&=22.5\times1.60^2=57.6\text{ kg}\\
\Delta m&=64-57.6=6.4\text{ kg}\\
\text{energy}&=6.4\times40{,}000=256{,}000\text{ kJ}\\
\text{deficit}&=11{,}000-9000=2000\text{ kJ per day}\\
\text{days}&=\frac{256{,}000}{2000}=128
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="128" /> days are needed.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="560" /> days uses the height <InlineMath math="1.60" /> without squaring it, so the mass to lose becomes <InlineMath math="28\text{ kg}" />.
- <InlineMath math="80" /> days uses a loss of <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ kg}" />, the BMI difference <InlineMath math="2.5" /> times <InlineMath math="1.60" />, but **the height must be squared**.
- <InlineMath math="50" /> days treats the BMI difference <InlineMath math="2.5" /> as a mass difference, but BMI and mass have different units.

# Given

A newborn receives <InlineMath math="160" /> units of maternal antibodies across the placenta that **halve every <InlineMath math="30" /> days**. Protection holds while the antibody level stays at or above <InlineMath math="20" /> units.

After the first injection of vaccine X, the baby's own antibodies take a <InlineMath math="14" />-day lag to reach <InlineMath math="20" /> units.

# Asked

On which day at the latest must the first vaccine be given so the baby stays continuously protected, and what type of immunity do the mother's antibodies provide?

# Solution

Picture the mother's antibodies as a borrowed bottle of <InlineMath math="160" /> drops that loses half every <InlineMath math="30" /> days (<InlineMath math="160" /> to <InlineMath math="80" /> to <InlineMath math="40" /> to <InlineMath math="20" /> on day <InlineMath math="90" />). Because the baby's immune system needs <InlineMath math="14" /> days to ramp up, **the vaccine must be given <InlineMath math="14" /> days before the borrowed supply hits the threshold**.

> The latest safe vaccination day is found by counting backwards by the immune response lag from the day maternal antibodies fall to the protection threshold.

Whenever <InlineMath math="a" /> goes past day <InlineMath math="76" />, the green ready marker slides right of the orange day-<InlineMath math="90" /> marker, leaving an unprotected gap.

## Half life and immunity type

Count how many halvings take <InlineMath math="160" /> down to <InlineMath math="20" /> units, then subtract the **<InlineMath math="14" />-day vaccine lag**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
160 \to 80 \to 40 \to 20 &: 3 \times 30 = 90\text{ days} \\
\text{latest vaccine day} &= 90 - 14 = \text{day } 76
\end{aligned}" />

Because the baby receives ready-made antibodies naturally across the placenta rather than making them itself, this is **natural passive immunity**.

## Why other options fail

- Day <InlineMath math="90" /> ignores the <InlineMath math="14" />-day lag, leaving **the baby unprotected from day <InlineMath math="90" /> to day <InlineMath math="104" />**.
- Natural active immunity requires the baby's own body to produce antibodies after infection, not receive them from the mother.
- Day <InlineMath math="104" /> adds the lag after maternal protection ends instead of subtracting it beforehand.

# Answer

The first vaccine must be given by day **<InlineMath math="76" />** at the latest, and the mother's antibodies give natural passive immunity.

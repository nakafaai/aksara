# When the mother's antibodies run out, then work back

The mother's antibodies halve every <InlineMath math="30" /> days. **The vaccine must come earlier by the <InlineMath math="14" /> day lag**, so that the baby's own antibodies are ready just as the mother's fall below the threshold.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
160\to80\to40\to20&:\ 3\times30=90\text{ days}\\
\text{latest vaccine day}&=90-14=76
\end{aligned}" />

The mother's antibodies are not made by the baby's body and arrive naturally across the placenta, so the immunity is natural passive. So the vaccine must be given by day <InlineMath math="76" /> at the latest.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Day <InlineMath math="90" /> forgets the <InlineMath math="14" /> day lag, so **the baby is unprotected from day <InlineMath math="90" /> to day <InlineMath math="104" />**.
- Active immunity means the baby's body makes its own antibodies, but these antibodies come from the mother.
- Day <InlineMath math="104" /> adds the lag, but the vaccine must come before the mother's antibodies run out, not after.

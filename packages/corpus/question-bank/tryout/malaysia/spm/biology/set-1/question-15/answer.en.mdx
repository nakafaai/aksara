# Given

In a reflex arc, a **sensory neurone of length <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ m}" />** conducts impulses at <InlineMath math="80\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, and a motor neurone of length <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ m}" /> conducts at <InlineMath math="40\text{ m s}^{-1}" />.

The relay neurone in the spinal cord has negligible conduction time, and each synapse delays the impulse by <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ ms}" />.

# Asked

What is the minimum time from receptor stimulation until the impulse reaches the effector?

# Solution

Picture a two-runner relay race: the first runner covers <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ m}" /> fast (<InlineMath math="10\text{ ms}" />), the second runner covers <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ m}" /> slower (<InlineMath math="15\text{ ms}" />), and **the relay neurone creates two baton handoffs (synapses) in the spinal cord** (sensory to relay, then relay to motor).

> Total travel time across a three-neurone reflex arc equals the sum of each individual axon time plus the delay across exactly two synapses.

Slide the synapse count <InlineMath math="n" /> on the cumulative time graph below to see how synaptic delay shifts the total arrival time to <InlineMath math="26.0\text{ ms}" />.

The motor segment climbs twice as steeply as the sensory segment because it conducts at half the speed. Setting <InlineMath math="n = 2" /> lifts the final marker from <InlineMath math="25.0" /> to <InlineMath math="26.0\text{ ms}" />.

## Segment by segment timing

Time each neurone separately in milliseconds, then add **the two synaptic delays**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
t_{\text{sensory}} &= \frac{0.8}{80} = 0.010\text{ s} = 10.0\text{ ms} \\
t_{\text{motor}} &= \frac{0.6}{40} = 0.015\text{ s} = 15.0\text{ ms} \\
t_{\text{synapses}} &= 2 \times 0.5 = 1.0\text{ ms} \\
t_{\text{total}} &= 10.0 + 15.0 + 1.0 = 26.0\text{ ms}
\end{aligned}" />

Unit check: both axon times are converted from seconds into milliseconds before summing with the synaptic delay.

## Why other options fail

- Choosing <InlineMath math="25.0\text{ ms}" /> ignores synaptic delay, even though **two synapses add <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ ms}" />**.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="24.3\text{ ms}" /> averages the speeds to <InlineMath math="60\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, even though each neurone runs a different distance.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="26.5\text{ ms}" /> counts three synapses instead of the two spinal synapses in this reflex arc.

# Answer

The minimum time for the impulse to reach the effector is <InlineMath math="26.0\text{ ms}" />.

# Given

Benedict's colour standards are green (<InlineMath math="0.5\%" />), yellow (<InlineMath math="1.0\%" />), orange (<InlineMath math="1.5\%" />), and brick red (<InlineMath math="2.0\%" />). The fruit juice is **diluted <InlineMath math="4" /> times** before testing.

Portion A (direct test) turns yellow (<InlineMath math="1.0\%" />), while Portion B (acid hydrolysis, neutralised, then tested) turns brick red (<InlineMath math="2.0\%" />).

# Asked

What are the percentages of reducing sugar and non-reducing sugar in the original undiluted juice?

# Solution

Picture watering down fruit syrup <InlineMath math="4" /> times before tasting it. To recover the true strength of the original bottle, **every diluted reading must be multiplied back by <InlineMath math="4" />**.

Portion A detects only reducing sugar, whereas Portion B detects all sugar after hydrolysis breaks non-reducing sugar into reducing units.

> The difference between the post-hydrolysis and direct readings gives the non-reducing sugar, and every diluted reading must be scaled by the dilution factor.

The vertical gap between the two lines matches the height of the blue line at every dilution. At <InlineMath math="d = 4" />, both sugars equal <InlineMath math="4\%" />.

## Original juice calculation

Multiply both diluted readings by <InlineMath math="4" />, then subtract **reducing sugar from total sugar**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{reducing sugar} &= 1.0\% \times 4 = 4\% \\
\text{total sugar} &= 2.0\% \times 4 = 8\% \\
\text{non-reducing sugar} &= 8\% - 4\% = 4\%
\end{aligned}" />

Quick check: diluting <InlineMath math="4\%" /> reducing and <InlineMath math="4\%" /> non-reducing sugar by <InlineMath math="4" /> yields <InlineMath math="1.0\%" /> (yellow) in Portion A and <InlineMath math="2.0\%" /> (brick red) in Portion B.

## Why other options fail

- Choosing <InlineMath math="1\%" /> and <InlineMath math="1\%" /> forgets to multiply by <InlineMath math="4" />, even though **the tested juice was diluted**.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="4\%" /> and <InlineMath math="8\%" /> treats total sugar from Portion B as non-reducing sugar without subtracting Portion A.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="8\%" /> and <InlineMath math="4\%" /> assigns total sugar to reducing sugar, even though Portion A only turned yellow.

# Answer

The original fruit juice contains <InlineMath math="4\%" /> reducing sugar and <InlineMath math="4\%" /> non-reducing sugar.

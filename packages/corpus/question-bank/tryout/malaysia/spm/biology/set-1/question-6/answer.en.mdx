# Two readings, one dilution factor

Portion A detects only reducing sugar. Portion B detects reducing sugar plus the non-reducing sugar after hydrolysis. **The difference between the readings is the non-reducing sugar**, then multiply by the dilution factor <InlineMath math="4" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{reducing sugar}&=1.0\%\times4=4\%\\
\text{total sugar}&=2.0\%\times4=8\%\\
\text{non-reducing sugar}&=8\%-4\%=4\%
\end{aligned}" />

So the original juice contains <InlineMath math="4\%" /> reducing sugar and <InlineMath math="4\%" /> non-reducing sugar.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1\%" /> and <InlineMath math="1\%" /> forgets to multiply by <InlineMath math="4" />, but **the juice tested had been diluted**.
- <InlineMath math="8\%" /> for the non-reducing sugar uses the portion B reading directly, but that reading still includes the reducing sugar.
- <InlineMath math="8\%" /> for the reducing sugar swaps the two readings, but reducing sugar shows up without hydrolysis.

# Oxygen used, then the dilution factor

BOD is the oxygen used by microorganisms over <InlineMath math="5" /> days in the dark, where no photosynthesis adds oxygen. **The sample was diluted twofold**, so the fall in oxygen must be multiplied by two.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{oxygen used}&=9.0-2.5=6.5\text{ mg dm}^{-3}\\
\text{BOD}&=6.5\times2=13\text{ mg dm}^{-3}
\end{aligned}" />

A value of <InlineMath math="13" /> is above <InlineMath math="10" />, so the river water is heavily polluted.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.5\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" /> forgets the dilution factor, but **the original sample is twice as concentrated**.
- <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" /> doubles the oxygen left, but BOD is the oxygen used up.
- <InlineMath math="23\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" /> adds the two readings, but what is needed is their difference.

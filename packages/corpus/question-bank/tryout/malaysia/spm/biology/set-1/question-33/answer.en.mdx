# Given

The river water is **diluted twofold** (dilution factor <InlineMath math="2" />). Its initial dissolved oxygen is <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" />, and after <InlineMath math="5" /> days in the dark at <InlineMath math="20^\circ\text{C}" /> it drops to <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" />.

Water is clean below <InlineMath math="3" />, moderately polluted from <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="10" />, and **heavily polluted above <InlineMath math="10\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" />**.

# Asked

Find the biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) of the river water and classify its water quality.

# Solution

Imagine tasting sweet syrup after mixing it half-and-half with plain water. Whatever sweetness you measure in the diluted glass, the original syrup was twice as strong, so you multiply the measured drop by two.

> Biochemical oxygen demand measures the oxygen consumed in the dark multiplied by the dilution factor of the sample.

First subtract the final oxygen reading from the initial reading to get the **oxygen consumed in the diluted bottle**, then multiply by <InlineMath math="2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta \mathrm{O}_2 &= 9.0 - 2.5 = 6.5\text{ mg dm}^{-3} \\
\text{BOD} &= 6.5 \times 2 = 13.0\text{ mg dm}^{-3}
\end{aligned}" />

Because **<InlineMath math="13\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" /> exceeds <InlineMath math="10\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" />**, the river water falls firmly into the heavily polluted class.

## Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6.5\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" /> omits the twofold dilution factor, **ignoring that the undiluted river sample is twice as concentrated**.
- <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" /> doubles the remaining oxygen (<InlineMath math="2.5" />) instead of the oxygen consumed.
- <InlineMath math="23\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" /> adds the two readings (<InlineMath math="9.0 + 2.5 = 11.5" />) before doubling instead of taking their difference.

# Answer

The BOD of the river sample is **<InlineMath math="13\text{ mg dm}^{-3}" />**, and the river water is heavily polluted.

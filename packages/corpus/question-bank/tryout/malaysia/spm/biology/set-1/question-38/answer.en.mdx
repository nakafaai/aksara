# Given

A **circular plasmid** contains <InlineMath math="6000" /> base pairs (bp). A restriction enzyme makes **three complete cuts** at positions <InlineMath math="1000" />, <InlineMath math="2500" />, and <InlineMath math="4500" /> bp measured around the ring from a single reference mark.

# Asked

Find how many DNA fragments are produced and the length of the largest fragment.

# Solution

Imagine cutting a closed rubber band with scissors. The very first snip only opens the loop into one piece, so three snips on a closed ring create exactly <InlineMath math="3" /> pieces, not <InlineMath math="4" />.

> A circular plasmid has no free ends, so <InlineMath math="n" /> restriction cuts always yield <InlineMath math="n" /> fragments, and the last fragment wraps past the reference mark back to the first cut.

Measure the **distance between adjacent cut sites** around the <InlineMath math="6000" /> bp circle, remembering that position <InlineMath math="6000" /> joins seamlessly to position <InlineMath math="0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1000 \to 2500 &: 2500 - 1000 = 1500\text{ bp} \\
2500 \to 4500 &: 4500 - 2500 = 2000\text{ bp} \\
4500 \to 6000 \to 1000 &: (6000 - 4500) + 1000 = 2500\text{ bp}
\end{aligned}" />

## Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4" /> fragments treats the plasmid as a linear strand, ignoring that **the first cut merely opens the closed loop**.
- <InlineMath math="3500" /> bp subtracts <InlineMath math="4500 - 1000" />, measuring across the cut at <InlineMath math="2500" /> instead of wrapping past <InlineMath math="6000" />.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> fragments with largest <InlineMath math="2000" /> bp forgets to join the <InlineMath math="4500" /> to <InlineMath math="6000" /> span (<InlineMath math="1500" /> bp) with the <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="1000" /> span (<InlineMath math="1000" /> bp).

# Answer

Exactly **<InlineMath math="3" />** fragments are produced, and the largest fragment is **<InlineMath math="2500" />** base pairs long.

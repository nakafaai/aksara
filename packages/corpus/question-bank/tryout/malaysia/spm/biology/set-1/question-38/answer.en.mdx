# A circle has no ends

Linear DNA cut three times gives four pieces, but **a circular plasmid has no ends**, so three cuts give only three pieces. The last piece crosses the reference point and joins up with the start.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1000\to2500&:\ 1500\\
2500\to4500&:\ 2000\\
4500\to6000\to1000&:\ 1500+1000=2500\\
\text{total}&=1500+2000+2500=6000
\end{aligned}" />

So there are <InlineMath math="3" /> fragments and the largest is <InlineMath math="2500" /> base pairs.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4" /> fragments treats the plasmid as linear DNA, but **the first cut only opens the circle**.
- <InlineMath math="3500" /> subtracts <InlineMath math="4500-1000" />, but that fragment runs from <InlineMath math="4500" /> past the reference point to <InlineMath math="1000" />.
- <InlineMath math="3" /> fragments with a largest of <InlineMath math="2000" /> counts only the part from <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="1000" /> and forgets the part from <InlineMath math="4500" /> to <InlineMath math="6000" />.

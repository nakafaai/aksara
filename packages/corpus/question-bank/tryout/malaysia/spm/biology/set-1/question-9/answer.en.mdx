# Given

One **diploid parent cell with <InlineMath math="2n = 8" />** divides by mitosis twice in a row. Every daughter cell then undergoes meiosis.

# Asked

How many gametes form in total, and how many chromatids exist across all cells at metaphase II?

# Solution

Picture one cell as a box holding <InlineMath math="8" /> pencils. Two rounds of mitosis **double the boxes to <InlineMath math="4" /> identical diploid cells**.

When meiosis starts, each box first splits into <InlineMath math="2" /> cells by metaphase II (<InlineMath math="8" /> cells total). Each cell now holds half as many chromosomes (<InlineMath math="n = 4" />), yet every chromosome is still X-shaped with <InlineMath math="2" /> sister chromatids attached.

> At metaphase II each cell already carries the haploid chromosome count (<InlineMath math="n" />), but every chromosome still holds two sister chromatids.

Each extra round of mitosis doubles every count. At <InlineMath math="k = 2" />, the graph reads <InlineMath math="16" /> gametes and <InlineMath math="64" /> chromatids.

## Cell and chromatid count

Track **the cells and chromatids** sequentially from the two mitosis rounds through metaphase II to the final gametes.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{cells after }2\text{ mitoses} &= 2^2 = 4\text{ diploid cells} \\
\text{total gametes} &= 4 \times 4 = 16\text{ gametes} \\
\text{cells at metaphase II} &= 4 \times 2 = 8\text{ haploid cells} \\
\text{total chromatids at metaphase II} &= 8 \times 4 \times 2 = 64\text{ chromatids}
\end{aligned}" />

Quick check: after metaphase II those <InlineMath math="64" /> chromatids split evenly across <InlineMath math="16" /> gametes, giving <InlineMath math="4" /> chromosomes per gamete (<InlineMath math="16 \times 4 = 64" />).

## Why other options fail

- Choosing <InlineMath math="16" /> and <InlineMath math="32" /> counts chromosomes at metaphase II, forgetting that **each chromosome still has two chromatids**.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="8" /> and <InlineMath math="64" /> uses the metaphase II cell count as gametes, forgetting the second meiotic division.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="16" /> and <InlineMath math="128" /> assigns <InlineMath math="8" /> chromosomes per metaphase II cell, even though meiosis I already halved the count.

# Answer

A total of <InlineMath math="16" /> gametes are produced and there are <InlineMath math="64" /> chromatids altogether at metaphase II.

# Count the cells at each stage

Two rounds of mitosis give <InlineMath math="2^2=4" /> diploid cells. Each of them undergoes meiosis, so at metaphase II there are <InlineMath math="4\times2=8" /> cells, and **each of those cells carries <InlineMath math="4" /> chromosomes, each still made of two chromatids**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{after mitosis}&=2^2=4\text{ cells}\\
\text{gametes}&=4\times4=16\\
\text{cells at metaphase II}&=4\times2=8\\
\text{chromatids}&=8\times4\times2=64
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="16" /> gametes form and there are <InlineMath math="64" /> chromatids at metaphase II.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="32" /> counts chromosomes at metaphase II, but **each chromosome is still made of two chromatids**.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> gametes is the number of cells at metaphase II, but each of those cells divides once more.
- <InlineMath math="128" /> gives each metaphase II cell <InlineMath math="8" /> chromosomes, but meiosis I has already halved the chromosome number.

# The base-pairing rule

Adenine always pairs with thymine and guanine with cytosine, so **A equals T, and G equals C**. What is left after A and T is shared equally between G and C.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A=T&=30\%\ \Rightarrow\ G+C=40\%\ \Rightarrow\ C=20\%\\
C&=0.20\times2000=400\\
\text{A and T pairs}&=0.30\times2000=600,\quad\text{G and C pairs}=400\\
\text{hydrogen bonds}&=600(2)+400(3)=2400
\end{aligned}" />

So there are <InlineMath math="400" /> cytosine bases and <InlineMath math="2400" /> hydrogen bonds.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="400" /> and <InlineMath math="2600" /> swaps the bond numbers, but **the G and C pairs have three bonds**.
- <InlineMath math="600" /> and <InlineMath math="2600" /> assumes there is as much cytosine as adenine, but cytosine pairs with guanine.
- <InlineMath math="800" /> and <InlineMath math="2800" /> takes the whole <InlineMath math="40\%" /> as cytosine, but that <InlineMath math="40\%" /> is shared equally with guanine.

# Given

Red-green colour blindness is controlled by an **X-linked recessive allele** (<InlineMath math="\mathrm{X}^b" />). The woman has normal vision, yet her father is colour-blind (<InlineMath math="\mathrm{X}^b\mathrm{Y}" />).

She marries a colour-blind man (<InlineMath math="\mathrm{X}^b\mathrm{Y}" />).

# Asked

Find the probability that their first son is colour-blind, and the probability that their first child is a colour-blind daughter.

# Solution

Think of four equally likely genetic tickets drawn from a hat: two boy tickets and two girl tickets. Knowing the child is already a son lets you look inside only the <InlineMath math="2" /> boy tickets, whereas asking about the first child keeps all <InlineMath math="4" /> tickets in play.

> A daughter always inherits her father's single X chromosome, so a woman with normal vision whose father is colour-blind must be a carrier.

Because her father could only pass on <InlineMath math="\mathrm{X}^b" /> and she sees normally, **her genotype is <InlineMath math="\mathrm{X}^B\mathrm{X}^b" />**. Cross her gametes (<InlineMath math="\mathrm{X}^B, \mathrm{X}^b" />) with her husband's gametes (<InlineMath math="\mathrm{X}^b, \mathrm{Y}" />).

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{X}^B\mathrm{X}^b \times \mathrm{X}^b\mathrm{Y} &\to \mathrm{X}^B\mathrm{X}^b,\ \mathrm{X}^b\mathrm{X}^b,\ \mathrm{X}^B\mathrm{Y},\ \mathrm{X}^b\mathrm{Y} \\
P(\text{colour-blind} \mid \text{son}) &= \frac{1}{2} \\
P(\text{colour-blind daughter}) &= \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}" />

Notice the distinction in **sample space**: the first part fixes the child as a son (denominator <InlineMath math="2" />), while the second part covers every possible first child (denominator <InlineMath math="4" />).

## Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{4}" /> for the son divides by all <InlineMath math="4" /> children, ignoring that **the child is already known to be a son**.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> assumes daughters cannot be colour-blind, forgetting that both parents can contribute <InlineMath math="\mathrm{X}^b" /> to form <InlineMath math="\mathrm{X}^b\mathrm{X}^b" />.
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}" /> for the daughter counts only among daughters rather than among all possible first children.

# Answer

The probability that their first son is colour-blind is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}" />, and the probability that their first child is a colour-blind daughter is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{4}" />.

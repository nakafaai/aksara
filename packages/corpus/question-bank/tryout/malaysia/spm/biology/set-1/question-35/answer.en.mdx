# Genotypes from the family, then read the question

The woman's father is colour-blind, so he passed <InlineMath math="\text{X}^b" /> to her. Her vision is normal, so **she is a carrier**, <InlineMath math="\text{X}^B\text{X}^b" />. Her husband is <InlineMath math="\text{X}^b\text{Y}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{X}^B\text{X}^b\times\text{X}^b\text{Y}\ \rightarrow\ &\text{X}^B\text{X}^b,\ \text{X}^b\text{X}^b,\ \text{X}^B\text{Y},\ \text{X}^b\text{Y}\\
P(\text{colour-blind}\mid\text{son})&=\tfrac12\\
P(\text{colour-blind daughter})&=\tfrac14
\end{aligned}" />

The first question already knows the child is a son, so the probability is counted among sons only. The second question covers all children. So the answer is <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> for the son counts among all children, but **the child is already known to be a son**.
- <InlineMath math="0" /> assumes daughters cannot be colour-blind, but the father gives <InlineMath math="\text{X}^b" /> and the mother can give <InlineMath math="\text{X}^b" /> too.
- <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> for the daughter counts among daughters only, but the question asks about the first child out of all outcomes.

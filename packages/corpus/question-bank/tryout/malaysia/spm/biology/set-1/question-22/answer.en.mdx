# Given

Four statements describe stomatal opening during the day: **statement I** (active transport of potassium ions into guard cells), **statement II** (water diffuses out so guard cells become turgid), **statement III** (the thicker inner wall beside the pore makes turgid guard cells curve open), and **statement IV** (stomata close at night because guard cells photosynthesise faster).

# Asked

Which of the statements about stomatal opening during the day are correct?

# Solution

Picture two curved sausages taped with extra-thick tape along their inner faces. When potassium ions are pumped into the guard cells in daylight, water rushes in behind them so **the guard cells swell tightly (turgid) and bow outwards to open the stomatal pore**.

> Stomatal opening follows one causal chain: active potassium uptake lowers water potential, water enters by osmosis until guard cells turn turgid, and the thicker inner wall forces them to curve apart.

## Checking all four statements

Test every statement against the **osmotic mechanism** of guard cells.

- **Statement I is correct** because potassium ions (<InlineMath math="\text{K}^+" />) are actively transported into guard cells during daylight.
- **Statement II is wrong** because water diffuses into (not out of) guard cells by osmosis to make them turgid.
- **Statement III is correct** because the thicker, less elastic inner wall forces turgid guard cells to bow away from the pore.
- **Statement IV is wrong** because there is no light for photosynthesis at night, so potassium ions and water leave and the stoma closes.

## Why other options fail

- Choosing I, II, and III accepts statement II, even though guard cells turn turgid because **water enters**, not leaves.
- Choosing I, III, and IV accepts statement IV, even though photosynthesis cannot occur in darkness at night.
- Choosing II and IV discards both correct statements and picks only the two reversed claims.

# Answer

Only statements I and III are **correct**.

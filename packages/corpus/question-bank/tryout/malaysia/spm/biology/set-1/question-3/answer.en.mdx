# Given

Cell P is a cube of side <InlineMath math="10\,\mu\text{m}" />. Cell R has the same volume as a flat cuboid measuring <InlineMath math="40\,\mu\text{m} \times 10\,\mu\text{m} \times 2.5\,\mu\text{m}" />.

Both cells hold **the exact same volume of <InlineMath math="1000\,\mu\text{m}^3" />**.

# Asked

How many times larger is the surface area to volume ratio of cell R than that of cell P?

# Solution

Picture pressing a cube of clay into a thin flat tile. The amount of clay inside stays identical, yet **far more of its outer skin now faces the air**.

> When two shapes share the same volume, the ratio of their surface area to volume ratios equals the ratio of their surface areas.

Slide the cell thickness <InlineMath math="h" /> below while keeping width <InlineMath math="10\,\mu\text{m}" /> and volume fixed at <InlineMath math="1000\,\mu\text{m}^3" />, then compare cube P with flat cell R.

The flatter the cell gets, the steeper its surface area climbs. At <InlineMath math="h = 2.5\,\mu\text{m}" />, cell R sits well above the cube P reference line.

## Area and ratio calculation

Compute both surface areas, then divide the **surface area to volume ratios** so the shared volume cancels out.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_P &= 6(10)^2 = 600\,\mu\text{m}^2 \\
A_R &= 2(40 \times 10 + 40 \times 2.5 + 10 \times 2.5) = 2(400 + 100 + 25) = 1050\,\mu\text{m}^2 \\
\frac{A_R / V}{A_P / V} &= \frac{A_R}{A_P} = \frac{1050}{600} = 1.75
\end{aligned}" />

Quick check: cell P has a ratio of <InlineMath math="0.60\,\mu\text{m}^{-1}" /> while cell R reaches <InlineMath math="1.05\,\mu\text{m}^{-1}" />. Red blood cells use this same flat disc design to exchange oxygen faster.

## Why other options fail

- Choosing <InlineMath math="1.00" /> assumes equal volumes force equal ratios, yet **their surface areas differ sharply**.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="4.00" /> only divides the longest edges <InlineMath math="40" /> and <InlineMath math="10" />, ignoring all six faces.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="0.57" /> flips the division to P over R, even though flat cell R has the larger ratio.

# Answer

The surface area to volume ratio of cell R is **<InlineMath math="1.75" />** times larger than that of cell P.

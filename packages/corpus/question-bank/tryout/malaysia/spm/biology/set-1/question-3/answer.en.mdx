# Same volume, different shape

Both cells have a volume of <InlineMath math="1000\,\mu\text{m}^3" />, so the difference lies only in the surface area. **A flat shape spreads the same volume over a larger surface**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
A_P&=6(10)^2=600\,\mu\text{m}^2\\
A_R&=2(40\times10+40\times2.5+10\times2.5)=2(400+100+25)=1050\,\mu\text{m}^2\\
\frac{A_R/V}{A_P/V}&=\frac{1050}{600}=1.75
\end{aligned}" />

So the ratio of cell R is <InlineMath math="1.75" /> times that of cell P. The same principle gives red blood cells their flat disc shape.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="1.00" /> assumes equal volumes give equal ratios, but **the surface areas differ**.
- <InlineMath math="4.00" /> compares the longest sides, <InlineMath math="40" /> and <InlineMath math="10" />, not the surface areas.
- <InlineMath math="0.57" /> inverts the comparison, but cell R has the larger ratio.

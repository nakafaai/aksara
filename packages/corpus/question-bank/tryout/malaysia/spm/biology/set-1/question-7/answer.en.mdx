# Given

At <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" />, an amylase solution hydrolyses <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ g}" /> of starch in <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes at a constant saturated rate. The reaction has **temperature coefficient <InlineMath math="Q_{10} = 2" />**, so a <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" /> drop halves the reaction rate.

# Asked

How many minutes does the same amylase solution take to hydrolyse <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ g}" /> of starch at <InlineMath math="27^\circ\text{C}" />?

# Solution

Picture a chef slicing <InlineMath math="1\text{ kg}" /> of carrots in <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes in a warm kitchen. Move the chef to a chilly room where chopping speed halves, and slicing three times as many carrots **takes three times two, or six times as long**.

> At a constant saturated rate, reaction time grows directly with substrate mass and inversely with enzyme rate.

The slope at <InlineMath math="27^\circ\text{C}" /> is half the slope at <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" />, so every mass takes twice as long to hydrolyse.

## Rate and time calculation

Find the rate at <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" />, halve it for <InlineMath math="27^\circ\text{C}" />, then divide **the <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ g}" /> starch mass** by the slower rate.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{rate}_{37} &= \frac{1.0}{4} = 0.25\text{ g min}^{-1} \\
\text{rate}_{27} &= \frac{0.25}{2} = 0.125\text{ g min}^{-1} \\
t &= \frac{3.0}{0.125} = 24\text{ min}
\end{aligned}" />

Quick check: hydrolysing at <InlineMath math="0.125\text{ g min}^{-1}" /> for <InlineMath math="24" /> minutes breaks down exactly <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ g}" /> of starch.

## Why other options fail

- Choosing <InlineMath math="6" /> minutes doubles the rate when cooled, even though **cooling by <InlineMath math="10^\circ\text{C}" /> halves the rate**.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="12" /> minutes scales the mass to <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ g}" /> at <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" /> without adjusting for temperature.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="8" /> minutes corrects temperature for <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ g}" /> only and forgets the threefold mass increase.

# Answer

The amylase solution takes **<InlineMath math="24" />** minutes to hydrolyse **<InlineMath math="3.0\text{ g}" />** of starch at **<InlineMath math="27^\circ\text{C}" />**.

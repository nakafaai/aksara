# Constant rate, then correct for temperature

Because the enzyme is saturated, **the rate is a constant mass per unit time**. Find the rate at <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" />, halve it for <InlineMath math="27^\circ\text{C}" />, then find the time for <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ g}" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{rate at }37^\circ\text{C}&=\frac{1.0}{4}=0.25\text{ g per minute}\\
\text{rate at }27^\circ\text{C}&=\frac{0.25}{2}=0.125\text{ g per minute}\\
t&=\frac{3.0}{0.125}=24\text{ minutes}
\end{aligned}" />

So it takes <InlineMath math="24" /> minutes.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="6" /> minutes doubles the rate, but **a fall in temperature halves the rate**.
- <InlineMath math="12" /> minutes ignores the change in temperature and only allows for three times the mass.
- <InlineMath math="8" /> minutes corrects only for temperature and forgets that there is three times as much starch.

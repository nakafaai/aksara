# Count the ovulation day from the end of the cycle

The luteal phase is the <InlineMath math="14" /> days **after the day of ovulation up to the last day of the cycle**, so ovulation does not always fall on day <InlineMath math="14" />. The last day of this cycle is day <InlineMath math="32" />, so ovulation falls on day <InlineMath math="18" /> and the luteal phase runs from day <InlineMath math="19" /> to day <InlineMath math="32" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{ovulation}&=32-14=\text{day }18\\
\text{earliest}&=18-3=\text{day }15\ \text{(3 days before ovulation)}\\
\text{latest}&=18+1=\text{day }19\ \text{(1 day after ovulation)}
\end{aligned}" />

So intercourse from day <InlineMath math="15" /> to day <InlineMath math="19" /> can lead to fertilisation.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Day <InlineMath math="11" /> to day <InlineMath math="15" /> assumes ovulation is always on day <InlineMath math="14" />, but **this cycle is <InlineMath math="32" /> days long**.
- Day <InlineMath math="18" /> to day <InlineMath math="21" /> counts three days forwards from ovulation, but the three-day limit applies **before** the day of ovulation.
- Day <InlineMath math="14" /> to day <InlineMath math="18" /> shifts the window one day too early and misses day <InlineMath math="19" />, when the ovum can still be fertilised.

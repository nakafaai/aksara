# The factor that raises the rate when increased

A limiting factor is the one that, **when increased, makes the rate of photosynthesis rise**. Compare rows of the table that differ in only one factor.

At <InlineMath math="1" /> unit of light, all three rows give <InlineMath math="10" />, even though the carbon dioxide and temperature differ. So raising carbon dioxide or temperature has no effect, and light is limiting. At <InlineMath math="5" /> units with <InlineMath math="0.10\%" /> carbon dioxide and <InlineMath math="25^\circ\text{C}" />, the rate stays at <InlineMath math="40" /> as light rises from <InlineMath math="4" /> to <InlineMath math="5" />, so light is no longer limiting. Raising the temperature to <InlineMath math="35^\circ\text{C}" /> lifts the rate to <InlineMath math="52" />, so temperature is limiting. So at <InlineMath math="1" /> unit the limiting factor is light intensity, and at <InlineMath math="5" /> units it is temperature.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Light at <InlineMath math="5" /> units is wrong, because the rate is already flat from <InlineMath math="4" /> to <InlineMath math="5" /> units, so **adding light does not raise the rate**.
- Carbon dioxide at <InlineMath math="1" /> unit is wrong, because raising it from <InlineMath math="0.04\%" /> to <InlineMath math="0.10\%" /> leaves the rate at <InlineMath math="10" />.
- Temperature at <InlineMath math="1" /> unit is wrong, because raising the temperature to <InlineMath math="35^\circ\text{C}" /> also leaves the rate unchanged there.

# The volume of the cylinder of water taken up

The water taken up by the shoot pulls the bubble along the tube, so the volume of water taken up equals the volume of a <InlineMath math="4.5\text{ cm}" /> cylinder inside the capillary tube, <InlineMath math="\pi r^2 l" />. **Match the units first**: the radius is <InlineMath math="0.50\text{ mm}=0.050\text{ cm}" />.

Press play and watch the bubble creep along the rule towards the shoot.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
V&=\pi r^2 l=\pi(0.050)^2(4.5)\approx0.0353\text{ cm}^3\\
\text{rate}&=\frac{0.0353}{5.0}\approx7.1\times10^{-3}\text{ cm}^3\text{ min}^{-1}
\end{aligned}" />

So the shoot takes up water at about <InlineMath math="7.1\times10^{-3}\text{ cm}^3" /> per minute.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="0.71\text{ cm}^3\text{ min}^{-1}" /> uses the radius in millimetres and the length in centimetres, so **the units are mixed**.
- <InlineMath math="2.8\times10^{-2}\text{ cm}^3\text{ min}^{-1}" /> uses the diameter of <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ mm}" /> as the radius.
- <InlineMath math="3.5\times10^{-2}\text{ cm}^3\text{ min}^{-1}" /> is the total volume over five minutes, not yet divided by the time.

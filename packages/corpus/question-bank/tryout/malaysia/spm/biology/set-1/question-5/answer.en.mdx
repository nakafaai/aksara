# Given

Onion epidermis strips are soaked for <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes in sucrose solutions, yielding plasmolysed cell percentages of <InlineMath math="0\%" /> at <InlineMath math="0.2" />, <InlineMath math="20\%" /> at <InlineMath math="0.4" />, <InlineMath math="80\%" /> at <InlineMath math="0.6" />, and <InlineMath math="100\%" /> at <InlineMath math="0.8\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

The concentration that **plasmolyses exactly <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of the cells** is taken as isotonic with average cell sap.

# Asked

Which of statements I, II, III, and IV are correct?

# Solution

Picture soaking dozens of onion cells in increasingly sugary water. The moment exactly half the cells (<InlineMath math="50\%" />) shrink away from their walls, **the outside sweetness matches the average inside sap**.

> The isotonic concentration of a plant tissue sits at the external solute strength that plasmolyses exactly half of its cells.

Between <InlineMath math="0.4" /> and <InlineMath math="0.6\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />, the share climbs steadily from <InlineMath math="20\%" /> to <InlineMath math="80\%" />, placing <InlineMath math="50\%" /> exactly halfway at <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

## Checking all four statements

Verify the <InlineMath math="50\%" /> crossing algebraically, then test each statement against cell biology.

<BlockMath math="\text{isotonic concentration} = 0.4 + \frac{50 - 20}{80 - 20} \times (0.6 - 0.4) = 0.5\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />

- **Statement I is correct** because the average cell sap matches about <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" /> sucrose.
- **Statement II is correct** because identical cell saps would all plasmolyse at one single concentration instead of rising gradually.
- **Statement III is wrong** because the cell wall is fully permeable, so **the sucrose solution** fills the gap between wall and membrane, not air.
- **Statement IV is wrong** because the table shows **<InlineMath math="20\%" /> of cells already plasmolysed** at <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.

## Why other options fail

- Choosing I and IV accepts statement IV even though **<InlineMath math="20\%" /> of cells already lose water** at <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />.
- Choosing II and III accepts statement III even though outside sucrose freely crosses the cell wall.
- Choosing I, II, and IV also keeps statement IV, directly contradicting the measured <InlineMath math="20\%" />.

# Answer

Only statements I and II are correct.

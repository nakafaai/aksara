# Split the glucose between two pathways

The oxygen available decides **how much glucose can be oxidised aerobically**. The rest is fermented.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n_{\text{glucose}}&=\frac{18}{180}=0.10\text{ mol}\\
n_{\text{O}_2}&=\frac{1.44}{24}=0.06\text{ mol}\ \Rightarrow\ 0.01\text{ mol glucose aerobic}\\
\text{fermented}&=0.10-0.01=0.09\text{ mol glucose}\\
n_{\text{CO}_2}&=6(0.01)+2(0.09)=0.24\text{ mol}=5.76\text{ dm}^3\\
E&=0.01(2898)+0.09(210)=28.98+18.9\approx47.9\text{ kJ}
\end{aligned}" />

So <InlineMath math="5.76\text{ dm}^3" /> of carbon dioxide and <InlineMath math="47.9\text{ kJ}" /> of energy are produced.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="21.0\text{ kJ}" /> ferments all the glucose, but **the oxygen present is used aerobically first**.
- <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ dm}^3" /> assumes fermentation gives one carbon dioxide molecule per glucose, but the equation gives two.
- <InlineMath math="14.4\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="289.8\text{ kJ}" /> oxidises all the glucose aerobically, but the oxygen is enough for only <InlineMath math="0.01\text{ mol}" />.

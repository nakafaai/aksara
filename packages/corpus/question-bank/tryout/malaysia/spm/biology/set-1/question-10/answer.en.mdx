# Given

A sealed flask contains yeast, <InlineMath math="18\text{ g}" /> of glucose (<InlineMath math="M_r = 180" />), and **<InlineMath math="1.44\text{ dm}^3" /> of oxygen** at room conditions (<InlineMath math="24\text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}" />).

Aerobic respiration (<InlineMath math="6\text{ O}_2 \to 6\text{ CO}_2" />, <InlineMath math="2898\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />) consumes all oxygen first, then fermentation (<InlineMath math="2\text{ CO}_2" />, <InlineMath math="210\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />) breaks down the remaining glucose.

# Asked

What are the total volume of carbon dioxide produced and the total energy released?

# Solution

Picture having <InlineMath math="10" /> logs of firewood (<InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" /> glucose) with just enough air to burn <InlineMath math="1" /> log (<InlineMath math="0.01\text{ mol}" />) cleanly. The remaining <InlineMath math="9" /> logs (<InlineMath math="0.09\text{ mol}" />) **must break down without oxygen by fermentation**.

> Limited oxygen caps how much glucose goes through aerobic respiration, and the leftover glucose shifts to fermentation so both pathways contribute carbon dioxide and energy.

Slide oxygen volume <InlineMath math="v" /> below from <InlineMath math="0" /> (pure fermentation) to <InlineMath math="14.4\text{ dm}^3" /> (full aerobic oxidation) and inspect the flask at <InlineMath math="v = 1.44\text{ dm}^3" />.

The two orange endpoints expose the extreme traps: <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" /> for zero oxygen and <InlineMath math="14.4\text{ dm}^3" /> for excess oxygen. With <InlineMath math="1.44\text{ dm}^3" />, the flask sits cleanly between them.

## Pathway split and totals

Convert glucose and oxygen to moles, separate **aerobic glucose (<InlineMath math="0.01\text{ mol}" />)** from **fermented glucose (<InlineMath math="0.09\text{ mol}" />)**, then sum both outputs.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
n_{\text{glucose}} &= \frac{18}{180} = 0.10\text{ mol} \\
n_{\text{O}_2} &= \frac{1.44}{24} = 0.06\text{ mol} \implies n_{\text{aerobic}} = \frac{0.06}{6} = 0.01\text{ mol} \\
n_{\text{fermented}} &= 0.10 - 0.01 = 0.09\text{ mol} \\
n_{\text{CO}_2} &= 6(0.01) + 2(0.09) = 0.06 + 0.18 = 0.24\text{ mol} \\
V_{\text{CO}_2} &= 0.24 \times 24 = 5.76\text{ dm}^3 \\
E &= 0.01(2898) + 0.09(210) = 28.98 + 18.90 = 47.88 \approx 47.9\text{ kJ}
\end{aligned}" />

Quick check: the two glucose shares add back to <InlineMath math="0.10\text{ mol}" />, and both totals match the graph readouts.

## Why other options fail

- Choosing <InlineMath math="4.80\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="21.0\text{ kJ}" /> ferments all glucose, even though **the available oxygen runs aerobic respiration first**.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="3.60\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="47.9\text{ kJ}" /> counts only one carbon dioxide molecule per fermented glucose instead of two.
- Choosing <InlineMath math="14.4\text{ dm}^3" /> and <InlineMath math="289.8\text{ kJ}" /> oxidises all glucose aerobically, even though oxygen covers only <InlineMath math="0.01\text{ mol}" />.

# Answer

The total volume of carbon dioxide produced is **<InlineMath math="5.76\text{ dm}^3" />** and the total energy released is **<InlineMath math="47.9\text{ kJ}" />**.

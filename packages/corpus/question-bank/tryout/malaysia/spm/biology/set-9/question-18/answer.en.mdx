# Divide the amount by the fixed rate

When an enzyme is already **working at full speed**, extra substrate cannot speed it up, so the amount removed each hour stays the same. The time needed is then simply the amount divided by the rate.

- I: <InlineMath math="40\text{ g}" /> at <InlineMath math="7\text{ g}" /> an hour takes about <InlineMath math="5.7" /> hours. Statement I is correct.
- II: at a fixed rate, twice the alcohol takes twice as long, about <InlineMath math="11.4" /> hours. Statement II is correct.
- III: the liver's enzymes are already working flat out, and coffee does not add enzyme. It may make a drinker feel more alert, but the alcohol is removed no faster. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: heavy drinking over years kills liver cells and replaces them with scar tissue, so the liver can handle less alcohol and store less glycogen. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the lasting damage that heavy drinking does to liver cells**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **lets coffee speed up an enzyme that is already at full speed**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **although a fixed rate means double the alcohol takes double the time**.

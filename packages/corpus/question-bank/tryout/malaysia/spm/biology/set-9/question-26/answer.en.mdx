# Follow where each seed's food ends up

The endosperm comes from the **triploid** nucleus formed when a male gamete fuses with the two polar nuclei. In some seeds the embryo absorbs it before the seed is ripe, and in others it stays as the food store. Compare the gain of the embryo with the loss of the endosperm to see whether anything else is feeding it.

- I: the bean endosperm falls to <InlineMath math="0" /> as the embryo reaches <InlineMath math="300\text{ mg}" />, so the bean stores its food in the cotyledons. The maize endosperm keeps growing to <InlineMath math="260\text{ mg}" />, so maize stores its food in the endosperm. Statement I is wrong.
- II: <InlineMath math="260" /> out of <InlineMath math="290\text{ mg}" /> is about <InlineMath math="90\%" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: the bean embryo gains <InlineMath math="190\text{ mg}" /> while the endosperm loses only <InlineMath math="35\text{ mg}" />. At least <InlineMath math="155\text{ mg}" /> must come from the parent plant through the stalk. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the endosperm forms from one male gamete and two polar nuclei, so its cells are triploid. Only the embryo is diploid. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts I, **although the maize endosperm keeps growing to the end**.
- II, III and IV only accepts IV, **missing the triploid nucleus of double fertilisation**.
- I and IV only rejects both readings from the table, **the <InlineMath math="90\%" /> share and the gap between <InlineMath math="190" /> and <InlineMath math="35\text{ mg}" />**.

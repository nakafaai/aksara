# Follow the loop round, and count the doublings

Each contraction stretches the cervix more, which releases more oxytocin, which causes a stronger contraction. A loop that **amplifies** a change is positive feedback. A loop that **reverses** a change is negative feedback.

- I: stretching triggers oxytocin, and oxytocin causes more stretching, so each step strengthens the next. Statement I is correct.
- II: negative feedback brings a quantity back to a set point, but this loop keeps building until the baby is born and the stretching stops. Statement II is correct.
- III: two hours is four doublings, so the level rises <InlineMath math="2^4=16" /> times, not <InlineMath math="8" />. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: body temperature is held near <InlineMath math="37\,^\circ\text{C}" /> by negative feedback, which reverses any rise or fall. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts eight times, **counting three doublings in two hours instead of four**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **gives temperature control a positive loop**.
- I and IV only rejects II and accepts IV, **confusing the two kinds of feedback**.

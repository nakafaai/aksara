# Compare the risk with and without the vaccine

Efficacy compares the **two groups**. It is the fall in the case rate, as a share of the case rate without the vaccine.

<BlockMath math="\text{efficacy}=1-\frac{20}{200}=90\%,\qquad 100\,000\times0.02\times0.90=1800" />

- I: the vaccine group has a tenth of the placebo group's cases, so <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of cases are prevented. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="99.8\%" /> is the share of vaccinated people who stayed well, but most of them would have stayed well anyway. Efficacy must be measured against the placebo group. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the vaccine primes the immune system to make memory cells, which divide quickly into plasma cells on a later exposure. Statement III is correct.
- IV: unvaccinated, <InlineMath math="100\,000\times0.02=2000" /> people would fall ill, and <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of those cases, <InlineMath math="1800" />, are prevented. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects IV, **although <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of 2000 cases is 1800**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **measures efficacy without the placebo group**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts II, **confusing the share who stayed well with the share of cases prevented**.

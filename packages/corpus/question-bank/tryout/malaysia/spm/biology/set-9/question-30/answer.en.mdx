# Compare what the water can hold with what the fish need

Warm water **holds less oxygen**, while warm fish **need more**. Wherever the supply line drops below the demand line, the fish cannot get enough even in the best case.

- I: the oxygen the water can hold falls from <InlineMath math="9.1" /> to <InlineMath math="7.6\text{ mg}" /> per litre, a cut of about <InlineMath math="16\%" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: at <InlineMath math="30" /> °C the water holds at most <InlineMath math="7.6\text{ mg}" /> per litre but trout need <InlineMath math="9.4\text{ mg}" />. No amount of mixing with air can make up the gap. Statement II is correct.
- III: warmth speeds up the enzymes of bacteria, so they break down waste faster and use oxygen faster, pushing the oxygen even lower. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: losing <InlineMath math="30\%" /> of <InlineMath math="9.1\text{ mg}" /> per litre leaves about <InlineMath math="6.4\text{ mg}" /> per litre, still above the <InlineMath math="6.2\text{ mg}" /> trout need at <InlineMath math="20" /> °C. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **although <InlineMath math="6.4\text{ mg}" /> per litre still covers the <InlineMath math="6.2" /> needed**.
- I, II and III only accepts III and rejects IV, **reversing the effect of warmth on bacteria**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **lets warm bacteria use less oxygen**.

# Find the group where the leaves are still full of water

If the leaves themselves set off closing, the stomata would close only once the leaves had lost water. Group Q is the test: its **leaves hold as much water as P's**, yet its stomata have closed by almost half. The message must travel up from the roots in the dry pot, and the rise in abscisic acid in the sap shows what it is.

- I: Q's leaves hold <InlineMath math="85\%" /> water, the same as P's, but its stomata are open only <InlineMath math="55\%" /> as wide. Nothing in the leaf has changed, so the roots in the dry pot must send the signal. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="120" /> against <InlineMath math="20\text{ nmol}" /> per litre is six times as much. Statement II is correct.
- III: only group R's leaves lose water, falling to <InlineMath math="72\%" />. Group Q closes its stomata with no wilting at all, so wilting cannot be the cause in every group. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: carbon dioxide enters the leaf through the same pores that let water vapour out. Closing them early saves water but slows photosynthesis. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the cost in carbon dioxide that every closed stoma brings**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **blames wilting even where the leaves hold full water**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **although group Q closes with no loss of leaf water**.

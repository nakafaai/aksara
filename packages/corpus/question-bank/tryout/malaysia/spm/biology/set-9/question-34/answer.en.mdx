# Every blue bird is heterozygous, so work each cross from that

With **no dominance**, the colour shows the genotype directly: blue always means <InlineMath math="C^BC^W" />. A blue parent passes on <InlineMath math="C^B" /> or <InlineMath math="C^W" /> with equal chance, so a cross of two blue birds splits into all three colours.

<BlockMath math="C^BC^W\times C^BC^W\to\tfrac14\,C^BC^B:\tfrac12\,C^BC^W:\tfrac14\,C^WC^W,\qquad \tfrac12\times40=20" />

- I: black (<InlineMath math="C^BC^B" />) with white (<InlineMath math="C^WC^W" />) can only give <InlineMath math="C^BC^W" />, so <InlineMath math="100\%" /> blue, while blue with blue gives <InlineMath math="50\%" /> blue. That is twice the share. Statement I is correct.
- II: half of blue with blue chicks are blue, so about <InlineMath math="20" /> of <InlineMath math="40" />, not <InlineMath math="10" />. The <InlineMath math="10" /> is the share of black or of white. Statement II is wrong.
- III: blue chicks are <InlineMath math="C^BC^W" /> like their parents, so mating them repeats the same <InlineMath math="\tfrac14:\tfrac12:\tfrac14" /> split, half blue every generation. Blue can never breed true. Statement III is correct.
- IV: blue with blue gives <InlineMath math="1" /> black to <InlineMath math="2" /> blue to <InlineMath math="1" /> white. A <InlineMath math="3:1" /> ratio would need a dominant allele, and here there is none. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **taking the share of one pure colour as the share of blue**.
- III and IV only rejects I and **treats blue as dominant**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **a dominant ratio that contradicts the three colours in the table**.

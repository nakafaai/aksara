# Expect speed to follow oil solubility, then look for the exception

Substances that cross by **simple diffusion** must dissolve through the fatty middle of the bilayer, so the more oil-soluble they are, the faster they cross. A substance far faster than its oil solubility predicts must have **another route**, through proteins.

- I: ethanol is about <InlineMath math="70" /> times as oil-soluble as glycerol and crosses <InlineMath math="100" /> times as fast, because it slips through the lipid layer. Statement I is correct.
- II: glucose is about as poorly oil-soluble as urea, yet crosses some <InlineMath math="1700" /> times faster. Only carrier proteins can explain that. Statement II is correct.
- III: urea crosses at <InlineMath math="0.03" /> and glucose at <InlineMath math="50" />, so glucose is far faster despite being the larger molecule. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: charged ions are surrounded by water and are repelled by the fatty core of the bilayer, so they cross only through channel and carrier proteins. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts III, **ranking by size instead of reading the rates**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **lets charged ions dissolve in the bilayer**.
- I and IV only rejects II and accepts IV, **missing the carriers that rush glucose across**.

# Compare every wheal with the two controls

Saline is the **negative control**: it shows the wheal caused by the prick alone. Histamine is the **positive control**: it shows the skin can react at all. A test wheal means something only when set against both.

- I: allergen X gives a wheal <InlineMath math="8\text{ mm}" /> wider than saline, far over the <InlineMath math="3\text{ mm}" /> needed. Allergen Y gives only <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" /> more. Statement I is correct.
- II: antihistamine blocks the effect of histamine on the skin, so even a real allergy would make no wheal. The histamine prick would then also fail, warning that the test is invalid. Statement II is correct.
- III: pollen and similar allergens are harmless, but in an allergy the body makes antibodies against them and releases histamine as if fighting a pathogen. Statement III is correct.
- IV: saline shows how big a wheal the prick itself causes, so it can be subtracted from the others. It is not a test for a salt allergy. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **the definition of an allergy that the test rests on**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **misreads the saline control as a salt test**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **although antihistamine would hide every reaction**.

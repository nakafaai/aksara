# The catch can never be more than the yearly increase

A population stays the same size only if the fish removed each year are no more than the **births minus deaths** that year. So the safe catch at any size is the increase at that size, and the largest safe catch is where the increase peaks.

- I: the increase peaks at <InlineMath math="2500" /> when there are <InlineMath math="5000" /> fish, half of the <InlineMath math="10\,000" /> the lake supports. Statement I is correct.
- II: at <InlineMath math="5000" /> fish the population adds <InlineMath math="2500" /> a year, so catching up to <InlineMath math="2500" /> leaves the numbers steady. Statement II is correct.
- III: at <InlineMath math="9000" /> fish the population adds only <InlineMath math="900" /> a year. Catching <InlineMath math="2100" /> removes <InlineMath math="1200" /> more than are added, so the stock keeps falling. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: at <InlineMath math="10\,000" /> the increase is <InlineMath math="0" />. Competition for food and space has raised deaths or cut births until they match. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and IV only rejects II, **the catch that exactly matches the peak increase**.
- II and III only rejects I and IV, **missing where the increase peaks and why it stops at <InlineMath math="10\,000" />**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts III, **although <InlineMath math="2100" /> is more than the <InlineMath math="900" /> added at that size**.

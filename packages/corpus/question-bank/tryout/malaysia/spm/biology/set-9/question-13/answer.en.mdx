# Divide each urine concentration by the blood's, then link it to the loops

How concentrated urine can get depends on the **salt gradient** the loops of Henle build in the medulla. Urine in the collecting duct loses water to that salty tissue, so it can never be more concentrated than the deepest part of the medulla.

Longer loops pump more salt into the medulla, so more water is drawn out of the collecting ducts. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="1400" /> against <InlineMath math="300" /> is about <InlineMath math="4.7" /> times. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the beaver lives in water and has plenty to drink, so it does not need to save water. Short loops are enough. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

- III and IV only rejects II and **has a river animal saving water**.
- I and II only accepts I and rejects III, **although <InlineMath math="1400" /> against <InlineMath math="300" /> is about <InlineMath math="4.7" />**.

# Compare the salt in the sap with the salt in the sea

The share of salt kept out is the salt that does not get in, divided by the salt on offer. Then remember the rule for taking in water: water moves from **higher water potential to lower**, and dissolved salt lowers water potential.

- I: Rhizophora's sap holds <InlineMath math="0.7\text{ g}" /> against the sea's <InlineMath math="35\text{ g}" />, so its roots keep out <InlineMath math="98\%" /> of the salt. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="3.5" /> is five times <InlineMath math="0.7" />. The salt crystals on Avicennia's leaves show the extra being pumped out by its leaf glands. Statement II is correct.
- III: dissolved salt lowers water potential, it does not raise it. The root cells must have a lower water potential than seawater to draw water in, and the salt they hold helps them do that. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: waterlogged mud has almost no air, so roots buried in it cannot get oxygen for respiration. The pneumatophores reach above the mud and let air into the roots. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the oxygen supply that the airless mud cannot give**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **gets water potential backwards in III**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and **lets salt raise water potential**.

# Smaller differences mean a more recent split

Two species that split recently have had little time to build up differences. A **steady rate** turns each difference into a time: if <InlineMath math="1.2\%" /> took <InlineMath math="6" /> million years, every <InlineMath math="1\%" /> takes <InlineMath math="5" /> million years.

<BlockMath math="\frac{6}{1.2}=5\text{ million years per }1\%,\qquad 3.1\times5\approx15.5\text{ million years}" />

- I: the chimpanzee differs from humans by only <InlineMath math="1.2\%" />, less than any other ape. Statement I is correct.
- II: the gorilla differs from both humans and chimpanzees by <InlineMath math="1.6\%" />. It split from their shared line before humans and chimpanzees separated from each other. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="3.1\%" /> at <InlineMath math="5" /> million years per <InlineMath math="1\%" /> is about <InlineMath math="15" /> million years, not <InlineMath math="9" />. The figure of <InlineMath math="9" /> comes from adding <InlineMath math="3.1" /> to <InlineMath math="6" />, which mixes a percentage with years. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: humans and chimpanzees share an ancestor that lived about <InlineMath math="6" /> million years ago, and both lines have changed since. Neither evolved from the other. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts III, **adding a percentage to a number of years instead of scaling by the rate**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **turns a shared ancestor into one ape descending from the other**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and accepts two errors, **the wrong date and the descent from chimpanzees**.

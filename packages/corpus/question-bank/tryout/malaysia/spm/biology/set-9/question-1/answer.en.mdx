# Compare each difference with the size of the uncertainty

An uncertainty is the same **absolute** amount whatever is measured, so it matters far more for small volumes. A difference between two results is meaningful only if it is larger than the uncertainties of both readings together.

- I: <InlineMath math="0.5" /> out of <InlineMath math="5.0" /> is <InlineMath math="10\%" />, and <InlineMath math="0.5" /> out of <InlineMath math="50.0" /> is <InlineMath math="1\%" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: a burette read to <InlineMath math="0.05\text{ cm}^3" /> gives <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.05}{5.0}=1\%" /> for the same <InlineMath math="5\text{ cm}^3" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: averaging repeats cancels readings that scatter up and down, but a scale that is always <InlineMath math="0.3\text{ cm}^3" /> too high shifts every reading the same way. Statement III is correct.
- IV: each reading may be off by <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ cm}^3" />, so two readings can differ by up to <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ cm}^3" /> by chance alone. A difference of <InlineMath math="0.5" /> is smaller than that, so it shows no clear effect. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although averaging cannot remove an error that is always the same way**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **trusts a difference smaller than the combined uncertainty**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **although a finer scale shrinks the uncertainty**.

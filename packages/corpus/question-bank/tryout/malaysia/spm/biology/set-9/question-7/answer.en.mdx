# Match the length of the effort to the source that can last that long

The fastest sources run out first. **Stored ATP and phosphocreatine** cover about <InlineMath math="10" /> seconds, **anaerobic respiration** carries on to about a minute, and **aerobic respiration** takes over for longer efforts.

- I: stored ATP lasts about <InlineMath math="2" /> seconds and phosphocreatine about <InlineMath math="8" /> more, together about <InlineMath math="10" /> seconds, enough for a short sprint. Statement I is correct.
- II: by <InlineMath math="45" /> seconds the ATP and phosphocreatine are spent, so fast anaerobic respiration supplies much of the ATP and lactic acid builds up. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="30" /> against <InlineMath math="2" /> ATP per glucose is <InlineMath math="15" /> times as much, not twice. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: phosphocreatine simply hands its phosphate to ADP to remake ATP. It is not a form of respiration and does not replace oxygen. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts III, **dividing <InlineMath math="30" /> by <InlineMath math="15" /> instead of by <InlineMath math="2" />**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **turns phosphocreatine into respiration without oxygen**.
- I and IV only rejects II and accepts IV, **missing where lactic acid comes from**.

# Read each pair of levels as a feedback loop

Thyroxine and TSH normally hold each other steady. If **both** move the same way, the fault is upstream in the pituitary. If they move in **opposite** directions, the loop is working and the fault lies in the thyroid or its supplies.

- I: without enough iodine the thyroid cannot make enough thyroxine, so nothing holds TSH back. TSH rises fourfold and drives the thyroid to grow into a goitre. Statement I is correct.
- II: Q's thyroxine is three times normal and TSH has fallen to a tenth, as negative feedback predicts. Q's thyroid is overactive on its own. Statement II is correct.
- III: R has low thyroxine but also low TSH. If the thyroid alone were failing, TSH would rise as in P. Low TSH means the pituitary is not sending the signal. Statement III is correct.
- IV: Q already makes too much thyroxine, and extra iodine would supply the raw material for even more. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although low TSH alongside low thyroxine points to the pituitary**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **treats an overactive thyroid with more iodine**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **missing the feedback that holds Q's TSH down**.

# Compare each group with the one that differs from it in one way

Water is **pulled up the xylem** as a continuous column by transpiration from the leaves. Anything that breaks or blocks that column cuts uptake. Q is the fair baseline, because its stem never met air.

- I: P takes up <InlineMath math="8\text{ mL}" /> against Q's <InlineMath math="14\text{ mL}" />, a fall of about <InlineMath math="43\%" />. Air drawn into the cut vessels breaks the water column. Statement I is correct.
- II: R was cut under water like Q but then held in air for a minute, and its uptake fell to <InlineMath math="9\text{ mL}" />, close to P. A minute was enough for air to enter. Statement II is correct.
- III: a cut stem draws vase water straight into the xylem, so dissolved sucrose rises with it and gives the petals sugar to respire. The bleach slows bacteria that would otherwise clog the cut ends, and S lasts longest at <InlineMath math="11" /> days. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the stems have no roots, so there is no root pressure. The water is pulled up by transpiration from the leaves and petals. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although S takes up the most water and lasts longest**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **credits roots that a cut stem no longer has**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **ignoring that one minute in air nearly matched cutting in air**.

# Ask what each condition takes away from the root cells

A root that holds <InlineMath math="40" /> times more potassium than its surroundings is moving ions **against the gradient**, which only active transport can do. Active transport runs on ATP. Each condition removes something that ATP production depends on.

- I: building up potassium to <InlineMath math="40" /> times the outside level is against the gradient, and uptake collapses when oxygen or aerobic respiration is removed. The energy comes from ATP made in aerobic respiration. Statement I is correct.
- II: without oxygen uptake falls from <InlineMath math="10" /> to <InlineMath math="1" />, a <InlineMath math="90\%" /> cut, exactly the same as with cyanide, because both stop aerobic respiration. Statement II is correct.
- III: cooling slows diffusion only slightly. Uptake falls to a fifth because cold slows the enzymes of respiration and the carrier proteins that pump the ions. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: water fills the air spaces in waterlogged soil, so the roots get little oxygen, respire less and make less ATP for active uptake. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the field case of the roots without oxygen**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **explains a fivefold fall by slower diffusion alone**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and accepts III, **missing that oxygen loss and cyanide both cut uptake by <InlineMath math="90\%" />**.

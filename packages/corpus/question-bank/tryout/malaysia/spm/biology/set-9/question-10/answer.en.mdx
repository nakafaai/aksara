# Multiply the small mismatch by the time it lasts

The circuits are **in series**, so every litre the right ventricle sends to the lungs must come back to the left ventricle. A tiny difference in output therefore piles up steadily.

- I: <InlineMath math="70\text{ cm}^3" /> times <InlineMath math="72" /> beats is <InlineMath math="5040\text{ cm}^3" />, about <InlineMath math="5\text{ L}" /> a minute. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="1\%" /> of <InlineMath math="5040" /> is about <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" /> a **minute**, so in an hour about <InlineMath math="3\text{ L}" /> would collect in the lungs, not <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" />. Fluid would be forced into the air spaces long before that. Statement II is wrong.
- III: since all blood passes through both ventricles in turn, any long-term difference would empty one circuit and flood the other. Their outputs must match. Statement III is correct.
- IV: each ventricle pumps the same volume, but the left must drive blood through the whole body at much higher pressure, so it needs a thicker muscular wall. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts <InlineMath math="50\text{ cm}^3" />, **forgetting to multiply by <InlineMath math="60" /> minutes**, and rejects IV.
- II and IV only rejects I and III, **the output per minute and the reason it must match**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts II, **which treats a rate per minute as a total per hour**.

# Turn nitrogen into protein by dividing by its share

If protein is <InlineMath math="16\%" /> nitrogen, each gram of nitrogen stands for <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{0.16}=6.25\text{ g}" /> of protein. The test counts **all nitrogen**, whatever it comes from.

<BlockMath math="\frac{2.4}{0.16}=15\text{ g},\qquad \text{melamine}: \frac{1\times0.66}{0.16}\approx4.1\text{ g reported}" />

- I: <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ g}" /> of nitrogen at <InlineMath math="16\%" /> means <InlineMath math="15\text{ g}" /> of protein. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="1\text{ g}" /> of melamine holds <InlineMath math="0.66\text{ g}" /> of nitrogen, which the test reads as <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.66}{0.16}\approx4.1\text{ g}" /> of protein, not <InlineMath math="0.66\text{ g}" />. That is why melamine has been used to fake protein in milk. Statement II is wrong.
- III: lipids are made of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen only, so they contain no nitrogen at all. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: amino groups are removed from amino acids in the liver by deamination, and the nitrogen is turned into urea and excreted by the kidneys. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only accepts II, **forgetting to divide the melamine nitrogen by <InlineMath math="0.16" />**.
- II and III only rejects I and IV and **gives lipids nitrogen they do not have**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III, **although lipids contain only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen**.

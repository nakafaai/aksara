# Count doublings with powers of two, then turn a lifespan into a rate

Each round of mitosis doubles the cells, so <InlineMath math="n" /> rounds give <InlineMath math="2^n" />. Write the body's cells as <InlineMath math="35\times10^{12}" /> and use <InlineMath math="2^{10}\approx10^3" /> four times. A population that is fully replaced every <InlineMath math="120" /> days must be made at its **size divided by its lifespan**.

<BlockMath math="3.5\times10^{13}=35\times10^{12}\approx2^5\times2^{40}=2^{45},\qquad \frac{2.5\times10^{13}}{120\times24\times3600}\approx\frac{2.5\times10^{13}}{1.04\times10^{7}}\approx2.4\times10^{6}" />

- I: <InlineMath math="10^{12}\approx2^{40}" /> and <InlineMath math="35\approx2^5" />, so <InlineMath math="3.5\times10^{13}\approx2^{45}" />, about <InlineMath math="45" /> rounds of doubling. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="120" /> days is about <InlineMath math="1.04\times10^7" /> seconds, and <InlineMath math="2.5\times10^{13}" /> divided by that is about <InlineMath math="2.4\times10^6" /> cells a second. Statement II is correct.
- III: mitosis gives every cell the same genes. A nerve cell and a muscle cell differ because each switches on its own set of genes as it differentiates. Statement III is correct.
- IV: a red blood cell has no nucleus, so it cannot divide. New red blood cells come from stem cells in the bone marrow, which divide by mitosis. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, **the replacement rate set by a <InlineMath math="120" />-day life**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **lets cells without a nucleus divide**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts IV, **although red blood cells have no nucleus and come from bone marrow stem cells**.

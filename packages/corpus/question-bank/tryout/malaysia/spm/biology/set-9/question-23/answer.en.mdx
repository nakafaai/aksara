# Read the gap as the time the enzymes get to catch up

A flash is so short that only the **light-dependent reactions** happen during it, making ATP and NADPH. The **light-independent reactions** use these in the dark gap. If the gap is too short, the next flash arrives before the products are used up and cannot all be put to work.

- I: with a <InlineMath math="0.005\text{ s}" /> gap, oxygen per flash is only <InlineMath math="1.0" />, a third of the <InlineMath math="3.0" /> possible, because the products of the last flash are still waiting to be used. Statement I is correct.
- II: at <InlineMath math="25" /> °C the yield climbs until the gap is <InlineMath math="0.04\text{ s}" /> and then stops rising, so that is the time the enzymes need to clear one flash's products. Statement II is correct.
- III: the light-dependent reactions are hardly affected by temperature. The cold slows the enzyme-catalysed light-independent reactions, which is why the gap must grow to <InlineMath math="0.16\text{ s}" />. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: both temperatures level off at <InlineMath math="3.0" />. Once the enzymes have enough time, the yield is limited only by the light one flash captures, which the cold does not change. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **although both curves level off at the same <InlineMath math="3.0" />**.
- I, II and III only accepts III and rejects IV, **blaming the cold on the stage that hardly feels temperature**.
- III and IV only rejects I and II, **the two readings that show the enzymes need time after each flash**.

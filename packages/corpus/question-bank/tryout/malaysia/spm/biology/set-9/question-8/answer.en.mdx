# Divide the distance by each speed, then follow what is left behind

Time is distance divided by speed, so a fifth of the speed means **five times** the time. Haemoglobin taken up by carbon monoxide is lost to oxygen transport while it stays bound.

- I: the non-smoker takes <InlineMath math="15" /> minutes, but the smoker takes <InlineMath math="\tfrac{150}{2}=75" /> minutes, not <InlineMath math="30" />. Statement I is wrong.
- II: bacteria trapped in mucus that moves five times more slowly stay in the airways much longer, with more time to infect them. Statement II is correct.
- III: each haemoglobin site holding carbon monoxide cannot hold oxygen, so <InlineMath math="8\%" /> of the oxygen-carrying capacity is lost. Statement III is correct.
- IV: carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin far more tightly than oxygen, so it stays bound for hours instead of being breathed straight out. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes, **halving the time instead of multiplying by five**.
- III and IV only rejects II and **calls carbon monoxide harmless**.
- I and II only accepts I and rejects III, **missing the haemoglobin taken up by carbon monoxide**.

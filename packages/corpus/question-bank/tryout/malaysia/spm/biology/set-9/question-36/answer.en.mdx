# Follow the protein from the litres of milk to the number of goats

Multiply the milk by the concentration and by the share recovered to get one goat's yield, then divide the demand by it. The **milk-protein switch** decides where the gene works, so a goat carrying the gene makes the protein only in a udder producing milk.

- I: <InlineMath math="800\text{ L}" /> at <InlineMath math="2\text{ g}" /> per litre is <InlineMath math="1600\text{ g}" />, and <InlineMath math="70\%" /> of that is <InlineMath math="1120\text{ g}" />, about <InlineMath math="1.1\text{ kg}" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="100\text{ kg}" /> is <InlineMath math="100\,000\text{ g}" />, and dividing by <InlineMath math="1120\text{ g}" /> gives <InlineMath math="89.3" />, so <InlineMath math="90" /> goats are needed, since <InlineMath math="89" /> would fall short. Statement II is correct.
- III: the male has one copy, so each kid has a <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> chance of getting it. The milk switch works only in females producing milk, so male kids with the gene never make the protein. Statement III is correct.
- IV: every cell carries the gene, but the milk-protein switch is turned on only in the udder. Other tissues keep the gene switched off. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **the half chance and the female-only switch**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **forgets that a gene's switch limits where it works**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **although <InlineMath math="89.3" /> rounds up to <InlineMath math="90" /> goats**.

# Work out the ring's area, then ask where bending strains the stem

A ring's area is the area of the outer circle minus the area of the inner one. The share of the stem is that area divided by the area of the whole stem, so <InlineMath math="\pi" /> cancels. Then use the given fact that **bending strains the outside most and the centre least**.

- I: the fibre ring is <InlineMath math="0.17" /> of the cross-section, about <InlineMath math="17\%" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: fibres at the outside sit where the bending strain is largest, so they resist it. A rod at the centre sits where there is hardly any strain and does little. Statement II is correct.
- III: fibres are dead with rigid lignified walls, so they cannot grow longer. Collenchyma is living and its cellulose walls can stretch as the young stem lengthens. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the pith is at the centre, where the strain is smallest, so losing it costs little strength. Hollow stems such as bamboo are still stiff. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, **although the fibres sit exactly where the bending strain is greatest**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **gives the barely strained pith a key role in strength**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **reversing which part of the stem carries the bending load**.

# Turn each percentage into millimetres of rain

Each share applies to the same <InlineMath math="2000\text{ mm}" />, so multiply to compare amounts. Then follow each route: water caught by leaves goes **back to the air**, runoff goes **straight to the rivers**, and water that soaks in **drains out slowly**.

<BlockMath math="0.05\times2000=100\text{ mm},\qquad 0.40\times2000=800\text{ mm},\qquad \frac{30}{0.2}=150" />

- I: runoff rises from <InlineMath math="100\text{ mm}" /> to <InlineMath math="800\text{ mm}" />, eight times as much. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="30" /> tonnes against <InlineMath math="0.2" /> tonnes per hectare is <InlineMath math="150" /> times as much soil lost. Statement II is correct.
- III: the share soaking in falls from <InlineMath math="70\%" /> to <InlineMath math="60\%" />, so less soaks in, not more. With eight times the runoff reaching the rivers quickly, and soil washing in to fill their beds, floods come more often. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the forest returns <InlineMath math="500\text{ mm}" /> a year to the air from its leaves, plus water transpired from deeper in the soil. Without it this moisture is lost, and local rain can fall. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the water the trees send back to the air**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **reads <InlineMath math="60\%" /> as more than <InlineMath math="70\%" />**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and **claims fewer floods despite eight times the runoff**.

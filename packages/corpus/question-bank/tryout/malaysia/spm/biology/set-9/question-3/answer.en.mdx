# Follow the solutes first, because water only follows them

Water moves by **osmosis** towards the higher solute concentration. The carrier moves sodium and glucose into the gut cells together, so the solutes build up inside and water follows.

- I: water absorbed rises from <InlineMath math="20" /> to <InlineMath math="60\text{ mL}" /> an hour, three times as much. Statement I is correct.
- II: each glucose brings a sodium ion with it. As these solutes enter the cells and pass on into the blood, the water potential gradient draws water after them. Statement II is correct.
- III: blocking the carrier cuts water absorption back to <InlineMath math="20" />, so the carrier matters, but it moves solutes, not water. Water crosses by osmosis. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: cholera toxin makes the gut secrete salt and water, but it does not stop the sodium-glucose carrier. Glucose and salt taken by mouth still pull water back in, which is why oral rehydration saves lives. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **although the carrier still works in cholera**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **lets the carrier pump water directly**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **reversing which moves first, water or solute**.

# Weight each length by how many leaves have it

The mean of grouped data is the **sum of length times number**, divided by the total number of leaves. The most common length, the mode, is the tallest bar.

<BlockMath math="\bar{x}=\frac{4(5)+5(20)+6(45)+7(25)+8(5)}{100}=\frac{20+100+270+175+40}{100}=\frac{605}{100}=6.05\text{ cm}" />

- I: the weighted sum is <InlineMath math="605\text{ cm}" /> over <InlineMath math="100" /> leaves, a mean of <InlineMath math="6.05\text{ cm}" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: every leaf on one tree carries the same genes, so genes cannot explain the differences. Light, water and position on the tree do. Statement II is correct.
- III: the mode is <InlineMath math="6\text{ cm}" />. The leaves at <InlineMath math="5" />, <InlineMath math="6" /> and <InlineMath math="7\text{ cm}" /> number <InlineMath math="20+45+25=90" />, which is <InlineMath math="90\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: leaf length takes a whole range of values with most leaves near the middle, the bell shape of continuous variation. Such traits are controlled by many genes together with the environment, unlike the few clear-cut ABO groups. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, **although leaves from one tree cannot differ in their genes**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **puts a bell-shaped trait with blood groups**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **giving genes a role the single tree rules out**.

Muscles hold only enough ATP for about <InlineMath math="2" /> seconds of full effort. Phosphocreatine, stored in muscle, can remake ATP quickly for about <InlineMath math="8" /> seconds more. After that, anaerobic respiration to lactic acid can supply ATP fast for up to about a minute, and aerobic respiration supplies ATP more slowly for as long as fuel and oxygen last. Aerobic respiration gives about <InlineMath math="30" /> ATP per glucose and anaerobic respiration <InlineMath math="2" />.

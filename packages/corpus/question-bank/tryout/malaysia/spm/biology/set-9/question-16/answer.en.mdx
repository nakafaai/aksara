# Compare the enzymes' best temperature with the heating it saves

Enzymes speed up with temperature until their **active sites start to lose their shape**. Above that, activity falls fast. Heating water costs energy in proportion to the rise from tap temperature.

- I: protease hydrolyses the peptide bonds of egg protein, giving small soluble amino acids that rinse out. Statement I is correct.
- II: at <InlineMath math="40\,^\circ\text{C}" /> the enzymes remove <InlineMath math="90\%" />, more than the <InlineMath math="85\%" /> removed by hot water alone, and heating only <InlineMath math="20" /> degrees instead of <InlineMath math="70" /> saves about <InlineMath math="71\%" /> of the energy. Statement II is correct.
- III: at <InlineMath math="60\,^\circ\text{C}" /> removal falls to <InlineMath math="20\%" />. More collisions cannot help once the enzymes are denatured. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: lipase hydrolyses fats into fatty acids and glycerol, which are easier to wash away. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the lipase that deals with the butter**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **puts the enzymes' peak at <InlineMath math="60^\circ\text{C}" />**, where they are denatured.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **although <InlineMath math="40^\circ\text{C}" /> gives more cleaning for less heating**.

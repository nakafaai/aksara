# Multiply the share kept, once for every generation

The share kept applies to what is left each time, so after <InlineMath math="t" /> generations the variety kept is <InlineMath math="\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2N}\right)^t" />. Subtracting <InlineMath math="5\%" /> ten times would wrongly give <InlineMath math="50\%" />, because each loss is taken from a **smaller remainder**.

<BlockMath math="N=10:\ 0.95^{10}\approx0.60,\qquad N=50:\ 0.99^{10}\approx0.90" />

- I: <InlineMath math="1-\tfrac{1}{20}=0.95" /> each generation, and <InlineMath math="0.95^{10}\approx0.60" />, so about <InlineMath math="60\%" /> is kept. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="1-\tfrac{1}{100}=0.99" /> each generation, and <InlineMath math="0.99^{10}\approx0.90" />, a loss of about <InlineMath math="10\%" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: the rule holds only when no animals join from outside. A corridor lets deer from the large patch bring alleles the small one has lost. Statement III is correct.
- IV: with little variety, the animals are more alike and more often inbred, so harmful recessive alleles show up more and a new disease can strike them all. Steady numbers do not prevent this. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **the corridor that breaks the rule's condition of no newcomers**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **treats lost variety as harmless**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **although <InlineMath math="0.99^{10}" /> leaves about <InlineMath math="90\%" />**.

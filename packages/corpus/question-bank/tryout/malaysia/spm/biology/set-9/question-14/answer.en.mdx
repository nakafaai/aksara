# Scale mass with the cube and strength with the square

Mass grows as **length cubed** but a leg's strength grows only as **length squared**. Dividing the two gives the extra load that each square millimetre of leg must bear.

- I: ten times the length gives <InlineMath math="10^3=1000" /> times the volume and mass but only <InlineMath math="10^2=100" /> times the cross-section. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="1000" /> times the weight on <InlineMath math="100" /> times the area is <InlineMath math="10" /> times the load per square millimetre, enough to crush a thin exoskeleton. Statement II is correct.
- III: a hard exoskeleton cannot stretch, so an insect sheds it and grows a larger one each time it grows. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the same scaling problem faces vertebrates, so large animals such as elephants have bones that are thicker for their length, inside the body where they add less mass than a thick outer shell would. Statement IV is correct.

So all four statements are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only rejects IV, **although big animals solve the same problem with thicker bones**.
- I and III only rejects II and IV, **although <InlineMath math="1000" /> times the mass on <InlineMath math="100" /> times the area is <InlineMath math="10" /> times the load**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **the cube and square that the whole argument rests on**.

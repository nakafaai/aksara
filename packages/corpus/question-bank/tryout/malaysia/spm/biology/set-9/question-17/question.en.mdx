In a skin prick test, drops of different substances are placed on a patient's forearm and the skin under each is pricked. In an allergy, antibodies on mast cells in the skin bind the allergen, and the mast cells release histamine, which causes a raised, itchy wheal. A result counts as positive if the wheal is at least <InlineMath math="3\text{ mm}" /> wider than the saline wheal.

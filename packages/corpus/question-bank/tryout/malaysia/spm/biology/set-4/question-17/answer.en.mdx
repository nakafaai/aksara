# Compare ratios to judge the gradient, then use cyanide to test for ATP

How steeply an ion is concentrated is shown by the **ratio** of sap to pond, not by the amount in the sap. Cyanide stops aerobic respiration, so any uptake it stops must have needed ATP.

- I: the sap holds <InlineMath math="1080" /> times the potassium of the pond, so the ion is moved against a gradient over a thousand times steep. Statement I is correct.
- II: chloride reaches <InlineMath math="90\text{ mM}" />, more than potassium's <InlineMath math="54\text{ mM}" />, but its ratio is only <InlineMath math="90" /> against <InlineMath math="1080" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: every uptake rate falls to <InlineMath math="0.1" /> unit per hour with cyanide. Moving ions against such gradients is **active transport**, which needs ATP from respiration. Statement III is correct.
- IV: facilitated diffusion uses carrier proteins too, but it only moves substances down a gradient and needs no ATP. Potassium moves up a steep gradient and stops with cyanide. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, judging by the amount in the sap, **when the ratio decides how steep the gradient is**.
- I, II and IV only accepts facilitated diffusion, **but that cannot move an ion against a gradient or depend on ATP**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **although** <InlineMath math="\tfrac{54}{0.05}" /> is over a thousand.

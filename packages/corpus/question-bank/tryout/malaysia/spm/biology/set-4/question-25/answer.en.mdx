# Use the bending angle as a measure of auxin, then compare totals and shares

Each degree of bending stands for a fixed amount of auxin, so the angles can be **added and compared** like amounts. Totals show whether light destroys auxin, and the split blocks show where it goes.

<BlockMath math="\text{totals}: \text{A}=24,\quad \text{B}=24,\quad \text{C}=8+16=24,\qquad \text{share in C}: \frac{16}{8}=2" />

- I: the total is <InlineMath math="24^\circ" /> in the dark, in side light, and in the split block under side light. Light does not reduce the amount of auxin. Statement I is correct.
- II: in C the shaded half causes <InlineMath math="16^\circ" /> and the lit half <InlineMath math="8^\circ" />, twice as much. Light makes auxin **move sideways** to the shaded side. Statement II is correct.
- III: the extra auxin on the shaded side makes those cells **elongate faster**, so the shaded side grows longer and the shoot curves towards the light. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: with the tip itself split by glass, auxin can no longer move across it. Each half keeps its own share, so each would cause half the total, about <InlineMath math="12^\circ" />, as in the dark. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, missing that the sideways movement happens **inside the tip** and a full split blocks it.
- I, II and III only reverses the effect of auxin on shoots, **which speeds up growth**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **although every treatment collects the same** <InlineMath math="24^\circ" /> of auxin.

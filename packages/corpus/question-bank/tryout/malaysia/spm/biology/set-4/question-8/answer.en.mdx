# Let the pressure difference decide which way air flows

Air flows from **higher pressure to lower pressure**. When the alveoli are below the outside pressure, air flows in. When they are above it, air flows out. When the two are equal, air stops, even if the lungs are full.

- I: at <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ s}" /> the alveoli are <InlineMath math="200\text{ Pa}" /> below the outside, and the volume is rising. The diaphragm contracts and flattens, the thorax grows, and air flows in. Statement I is correct.
- II: at <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ s}" /> the pressure difference is <InlineMath math="0" />, so air stops flowing, at the moment the lungs are fullest. Statement II is correct.
- III: in a quiet breath, breathing out is passive. The diaphragm and the external intercostal muscles **relax**, the elastic lungs recoil, the thorax gets smaller and the pressure rises to <InlineMath math="+200\text{ Pa}" />. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the flow is fastest where the pressure difference is largest, at <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ s}" />. At <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ s}" /> there is no difference, so there is **no flow**. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only makes the external intercostals push air out, **but in quiet breathing they relax during exhalation**.
- I, II and IV only links the fastest flow to the fullest lungs, **when flow needs a pressure difference**.
- II, III and IV only rejects inhalation at <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ s}" />, **although the pressure is lowest and the volume is rising**.

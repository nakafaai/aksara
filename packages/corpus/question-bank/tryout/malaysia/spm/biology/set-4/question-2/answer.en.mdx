# Add the hair to the cell, then compare with the cell alone

The question asks how many times the **whole surface** has grown, so compare the cell with its hair against the cell without it.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{hair}=\pi\times10\times500=3.14\times5000=15\,700\,\mu\text{m}^2" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{2000+15\,700}{2000}=\frac{17\,700}{2000}\approx8.9" />
</MathContainer>

Water enters the root hair by **osmosis** and mineral ions by active transport, both across the cell surface. Almost nine times the surface means almost nine times the area for this uptake. A root hair has no chloroplasts, because no light reaches it underground. The energy for active transport comes from respiration in its mitochondria.

So the root hair increases the surface about <InlineMath math="8.9" /> times, and the cell absorbs water and mineral ions over the whole of this larger surface.

# Why the other options are wrong

- About <InlineMath math="7.9" /> times divides the hair alone by the cell, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{15\,700}{2000}" />, and **leaves out the cell's own surface**.
- The chloroplast reason has the right number, **but root hair cells have no chloroplasts**.
- About <InlineMath math="16.7" /> times uses <InlineMath math="10\,\mu\text{m}" /> as the radius instead of the **diameter**, doubling the hair's area.

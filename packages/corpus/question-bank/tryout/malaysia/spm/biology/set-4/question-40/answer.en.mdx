# Compare what each animal absorbs, burns and keeps

Absorbed energy is what is eaten minus what leaves in the faeces. Of that, a warm-blooded animal burns a **much larger share** in respiration to keep its body warm, so less is left to build tissue for the next level.

- I: the mouse absorbs <InlineMath math="900\text{ kJ}" />, or <InlineMath math="90\%" />, against <InlineMath math="600\text{ kJ}" />, or <InlineMath math="60\%" />, for the grasshopper. Statement I is correct.
- II: the grasshopper stores <InlineMath math="150\text{ kJ}" /> and the mouse only <InlineMath math="20\text{ kJ}" /> from the same food, <InlineMath math="7.5" /> times as much. Statement II is correct.
- III: the mouse uses <InlineMath math="880" /> of its <InlineMath math="900\text{ kJ}" /> absorbed, about <InlineMath math="98\%" />, in respiration, much of it to keep warm. Statement III is correct.
- IV: warm-blooded animals pass on only a small share of their energy, so energy runs out after **fewer** levels. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although** <InlineMath math="\tfrac{880}{900}" /> is about <InlineMath math="98\%" />.
- II and III only rejects I, judging by what is stored rather than by **what is absorbed**.
- I, II and IV only gives warm-blooded chains more levels, **when each of them passes on less energy**.

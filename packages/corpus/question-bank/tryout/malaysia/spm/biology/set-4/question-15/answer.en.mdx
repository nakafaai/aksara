# Follow the blood out of the fetus and back, and compare the two haemoglobins

The **umbilical arteries** carry blood away from the fetus and the **umbilical vein** brings it back. The separate test shows how strongly each kind of haemoglobin holds oxygen at the low oxygen level of the placenta.

- I: at the same low oxygen level, fetal haemoglobin is <InlineMath math="70\%" /> saturated against <InlineMath math="45\%" /> for adult haemoglobin. It holds oxygen more readily, so oxygen released from the mother's blood is picked up by the fetus's. Statement I is correct.
- II: urea falls from <InlineMath math="5.5" /> in the umbilical artery to <InlineMath math="4.5" /> in the vein, while the mother's blood holds only <InlineMath math="4.0" />. Urea diffuses down this gradient into her blood. Statement II is correct.
- III: if the bloods mixed, the umbilical vein would match the mother's <InlineMath math="95\%" />. It reaches <InlineMath math="70\%" />, the value fetal haemoglobin reaches at placental oxygen levels, because the bloods stay separate. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the umbilical arteries carry blood **away from the fetus** at only <InlineMath math="25\%" /> saturation. An artery is named by its direction from the heart, not by its oxygen. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only assumes every artery is oxygen-rich, **but the umbilical artery holds only** <InlineMath math="25\%" />.
- I, II and III only lets the bloods mix, **although the vein stops at the** <InlineMath math="70\%" /> fetal haemoglobin reaches.
- II and IV only rejects the stronger hold of fetal haemoglobin and swaps the roles of the vessels.

# Match the rate to the light that the pigments catch

Light drives photosynthesis only after a **pigment absorbs it**. So the rate at each wavelength should follow how much light all the pigments together absorb there.

- I: chlorophyll absorbs most at <InlineMath math="450\text{ nm}" /> and from <InlineMath math="650" /> to <InlineMath math="680\text{ nm}" />, <InlineMath math="70" /> to <InlineMath math="85\%" />, and the rate is highest there, <InlineMath math="85" /> to <InlineMath math="95" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: at <InlineMath math="500\text{ nm}" /> chlorophyll absorbs only <InlineMath math="30\%" />, yet the rate is <InlineMath math="60" />, double what that would suggest. Carotenoids absorb <InlineMath math="60\%" /> there and hand the energy to chlorophyll. Statement II is correct.
- III: the rate at <InlineMath math="550\text{ nm}" /> is <InlineMath math="20" />, low but not zero. A little green light is absorbed and used. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: chlorophyll absorbs green light **least**, only <InlineMath math="10\%" />. Most green light is reflected, and that reflected light is what makes leaves look green. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only reads the lowest rate as no rate, **but the rate at** <InlineMath math="550\text{ nm}" /> is still <InlineMath math="20" />.
- I and IV only gets the colour of leaves backwards, **since chlorophyll absorbs only** <InlineMath math="10\%" /> of green light.
- II, III and IV only rejects the clear match between chlorophyll absorption and the rate.

# Place each tube on the chain of clotting steps

Clotting is a **chain of steps**, each making the substance the next one needs. Removing a substance blocks every step after it, and adding a later substance skips the steps before it.

- I: citrate removes calcium ions and the blood never clots, in tube <InlineMath math="2" />. Adding calcium back restores clotting, in tube <InlineMath math="3" />. Calcium is needed where thrombokinase turns prothrombin into thrombin. Statement I is correct.
- II: the liver needs vitamin K to make prothrombin. With too little prothrombin, too little thrombin forms and the blood clots slowly. Statement II is correct.
- III: prothrombin becomes thrombin, and **thrombin** turns fibrinogen into fibrin. Fibrin does not come from prothrombin. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: tube <InlineMath math="4" /> still has no calcium, yet it clots in <InlineMath math="1" /> minute once thrombin is added. Thrombin works on fibrinogen at a step after the calcium-dependent one, and skipping the early steps makes it faster than fresh blood. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **although tube <InlineMath math="4" /> clots without any calcium ions**.
- I, II and III only makes fibrin from prothrombin, **skipping thrombin and fibrinogen**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **although citrated blood clots only when calcium is returned**.

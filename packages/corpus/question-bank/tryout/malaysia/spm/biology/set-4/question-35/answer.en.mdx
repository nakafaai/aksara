# Link the two curves through selection and the recessive allele

Bt cotton kills every bollworm **except those with two resistant alleles**, so those few pass on their alleles. A refuge changes who they mate with, and that decides whether their offspring survive.

- I: only bollworms with two resistant alleles survive on Bt cotton, so they leave the offspring and the resistant allele spreads. This is **natural selection**. Statement I is correct.
- II: from <InlineMath math="0.1\%" /> to <InlineMath math="30\%" /> is <InlineMath math="300" /> times. Statement II is correct.
- III: a rare resistant moth almost always mates with one of the many susceptible moths from the refuge. Their offspring carry one resistant allele and one susceptible allele, so the recessive resistance does not show and they die on Bt cotton. Resistance rises only <InlineMath math="16" /> times instead of <InlineMath math="300" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the gene comes from the bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis* and was inserted into cotton, not the other way round. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, missing that **heterozygous offspring still die**, so mating with refuge moths removes resistant alleles.
- II and III only rejects I, **although the spread of resistance is natural selection**.
- I, II and IV only reverses the gene transfer, **which went from the bacterium into cotton**.

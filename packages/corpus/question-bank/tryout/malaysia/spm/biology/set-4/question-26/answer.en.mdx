# Trace each part of the seed back to the nuclei that made it

Double fertilisation uses **two male gametes**. One joins the egg cell, the other joins the two polar nuclei. The seed coat is not part of fertilisation at all: it grows from the mother plant's own tissue.

The mother is <InlineMath math="AA" />, so every nucleus in her embryo sac carries <InlineMath math="A" /> and has <InlineMath math="n=10" /> chromosomes. The father is <InlineMath math="aa" />, so each male gamete carries <InlineMath math="a" /> with <InlineMath math="10" /> chromosomes.

<BlockMath math="\text{embryo}: A+a=Aa,\ 10+10=20,\qquad \text{endosperm}: A+A+a=AAa,\ 10+10+10=30,\qquad \text{testa}: AA,\ 20" />

So the endosperm has <InlineMath math="30" /> chromosomes and genotype <InlineMath math="AAa" />.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Endosperm <InlineMath math="Aaa" /> takes two sets from the father, **but both polar nuclei come from the mother**.
- Testa <InlineMath math="Aa" /> treats the seed coat as a product of fertilisation, **but it grows from the mother's integuments and stays** <InlineMath math="AA" />.
- Embryo with <InlineMath math="30" /> chromosomes gives the embryo the endosperm's triploid number, **while the embryo comes from one egg and one male gamete**.

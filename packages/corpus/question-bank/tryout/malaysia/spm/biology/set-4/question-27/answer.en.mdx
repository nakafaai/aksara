# Ask where the stomata end up and what the air next to them is like

Water vapour diffuses out of a stoma faster when the air beside it is **drier**. So what matters is the humidity right next to the stomata, and that depends on which way the leaf rolls.

- I: the stomata are on the upper surface, which ends up inside the tube. Air trapped there reaches <InlineMath math="85\%" /> humidity against <InlineMath math="30\%" /> outside, so the difference in water vapour is small and diffusion slows. Statement I is correct.
- II: the flat leaf loses <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ mg}" /> against <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ mg}" /> for the rolled one, four times as much. Statement II is correct.
- III: the stomata are on the upper surface, which faces **inwards** when the leaf rolls. The waxy lower surface is what faces the wind. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the leaf rolls in **dry** weather, when special cells along its grooves lose water and become flaccid. In wet weather these cells become turgid and the leaf opens out. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only puts the stomata on the outside of the tube, **although they are on the surface that rolls inwards**.
- I and IV only rejects the four-fold difference and links rolling with wet weather, **when it is a response to drought**.
- II and III only rejects the humid air inside the tube, **the reason the rolled leaf loses so little water**.

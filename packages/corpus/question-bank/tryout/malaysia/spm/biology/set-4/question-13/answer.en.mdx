# Read what changed in the blood and ask why

Small molecules cross the membrane by **diffusion**, down their concentration gradient, while large molecules cannot pass through the pores. So compare the blood before and after with the fluid to see what crossed.

- I: urea falls from <InlineMath math="30" /> to <InlineMath math="12\text{ mmol/L}" />, so <InlineMath math="60\%" /> is removed. Statement I is correct.
- II: with the flows in opposite directions, blood that has already lost urea meets the freshest fluid, so a gradient remains along the **whole membrane**. With both flowing the same way, the two would soon reach equal levels and diffusion would stop. Statement II is correct.
- III: the proteins stay at <InlineMath math="70\text{ g/L}" /> because they are far too large to pass through the pores, so none crosses at all. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: without glucose in the fluid, glucose would diffuse down its gradient out of the blood. That is why the fluid holds the same <InlineMath math="5\text{ mmol/L}" /> as blood. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **although a gradient would drive glucose out**.
- I, II and III only explains the steady protein level by movement, **when proteins cannot cross the membrane at all**.
- II, III and IV only rejects the <InlineMath math="60\%" /> that the urea readings give directly.

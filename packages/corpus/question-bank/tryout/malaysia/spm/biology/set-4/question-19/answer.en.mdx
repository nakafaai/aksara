# Treat the sugar as a pool filled by one microbe and drained by another

Sugar sits **between two processes**: the mould's amylase makes it from starch, and the yeast uses it up. Whether the sugar rises or falls depends on which process is faster.

- I: starch falls from <InlineMath math="30" /> to <InlineMath math="5" /> while sugar first rises, and only the mould releases amylase. Statement I is correct.
- II: from day <InlineMath math="2" /> to day <InlineMath math="4" /> starch keeps falling, from <InlineMath math="14" /> to <InlineMath math="5" />, so sugar is still being made. Yet sugar falls from <InlineMath math="10" /> to <InlineMath math="7" />, because the yeast now uses it faster than it is made. Statement II is correct.
- III: in the covered container oxygen runs low, so the yeast respires **anaerobically**, turning sugar into alcohol and carbon dioxide. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the yeast cannot break down starch, so without the mould no sugar would form, and the yeast would have nothing to ferment. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, as if falling sugar meant starch had stopped breaking down, **but starch keeps falling**.
- II, III and IV only rejects the mould's role, **although the yeast cannot use starch**.
- I, II and IV only accepts IV and rejects anaerobic respiration, **the only process here that makes alcohol**.

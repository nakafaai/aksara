# Turn each distance into an Rf value and match it to the table

In the same solvent, each pigment always travels the **same fraction** of the solvent's distance. So dividing each distance by <InlineMath math="80\text{ mm}" /> identifies the pigment.

<BlockMath math="\frac{76}{80}=0.95,\qquad \frac{56}{80}=0.70,\qquad \frac{36}{80}=0.45,\qquad \frac{24}{80}=0.30,\qquad \frac{20}{80}=0.25" />

The spot at <InlineMath math="36\text{ mm}" /> has <InlineMath math="\text{R}_\text{f}=0.45" />, which is chlorophyll a. The seaweed's spots at <InlineMath math="76" /> and <InlineMath math="36\text{ mm}" /> match carotene and chlorophyll a, but its spot at <InlineMath math="20\text{ mm}" /> has <InlineMath math="\text{R}_\text{f}=0.25" />, which matches **none** of the leaf pigments. Chlorophyll b would always appear at <InlineMath math="0.30" />, <InlineMath math="24\text{ mm}" />, in this solvent.

So the spot at <InlineMath math="36\text{ mm}" /> is chlorophyll a, and the seaweed holds a pigment that the leaf does not have.

# Why the other options are wrong

- Chlorophyll b reads the spot at <InlineMath math="24\text{ mm}" />, **not the one at** <InlineMath math="36\text{ mm}" />.
- Chlorophyll b travelling less far forgets that a pigment's <InlineMath math="\text{R}_\text{f}" /> is **fixed in a given solvent**, so it cannot move to <InlineMath math="0.25" />.
- Xanthophyll has <InlineMath math="\text{R}_\text{f}=0.70" />, which belongs to the spot at <InlineMath math="56\text{ mm}" />.

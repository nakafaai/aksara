# Split the carbon dioxide into the part that matches oxygen and the extra

Aerobic respiration releases **one volume of carbon dioxide for each volume of oxygen**. Any carbon dioxide beyond the oxygen used must come from anaerobic respiration, which uses no oxygen.

- I: in hour <InlineMath math="1" /> the carbon dioxide equals the oxygen used, so all respiration is aerobic. Statement I is correct.
- II: in hour <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}^3" /> of carbon dioxide matches the oxygen and the other <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}^3" /> is anaerobic. Statement II is correct.
- III: in hour <InlineMath math="3" /> no oxygen is left, and carbon dioxide still forms, so the yeast ferments glucose into **ethanol** and carbon dioxide. Statement III is correct.
- IV: anaerobic respiration breaks glucose down only partly, so it releases far **less** energy per glucose than aerobic respiration. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, often after taking all <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}^3" /> as anaerobic, **forgetting the** <InlineMath math="3\text{ cm}^3" /> that matches the oxygen.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and accepts IV, **although fermentation yields much less energy**.
- I, II and IV only rejects ethanol in hour <InlineMath math="3" />, **the only product besides carbon dioxide that yeast makes without oxygen**.

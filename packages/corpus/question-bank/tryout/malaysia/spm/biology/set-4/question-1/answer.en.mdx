# Divide out the light each filter lets through before comparing colours

The colour is the manipulated variable, but the filters also change **how much light** reaches the plant. Until that second difference is removed, the raw counts compare two things at once.

- I: red gives the most bubbles only because its filter passes twice as much light as the blue one. Per unit of light, red is beaten by blue. Statement I is wrong.
- II: blue gives <InlineMath math="13" /> bubbles per minute for each <InlineMath math="10\%" /> of light, the highest value. Statement II is correct.
- III: green gives only about <InlineMath math="2.7" />, the lowest. Chlorophyll absorbs little green light and reflects much of it, which is why leaves look green. Statement III is correct.
- IV: light intensity changed with the filter, so it should have been a **fixed variable**, for example by moving the lamp until the meter reads the same. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, III and IV only trusts the raw counts for red, **although the red filter passes twice the light of the blue one**.
- II and IV only rejects III, **but green does give the fewest bubbles per unit of light**.
- I and III only reads the raw counts and misses the **uncontrolled light intensity**.

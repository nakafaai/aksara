# Compare like with like, then turn a speed into a time

To see what myelin does on its own, compare two axons of the **same diameter**. To find a time, divide the distance by the speed and keep the units consistent.

<BlockMath math="\frac{60}{3}=20,\qquad \text{time}=\frac{1.2\text{ m}}{60\text{ m/s}}=0.02\text{ s}=20\text{ ms}" />

- I: at <InlineMath math="10\,\mu\text{m}" />, myelin raises the speed from <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="60\text{ m/s}" />, <InlineMath math="20" /> times. Making an unmyelinated axon ten times wider only triples its speed. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ m}" /> at <InlineMath math="60\text{ m/s}" /> takes <InlineMath math="0.02\text{ s}" />, which is <InlineMath math="20" /> milliseconds. Statement II is correct.
- III: myelin insulates the axon, so the impulse only has to occur at the **nodes of Ranvier** and jumps between them. Without myelin it must pass along every part of the membrane, which is slower. Statement III is correct.
- IV: jumping from node to node happens in **myelinated** axons, and that is why they are faster. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although losing myelin removes the very jumping that makes impulses fast**.
- II and III only rejects I, often after comparing <InlineMath math="60" /> with <InlineMath math="1" />, axons of **different diameters**.
- I, II and IV only places the jumping in unmyelinated axons, **the opposite of where the nodes are**.

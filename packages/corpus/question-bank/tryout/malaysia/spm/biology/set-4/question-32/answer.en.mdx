# Follow each Z chromosome from parent to chick

A daughter is <InlineMath math="ZW" />, and her <InlineMath math="W" /> comes from her mother, so her **only Z comes from her father**. A son gets one <InlineMath math="Z" /> from each parent.

Every first-generation son gets <InlineMath math="Z^B" /> from his barred mother and is barred, while every daughter gets <InlineMath math="Z^b" /> from her plain father and is plain. Farmers use this cross to sort day-old chicks by sex from their feathers. In the second generation, each daughter gets one of her father's two <InlineMath math="Z" /> chromosomes, so half are barred.

# Why the other options are wrong

- All chicks barred treats the gene as **autosomal**, so a dominant allele from one parent reaches every chick.
- Half of each sex barred would need a **heterozygous parent**, but the male is <InlineMath math="Z^bZ^b" /> and the female has only one <InlineMath math="Z" />.
- Sons plain and daughters barred applies the **human pattern**, where the father passes his X to daughters, to a bird where the mother is the one with two different sex chromosomes.

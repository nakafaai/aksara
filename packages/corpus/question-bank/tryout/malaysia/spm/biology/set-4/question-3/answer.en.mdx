# Work out each new concentration, then read the burst level off the curve

Water enters a red cell by **osmosis** whenever the solution is weaker than <InlineMath math="0.90\%" />. How many cells burst depends on how much weaker, because cells differ in how much swelling they can stand. So each mixture must be turned into a concentration first.

<BlockMath math="\text{I}: \tfrac{0.30+0.90}{2}=0.60\%,\qquad \text{III}: \tfrac{0.40+0.50}{2}=0.45\%,\qquad \text{IV}: \tfrac{0.90+0}{2}=0.45\%" />

- I: the mixture is <InlineMath math="0.60\%" />, where no cells burst, although water still enters and the cells swell. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="0.45\%" /> is only half the strength of plasma, so it is still **hypotonic**. Half the cells burst because only the weaker half cannot stand the swelling. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the mixture is <InlineMath math="0.45\%" />, where <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of the cells burst. Statement III is correct.
- IV: diluting <InlineMath math="0.90\%" /> with an equal volume of water halves it to <InlineMath math="0.45\%" />, so again about half the cells burst. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only treats the halfway point as isotonic, **but isotonic is** <InlineMath math="0.90\%" />, where no cell bursts.
- III and IV only rejects I, forgetting that a <InlineMath math="0.60\%" /> mixture is **too mild to burst** any cell.
- I and IV only rejects III, **although the two solutions average exactly** <InlineMath math="0.45\%" />.

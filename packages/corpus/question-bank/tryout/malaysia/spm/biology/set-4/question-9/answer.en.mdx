# Separate what needs energy from what follows a gradient

Every sac starts with **no concentration gradient**: <InlineMath math="5\text{ mM}" /> on both sides. Any later difference must have been built by active transport, and the gas shows whether that needed aerobic respiration.

- I: in sac <InlineMath math="1" />, glucose leaves the gut side, where it falls to <InlineMath math="2\text{ mM}" />, and piles up on the blood side at <InlineMath math="12\text{ mM}" />. It moves from low to high concentration, against the gradient. Statement I is correct.
- II: with nitrogen instead of oxygen, in sac <InlineMath math="2" />, no difference builds up. Without aerobic respiration there is too little ATP for the **active transport** of glucose. Statement II is correct.
- III: fructose stays at <InlineMath math="5\text{ mM}" /> on both sides, but that only shows it is not pumped. With no gradient, molecules cross equally both ways, so the levels stay equal. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: fructose crosses the lining by **facilitated diffusion**, which needs no ATP. With <InlineMath math="20\text{ mM}" /> on one side and none on the other, it moves down the gradient even under nitrogen. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, as if all absorption needed oxygen, **but diffusion down a gradient needs no ATP**.
- I, II and III only reads equal levels as no crossing, **when equal levels mean equal movement both ways**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I, **although glucose ends six times higher on the blood side**.

# See root pressure as a push from below that transpiration hides by day

Root cells pump ions into the xylem, water follows by osmosis, and the build-up **pushes sap upwards**. By day, transpiration pulls water out of the xylem faster than this push supplies it, so the measured push falls.

- I: the pressure peaks at <InlineMath math="150\text{ kPa}" /> at hour <InlineMath math="4" />, before dawn, and is lowest, <InlineMath math="20" />, at noon. Statement I is correct.
- II: cyanide stops respiration and the pressure drops to <InlineMath math="0" />, so the push needs ATP to **pump ions** into the xylem. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="150\text{ kPa}" /> holds up only about <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" /> of water, half of <InlineMath math="30\text{ m}" />. Tall trees rely on transpiration pull. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: at dawn, before transpiration starts, root pressure forces water out through pores at the leaf edges. This is guttation. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, missing that guttation happens when **root pressure is high and transpiration is low**.
- I, II and III only forgets to convert the pressure into a height, which gives **only fifteen metres**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and accepts the impossible thirty metres.

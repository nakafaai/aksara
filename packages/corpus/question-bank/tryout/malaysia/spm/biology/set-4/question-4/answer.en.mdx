# Compare the area of the lipid with the area of membrane it came from

If the membrane were **one** layer of lipid molecules, the lipid would cover the same area as the cell surfaces. Each extra layer adds another copy of that area, so the ratio counts the layers.

- I: <InlineMath math="2\times10^9" /> cells of <InlineMath math="140\text{ µm}^2" /> give <InlineMath math="2.8\times10^{11}\text{ µm}^2" />, which is <InlineMath math="2.8\times10^5\text{ mm}^2" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: the layer covers exactly twice the cell surface, so the membrane holds enough lipid for **two layers**, a bilayer. Statement II is correct.
- III: fatty acid tails are hydrophobic. They point **inwards**, towards each other in the middle of the bilayer, while the phosphate heads face the water on both sides. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: a molecule with a phosphate head and two fatty acid tails is a **phospholipid**. A triglyceride has three fatty acids and no phosphate. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts III, **although water-repelling tails turn away from water**.
- II and IV only rejects the area calculation and **calls a phospholipid a triglyceride**.
- I and IV only rejects II, **the two layers that the ratio of <InlineMath math="2" /> shows**.

# Separate the seasonal swing from the yearly rise, then compare the hemispheres

Each curve has a **seasonal swing**, driven by plants growing in summer, and a **yearly rise**, driven by burning fossil fuels and clearing forests. The size and timing of the swing show where the plants are and when their summer comes.

- I: in the northern summer, plants photosynthesise faster than all living things respire, so carbon dioxide drops from <InlineMath math="421" /> to <InlineMath math="414.5" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: the southern station moves less than <InlineMath math="1\text{ ppm}" /> against <InlineMath math="6.5" /> in the north. The southern hemisphere is mostly ocean, with far less land under plants, so its seasonal uptake is much smaller. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="29.5\text{ ppm}" /> more at <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ ppm}" /> a year takes about <InlineMath math="12" /> years. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the southern station is lowest in July, while the north is lowest in September. The seasons are reversed: southern summer falls around January. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although** <InlineMath math="\tfrac{29.5}{2.5}" /> is about twelve.
- II and III only rejects the role of photosynthesis, **the reason the northern curve dips every summer**.
- I, II and IV only assumes one summer for the whole Earth, **although the seasons are reversed between hemispheres**.

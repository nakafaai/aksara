# Expect the usual one to two to one, then find the chance that both implants fail

Two carriers give embryos in the ratio **<InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> unaffected non-carriers, <InlineMath math="\tfrac12" /> carriers and <InlineMath math="\tfrac14" /> affected**. Testing only **sorts** the embryos, it changes no genes. For "at least one baby", work out the chance that neither embryo develops and take it from <InlineMath math="1" />.

<BlockMath math="8\times\tfrac34=6\ \text{free of disease},\qquad 8\times\tfrac12=4\ \text{carriers},\qquad 1-0.7\times0.7=1-0.49=0.51" />

- I: three quarters of <InlineMath math="8" /> embryos, <InlineMath math="6" />, are expected to be free of the disease, and half of all <InlineMath math="8" />, which is <InlineMath math="4" />, are expected to be carriers. Statement I is correct.
- II: at the <InlineMath math="8" />-cell stage each cell can still give rise to any tissue, so the embryo can make up for the lost cell and develop normally. Statement II is correct.
- III: each embryo fails with chance <InlineMath math="0.7" />, so both fail with chance <InlineMath math="0.49" />, and at least one develops with chance <InlineMath math="0.51" />, about <InlineMath math="51\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the test only reads the DNA of one cell to choose embryos. It does not change any gene, so a carrier embryo stays a carrier. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, **although cells at the <InlineMath math="8" />-cell stage can still form any tissue**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **confuses choosing embryos with changing their genes**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts IV, **although the test only reads the DNA**.

# Divide pollen by ovules, then ask how many grains actually arrive

Each ovule needs one pollen grain. The **pollen per ovule** shows how many grains the plant makes for every one it needs, and the **share that arrives** decides how many ovules are fertilised. If fewer grains arrive than there are ovules, some ovules stay unfertilised.

- II: wind does not aim, so nearly all of P's pollen lands on the ground, on leaves or on other species. Only huge numbers make sure a few grains reach a stigma. Statement II is correct.
- III: at <InlineMath math="1" /> in <InlineMath math="100\,000" />, a flower's <InlineMath math="60\,000" /> grains deliver on average only <InlineMath math="0.6" /> of a grain, enough for at most <InlineMath math="0.6" /> of its <InlineMath math="2" /> ovules, about <InlineMath math="30\%" />. That is fewer than half. Statement III is correct.
- IV: petals and nectar attract animals. A wind-pollinated flower such as P's usually has small, dull petals or none, and hanging anthers and feathery stigmas instead. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements II and III are correct.

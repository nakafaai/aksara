# Compare tubes that differ in one thing

Each pair of tubes isolates one factor. B against C asks what **specific antibodies** add, and C against D asks what **complement** adds on top of them. Antibodies stick to the bacteria and mark them, so phagocytes grab them faster.

- I: <InlineMath math="12" /> bacteria with vaccinated serum against <InlineMath math="3" /> with unvaccinated serum is <InlineMath math="4" /> times as many. Statement I is correct.
- II: heating destroys complement but leaves antibodies working, and the count falls from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="8" />. Complement adds about <InlineMath math="4" /> bacteria per phagocyte. Statement II is correct.
- III: unvaccinated serum raises the count only from <InlineMath math="2" /> to <InlineMath math="3" />, so plain serum does little. The big rise needs antibodies that recognise this bacterium, made after vaccination. Statement III is correct.
- IV: phagocytes engulf and digest pathogens. Antibodies are made by lymphocytes and were brought in with the serum. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **the control that shows the antibodies must be specific**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **mixes up phagocytes with lymphocytes**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **although heating lowers the count from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="8" />**.

# Read the fall in water potential per metre, and ask what does the lifting

Water rises because transpiration at the leaves **pulls** on a continuous column held together by **cohesion**. The higher the leaf, the harder it must pull. Take the change in water potential over the change in height, and watch the units.

- I: as water evaporates from the leaves, it pulls the water below it up the xylem, and cohesion keeps the long column from breaking. Statement I is correct.
- II: the water potential falls by <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ MPa}" /> over <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" />, which is <InlineMath math="0.15\text{ MPa}" /> every <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" />, not <InlineMath math="1.5" />. Statement II is wrong.
- III: leaves at the top must pull water against the greatest height, so their cells end up with too little water to swell and grow fully. Statement III is correct.
- IV: root pressure lifts water only a few metres, far short of <InlineMath math="110\text{ m}" />. The lift comes from the pull of transpiration. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **reading the fall per <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> as the fall per <InlineMath math="10\text{ m}" />**.
- III and IV only rejects I and **gives root pressure a lift it cannot reach**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **although root pressure pushes only a few metres**.

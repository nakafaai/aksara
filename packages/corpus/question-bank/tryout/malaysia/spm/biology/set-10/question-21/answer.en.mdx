# Work out the yearly widening and the area, then ask which meristems each tree has

A dicot tree widens through **secondary growth**: its vascular cambium keeps adding xylem and phloem. A palm has **no vascular cambium**, so its trunk reaches its width early and then grows only in height, from one apical meristem. Area grows with the **square** of the diameter.

- I: with radii of <InlineMath math="5" />, <InlineMath math="11" /> and <InlineMath math="22.5\text{ cm}" />, the area is about <InlineMath math="79" />, <InlineMath math="380" /> and <InlineMath math="1590\text{ cm}^2" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: the rain tree widens <InlineMath math="4\text{ cm}" /> a year from year <InlineMath math="3" /> to <InlineMath math="6" /> and about <InlineMath math="3.8\text{ cm}" /> a year from year <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="12" />, nearly the same rate, not twice as fast. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the diameter grows <InlineMath math="4.5" /> times, so the area grows <InlineMath math="4.5^2\approx20" /> times. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the palm's only growing point is at its tip. Remove it and nothing can make new leaves, so the palm dies, unlike a dicot tree with side buds. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, III and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects IV, **the palm's dependence on its single growing point**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **misses that the same widening adds more area around a thicker trunk**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts II, **although the yearly widening is nearly the same in both periods**.

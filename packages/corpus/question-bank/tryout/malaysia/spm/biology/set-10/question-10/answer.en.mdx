# Add or subtract <InlineMath math="10\text{ kPa}" /> for every metre above or below the heart

The heart sets the pressure at its own level. Lifting blood **uses up pressure**, and blood below the heart is **pushed harder** by the column above it. High pressure in capillaries forces fluid out into the tissues, so places with very high pressure need protection.

- I: the giraffe's brain gets <InlineMath math="35-20=15\text{ kPa}" /> and the human's <InlineMath math="13-4=9\text{ kPa}" />, so the giraffe's is higher. Statement I is correct.
- II: with the head <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> below the heart, the pressure is <InlineMath math="35+20=55\text{ kPa}" />, high enough to burst fine vessels unless valves and vessel networks in the neck limit it. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ m}" /> below the heart, the pressure is <InlineMath math="35+25=60\text{ kPa}" />. Such pressure would push fluid out of the capillaries and cause swelling, which tight skin and thick walls resist. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the giraffe's heart beats at <InlineMath math="60" /> a minute, slower than the human's <InlineMath math="70" />. Its high pressure comes from the thick, muscular wall of its left ventricle. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, **although <InlineMath math="35+20" /> is <InlineMath math="55\text{ kPa}" />**.
- II and IV only rejects I and III and **puts the source of the pressure in the heart rate**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV, **although the giraffe's heart beats more slowly**.

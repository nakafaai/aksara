# Compare the blood going in with the blood coming out

The oxygen used by the tissues is the **drop in saturation** from arterial to venous blood. Cyanide leaves the blood full of oxygen but stops the cells using it, so the drop shrinks while the cells turn to anaerobic respiration.

- I: the healthy tissues take the saturation from <InlineMath math="98" /> to <InlineMath math="75\%" />, a drop of <InlineMath math="23\%" />. The poisoned tissues take it only to <InlineMath math="95\%" />, a drop of <InlineMath math="3\%" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: with aerobic respiration blocked, cells break glucose down to lactic acid, which releases only a small part of its energy. Lactate rises from <InlineMath math="1" /> to <InlineMath math="12\text{ mmol}" /> per litre. Statement II is correct.
- III: the arterial blood is already <InlineMath math="98\%" /> saturated, and the venous blood still holds <InlineMath math="95\%" />. The problem is that the cells cannot use the oxygen, so breathing more of it does not help much. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: lactic acid from anaerobic respiration enters the blood and lowers its pH from <InlineMath math="7.4" /> to <InlineMath math="7.1" />. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the acid that lowers the pH to <InlineMath math="7.1" />**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **treats a full blood supply as a shortage**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **although the venous blood is still <InlineMath math="95\%" /> saturated**.

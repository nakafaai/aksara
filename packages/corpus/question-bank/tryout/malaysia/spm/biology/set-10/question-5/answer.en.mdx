# Separate the speed of the reaction from how far it can go

An enzyme is a **catalyst**: it speeds the reaction but is not used up. More enzyme makes product form faster, while the **amount of substrate** sets the final volume. Read the time to half of the final <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}^3" />, that is <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}^3" />, from each curve.

- I: P collects <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}^3" /> and Q <InlineMath math="20\text{ cm}^3" /> in the first <InlineMath math="20" /> seconds, so the starting rate doubles from <InlineMath math="0.5" /> to <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ cm}^3" /> per second. Statement I is correct.
- II: P passes <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}^3" /> between <InlineMath math="20" /> and <InlineMath math="40" /> seconds, at about <InlineMath math="32" /> seconds, while Q passes it at about <InlineMath math="15" /> seconds, roughly half the time. Statement II is correct.
- III: twice the hydrogen peroxide holds twice the oxygen, so the final volume would be about <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}^3" />, reached more slowly with only <InlineMath math="1" /> unit of enzyme. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the rate falls because the hydrogen peroxide is being used up, so the enzyme meets substrate less often. The catalase comes out of each reaction unchanged. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **although the substrate sets the final volume**.
- II and IV only rejects I and **treats the enzyme as a reactant that runs out**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **misreading when each tube reaches <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}^3" />**.

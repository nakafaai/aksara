# Diffusion time grows with the square of the distance

Doubling a distance makes diffusion take **four times** as long, and ten times the distance takes a hundred times as long. That is why diffusion works only over short paths, and why air tubes limit how big an insect can be. Open spiracles also cost water.

- I: ten times the length gives <InlineMath math="10^2=100" /> times the diffusion time. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> is <InlineMath math="10" /> times <InlineMath math="1\text{ mm}" />, so the time is <InlineMath math="100" /> times <InlineMath math="0.01\text{ s}" />, which is <InlineMath math="1\text{ s}" />. Statement II is correct.
- III: water loss falls from <InlineMath math="8" /> to <InlineMath math="3\text{ mg}" /> an hour, a cut of about <InlineMath math="63\%" />, more than half. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the tracheae take oxygen straight to the cells, so insect blood carries little oxygen and most insects have no haemoglobin. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, **although ten times the distance means a hundred times the time**.
- II and IV only rejects I and III and **sends the oxygen through the blood instead of the air tubes**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts IV, **although the tracheae bypass the blood**.

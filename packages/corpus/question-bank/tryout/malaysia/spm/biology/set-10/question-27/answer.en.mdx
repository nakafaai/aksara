# Divide the stored water by the daily loss, then compare it with the dry spell

A plant on a high branch gains light but has no soil to hold water between showers. Its adaptations **catch water fast, store it and lose it slowly**. How long the store lasts is the water stored divided by the water lost each day. Taking nothing from the tree's tissues makes it an epiphyte, not a parasite.

- I: <InlineMath math="20\text{ g}" /> at <InlineMath math="2\text{ g}" /> a day lasts <InlineMath math="10" /> days, while at <InlineMath math="8\text{ g}" /> a day it would last <InlineMath math="2.5" /> days, a quarter as long. Statement I is correct.
- II: the store lasts <InlineMath math="10" /> days, so a <InlineMath math="15" />-day dry spell empties it <InlineMath math="5" /> days before rain returns. The orchid must survive those days by drying out or closing down. Statement II is correct.
- III: the orchid only perches on the tree. It gets water from rain and minerals from dust and debris, and takes nothing from the tree's tissues, so it is not a parasite. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: rain runs off bark quickly. A layer that soaks up water in seconds lets the orchid take its share before it is gone. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the root layer that catches each brief shower**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **makes the orchid a parasite on its host**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **although <InlineMath math="10" /> days of water cannot cover <InlineMath math="15" /> dry days**.

# Ask which parent the trait passes from, and to which children

A father gives his **Y only to sons**, so a Y-linked trait goes from father to every son and never to a daughter. Mitochondria come **only from the egg**, so a mitochondrial trait goes from a mother to all her children and never from a father. A dominant X-linked allele would go from a father to **every daughter**.

- I: every son of an affected man is affected, no daughter is, and the trait carries on from son to grandson. That is the pattern of a gene on the Y chromosome. Statement I is correct.
- II: an affected son with a dominant <InlineMath math="X" />-linked allele would give his only X to every daughter, so all of them would be affected. Yet none of his children are, so trait Q is not <InlineMath math="X" />-linked dominant. Statement II is correct.
- III: every child of an affected woman is affected, and no child of an affected man is, exactly as expected if the trait rides in the mitochondria that only eggs pass on. Statement III is correct.
- IV: women have no Y chromosome, so they cannot carry trait P at all. A son gets his Y only from his father. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **the maternal pattern that fits genes in the mitochondria**.
- II and IV only rejects I and III and **gives women a Y chromosome to carry**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **although an X-linked dominant father would affect all his daughters**.

# Track the mass of haemoglobin, then the volume it is spread through

Whole blood carries plasma and red cells together, so a donation removes haemoglobin **in proportion to the volume** given. Plasma comes back within days, but red cells take weeks. Once the volume is restored, the remaining haemoglobin is **spread through more fluid**.

- IV: the body held <InlineMath math="750\text{ g}" /> and now holds <InlineMath math="682.5\text{ g}" />. Spread through <InlineMath math="5.0\text{ L}" /> again, that is about <InlineMath math="13.7\text{ g}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ mL}" />, not <InlineMath math="15" />, until the marrow catches up. So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, **although <InlineMath math="67.5\times3.4" /> is about <InlineMath math="230" />**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **forgets that refilling the volume dilutes what remains**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts IV, **although the haemoglobin mass is still <InlineMath math="67.5\text{ g}" /> short**.

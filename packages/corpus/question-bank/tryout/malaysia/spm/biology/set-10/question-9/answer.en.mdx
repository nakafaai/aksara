# Work out the daily shortfall, then follow the parts of the gut that food now skips

The body burns stored fat to cover the gap between the energy it uses and the energy it takes in. Divide the **daily shortfall** by the energy in each gram of fat, then scale up to a week. Food that skips the duodenum also skips the place where much of the **iron and calcium** is absorbed.

- I: <InlineMath math="30\text{ mL}" /> out of <InlineMath math="1000\text{ mL}" /> is <InlineMath math="3\%" /> of the original volume, so the patient feels full after very little food. Statement I is correct.
- II: the body uses <InlineMath math="3000\text{ kJ}" /> a day more than it takes in. At <InlineMath math="37\text{ kJ}" /> per gram that is about <InlineMath math="81\text{ g}" /> of fat a day, or about <InlineMath math="0.57\text{ kg}" /> a week. Statement II is correct.
- III: with the duodenum bypassed, less iron is absorbed for haemoglobin and less calcium for bones, so anaemia and weak bones can follow unless supplements make up the shortfall. Statement III is correct.
- IV: pepsin is made by the stomach, not the duodenum, and protein digestion also goes on in the small intestine with trypsin from the pancreas. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects III, **the minerals lost when food skips the duodenum**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **places pepsin in the wrong organ**.
- I, III and IV only accepts IV and rejects II, **although <InlineMath math="3000\text{ kJ}" /> a day is about <InlineMath math="81\text{ g}" /> of fat**.

# Pick the right period for each species, then turn the fall into a percentage

Three generations is <InlineMath math="15" /> years for X, <InlineMath math="6" /> years for Y and <InlineMath math="30" /> years for Z. Take whichever is longer, that or <InlineMath math="10" /> years, so the periods are **<InlineMath math="15" />, <InlineMath math="10" /> and <InlineMath math="30" /> years**. The fall is the loss divided by the number at the start.

<BlockMath math="X: \frac{2000-300}{2000}=0.85,\qquad Y: \frac{1000-400}{1000}=0.60,\qquad Z: \frac{5000-3000}{5000}=0.40" />

- I: three generations of X is <InlineMath math="15" /> years, longer than <InlineMath math="10" />, and the fall from <InlineMath math="2000" /> to <InlineMath math="300" /> is <InlineMath math="85\%" />, at least <InlineMath math="80\%" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: three generations of Y is only <InlineMath math="6" /> years, so the <InlineMath math="10" />-year period is used, and the fall from <InlineMath math="1000" /> to <InlineMath math="400" /> is <InlineMath math="60\%" />. That makes Y endangered, not critically endangered. Statement II is wrong.
- III: three generations of Z is <InlineMath math="30" /> years, and the fall from <InlineMath math="5000" /> to <InlineMath math="3000" /> is <InlineMath math="40\%" />, which is at least <InlineMath math="30\%" /> but below <InlineMath math="50\%" />. Statement III is correct.
- IV: Z is still declining and qualifies as vulnerable. A large number now does not stop a steady decline, and acting early is cheaper than rescuing a species near the end. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **although <InlineMath math="60\%" /> falls short of the <InlineMath math="80\%" /> needed**.
- III and IV only rejects I and **ignores a species that is still declining**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts II and IV, **misreading Y's fall and dismissing Z's**.

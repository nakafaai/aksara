# Find the means without the anomaly, then compare each change with the scatter of the readings

A reading far from the other two replicates is **anomalous** and is left out. A difference between means is convincing only if it is **large compared with the scatter** of the replicates. A test of five concentrations shows the best tested value, **not the exact optimum** between them.

- I: both steps raise the mean by <InlineMath math="1.9\text{ cm}" />, from <InlineMath math="10.4" /> to <InlineMath math="12.3" /> and from <InlineMath math="12.3" /> to <InlineMath math="14.2\text{ cm}" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: with <InlineMath math="8.1\text{ cm}" /> included, the mean at <InlineMath math="2\text{ g}" /> per litre would be about <InlineMath math="12.2\text{ cm}" />, below the <InlineMath math="12.3\text{ cm}" /> at <InlineMath math="1\text{ g}" /> per litre, a false dip. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="3\text{ g}" /> per litre is only the best of the values tested. The mean falls at <InlineMath math="4" />, so the true optimum could lie anywhere between <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="4\text{ g}" /> per litre, and only more concentrations in that range can find it. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the replicates at <InlineMath math="3\text{ g}" /> per litre range from <InlineMath math="15.0" /> to <InlineMath math="15.4\text{ cm}" />, a spread of <InlineMath math="0.4\text{ cm}" />, while the mean falls by <InlineMath math="1.2\text{ cm}" />, three times as much. Such a fall is unlikely to come from random scatter alone. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the comparison of the fall with the scatter of the replicates**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and accepts III, **treating the best tested value as the exact optimum**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II, **although the anomaly drags the mean below that of <InlineMath math="1\text{ g}" /> per litre**.

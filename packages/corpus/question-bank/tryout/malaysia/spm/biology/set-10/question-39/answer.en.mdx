# Compare the phosphate per gram as well as the totals, then weigh what each partner gets

A bigger plant holds more phosphate simply because it is bigger, so compare the **phosphate in each gram** too. If the plant still grows better after paying the fungus in sugar, **both partners gain**, which is mutualism, not parasitism.

- I: phosphate per gram rises from about <InlineMath math="1.33" /> to <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mg}" />, a rise of <InlineMath math="50\%" />, so the plant with the fungus is richer in phosphate as well as bigger. Statement I is correct.

- III: a parasite harms its host, but this plant grows to <InlineMath math="2.7" /> times the dry mass with the fungus. The fungus gets sugar and the plant gets phosphate, so both benefit. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ g}" /> against <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ g}" /> is about <InlineMath math="2.7" /> times, more than double, even after the plant gives away <InlineMath math="15\%" /> of its sugar. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **the growth gain that pays back the sugar**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **calls a partner that improves growth a parasite**.
- I, III and IV only accepts III and rejects II, **although the plant gains far more than it gives**.

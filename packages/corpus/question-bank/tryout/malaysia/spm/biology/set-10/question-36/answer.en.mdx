# Turn the fall into a percentage loss, then apply the rule

From <InlineMath math="300" /> to <InlineMath math="100" />, **two thirds** of the ozone is lost. Twice that percentage is the rise in UV-B, and a rise of more than <InlineMath math="100\%" /> means more than double. The ban stops new CFCs, but those already in the air **keep working for decades**. Ozone loss and global warming are **different problems**.

- I: a two-thirds loss is about <InlineMath math="67\%" />, and twice that is a rise of about <InlineMath math="133\%" />, so UV-B would be about <InlineMath math="2.3" /> times as strong, more than double. Statement I is correct.
- II: CFCs released before <InlineMath math="1987" /> stay in the air for <InlineMath math="50" /> to <InlineMath math="100" /> years, and each chlorine atom destroys about <InlineMath math="100\,000" /> ozone molecules, so the damage goes on long after the ban. Statement II is correct.
- III: UV-B damages DNA, which can lead to skin cancer, and harms phytoplankton near the sea surface, the producers that ocean food chains depend on. Statement III is correct.
- IV: global warming comes mainly from greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and methane trapping heat. A thinner ozone layer lets in more UV-B but is not the main cause of warming. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- II and III only rejects I, **although a <InlineMath math="133\%" /> rise is more than double**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and **confuses ozone loss with the greenhouse effect**.
- I, II and IV only rejects III, **the harm that extra UV-B does to living things**.

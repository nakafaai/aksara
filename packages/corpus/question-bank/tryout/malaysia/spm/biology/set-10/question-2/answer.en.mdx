# Divide each tissue's share of energy by its share of mass

A tissue that uses a large share of energy but is a small share of mass is **burning energy fast per gram**. Such tissues are packed with mitochondria, so a fault in mitochondria hits them hardest. The average for the whole body is <InlineMath math="1" />, because <InlineMath math="100\%" /> of energy is spread over <InlineMath math="100\%" /> of mass.

- I: the brain is <InlineMath math="2\%" /> of the mass but uses <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of the energy, <InlineMath math="10" /> times the body's average rate per gram. Statement I is correct.
- II: heart muscle scores <InlineMath math="20" /> and resting skeletal muscle <InlineMath math="0.5" />, so the heart uses <InlineMath math="40" /> times as much per gram. Statement II is correct.
- III: red blood cells have no mitochondria and make all their ATP by anaerobic respiration in the cytoplasm, so a mitochondrial fault barely touches them. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the brain, the heart and working muscles have the most mitochondria and the highest demand, so they fail first. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and IV only rejects II, **although <InlineMath math="20" /> divided by <InlineMath math="0.5" /> is <InlineMath math="40" />**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **forgets that red blood cells have no mitochondria to damage**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts III, **picking the cells that do not use mitochondria at all**.

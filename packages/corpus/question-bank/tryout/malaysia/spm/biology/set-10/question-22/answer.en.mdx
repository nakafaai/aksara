# Turn each time into a rate and compare it with the light

A disc floats once enough **oxygen fills the air spaces** of its spongy mesophyll. A shorter time means a faster rate, so the rate is <InlineMath math="1\div" />time. If light alone limited photosynthesis, four times the light would give four times the rate. A smaller rise shows that **another factor** is starting to limit it.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1/6}{1/12}=2,\qquad \frac{1/6}{1/30}=5" />

- I: the spongy mesophyll has large air spaces. Photosynthesis releases oxygen into them, and the gas makes the disc buoyant. Statement I is correct.
- II: in plain water, with light but no hydrogencarbonate, the discs never float, so they make no oxygen without carbon dioxide. Statement II is correct.
- III: the time falls from <InlineMath math="12" /> to <InlineMath math="6" /> minutes, so the rate only doubles, although the light is four times as bright. Something else, such as carbon dioxide, is starting to limit it. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the rate is <InlineMath math="\tfrac16" /> per minute at <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="\tfrac1{30}" /> at <InlineMath math="40\text{ cm}" />, a ratio of <InlineMath math="5" />. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I, II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only rejects IV, **although <InlineMath math="30" /> minutes against <InlineMath math="6" /> is a fivefold rate**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **assumes the rate follows the light exactly**.
- I, III and IV only rejects II and **ignores the control in plain water**.

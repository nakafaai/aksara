# Subtract the travel time along the axons to find the time at the synapse

Along an axon an impulse spreads **both ways** from where it starts. At a synapse it crosses **one way only**, because only the ending of the first neurone releases neurotransmitter, and only the next neurone has receptors for it. Crossing takes time, because the chemical must diffuse across the gap.

- I: in both experiments impulses reach both ends of the stimulated axon, so they spread both ways along it. Statement I is correct.
- II: stimulating Q gives no impulse in P at all, not a slower one. Neurotransmitter is released only by P's ending, so the synapse passes impulses only from P to Q. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the <InlineMath math="60\text{ mm}" /> of axon take <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ ms}" /> at <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> per second, so <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ ms}" /> of the <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ ms}" /> is spent at the synapse. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the impulse is carried across by a neurotransmitter that diffuses over the gap and binds to receptors on Q. The delay comes from this chemical step. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only accepts II, **although stimulating Q produced nothing in P**.
- III and IV only rejects I and **turns a chemical synapse into an electric jump**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts II and IV, **both of which ignore the one-way chemical crossing**.

# Weigh the extra tomatoes against the cost of each step

Extra carbon dioxide only pays where **carbon dioxide is the limiting factor**. Turn each step's extra yield into money and compare it with the RM<InlineMath math="3" /> it costs. A rise in kilograms must also be turned into a percentage of the starting yield.

- I: the yield rises by <InlineMath math="12\text{ kg}" /> on <InlineMath math="40\text{ kg}" />, which is <InlineMath math="30\%" />, not <InlineMath math="12\%" />. The <InlineMath math="12" /> is the rise in kilograms. Statement I is wrong.
- II: in the dull season doubling carbon dioxide adds only <InlineMath math="2\text{ kg}" />, while in bright light it adds <InlineMath math="12\text{ kg}" />. With little light, light is the limiting factor. Statement II is correct.
- III: from <InlineMath math="800" /> to <InlineMath math="1200" /> ppm the extra <InlineMath math="2\text{ kg}" /> earns RM<InlineMath math="4" /> against a cost of RM<InlineMath math="3" />, a profit of RM<InlineMath math="1" />. The step from <InlineMath math="400" /> to <InlineMath math="800" /> ppm earns RM<InlineMath math="24" /> for the same RM<InlineMath math="3" />, so it is far more profitable. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: the curve flattens above <InlineMath math="800" /> ppm, so carbon dioxide no longer limits the rate, and something else, such as light or temperature, does. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only accepts I, **reading a rise of <InlineMath math="12\text{ kg}" /> as <InlineMath math="12\%" />**.
- II, III and IV only accepts III, **although the last step makes only RM<InlineMath math="1" /> of profit**.
- I and III only rejects both statements on limiting factors and **accepts both calculation traps**.

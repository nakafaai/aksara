# Total the loss over six months, then divide it by the rate of recovery

Bone is **living tissue** that is removed and rebuilt all the time, and load tips the balance towards building. Multiply each monthly loss by <InlineMath math="6" />, then divide by the monthly gain on Earth. For muscle, the share prevented is the loss avoided out of the loss without exercise.

- I: <InlineMath math="1.5\%" /> a month for <InlineMath math="6" /> months is about <InlineMath math="9\%" />, and at <InlineMath math="0.5\%" /> a month it takes <InlineMath math="18" /> months to win back, three times as long as the stay. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="0.5\%" /> a month for <InlineMath math="6" /> months is <InlineMath math="3\%" />, which takes <InlineMath math="6" /> months to regain. Statement II is correct.
- III: exercise cuts the muscle loss from <InlineMath math="20\%" /> to <InlineMath math="5\%" />, preventing <InlineMath math="15" /> of the <InlineMath math="20" /> percentage points, which is three quarters. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the food supplies normal calcium, and the same astronauts lose far less bone when they exercise. The cause is the missing load, not missing calcium. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and III only rejects II, **although <InlineMath math="3\%" /> at <InlineMath math="0.5\%" /> a month takes <InlineMath math="6" /> months**.
- II, III and IV only rejects I and **blames calcium that the food already supplies**.
- I, II, III and IV accepts IV, **ignoring that exercise alone cuts the loss**.

# The maltose left in a tube is only what amylase made minus what maltase took, so add back the maltose that became glucose

Each tube shows what is **left** after <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes, not what each enzyme did. Work back to the two rates:

- Every maltose that maltase breaks down gives **two** glucose, so maltose broken down <InlineMath math="= \tfrac{\text{glucose}}{2}" />.
- Amylase made the maltose still there **plus** the maltose already broken down.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{P}: \quad \text{maltase} = \tfrac{4}{2} = 2, &\quad \text{amylase} = 6+2 = 8\\
\text{Q}: \quad \text{maltase} = \tfrac{4}{2} = 2, &\quad \text{amylase} = 14+2 = 16\\
\text{R}: \quad \text{maltase} = \tfrac{8}{2} = 4, &\quad \text{amylase} = 4+4 = 8\\
\text{S}: \quad \text{maltase} = \tfrac{8}{2} = 4, &\quad \text{amylase} = 12+4 = 16
\end{aligned}" />

All values are mmol of maltose in <InlineMath math="10" /> minutes.

- I: maltase breaks down <InlineMath math="4" /> mmol in R against <InlineMath math="2" /> in P, twice as fast. Statement I is correct.
- II: amylase makes <InlineMath math="8" /> mmol in both P and R. Tube R simply has less maltose **left**, because more of it has turned into glucose. Statement II is correct.
- III: tube Q holds <InlineMath math="14" /> mmol of maltose against <InlineMath math="6" /> in P, yet maltase still breaks down only <InlineMath math="2" /> mmol. Extra substrate doesn't help, so its active sites are already all busy. Only extra maltase, in R, raises the rate. Maltase is limited by **its own amount**, not by maltose. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: in S, amylase makes <InlineMath math="16" /> and maltase breaks down <InlineMath math="4" />, so amylase is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{16}{4} = 4" /> times as fast, not twice. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only reads the unchanged glucose in Q as a shortage of maltose, **when Q has more than twice the maltose and still no extra glucose**, which shows that maltase is saturated.
- I, II and IV only takes the <InlineMath math="8" /> mmol of glucose in S as <InlineMath math="8" /> mmol of maltose broken down, **forgetting that each maltose gives two glucose**, so it finds <InlineMath math="\tfrac{16}{8} = 2" />.
- I only compares the maltose left in P and R, <InlineMath math="6" /> against <InlineMath math="4" />, and decides that extra maltase slowed amylase. **The maltose that maltase has already turned into glucose must be added back** before judging amylase.

# Place X first, list every order of the Y fragments, and keep the one that gives the double digest

Each fragment size tells you a gap between two cuts or between a cut and an end. You have to find the **order** of the gaps, and only the digest with both enzymes can decide it.

## Place the cut made by X

Enzyme X cuts once and gives <InlineMath math="3\text{ kb}" /> and <InlineMath math="7\text{ kb}" />. The probe puts end L on the <InlineMath math="3\text{ kb}" /> piece, so **X cuts <InlineMath math="3\text{ kb}" /> from end L**.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="5" /> and <InlineMath math="8\text{ kb}" /> **lays the Y pieces out in the order the table lists them**, <InlineMath math="5" />, <InlineMath math="3" />, <InlineMath math="2" />. With X at <InlineMath math="3\text{ kb}" /> that map gives <InlineMath math="3" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="2\text{ kb}" />, not the observed double digest. It is the true map read from end R.
- <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="7\text{ kb}" /> checks **only that a <InlineMath math="1\text{ kb}" /> piece appears**. Its double digest is <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="3\text{ kb}" />, with no <InlineMath math="5\text{ kb}" /> piece.
- <InlineMath math="5" /> and <InlineMath math="7\text{ kb}" /> checks **only that two <InlineMath math="2\text{ kb}" /> pieces appear**. Its double digest is <InlineMath math="3" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3\text{ kb}" />, with no <InlineMath math="1" /> or <InlineMath math="5\text{ kb}" /> piece.

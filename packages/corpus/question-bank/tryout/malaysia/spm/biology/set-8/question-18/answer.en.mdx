# Mass lost is volume times concentration, and the protein that matters is what the liver cannot replace

Every statement needs the **mass carried out per day**, not just the concentration. Convert the volume to <InlineMath math="\text{dm}^3" /> first, since the concentrations are per <InlineMath math="\text{dm}^3" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{fat lost, diet P} &= 1.5\times24 = 36\text{ g per day}\\
\text{fat lost, diets Q and R} &= 0.8\times3 = 2.4\text{ g per day}\\
\text{protein lost, diet P} &= 1.5\times30 = 45\text{ g per day}\\
\text{protein lost, diets Q and R} &= 0.8\times30 = 24\text{ g per day}
\end{aligned}" />

## Statement I

<BlockMath math="\frac{36}{80}\times100\% = 45\%,\qquad \frac{36-2.4}{36}\times100\% \approx 93\%" />

The drain removes <InlineMath math="45\%" /> of the fat eaten on diet P, and diet R cuts the daily fat loss by about <InlineMath math="93\%" />. **Both parts hold**, and even without the <InlineMath math="2.4\text{ g}" /> that drains on a fat-free diet the share is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{33.6}{80}=42\%" />. Statement I is correct.

## Statement II

The drain is not the whole story, because the liver puts <InlineMath math="15\text{ g}" /> back every day. What lowers the plasma protein is the **net** loss:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{net loss, diet P} &= 45-15 = 30\text{ g per day}\\
\text{net loss, diet R} &= 24-15 = 9\text{ g per day}
\end{aligned}" />

A fall from <InlineMath math="70" /> to <InlineMath math="60\text{ g per dm}^3" /> in <InlineMath math="3.0\text{ dm}^3" /> of plasma means losing

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="(70-60)\times3.0 = 30\text{ g of plasma protein}" />
<BlockMath math="\text{time on diet P} = \frac{30}{30} = 1\text{ day},\qquad \text{time on diet R} = \frac{30}{9} \approx 3.3\text{ days}" />
</MathContainer>

Diet R takes about <InlineMath math="3.3" /> days, which is more than <InlineMath math="3" /> days and <InlineMath math="3.3" /> times as long as diet P. Statement II is correct.

## Statements III and IV

Now read diet Q as the control. With no fat at all in the food, <InlineMath math="800\text{ cm}^3" /> of lymph still drains each day, carrying protein. That is the tissue fluid of the other organs, and of the gut between meals, returning to the blood. Diet R gives **exactly** the same volume and fat as diet Q, so its <InlineMath math="80\text{ g}" /> of medium-chain fat adds nothing to the lymph.

- III: on diet R the drain still removes <InlineMath math="24\text{ g}" /> of plasma protein a day, the very loss that statement II follows. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: diet R matches the fat-free diet in both volume and fat, so the medium-chain fat does not enter the lacteals. It passes into the blood capillaries of the villi instead. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I only **leaves out the liver's <InlineMath math="15\text{ g}" /> a day**. Then diet R loses <InlineMath math="24\text{ g}" /> a day and takes <InlineMath math="\tfrac{30}{24}=1.25" /> days, and diet P takes <InlineMath math="\tfrac{30}{45}\approx0.67" /> days, only <InlineMath math="1.9" /> times shorter, so II looks wrong.
- I, II and IV only applies the rule that **all absorbed fat enters the lacteals**, without checking diet R against the control, diet Q, which shows that medium-chain fat adds nothing to the lymph.
- I, II and III only treats the thoracic duct as carrying **only lymph from the intestine**, but tissue fluid from most of the body drains into it too, and it still carries <InlineMath math="24\text{ g}" /> of protein a day on diet R.

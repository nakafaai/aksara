*Wolbachia* is a bacterium that lives inside the cells of some *Aedes aegypti* mosquitoes. Mosquitoes that carry it transmit the dengue virus poorly. A mother that carries *Wolbachia* passes it to all her offspring, but a father never passes it on. Whether the eggs hatch depends on which parents carry the bacterium.

| Female | Male | Eggs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| carries *Wolbachia* | carries *Wolbachia* | hatch, and all the offspring carry *Wolbachia* |
| carries *Wolbachia* | no *Wolbachia* | hatch, and all the offspring carry *Wolbachia* |
| no *Wolbachia* | carries *Wolbachia* | do not hatch |
| no *Wolbachia* | no *Wolbachia* | hatch, and no offspring carry *Wolbachia* |

Mosquitoes carrying *Wolbachia* are released in a housing area until <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of the males and <InlineMath math="50\%" /> of the females carry it, and then the releases stop. Assume that each female mates once with a male chosen at random, that every female lays the same number of eggs, that every hatched egg grows into an adult, that carrying *Wolbachia* has no other effect, and that males and females carry it in equal shares in every generation.

Which of the following statements are correct?

I. In the next generation, about <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2}{3}" /> of the mosquitoes carry *Wolbachia*.

II. A male carrying *Wolbachia* has no effect on the next generation, because only mothers pass the bacterium on.

III. Two generations after the releases stop, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{6}{7}" />, about <InlineMath math="86\%" />, of the mosquitoes carry *Wolbachia*.

IV. Releasing only males carrying *Wolbachia* into a population with none would not make *Wolbachia* spread, but it would reduce the number of eggs that hatch.

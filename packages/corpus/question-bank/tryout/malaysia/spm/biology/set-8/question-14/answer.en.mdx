# Receptors on a grid: the spacing is a length, but the count per area goes with its square

The threshold tells you the **spacing** of the receptors in one direction. Call it <InlineMath math="d" />, in millimetres. A square of skin <InlineMath math="1\text{ cm}" /> across is <InlineMath math="10\text{ mm}" /> long and <InlineMath math="10\text{ mm}" /> wide, so it holds <InlineMath math="\tfrac{10}{d}" /> receptors along each side. So

<BlockMath math="\text{receptors per cm}^2 = \left(\frac{10}{d}\right)^2" />

Multiply by the area of each region to get its whole count.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{fingertip pad} &: \left(\tfrac{10}{2}\right)^2 = 25\text{ per cm}^2, & 25\times2 &= 50\\
\text{palm} &: \left(\tfrac{10}{10}\right)^2 = 1\text{ per cm}^2, & 1\times100 &= 100\\
\text{forearm} &: \left(\tfrac{10}{40}\right)^2 = 0.0625\text{ per cm}^2, & 0.0625\times400 &= 25\\
\text{back} &: \left(\tfrac{10}{50}\right)^2 = 0.04\text{ per cm}^2, & 0.04\times1500 &= 60
\end{aligned}" />

This is why the fingertips read fine textures and Braille. Many receptors, each with its own sensory neurone, crowd into a small area, so the brain receives detailed information from them.

- I: per <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^2" />, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{25}{0.04} = 625" />, which is <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac{50}{2}\right)^2" />. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="100" /> receptors in the palm against <InlineMath math="60" /> in the back. The back is <InlineMath math="15" /> times larger, but its receptors are <InlineMath math="25" /> times sparser. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="25" /> receptors in the whole forearm against <InlineMath math="50" /> in one fingertip pad. Statement III is correct.
- IV: carry the grid rule backwards. A threshold of <InlineMath math="3\text{ mm}" /> means a spacing of <InlineMath math="3\text{ mm}" />, so

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{receptors per cm}^2 &= \left(\tfrac{10}{3}\right)^2 \approx 11.1\\
\text{receptors left in the pad} &\approx 11.1\times2 = 22.2\\
\text{receptors lost} &\approx 50-22.2 = 27.8
\end{aligned}" />

The pad has lost about <InlineMath math="27.8" /> receptors, more than the <InlineMath math="25" /> in the whole forearm. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- IV only **forgets to square** the spacing. It counts receptors per centimetre as <InlineMath math="\tfrac{10}{d}" />, so the fingertip seems only <InlineMath math="25" /> times denser than the back, the back and the forearm seem to hold the most receptors, and the injured pad seems to lose only a few receptors against a forearm of <InlineMath math="100" />.
- I only judges the whole regions **by their area alone**, so the large back and forearm seem to hold more receptors than the palm and the fingertip.
- I, II, III and IV squares the spacing for I to III but then lets the count fall **in step with the threshold rise**, to <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2}{3}" /> of <InlineMath math="50" />, so the pad seems to lose only about <InlineMath math="16.7" /> receptors instead of its square, <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^2" />.

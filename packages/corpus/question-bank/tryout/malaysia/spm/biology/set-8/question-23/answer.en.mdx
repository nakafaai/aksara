# The tracers show which plant is joined to the lime tree's vessels, and the nitrogen in a leaf sets how long it takes to build

Two tracers tell the vessels apart. Radioactive carbon from a leaf travels as sugar in the **phloem**, and the red dye travels with water in the **xylem**. So follow each tracer to see what each plant on the branches takes from the lime tree.

- Experiment <InlineMath math="2" />: the dye reaches the mistletoe but not the fern, so **only the mistletoe is joined to the xylem**.
- Experiment <InlineMath math="1" />: the lime tree's labelled sugar reaches neither plant, so neither taps the phloem.
- Experiment <InlineMath math="3" />: the mistletoe fixes its own carbon dioxide. It is a partial parasite that photosynthesises, while the fern is an epiphyte that only uses the tree to reach the light.

## Nitrogen for new leaves

The nitrogen a leaf receives each day is the water it transpires times the nitrogen in each <InlineMath math="\text{dm}^3" /> of sap. The time to gather enough for a new square metre of leaf is the nitrogen that leaf holds divided by that daily supply. The two leaves **hold different amounts of nitrogen**, so work out each one:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{mistletoe: } 3.0\times70 &= 210\text{ mg per day}, & \frac{2{,}100\text{ mg}}{210\text{ mg per day}} &= 10\text{ days}\\
\text{lime: } 1.0\times70 &= 70\text{ mg per day}, & \frac{1{,}400\text{ mg}}{70\text{ mg per day}} &= 20\text{ days}
\end{aligned}" />

The mistletoe transpires three times as fast, but each square metre of its leaf needs one and a half times as much nitrogen, so it is only twice as fast: <InlineMath math="\tfrac{10}{20}=\tfrac{1}{2}" />.

- I: the dye shows a xylem link, and experiments <InlineMath math="1" /> and <InlineMath math="3" /> show no sugar passes from the lime tree to the mistletoe. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="10" /> days against <InlineMath math="20" /> days is half the time. Statement II is correct.
- III: no dye reached the fern, so it has **no link to the xylem**. Its roots take water and mineral salts from rainwater and rotting leaves caught round them. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: <InlineMath math="7" /> days would hold only if mistletoe leaves held as little nitrogen as lime leaves. They hold <InlineMath math="2.1\text{ g per m}^2" />, so the time is <InlineMath math="10" /> days. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and IV only takes the transpiration ratio of three as the whole answer, **ignoring that a mistletoe leaf holds more nitrogen** than a lime leaf.
- I, II and III only treats every plant growing on a tree as a parasite, **overlooking that no dye reached the fern**.
- II only assumes every parasite on a tree takes its sugar too, **ignoring that no labelled sugar reached the mistletoe** in experiment <InlineMath math="1" />.

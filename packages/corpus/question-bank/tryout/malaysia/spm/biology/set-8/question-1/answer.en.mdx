# Two things change between groups P and S, so split the gap by comparing groups that differ in one thing only

Groups of different sizes can't be compared by their totals, so first turn every total into a **mean score per student**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{P}: \quad \tfrac{456}{12} &= 38\\
\text{Q}: \quad \tfrac{340}{10} &= 34\\
\text{R}: \quad \tfrac{248}{8} &= 31\\
\text{S}: \quad \tfrac{270}{10} &= 27
\end{aligned}" />

Two variables are mixed in this design: what the students **actually drink** and what they **are told** they drink. To see the effect of one, keep the other fixed.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{glucose, told glucose}: \quad \text{P}-\text{Q} &= 38-34 = 4\\
\text{glucose, told sweetener}: \quad \text{R}-\text{S} &= 31-27 = 4\\
\text{being told glucose, drink glucose}: \quad \text{P}-\text{R} &= 38-31 = 7\\
\text{being told glucose, drink sweetener}: \quad \text{Q}-\text{S} &= 34-27 = 7\\
\text{P}-\text{S} &= 38-27 = 11 = 4+7
\end{aligned}" />

- I: glucose adds <InlineMath math="4" /> marks in both pairs. The gap between P and S is <InlineMath math="11" /> marks, and half of it is <InlineMath math="5.5" />, so <InlineMath math="4" /> is less than half. Statement I is correct.
- II: group P drinks glucose **and** is told glucose, while group S drinks sweetener **and** is told sweetener. Two variables change at once, and <InlineMath math="7" /> of the <InlineMath math="11" /> marks come from what the students were told. Statement II is wrong.
- III: group R has only <InlineMath math="8" /> students, so its total is small. Its mean, <InlineMath math="31" />, is higher than the mean of group S, <InlineMath math="27" />. Glucose raises the score here too. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: <InlineMath math="\text{P}-\text{R} = 7" /> for students who drink glucose and <InlineMath math="\text{Q}-\text{S} = 7" /> for students who drink sweetener. Statement IV is correct.

So statements I and IV are correct, and the results do support the hypothesis: glucose raises the mean score by <InlineMath math="4" /> marks whatever the students believe.

# Why the other options are wrong

- II and IV only takes groups P and S as a fair test because both were told the truth, and so gives all <InlineMath math="11" /> marks to glucose. **Being told glucose is a second variable that also differs between P and S**.
- I only finds the effect of being told glucose from groups P and Q, which gives <InlineMath math="4" />. **P and Q are told the same thing and differ only in the drink**, so that gap is the glucose effect.
- I, III and IV only compares the totals of groups R and S, <InlineMath math="248" /> against <InlineMath math="270" />, **without dividing by the different numbers of students**.

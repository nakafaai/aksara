# Volume follows the cube of the diameter, and a fair share counts only the grains that could still grow

Each statement needs its own base. Volume needs the **cube** of the diameter, the mannitol comparison needs the sucrose solution with the same water potential, and a share of grains needs the grains that were still whole.

## Statement I

A sphere's volume grows with the **cube of its diameter**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{distilled water:}\quad 1.10^3 &\approx 1.331, \text{ a rise of about } 33\%\\
10\%\text{ sucrose:}\quad 1.02^3 &\approx 1.061, \text{ a rise of about } 6.1\%\\
\frac{33.1}{6.1} &\approx 5.4
\end{aligned}" />

A rise of <InlineMath math="33\%" /> is about a third, and it is more than five times the <InlineMath math="6.1\%" /> rise in <InlineMath math="10\%" /> sucrose. Statement I is correct.

## Statement II

M has the same water potential as <InlineMath math="12\%" /> sucrose, so compare M with the tube length expected at <InlineMath math="12\%" />, which lies <InlineMath math="0.4" /> of the way from <InlineMath math="480" /> to <InlineMath math="420\,\mu\text{m}" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{expected length at }12\% &= 480+0.4\times(420-480) = 456\,\mu\text{m}\\
\text{share reached in M} &= \frac{120}{456}\approx0.26\\
\text{share that needs sucrose} &= \frac{456-120}{456}\approx0.74
\end{aligned}" />

The water balance is the same in M and in <InlineMath math="12\%" /> sucrose, and almost as many grains germinate. The difference left is that the grains **can use sucrose**, and without it the tubes reach only about a quarter of their length. Statement II is correct.

## Statement III

A burst grain can never grow a tube, so the fair base is the **grains that stayed whole**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{distilled water:}\quad \frac{2}{50-30} &= \frac{2}{20} = 10\%\\
5\%\text{ sucrose:}\quad \frac{30}{50-6} &= \frac{30}{44} \approx 68\%\\
\frac{68}{10} &\approx 6.8
\end{aligned}" />

The share is about <InlineMath math="6.8" /> times as high, not <InlineMath math="15" /> times. Statement III is wrong.

## Statement IV

In <InlineMath math="20\%" /> sucrose the grains shrink by <InlineMath math="6\%" />, so water moves **out of** them, not in. They are short of the water a growing tube needs. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and III only divides by **all <InlineMath math="50" /> grains**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{30}{50}=60\%" /> against <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2}{50}=4\%" />, which gives <InlineMath math="15" /> times. The <InlineMath math="30" /> burst grains in distilled water never had a chance to grow a tube.
- II only treats volume like diameter, a <InlineMath math="10\%" /> rise against a <InlineMath math="2\%" /> rise, which is only <InlineMath math="5" /> times, so it rejects I.
- I, II and IV gets the **direction of osmosis** wrong: a grain that shrinks is losing water.

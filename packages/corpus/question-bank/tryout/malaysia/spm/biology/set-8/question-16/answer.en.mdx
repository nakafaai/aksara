# Stiffness follows the mineral salts in each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" />, so the bone without vitamin D needs both its mineral per gram and its density

The stem tells you what to compare: the mass of mineral salts in each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" /> of bone. No single piece of the chicken fed without vitamin D gives you that. Piece S tells you what its bone is made of, and piece R tells you how much bone fits in each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" />. You join the two, do the same for the normal bone, then compare.

**Step 1: mineral salts in each gram of the bone without vitamin D**. Acid dissolves the mineral salts and leaves the organic matter, so what is left of S is its collagen:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{mineral salts in S} &= 3.0-1.56 = 1.44\text{ g}\\
\text{per gram} &= \frac{1.44}{3.0} = 0.48\text{ g}
\end{aligned}" />

**Step 2: density of the bone without vitamin D**. R comes from the same chicken, so it has the same make-up as S:

<BlockMath math="\text{density} = \frac{3.5}{2.0} = 1.75\text{ g per cm}^3" />

**Step 3: mineral salts in each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" /> of that bone**. Each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" /> holds <InlineMath math="1.75\text{ g}" /> of bone, and each gram of it holds <InlineMath math="0.48\text{ g}" /> of mineral salts:

<BlockMath math="0.48\times1.75 = 0.84\text{ g per cm}^3" />

**Step 4: mineral salts in each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" /> of normal bone**. Heating P leaves only the mineral salts, and P filled <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ cm}^3" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{per cm}^3 &= \frac{2.8}{2.5} = 1.12\text{ g}\\
\text{check with Q}: \quad 2.8+2.2 &= 5.0\text{ g, the whole piece}
\end{aligned}" />

**Step 5: compare the two**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{0.84}{1.12} = 0.75 = 75\%" />

**Step 6: what that does to the bone**. Read P and Q as a pair. P keeps only the mineral salts and crumbles, so the mineral salts make bone **hard and rigid**. Q keeps only the collagen and bends into a loop, so collagen makes bone **flexible**. Without vitamin D, the chicken can't absorb and lay down enough calcium, so each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" /> of its bone holds less hard mineral, while its collagen is still there. The bone turns soft and bends under the body's weight. This is rickets.

So each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" /> of the bone holds <InlineMath math="75\%" /> of the mineral salts in each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" /> of normal bone, and the bone is softer and bends under the chicken's weight.

# Why the other options are wrong

- <InlineMath math="86\%" /> compares the mineral salts **per gram**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.48}{0.56} \approx 0.86" />. The bone without vitamin D is also less dense, <InlineMath math="1.75" /> against <InlineMath math="\tfrac{5.0}{2.5} = 2.0\text{ g per cm}^3" />, so each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" /> holds less bone, and the stem asks for each <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^3" />.
- <InlineMath math="64\%" /> divides the <InlineMath math="1.44\text{ g}" /> of mineral salts in S by the volume of R, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1.44}{2.0} = 0.72" />, then <InlineMath math="\tfrac{0.72}{1.12} \approx 0.64" />. **S and R are pieces of different size**: S fills only <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3.0}{1.75} \approx 1.71\text{ cm}^3" />, and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1.44}{1.71} \approx 0.84" /> again.
- <InlineMath math="75\%" /> with crumbling like P links less mineral with brittleness, **but P crumbled because its collagen was burnt away**. Less mineral with the collagen still there makes bone bend, not crumble.

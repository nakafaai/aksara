# Each group of trees raises the mud at its own rate, so add the stages one at a time

Each stage of this succession lasts until the mud reaches the bottom of the next group's range. The rate of rise changes at every stage, so **time each stage with its own rate** and add the times.

## Without a change in sea level

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\textit{Avicennia}\text{ and }\textit{Sonneratia}: \frac{60-30}{1.5} &= 20\text{ years}\\
\textit{Rhizophora}: \frac{120-60}{1.0} &= 60\text{ years, so }\textit{Bruguiera}\text{ arrives in year }20+60=80\\
\textit{Bruguiera}: \frac{180-120}{0.5} &= 120\text{ years, so inland forest arrives in year }80+120=200
\end{aligned}" />

The pioneers trap silt fastest, so they **raise the mud out of their own range soonest** and hold the plot for the shortest time.

## With the sea level rising

If mean low tide rises <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ cm}" /> a year, the height of the mud above it rises <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ cm}" /> a year more slowly in every stage:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{pioneers: } 1.5-0.5 = 1.0\text{ cm per year}, &\quad \frac{60-30}{1.0} = 30\text{ years}\\
\textit{Rhizophora}: 1.0-0.5 = 0.5\text{ cm per year}, &\quad \frac{120-60}{0.5} = 120\text{ years, so }\textit{Bruguiera}\text{ arrives in year }150\\
\textit{Bruguiera}: 0.5-0.5 = 0\text{ cm per year}, &\quad \text{the mud stays at }120\text{ cm}
\end{aligned}" />

Under *Bruguiera*, the mud now rises exactly as fast as the sea, so it **never climbs past <InlineMath math="120\text{ cm}" />** and the plot never reaches the inland forest's <InlineMath math="180\text{ cm}" />.

- I: <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> at <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ cm}" /> a year takes <InlineMath math="20" /> years. Statement I is correct.
- II: <InlineMath math="20+60=80" /> years. Statement II is correct.
- III: <InlineMath math="30+120=150" /> years to *Bruguiera*, then no further rise. Statement III is correct.
- IV: the pioneers stay <InlineMath math="20" /> years, the shortest of the three, precisely because they raise the mud fastest. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I, II and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I only keeps the pioneers' rate of <InlineMath math="1.5\text{ cm}" /> a year for the whole climb, **ignoring that each group raises the mud at its own rate**. It gets <InlineMath math="\tfrac{120-30}{1.5}=60" /> years for II and <InlineMath math="\tfrac{120-30}{1.0}=90" /> years for III.
- I, II and IV only thinks the fastest silt trappers stay longest, **when fast trapping lifts the mud out of their range soonest**.
- I and II only takes the sea-level rise away from the pioneers' rate only, gets <InlineMath math="30+60=90" /> years, and so **forgets that the rise slows every stage**.

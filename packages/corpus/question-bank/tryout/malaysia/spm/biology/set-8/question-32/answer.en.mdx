# A plant breeds true only if it is homozygous for both genes, and a tall plant may still hide a dwarf allele

With incomplete dominance, the colour you see tells you the colour genotype: red is <InlineMath math="RR" />, pink is <InlineMath math="RW" />, white is <InlineMath math="WW" />. Height is different. A tall plant can be <InlineMath math="TT" /> or <InlineMath math="Tt" />, so **the phenotype does not show the genotype**. Keep that difference in mind for every statement.

## Statement I

Group the counts by height and by colour:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{tall: } & 148:304:151\approx1:2:1, \qquad \text{dwarf: } 49:98:50\approx1:2:1\\
\text{red: } & 148:49\approx3:1,\qquad \text{pink: } 304:98\approx3:1,\qquad \text{white: } 151:50\approx3:1
\end{aligned}" />

The colour ratio **does not depend on height**, and the height ratio **does not depend on colour**. The <InlineMath math="800" /> plants follow <InlineMath math="3:6:3:1:2:1" />, which is <InlineMath math="(1:2:1)\times(3:1)" />. Statement I is correct.

## Statement II

A plant gives offspring that all match it only if it is **homozygous for both genes**. Pink plants are always <InlineMath math="RW" />, so they never breed true. Every dwarf plant is <InlineMath math="tt" />, so red dwarf and white dwarf plants breed true. Among tall plants, <InlineMath math="TT:Tt=1:2" />, so only one third of the red tall and white tall plants breed true:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{red dwarf} + \text{white dwarf} &= 49+50 = 99\\
\tfrac{1}{3}\times(\text{red tall} + \text{white tall}) &= \tfrac{1}{3}\times(148+151) \approx 100\\
\text{total} &\approx 199 \approx 200
\end{aligned}" />

This matches the genotypes <InlineMath math="RRTT" />, <InlineMath math="WWTT" />, <InlineMath math="RRtt" /> and <InlineMath math="WWtt" />, each <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{16}" /> of the plants, so <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4}{16}\times800=200" />. Statement II is correct.

## Statement III

The first-generation plant is <InlineMath math="RWTt" /> and the white dwarf plant is <InlineMath math="WWtt" />. Every offspring gets <InlineMath math="W" /> from the white parent, so it is <InlineMath math="RW" /> (pink) or <InlineMath math="WW" /> (white), **never red**. The offspring are pink tall, pink dwarf, white tall and white dwarf in the ratio <InlineMath math="1:1:1:1" />. Statement III is wrong.

## Statement IV

Among red tall plants, one third are <InlineMath math="RRTT" /> and two thirds are <InlineMath math="RRTt" />. Only the <InlineMath math="RRTt" /> plants give dwarf offspring, one quarter of their offspring:

<BlockMath math="P(\text{dwarf}) = \tfrac{1}{3}\times0+\tfrac{2}{3}\times\tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{1}{6}" />

That is **one sixth, not one quarter**. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and II are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I only takes **every red, white or dwarf plant as true-breeding**, because their colour or height looks pure: <InlineMath math="148+151+49+50\approx400" />. That forgets that two thirds of the red tall and white tall plants are <InlineMath math="Tt" />.
- I, II and IV only treats **every red tall plant as <InlineMath math="RRTt" />**, so it expects a <InlineMath math="3:1" /> ratio on selfing. One third of them are <InlineMath math="RRTT" /> and give no dwarf offspring.
- I, II and III only reads the cross with the white dwarf plant like a test cross with full dominance, which gives four classes in equal numbers, **forgetting that the white parent can give only <InlineMath math="W" />**, so no offspring can be red.

# A dwarf that grows with added gibberellin lacks the hormone, and one that does not cannot respond

Adding a hormone separates two kinds of short plant. If the plant **makes too little gibberellin**, a dose replaces what is missing and the stem shoots up. If the plant **cannot respond** to gibberellin, extra hormone changes nothing.

Without its own gibberellin, the tall plant is as short as a dwarf, and the added hormone restores it. So the tall plant **already makes enough gibberellin** to grow fully, which is why a little more adds almost nothing.

## The cross between the two dwarfs

The dose test now fixes each dwarf's genotype. D1 grows with gibberellic acid, so its receptor works and its fault is in making the hormone: D1 is <InlineMath math="aaBB" />. D2 ignores even <InlineMath math="10\,\mu\text{g}" />, so its receptor is faulty: D2 is <InlineMath math="AAbb" />. Their <InlineMath math="F_1" /> is <InlineMath math="AaBb" />, which makes gibberellin and responds to it, so it is tall.

Selfing <InlineMath math="AaBb" /> gives the four classes <InlineMath math="A\_B\_" />, <InlineMath math="aaB\_" />, <InlineMath math="A\_bb" /> and <InlineMath math="aabb" /> in the ratio <InlineMath math="9:3:3:1" />. - I: D2 stays near <InlineMath math="11\text{ cm}" /> even with <InlineMath math="10\,\mu\text{g}" />. A plant short of gibberellin would grow when given it, so D2 **cannot respond** to the hormone. Statement I is wrong.
- II: D1 grows from <InlineMath math="10" /> to <InlineMath math="41\text{ cm}" /> as the dose rises, so it responds well and was short only because it made too little. Statement II is correct.
- III: the blocker test shows the tall stem shrinks to <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" /> without gibberellin, so the hormone is essential. The small response only means its own supply is already enough. Statement III is wrong.
- IV: only the <InlineMath math="aaB\_" /> dwarfs respond, which is <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{16}" /> of the <InlineMath math="F_2" />. Statement IV is correct.

So statements II and IV are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I, II and IV only assumes **every dwarf lacks gibberellin**, although D2 ignores even a large dose.
- II, III and IV only reads the tall variety's flat line as no role for gibberellin and **ignores the blocker test**.
- II only counts the <InlineMath math="aabb" /> plants as dwarfs that respond, because they lack gibberellin, and gets <InlineMath math="\tfrac{4}{16}=\tfrac{1}{4}" />, **forgetting they have no working receptor**.

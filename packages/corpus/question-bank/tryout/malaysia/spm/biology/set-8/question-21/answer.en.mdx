# Two DCPIP molecules are reduced for every oxygen molecule, and the tubes tell you what limits the rate

DCPIP stands in for NADP, so it catches the hydrogen that the **light-dependent reaction** strips from water. Tubes S and T stay blue, so that reaction needs light and chloroplast proteins that boiling destroys. Tubes P and Q decolourise in the same <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes with and without hydrogencarbonate, so it **doesn't need carbon dioxide**. Carbon dioxide is only used later, in the light-independent reaction.

## From DCPIP to oxygen

In tube P, all <InlineMath math="0.080\,\mu\text{mol}" /> of DCPIP is reduced in <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes. Photolysis of one oxygen molecule frees **four** hydrogen ions and four electrons, and each DCPIP molecule takes **two** of each. So one oxygen molecule is released for every two DCPIP molecules reduced:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{DCPIP reduced} &= \frac{0.080\,\mu\text{mol}}{4\text{ min}} = 0.020\,\mu\text{mol per min}\\
\text{oxygen released} &= \frac{0.020}{2} = 0.010\,\mu\text{mol per min}
\end{aligned}" />

## From oxygen to glucose

Scale the oxygen to one milligram of chlorophyll and one hour. In a leaf, the equation of photosynthesis, <InlineMath math="6\text{CO}_2+12\text{H}_2\text{O}\rightarrow\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6+6\text{O}_2+6\text{H}_2\text{O}" />, releases **six oxygen molecules for each glucose molecule**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\text{oxygen per mg chlorophyll} &= \frac{0.010}{0.040}\times60 = 15\,\mu\text{mol per hour}\\
\text{glucose} &= \frac{15}{6} = 2.5\,\mu\text{mol per hour}\\
\text{mass of glucose} &= 2.5\times180 = 450\,\mu\text{g} = 0.45\text{ mg per hour}
\end{aligned}" />

Tube R gets only half the light of tube P, yet its bar is just as tall. So at the brightness of tube P, **light is no longer the limiting factor**, and more light can't speed the reaction up.

- I: P and Q match with and without carbon dioxide, and S and T, without light or with boiled chloroplasts, stay blue. Statement I is correct.
- II: oxygen is released at <InlineMath math="0.010\,\mu\text{mol}" /> per minute, half the DCPIP rate, not <InlineMath math="0.020" />. Statement II is wrong.
- III: the chain gives <InlineMath math="0.45\text{ mg}" /> of glucose per <InlineMath math="\text{mg}" /> of chlorophyll every hour. Statement III is correct.
- IV: halving the light in tube R left the time at <InlineMath math="4" /> minutes, so doubling it won't cut the time to <InlineMath math="2" /> minutes. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- I and II only counts **one oxygen molecule for each DCPIP molecule**. That doubles the oxygen to <InlineMath math="0.020\,\mu\text{mol}" /> per minute and the glucose to <InlineMath math="0.90\text{ mg}" />, so it rejects III.
- I, III and IV only assumes the rate always follows the light, **ignoring tube R**, where half the light gave the same time.
- I only forgets that **six oxygen molecules come with each glucose molecule**, gets <InlineMath math="15\times180\,\mu\text{g}=2.7\text{ mg}" />, and so rejects III.

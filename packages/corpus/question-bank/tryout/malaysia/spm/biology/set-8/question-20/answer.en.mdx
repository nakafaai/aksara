# A root lengthens by adding fully elongated cells, and the same flow of cells passes every point of the file

The regions keep the same length, so every extra millimetre of root is made of cells that have **finished** elongating, each <InlineMath math="200\,\mu\text{m}" /> long. In one hour the file gains <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm} = 2000\,\mu\text{m}" /> of such cells:

<BlockMath math="\frac{2000\,\mu\text{m}}{200\,\mu\text{m}} = 10\text{ cells per hour}" />

The regions also keep the same number of cells, and no cell divides outside the meristem, so this same flow of <InlineMath math="10" /> cells per hour leaves the meristem and passes every point along the file.

## Statements II and III

The meristem must add those <InlineMath math="10" /> cells per hour. It holds

<BlockMath math="\frac{1500\,\mu\text{m}}{15\,\mu\text{m}} = 100\text{ cells}" />

<InlineMath math="100" /> cells that make <InlineMath math="10" /> new cells per hour each divide, on average, **once every <InlineMath math="\tfrac{100}{10} = 10" /> hours**. Statement III is correct, and statement II, with its <InlineMath math="133" /> cells per hour, is wrong.

## Statement I

Count the cells between <InlineMath math="1.5" /> and <InlineMath math="9\text{ mm}" />, region by region:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1.5\text{ to }3\text{ mm:}\quad \frac{1500}{20} &= 75\text{ cells}\\
3\text{ to }6\text{ mm:}\quad \frac{3000}{50} &= 60\text{ cells}\\
6\text{ to }9\text{ mm:}\quad \frac{3000}{120} &= 25\text{ cells}
\end{aligned}" />

That is <InlineMath math="75+60+25 = 160" /> cells in the zone of elongation. Cells enter and leave it at <InlineMath math="10" /> per hour, so **each cell stays in it** for

<BlockMath math="\frac{160\text{ cells}}{10\text{ cells per hour}} = 16\text{ hours}" />

The short cells near the meristem move away from the tip slowly, and only the finished cells move away from it at the full <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm}" /> per hour. Statement I is correct.

## Statement IV

The same <InlineMath math="10" /> cells an hour, each only <InlineMath math="100\,\mu\text{m}" /> long when finished, add <InlineMath math="10\times100 = 1000\,\mu\text{m} = 1.0\text{ mm}" /> per hour. Most of the growth in length comes from **cell elongation**, not from division alone. Statement IV is wrong.

So statements I and III are correct.

# Why the other options are wrong

- III only divides the <InlineMath math="7.5\text{ mm}" /> of the zone by the **root's growth rate**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{7.5}{2.0}\approx3.8" /> hours, as if every cell moved away from the tip at <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ mm}" /> per hour. Only finished cells do. The cell count gives <InlineMath math="16" /> hours.
- I and II only divides the growth by the **meristem cell length**, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{2000}{15}\approx133" />, as if new cells added length before they elongate. Then each meristem cell would divide every <InlineMath math="\tfrac{100}{133}\approx0.75" /> hours, so III looks wrong too.
- I, III and IV only thinks a root grows **by cell division alone**, so halving the final cell length seems to change nothing.

# Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 8, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how PT NAKAFA TEKNO KREATIF ("Nakafa," "we," "us," or "our") collects, uses, and discloses Personal Data when you access or use our websites, applications, and related services, including Nakafa School (collectively, the "Services").

Your use of the Services is at all times subject to our [Terms of Service](/en/terms-of-service), which incorporates this Privacy Policy.

## What this Privacy Policy covers

This Privacy Policy covers how we treat Personal Data that we gather when you access or use the Services.

When a School uses Nakafa School, the School decides how the Personal Data of its students, staff, and Guardians is processed there, and Nakafa processes that data on the School's behalf. The Nakafa School section below explains how that processing works.

This Privacy Policy does not cover the practices of companies we do not own or control, or people we do not manage, including third-party services you may access through the Services. For information about third-party services' data practices, please refer to their respective privacy policies linked throughout this document.

## Key definitions

- Personal Data means any information that identifies or relates to a particular individual. Personal Data may also be referred to as "personally identifiable information" or "personal information" under applicable privacy laws.
- User Content means information you submit through the Services, such as chat messages, files you attach to a chat, comments, and other text you provide.
- Service Providers means vendors that process Personal Data for us to provide a defined part of the Services. Their role and legal obligations depend on the service and applicable law.
- School means a school, foundation, or other educational institution that uses Nakafa School under a written agreement with Nakafa.
- School Data means Personal Data that Nakafa processes for a School in Nakafa School, such as data about its students, staff, and Guardians.
- Guardian means a parent or legal guardian of a student.

## How third parties process data

Nakafa uses the third-party services named in this Policy to run the Services. Each service processes Personal Data under its own terms and privacy policy, which this Policy links.

Polar acts as the Merchant of Record and authorized reseller for checkout transactions. Polar collects and processes checkout data under its own terms and privacy policy, while Nakafa receives the customer, checkout, and subscription information needed to provide and manage access to the purchased Service. Nakafa also sends Polar your name, email address, and account identifiers when you create an account and whenever your profile details change, so that Polar holds the customer record used at checkout.

For School Data, the School is the controller and Nakafa is its processor. The Service Providers named in this Policy process School Data for Nakafa as sub-processors under the data processing agreement between Nakafa and the School.

We limit disclosures to the data needed for the stated purpose. The provider links below identify the terms that govern their processing and may be updated by those providers.

## AI processing

Nakafa routes Nina's language-model requests through the Convex AI gateway to Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite or Gemini 3.7 Flash, and to no other model. Research features send search queries derived from your request, and the addresses of pages they read, to Firecrawl, which returns search results and page content.

Convex routes these requests as its [AI gateway page](https://www.convex.dev/ai-gateway), [Privacy Policy](https://www.convex.dev/legal/privacy/v2024-09-24), and [list of subprocessors](https://www.convex.dev/legal/subprocessors) describe, and Google processes them under the [Gemini API Additional Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) or the [Google Cloud Terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms).

### Nina's memory, files, and context

You can attach images, PDF files, and plain-text files of up to 8 MiB each to a message for Nina. Nakafa stores an attached file in Convex and sends it to the AI route with your message. For long conversations, Nina keeps a summary of the earlier messages, which a model on the same AI routes writes and Nakafa stores with the conversation.

Nina memory is off until you turn it on in your settings. While it is on, a model on the same AI routes reads your newest message after each reply and can save up to 30 short facts that you state about yourself, such as your grade, target exams, goals, and topics you find hard. Nina is instructed not to save health, religion, ethnicity, political views, precise location, contact details, passwords, or financial details. You can see and delete each fact in your settings. Deleting a chat also deletes the facts that came from it, and turning memory off deletes every fact.

Nina's context also includes the page you are reading when you ask and these account facts: your learning focus and region from onboarding, your role and preferred curriculum, your preferred try-out country, and the result of your latest finished try-out. Nakafa does not add your name or email address to that context.

### Answer scoring with Jev

Some try-out and School assessment answers need a structured scoring decision, for example which rubric level an essay reaches, how likely an answer is to contain each key point, or which category an answer belongs to. For these decisions, Nakafa sends the answer text and the material needed to score it, such as the question, its rubric, key points, or a reference answer, through Vercel AI Gateway to Jev, an AI scoring model from TypeSafe AI, Inc. Nakafa never includes your name, email address, account identifiers, or other profile details in these requests. Do not write personal details in a free-text answer.

Nakafa lets the Gateway run Jev only through two inference providers, TypeSafe and DigitalOcean. Each request goes to one of them, and if that provider fails, the Gateway can retry the request with the other. Nakafa does not set up a fallback that would send these requests to another model.

TypeSafe and DigitalOcean process these requests under their own terms, set out in the [TypeSafe Privacy Policy](https://typesafe.ai/legal/privacy-policy) and the [DigitalOcean Privacy Policy](https://www.digitalocean.com/legal/privacy-policy). Jev returns only scores and probabilities, which Nakafa stores with your attempt. Scores in try-outs outside Nakafa School are calculated automatically, and a score that Jev decides is marked as automatic. If you think a score is wrong, you can contact us to question it. In Nakafa School, a Jev score for an exam is a suggestion that a teacher reviews before any grade is released. For practice work, a School can let Jev scores be released automatically.

## Cookies, local storage, and product analytics

We separate storage that is necessary to operate the Services from optional product analytics.

- Strictly necessary storage supports functions such as authentication, security, language and theme preferences, the unsent message you are writing to Nina, and remembering your privacy choice. It is not used for product analytics.
- When you are signed in or have selected "Allow" in the privacy controls, the Services record a view in Convex once a lesson, article, or other content page has stayed visible for at least three seconds. If you are signed out and have not selected "Allow", the Services do not record the view and store nothing in your browser for it. A view record holds the page, its language and section, and your account if you are signed in. After you select "Allow", it also holds a random device identifier that the Services store in your browser's local storage. The identifier stays only while your consent is in effect, so declining or withdrawing consent removes it. Nakafa uses these records to count a view once per account or device, rank popular content, and show your recently viewed content. The identifier is not sent to PostHog or used for advertising.
- Every visit loads the analytics library and counts one pageview per viewed page without an account, cookies, or browser storage: the page path without query strings or fragments. Your IP address reaches our EU-hosted PostHog endpoint through our first-party proxy and is replaced there with a daily rotating one-way hash that can link views from the same network and browser during that day. We process these counts on the basis of our legitimate interests in operating, securing, and measuring the Services. Identified analytics with full page addresses and approximate location starts only after you select "Allow" in the privacy controls.
- We do not use PostHog session replay, autocapture, heatmaps, surveys, product tours, browser performance capture, or automatic page-view and page-leave capture. Admitted analytics events keep full page addresses and approximate location, restricted to reviewed event contracts; pseudonymous counts stay minimized as described above.
- Rejecting analytics does not limit core features. You can later accept or withdraw consent through the privacy controls. Withdrawing stops identified collection and clears the browser analytics state controlled by Nakafa; visits continue to be counted pseudonymously as described above.
- An anonymous browser choice does not silently authorize identified analytics after sign-in. Identified analytics requires the consent recorded for the signed-in account.
- A new privacy-notice version requires a fresh decision before optional analytics can resume.
- If your browser signals that Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control is enabled, identified analytics stays disabled, and an existing account grant is replaced with a denial so backend product events also fail closed. Visits from such browsers are still counted pseudonymously as described above.

Vercel may still process request and security logs as our hosting provider, and Nakafa may report service exceptions without an advertising or product-analytics identifier.

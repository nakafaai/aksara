## The Angle Determines the Components

When a vector forms an angle with an axis, trigonometry splits it cleanly into axis components. The slanted vector acts as <Highlight>the hypotenuse of a right triangle</Highlight>, while its horizontal and vertical components form the two perpendicular legs.

For a vector in quadrant I, measure <InlineMath math="\theta" /> from the positive <InlineMath math="x" /> axis. The horizontal component is adjacent to the angle and the vertical component lies opposite it. Dividing each leg by the positive magnitude <InlineMath math="F" /> and multiplying back by <InlineMath math="F" /> gives:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\cos\theta=\frac{F_x}{F}\quad\Rightarrow\quad F_x=F\cos\theta" />
  <BlockMath math="\sin\theta=\frac{F_y}{F}\quad\Rightarrow\quad F_y=F\sin\theta" />
</MathContainer>

> Identify the side adjacent to the reference angle before assigning cosine, and the opposite side before assigning sine.

On a ramp tilted at <InlineMath math="37^\circ" />, a <InlineMath math="19.6\text{ N}" /> weight splits into <InlineMath math="11.8\text{ N}" /> down the slope and <InlineMath math="15.65\text{ N}" /> perpendicular into the plank. Changing the tilt trades strength between the two components while **the total weight stays constant**.

## Resolving a Slanted Force on Coordinate Axes

Imagine pulling a crate with a rope slanted upward and to the right. The pull decomposes into **rightward and upward components** whose vector sum reproduces the exact original force without altering its net effect.

Using the **trigonometric approximations** <InlineMath math="\cos37^\circ\approx0.8" /> and <InlineMath math="\sin37^\circ\approx0.6" /> gives quick classroom estimates:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
  F_x&=F\cos\theta \\
  &\approx50(0.8) \\
  &=40\text{ N}
  \end{aligned}" />

  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
  F_y&=F\sin\theta \\
  &\approx50(0.6) \\
  &=30\text{ N}
  \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Without early rounding, the exact values are <InlineMath math="39.93\text{ N}" /> rightward and <InlineMath math="30.09\text{ N}" /> upward. Both components rebuild the full <InlineMath math="50\text{ N}" /> rope force.

## Angle Measured from the Vertical Axis

Keep the force in quadrant I, yet measure the **angle from the positive vertical axis** instead of the horizontal. Now <InlineMath math="F_y" /> lies adjacent to <InlineMath math="\theta" /> and takes the cosine, while <InlineMath math="F_x" /> lies opposite and takes the sine:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="F_y=F\cos\theta" />
  <BlockMath math="F_x=F\sin\theta" />
</MathContainer>

Always check which axis anchors the angle before writing sine or cosine.

## Component Signs Follow the Quadrant

When <InlineMath math="\theta" /> is the **full standard-position angle** counterclockwise from positive <InlineMath math="x" />, <InlineMath math="\cos\theta" /> and <InlineMath math="\sin\theta" /> deliver the correct component signs automatically. If you use an acute reference angle instead, find component magnitudes first and assign signs from the quadrant.

| Vector direction | Sign of <InlineMath math="F_x" /> | Sign of <InlineMath math="F_y" /> |
| :--------------- | :------------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| up and right | positive | positive |
| up and left | negative | positive |
| down and left | negative | negative |
| down and right | positive | negative |

## Adding Vectors Graphically

When several displacements occur in sequence, or several vectors act at one point, their vector sum can be represented by one arrow called the **resultant**. For forces applied at different points, the resultant force describes the net translational effect, but the turning effect must be checked separately through torque.

The graphical method finds the resultant using a scaled drawing. Arrow lengths are proportional to vector magnitudes, and arrow directions follow the given angles or compass directions.

> In the graphical method, the accuracy depends on drawing scale, ruler work, and angle measurement.

### Parallelogram Method for Two Vectors with One Tail

## Polygon Method for Many Vectors

The drawing order may be rearranged as long as <Highlight>each vector keeps its magnitude and direction</Highlight>. The resultant is drawn from the tail of the first chosen vector to the tip of the last vector.

<BlockMath math="\vec{R}=\vec{a}+\vec{b}+\vec{c}" />

If the polygon returns to the starting point, the vector sum is zero. For forces, zero net force means translational balance. Rotational balance additionally requires zero net torque.

For example, add <InlineMath math="\vec{c}=(-6,-4)" /> after the two displacements in the model. The third arrow follows the diagonal back to the starting point. The journey still has a length, but the total displacement is zero.

<BlockMath math="\vec{a}+\vec{b}+\vec{c}=(4+2-6,1+3-4)=(0,0)" />

A four-leg walk traces a polygon of four arrows, each starting where the previous leg ended: <InlineMath math="300\text{ m}" /> east, <InlineMath math="200\text{ m}" /> north, <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}" /> west, and a final northward leg of <InlineMath math="n" /> meters. At <InlineMath math="n = 100\text{ m}" />, the closing arrow from start to finish reaches <InlineMath math="200\text{ m}" /> east and <InlineMath math="300\text{ m}" /> north, <InlineMath math="361\text{ m}" /> long.

## Graphical Vector Subtraction

For subtraction, **reverse the vector being subtracted**. With the same pair, the components <InlineMath math="\vec{b}=(2,3)" /> become <InlineMath math="-\vec{b}=(-2,-3)" />.

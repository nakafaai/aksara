## Inertia and Newton Laws

Picture a loaded truck beside an empty bicycle parked on flat ground: pushing the truck takes far more effort because its mass is huge. **Inertia** is a body's resistance to changing its motion, organised by **Newton's three laws** linking **momentum**, **force**, and **impulse**:

<BlockMath math="p = mv, \qquad F = ma, \qquad Ft = mv - mu" />

By **Newton's first law**, steady velocity needs zero resultant force because a net force appears only when velocity changes. Seat belts supply the stopping force when a passenger tends to keep moving forward, while **Newton's third law** pairs every action force with an equal and opposite reaction.

## Momentum Conservation

The **principle of conservation of momentum** states that total momentum before a collision or explosion equals total momentum afterwards whenever no net external force acts:

<BlockMath math="m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2" />

Momentum is a **vector quantity**, so always choose one positive direction first. In a **completely inelastic collision**, the colliding bodies stick together and share one velocity.

### Bullet and Block Impact

When a bullet embeds in a wooden block, **momentum is conserved during impact** even though most kinetic energy turns into heat and sound, and floor friction then slows the sliding block to rest.

> Use momentum conservation to find the shared velocity right after impact, then apply floor friction to find deceleration and sliding distance.

### Rifle Recoil Impulse

In an **explosion** starting from rest, initial momentum is zero so **forward bullet momentum and backward rifle momentum match in size and oppose in direction**.

> Total momentum before firing is zero, so backward rifle momentum matches forward bullet momentum before the shoulder stops the recoil.

## Force Mass and Acceleration

By **Newton's second law**, acceleration grows proportionally with **resultant force** and inversely with mass (<InlineMath math="a = F/m" />). Mass never changes with location, whereas **weight** (<InlineMath math="W = mg" />) depends on local gravity.

### Two Connected Blocks

When a taut light string joins two pulled blocks, **both blocks share identical acceleration**. Solve the whole system first for acceleration, then isolate one block to get **string tension**.

> Divide net pull minus friction by combined mass to get acceleration, then apply Newton's second law to a single block to determine tension.

### Apparent Weight in Lifts

Scales inside a lift measure the **upward normal reaction**, not true weight. Whenever the lift **accelerates upward** (speeding up while rising or slowing down while descending), the scale **exceeds true weight**.

> Scale normal force exceeds true weight whenever lift acceleration points upward, and falls below true weight whenever acceleration points downward.

## Impulse and Crash Safety

An **impulsive force** acts strongly over a brief contact interval. Since impulse <InlineMath math="Ft" /> stays fixed for a given momentum change, **lengthening impact time cuts peak force**, which is how airbags and crumple zones protect passengers.

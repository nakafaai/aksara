## Distinguishing Energy Sources and Energy Forms

An energy source identifies <Highlight>where the energy comes from before conversion</Highlight>. An [energy form](/en/subjects/physics/renewable-energy/energy-forms) identifies how energy is stored or transferred.

Trace the energy from its source through a [conversion device](/en/subjects/physics/renewable-energy/energy-transformation) to the useful energy it supplies.

[**Primary energy sources**](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/sources-of-energy.php) occur in nature. **Energy carriers** such as electricity and hydrogen are produced using those sources. Electricity can be generated from coal, flowing water, sunlight, wind, geothermal heat, or biogas.

> Electricity is an energy carrier, never a primary energy source. Every kilowatt-hour in a wire first came from a natural source such as sunlight, wind, falling water, geothermal heat, biomass, or fossil fuel.

## Renewable Sources Still Depend on Time and Place

A renewable energy source is replenished by natural processes on a human time scale. It is **not necessarily available at every moment**. Solar output depends on the time of day and weather, wind output on wind speed, and hydropower output on season and water flow rate.

Wind shows how strongly "available right now" can swing. The power carried through a rotor of area <InlineMath math="A" /> is <InlineMath math="P=\tfrac12\rho Av^3" />, because faster air brings more mass each second and more energy per kilogram.

## Conversion Paths and Source Terms

Evaluating any energy option starts by mapping its **conversion path** and keeping closely related source names distinct. Tracing each step from fuel or flow to electricity prevents mixing up primary resources with carriers.

### Compare Sources through Energy Conversion Paths

Compare an energy source by tracing its initial energy form, conversion device, and **practical limitation**. The table summarizes each pattern briefly.

| Energy source | Short conversion pattern | Constraint to check |
| :------------ | :----------------------- | :--- |
| Coal, petroleum, natural gas | Burning fossil fuel converts its chemical energy into heat that can drive a turbine. | Finite reserves |
| Biomass | Chemical energy stored in organic material becomes heat through combustion, or the material is processed into another fuel before use. | Feedstock that protects ecosystems and maintained equipment |
| Biogas | Microorganisms produce methane-rich gas from organic material without oxygen. Burning the gas releases its chemical energy as heat. That heat can drive machinery or generate electricity. | Feedstock that protects ecosystems and maintained equipment |
| Water | Gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy of flowing water spin a turbine. | Flow rate and height difference |
| Wind | Kinetic energy from moving air spins turbine blades. | Wind speed |
| Sunlight | Radiant energy from the Sun becomes electricity in solar cells or heat in solar heating systems. | Incoming sunlight |
| Ocean waves and tides | Technologies such as buoys, oscillating water columns, and marine turbines convert the mechanical energy of moving seawater into electricity. | Coastal conditions |
| Geothermal heat | Hot fluid or steam carries thermal energy from inside Earth and drives a turbine. | Geological conditions |

[Coal, petroleum, and natural gas](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/sources-of-energy.php) formed from ancient living remains over very long periods.

Ocean waves show how strongly a source depends on local conditions. A regular deep-water wave of height <InlineMath math="H" /> and period <InlineMath math="T" /> carries power per metre of wave crest given by:

<BlockMath math="P = \frac{\rho g^2 H^2 T}{64\pi}" />

### Distinguishing Similar Energy Terms

**Biogas** forms when microorganisms break down organic matter without oxygen through [*anaerobic digestion*](https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/basic-information-about-anaerobic-digestion), producing a gas rich in methane (<InlineMath math="\text{CH}_4" />) and carbon dioxide (<InlineMath math="\text{CO}_2" />).

**Solar energy** can be converted in different ways. [Photovoltaic panels](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/solar/photovoltaics-and-electricity.php) convert light directly into electricity, while solar thermal systems collect heat from sunlight.

[**Marine energy**](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/marine-energy-basics) includes waves, tides, ocean currents, and temperature differences in seawater. Some technologies capture mechanical motion from waves, currents, and tides, while ocean thermal conversion taps the temperature gap between warm surface water and cold deep water.

[**Geothermal energy**](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/geothermal/geothermal-basics) is thermal energy drawn from beneath Earth's surface to generate electricity, heat or cool buildings, and supply direct industrial heat.

In a household biogas digester, fresh cattle dung feeds oxygen-free microbes that release methane-rich gas under a sealed dome. Typically, <InlineMath math="25\text{ kg}" /> of dung per day yields <InlineMath math="1\text{ m}^3" /> of biogas containing <InlineMath math="21.6\text{ MJ}" /> of methane energy, enough for <InlineMath math="2.5\text{ h}" /> of stove cooking.

## Compare Energy Sources in Indonesia

In Indonesian energy policy, the [Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources](https://www.esdm.go.id/en/media-center/news-archives/miliki-potensi-ebt-3686-gw-sekjen-rida-modal-utama-jalankan-transisi-energi-indonesia) groups solar, wind, hydropower, bioenergy, geothermal, and ocean energy under the label New and Renewable Energy, abbreviated EBT.

For any steam, wind, solar, hydroelectric, or geothermal plant, trace the **complete conversion** path by naming the source, its initial energy form, and the device that delivers electricity.

Once the conversion path is clear, compare the source's physical conditions, cost, emissions, land or water needs, and maintenance requirements.

Every steam plant turns heat into work, and heat cannot all become work. Even an ideal engine reaches only <InlineMath math="\eta=1-T_c/T_h" />, so hotter steam allows a higher ceiling. With cooling water at <InlineMath math="300\text{ K}" />, geothermal steam near <InlineMath math="450\text{ K}" /> caps out well below a fuel-fired boiler.

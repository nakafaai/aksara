## An Energy Source Precedes Conversion

An energy source is where energy comes from before it is converted into a form we use. So, "what is the energy source?" is not the same question as "what form of energy is present?".

<BlockMath math="\text{energy source} \rightarrow \text{conversion device} \rightarrow \text{useful energy}" />

[EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/sources-of-energy.php) distinguishes primary energy sources in their original form from secondary energy sources, such as electricity or hydrogen, that are produced from other sources.

That means electricity is not a primary energy source. Electricity is energy that has already been produced from another source, such as coal, flowing water, sunlight, wind, geothermal heat, or biogas.

## Renewable Sources Still Depend on Time and Place

A renewable energy source is replenished by natural processes on a human time scale. However, renewable does not mean available every moment. Sunlight depends on day and weather, wind depends on air conditions, and flowing water depends on season and how much water is actually moving.

[EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/renewable-sources/) explains that renewable energy sources are naturally replenishing but still limited by resource availability. EIA lists biomass, hydropower, geothermal, wind, and solar as major renewable sources.

## Compare Sources through Energy Conversion Paths

Compare an energy source by tracing its initial energy form, conversion device, and practical limitation. The table summarizes each pattern briefly.

| Energy source | Short conversion pattern |
| :------------ | :----------------------- |
| Coal, petroleum, natural gas | Burning fossil fuel converts its chemical energy into heat that can drive a turbine. |
| Biomass | Chemical energy stored in organic material becomes heat through combustion, or the material is processed into another fuel before use. |
| Biogas | Microorganisms produce methane-rich gas from organic material without oxygen. Burning the gas releases its chemical energy as heat. That heat can drive machinery or generate electricity. |
| Water | Gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy of flowing water spin a turbine. |
| Wind | Kinetic energy from moving air spins turbine blades. |
| Sunlight | Radiant energy from the Sun becomes electricity in solar cells or heat in solar heating systems. |
| Ocean waves and tides | Technologies such as buoys, oscillating water columns, and marine turbines convert the mechanical energy of moving seawater into electricity. |
| Geothermal heat | Hot fluid or steam carries thermal energy from inside Earth and drives a turbine. |

Each source also has a practical limitation. Fossil fuels depend on limited stock, biomass and biogas on responsibly supplied feedstock and maintained equipment, water on flow rate and height difference, wind on air speed, solar on solar radiation, ocean energy on coastal conditions, and geothermal on geology.

[EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/sources-of-energy.php) explains that fossil fuels such as coal, petroleum, and natural gas formed from ancient living remains over very long time scales.

## Distinguishing Similar Energy Terms

**Biogas** comes from biomass broken down by microorganisms without oxygen. This process is called *anaerobic digestion*. [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/basic-information-about-anaerobic-digestion) explains that biogas is mostly methane, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_4" />, and carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="\text{CO}_2" />.

**Solar energy** does not always mean photovoltaic panels, which convert light directly into electricity. Solar thermal systems collect heat from sunlight. [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/solar/photovoltaics-and-electricity.php) explains how photovoltaic cells convert light into electricity.

**Marine energy** includes waves, tides, ocean currents, and temperature differences in seawater. [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/marine-energy-basics) explains that marine energy technologies can use the kinetic energy of waves, currents, and tides. Ocean thermal energy conversion uses the temperature difference between warm surface water and colder deep water.

**Geothermal energy** is heat from inside Earth. [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/geothermal/geothermal-basics) explains that geothermal resources can be used for electricity, heating, cooling, and direct use.

## Compare Energy Sources in Indonesia

Indonesia's Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources uses the term New and Renewable Energy, or EBT in Indonesian policy language, for sources such as solar, wind, hydropower, bioenergy, geothermal, and ocean energy.

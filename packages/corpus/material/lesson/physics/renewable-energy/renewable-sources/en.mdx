## Natural Processes Replenish Renewable Resources

Sunlight, wind, flowing water, geothermal heat, and ocean energy are supplied by ongoing natural processes. Biomass can regrow if it is managed. Fossil fuels take millions of years to form.

Renewable sources are [**naturally replenished**](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/renewable-sources/), but the available energy still depends on local resource conditions.

For biomass, compare **the harvest rate with the regrowth rate**. A forest harvested faster than its trees grow loses wood stocks even though biomass is a renewable resource.

The term renewable describes how a source <Highlight>becomes available again through natural processes</Highlight>. Available power still depends on the place, time, and condition of the resource.

Here's what "depends on time" looks like for a solar panel. In a simple model, a panel system rated <InlineMath math="3\text{ kW}" /> at its peak follows half a sine wave from sunrise at 06:00 to sunset at 18:00 and gives nothing at night. The energy of a day is the area under the curve, <InlineMath math="E=P_{\text{peak}}\cdot\frac{24}{\pi}\text{ h}" />, so a clear day gives about <InlineMath math="22.9\text{ kWh}" />. On a cloudy day with half the peak, the violet curve holds only half as much. The source renews itself every morning, but the power isn't there whenever you want it.

## Match Each Source to the Site

Choose a renewable source by checking the **physical conditions at the site**. Each source needs different amounts of sunlight, wind, water flow, heat, or usable material.

| Renewable source | Conditions to check |
| :--------------- | :------------- |
| Sunlight | Check the available solar radiation and whether panels receive enough light without persistent shading. |
| Wind | Start from kinetic energy of air, then check whether wind speed is stable enough for a turbine. |
| Water | Start from the water's gravitational potential and kinetic energy, then check whether the height difference is sufficient and the flow remains adequate across seasons. |
| Geothermal heat | Start from thermal energy inside Earth, then check whether the geothermal reservoir can be reached and used safely. |
| Biomass | Start from chemical energy in organic material, then check whether using the feedstock competes with food supply or harms ecosystems. |
| Biogas | Start from chemical energy in methane, then check whether organic waste is supplied regularly and the biogas equipment is maintained. |
| Ocean | Start from mechanical energy in waves, tides, and currents, or thermal energy in the difference between warm surface water and cold deep water. Then check whether the local waves, currents, tides, or temperature difference are sufficient for the chosen technology. |

Use these conditions to compare a source with the site. For solar panels, check sunlight, available space, weather, maintenance, and electricity needs.

Sunlight at a site also depends on the angle it arrives at. Rays square to a surface bring about <InlineMath math="1\,000\text{ W/m}^2" />. When the surface is turned away by an angle <InlineMath math="\theta" />, the same rays spread out and each square metre catches <InlineMath math="1\,000\cos\theta\text{ W/m}^2" />. With the sun <InlineMath math="30^\circ" /> high, a flat panel catches <InlineMath math="500\text{ W/m}^2" />. Tilt it <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> toward the sun and it catches the full <InlineMath math="1\,000\text{ W/m}^2" />. Count the rays that land on the panel as you tilt it.

## Every Source Has a Conversion Path

Every renewable source should be explainable as an energy conversion path. If the path is unclear, the claim is hard to check.

Name the source and its initial energy form, then identify the **device that converts this energy** into a useful form.

[Photovoltaic cells](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/solar/photovoltaics-and-electricity.php) convert sunlight directly into electricity. Photovoltaic means that the material in a solar cell produces an electric potential difference when it absorbs light.

In a [wind turbine](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/how-do-wind-turbines-work), wind turns the turbine blades, the rotor drives a generator, and the generator produces electricity.

In [hydropower](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/how-hydropower-works), the available energy depends on water flow and height difference. Water flows through a penstock to spin a turbine, which then drives a generator. The generator produces electricity.

Each path ends at a generator, but each starts with a different share of the incoming energy. Rotate the bars for typical values: a solar cell turns about a fifth of the sunlight into electricity, a wind turbine can never pass the Betz limit, and a hydro turbine passes on almost all of the water's energy.

## Some Sources Need More Specific Conditions

Geothermal heat, biogas, and ocean energy each require specific local conditions.

[**Geothermal energy**](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/geothermal/geothermal-basics) is thermal energy from inside Earth. Geothermal resources can be natural or human-made hot-water reservoirs below Earth's surface.

**Biogas** is produced when microorganisms break down organic material without oxygen. This process is called [*anaerobic digestion*](https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/basic-information-about-anaerobic-digestion). Biogas consists mainly of methane, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_4" />, and carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="\text{CO}_2" />.

[**Ocean energy**](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/marine-energy-basics) can come from waves, tides, currents, and temperature differences in seawater. Some systems use the kinetic energy of waves, currents, and tides. Ocean thermal energy conversion uses the temperature difference between warm surface water and colder deep water.

| Source | Practical limit |
| :----- | :-------------- |
| Geothermal heat | The heat source can be steady day and night, but drilling and geology must be suitable. |
| Biogas | The system needs a steady supply of organic waste and regular maintenance. |
| Ocean | Waves, currents, and tides can drive conversion equipment, but site access, corrosion, and marine safety must be considered. |

Here's why geothermal power needs a hot enough reservoir. In this plant, <InlineMath math="100\text{ kg}" /> of water rises every second, gives up heat until it cools to <InlineMath math="70^\circ\text{C}" />, and goes back down. A heat engine between that water and <InlineMath math="27^\circ\text{C}" /> air can never pass <InlineMath math="\eta=1-T_c/T_h" />, and a real plant reaches about <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of that limit. At <InlineMath math="200^\circ\text{C}" /> the water gives <InlineMath math="54.6\text{ MW}" /> of heat and the plant makes about <InlineMath math="8\text{ MW}" />. Hotter water gives more heat and converts a larger share of it.

## Renewable Energy Potential in Indonesia

Indonesia's Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources uses the term **New and Renewable Energy**, or EBT in Indonesian policy language, for sources such as solar, wind, hydropower, bioenergy, geothermal, and ocean energy. A [press release](https://www.esdm.go.id/en/media-center/news-archives/miliki-potensi-ebt-3686-gw-sekjen-rida-modal-utama-jalankan-transisi-energi-indonesia) dated <InlineMath math="4" /> February <InlineMath math="2023" /> estimated Indonesia's EBT potential at about <InlineMath math="3\,686 \text{ GW}" />.

The potential estimates the possible power capacity of the equipment. Actual electricity generation also depends on technology, cost, grid access, permits, maintenance, operating time, and environmental protection.

> A renewable source that works well in one area may not work well in another area.

An area with wide roofs and frequent clear skies may be suitable for solar panels. A village near a steady river may be suitable for small hydropower if the flow, height difference, seasonal changes, and ecosystem effects are acceptable.

## Checking Renewable Energy Claims

When you hear a claim about renewable energy, use these short questions.

| Claim that sounds convincing | Physics check |
| :--------------------------- | :------------ |
| This source will never run out. | Check **when and where the natural flow is available**. For managed biomass and water resources, also check recovery and ecosystem limits. |
| This source works everywhere. | Check local weather, geology, water, wind, feedstock, or ocean conditions. |
| This source is always environmentally friendly. | Check land, waste, feedstock, ecosystems, and construction impacts. |
| This source is always cheap. | Check equipment, maintenance, storage, grid connection, and equipment lifetime. |

A complete assessment records the natural source, conversion path, useful energy, site limits, and measurable impacts.

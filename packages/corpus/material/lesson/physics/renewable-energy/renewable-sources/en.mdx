## Renewable Energy Sources Can Be Replenished

A renewable energy source can be replenished by natural processes. Examples include sunlight, wind, water, biomass, Earth's heat, and the ocean. Fossil fuels take millions of years to form.

According to the [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/renewable-sources/), renewable sources are **naturally replenished**. The amount of energy available still depends on local resource conditions.

The available flow and recovery rate remain finite. Local use that exceeds either limit can reduce the energy supply.

The term renewable describes how a source becomes available again through natural processes. Available power still depends on the place, time, and condition of the resource.

After asking whether a source is renewable, ask whether enough of it is available at the location and time when energy is needed. For managed resources such as biomass and water, also check ecosystem limits and how quickly the local resource can recover.

## Match Each Source to the Site

Choose a renewable source by checking the physical conditions at the site. Each source needs different amounts of sunlight, wind, water flow, heat, or usable material.

| Renewable source | Conditions to check |
| :--------------- | :------------- |
| Sunlight | Check the available solar radiation and whether panels receive enough light without persistent shading. |
| Wind | Start from kinetic energy of air, then check whether wind speed is stable enough for a turbine. |
| Water | Start from the water's gravitational potential and kinetic energy, then check whether the height difference is sufficient and the flow remains adequate across seasons. |
| Geothermal heat | Start from thermal energy inside Earth, then check whether the geothermal reservoir can be reached and used safely. |
| Biomass | Start from chemical energy in organic material, then check whether using the feedstock competes with food supply or harms ecosystems. |
| Biogas | Start from chemical energy in methane, then check whether organic waste is supplied regularly and the biogas equipment is maintained. |
| Ocean | Start from mechanical energy in waves, tides, and currents, or thermal energy in the difference between warm surface water and cold deep water. Then check whether the local waves, currents, tides, or temperature difference are sufficient for the chosen technology. |

Use these conditions to compare a source with the site. For solar panels, check sunlight, available space, weather, maintenance, and electricity needs.

## Every Source Has a Conversion Path

Every renewable source should be explainable as an energy conversion path. If the path is unclear, the claim is hard to check.

For example, photovoltaic cells convert sunlight directly into electricity. **Photovoltaic** means the material in a solar cell produces an electric potential difference when it absorbs light. The [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/solar/photovoltaics-and-electricity.php) also explains this conversion.

<BlockMath math="\text{solar radiation}\rightarrow\text{photovoltaic cell}\rightarrow\text{electrical energy}" />

In a wind turbine, wind spins the turbine blades, the rotor drives a generator, and the generator produces electricity. This sequence is explained by the [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/how-do-wind-turbines-work).

<BlockMath math="\text{wind}\rightarrow\text{rotor}\rightarrow\text{generator}\rightarrow\text{electricity}" />

In hydropower, the available energy depends on water flow and height difference. According to the [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/how-hydropower-works), water flows through a penstock to spin a turbine, which then drives a generator.

## Some Sources Need More Specific Conditions

Geothermal heat, biogas, and ocean energy each require specific local conditions.

**Geothermal energy** is thermal energy from inside Earth. The [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/geothermal/geothermal-basics) explains that geothermal resources can be natural or human-made hot-water reservoirs below Earth's surface.

**Biogas** is produced when microorganisms break down organic material without oxygen. This process is called *anaerobic digestion*. According to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/basic-information-about-anaerobic-digestion), biogas consists mainly of methane, <InlineMath math="\text{CH}_4" />, and carbon dioxide, <InlineMath math="\text{CO}_2" />.

**Ocean energy** can come from waves, tides, currents, and temperature differences in seawater. The [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/marine-energy-basics) explains that marine energy technologies can use the kinetic energy of waves, currents, and tides. Ocean thermal energy conversion uses the temperature difference between warm surface water and colder deep water.

| Source | Practical limit |
| :----- | :-------------- |
| Geothermal heat | The heat source can be steady day and night, but drilling and geology must be suitable. |
| Biogas | Organic waste can become fuel, but feedstock supply and processing-equipment care must be routine. |
| Ocean | Waves, currents, and tides can drive conversion equipment, but site access, corrosion, and marine safety must be considered. |

## Indonesian Context

Indonesia's Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources uses the term New and Renewable Energy, or EBT in Indonesian policy language, for sources such as solar, wind, hydropower, bioenergy, geothermal, and ocean energy. In a press release dated February <InlineMath math="4" />, <InlineMath math="2023" />, [ESDM](https://www.esdm.go.id/en/media-center/news-archives/miliki-potensi-ebt-3686-gw-sekjen-rida-modal-utama-jalankan-transisi-energi-indonesia) estimated Indonesia's EBT potential at about <InlineMath math="3\,686 \text{ GW}" />.

Potential estimates how much energy a natural source could provide. Usable electricity also depends on technology, cost, grid access, permits, maintenance, and environmental protection.

> A renewable source that works well in one area may not work well in another area.

A simple example: an area with wide roofs and frequent clear skies may suit solar energy, while a village near a steady river may suit a small hydropower study. Compare the initial energy, conversion device, and resource limits to determine that fit.

## Checking Renewable Energy Claims

When you hear a claim about renewable energy, use these short questions.

| Claim that sounds convincing | Physics check |
| :--------------------------- | :------------ |
| This source will never run out. | Check when and where the natural flow is available. For managed biomass and water resources, also check recovery and ecosystem limits. |
| This source works everywhere. | Check local weather, geology, water, wind, feedstock, or ocean conditions. |
| This source is always environmentally friendly. | Check land, waste, feedstock, ecosystems, and construction impacts. |
| This source is always cheap. | Check equipment, maintenance, storage, grid connection, and equipment lifetime. |

A complete assessment records the natural source, conversion path, useful energy, site limits, and measurable impacts.

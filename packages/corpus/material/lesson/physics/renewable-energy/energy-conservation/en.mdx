## Energy Is Transferred or Converted

The law of energy conservation states that <Highlight>energy cannot be created or destroyed</Highlight>. Energy can be transferred from one object or system to another and converted from one form to another.

In the International System of Units (SI), every form of energy can still be expressed in joules with symbol <InlineMath math="\text{J}" />.

If we include the system and its surroundings within one outer boundary, the total energy inside stays constant **provided no energy crosses that outer boundary**.

If a device supplies less electrical energy than it receives, the difference is transferred to the surroundings or stored as internal energy in the device. It may appear as heating, sound, vibration, or unwanted radiation.

Energy conservation applies to each transfer and transformation in a system.

Watch it happen in a swinging bob. A <InlineMath math="1\text{ kg}" /> bob on a <InlineMath math="2\text{ m}" /> string starts at <InlineMath math="40^\circ" /> with <InlineMath math="4.59\text{ J}" /> of potential energy. Without drag, that energy only trades between potential and kinetic. With air drag, part of it moves to the surroundings as heat, yet the three bands always stack to the same <InlineMath math="4.59\text{ J}" />.

## Choose the System First

A **system** is the part we choose to analyze. Everything outside the system is called the **surroundings**. State the system boundary before applying energy conservation, because that boundary determines which energy transfers cross into or out of the calculation.

The diagram tracks every output. A system can deliver useful energy while also transferring energy to the surroundings as heat, sound, or vibration.

| Chosen system | Energy entering the system | Energy leaving the system |
| :------------ | :------------------------- | :------------------------ |
| Water and Earth, with no air resistance or energy transfer outside | none | none. Potential energy becomes kinetic energy within the system |
| Turbine and generator | rotational kinetic energy | electrical energy, heat, sound |
| Solar panel | radiant energy from sunlight | electrical energy, heat, reflected light |

In the first row, Earth is part of the system because gravitational potential energy belongs to the interaction between water and Earth. A change of energy form inside a system differs from a transfer across its boundary.

## When Friction Is Ignored

Many introductory problems **ignore friction and air resistance** so that only kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy remain in the model. Mechanical energy is then written as:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
E_m &= E_k+E_p \\
&= \frac{1}{2}mv^2+mgh
\end{aligned}" />

If there is no friction, no air resistance, and no external work, mechanical energy remains constant.

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{2}mv_i^2+mgh_i=\frac{1}{2}mv_f^2+mgh_f" />

The subscript <InlineMath math="i" /> means initial state, while the subscript <InlineMath math="f" /> means final state.

Suppose water with mass <InlineMath math="1 \text{ kg}" /> falls from a height of <InlineMath math="12 \text{ m}" />. If we use <InlineMath math="g=10 \text{ m/s}^2" /> and treat the water as initially at rest, its initial gravitational potential energy is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
E_p &= mgh \\
&= (1 \text{ kg})(10 \text{ m/s}^2)(12 \text{ m}) \\
&= 120 \text{ J}
\end{aligned}" />

If no energy crosses into the surroundings, that <InlineMath math="120 \text{ J}" /> changes into the kinetic energy of the water. In a real device, some energy moves to the surroundings as heat, sound, and vibration.

Follow the water down the graph below, from <InlineMath math="h=12\text{ m}" /> on the right to the ground on the left. The orange potential energy shrinks by exactly as much as the teal kinetic energy grows, so the violet line for their sum never moves from <InlineMath math="120\text{ J}" />. Halfway down, at <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" />, the two forms share the energy equally.

## Useful Energy Depends on the Device Purpose

In renewable-energy technology, the desired output is usually **useful energy**, such as electrical energy. Energy conservation still applies, but the energy input does not all become electricity.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} E_{\text{input}} &amp;=E_{\text{useful}} +E_{\text{surroundings}} \\ &amp;\quad{}+\Delta E_{\text{stored}} \end{aligned}" />

In the following example, the stored energy of the device does not change, so <InlineMath math="\Delta E_{\text{stored}}=0" />. Flowing water carries <InlineMath math="120 \text{ J}" /> of energy toward a small turbine. The generator produces <InlineMath math="90 \text{ J}" /> of electrical energy. The energy transferred to the surroundings is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
E_{\text{surroundings}}
&= E_{\text{input}}-E_{\text{useful}} \\
&= 120 \text{ J}-90 \text{ J} \\
&= 30 \text{ J}
\end{aligned}" />

So the <InlineMath math="30 \text{ J}" /> has not vanished. It may leave through heat transfer from the shaft, sound carried away through the air, or vibration transferred to surrounding structures.

[Hydropower plants](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/how-hydropower-works) use a difference in water height to drive turbines and generators.

Rotate the columns: the energy that goes into the turbine all comes out again. Useful electrical energy and the share lost to heat, sound and vibration add back to the input.

## Testing Energy Claims with Conservation

The law of energy conservation can test an energy claim by accounting for every transfer and transformation.

For the three claims below, assume **stored energy does not change** during the observation.

| Claim | Check with energy conservation |
| :---- | :----------------------------- |
| A panel receives <InlineMath math="500 \text{ J}" /> of radiation and produces <InlineMath math="500 \text{ J}" /> of electricity | This claim is not realistic for a real panel. Under the law of energy conservation, it would be possible only if no energy were reflected or converted to heat. |
| A turbine receives <InlineMath math="120 \text{ J}" /> and produces <InlineMath math="90 \text{ J}" /> of electricity | This claim is reasonable if the remaining <InlineMath math="30 \text{ J}" /> moves to the surroundings. |
| A machine produces <InlineMath math="150 \text{ J}" /> of electricity from <InlineMath math="120 \text{ J}" /> of input without another source | This claim violates the law of energy conservation. |

A [photovoltaic cell](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/solar-performance-and-efficiency) does not convert all incident light into electricity. Some light is reflected, and some becomes heat.

An efficient device converts a larger share of its input into useful energy and transfers a smaller share to the surroundings. Calling a device *clean* requires additional evidence about emissions, materials, land or water use, and waste.

## Energy Conservation Applies to Renewable Energy

Renewable energy sources still obey the **law of energy conservation**. Sunlight, wind, water, geothermal heat, and biomass provide energy input. Technology helps transform that input into the energy we need.

Follow the energy from its source into the conversion device. Part becomes useful output, while part is transferred to the surroundings.

To check energy conservation in a renewable-energy technology, answer three questions:

- Where does the input energy come from?
- Where is energy transferred when it does not become useful output?
- What fraction of the input energy becomes useful output?

These answers identify the energy input, every transfer out of the system, and the useful fraction of the output.

Try those three questions on a solar panel. In full sun, <InlineMath math="1\,000\text{ W/m}^2" /> falls on a <InlineMath math="1.6\text{ m}^2" /> panel, so <InlineMath math="1\,600\text{ W}" /> comes in. About <InlineMath math="64\text{ W}" /> bounces off the glass, <InlineMath math="274\text{ W}" /> leaves as electricity, and the remaining <InlineMath math="1\,262\text{ W}" /> heats the panel to about <InlineMath math="61^\circ\text{C}" />. Weaken the sunlight and check that the three outputs still add back to the input.

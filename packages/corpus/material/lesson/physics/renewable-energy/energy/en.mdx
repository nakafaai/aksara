## Energy Is Identified through Change

When a lamp turns on, water boils, a bicycle moves, or a phone charges, energy is being transferred or transformed. In physics, [**energy**](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/) is the ability to do work or cause change.

Energy is <Highlight>identified through its effects</Highlight>: an object moves, temperature changes, light appears, sound is produced, or electric charge flows.

Here's that idea in a real device. A gravity lamp has no battery and no plug: a <InlineMath math="12\text{ kg}" /> bag of sand sinks <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ m}" /> and turns a small generator that lights an LED. Watch two changes happen together: the bag gets lower and light appears. Taking <InlineMath math="g=10\text{ m/s}^2" />, the bag always releases <InlineMath math="mgh=216\text{ J}" />, so a slower descent only spreads that energy over more time and the lamp glows dimmer.

## Mechanical Work Transfers Energy

When a constant force acts in the **same direction** as an object's displacement, the energy transferred by mechanical work is:

<BlockMath math="W = F \Delta s" />

From here on, we use the International System of Units (SI) so force, displacement, and energy use one consistent unit system.

Symbol guide:

| Symbol | Meaning | SI unit |
| :----- | :------ | :------ |
| <InlineMath math="W" /> | work or transferred energy | <InlineMath math="\text{J}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="F" /> | force | <InlineMath math="\text{N}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\Delta s" /> | displacement | <InlineMath math="\text{m}" /> |

Suppose you push a box with a force of <InlineMath math="15 \text{ N}" /> until it moves <InlineMath math="2 \text{ m}" />. The energy transferred to the box through work is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
W &= F \Delta s \\
&= 15 \text{ N} \times 2 \text{ m} \\
&= 30 \text{ J}
\end{aligned}" />

So, the box receives <InlineMath math="30 \text{ J}" /> of energy through that push.

> If a force is applied but the object does not move, the mechanical work on the object is <InlineMath math="0 \text{ J}" />. The person still uses metabolic energy and may feel tired, but the stationary object receives no energy through displacement.

## Energy Is Measured in Joules

In SI, the unit for energy is the joule, with symbol <InlineMath math="\text{J}" />. Because energy can be calculated from **force times displacement**, a joule can be read as:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1 \text{ J}
&= 1 \text{ N} \cdot 1 \text{ m} \\
&= 1 \text{ kg m}^2\text{ s}^{-2}
\end{aligned}" />

The joule is the [SI derived unit](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-330/sp-330-section-2) of energy. [One joule equals one newton metre](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/26261), or one kilogram metre squared per second squared.

You can feel the joule on a building site. A motor hoist lifts a <InlineMath math="20\text{ kg}" /> crate onto a platform <InlineMath math="5\text{ m}" /> up. With <InlineMath math="g=10\text{ m/s}^2" /> the rope pulls with the crate's weight, <InlineMath math="200\text{ N}" />, so the crate gains <InlineMath math="200\text{ N}\times5\text{ m}=1\,000\text{ J}" /> whichever motor runs the hoist. Switch motors and notice what changes: only the time.

## Energy Transfer and Conversion

Energy can be **transferred from a source to a device**. The device can then convert it into a useful output such as light, motion, heating, or electricity.

In a solar panel, light energy changes into electrical energy. In hydropower, gravitational potential energy of water changes into turbine motion, then into electrical energy. In the body, chemical energy from food helps muscles do work.

Energy is never lost in a conversion, only shared out. ## Power Measures the Rate of Energy Transfer

Energy answers the question *how much energy is transferred or transformed*. Power answers the question *how quickly that transfer or transformation happens*.

Average power is transferred energy divided by elapsed time. In the example below, we assume **constant power** while the device is on.

<BlockMath math="P = \frac{E}{t}" />

From that equation, energy can be written as:

<BlockMath math="E = P t" />

The unit of power is the watt, with symbol <InlineMath math="\text{W}" />. One watt means <InlineMath math="1 \text{ J}" /> per <InlineMath math="1 \text{ s}" />.

<BlockMath math="1 \text{ W} = 1 \text{ J/s}" />

Watch the symbol context: italic <InlineMath math="W" /> usually means work, while upright <InlineMath math="\text{W}" /> means the unit watt.

A watt measures power, which is energy per unit time. The multiplication of power by time makes <InlineMath math="\text{kWh}" /> a unit of energy, and that is why electricity bills use kilowatt-hour.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
1 \text{ kWh}
&= 1000 \text{ W} \times 1 \text{ h} \\
&= 1000 \text{ J/s} \times 3600 \text{ s} \\
&= 3.6 \times 10^6 \text{ J}
\end{aligned}" />

A <InlineMath math="10 \text{ W}" /> lamp that stays on for <InlineMath math="5 \text{ h}" /> uses:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
E &= P t \\
&= 10 \text{ W} \times 5 \text{ h} \\
&= 50 \text{ Wh} \\
&= 0.05 \text{ kWh}
\end{aligned}" />

If the same lamp stays on longer, its energy use increases. If the power is higher, the energy use also increases for the same duration.

## Energy Analysis for Renewable Sources

To analyze a renewable-energy system, identify the energy source, track each conversion, and compare the useful output energy with the input energy.

| Situation | Physics question |
| :-------- | :--------------- |
| Solar panels are installed on a roof | How much light energy becomes electrical energy? |
| Flowing water turns a turbine | How much of the water's kinetic energy can be transferred to a generator? |
| Home lamps turn on every night | How much electrical energy is used in <InlineMath math="\text{kWh}" />? |
| Fuel burns inside an engine | How much chemical energy becomes motion and heat? |

An energy source is [**renewable**](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/renewable-sources/) when natural processes replenish it on a human timescale. Examples include sunlight, wind, flowing water, geothermal heat, and properly managed biomass. **Non-renewable** sources form much more slowly than people use them. Examples include coal, oil, and natural gas.

[Global renewable capacity additions](https://www.iea.org/reports/renewables-2023/executive-summary) reached nearly <InlineMath math="510 \text{ GW}" /> in <InlineMath math="2023" />. Gigawatts measure the rated power of the newly added equipment. The energy generated depends on the power used and the operating time.

Energy is an amount measured in joules. Power is the rate at which energy is transferred, and an energy source supplies the energy that a device converts.

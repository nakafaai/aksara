## Trace Energy through Each Device

**Energy transformation** is the change of energy from one form into another. The word "transformation" does not mean energy is created from nothing. Energy still follows the law of energy conservation, but its form and transfer path change.

[EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/what-is-energy/laws-of-energy.php) explains that the total amount of energy stays constant while energy changes form. Every conversion produces useful energy along with energy that the device cannot use for its purpose.

Write the conversion path to analyze an energy technology.

<BlockMath math="\text{energy source} \rightarrow \text{initial form} \rightarrow \text{conversion device} \rightarrow \text{useful energy}+\text{dissipated energy}" />

Dissipated energy is not lost energy. It spreads to the surroundings, for example as heat, sound, vibration, or reflected light.

[DOE](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/geothermal/geothermal-electricity-generation) explains that geothermal power plants use hot fluid or steam from below Earth's surface to drive turbines and produce electricity.

## Efficiency Measures the Useful Share

Because not all input energy becomes the output we need, we use **efficiency**. Efficiency compares useful energy with input energy.

<BlockMath math="\eta=\frac{E_{\text{useful}}}{E_{\text{input}}}\times 100\%" />

Symbol guide:

| Symbol | Meaning |
| :----- | :------ |
| <InlineMath math="\eta" /> | efficiency |
| <InlineMath math="E_{\text{useful}}" /> | energy that matches the device's purpose |
| <InlineMath math="E_{\text{input}}" /> | energy received by the device |

Suppose a panel receives <InlineMath math="800 \text{ J}" /> of light energy and produces <InlineMath math="160 \text{ J}" /> of electrical energy. Its efficiency is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\eta
&= \frac{E_{\text{useful}}}{E_{\text{input}}}\times 100\% \\
&= \frac{160 \text{ J}}{800 \text{ J}}\times 100\% \\
&= 20\%
\end{aligned}" />

The energy that does not become useful electricity is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
E_{\text{dissipated}}
&= E_{\text{input}}-E_{\text{useful}} \\
&= 800 \text{ J}-160 \text{ J} \\
&= 640 \text{ J}
\end{aligned}" />

So, an efficiency of <InlineMath math="20\%" /> does not mean the remaining <InlineMath math="80\%" /> disappears. The remaining energy moves into the surroundings, for example as heat and light that the panel does not absorb.

[EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/use-of-energy/efficiency-and-conservation.php) uses energy efficiency to describe the technical performance of energy conversion and energy-consuming devices.

## Trace the Energy Path for Each Device

Solar panels, wind turbines, generators, and biogas reactors convert existing energy from one form to another. The device name alone does not state the source, conversion path, useful output, or dissipated output.

| When you see this | Ask this |
| :---------------- | :------- |
| Solar panel | How much radiant energy becomes electricity? |
| Wind turbine | Is the wind fast enough to rotate the rotor? |
| Hydropower plant | How large are the water flow rate and height difference? |
| Biomass | Is the feedstock sufficient and managed sustainably? |
| Geothermal power plant | Does the site provide sufficient underground heat, fluid, and flow for power generation? |

The conversion path records the source, initial energy form, output form, useful energy, and dissipated energy.

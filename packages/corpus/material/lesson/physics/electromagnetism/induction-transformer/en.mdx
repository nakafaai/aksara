## Force on a Current Wire

**Electromagnetism** links electric current and magnetic fields. When a wire carrying current sits inside an external magnetic field, both fields combine into a **catapult field** that pushes the wire towards the weaker side with force:

<BlockMath math="F = BIL" />

Use **Fleming's left-hand rule** for force direction: first finger along magnetic field, second along current, thumb along force. Keep the rule straight: **motors use the left hand**, while **generators use the right hand**.

Picture a thin copper wire running across a magnet gap and hovering in mid-air with no support. To float, **the upward magnetic force must balance the wire's downward weight**:

<BlockMath math="BIL = mg \quad \Longrightarrow \quad I = \frac{mg}{BL}" />

> With the magnetic field pointing north, current flowing east lifts the wire straight upward until magnetic force balances weight.

In a **direct current motor**, opposite sides of a current-carrying coil feel opposite forces and spin. A **commutator** flips the current every half turn to sustain steady rotation, which speeds up with larger current, stronger magnets, or more turns.

## Induction and Lenz Law

Whenever a conductor cuts magnetic field lines or the magnetic flux through a coil changes, an **induced voltage** (e.m.f.) appears. **Faraday's law** links its size to the rate of flux change, while **Lenz's law** ensures the induced current **always opposes the change producing it**, conserving energy.

### Copper Pipe Braking

Drop a strong magnet down three identical tubes: plastic, solid copper, and copper with a lengthwise slit. Even though copper is non-magnetic at rest, the magnet creeps slowly down the solid copper pipe because **circular eddy currents in the copper wall brake its fall**.

> Eddy currents induced around a solid copper pipe create an opposing magnetic field that brakes the falling magnet and converts mechanical energy into heat.

## Step Down Transformers

A **transformer** links primary and secondary coils through changing magnetic flux in a soft iron core, so it **works only with alternating current**, never steady direct current:

<BlockMath math="\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}, \qquad \eta = \frac{V_s I_s}{V_p I_p} \times 100\%" />

A step-up transformer **never increases power**: raising voltage lowers current. Real transformers lose some energy as heat in coils and core eddy currents, so **input power always exceeds output power**.

> Voltage ratio fixes the turns ratio, while primary current must come from the full input power covering both load power and heat losses.

## Thermionic Cathode Rays

**Electronics** controls electron flow through vacuum tubes and semiconductors. Heating a metal cathode inside an evacuated tube frees electrons by **thermionic emission**, accelerating them toward the positive anode as a **cathode ray**. Because electrons carry negative charge, they always deflect toward a positive plate while turning electrical potential energy into kinetic energy:

<BlockMath math="eV = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m}}" />

## Semiconductors and Diodes

Pure silicon conducts weakly until **doping** adds trace impurity atoms: phosphorus forms **n-type** silicon with free electrons as majority carriers, while boron forms **p-type** silicon with holes as majority carriers, yet both crystals remain electrically neutral overall.

Joining p-type and n-type silicon builds a **diode** with a carrier-free **depletion layer**. In **forward bias**, the depletion layer thins and silicon conducts steeply near <InlineMath math="0.7\text{ V}" />, whereas in **reverse bias**, the depletion layer widens and blocks current.

## Rectification with Diodes

**Rectification** converts alternating current into direct current. **Half-wave rectification** uses one diode and discards negative half-cycles, while **full-wave rectification** uses four bridge diodes to flip negative half-cycles positive before a parallel **capacitor** smooths the ripples.

> A four-diode bridge flips negative half-cycles positive to double the output pulse rate, and a parallel capacitor charges at each peak and discharges into the valleys to smooth voltage ripple.

### Automatic Light Switch

In an automatic street lamp, a fixed resistor and a **light-dependent resistor** (<InlineMath math="\text{LDR}" />) form a potential divider feeding the transistor base:

<BlockMath math="V_B = \frac{R_{\text{LDR}}}{R + R_{\text{LDR}}}\,V" />

As darkness falls, LDR resistance climbs steeply until **base voltage crosses <InlineMath math="0.7\text{ V}" />** and turns the transistor and lamp on.

> A potential divider with a light sensor in the lower branch raises base voltage in darkness so the transistor switches on automatically above its silicon threshold.

### Bias Circuit Analysis

To solve all currents and voltages in a biased transistor cleanly, solve the **base loop first** to get <InlineMath math="I_B" />, multiply by <InlineMath math="\beta" /> to get <InlineMath math="I_C" />, and then find collector-emitter voltage <InlineMath math="V_{CE}" /> around the collector loop.

> Subtract the silicon base threshold in the base loop to get base current first, multiply by current gain for collector current, then subtract the collector resistor drop from the collector supply.

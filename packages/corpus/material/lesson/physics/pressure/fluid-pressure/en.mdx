## Pressure Inside Liquids

Picture diving deeper toward the bottom of a pool: your ears feel a stronger squeeze because the whole column of water above pushes down. **Pressure** is normal force per unit area, and **liquid pressure** at depth <InlineMath math="h" /> depends on depth, density <InlineMath math="\rho" />, and gravity <InlineMath math="g" />:

<BlockMath math="P = \frac{F}{A}, \qquad P = h\rho g" />

Liquid pressure **never depends on container shape or width**, only on depth below the surface. That explains why a dam wall is always built thickest at its base.

## Atmospheric and Gas Pressure

**Atmospheric pressure** comes from the weight of the air blanket above Earth. At sea level it supports a <InlineMath math="76\text{ cm}" /> mercury column inside a barometer:

<BlockMath math="P_{\text{atm}} = h\rho g = 0.76 \times 13\,600 \times 9.81 = 1.01 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}" />

Climbing a mountain leaves less air overhead, so atmospheric pressure falls smoothly. Inside a closed vessel, **gas pressure** arises from molecules colliding with the walls and is measured with a manometer or Bourdon gauge.

## Pascal Principle Hydraulics

**Pascal's principle** states that pressure applied to an enclosed fluid transmits **equally in every direction**. In a **hydraulic jack**, the very same oil pressure pushes on both the small piston and the large piston:

<BlockMath math="\frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}, \qquad A_1 d_1 = A_2 d_2" />

## Buoyancy and Archimedes Law

**Archimedes' principle** states that any body immersed in a fluid feels a **buoyant force** equal to the **weight of the fluid it displaces**:

<BlockMath math="F_B = \rho V g" />

Always substitute the **fluid density** <InlineMath math="\rho" />, not the object's density. An object floats right where buoyant force matches its weight.

## Bernoulli Flow and Lift

In steady moving fluids, pressure energy and kinetic energy continuously trade places under energy conservation. **Bernoulli's principle** captures that trade-off directly: wherever fluid flows faster, its static pressure drops lower.

### Venturi Tube Flow

When steady water enters a narrower pipe, the same volume flow per second forces the liquid to speed up sharply. By **Bernoulli's principle**, **faster-moving fluid always exerts lower static pressure**.

> A narrower pipe accelerates steady flow by continuity, and higher speed lowers static pressure inside the constriction.

### Aerofoil Wing Lift

That same Bernoulli trade-off powers an **aerofoil wing**: curved upper flow moves faster than lower flow, leaving **higher pressure underneath the wing** to push it upward with pressure difference:

<BlockMath math="\Delta P = \frac{1}{2}\rho\left(v_{\text{top}}^2 - v_{\text{bottom}}^2\right)" />

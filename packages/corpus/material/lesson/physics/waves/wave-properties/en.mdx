## Wave Speed and Features

A **wave** carries energy without transporting matter: picture a float bobbing up and down without drifting along. Its speed links frequency and wavelength:

<BlockMath math="v = f\lambda, \qquad f = \frac{1}{T}" />

In **transverse waves** like light, particles vibrate perpendicular to travel, while in **longitudinal waves** like sound they vibrate parallel to travel. Always measure **wavelength** <InlineMath math="\lambda" /> from crest to next crest, never crest to trough.

## Reflection and Refraction

When a wave meets a barrier or a new medium, **its frequency never changes** because the source fixes it. Reflection reverses direction, refraction alters speed and wavelength together, and diffraction curves wavefronts around narrow openings.

Picture blowing a ship's horn while cruising straight towards a cliff. While the sound races out and back, the boat glides forward, so **the echo meets the boat closer to the cliff**.

> When a moving source heads towards a reflecting wall, the distance covered by the sound plus the distance moved by the source equals twice the starting distance.

With boat speed <InlineMath math="10\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, sound speed <InlineMath math="340\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, and echo delay <InlineMath math="4.0\text{ s}" />:

<BlockMath math="2d = (340 + 10)(4.0) = 1400\text{ m} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad d = 700\text{ m}" />

Like marching rows stepping diagonally from dry pavement into mud, the side of a water wavefront entering shallow water slows down first, pivoting the wave **towards the normal** while keeping frequency constant.

> When water waves cross from deep into shallow water, the source fixes the frequency so wave speed and wavelength drop together.

## Interference of Waves

**Interference** is the superposition of two coherent waves: crest on crest builds **constructive interference** (bright or loud), while crest on trough creates **destructive interference** (dark or quiet). Adjacent maxima lie apart by:

<BlockMath math="x = \frac{\lambda D}{a}" />

## Quick Wave Checks

Convert centimetres and millimetres to metres first, then **check every answer** against the summary below.

1. A wave has period <InlineMath math="0.2\text{ s}" /> and <InlineMath math="\lambda = 1.5\text{ m}" />. Calculate its speed.
2. A water wave slows from <InlineMath math="0.30\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> (<InlineMath math="\lambda = 6\text{ cm}" />) to <InlineMath math="0.20\text{ m s}^{-1}" /> in shallow water. Find the new wavelength.
3. In a double-slit setup with <InlineMath math="a = 0.4\text{ mm}" />, <InlineMath math="D = 1.6\text{ m}" />, and <InlineMath math="x = 2.2\text{ mm}" />, find the wavelength.
4. Why can soldiers marching in step make a bridge sway dangerously?

Quick answers: problem one gives <InlineMath math="f = 5\text{ Hz}" /> and <InlineMath math="v = 7.5\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, problem two gives <InlineMath math="\lambda = 4\text{ cm}" /> with constant frequency, problem three yields <InlineMath math="550\text{ nm}" /> (green light), and problem four happens through **resonance** when step frequency matches the bridge's natural frequency.

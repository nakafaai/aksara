## Average Values Use a Complete Time Interval

Average velocity uses displacement. Since displacement has direction, average velocity <Highlight>keeps direction too</Highlight>. Average speed uses the total distance actually traveled, so it only tells how quickly the path was covered without direction.

> If the path bends or returns toward the start, distance and displacement can have different values.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\bar{v}=\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}" />
  <BlockMath math="\text{average speed}=\frac{s_{\text{total}}}{\Delta t}" />
</MathContainer>

The symbol <InlineMath math="\Delta x" /> or <InlineMath math="\Delta \vec{r}" /> means the change in position from start to finish. The symbol <InlineMath math="s_{\text{total}}" /> means the length of the path traveled during the trip.

The numbers in the card come from the route drawn in the scene. The teal path shows the total distance because it follows the whole track. The violet line shows the displacement because it connects the starting position directly to the ending position.

To calculate the total distance, split the teal route into straight and curved segments. A straight segment uses its line length. A curved segment uses its arc length, which is the radius multiplied by the angle in radians. Add all segments, then compare the result with the same elapsed time.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;s_{\text{total}}=\sum L_{\text{segment}} \\ &amp;L_{\text{line}}=d \\ &amp;L_{\text{arc}}=r|\theta| \end{aligned}" />

For displacement, first find the change in each coordinate:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\Delta x=x_{\text{end}}-x_{\text{start}} \\ &amp;\Delta z=z_{\text{end}}-z_{\text{start}} \\ &amp;|\Delta\vec r|=\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2+(\Delta z)^2} \end{aligned}" />

Divide the total distance and the displacement magnitude by the same elapsed time:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\text{average speed}&amp;=\frac{s_{\text{total}}}{\Delta t} \\ &amp;|\bar{\vec v}|=\frac{|\Delta \vec r|}{\Delta t} \end{aligned}" />

In the Smooth Bend case, the ball follows the curve, so its total distance is about <InlineMath math="16.7 \text{ m}" />. The start and finish positions, however, are only <InlineMath math="14 \text{ m}" /> apart. Because the trip takes <InlineMath math="5 \text{ s}" />, the average speed and average velocity magnitude are:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;s_{\text{total}}\approx 16.7\text{ m} \\ &amp;|\Delta \vec r|=14\text{ m} \\ &amp;\Delta t=5\text{ s} \\ &amp;\text{average speed}&amp;\approx \frac{16.7}{5} \\ &amp;\approx3.3\text{ m/s} \\ &amp;|\bar{\vec v}| =\frac{14}{5} \\ &amp;=2.8\text{ m/s} \end{aligned}" />

## The Same Time Interval Can Describe Different Trips

Suppose two trips both take <InlineMath math="3 \text{ s}" />. The first trip goes straight <InlineMath math="6 \text{ m}" /> to the right. The second trip goes <InlineMath math="6 \text{ m}" /> to the right and then <InlineMath math="3 \text{ m}" /> back to the left. The time is the same, but the **distance and displacement are different**.

| Trip | Total Distance | Displacement | Comparison Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Straight to the right | <InlineMath math="6 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="6 \text{ m}" /> to the right | Average speed and average velocity have the same magnitude |
| Right then back | <InlineMath math="9 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="3 \text{ m}" /> to the right | Average speed is larger than average velocity magnitude |

Both calculations use the same elapsed time, but average speed divides total distance by that time and average velocity divides displacement by it.

## Average Speed and Average Velocity on a Return Route

On the return route, the object moves <InlineMath math="6 \text{ m}" /> to the right and then <InlineMath math="3 \text{ m}" /> to the left in <InlineMath math="3 \text{ s}" />.

The total distance is <InlineMath math="9 \text{ m}" />, but the displacement is only <InlineMath math="3 \text{ m}" /> to the right.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{average speed}=\frac{9}{3}=3\text{ m/s}" />
  <BlockMath math="\bar{v}=\frac{3}{3}=1\text{ m/s to the right}" />
</MathContainer>

Average speed uses the **entire path** in the numerator, while average velocity uses the change in position.

Here are both trips as position against time, with A moving at a steady <InlineMath math="2\text{ m/s}" /> and B at <InlineMath math="3\text{ m/s}" />. Average velocity is the slope of the straight line from the first point to the last. For A that line is the path itself, <InlineMath math="2\text{ m/s}" />. For B, the fuchsia chord skips the turn and gives only <InlineMath math="1\text{ m/s}" />, even though B covered <InlineMath math="9\text{ m}" />.

## The Sign of Average Velocity Shows Direction

If we choose the **rightward direction as positive**, displacement to the right is positive and displacement to the left is negative. That sign carries into average velocity.

For example, an object starts at <InlineMath math="x=8 \text{ m}" /> and ends at <InlineMath math="x=2 \text{ m}" /> in <InlineMath math="2 \text{ s}" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\Delta x=2-8=-6\text{ m}" />
  <BlockMath math="\bar{v}=\frac{-6}{2}=-3\text{ m/s}" />
</MathContainer>

A negative value does not mean the motion is "slower than zero." It means the average velocity points to the left relative to the axis we chose.

## A Loop Can Have Zero Average Velocity

If an object returns to its starting point, its displacement is <InlineMath math="0 \text{ m}" />. As a result, its average velocity is <InlineMath math="0 \text{ m/s}" /> even though its average speed can still be greater than zero.

For a loop trip, the path still has length. But the start-to-finish change in position becomes zero because the starting point and ending point are the same.

| Question | Quantity Used | Value Being Asked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How much path is covered each second? | Total distance | Average speed |
| How much does position change each second? | Displacement | Average velocity |

Before using a formula, check whether the question <Highlight>asks for distance or displacement</Highlight>. This choice determines whether to calculate average speed or average velocity.

Trip B goes <InlineMath math="6\text{ m}" /> right and <InlineMath math="3\text{ m}" /> back in <InlineMath math="3\text{ s}" />. Ask about the path and you divide the total distance, ask about the change in position and you divide the displacement. Zoom in on the two answers.

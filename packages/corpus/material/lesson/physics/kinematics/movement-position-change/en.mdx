## Comparing Positions at Different Times

An object is moving relative to a frame of reference when its position changes as time passes. One observation is therefore not enough. Compare its positions at several times to determine whether it moved. The **initial position** and **final position** summarize its net change over one interval.

If the initial position is <InlineMath math="x_{\text{initial}}" /> and the final position is <InlineMath math="x_{\text{final}}" />, the change in position is:

<BlockMath math="\Delta x=x_{\text{final}}-x_{\text{initial}}" />

The symbol <InlineMath math="\Delta x" /> is read as "delta x" and means displacement on this line. Its value can be positive, negative, or zero.

> Motion is identified from position changing with time. Displacement compares only the two chosen endpoints.

## Position Difference on a Straight Track

On a straight track with a fixed zero point, position change depends only on **final position minus initial position**. For example, if an object starts at <InlineMath math="20 \text{ m}" /> and ends at <InlineMath math="50 \text{ m}" />, the change in position is <InlineMath math="+30 \text{ m}" />.

If the initial and final positions are the same, the displacement is zero. Those two endpoints alone do not tell us whether the object stayed still or moved away and returned. Intermediate positions or the traveled path are needed to distinguish those cases.

Compare these two cases. Object A stays still throughout the observation. Object B moves straight from <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> to <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" />, then returns directly.

| Time | Position of A | Position of B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="0\text{ s}" /> | <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="3\text{ s}" /> | <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="6\text{ s}" /> | <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" /> |

Both have displacement <InlineMath math="20-20=0\text{ m}" />. A travels no distance, while B travels <InlineMath math="(50-20)+(50-20)=60\text{ m}" />. The intermediate position distinguishes the two cases.

Draw both cases as position against time, then follow each line from left to right. A stays flat at <InlineMath math="20\text{ m}" />, while B climbs to <InlineMath math="50\text{ m}" /> at <InlineMath math="3\text{ s}" /> and comes back. Both lines start and end at the same point, and that's exactly why both displacements are zero.

## Keep the Reference Consistent

When calculating a change in position, the initial and final positions must be read from the **same reference point**. Do not take the initial position from the store and the final position from another house, because that no longer describes the same motion in one reference frame.

| Reference | Initial position | Final position | Change in position |
| :-------- | :--------------- | :------------- | :----------------- |
| Store at <InlineMath math="0 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="+20 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="+50 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="+30 \text{ m}" /> |
| Rani's house at <InlineMath math="20 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="0 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="+30 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="+30 \text{ m}" /> |
| Naya's house at <InlineMath math="50 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="-30 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="0 \text{ m}" /> | <InlineMath math="+30 \text{ m}" /> |

The three rows have different initial and final position numbers, but the same change in position. The reference point shifts the coordinate numbers. The final-minus-initial difference still describes the motion from start to finish.

## Reading the Sign of Position Change

On a straight line, we may choose one direction as positive. In this example, **east is positive**.

- <InlineMath math="\Delta x>0" /> means the final position is east of the initial position.
- <InlineMath math="\Delta x<0" /> means the final position is west of the initial position.
- <InlineMath math="\Delta x=0" /> means the initial and final positions are the same.

A negative sign does not mean the motion is smaller or wrong. It only tells the direction of the position change.

Take both positions from the same reference, subtract <InlineMath math="\Delta x=x_\text{final}-x_\text{initial}" />, then read the sign. The same two positions give <InlineMath math="+30\text{ m}" /> going east and <InlineMath math="-30\text{ m}" /> coming back.

## Subtract the Final and Initial Positions

A student starts <InlineMath math="10 \text{ m}" /> east of a gate. A few seconds later, the student is <InlineMath math="40 \text{ m}" /> east of the gate. The change in position is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}
\Delta x
&=x_{\text{final}}-x_{\text{initial}} \\
&=40 \text{ m}-10 \text{ m} \\
&=+30 \text{ m}
\end{aligned}" />

The student's **displacement** is therefore <InlineMath math="30 \text{ m}" /> east. These two positions do not determine the distance traveled. If the student reversed direction during the trip, the distance exceeds <InlineMath math="30 \text{ m}" />.

Notice the order of subtraction. We always read final position minus initial position, then use <Highlight>the sign of the result</Highlight> to decide the direction of the position change.

The arrow below starts at the initial position and ends at the final one. Its length is the size of the displacement, and pointing east makes the sign positive.

## Blood and Clotting

Picture a busy city wired with a two-way delivery grid: the human **circulatory system** is closed and double, linking **heart, blood vessels, and blood** so oxygen and nutrients reach every cell quickly while carbon dioxide and urea leave. Blood combines **plasma** (roughly <InlineMath math="55\%" /> of volume) with three main cellular components.

| Component | Structure | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Erythrocytes | Biconcave discs with no nucleus | Carry oxygen with haemoglobin |
| Leucocytes | Have a nucleus, larger than erythrocytes | Fight pathogens via phagocytosis and antibodies |
| Platelets | Small cell fragments | Start blood clotting |
| Plasma | Pale yellow fluid, about <InlineMath math="90\%" /> water | Carries dissolved solutes and heat |

### Blood Clotting Steps

When a vessel tears, platelets release **thromboplastin**, which with calcium ions and vitamin K converts prothrombin to **thrombin**, turning soluble fibrinogen into a mesh of **fibrin** threads that seals the wound.

### Blood Group Testing

Blood compatibility depends on **antigens** on red cell surfaces and **antibodies** in plasma. Donor blood must never carry an antigen matched by the recipient's antibodies or the red cells will clump.

| Group | Antigen | Antibody | Can receive from |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A | A | Anti-B | A, O |
| B | B | Anti-A | B, O |
| AB | A and B | None | A, B, AB, O |
| O | None | Anti-A and anti-B | O only |

> Blood drops clump wherever an antiserum matches an antigen on the red cell surface, revealing both ABO and Rhesus status directly on the tile.

## Cardiac Cycle and Output

The human heart has **four chambers** paced by the **sinoatrial node**. The left ventricle owns the thickest wall to drive oxygenated blood body-wide via the **aorta**, while bicuspid, tricuspid, and semilunar valves enforce one-way flow.

> Stroke volume equals end-diastolic volume minus end-systolic volume, and multiplying stroke volume by heart rate gives cardiac output.

## Vessels and Blood Pressure

**Arteries always carry blood away from the heart** inside thick elastic walls, whereas **veins always carry blood back** at the lowest pressure and depend on valves against backflow.

| Vessel | Wall | Lumen | Valves | Pressure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Artery | Thick, muscular and elastic | Small | None | High and pulsing |
| Capillary | One cell thick | Very small | None | Low |
| Vein | Thin, less muscular | Large | Present | Lowest |

## Capillary Fluid Exchange

Across a **capillary bed**, blood pressure pushes fluid out between cells as **tissue fluid**, while plasma protein osmotic pull draws fluid back in. Excess fluid drains into **lymph capillaries** as lymph, passes through lymph nodes, and rejoins the blood at the subclavian veins.

> At the arterial end blood pressure exceeds oncotic pull so fluid filters out, while at the venous end lower pressure lets most return and lymph vessels collect the remainder.

## Animal Circulatory Systems

Different animal groups evolve circulatory architectures matching their oxygen demands, from open insect haemolymph to complete mammalian double circulation.

| Animal | Type of system | Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Insects | Open | Haemolymph bathes organs directly in the haemocoel |
| Fish | Closed, single circulation | Two-chambered heart, blood passes heart once per loop |
| Amphibians | Closed, incomplete double circulation | Three-chambered heart, partial mixing in the ventricle |
| Humans | Closed, complete double circulation | Four-chambered heart, oxygenated and deoxygenated stay separate |

**Complete double circulation** re-pressurises blood after the lungs so oxygen reaches tissues fast. Quick self-checks: a group B patient safely receives B or O blood, and the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood because arteries are defined strictly by flow away from the heart.

## Homeostasis Keeps the Internal Environment Stable

**Homeostasis** is keeping the body's internal environment, the tissue fluid, in a nearly constant state even when outside conditions change. Body temperature, blood glucose level and blood osmotic pressure are controlled by **negative feedback**: when a factor moves away from its normal value, the body triggers actions that bring it back. The kidneys control the water and salt content of the blood and remove urea. In the models here, you watch temperature and glucose return to normal, and follow filtrate through a nephron.

## Negative Feedback

Every negative feedback mechanism follows the same sequence: **change, receptor, control centre, effector, correction**. For example, when body temperature rises, receptors in the hypothalamus detect it, and the control centre sends impulses to the sweat glands and the blood vessels of the skin.

| Factor | If too high | If too low |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Body temperature | Sweating, vasodilation of skin arterioles | Shivering, vasoconstriction of skin arterioles |
| Blood glucose | Insulin secreted, glucose stored as glycogen | Glucagon secreted, glycogen broken down to glucose |
| Blood osmotic pressure | More ADH, more water reabsorbed | Less ADH, more dilute and larger volume of urine |

### Controlling Blood Glucose

## The Urinary System and the Nephron

The urinary system is made up of **the kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra**. Each kidney holds about a million **nephrons**. Urine forms in three stages:

- **Ultrafiltration**: high blood pressure in the glomerulus forces water, glucose, amino acids, salts and urea into the Bowman's capsule. Blood cells and plasma proteins are too large to be filtered.
- **Reabsorption**: in the proximal convoluted tubule, all the glucose and amino acids and most of the water and salts are reabsorbed into the blood.
- **Secretion**: substances such as hydrogen ions, drugs and excess urea are secreted into the distal convoluted tubule.

The kidneys filter about <InlineMath math="125\text{ cm}^3" /> of plasma a minute. This model follows glucose and urea through a nephron. Raise the plasma glucose level and see when glucose starts to appear in the urine.

### Example The Renal Threshold for Glucose

The tubules can reabsorb at most <InlineMath math="375\text{ mg}" /> of glucose a minute, and the filtration rate is <InlineMath math="125\text{ cm}^3" /> a minute. Calculate **the plasma glucose level** at which glucose starts to appear in the urine.

<BlockMath math="\text{Threshold} = \frac{375}{125} = 3.0\text{ mg cm}^{-3}" />

At the normal level of <InlineMath math="1.0\text{ mg cm}^{-3}" />, the filtered load is <InlineMath math="125 \times 1.0 = 125\text{ mg}" /> a minute, well below <InlineMath math="375" />, so all the glucose is reabsorbed. Check with the model: raise plasma glucose past <InlineMath math="3.0" /> and glucose starts to appear in the urine, as happens in uncontrolled diabetes.

### Example Concentrating Urea

The urea concentration is <InlineMath math="0.03\text{ g}" /> in every <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of plasma and <InlineMath math="2.0\text{ g}" /> in every <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> of urine. Calculate **the concentration factor**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{2.0}{0.03} = 67" />

Urea becomes <InlineMath math="67" /> times more concentrated because most of the water is reabsorbed while the urea is left behind. Check with the model: choose urea, and the factor reads <InlineMath math="66.7" />.

ADH makes the distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct more permeable to water, so more water is reabsorbed and the urine becomes concentrated and small in volume. ## Common Mistakes about Homeostasis

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | How to check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Insulin turns glycogen into glucose. | Insulin turns glucose into glycogen. Glucagon does the opposite. | Insulin lowers blood glucose. |
| Plasma proteins are filtered into the Bowman's capsule. | Plasma proteins are too large to pass through the glomerulus wall. | Protein in urine is a sign of kidney damage. |
| High ADH produces a lot of urine. | High ADH increases water reabsorption, so urine is small in volume and concentrated. | Antidiuretic means working against urine production. |
| Homeostasis keeps a value perfectly fixed. | The value changes slightly around normal and is constantly corrected. | Look at the temperature swings in the model. |

The first mistake is the most common one in blood glucose questions. **Insulin stores, glucagon releases**.

## Practice on Homeostasis and the Urinary System

These questions move from negative feedback to nephron calculations. **Name the effector** in every answer about negative feedback.

1. Explain how the body corrects a temperature that is too low on a cold day.

2. A patient's filtration rate falls to <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> a minute, and their tubules can still reabsorb <InlineMath math="375\text{ mg}" /> of glucose a minute. Calculate the new renal threshold for glucose.

3. The urea concentration is <InlineMath math="0.03\text{ g}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> in plasma and <InlineMath math="1.8\text{ g}" /> per <InlineMath math="100\text{ cm}^3" /> in urine. Calculate the urea concentration factor.

4. Explain why urine becomes concentrated and small in volume after playing football on a hot day.

### Solutions

**Solution 1**: Receptors in the skin and hypothalamus detect the low temperature. **Skeletal muscles** shiver to produce heat, **skin arterioles** constrict so less heat is lost, and the hair erector muscles contract. Body temperature rises back to <InlineMath math="37^\circ\text{C}" />.

**Solution 2**: Divide the maximum reabsorption by the filtration rate:

<BlockMath math="\frac{375}{100} = 3.75\text{ mg cm}^{-3}" />

Check: the threshold rises from <InlineMath math="3.0" /> to <InlineMath math="3.75" />, because the filtered glucose load is now smaller for the same plasma level.

**Solution 3**: Divide the concentration in urine by the concentration in plasma:

<BlockMath math="\frac{1.8}{0.03} = 60" />

Check: <InlineMath math="0.03 \times 60 = 1.8" />.

**Solution 4**: The body loses a lot of water as sweat, so **blood osmotic pressure rises**. The hypothalamus detects this and the pituitary secretes more **ADH**. The collecting ducts become more permeable, more water is reabsorbed, and the urine produced is small in volume and concentrated.

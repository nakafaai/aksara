## Food Energy Values

A **balanced diet** supplies seven nutrient classes: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, vitamins, mineral salts, fibre, and water. Because carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids are large insoluble polymers that cannot cross gut walls, **digestion** breaks them down into glucose, amino acids, fatty acids, and glycerol for absorption. Per gram oxidised, **lipids release more than double the energy** of carbohydrates or proteins.

## Digestive Enzymes at Work

Digestion pairs **mechanical breakdown** (chewing and peristalsis) with **chemical hydrolysis** by specific enzymes along the gut. **Bile contains no enzymes**, instead emulsifying lipids into fine droplets for lipase while neutralising stomach acid.

| Place | Secretion | Enzymes | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mouth | Saliva | Salivary amylase | Starch to maltose |
| Stomach | Gastric juice and hydrochloric acid | Pepsin, rennin | Protein to polypeptides, caseinogen to casein |
| Duodenum | Pancreatic juice and bile | Amylase, trypsin, lipase | Breaks down starch, polypeptides, and lipids |
| Ileum | Intestinal juice | Maltase, sucrase, lactase, erepsin, lipase | Disaccharides to monosaccharides, peptides to amino acids |

### Pancreatic Secretion

Picture a dispatch warehouse inside a pancreatic cell: intracellular enzymes assemble on free ribosomes, whereas **extracellular digestive enzymes like pancreatic amylase** are built on rough endoplasmic reticulum, packaged in the Golgi apparatus, and exported by **exocytosis**.

> Extracellular enzymes travel from bound ribosomes through rough endoplasmic reticulum, transport vesicles, Golgi apparatus, and secretory vesicles before exocytosis releases them into the gut.

### Active Site Specificity

Enzymes act **with strict specificity** because the three-dimensional **active site** accepts only a complementary substrate under lock-and-key or induced-fit binding, releasing products while leaving the enzyme **unchanged and reusable**.

> Only a substrate complementary to the active site forms an enzyme-substrate complex, splits with water, and frees the unchanged enzyme to catalyse again.

### Heat and pH Denaturation

Every enzyme operates fastest at its **optimum temperature** and **optimum pH**. Cooling merely slows effective collisions (reversible upon warming), whereas **excess heat or extreme pH permanently distorts the active site** through **denaturation**.

> Low temperature only slows enzyme collisions without damaging shape, whereas overheating and extreme pH deform the active site irreversibly.

## Saturation and Lactase

When substrate is scarce, adding more substrate speeds up catalysis directly. Once **every active site is busy**, the rate curve levels off and **enzyme concentration becomes the limiting factor**.

> Once all active sites stay saturated, extra substrate cannot raise reaction speed until more enzyme molecules are supplied.

## Absorption and Assimilation

The ileum wall folds into countless **villi** lined with **microvilli** for vast surface area, has one-cell-thick epithelium for short diffusion distance, and houses rich **blood capillaries** plus a central **lacteal**.

- Glucose and amino acids enter blood capillaries and travel via the **hepatic portal vein** to the liver.
- Fatty acids and glycerol enter the **lacteal** and lymphatic vessels before reaching the blood.
- Remaining water and minerals are absorbed in the **colon**, while indigestible fibre forms faeces.

During **assimilation**, cells put absorbed nutrients to work: the liver stores excess glucose as glycogen, amino acids build new proteins, and lipids form membranes or energy stores.

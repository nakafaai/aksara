## Coordination Links a Stimulus to a Response

**Coordination** is the process that links a stimulus to a suitable response. Every response follows the same sequence: **stimulus, receptor, integration centre, effector, response**. Humans have two coordination systems. The **nervous system** sends fast electrical impulses along neurones, while the **endocrine system** secretes hormones into the blood, which act more slowly but for longer. In the models here, you test your reaction time, compare impulse speeds in axons and compare a nervous response with a hormonal one.

## The Human Nervous System

The nervous system has two parts. The **central nervous system** is the brain and spinal cord, and the **peripheral nervous system** is the nerves that link the central nervous system with the rest of the body. Its basic unit is the **neurone**.

| Neurone | Direction of impulse | Location |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Afferent neurone | From receptor to the central nervous system | Cell body outside the spinal cord |
| Interneurone | Within the central nervous system | Brain and spinal cord |
| Efferent neurone | From the central nervous system to an effector | Cell body inside the spinal cord |

Every neurone has **dendrites** that receive impulses, a **cell body** that holds the nucleus and an **axon** that carries impulses away. Most axons are wrapped in a **myelin sheath** that insulates the axon, so the impulse jumps from one node of Ranvier to the next.

## Synapses

A **synapse** is the junction between two neurones. When an impulse reaches the end of an axon, the synaptic knob releases a **neurotransmitter** such as acetylcholine into the synaptic cleft. The neurotransmitter diffuses across the cleft and binds to receptors on the next neurone, triggering a new impulse. Neurotransmitter is released from one side only, so **impulses travel in one direction**.

### The Reflex Arc

In the knee jerk **reflex arc**, a tap on the tendon stretches the thigh muscle. Stretch receptors send impulses along an **afferent neurone** to the spinal cord. The impulse passes straight to an **efferent neurone**, and the thigh muscle contracts so the leg kicks forward. The reflex that pulls a hand away from a hot object also involves an **interneurone** in the spinal cord.

## The Endocrine System

**Endocrine glands** have no ducts and secrete **hormones** straight into the blood. The blood carries hormones to their target organs.

| Gland | Hormone | Main effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pituitary | Growth hormone, ADH, FSH, LH | Controls growth and other glands |
| Thyroid | Thyroxine | Raises the metabolic rate |
| Adrenal | Adrenaline | Prepares the body for fight or flight |
| Pancreas | Insulin, glucagon | Controls blood glucose level |
| Ovaries and testes | Oestrogen, progesterone, testosterone | Control secondary sexual characteristics |

Nervous and hormonal responses differ in timing. This model compares a **nervous response**, short and sharp, with a **hormonal response**, slow but long-lasting, after a single stimulus.

## Health Issues of the Coordination System

Drug and alcohol abuse disrupt the transmission of impulses at synapses. Alcohol lengthens reaction time, so a drunk driver needs a longer distance to stop. **Alzheimer's disease** damages brain neurones and affects memory, while **Parkinson's disease** happens when neurones that make dopamine die and movement becomes shaky.

## Common Mistakes about Coordination

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | How to check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Every action is controlled by the brain. | Reflex actions are processed by the spinal cord without waiting for the brain. | Ask whether you were aware of the action first. |
| Impulses can travel both ways across a synapse. | Neurotransmitter is released only by the presynaptic neurone. | Find where the synaptic knob is. |
| Hormones are secreted through ducts. | Endocrine glands have no ducts and secrete straight into the blood. | Tell endocrine glands apart from exocrine glands. |
| Hormonal responses are faster than nervous ones. | Hormones have to be carried by the blood, so the response is slower but lasts longer. | Compare the peaks of the two curves in the model. |

The first mistake is the most common one in reflex arc questions. **Reflexes pass through the spinal cord**, and the brain only becomes aware afterwards.

## Practice on Coordination and Response

These questions move from neurones to reflexes and hormones. **Name each neurone** in answers about the path of an impulse.

1. Write the sequence of the impulse's path in the reflex that pulls a hand away from a hot object.

2. A ruler falls <InlineMath math="12\text{ cm}" /> before it's caught. Calculate the reaction time.

3. An impulse travels <InlineMath math="0.80\text{ m}" /> along a myelinated axon at <InlineMath math="80\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. Calculate the time taken in milliseconds.

4. Explain why adrenaline suits an emergency even though it acts more slowly than a nerve impulse.

### Solutions

**Solution 1**: The hot object stimulates **receptors** in the skin. The impulse travels along an **afferent neurone** to the spinal cord, then an **interneurone**, then an **efferent neurone** to the biceps muscle, the effector that contracts and pulls the hand away.

**Solution 2**: Convert the distance to metres, then use <InlineMath math="t = \sqrt{2d/g}" />:

<BlockMath math="t = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 0.12}{9.8}} = \sqrt{0.0245} = 0.16\text{ s}" />

Check with the model: at <InlineMath math="c = 2.4" />, it reads <InlineMath math="156" /> milliseconds.

**Solution 3**: Divide the distance by the speed:

<BlockMath math="t = \frac{0.80}{80} = 0.010\text{ s} = 10\text{ ms}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="80 \times 0.010 = 0.80\text{ m}" />.

**Solution 4**: The blood carries adrenaline to **many organs at once**: the heart beats faster, the liver releases glucose and the airways widen. Its effect is also **long-lasting**, so the body stays ready for as long as the emergency goes on.

## Nervous System and Neurones

Picture the body like an orchestra answering every change in one fixed chain: **stimulus, receptor, integration centre, effector, response**. The **nervous system** fires rapid electrical impulses across the brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nerves through **neurones**, while the **endocrine system** sends blood-borne hormones that act more slowly yet last far longer.

| Neurone | Direction of impulse | Location |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Afferent neurone | From receptor to central nervous system | Cell body outside spinal cord |
| Interneurone | Within central nervous system | Brain and spinal cord |
| Efferent neurone | From central nervous system to effector | Cell body inside spinal cord |

Each neurone owns **dendrites** receiving signals, a nucleated **cell body**, and an **axon** carrying impulses outward. Many axons carry an insulating **myelin sheath** so impulses leap rapidly from node to node of Ranvier.

### Nerve Impulse Speed

**Myelinated axons transmit impulses far faster** because the signal jumps between nodes of Ranvier instead of creeping continuously along the membrane.

> A myelin sheath insulates the axon so impulses leap across nodes of Ranvier and cover one metre within milliseconds, over ten times faster than an unmyelinated fibre.

### Synapses and Drugs

A **synapse** separates one neurone from the next across a narrow cleft. Arriving impulses trigger presynaptic vesicles to release a **neurotransmitter** (such as acetylcholine) that diffuses across the cleft, binds postsynaptic receptors, and is then broken down by enzymes so the signal ends cleanly.

> Because neurotransmitter is released only from the presynaptic knob while receptors sit only on the postsynaptic membrane, impulses cross a synapse strictly one way.

## Reflex Arc and Reaction Time

A **voluntary action** like braking requires conscious brain processing and takes longer, whereas a **reflex action** like the knee jerk or pulling away from a hot stove shortcuts via a spinal **reflex arc** through afferent neurone, interneurone, and efferent neurone.

### Spinal Reflex Pathways

In a **withdrawal reflex**, impulses enter the dorsal root, cross a spinal **interneurone** in grey matter, and exit the ventral root to muscles (three neurones and two synapses), whereas the **knee-jerk reflex** connects sensory and motor neurones directly (two neurones and one synapse).

> A spinal reflex arc shortcuts straight through the grey matter of the spinal cord so muscles protect the body before conscious pain awareness reaches the brain.

## Endocrine Glands and Hormones

**Endocrine glands** are ductless and secrete **hormones** straight into the bloodstream to reach target organs.

| Gland | Hormone | Main effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pituitary | Growth hormone, ADH, FSH, LH | Controls growth and other glands |
| Thyroid | Thyroxine | Raises metabolic rate |
| Adrenal | Adrenaline | Prepares body for fight or flight |
| Pancreas | Insulin, glucagon | Controls blood glucose level |
| Ovaries and testes | Oestrogen, progesterone, testosterone | Control secondary sexual traits |

## Coordination Health Issues

Alcohol and illicit drugs disrupt synaptic transmission and lengthen driver reaction time. **Alzheimer's disease** damages brain neurones and impairs memory, while **Parkinson's disease** occurs when dopamine-producing neurones die and movements become shaky.

Quick self-checks: pulling a hand from a hot object runs from skin receptors via an afferent neurone, a spinal interneurone, and an efferent neurone to the biceps before conscious awareness arrives.

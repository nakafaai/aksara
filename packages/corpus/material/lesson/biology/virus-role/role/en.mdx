## Virus Effects and Uses

A virus can damage tissue, change microbial populations, or deliver genetic material to selected cells. To understand which effect occurs, examine the host or target cell, how the virus reproduces or is modified, and how tightly the process is controlled.

### Disease and Ecological Effects

A **host** is the organism or cell that a virus uses to reproduce. Viral replication can damage host cells or provoke a harmful immune response. Viruses that infect bacteria can also change microbial populations and nutrient cycling in an ecosystem.

Viruses can cause disease, infect many kinds of hosts, and also serve as tools in modern biology.

Marine phages affect bacterial mortality, host metabolism, nutrient cycling, and horizontal gene transfer.

### Comparing Three Virus Roles

Viral surface structures bind compatible cells, and virus particles carry genetic material. During infection, these properties help a virus enter cells and reproduce. Researchers can modify a virus so it carries selected genetic material to target cells under controlled conditions. A virus modified for this task is called a **viral vector**.

| Context | Evidence to examine | How to assess the effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disease | Host cells are damaged or body responses are disrupted | The effect depends on the tissue affected and the route of infection |
| Ecology | Microbial populations change | Viruses can change bacterial abundance, nutrient cycling, and horizontal gene transfer |
| Biotechnology | Genetic material is delivered to selected cells | A viral vector should deliver its cargo mainly to the intended target cells, and testing must measure and limit off-target delivery |

## Viruses Use Different Transmission Routes

Influenza, dengue, and rabies spread through different biological routes. The disease name alone does not identify the preventive measure. First determine whether the virus spreads through respiratory particles, a carrier such as a mosquito, or another route.

### The Route Must Be Identified Separately

Viruses cause diseases in humans, animals, and plants. Human examples include flu, polio, hepatitis, and dengue. In other animals, viruses can cause rabies or certain poultry diseases. In plants, viruses may cause mosaic leaves, stunted growth, or reduced harvest.

To understand a viral disease, check its transmission route and the tissue it affects. Dengue viruses are transmitted mainly through bites from infected Aedes mosquitoes. Respiratory viruses can spread when someone inhales respiratory particles released by an infected person. Plant viruses can move through insect carriers, plant wounds, or infected planting material.

### The Transmission Route Guides Prevention

Respiratory particles, mosquito bites, and specific body fluids are different transmission routes. Selecting prevention requires the route, target cells, and conditions that support transmission.

## Controlled Uses of Viruses

Some viruses can enter particular cells and carry genetic material into them. During an uncontrolled natural infection, this ability can damage the host. To make a viral vector, researchers modify the virus and test which cells it enters and what genetic material it delivers.

Virus-derived systems are used in genetic research, gene therapy, vaccine development, and targeted pest control. Baculoviruses are insect-specific viruses used in several commercial viral biopesticides.

In gene therapy, viruses can be modified into controlled delivery tools by removing disease-causing viral genes and adding a treatment gene to form a vector. [NIH/NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/genetic-therapies/types)

Safety depends on design and testing. Researchers measure which cells receive the vector, whether it can replicate, and whether it can spread beyond the intended tissue.

> Assess a virus by its host, the observed effect, and the degree of control.

## How to Assess a Virus Effect

A virus that damages tissue causes a disease effect. A virus that changes microbial populations has an ecological effect. A virus that researchers modify to carry a gene into selected cells serves as a biotechnology vector. In each case, examine the host or target cell, the observed change, and the way the virus is controlled.

## The Host Relationship Determines the Effect

A virus's effect depends on the host, the environment, how the virus replicates, and the degree of control. These factors distinguish an infection that damages tissue from a deliberately designed and controlled use, such as a viral vector.

### The Host Relationship Determines the Outcome

Viruses can cause disease, affect ecological processes, or serve as controlled tools in biotechnology. What a virus does depends on its relationship with the **host**, the organism or cell the virus uses to reproduce.

If viral replication damages cells or provokes harmful host responses, disease can develop. If a virus infects bacteria in the environment, microbial populations and nutrient cycling can shift. If viral delivery is redesigned and controlled in a laboratory, a virus-derived **vector** can carry genetic material into target cells.

[OpenStax Biology 2e](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/21-2-virus-infections-and-hosts) discusses how viruses cause disease, infect many kinds of hosts, and are used in modern biology.

Marine phages affect bacterial mortality, host metabolism, nutrient cycling, and horizontal gene transfer. A review in [Nature Microbiology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-018-0166-y) summarizes the evidence for these ecological effects.

### Target and Control Determine the Effect

Viral surface structures can bind cells, and a virus particle can carry genetic material. The effect of those properties depends on the target, risk, and degree of control. An uncontrolled infection can damage tissue, while a modified viral vector can deliver a selected gene to target cells.

| Context | Evidence to examine | How to assess the effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disease | Host cells are damaged or body responses are disrupted | The effect depends on the tissue affected and the route of infection |
| Ecology | Microbial populations change | Viruses can shape relationships among microscopic organisms |
| Biotechnology | Genetic delivery is controlled | Benefit appears only when target and risk are limited |

## Viruses Use Different Transmission Routes

Influenza, dengue, and rabies spread through different biological routes. The disease name alone does not identify the preventive measure. First determine whether the virus spreads through respiratory particles, a vector, or another route.

### The Route Must Be Identified Separately

Viral diseases can affect humans, animals, and plants. In humans, viruses can be involved in flu, polio, hepatitis, or dengue fever. In animals, viruses can cause rabies or certain poultry diseases. In plants, viruses may cause mosaic leaves, stunted growth, or reduced harvest.

To understand a viral disease, check its entry route and the tissue it affects. Dengue viruses are transmitted mainly through bites from infected Aedes mosquitoes. Respiratory viruses can spread through respiratory particles in shared air. Plant viruses can move through insect vectors, plant wounds, or infected planting material.

### The Entry Route Determines Prevention

Respiratory particles, mosquito bites, and specific body fluids are different transmission routes. Selecting prevention requires the route, target cells, and conditions that support transmission.

## Controlled Uses of Viruses

Some viruses bind particular cell receptors and carry genetic material into cells. During an uncontrolled infection, these properties can damage the host. In a designed viral vector, researchers modify and test them for a specific task.

Virus-derived systems are used in genetic research, gene therapy, vaccine development, and targeted pest control. Baculoviruses are insect-specific viruses used in several commercial viral biopesticides. A review in [NCBI PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4452904/) summarizes their use.

In gene therapy, viruses are modified into controlled delivery tools. [NIH/NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/genetic-therapies/types) explains that disease-causing viral genes are often removed and a treatment gene is added to make a vector.

Control determines whether a viral vector can be useful. It must be designed, tested, and limited to its target. Uncontrolled reproduction or movement between cells creates risk.

> Assess a virus by its host, the observed effect, and the degree of control.

## Factors That Determine the Role of a Virus

Name the relationship between the virus and its host first. A virus that damages tissue is discussed as a disease agent. A virus that affects microbes is discussed in ecology. A virus that carries genes under control is discussed in biotechnology.

To assess a virus's effect, examine the host or target cell, the change it causes, and how the virus is controlled.

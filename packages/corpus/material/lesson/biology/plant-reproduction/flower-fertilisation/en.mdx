## Flowers Make Seeds through Double Fertilisation

**Sexual reproduction in flowering plants** happens inside the flower. The anther makes **pollen grains** that carry the male gametes, and each ovule inside the ovary makes an **embryo sac** that holds the egg cell.

> Double fertilisation pairs one male gamete with the egg cell to form a diploid zygote and the second with two polar nuclei to build triploid endosperm.

After pollen lands on the stigma, a pollen tube grows down to an ovule and carries two male gametes. One male gamete fuses with the egg cell to form a **diploid zygote**. The second male gamete fuses with the two polar nuclei to form a **triploid endosperm nucleus**.

That's **double fertilisation**. The ovule then becomes a seed and the ovary becomes a fruit. In these notes you follow a pollen tube all the way to fertilisation, count the chromosomes in each structure and work out how much pollen one fruit needs.

## The Structure of a Flower and What Each Part Does

A complete flower has four rings of parts. The male part is the **stamen** and the female part is the **pistil**, also called the carpel.

| Part | Structure | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Stamen | Anther | Makes pollen grains |
| Stamen | Filament | Holds up the anther so pollen spreads easily |
| Pistil | Stigma | Sticky surface that receives pollen |
| Pistil | Style | Path of the pollen tube from the stigma to the ovary |
| Pistil | Ovary | Holds the ovules and develops into the fruit |
| Pistil | Ovule | Holds the embryo sac and develops into the seed |
| Outer ring | Petal | Attracts insect pollinators with colour and scent |
| Outer ring | Sepal | Protects the flower while it's still a bud |

Insect-pollinated flowers usually have coloured petals, nectar and sticky pollen. Wind-pollinated flowers have small petals, anthers that hang outside the flower and feathery stigmas that **catch light pollen** from the air.

### Pollen and the Embryo Sac Form through Meiosis and Mitosis

Inside the anther, each diploid **pollen mother cell** goes through meiosis and makes a tetrad of four haploid microspores. The nucleus of each microspore divides once by mitosis into a **tube nucleus** and a **generative nucleus**, and the microspore becomes a thick-walled pollen grain. The generative nucleus divides once more by mitosis to make two male gametes.

Inside the ovule, the diploid **megaspore mother cell** goes through meiosis and makes four haploid megaspores. Three of them degenerate. The nucleus of the remaining megaspore divides **three times by mitosis** and makes eight haploid nuclei in the embryo sac: one egg cell, two synergids, three antipodal cells and two polar nuclei. ### Pollination Carries Pollen to the Stigma

**Pollination** is the transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma. **Self-pollination** happens within one flower or between flowers on the same plant. **Cross-pollination** happens between flowers on two different plants of the same species, so the offspring inherit a mix of genes from two parents. Insects, birds, wind and water are pollinating agents.

The stigma secretes a sucrose solution. A pollen grain takes up the solution and germinates, and **the pollen tube grows down the style** towards the ovary. The tube nucleus sits at the tip of the tube and the generative nucleus follows behind it. The tube enters the ovule through the **micropyle**. How long that takes depends on the length of the style and how fast the tube grows.

## Double Fertilisation Makes a Zygote and an Endosperm

While the pollen tube grows, the generative nucleus divides into two male gametes. When the tube reaches the embryo sac, the tube nucleus degenerates and **the two male gametes are released**. One male gamete fertilises the egg cell and makes a <InlineMath math="2n" /> zygote.

The second male gamete fuses with the two polar nuclei and makes a <InlineMath math="3n" /> endosperm nucleus. The model below uses maize, whose body cells have <InlineMath math="2n = 20" /> chromosomes. Move the stage from pollination to fertilisation.

## The Ovule Becomes the Seed and the Ovary Becomes the Fruit

After fertilisation, every part of the ovule and ovary changes. **The seed develops from the ovule**, while **the fruit develops from the ovary**. The synergids and antipodal cells degenerate, and the petals and stamens usually wither and fall.

| Before fertilisation | After fertilisation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Zygote | Embryo with plumule, radicle and cotyledons |
| Triploid endosperm nucleus | Endosperm, the food store |
| Integuments | Testa, the seed coat |
| Micropyle | Small pore in the testa that lets water in during germination |
| Ovule | Seed |
| Ovary wall | Pericarp, the wall of the fruit |
| Ovary | Fruit |

In maize, the endosperm stays as the food store in the ripe seed. In peas, the embryo absorbs the endosperm while the seed develops, so **the food is stored in the cotyledons**. The second model uses peas, <InlineMath math="2n = 14" />, with an ovary that holds <InlineMath math="8" /> ovules.

**Example 4.** How many pollen grains and male gametes are needed so that all <InlineMath math="8" /> ovules become seeds?

<BlockMath math="\text{pollen grains} = 8 \times 1 = 8, \quad \text{male gametes} = 8 \times 2 = 16" />

Each ovule receives one pollen tube, and each tube carries two male gametes, both used in double fertilisation. Check with the model: the pollen grain reading is <InlineMath math="8" />, and <InlineMath math="16 \div 8 = 2" /> male gametes per ovule.

Seeds help a plant survive. **The testa protects the embryo**, the food store in the endosperm or cotyledons supplies energy during germination, and a seed can stay dormant until conditions suit it. Seeds also spread away from the parent, so the young plants don't compete with it for light and water. Every seed from sexual reproduction carries a new mix of genes, so the species copes better when its surroundings change.

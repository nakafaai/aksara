## Leaf Lamina Anatomy

Think of a leaf as a paper-thin solar kitchen dotted with thousands of tiny breathing vents. It runs **gaseous exchange**, **transpiration**, and **photosynthesis** together using a broad thin lamina, chloroplast-packed mesophyll, air spaces, and stomata.

> Photosynthesis is how a plant turns sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water into glucose sugar while releasing oxygen into the air.

<BlockMath math="6\mathrm{CO_2} + 12\mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow[\text{chlorophyll}]{\text{light}} \mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} + 6\mathrm{O_2} + 6\mathrm{H_2O}" />

From top to bottom, a leaf cross-section stacks six specialised tissue layers built for light capture and gas diffusion.

| Structure | Features | Link to function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cuticle | Transparent waxy coat | Cuts water loss while letting light through |
| Upper epidermis | Single clear layer without chloroplasts | Transmits light into the mesophyll |
| Palisade mesophyll | Tall packed cells full of chloroplasts | Main site of photosynthesis on the bright side |
| Spongy mesophyll | Loose cells with wide air spaces | Rapid pathway for carbon dioxide and oxygen |
| Vascular bundle | Xylem and phloem in veins | Xylem brings water, phloem exports sucrose |
| Lower epidermis | Dense stomata flanked by guard cells | Main gateway for gas exchange and transpiration |

## Stomata and Transpiration

In daylight, photosynthesis outpaces respiration so carbon dioxide enters and oxygen leaves, while at night only respiration runs. Remember: plants respire nonstop, never only at night.

Guard cells open a stoma when **turgid**, never when flaccid. In light, they actively pump in **potassium ions** and build glucose, draw water by osmosis, and bow outward because their outer walls are thinner. In darkness or drought triggered by **abscisic acid**, potassium ions exit, cells go flaccid, and the pore closes.

> Stomata are not rigid hinged doors but living water valves: turgid guard cells bend apart to open the pore, while flaccid cells collapse flat to seal it.

Water vapour escaping through stomata drives **transpiration**, creating **transpiration pull** that hoists water up the xylem while cooling the leaf.

## Leaf Rate Calculations

Apply potometer geometry, the inverse-square law, and compensation balance to three checks.

1. In a potometer of radius <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ mm}" />, the bubble travels <InlineMath math="9.0\text{ cm}" /> in <InlineMath math="5" /> minutes. Find hourly water uptake. **Answer**: use radius <InlineMath math="0.05\text{ cm}" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="V = \pi(0.05)^2 \times 9.0 = 0.0707\text{ cm}^3" />
<BlockMath math="\text{Rate} = 0.0707 \times \frac{60}{5} = 0.848\text{ cm}^3\text{ an hour}" />
</MathContainer>

2. A lamp shifts from <InlineMath math="30\text{ cm}" /> to <InlineMath math="15\text{ cm}" /> from Hydrilla. By what factor does intensity rise? **Answer**: halving distance multiplies light by <InlineMath math="4" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{I_{15}}{I_{30}} = \left(\frac{30}{15}\right)^2 = 4" />

3. If warming raises respiration to <InlineMath math="3" />, find the new compensation point. **Answer**: faster respiration demands brighter light:

<BlockMath math="\frac{10I}{I + 4} = 3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad I = \frac{12}{7} = 1.71" />

## The Cell as the Basic Unit of Life

A **cell** is the basic unit of structure and function in every organism. Every cell is bounded by a **plasma membrane**, filled with **cytoplasm**, and holds genetic material. In animal and plant cells, that genetic material sits inside a **nucleus**. Organelles in the cytoplasm share out the cell's work, and cells of the same kind group together into **tissues, organs and systems**. In the models here, you cut a cell open to see its organelles, look at cheek and onion cells under a microscope, and compare the size of a cell with a bacterium.

Cell sizes are measured in micrometres, <InlineMath math="1\ \mu\text{m} = 10^{-3}\text{ mm}" />. A human cheek cell is about <InlineMath math="60\ \mu\text{m}" /> long, so you need a microscope to see it.

## Organelles of Animal and Plant Cells

Animal and plant cells both have a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, a nucleus, **mitochondria**, **ribosomes**, **endoplasmic reticulum**, a **Golgi apparatus** and lysosomes. This table links each organelle to its function. Learn the function together with the structure, because exam questions often ask which organelle is plentiful in a particular cell.

| Organelle | Function | Found in |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nucleus | Controls all cell activities and stores genetic material | Animal and plant |
| Mitochondrion | Site of cellular respiration that releases energy | Animal and plant |
| Ribosome | Site of protein synthesis | Animal and plant |
| Golgi apparatus | Processes, packages and transports proteins | Animal and plant |
| Chloroplast | Site of photosynthesis | Plant only |
| Cell wall | Gives support and a fixed shape | Plant only |
| Centriole | Forms spindle fibres during cell division | Animal only |

An active cell needs a lot of energy, so muscle cells and sperm cells have **many mitochondria**. Cells that secrete enzymes, such as pancreatic cells, have many ribosomes and a large Golgi apparatus.

### Example Magnification and Real Size

An onion epidermal cell is drawn <InlineMath math="45\text{ mm}" /> long at a magnification of <InlineMath math="\times 300" />. Find **the real size of the cell** in micrometres.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Real size} = \frac{\text{image size}}{\text{magnification}} = \frac{45}{300} = 0.15\text{ mm}" />
<BlockMath math="0.15\text{ mm} \times 1{,}000 = 150\ \mu\text{m}" />
</MathContainer>

Check it the other way round: <InlineMath math="0.15 \times 300 = 45\text{ mm}" />. A size of <InlineMath math="150\ \mu\text{m}" /> is reasonable for an elongated plant cell.

## Life Processes in Unicellular Organisms

A **unicellular organism** is a single cell, but that one cell carries out every life process: moving, feeding, respiring, reproducing and responding to stimuli. *Amoeba* moves with false feet, while bacteria reproduce by **binary fission**: one cell divides into two identical cells. ## Cell Organisation and Specialised Cells

In a **multicellular organism**, cells are arranged in levels: **cell, tissue, organ, system and organism**. A tissue is a group of cells with the same structure and function, an organ is made of several kinds of tissue, and a system is several organs working together.

Every specialised cell has a shape that suits its job. A **red blood cell** is a biconcave disc with no nucleus, so it can carry more oxygen. A **root hair cell** is long and thin to increase its surface area for absorbing water. A smaller or thinner cell has a larger **surface area to volume ratio**. For a cube of side <InlineMath math="L" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{\text{SA}}{\text{V}} = \frac{6L^2}{L^3} = \frac{6}{L}" />

## Common Mistakes about Cells

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | How to check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Plant cells have no mitochondria. | Plant cells respire too, so they have mitochondria. | Chloroplasts are an addition, not a replacement. |
| Real size equals image size times magnification. | Real size is image size divided by magnification. | The real size has to be smaller than the image. |
| The cell wall and the plasma membrane are the same structure. | The cell wall is outside and rigid, the plasma membrane inside and partially permeable. | Animal cells have a membrane but no wall. |
| A tissue is a group of organs. | Organs are made of tissues, not the other way round. | Follow the order cell, tissue, organ, system. |

The second mistake is the most common one in microscope questions. **Divide, don't multiply**, when you look for the real size.

## Practice on Cell Biology

These questions move from microscope calculations to explaining specialised cells. **Convert millimetres to micrometres** at the end of each calculation.

1. A <InlineMath math="\times 10" /> eyepiece is used with a <InlineMath math="\times 40" /> objective. Calculate the total magnification.

2. Name three structures found in plant cells but not in animal cells.

3. An image of a cheek cell <InlineMath math="24\text{ mm}" /> long is seen at a magnification of <InlineMath math="\times 400" />. Calculate the real length of the cell in micrometres.

4. Explain why a root hair cell is long and thin.

### Solutions

**Solution 1**: Multiply the two magnifications:

<BlockMath math="10 \times 40 = 400" />

The total magnification is <InlineMath math="\times 400" />.

**Solution 2**: The **cell wall**, **chloroplasts** and a **large vacuole** are found only in plant cells. Animal cells may have small vacuoles, but not a large central one.

**Solution 3**: Divide the image size by the magnification:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{24}{400} = 0.060\text{ mm}" />
<BlockMath math="0.060 \times 1{,}000 = 60\ \mu\text{m}" />
</MathContainer>

Check: <InlineMath math="60\ \mu\text{m}" /> matches the usual size of a cheek cell.

**Solution 4**: A long, thin shape gives the root hair cell **a large surface area to volume ratio**. More surface touches the soil water, so water and mineral ions are absorbed faster. The cell also has no chloroplasts because it sits in dark soil.

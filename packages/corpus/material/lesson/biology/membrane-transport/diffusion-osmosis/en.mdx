## Passive and Active Routes

Every living cell relies on its **plasma membrane**, a partially permeable phospholipid bilayer described by the **fluid mosaic model**, to decide what enters and what stays out. Small nonpolar gases like oxygen cross the lipid bilayer freely, whereas ions and polar sugars require embedded channel or carrier proteins.

Because the hydrophobic core repels charged particles, cells match every solute to one of four distinct transport pathways:

| Process | Gradient direction | ATP energy | Membrane protein |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Simple diffusion | Down gradient | No | None |
| Facilitated diffusion | Down gradient | No | Channel or carrier |
| Osmosis | Water down water potential | No | Bilayer or aquaporins |
| Active transport | Against gradient | Yes | Carrier pump |

### Carrier Saturation

**Simple diffusion** rises linearly as concentration increases because molecules slip straight between phospholipids. **Facilitated diffusion** is still passive and needs zero ATP, yet its rate flattens once every carrier protein is occupied. ### Active Transport Pumps

When a root hair cell absorbs mineral ions from dilute soil or a neuron pumps potassium inward, solutes must travel uphill from low to high concentration. **Active transport** drives specific carrier pumps using energy released by splitting **ATP** into ADP.

> Facilitated diffusion never spends ATP because solutes still move downhill, whereas active transport spends ATP to push solutes uphill against the gradient.

## Osmosis and Tonicity

**Osmosis** is the net movement of water molecules alone from a dilute **hypotonic** solution to a concentrated **hypertonic** solution across a partially permeable membrane. When both sides share equal solute concentration, the solution is **isotonic** and net water flow equals zero.

> Always follow the water, not the solute: water flows out of dilute hypotonic solutions and into concentrated hypertonic solutions.

In a standard potato-strip investigation, six identical strips are soaked in sucrose solutions from pure water up to <InlineMath math="0.5\text{ mol dm}^{-3}" />. Strips gain mass in hypotonic tubes, lose mass in hypertonic tubes, and show zero mass change where sucrose matches the cell sap.

## Plant and Animal Cells

Animal cells lack a rigid cellulose wall, so a red blood cell swells and bursts (**haemolysis**) in pure water and shrivels (**crenation**) in concentrated salt solution.

Plant cells behave differently because their cellulose wall resists bursting. In pure water they turn firm and **turgid**, whereas in hypertonic salt solution the vacuole shrinks and the plasma membrane pulls away from the wall (**plasmolysis**).

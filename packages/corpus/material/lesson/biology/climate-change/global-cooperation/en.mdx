## Greenhouse Gases Spread Beyond National Borders

<Highlight>Long-lived greenhouse gases become well mixed in the atmosphere</Highlight>, so their climate influence does not stop at a border. Cooperation is needed to connect shared goals with national action and comparable evidence.

### Some Greenhouse Gases Remain in the Atmosphere for a Long Time

Long-lived greenhouse gases emitted in one place mix widely through the atmosphere and influence the global climate. One city or country therefore cannot stabilize the climate by acting alone.

Greenhouse gas emissions and [climate impacts](/en/subjects/biology/climate-change/impact) <Highlight>cross national borders</Highlight>. Countries with large emissions therefore need to reduce greenhouse gas sources, while countries exposed to severe impacts need support for adaptation. Comparable data lets every country check progress.

### Comparable Data for Checking Progress

The [Paris Agreement](https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement) aims to limit the rise in global average temperature and strengthen the global response to climate change.

Without **comparable data**, a climate promise can sound ambitious but remain hard to check. Comparable data gives countries, researchers, and communities a common measure for checking progress.

## Temperature Targets Provide a Common Measure

International climate agreements use temperature goals to guide collective action. The Paris Agreement aims to hold the increase in global average temperature well below <InlineMath math="2\,^\circ\text{C}" /> and pursue efforts to limit it to <InlineMath math="1.5\,^\circ\text{C}" /> **above pre-industrial levels**.

[Climate risks and losses](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/resources/spm-headline-statements/) increase with every increment of warming. The <InlineMath math="1.5\,^\circ\text{C}" /> and <InlineMath math="2\,^\circ\text{C}" /> goals provide a shared measure for judging how quickly and deeply emissions need to fall.

A temperature target turns into a shared budget of carbon dioxide. The IPCC estimated that, from the start of 2020, about <InlineMath math="500" /> billion tonnes of <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CO_2}" /> could still be emitted for an even chance of staying within <InlineMath math="1.5\,^\circ\text{C}" />. The scene below spends that budget at a round <InlineMath math="40" /> billion tonnes per year in two ways. Both shapes have the same area, <InlineMath math="500" />. At constant emissions the pink rectangle ends after <InlineMath math="12.5" /> years, while cutting emissions steadily to zero stretches the teal triangle to <InlineMath math="25" /> years. That's why the target says how fast emissions must fall, not only how far.

## Roles in International Climate Cooperation

Global cooperation runs through several kinds of institutions, and each one carries a different job. **Some assess evidence**, some shape agreements, some run programmes, and some track progress. A body that publishes research does not decide national targets, and the process that records national reports does not set those targets either.

### Division of Tasks in Climate Cooperation

Global cooperation involves many institutions and processes. The Paris Agreement establishes shared goals and reporting cycles, while **each country determines the content of its national plan**. Negotiating forums bring countries together, and scientific bodies assess research so decisions can use reviewed evidence.

The work falls into three tasks: agreeing on goals, measuring and reporting progress, and carrying out measures in energy, land use, transport, food, and adaptation.

### Each Country States Its Climate Plan

[National climate plans](https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement/nationally-determined-contributions-ndcs) are called NDCs. Each NDC states a country's planned efforts to reduce emissions and adapt to climate impacts, so later reports can be **compared with that plan**.

After submitting an NDC, countries report actions, progress, and support under the enhanced transparency framework. The [global stocktake](https://unfccc.int/topics/global-stocktake) then assesses collective progress every five years and informs the next round of plans. It compares the combined response with the shared long-term goals.

| Cooperation element | What it records or sets | Why it is needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temperature target | Sets the shared long-term limit | Projected warming can be compared with the agreed target |
| NDC | States a country's planned mitigation and adaptation actions | Later reports can be compared with the submitted plan |
| Transparency reports | Record action, progress, and support | Countries and reviewers can compare implementation using reported evidence |
| Global stocktake | Assesses collective progress every five years | Identifies gaps between the combined response and the long-term goals |
| Adaptation support | Provides finance, technology, or capacity to vulnerable places | Climate risk and the resources available to reduce it are unevenly distributed |

> Targets set the direction, national plans state intended action, reports record progress, and the global stocktake assesses the combined result.

## Global Agreements Shape Local Decisions

Global agreements inform national plans. Those plans are then carried out through **energy policy, forest protection, and city planning**. Each policy affects electricity sources, carbon uptake, transport, or flood risk. Clear information lets communities compare the choices and their consequences.

International and national policy set the larger conditions. Understanding data, distinguishing mitigation from adaptation, reducing waste, and caring for local environments connect those conditions with decisions made in communities.

## Reports Show Whether Climate Plans Are Working

Suppose a fictional country reports 100 million tonnes of carbon-dioxide-equivalent emissions in its base year and targets 80 million tonnes in its target year. An interim report shows 90 million tonnes. Carbon dioxide equivalent combines the effects of several gases using a consistent comparison method.

| Comparison with the base year | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Targeted reduction | <InlineMath math="(100-80)/100\times100\%" /> | <InlineMath math="20\%" /> |
| Reported reduction | <InlineMath math="(100-90)/100\times100\%" /> | <InlineMath math="10\%" /> |

Emissions have fallen, but **the target has not yet been reached**. Before comparing real reports, check the base year, covered sectors and gases, accounting methods, and target year.

The cycle runs on two clocks that both tick every five years, three years apart. New national plans are due in the years <InlineMath math="2020+5k" />, and each global stocktake comes in <InlineMath math="2023+5k" />, two years before the next round of plans, so its findings can make those plans stronger.

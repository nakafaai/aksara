## Mitigation and Adaptation

Mitigation and adaptation both respond to climate change, but they have different goals. Mitigation reduces the causes of warming, while adaptation reduces harm that is already happening or likely to happen.

### Mitigation Targets Causes

When dealing with climate change, there are two different questions. First, how do we reduce the causes? Second, how do we reduce harm when impacts are already happening?

The first response is **mitigation**. Mitigation reduces greenhouse gas emissions or enhances removals by sinks. The second response is **adaptation**. Adaptation adjusts ecological, social, or economic systems so climate risks cause less harm.

### Adaptation Reduces Harm

[IPCC](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/) uses these two response frames. Mitigation reduces the causes of warming, while adaptation reduces impact risk.

Classify an action by its main result: reducing greenhouse gases, reducing harm from climate impacts, or doing both.

## Mitigation Reduces the Cause

Mitigation reduces greenhouse gas emissions or maintains the ability of forests, soils, and oceans to absorb carbon. Its success is measured by changes in net emissions and the amount of carbon stored.

### Ways to Reduce Net Emissions

Examples of mitigation include improving energy efficiency, shifting to low-emission energy, reducing fossil fuel combustion, cutting methane from organic waste, and protecting carbon-rich ecosystems. Evaluate each action using measured life-cycle emissions and the amount of carbon actually stored. A green label alone does not show that emissions fell.

Forest and ecosystem restoration can increase carbon storage while supporting biodiversity, but not every planting project has the same outcome. Species choice, previous land use, permanence, fire risk, water, and the rights and livelihoods of local people all matter. Land-based mitigation cannot compensate for delayed cuts in fossil emissions.

### Assess Waste Reduction Across the Life Cycle

Reducing unnecessary single-use plastic can support mitigation when it lowers life-cycle production and waste emissions. The alternative must also be assessed across its life cycle to make sure emissions or other environmental harm are not shifted elsewhere.

## Adaptation Reduces Harm

Adaptation responds to impacts that are already occurring or are projected. Depending on local evidence, farmers may use suitable crop varieties, adjust planting schedules, conserve soil moisture, or manage water differently. Coastal regions may combine ecosystem restoration, early warning, spatial planning, and engineered protection.

Adaptation prepares people and ecosystems for climate effects that are already happening or can no longer be avoided. A poorly designed action can shift risk to another place or group, creating maladaptation. Without mitigation, risks grow and adaptation becomes less effective. Without adaptation, communities and ecosystems remain more exposed.

## Classify Actions by Their Main Goal

Some actions serve both purposes. Identify the main goal first, then check whether the action also provides another benefit.

### Mangroves Show Two Benefits at Once

One activity can have two benefits. Replanting mangroves, for example, can store carbon and protect coasts from erosion and waves. If we emphasize carbon storage, that is the mitigation side. If we emphasize coastal protection, that is the adaptation side.

The [IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/summary-for-policymakers/) assesses ecosystem protection and restoration across both response types. Carbon storage can contribute to mitigation, while measures such as wetland restoration or urban greening can reduce some climate risks. Fossil-emission cuts remain necessary. The benefits of these actions depend on local ecology, governance, and implementation.

### The Main Verb Points to the Goal

The way to distinguish it is to read the main goal in the sentence. Phrases such as reducing emissions, absorbing carbon, or replacing fossil energy point to mitigation. Phrases such as protecting, adjusting, strengthening resilience, or reducing loss point to adaptation.

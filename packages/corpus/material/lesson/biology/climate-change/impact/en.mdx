## Climate Change Affects Connected Systems

Changes in temperature or rainfall can affect organisms, food supplies, water, health, and livelihoods. <Highlight>These areas are connected</Highlight>, so a shift in one of them usually reaches the others.

### One Physical Change Can Affect Several Systems

Higher air temperatures increase evaporation. **If rainfall does not replace the lost water**, soil moisture decreases. Less soil moisture can slow plant growth and reduce crop yields, while reduced access to clean water can harm health.

The sequence of effects differs by place because exposure and vulnerability vary. The important step is to connect each physical change to its effects on organisms, ecosystems, livelihoods, and health.

### Evidence Shows Impacts across Multiple Systems

Climate change has already [affected ecosystems, people, food, water, and health](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/) in many regions.

The IPCC findings connect **changes in water, temperature, and habitat** with effects on food, health, and biodiversity. The table below traces several of those pathways.

| Starting change | Biological pathway | Impact on people or ecosystems |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warmer seawater | Coral-algae symbiosis is stressed and reef habitat weakens | Fisheries and coastal protection can be disrupted |
| Longer drought | Soil water falls and crops are stressed | Food supply and clean water become less stable |
| Heavier rain or flooding | Water can be contaminated and habitats can change | Health, safety, and housing risks increase |
| Temperature and rainfall shift | Suitable ranges for species and vectors can move, changing ecological relationships | Food-web competition changes and health risks need new monitoring |

> To assess a climate impact, identify the initial physical change, then explain its effects on organisms, people, and ecosystems.

## Ocean Warming Can Trigger Coral Bleaching

The ocean has absorbed more than <InlineMath math="90\%" /> of the [excess heat in the Earth system](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-change-ocean-heat-content) from human-caused warming. A small local temperature change can disrupt organisms with **narrow thermal tolerances**. Corals are especially vulnerable because much of their energy supply depends on a heat-sensitive partnership.

### Corals Live with Symbiotic Algae

Coral reefs may look like rocks, but they are built by coral animals living with symbiotic algae that provide much of the coral's food. **Symbiosis** is a close relationship between two organisms. When seawater becomes too warm, this relationship can be disturbed and coral can appear white.

Coral bleaching is a sign of severe stress and can happen before a coral dies. If seawater stays too warm, corals can weaken and die. Fish lose habitat, while coastal communities may lose reef fisheries and protection from waves.

Heat stress on a reef adds up week by week. NOAA Coral Reef Watch measures it in **degree heating weeks**: each week, take how many degrees the water sits above the coral's bleaching threshold, then add the weeks together. In the illustrative case below, the amber line is that weekly excess, and the pink line is its running sum. Follow the pink line upward: it crosses <InlineMath math="4\ ^\circ\text{C-weeks}" />, where significant bleaching becomes likely, and then <InlineMath math="8" />, where severe bleaching and coral deaths become likely. Notice that the pink line keeps its height once the water cools, because the stress already happened.

### Pale Color Is a Stress Signal

[Coral bleaching](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/coral_bleach.html) is a stress response in which coral expels the algae living in its tissue. Unusually warm water can disrupt this relationship. Without enough algae, coral loses one of its main energy sources and can turn pale.

**Bleached does not mean dead**. Compare these possibilities:

| Condition | What happens to the coral? |
| --- | --- |
| Symbiosis is functioning | Algae supply much of the food and contribute color. |
| Bleaching occurs | The coral can still be alive, but loses many algae and much of its energy supply. |
| Stress subsides soon enough | The coral can regain algae and recover. |
| Stress persists | Disease and mortality risks increase, and reef habitat function can decline. |

Reef scientists add up heat stress. Every week the water is at least <InlineMath math="1\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}" /> above the coral's bleaching threshold adds that excess to a total called degree heating weeks. With illustrative weekly values, the total passes <InlineMath math="4" /> in week 6, when bleaching becomes likely, and reaches <InlineMath math="8" /> in week 8, when severe bleaching and coral death become likely. Zoom in and find the weeks that add nothing.

## Changing Rainfall Patterns Affect Food and Water

Agriculture depends on **water, temperature, soil, and planting time**. If rainy seasons shift, droughts lengthen, or rain falls too intensely, crops can suffer. Floods can damage fields, while droughts can leave plants without enough water.

Food production in fields, livestock systems, and fisheries, as well as food distribution, can be disrupted by extreme weather. Climate change therefore affects organisms and human life.

## Climate Affects Health Through Several Pathways

Climate affects health through **heat, water, air, food, and disease-carrying organisms**. Separate these pathways before identifying the cause of a health risk. One health outcome can have more than one climate driver, and separating them prevents a single cause from being credited with the whole effect.

### Climate Impact Pathways to Health

Extreme heat can cause heat exhaustion. Flooding can increase disease risk when water is contaminated. Drought can reduce access to clean water. Changes in temperature and rainfall can also shift climatic suitability for vectors such as mosquitoes, although disease risk also depends on **housing, health services, ecology, and prevention**.

Climate change can [affect health directly and indirectly](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-and-health) through heatwaves, disruptions to food and water supplies, and vector-borne disease. Each effect follows a different pathway.

### Shifting Habitats Change Relationships between Organisms

In wildlife, climate change can **shift the geographic range** in which a species can survive and reproduce. As populations move toward places where conditions remain suitable, feeding relationships and competition can change. Species that cannot move or adapt quickly enough may lose habitat.

## Ocean Changes Affect Coastal Communities

The coral example shows each step in a climate-impact pathway. Warmer seawater can disturb coral symbiosis. The coral then bleaches, shelter for reef fish weakens, and fishers and coastal communities can eventually be affected.

The same pattern can appear in food, water, and health. **Trace the full pathway** from the first event to the organisms and people that depend on the affected system.

On a gentle coast the land rises slowly, so a small rise in sea level moves the water's edge far inland.

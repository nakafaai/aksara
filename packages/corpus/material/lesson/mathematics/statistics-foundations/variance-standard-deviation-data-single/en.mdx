## Measuring Spread with Variance and Standard Deviation

Variance averages the **squared** distances from the mean. Standard deviation takes the square root of that average, returning the measure to the original unit of the data.

- A **small** variance or standard deviation means the observations cluster **close** to the mean.
- A **large** value means the observations are spread **farther** from the mean.

## Formulas for Variance and Standard Deviation

We treat these observations as the **complete population**. Variance uses squared units, whereas taking the square root restores the original unit. Standard deviation can therefore be compared directly with an observed value.

Square each deviation of Group One from its mean <InlineMath math="16" />. The bars are <InlineMath math="9, 4, 1, 1, 0, 0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 4" />, they add up to <InlineMath math="24" />, and their mean <InlineMath math="\frac{24}{12} = 2" /> is the variance.

### Variance

Subtract the mean from each observation, **square every difference**, and average the squared differences.

The formula is:

<BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}" />

The symbols mean:

- <InlineMath math="\sigma^2" /> = population variance
- <InlineMath math="x_i" /> = observed value
- <InlineMath math="\bar{x}" /> = arithmetic mean
- <InlineMath math="n" /> = number of observations
- <InlineMath math="\sum" /> = sum of all indicated terms
### Standard Deviation

Standard deviation is <Highlight>the square root of variance</Highlight>. Unlike variance, it has the same unit as the original data.

The formula is:

<BlockMath math="\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}}" />

These formulas use the population symbols and divide by the full count. A sample requires the corresponding sample-variance formula.

### Calculation for Group One

Calculate <InlineMath math="(x_i - \bar{x})^2" /> for **every observation** in Group 1, where <InlineMath math="\bar{x}=16" />:

| Age (years) | Frequency | Squared difference | Contribution to sum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="13" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="(13-16)^2=9" /> | <InlineMath math="1 \times 9=9" /> |
| <InlineMath math="14" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="(14-16)^2=4" /> | <InlineMath math="1 \times 4=4" /> |
| <InlineMath math="15" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="(15-16)^2=1" /> | <InlineMath math="2 \times 1=2" /> |
| <InlineMath math="16" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="(16-16)^2=0" /> | <InlineMath math="2 \times 0=0" /> |
| <InlineMath math="17" /> | <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="(17-16)^2=1" /> | <InlineMath math="5 \times 1=5" /> |
| <InlineMath math="18" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="(18-16)^2=4" /> | <InlineMath math="1 \times 4=4" /> |

Add the squared differences <InlineMath math="\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2" />:

<BlockMath math="9 + 4 + (1 \times 2) + (0 \times 2) + (1 \times 5) + 4 = 9 + 4 + 2 + 0 + 5 + 4 = 24" />

Calculate the variance:

<BlockMath math="\sigma^2_{\text{Group 1}} = \frac{\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n} = \frac{24}{12} = 2" />

Calculate the standard deviation:

<BlockMath math="\sigma_{\text{Group 1}} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41" />

## Alternative Formula for Variance

The following equivalent formula uses the two sums <InlineMath math="\sum x_i" /> and <InlineMath math="\sum x_i^2" />, which a calculator or program can accumulate directly:

<BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x_i^2}{n} - \left( \frac{\sum x_i}{n} \right)^2" />

First average the squared observations <InlineMath math="\sum x_i^2" /> by dividing their sum by <InlineMath math="n" />. Then subtract the square of the mean <InlineMath math="(\bar{x})^2 = (\frac{\sum x_i}{n})^2" />.

Use this formula to recalculate the variance of Group 1:

Use the **frequency of each age** so repeated observations are counted. There are two values of <InlineMath math="15" />, two of <InlineMath math="16" />, and five of <InlineMath math="17" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\sum x_i^2 &= 13^2+14^2+2(15^2)+2(16^2)+5(17^2)+18^2 \\ &=169+196+450+512+1445+324 \\ &=3096\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\sum x_i &=13+14+2(15)+2(16)+5(17)+18 \\ &=13+14+30+32+85+18 \\ &=192\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

With <InlineMath math="n=12" />, substitute both sums.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\sigma^2 &=\frac{3096}{12}-\left(\frac{192}{12}\right)^2 \\ &=258-16^2 \\ &=258-256 \\ &=2\end{aligned}" />

The variance is still <InlineMath math="2" /> square years, matching the squared-difference method. Standard deviation is measured in years.

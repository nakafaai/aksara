## Measuring Spread with Variance and Standard Deviation

Two common measures of spread are **variance** and **standard deviation**.

Variance averages the **squared** distances from the mean. Standard deviation takes the square root of that average, returning the measure to the original unit of the data.

- A **small** variance or standard deviation means the observations cluster **close** to the mean.
- A **large** value means the observations are spread **farther** from the mean.

## Formulas for Variance and Standard Deviation

1.  **Variance (<InlineMath math="\sigma^2" />)**

    Subtract the mean from each observation, square every difference, and average the squared differences.

    The formula is:

    <BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}" />

    where:

    - <InlineMath math="\sigma^2" /> = population variance
    - <InlineMath math="x_i" /> = observed value
    - <InlineMath math="\bar{x}" /> = arithmetic mean
    - <InlineMath math="n" /> = number of observations
    - <InlineMath math="\sum" /> = sum of all indicated terms

2.  **Standard Deviation (<InlineMath math="\sigma" />)**

    Standard deviation is the square root of variance. Unlike variance, it has the same unit as the original data.

    The formula is:

    <BlockMath math="\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}}" />

These formulas use the population symbols and divide by the full count. A sample requires the corresponding sample-variance formula.

## Comparing Variance in Two Age Groups

Consider two groups of age observations. Both have the **same mean (<InlineMath math="\bar{x}" />), namely <InlineMath math="16" />**, but their spreads are very different.

- **Group One (<InlineMath math="n=12" />):** <InlineMath math="13, 14, 15, 15, 16, 16, 17, 17, 17, 17, 17, 18" />
- **Group Two (<InlineMath math="n=12" />):** <InlineMath math="1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 12, 27, 28, 29, 32, 36" />

We will calculate the population variance and standard deviation of each group.

### Calculation for Group One

Calculate <InlineMath math="(x_i - \bar{x})^2" /> for every observation in Group <InlineMath math="1" />, where <InlineMath math="\bar{x}=16" />:

- <InlineMath math="(13-16)^2 = (-3)^2 = 9" />
- <InlineMath math="(14-16)^2 = (-2)^2 = 4" />
- <InlineMath math="(15-16)^2 = (-1)^2 = 1" /> (<InlineMath math="2 \text{ data points}" />)
- <InlineMath math="(16-16)^2 = (0)^2 = 0" /> (<InlineMath math="2 \text{ data points}" />)
- <InlineMath math="(17-16)^2 = (1)^2 = 1" /> (<InlineMath math="5 \text{ data points}" />)
- <InlineMath math="(18-16)^2 = (2)^2 = 4" />

Add the squared differences <InlineMath math="\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2" />:

<BlockMath math="9 + 4 + (1 \times 2) + (0 \times 2) + (1 \times 5) + 4 = 9 + 4 + 2 + 0 + 5 + 4 = 24" />

Calculate the variance:

<BlockMath math="\sigma^2_{\text{Group 1}} = \frac{\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n} = \frac{24}{12} = 2" />

Calculate the standard deviation:

<BlockMath math="\sigma_{\text{Group 1}} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41" />

### Calculation for Group Two

Repeat the calculation of <InlineMath math="(x_i - \bar{x})^2" /> for Group <InlineMath math="2" />, again with <InlineMath math="\bar{x}=16" />:

- <InlineMath math="(1-16)^2 = (-15)^2 = 225" />
- <InlineMath math="(3-16)^2 = (-13)^2 = 169" />
- <InlineMath math="(4-16)^2 = (-12)^2 = 144" />
- <InlineMath math="(5-16)^2 = (-11)^2 = 121" />
- <InlineMath math="(7-16)^2 = (-9)^2 = 81" />
- <InlineMath math="(8-16)^2 = (-8)^2 = 64" />
- <InlineMath math="(12-16)^2 = (-4)^2 = 16" />
- <InlineMath math="(27-16)^2 = (11)^2 = 121" />
- <InlineMath math="(28-16)^2 = (12)^2 = 144" />
- <InlineMath math="(29-16)^2 = (13)^2 = 169" />
- <InlineMath math="(32-16)^2 = (16)^2 = 256" />
- <InlineMath math="(36-16)^2 = (20)^2 = 400" />

Add the squared differences <InlineMath math="\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2" />:

<BlockMath math="225 + 169 + 144 + 121 + 81 + 64 + 16 + 121 + 144 + 169 + 256 + 400 = 1910" />

Calculate the variance:

<BlockMath math="\sigma^2_{\text{Group 2}} = \frac{\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n} = \frac{1910}{12} \approx 159.17" />

Calculate the standard deviation:

<BlockMath math="\sigma_{\text{Group 2}} = \sqrt{159.17} \approx 12.62" />

### Interpreting the Results

- The variance of Group <InlineMath math="1" /> (<InlineMath math="\sigma^2 = 2" />) is **much smaller** than that of Group <InlineMath math="2" /> (<InlineMath math="\sigma^2 \approx 159.17" />).
- The standard deviation of Group <InlineMath math="1" /> (<InlineMath math="\sigma \approx 1.41" />) is also **much smaller** than that of Group <InlineMath math="2" /> (<InlineMath math="\sigma \approx 12.62" />).

The observations in **Group <InlineMath math="1" /> are tightly clustered** around the mean of <InlineMath math="16" />. The observations in **Group <InlineMath math="2" /> are much more widely spread** around the same mean of <InlineMath math="16" />.

## Alternative Formula for Variance

The following equivalent formula uses the two sums <InlineMath math="\sum x_i" /> and <InlineMath math="\sum x_i^2" />, which a calculator or program can accumulate directly:

<BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x_i^2}{n} - \left( \frac{\sum x_i}{n} \right)^2" />

First average the squared observations <InlineMath math="\sum x_i^2" /> by dividing their sum by <InlineMath math="n" />. Then subtract the square of the mean <InlineMath math="(\bar{x})^2 = (\frac{\sum x_i}{n})^2" />.

Use this formula to recalculate the variance of Group <InlineMath math="1" />:

1.  Calculate <InlineMath math="\sum x_i^2" /> for Group <InlineMath math="1" />:

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="13^2 + 14^2 + 15^2 + 15^2 + 16^2 + 16^2 + 17^2 + 17^2 + 17^2 + 17^2 + 17^2 + 18^2" />
      <BlockMath math="= 169 + 196 + 225 + 225 + 256 + 256 + 289 + 289 + 289 + 289 + 289 + 324 = 3096" />
    </MathContainer>

2.  Calculate <InlineMath math="\sum x_i" /> for Group <InlineMath math="1" />:

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="13 + 14 + 15 + 15 + 16 + 16 + 17 + 17 + 17 + 17 + 17 + 18" />
      <BlockMath math="= 192" />
    </MathContainer>

    We also know <InlineMath math="n=12" />.

3.  Plug into the alternative formula:

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = \frac{3096}{12} - \left( \frac{192}{12} \right)^2" />
      <BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = 258 - (16)^2" />
      <BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = 258 - 256 = 2" />
    </MathContainer>

The result matches the first method exactly: <InlineMath math="\sigma^2 = 2" />.

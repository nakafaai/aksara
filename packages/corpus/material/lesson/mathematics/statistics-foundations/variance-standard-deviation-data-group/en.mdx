## Calculating Spread for Grouped Data

A grouped frequency table can show how often phone-battery durations fall within intervals such as <InlineMath math="6\text{-}10 \text{ hours}" /> and <InlineMath math="11\text{-}15 \text{ hours}" />. We still want to describe how widely those durations vary.

The table no longer contains each exact observation. Within <InlineMath math="11\text{-}15 \text{ hours}" />, for example, we cannot tell whether a battery lasted <InlineMath math="11 \text{ hours}" />, <InlineMath math="12 \text{ hours}" />, or another duration.

To estimate the spread, we **represent** every observation in a class by that class's **midpoint** <InlineMath math="x_i" />. This is a modeling approximation, so the resulting variance and standard deviation estimate the values we would obtain from the ungrouped observations.

## Formulas for Variance and Standard Deviation of Grouped Data

We weight each midpoint <InlineMath math="x_i" /> by its class frequency <InlineMath math="f" />. A sample requires the corresponding sample-variance formula.

We treat these observations as the **complete population**. Variance uses squared units, whereas taking the square root restores the original unit. Standard deviation can therefore be compared directly with an observed value.

### Variance

For **grouped data**, use the following computational formula:

<BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum (f \cdot x_i^2)}{\sum f} - \left( \frac{\sum (f \cdot x_i)}{\sum f} \right)^2" />

The first term is the frequency-weighted mean of the squared midpoints. The second is the square of the frequency-weighted mean. Their difference is the estimated population variance.
### Standard Deviation

Just like with ungrouped data, the standard deviation is the **square root of the variance**:

<BlockMath math="\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2}" />

  description="Each class contributes its midpoint as many times as its frequency, so the sums of f·x and f·x² give the mean and the variance."

## Calculating Variance and Standard Deviation of Phone Battery Duration

Suppose battery durations are recorded to the **nearest hour**, then grouped into the following intervals. The first class therefore contains recorded values of 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 hours.

| Battery duration (hours) | Frequency (<InlineMath math="f" />) |
| :----------------------: | :---------------------------------: |
| <InlineMath math="6\text{-}10" />  | <InlineMath math="2" />  |
| <InlineMath math="11\text{-}15" /> | <InlineMath math="10" /> |
| <InlineMath math="16\text{-}20" /> | <InlineMath math="18" /> |
| <InlineMath math="21\text{-}25" /> | <InlineMath math="45" /> |
| <InlineMath math="26\text{-}30" /> | <InlineMath math="5" />  |

### Create a Helper Table

For each class, calculate the midpoint <InlineMath math="x_i" />, then calculate <InlineMath math="f \cdot x_i" /> and <InlineMath math="f \cdot x_i^2" />.

| Battery duration (hours) |   Midpoint, <InlineMath math="x_i" />    |  Frequency, <InlineMath math="f" />   |     <InlineMath math="f \cdot x_i" />      |      <InlineMath math="f \cdot x_i^2" />      |
| :----------------------: | :--------------------------------------: | :-----------------------------------: | :----------------------------------------: | :-------------------------------------------: |
| <InlineMath math="6\text{-}10" />  |  <InlineMath math="\frac{6+10}{2}=8" />  | <InlineMath math="2" />  |   <InlineMath math="2 \times 8 = 16" />    |   <InlineMath math="2 \times 8^2 = 128" />    |
| <InlineMath math="11\text{-}15" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{11+15}{2}=13" /> | <InlineMath math="10" /> |  <InlineMath math="10 \times 13 = 130" />  |  <InlineMath math="10 \times 13^2 = 1690" />  |
| <InlineMath math="16\text{-}20" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{16+20}{2}=18" /> | <InlineMath math="18" /> |  <InlineMath math="18 \times 18 = 324" />  |  <InlineMath math="18 \times 18^2 = 5832" />  |
| <InlineMath math="21\text{-}25" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{21+25}{2}=23" /> | <InlineMath math="45" /> | <InlineMath math="45 \times 23 = 1035" />  | <InlineMath math="45 \times 23^2 = 23805" />  |
| <InlineMath math="26\text{-}30" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{26+30}{2}=28" /> | <InlineMath math="5" />  |  <InlineMath math="5 \times 28 = 140" />   |  <InlineMath math="5 \times 28^2 = 3920" />   |
|        **Total**         |                                          | <InlineMath math="\sum f = 80" /> | <InlineMath math="\sum fx_i = 1645" /> | <InlineMath math="\sum fx_i^2 = 35375" /> |

### Calculate Variance

Plug the **total values** from the table into the variance formula:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum (f \cdot x_i^2)}{\sum f} - \left( \frac{\sum (f \cdot x_i)}{\sum f} \right)^2" />
  <BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = \frac{35375}{80} - \left( \frac{1645}{80} \right)^2" />
  <BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = 442.1875 - (20.5625)^2" />
  <BlockMath math="\sigma^2 = 442.1875 - 422.81640625" />
  <BlockMath math="\sigma^2 \approx 19.37" />
</MathContainer>

The estimated variance is <InlineMath math="19.37" /> square hours. Variance uses squared units because every distance from the mean is squared before averaging.
### Calculate Standard Deviation

Take the square root of the variance:

<BlockMath math="\sigma = \sqrt{19.37109375} \approx 4.4" />

The estimated standard deviation is <InlineMath math="4.4 \text{ hours}" />. Around the estimated mean of <InlineMath math="\frac{1645}{80} \approx 20.56 \text{ hours}" />, durations differ by about <InlineMath math="4.4 \text{ hours}" /> in the root-mean-square sense.

When datasets use the same unit and a comparable scale, a smaller standard deviation indicates that values are <Highlight>clustered more tightly around their mean</Highlight>.

Mark the mean <InlineMath math="\bar{x} = 1645 / 80 \approx 20.56" /> on the frequency polygon and step one standard deviation <InlineMath math="\sigma \approx 4.4" /> to each side. The band from about <InlineMath math="16.2" /> to <InlineMath math="25.0" /> hours holds the tall peak at <InlineMath math="23" /> hours, the bulk of the phones.

## Expressing a Count as a Proportion

Absolute frequency tells us **how many times** a value appears in a dataset.

For example, if we ask <InlineMath math="30 \text{ students}" /> about their phone brands, and <InlineMath math="12 \text{ students}" /> answer "Apple", then the frequency for the "Apple" brand is <InlineMath math="12" />.

**Relative frequency** is the number of observations in a value or group divided by the total number of observations. It gives the **proportion of the whole** that belongs to that value or group.

### Uses of Relative Frequency

Relative frequency supports two kinds of comparison:

1. **Compare Proportions**: We can compare how large the proportion of one data group is compared to another group within the same dataset. For example, how large the proportion of Apple users is compared to Samsung users in one respondent group.
2. **Compare Groups of Different Sizes**: We can compare the percentage of students who like mathematics in a group of <InlineMath math="30 \text{ students}" /> with the percentage in a group of <InlineMath math="40 \text{ students}" />. Absolute counts alone do not account for the different group sizes.

If <InlineMath math="12" /> of <InlineMath math="30" /> students in one group and <InlineMath math="16" /> of <InlineMath math="40" /> in another like mathematics, the proportions are equal even though the counts differ:

<BlockMath math="\frac{12}{30}=\frac{16}{40}=\frac25=0.4=40\%" />

## Relative Frequency Formula

Divide the frequency of the group by the total frequency:

Let <InlineMath math="f" /> be the group frequency, <InlineMath math="n" /> the total number of observations, and <InlineMath math="h" /> the relative frequency. **Divide by all observations**:

<BlockMath math="h=\frac{f}{n}" />

Where:

- **Group Frequency**: number of observations in the selected group.
- **Total Frequency**: number of all observations in the dataset.

Relative Frequency is usually expressed in the form of:

- **Fraction**: The ratio of the group frequency to the total frequency.
- **Decimal**: The result of the fraction division.
- **Percentage**: The decimal form multiplied by <InlineMath math="100\%" />. This form states how many observations occur per hundred.

## Example of Calculating Relative Frequency

Consider the phone-brand data from the <InlineMath math="30 \text{ students}" /> above:

| Phone Brand | Frequency (Number of Students) |
| :---------- | :----------------------------- |
| Apple       | <InlineMath math="12" />       |
| Samsung     | <InlineMath math="10" />       |
| Xiaomi      | <InlineMath math="8" />        |
| **Total**   | <InlineMath math="30" />       |

Total frequency is <InlineMath math="12 + 10 + 8 = 30" /> students.

Calculate each percentage directly from the fraction so rounding a decimal first does not change the result. **Round only the final step** to the precision you want to display. Here, <InlineMath math="p" /> denotes the percentage.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="h_{\text{Apple}}=\frac{12}{30}=0.4,\quad p_{\text{Apple}}=\frac{12}{30}\cdot100\%=40\%" />
  <BlockMath math="h_{\text{Samsung}}=\frac{10}{30}=\frac13,\quad p_{\text{Samsung}}=\frac13\cdot100\%\approx33\%" />
  <BlockMath math="h_{\text{Xiaomi}}=\frac8{30}=\frac4{15},\quad p_{\text{Xiaomi}}=\frac4{15}\cdot100\%\approx27\%" />
</MathContainer>

**Relative Frequency Table**:

| Phone Brand | Frequency                | Relative Frequency (Decimal) | Relative Frequency (Percent) |
| :---------- | :----------------------- | :--------------------------- | :--------------------------- |
| Apple       | <InlineMath math="12" /> | <InlineMath math="0.40" />   | <InlineMath math="40\%" />   |
| Samsung     | <InlineMath math="10" /> | <InlineMath math="0.33" />   | <InlineMath math="33\%" />   |
| Xiaomi      | <InlineMath math="8" />  | <InlineMath math="0.27" />   | <InlineMath math="27\%" />   |
| **Total**   | <InlineMath math="30" /> | <InlineMath math="1.00" />   | <InlineMath math="100\%" />  |

The sum of relative frequencies (in decimal form) should always be <InlineMath math="1" />, and in percent should be <InlineMath math="100\%" />. There might be slight differences due to rounding, but it should be very close to <InlineMath math="1" /> or <InlineMath math="100\%" />.

## Interpreting Relative Frequency

From the relative frequency table above, we can say:

- <InlineMath math="40\%" /> of the students in that group use Apple brand phones.
- About <InlineMath math="33\%" /> students use Samsung.
- The rest, about <InlineMath math="27\%" />, use Xiaomi.

The table shows at a glance how the <InlineMath math="30 \text{ students}" /> are distributed among the three brands.

Relative frequency <Highlight>makes absolute counts comparable</Highlight> by relating each count to the size of the complete dataset.

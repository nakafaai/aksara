## When Individual Data Values Are Hidden in Intervals

For ungrouped data, we calculate the mean by adding all values and dividing by the number of values.

<BlockMath math="\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}" />

In a frequency distribution, however, values may be presented as **groups** or **intervals**. Test scores might be grouped into <InlineMath math="70\text{-}79" />, <InlineMath math="80\text{-}89" />, and <InlineMath math="90\text{-}99" />.

Once data is grouped, we **no longer know each exact value**. If <InlineMath math="5" /> students belong to the <InlineMath math="70\text{-}79" /> interval, for example, we cannot tell whether their individual scores were <InlineMath math="70" />, <InlineMath math="72" />, <InlineMath math="75" />, or other values in that interval.

## Using the Class Midpoint

Because the exact values are unavailable, we make an **approximation**: the midpoint of each class **represents every value** in that interval.

Let <InlineMath math="L_i" /> and <InlineMath math="U_i" /> be the lower and upper limits of class <InlineMath math="i" />:

<BlockMath math="x_i=\frac{L_i+U_i}{2}" />

## Formula for Mean of Grouped Data

After finding each midpoint, calculate the **estimated mean** with:

<BlockMath math="\bar{x} \approx \frac{\sum (f_i \times x_i)}{\sum f_i}" />

The quantities in this formula are:

- <InlineMath math="\bar{x}" /> is the estimated mean of the grouped data.
- <InlineMath math="f_i" /> is the frequency of class <InlineMath math="i" />.
- <InlineMath math="x_i" /> is the midpoint of class <InlineMath math="i" />.
- <InlineMath math="\sum (f_i \times x_i)" /> is the sum of each class frequency multiplied by its midpoint.
- <InlineMath math="\sum f_i" /> is the sum of all frequencies, equal to the total number of values <InlineMath math="n" />.

Each class contributes its midpoint times its frequency. The bars are <InlineMath math="76, 451, 704, 47" />, they add up to <InlineMath math="1278" />, and dividing by the <InlineMath math="30" /> students gives the grouped mean <InlineMath math="42.6" />.

## Average Shoe Size from Grouped Data

Consider the grouped data for shoe sales at Store A:

| Shoe Size (Class Interval) | Frequency (<InlineMath math="f_i" />) |
| :------------------------- | :------------------------------------ |
| <InlineMath math="37\text{-}39" /> | <InlineMath math="2" />               |
| <InlineMath math="40\text{-}42" /> | <InlineMath math="11" />              |
| <InlineMath math="43\text{-}45" /> | <InlineMath math="16" />              |
| <InlineMath math="46\text{-}48" /> | <InlineMath math="1" />               |
| **Total**                  | <InlineMath math="n=30" />        |

For each class, calculate the **midpoint** from its two class limits. Multiply the midpoint by the frequency to account for the number of observations that the class represents.

| Class | Midpoint | Frequency times midpoint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="37\text{-}39" /> | <InlineMath math="(37+39)/2=38" /> | <InlineMath math="2\times38=76" /> |
| <InlineMath math="40\text{-}42" /> | <InlineMath math="(40+42)/2=41" /> | <InlineMath math="11\times41=451" /> |
| <InlineMath math="43\text{-}45" /> | <InlineMath math="(43+45)/2=44" /> | <InlineMath math="16\times44=704" /> |
| <InlineMath math="46\text{-}48" /> | <InlineMath math="(46+48)/2=47" /> | <InlineMath math="1\times47=47" /> |

The sum of the products estimates the sum of all shoe sizes. Divide it by the **total frequency** to obtain the estimated mean.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\sum f_ix_i=76+451+704+47=1278" />
  <BlockMath math="\sum f_i=2+11+16+1=30" />
  <BlockMath math="\bar x\approx\frac{1278}{30}=42.6" />
</MathContainer>

This result is an **estimate** because <Highlight>the midpoint stands in for every value</Highlight> within its class. It is the standard estimate when only grouped frequencies are available.

The histogram shows why the shoe sizes cannot be listed one by one: we only know how many fall in each class. Most of the <InlineMath math="30" /> students sit in the classes <InlineMath math="40\text{-}42" /> and <InlineMath math="43\text{-}45" />.

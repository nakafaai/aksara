## Arithmetic Mean

Besides the median (middle value) and mode (most frequent value), the **mean**, or average, is another way to describe the center of a data set.

The mean is the value each member would receive if we **distributed the total evenly**. Imagine collecting all your friends' candies into one pile and then sharing them equally. The number each friend receives is the mean.

## How to calculate the mean

To calculate the mean:

1. Add all the data values.
2. Divide the sum by the number of values.

**Mean formula:**

<BlockMath math="\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n}" />

The quantities in this formula are:

- <InlineMath math="\bar{x}" />, read "x bar," represents the **mean**.
- <InlineMath math="\sum x" />, read "sigma x," represents the **sum of all data values**.
- <InlineMath math="n" /> is the **number of data values**.

## Effect of Large Values on Mean Median and Mode

We will compare the mean, median, and mode of the same data set before and after adding two unusually large values.

### OSIS Social Action

**Initial situation:**

Ten members of School A's student council collect wearable clothes for a community drive. The number of items collected by each member is:

<BlockMath math="3, 5, 7, 10, 5, 3, 4, 6, 9, 8" />

**Finding the initial mean, median, and mode:**

1.  **Sort the data** (for the median and mode):

    <InlineMath math="3, 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10" /> (there are <InlineMath math="10 \text{ data points}" />, <InlineMath math="n = 10" />)

2.  **Calculate the mean:**

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\sum x = 3+3+4+5+5+6+7+8+9+10 = 60" />
      <BlockMath math="\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n} = \frac{60}{10} = 6" />
    </MathContainer>

    So, the initial mean is <InlineMath math="6" />.

3.  **Find the median:**

    The number of data points is even (<InlineMath math="n = 10" />). The middle data are at positions <InlineMath math="10/2 = 5" /> and <InlineMath math="(10/2)+1 = 6" />.

    The value at position <InlineMath math="5" /> is <InlineMath math="5" />, and the value at position <InlineMath math="6" /> is <InlineMath math="6" />.

    <BlockMath math="\text{Median} = \frac{5+6}{2} = 5.5" />

    So, the initial median is <InlineMath math="5.5" />.

4.  **Find the mode:**

    The most frequent values are <InlineMath math="3" /> (<InlineMath math="2 \text{ times}" />) and <InlineMath math="5" /> (<InlineMath math="2 \text{ times}" />).

    So, the initial modes are <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> (bimodal).

**New situation:**

The next day, <InlineMath math="2 \text{ students}" /> joined and donated <InlineMath math="20" /> and <InlineMath math="22" /> items of clothing.

The new data becomes:

<BlockMath math="3, 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 20, 22" />

**Finding the new mean, median, and mode:**

1.  **Sort the data**:

    <InlineMath math="3, 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 20, 22" /> (there are <InlineMath math="12 \text{ data points}" />, <InlineMath math="n = 12" />)

2.  **Calculate the new mean:**

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\sum x = 60 + 20 + 22 = 102" />
      <BlockMath math="\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n} = \frac{102}{12} = 8.5" />
    </MathContainer>

    So, the new mean is <InlineMath math="8.5" />.

3.  **Find the new median:**

    The number of data points is even (<InlineMath math="n=12" />). The middle data are at positions <InlineMath math="12/2 = 6" /> and <InlineMath math="(12/2)+1 = 7" />.

    The value at position <InlineMath math="6" /> is <InlineMath math="6" />, the value at position <InlineMath math="7" /> is <InlineMath math="7" />.

    <BlockMath math="\text{Median} = \frac{6+7}{2} = 6.5" />

    So, the new median is <InlineMath math="6.5" />.

4.  **Find the new mode:**

    The most frequent values are still <InlineMath math="3" /> (<InlineMath math="2 \text{ times}" />) and <InlineMath math="5" /> (<InlineMath math="2 \text{ times}" />).

    So, the new modes are <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />.

## Impact of Adding Data

Compare the measures of central tendency before and after the two values are added:

- **Mean:** Changed from <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="8.5" /> (increased by <InlineMath math="2.5" />).
- **Median:** Changed from <InlineMath math="5.5" /> to <InlineMath math="6.5" /> (increased by <InlineMath math="1" />).
- **Mode:** Remained <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="5" /> (unchanged).

Adding <InlineMath math="20" /> and <InlineMath math="22" />, two values far above the original data, has the **largest effect on the mean**. The larger values pull the mean upward.

The median also changes, but less sharply. The mode does not change at all.

## Impact of an Extreme Value

Replace the <InlineMath math="12" />th student's donation with <InlineMath math="100 \text{ items}" />, then recalculate the mean to measure the effect of this unusually large value.

Data becomes: <InlineMath math="3, 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 20, 100" /> (<InlineMath math="n=12" />)

1.  **Calculate the mean:**

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\sum x = 60 + 20 + 100 = 180" />
      <BlockMath math="\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n} = \frac{180}{12} = 15" />
    </MathContainer>

    The mean becomes <InlineMath math="15" />, far above both the initial mean (<InlineMath math="6" />) and the previous mean (<InlineMath math="8.5" />).

2.  **Find the median:**

    Sorted data: <InlineMath math="3, 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 20, 100" />

    The median is still <InlineMath math="(6+7)/2 = 6.5" />, the same as before.

3.  **Find the mode:**

    Mode remains <InlineMath math="3" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />.

An extreme value may be classified as an **outlier** under a stated statistical rule or through domain judgment. In this example, <InlineMath math="100" /> strongly affects the mean but leaves the median and modes unchanged. This sensitivity is the mean's main weakness. The median and mode are more stable when one unusually large or small value appears.

The mean is easy to calculate, but an unusually large or small value can make it less representative of the rest of the data.

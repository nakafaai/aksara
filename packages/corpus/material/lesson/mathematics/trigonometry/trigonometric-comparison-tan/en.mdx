## Comparing Opposite and Adjacent Sides

The tangent ratio, written as <InlineMath math="\tan" />, compares the side opposite an angle with the adjacent side in a right triangle. Both sides are identified relative to **the same acute angle**. Because only the ratio is used, a larger triangle with the same angle gives the same tangent value.

### Tangent

Tangent is the <Highlight>ratio between the length of the opposite side</Highlight> (the side opposite to the known angle) and the adjacent side (the side adjacent to the angle) in a right-angled triangle.

<BlockMath math="\tan \theta = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}}" />

For angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> in a right triangle:

- The opposite side is the side facing the angle <InlineMath math="\theta" />.
- The adjacent side is the side next to the angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> other than the hypotenuse.
- Tangent is calculated by dividing the length of the opposite side by the adjacent side

### Examples of Tangent Values

Tangent values for special angles can be written exactly, with decimals shown only as approximations:

- <InlineMath math="\tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.577" />
- <InlineMath math="\tan 45^\circ = 1" />
- <InlineMath math="\tan 60^\circ = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732" />

These **exact values** come from side ratios in right triangles with angles of <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />, <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />, or <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />.

### Measuring Slopes and Heights

Tangent relates a slope angle to the **vertical and horizontal changes** in a right triangle. This relationship can be used to:

1. Calculate an object's height from its angle of elevation and horizontal distance
2. Calculate the slope of a road or staircase from its vertical rise and horizontal run
3. Calculate a roof angle from the height and width of its cross-section

Find <InlineMath math="\tan \theta" /> and explain **which two sides** determine the ratio.

### Worked Solution

Relative to <InlineMath math="\theta" />, the side of length <InlineMath math="15 \text{ cm}" /> is the opposite side and the side of length <InlineMath math="7 \text{ cm}" /> is the adjacent side. Tangent compares **opposite side to adjacent side**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\tan \theta = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}} \\ &amp;= \frac{15 \text{ cm}}{7 \text{ cm}} \\ &amp;= \frac{15}{7} \approx 2.14 \end{aligned}" />

The centimetre units cancel because this is a ratio. Therefore, <InlineMath math="\tan \theta \approx 2.14" />. The value is greater than <InlineMath math="1" />, which is reasonable because the opposite side is longer than the adjacent side.

As a consistency check, <InlineMath math="\theta=\arctan(15/7)\approx65.0^\circ" />, matching the acute angle shown in the diagram.

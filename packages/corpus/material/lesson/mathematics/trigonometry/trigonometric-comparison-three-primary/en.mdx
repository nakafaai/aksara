## Side Ratios for a Fixed Acute Angle

For a **fixed acute angle** in a right triangle, corresponding side lengths always have the same ratios. The three primary ratios are sine (<InlineMath math="\sin" />), cosine (<InlineMath math="\cos" />), and tangent (<InlineMath math="\tan" />).

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\sin \theta = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{hypotenuse}}" />
  <BlockMath math="\cos \theta = \frac{\text{adjacent side}}{\text{hypotenuse}}" />
  <BlockMath math="\tan \theta = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}}" />
</MathContainer>

### Sides of a Right Triangle

Trigonometric ratios use the following terms:

1. **Hypotenuse**: The longest side of a right triangle, always opposite to the right angle <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />.
2. **Opposite side**: The side that is opposite to the angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> we are examining.
3. **Adjacent side**: The leg that touches the angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> we are examining.

## Tangent

The tangent of angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> is <Highlight>the ratio between the opposite and adjacent sides</Highlight>. It is also the quotient of sine and cosine whenever <InlineMath math="\cos \theta\ne0" />.

In the unit circle:

- The <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate on the unit circle is <InlineMath math="\cos \theta" />.
- The <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate on the unit circle is <InlineMath math="\sin \theta" />.
- When <InlineMath math="\cos \theta\ne0" />, <InlineMath math="\tan \theta" /> is the slope of the line from the center to the point on the unit circle. At <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />, that line is vertical, so its slope and tangent are undefined.

## Relationships Between the Three Trigonometric Ratios

On the unit circle, the right triangle has legs <InlineMath math="\cos\theta" /> and <InlineMath math="\sin\theta" /> for an acute angle, and hypotenuse <InlineMath math="1" />. The **Pythagorean theorem** immediately relates the squares of the two ratios:

<BlockMath math="(\cos\theta)^2+(\sin\theta)^2=1^2=1" />

Outside the first quadrant, coordinates can be negative, but the circle equation <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2=1" /> still holds. The identity therefore holds for every angle.

Tangent divides the opposite side by the adjacent side. Let <InlineMath math="a" /> be the opposite side, <InlineMath math="b" /> the adjacent side, and <InlineMath math="c" /> the hypotenuse. Dividing sine by cosine cancels the hypotenuse factor:

<BlockMath math="\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\frac{a/c}{b/c}=\frac{a}{c}\cdot\frac{c}{b}=\frac{a}{b}=\tan\theta" />

The **denominator must be nonzero**. On the unit circle, this tangent relationship holds exactly when <InlineMath math="\cos\theta\ne0" />.

### Worked Solution

The exact special-angle ratios give the two leg lengths directly:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{opposite side}=1\cdot\sin 30^\circ=\frac{1}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="\text{adjacent side}=1\cdot\cos 30^\circ=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \tan 30^\circ &amp;=\frac{1/2}{\sqrt{3}/2} \\ &amp;=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \\ &amp;=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \\ &amp;\approx0.577 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Using **exact values before rounding** preserves the identity <InlineMath math="\tan \theta=\sin \theta/\cos \theta" /> and avoids error from dividing already rounded decimals.

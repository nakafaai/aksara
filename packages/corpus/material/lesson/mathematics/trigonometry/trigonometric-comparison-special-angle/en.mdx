## Exact Trigonometric Values from Triangles and the Unit Circle

Special angles are angles whose <Highlight>trigonometric values can be written exactly</Highlight> without a calculator. Their values follow from two familiar right triangles and from coordinates on the unit circle.

The most common special angles are <InlineMath math="0^\circ" />, <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />, <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />, <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, and <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />.

## Where the Exact Values Come From

The exact values for <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />, <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />, and <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> follow from two triangles whose side lengths are known exactly. One triangle comes from **half of a square** and the other from half of an equilateral triangle, so both sets of ratios can be read off without a calculator.

### The Forty Five Degree Triangle

Cutting a square along a diagonal produces an **isosceles right triangle**. If both legs have length <InlineMath math="1" />, the Pythagorean theorem gives a hypotenuse of <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2}" />:

### Triangles with Thirty and Sixty Degree Angles

Bisecting an **equilateral triangle** of side length <InlineMath math="2" /> produces two right triangles. Their side lengths, opposite <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />, opposite <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, and along the hypotenuse, are <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{3}" />, and <InlineMath math="2" />:

The values at <InlineMath math="0^\circ" /> and <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> come from the unit-circle points <InlineMath math="(1,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="(0,1)" />. Because tangent is <InlineMath math="\sin \theta/\cos \theta" />, <InlineMath math="\tan 90^\circ" /> is undefined: its denominator is zero.

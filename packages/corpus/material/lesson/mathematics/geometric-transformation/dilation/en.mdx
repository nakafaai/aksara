## Resizing a Figure from a Center

Dilation is a geometric transformation that changes the size of an object (enlarging or shrinking) <Highlight>without changing its shape</Highlight>. Each point on the object is mapped to a new position based on a center of dilation and a scale factor.

### Formal Definition of Dilation

The dilation center <InlineMath math="C" /> **remains fixed**. For a factor <InlineMath math="k\ne0" />, the vector from the center determines each image:

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{CA'}=k\overrightarrow{CA},\qquad CA'=|k|\,CA" />

The **absolute value of the factor** controls length ratios, while its sign controls direction from the center. The side descriptions apply to points different from the center.

| Factor | Size | Position from center |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="k>1" /> | Enlarged | Same side |
| <InlineMath math="0<k<1" /> | Reduced | Same side |
| <InlineMath math="k=1" /> | Unchanged size | Same side |
| <InlineMath math="-1<k<0" /> | Reduced | Opposite side |
| <InlineMath math="k=-1" /> | Unchanged size | Opposite side |
| <InlineMath math="k<-1" /> | Enlarged | Opposite side |

## Dilation with Respect to the Origin

With the origin as center, **multiply both coordinates by the scale factor**. This follows directly from the vector relation because the center coordinates are zero:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = kx" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = ky" />
</MathContainer>

### Dilating a Point with Respect to the Origin

If point <InlineMath math="A(1,2)" /> is dilated with respect to the origin <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> by a factor of <InlineMath math="2" />, determine the image of the point.

Here, <InlineMath math="x=1" />, <InlineMath math="y=2" />, and <InlineMath math="k=2" />.

The center is <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />. A factor of two makes the **distance from the center twice as large**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = 2 \cdot 1 = 2" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = 2 \cdot 2 = 4" />
</MathContainer>

The image is <InlineMath math="A'(2,4)" />.

## Dilation with Respect to an Arbitrary Point

If the center of dilation is an arbitrary point <InlineMath math="C(a,b)" /> and the scale factor is <InlineMath math="k" />, then for a point <InlineMath math="A(x,y)" />, its image <InlineMath math="A'(x',y')" /> is given by:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = a + k(x - a)" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = b + k(y - b)" />
</MathContainer>

The formula can be applied by **shifting the coordinates** so that <InlineMath math="C" /> becomes the origin, applying the dilation by factor <InlineMath math="k" />, and then shifting the coordinates back.

### Dilating a Point with Respect to an Arbitrary Point

If point <InlineMath math="C(5,2)" /> is dilated with respect to point <InlineMath math="P(2,3)" /> by a factor of <InlineMath math="2" />, determine the image of the point.

Here, the point to be dilated is <InlineMath math="C(5,2)" /> so <InlineMath math="x=5, y=2" />.

The **center of dilation** is <InlineMath math="P(2,3)" />, so <InlineMath math="a=2, b=3" />.

The scale factor is <InlineMath math="k=2" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= 2 + 2(5 - 2) \\ &amp;= 2 + 2(3) \\ &amp;= 2 + 6 \\ &amp;= 8 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= 3 + 2(2 - 3) \\ &amp;= 3 + 2(-1) \\ &amp;= 3 - 2 \\ &amp;= 1 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The image is <InlineMath math="C'(8,1)" />.

## Exercises

The first two problems dilate one point, first about the origin and then about a different center. The third dilates a whole triangle with a **negative factor**, so the image lands on the opposite side of the center.

1.  Determine the image of <InlineMath math="B(2,5)" /> under dilation <InlineMath math="D_{(O,3)}" /> (center at <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />, factor <InlineMath math="3" />).
2.  Determine the image of <InlineMath math="B(2,5)" /> under dilation with center <InlineMath math="P(1,3)" /> and factor <InlineMath math="3" />.
3.  A triangle with vertices <InlineMath math="A(1,1)" />, <InlineMath math="B(3,1)" />, and <InlineMath math="C(1,4)" /> is dilated with respect to the origin <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> by a scale factor <InlineMath math="k=-2" />. Determine the coordinates of the image triangle <InlineMath math="A'B'C'" />!

### Solutions

Distinguish the **center from the point being dilated**. Subtract the center coordinates, multiply by the factor, then add the center back. With the origin as center, subtracting and adding zero leaves the result unchanged.

**Solution 1**

Point <InlineMath math="B(2,5)" />, center <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="k=3" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = 3 \cdot 2 = 6" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = 3 \cdot 5 = 15" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the image is <InlineMath math="B'(6,15)" />.

**Solution 2**

Point <InlineMath math="B(2,5)" />, center <InlineMath math="P(1,3)" />, <InlineMath math="k=3" />. (<InlineMath math="x=2, y=5, a=1, b=3" />)

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= 1 + 3(2 - 1) \\ &amp;= 1 + 3(1) \\ &amp;= 1 + 3 \\ &amp;= 4 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= 3 + 3(5 - 3) \\ &amp;= 3 + 3(2) \\ &amp;= 3 + 6 \\ &amp;= 9 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the image is <InlineMath math="B'(4,9)" />.

**Solution 3**

Center <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="k=-2" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="A'=(-2\cdot1,-2\cdot1)=(-2,-2)" />
  <BlockMath math="B'=(-2\cdot3,-2\cdot1)=(-6,-2)" />
  <BlockMath math="C'=(-2\cdot1,-2\cdot4)=(-2,-8)" />
</MathContainer>

The coordinates of the image triangle are: <InlineMath math="A'(-2,-2)" />, <InlineMath math="B'(-6,-2)" />, <InlineMath math="C'(-2,-8)" />.

The original perpendicular sides have lengths <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />. Their image lengths are <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="6" />. Thus **lengths double**, while area becomes four times as large:

<BlockMath math="L=\frac12\cdot2\cdot3=3,\qquad L'=\frac12\cdot4\cdot6=12=4L" />

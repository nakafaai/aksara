## Reflection Matrix for Horizontal Axis

Recall that reflecting a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> across the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis results in the image <InlineMath math="(x,-y)" />. The <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate remains unchanged, while the <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate changes sign.

Start with <Highlight>a matrix whose entries are unknown</Highlight>:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} r & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix}" />

For reflection across the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, the matrix must satisfy:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} r & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ -y \end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} rx + sy \\ tx + uy \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1x + 0y \\ 0x - 1y \end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

By equating the coefficients, we get:

- <InlineMath math="rx + sy = 1x + 0y \implies r=1, s=0" />
- <InlineMath math="tx + uy = 0x - 1y \implies t=0, u=-1" />

The reflection matrix for the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

## Reflection Matrix for Vertical Axis

Reflecting a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> across the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis results in <InlineMath math="(-x,y)" />.

Only the **horizontal coordinate changes sign**. The first row selects the negative horizontal coordinate, while the second preserves the vertical coordinate.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} r & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -x \\ y \end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} rx + sy \\ tx + uy \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1x + 0y \\ 0x + 1y \end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

This gives <InlineMath math="r=-1, s=0, t=0, u=1" />.

The matrix is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

## Reflection Matrix for Main Diagonal Line

Reflecting a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> across the line <InlineMath math="y=x" /> results in <InlineMath math="(y,x)" />.

This matrix **exchanges the coordinates**. Its ones lie off the main diagonal because the first row selects the vertical coordinate and the second selects the horizontal coordinate.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} r & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} y \\ x \end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} rx + sy \\ tx + uy \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0x + 1y \\ 1x + 0y \end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

This gives <InlineMath math="r=0, s=1, t=1, u=0" />.

The matrix is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />

## Reflection Matrix for Negative Diagonal Line

Reflecting a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> across the line <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> results in <InlineMath math="(-y,-x)" />.

This matrix **exchanges and negates both coordinates**. The off-diagonal negative ones apply both operations in one multiplication.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} r & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -y \\ -x \end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} rx + sy \\ tx + uy \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0x - 1y \\ -1x + 0y \end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

This gives <InlineMath math="r=0, s=-1, t=-1, u=0" />.

The matrix is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />

## Finding the Image of a Point using Matrix

Find the image of <InlineMath math="(3,-4)" /> reflected across the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

**Solution**:

Use **row-by-column multiplication** with the reflection matrix for the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 1 &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} (1)(3) + (0)(-4) \\ (0)(3) + (-1)(-4) \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

The image is <InlineMath math="(3,4)" />.

In the plane, the multiplication keeps <InlineMath math="x = 3" /> and flips <InlineMath math="y = -4" /> to <InlineMath math="4" />. <InlineMath math="P(3, -4)" /> and <InlineMath math="P'(3, 4)" /> lie on one vertical segment, and the x-axis cuts it in half.

## Finding the Image of a Triangle using Matrix

Determine the image of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> with vertices <InlineMath math="A(3,1)" />, <InlineMath math="B(-2,3)" />, and <InlineMath math="C(2,-1)" /> reflected across the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis!

**Solution**:

The reflection matrix for the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

The matrix of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" />'s vertices:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

The columns represent <InlineMath math="A" />, <InlineMath math="B" /> and <InlineMath math="C" />, from left to right. Multiply **each point column** by the reflection matrix to find both coordinates of its image.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\begin{pmatrix} x'_A &amp; x'_B &amp; x'_C \\ y'_A &amp; y'_B &amp; y'_C \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;=\begin{pmatrix} -1 &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 3 &amp; -2 &amp; 2 \\ 1 &amp; 3 &amp; -1 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x'_A&amp;=(-1)(3)+0=-3 \\ y'_A&amp;=0+(1)(1)=1 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x'_B&amp;=(-1)(-2)+0=2 \\ y'_B&amp;=0+(1)(3)=3 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x'_C&amp;=(-1)(2)+0=-2 \\ y'_C&amp;=0+(1)(-1)=-1 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\begin{pmatrix} x'_A &amp; x'_B &amp; x'_C \\ y'_A &amp; y'_B &amp; y'_C \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;=\begin{pmatrix} -3 &amp; 2 &amp; -2 \\ 1 &amp; 3 &amp; -1 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The image is triangle <InlineMath math="A'B'C'" /> with vertices <InlineMath math="A'(-3,1)" />, <InlineMath math="B'(2,3)" />, and <InlineMath math="C'(-2,-1)" />.

## Exercises

Choose the **matrix for the mirror line**, then multiply by the coordinate vector. For the triangle, keep the three vertices in the same column order.

1.  Find the image of point <InlineMath math="(3,5)" /> reflected across the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis using the following reflection matrix:

    <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />
2.  Determine the image of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> with vertices <InlineMath math="A(3,1)" />, <InlineMath math="B(-2,3)" />, and <InlineMath math="C(2,-1)" /> reflected across the line <InlineMath math="y=-x" />!

### Solutions

The first row gives the horizontal coordinate and the second gives the vertical coordinate. Reflection across the negative diagonal **exchanges the coordinates and reverses their signs**.

**Solution 1**

Multiply the position vector of <InlineMath math="(3,5)" /> by the reflection matrix for the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}" />

Image: <InlineMath math="(3,-5)" />.

**Solution 2**

The reflection matrix for <InlineMath math="y=-x" /> is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}" />

Matrix of <InlineMath math="ABC" /> vertices:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>
Compute each entry by multiplying a row by a column:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;m_{11}=(0)(3)+(-1)(1) \\ &amp;m_{12}=(0)(-2)+(-1)(3) \\ &amp;m_{13}=(0)(2)+(-1)(-1) \\ &amp;m_{21}=(-1)(3)+(0)(1) \\ &amp;m_{22}=(-1)(-2)+(0)(3) \\ &amp;m_{23}=(-1)(2)+(0)(-1) \end{aligned}" />

  <BlockMath math="=\begin{pmatrix}m_{11}&amp;m_{12}&amp;m_{13}\\m_{21}&amp;m_{22}&amp;m_{23}\end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="= \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -3 & 1 \\ -3 & 2 & -2 \end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

Image: <InlineMath math="A'(-1,-3)" />, <InlineMath math="B'(-3,2)" />, <InlineMath math="C'(1,-2)" />.

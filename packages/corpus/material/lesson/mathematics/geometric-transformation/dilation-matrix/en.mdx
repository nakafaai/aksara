## Matching Matrix Entries to Scaled Coordinates

How to find <Highlight>the matrix associated with a dilation operation</Highlight>? Recall that a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> is mapped by a dilation with a factor <InlineMath math="k \neq 0" /> and center <InlineMath math="O" /> to <InlineMath math="(kx,ky)" />.

Suppose the matrix we are looking for is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} r & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix}" />

Find <InlineMath math="r, s, t, u" /> such that it satisfies

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} r & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} kx \\ ky \end{pmatrix}" />

From the matrix multiplication on the left side, we get:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} rx + sy \\ tx + uy \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} kx \\ ky \end{pmatrix}" />

By equating the corresponding components:

- **First row**: <InlineMath math="rx + sy = kx" />. For this equation to hold for all <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, the coefficients of <InlineMath math="x" /> must be equal, and the coefficients of <InlineMath math="y" /> must be equal. Thus, <InlineMath math="r = k" /> and <InlineMath math="s = 0" />.
- **Second row**: <InlineMath math="tx + uy = ky" />. Similarly, <InlineMath math="t = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="u = k" />.

## Dilation Matrix with Respect to the Origin

A dilation matrix centered at the origin is a **multiple of the identity matrix**. For <InlineMath math="k\ne0" />, the inverse dilation has factor <InlineMath math="1/k" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="D_k=kI=\begin{pmatrix}k&0\\0&k\end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="D_{1/k}D_k=\begin{pmatrix}1/k&0\\0&1/k\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}k&0\\0&k\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

<InlineMath math="D_2" /> doubles every position vector. The triangle with vertices <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" />, <InlineMath math="(3, 1)" /> and <InlineMath math="(1, 2)" /> becomes <InlineMath math="(2, 2)" />, <InlineMath math="(6, 2)" /> and <InlineMath math="(2, 4)" />: each image vertex lies on the ray from <InlineMath math="O" /> through the original vertex, twice as far out, and every side doubles in length.

## Matrix Operation for Dilation with Respect to an Arbitrary Point

A point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> dilated by a factor <InlineMath math="k \neq 0" /> and center <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> will be mapped to <InlineMath math="(k(x-a)+a,\, k(y-b)+b)" />.

After **subtracting the center coordinates**, the position vector is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x-a \\ y-b \end{pmatrix}" />

Find the matrix operations that transform this vector into the required image:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} k(x-a)+a \\ k(y-b)+b \end{pmatrix}" />

The matrix operation associated with a dilation by a factor <InlineMath math="k \neq 0" /> with respect to the point <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> is

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} k & 0 \\ 0 & k \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x-a \\ y-b \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}" />

An equivalent form is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} k &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; k \end{pmatrix} \left( \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \right) \end{aligned}" />

With <InlineMath math="k = \tfrac12" /> and center <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" />, the vector from the center to <InlineMath math="(5, 3)" /> is <InlineMath math="(4, 2)" />. Half of it is <InlineMath math="(2, 1)" />, so the image is <InlineMath math="(1, 1) + (2, 1) = (3, 2)" />, exactly halfway along the segment from the center to the point.

## Finding the Image of a Dilation Using Matrices

Determine the image of point <InlineMath math="A(2,4)" /> transformed by a dilation with a factor of <InlineMath math="2" /> with respect to the center point <InlineMath math="P(1,1)" />!

**Solution**:

Use the **vector from the center to the point**, not the point coordinates alone. Given <InlineMath math="x=2, y=4, k=2, a=1, b=1" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2-1 \\ 4-1 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} (2)(1) + (0)(3) \\ (0)(1) + (2)(3) \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

The image of point <InlineMath math="A(2,4)" /> is <InlineMath math="A'(3,7)" />.

## Exercises

Each problem either builds the dilation matrix or applies it. The third problem moves the center away from the origin, so the **translation step** is needed as well.

1.  Find the coordinates of the image of the point <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> under the dilation <InlineMath math="[O,3]" />!
2.  Determine the matrix corresponding to a dilation with a scale factor of <InlineMath math="-2" /> and centered at <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />.
3.  A point <InlineMath math="B(-1, 5)" /> is dilated with center <InlineMath math="P(2, -3)" /> and scale factor <InlineMath math="k = \frac{1}{2}" />. Determine the coordinates of the image of point <InlineMath math="B" />!
4.  A triangle <InlineMath math="KLM" /> with vertices <InlineMath math="K(1,1)" />, <InlineMath math="L(5,1)" />, and <InlineMath math="M(3,4)" /> is dilated with center <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> and scale factor <InlineMath math="2" />. Draw the original triangle and its image, then determine the coordinates of the image vertices!

### Solutions

With the origin as center, multiply each coordinate by the factor. For any other center, **subtract the center before multiplying**, then add it back. This keeps the dilation center fixed.

**Solution 1**

The dilation <InlineMath math="[O,3]" /> means the center of dilation is <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> and the scale factor is <InlineMath math="k=3" />.

Let the point be <InlineMath math="Q(a,b)" />.

Then <InlineMath math="x=a, y=b, k=3" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3a \\ 3b \end{pmatrix}" />

Thus, the coordinates of the image of point <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> are <InlineMath math="(3a, 3b)" />.

**Solution 2**

Scale factor <InlineMath math="k=-2" />, center <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />.

The dilation matrix is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}" />

**Solution 3**

Point <InlineMath math="B(-1, 5)" />, center <InlineMath math="P(2, -3)" />, scale factor <InlineMath math="k = \frac{1}{2}" />.

<InlineMath math="x=-1, y=5, a=2, b=-3, k=\frac{1}{2}" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; \frac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} -1-2 \\ 5-(-3) \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;\quad{}+ \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>
Calculate the two coordinates, including the translation vector:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x'=(\frac12)(-3)+(0)(8)+2 \\ &amp;y'=(0)(-3)+(\frac12)(8)-3 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{3}{2} \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{4}{2} \\ 4 - 3 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The coordinates of the image of point <InlineMath math="B" /> are <InlineMath math="B'(\frac{1}{2}, 1)" />.

**Solution 4**

Triangle <InlineMath math="KLM" /> with <InlineMath math="K(1,1)" />, <InlineMath math="L(5,1)" />, <InlineMath math="M(3,4)" />.

Center <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />, <InlineMath math="k=2" />.

Image of point <InlineMath math="K(1,1)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x_K' \\ y_K' \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 2 &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;\implies K'(2,2) \end{aligned}" />

Image of point <InlineMath math="L(5,1)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x_L' \\ y_L' \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 2 &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;\implies L'(10,2) \end{aligned}" />

Image of point <InlineMath math="M(3,4)" />:

## How Reflection Changes the Horizontal Coordinate

Imagine the vertical axis as a mirror. A point and its image have <Highlight>equal distances from the axis</Highlight>. Points outside the axis move to the opposite side, while points on the axis remain fixed.

### Rule for Reflection over the Vertical Axis

If a point <InlineMath math="P(x, y)" /> is reflected over the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis, its image's coordinates, <InlineMath math="P'(x', y')" />, will follow the rule:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = -x" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = y" />
</MathContainer>

The image of point <InlineMath math="P(x, y)" /> is <InlineMath math="P'(-x, y)" />.

The <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate **stays the same**, while the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate changes sign.

## Reflecting a Point

Suppose we have point <InlineMath math="A(3, 4)" />. If point <InlineMath math="A" /> is reflected over the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis, its image, <InlineMath math="A'" />, can be determined as follows:

The original <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate is <InlineMath math="3" />, so <InlineMath math="x' = -3" />.

The original <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate is <InlineMath math="4" />, so <InlineMath math="y' = 4" />.

The image of point <InlineMath math="A" /> is <InlineMath math="A'(-3, 4)" />.

Both points are **three units from the axis** at the same height. The horizontal joining segment shows the equal distances on either side:

## Reflecting a Triangle

To reflect a triangle, we need to <Highlight>reflect each of its vertices</Highlight>.

1.  Point <InlineMath math="P(1, 2)" />: Its image is <InlineMath math="P'(-1, 2)" />.
2.  Point <InlineMath math="Q(4, 4)" />: Its image is <InlineMath math="Q'(-4, 4)" />.
3.  Point <InlineMath math="R(2, 0)" />: Its image is <InlineMath math="R'(-2, 0)" />.

By connecting the image points <InlineMath math="P'Q'R'" />, we obtain the reflected triangle.

## Reflecting a Line Equation

Suppose we have a line with the equation <InlineMath math="y = x + 2" />. To find the equation of its image after reflection over the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis, we substitute <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="-x" /> (because <InlineMath math="x' = -x" />) and <InlineMath math="y" /> with <InlineMath math="y" /> (because <InlineMath math="y' = y" />) into the **original equation**.

Original equation:

<BlockMath math="y = x + 2" />

Substitute <InlineMath math="x \rightarrow -x" />:

<BlockMath math="y = (-x) + 2" />

The equation of the image is:

<BlockMath math="y = -x + 2" />

## Exercises

The problems reflect a point, a triangle, and a line over the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis. Reflecting the line means replacing <InlineMath math="x" /> with its negative in the equation, while each point keeps its <InlineMath math="y" /> coordinate and only **changes the sign** of <InlineMath math="x" />.

1.  Determine the coordinates of the image of point <InlineMath math="K(-5, 8)" /> if it is reflected over the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis!
2.  A triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> has vertices <InlineMath math="A(2, 1)" />, <InlineMath math="B(5, 3)" />, and <InlineMath math="C(3, 6)" />. Determine the coordinates of the image triangle <InlineMath math="A'B'C'" /> after reflection over the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis!
3.  Determine the equation of the image of the line <InlineMath math="3x - 2y + 6 = 0" /> if it is reflected over the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis!
4.  A line has the equation <InlineMath math="y = -3x - 4" />. Determine the equation of its image after reflection over the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis.

### Solutions

For a point, **reverse the horizontal coordinate sign** and keep the vertical coordinate. For a line, replace the horizontal coordinate in the original equation by its negative, then simplify the products of negative signs.

**Solution 1**

The image of point <InlineMath math="K(-5, 8)" /> is <InlineMath math="K'(5, 8)" />.

**Explanation**: <InlineMath math="x' = -(-5) = 5" />, <InlineMath math="y' = 8" />.

**Solution 2**

The coordinates of the image triangle <InlineMath math="A'B'C'" /> are:

<BlockMath math="A(2,1)\mapsto A'(-2,1),\quad B(5,3)\mapsto B'(-5,3),\quad C(3,6)\mapsto C'(-3,6)" />

**Solution 3**

The equation of the image of the line <InlineMath math="3x - 2y + 6 = 0" /> is <InlineMath math="-3x - 2y + 6 = 0" />.

**Explanation**: Substitute <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="-x" /> into the original equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3(-x) - 2y + 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="-3x - 2y + 6 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 4**

The equation of the image of the line <InlineMath math="y = -3x - 4" /> is <InlineMath math="y = 3x - 4" />.

**Explanation**: Substitute <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="-x" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="y = -3(-x) - 4" />
  <BlockMath math="y = 3x - 4" />
</MathContainer>

## Moving Points by Adding a Vector

A translation moves a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> horizontally by <InlineMath math="a" /> and vertically by <InlineMath math="b" />, giving the image <InlineMath math="(x+a, y+b)" />.

This operation can be written in the form of vector addition (column matrix):

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x+a \\ y+b \end{pmatrix}" />

Rotations about the origin and reflections in lines through the origin can be represented by <InlineMath math="2 \times 2" /> matrix multiplication. A nonzero translation instead adds a vector and cannot be represented this way.

To combine translation with linear transformations using matrix multiplication, we use **homogeneous coordinates**. The point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> is represented by the column:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

The transformation then uses a <InlineMath math="3 \times 3" /> matrix. For a horizontal shift of <InlineMath math="a" /> and a vertical shift of <InlineMath math="b" />, it is:

<BlockMath math="T_{(a,b)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & a \\ 0 & 1 & b \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

The calculation gives:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 1 &amp; 0 &amp; a \\ 0 &amp; 1 &amp; b \\ 0 &amp; 0 &amp; 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} x+a \\ y+b \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

### Matrix Operation

For a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" />, add the translation vector component by component:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}" />

## Finding the Image of a Point with Matrix Operation

Use matrix operations to translate the point <InlineMath math="(-2,3)" /> by the following vector and determine its image:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}" />

**Solution:**

Apply the vector addition directly:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -2 + (-3) \\ 3 + 4 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

Its image is <InlineMath math="(-5,7)" />.

## Exercises

1.  Use matrix operations to translate the point <InlineMath math="(4,-5)" /> by the following vector and determine its image:

    <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}" />

2.  A triangle <InlineMath math="KLM" /> has vertices <InlineMath math="K(1,0)" />, <InlineMath math="L(4,2)" />, and <InlineMath math="M(2,5)" />. Translate it by the vector below and determine the coordinates of <InlineMath math="K'L'M'" />:

    <BlockMath math="T = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

### Solutions

1.  For the point <InlineMath math="(4,-5)" />, use the shifts <InlineMath math="a=-5" /> and <InlineMath math="b=4" />.

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 4-5 \\ -5+4 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

    Image: <InlineMath math="(-1,-1)" />.

2.  Apply the given translation vector <InlineMath math="T" /> to each vertex:

    - For <InlineMath math="K(1,0)" />:

      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x'_K \\ y'_K \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

      So <InlineMath math="K'(-1,1)" />.

    - For <InlineMath math="L(4,2)" />:

      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x'_L \\ y'_L \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

      So <InlineMath math="L'(2,3)" />.

    - For <InlineMath math="M(2,5)" />:

      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x'_M \\ y'_M \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

      So <InlineMath math="M'(0,6)" />.

    Image coordinates: <InlineMath math="K'(-1,1)" />, <InlineMath math="L'(2,3)" />, <InlineMath math="M'(0,6)" />.

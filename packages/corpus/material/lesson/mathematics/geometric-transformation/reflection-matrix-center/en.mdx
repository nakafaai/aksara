## Negating Both Coordinates with a Matrix

Reflecting a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> over the origin <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> results in the image <InlineMath math="(-x,-y)" />. This is equivalent to a <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation about the origin.

To find the <InlineMath math="2 \times 2" /> matrix for this transformation, start with unknown entries:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} r & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix}" />

We want to find <InlineMath math="r, s, t, u" /> such that:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} r & s \\ t & u \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -x \\ -y \end{pmatrix}" />

From matrix multiplication, we can write:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} rx + sy \\ tx + uy \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1x + 0y \\ 0x - 1y \end{pmatrix}" />

By equating <Highlight>the corresponding coefficients</Highlight>, we get:

- For the first row: <InlineMath math="rx + sy = -1x + 0y" />. This means <InlineMath math="r = -1" /> and <InlineMath math="s = 0" />.
- For the second row: <InlineMath math="tx + uy = 0x - 1y" />. This means <InlineMath math="t = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="u = -1" />.

### Reflection Matrix over the Origin

This matrix is the **negative identity matrix**. Reflecting twice reverses both signs twice and restores the original point:

<BlockMath math="M=-I=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad M^2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=I" />

Apply <InlineMath math="M" /> to <InlineMath math="P(2, 1)" /> and you get <InlineMath math="P'(-2, -1)" />. Apply <InlineMath math="M" /> again and the signs flip back to <InlineMath math="(2, 1)" />, so two reflections over the origin return every point to where it started, which is the statement <InlineMath math="M^2 = I" />.

## Applying the Reflection Matrix over the Origin

One matrix multiplication can act on **several columns at once**. Each column stores a point, so the column order determines the order of the vertices in the figure.

<BlockMath math="M\begin{pmatrix}x_A&x_B\\y_A&y_B\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-x_A&-x_B\\-y_A&-y_B\end{pmatrix}" />

Put the vertices <InlineMath math="A(1, 1)" />, <InlineMath math="B(3, 1)" /> and <InlineMath math="C(2, 3)" /> in three columns and multiply once by <InlineMath math="M" />. The image columns <InlineMath math="(-1, -1)" />, <InlineMath math="(-3, -1)" /> and <InlineMath math="(-2, -3)" /> give <InlineMath math="A'B'C'" /> in the same vertex order, turned a half-turn about <InlineMath math="O" />.

### Reflecting Two Points

Determine the images of points <InlineMath math="A(-1,1)" /> and <InlineMath math="B(3,-2)" /> when reflected over the origin!

**Solution**:

Use the **first row for the horizontal coordinate** and the second row for the vertical coordinate:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

For point <InlineMath math="A(-1,1)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x'_A \\ y'_A \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -1 &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} (-1)(-1) + (0)(1) \\ (0)(-1) + (-1)(1) \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

The image of point <InlineMath math="A" /> is <InlineMath math="A'(1,-1)" />.

For point <InlineMath math="B(3,-2)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x'_B \\ y'_B \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -1 &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} (-1)(3) + (0)(-2) \\ (0)(3) + (-1)(-2) \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

The image of point <InlineMath math="B" /> is <InlineMath math="B'(-3,2)" />.

## Exercises

Both problems reflect over the origin. The second applies the reflection matrix to **each vertex of a triangle**.

1.  Determine the images of points <InlineMath math="A(1,-7)" /> and <InlineMath math="B(-7,-2)" /> when reflected over the origin!
2.  A triangle <InlineMath math="PQR" /> has vertices <InlineMath math="P(2,2)" />, <InlineMath math="Q(5,2)" />, and <InlineMath math="R(3,5)" />. Determine the coordinates of the image triangle <InlineMath math="P'Q'R'" /> after reflection over the origin using matrix multiplication.

### Solutions

Keep the **point columns in the same order** during multiplication. This diagonal matrix changes the sign of every entry without exchanging horizontal and vertical positions.

**Solution 1**

The reflection matrix over the origin is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}" />

For <InlineMath math="A(1,-7)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -7 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix}" />

Image <InlineMath math="A'(-1,7)" />. For <InlineMath math="B(-7,-2)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} -7 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}" />

Image <InlineMath math="B'(7,2)" />.

**Solution 2**

Matrix of <InlineMath math="PQR" /> vertices:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 5 & 3 \\ 2 & 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}" />

  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\begin{pmatrix} -1 &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2 &amp; 5 &amp; 3 \\ 2 &amp; 2 &amp; 5 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -2 &amp; -5 &amp; -3 \\ -2 &amp; -2 &amp; -5 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Image: <InlineMath math="P'(-2,-2)" />, <InlineMath math="Q'(-5,-2)" />, <InlineMath math="R'(-3,-5)" />.

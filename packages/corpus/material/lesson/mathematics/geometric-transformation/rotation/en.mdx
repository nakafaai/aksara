## Center and Angle of a Rotation

Rotation turns every point of a figure <Highlight>through the same angle</Highlight> around a fixed center. The figure changes position and turns, while distances, angles, shape, size, and the order of its vertices are preserved.

A rotation needs two pieces of information:

- **Center of Rotation (C)**: The fixed point around which the rotation occurs.
- **Angle of Rotation (<InlineMath math="\theta" />)**: A directed angle that gives both the amount and direction of the turn. A positive angle rotates counter-clockwise, while a negative angle rotates clockwise.

### Definition of Rotation

The center <InlineMath math="C" /> **remains fixed**. For a point <InlineMath math="P\ne C" />, rotation preserves <InlineMath math="CP=CP'" /> and turns the direction from the center through the directed angle <InlineMath math="\theta" />. Measure from ray <InlineMath math="CP" /> toward ray <InlineMath math="CP'" />, taking counterclockwise as positive.

Angles differing by a full turn give the same image. For example, a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> counterclockwise turn gives the same result as a <InlineMath math="270^\circ" /> clockwise turn:

<BlockMath math="90^\circ-360^\circ=-270^\circ" />

## Rotation about the Origin

For rotation about the origin, the **sine and cosine of the turn angle** determine how the coordinates combine. Substituting special-angle values gives shorter rules:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = x \cos \theta - y \sin \theta" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = x \sin \theta + y \cos \theta" />
</MathContainer>

| Angle | Image | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> | <InlineMath math="(-y,x)" /> | Quarter turn counterclockwise |
| <InlineMath math="-90^\circ" /> | <InlineMath math="(y,-x)" /> | Quarter turn clockwise |
| <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> | <InlineMath math="(-x,-y)" /> | Half-turn |


## Rotating a Point by a Quarter Turn

A point <InlineMath math="B(0,4)" /> is rotated about the origin <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. Determine its image.

Here, <InlineMath math="x=0" />, <InlineMath math="y=4" />, and <InlineMath math="\theta = 90^\circ" />.

We know <InlineMath math="\cos 90^\circ = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="\sin 90^\circ = 1" />.

Use a **positive angle** for this counterclockwise turn:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= (0) \cos 90^\circ - (4) \sin 90^\circ \\ &amp;= 0 \cdot 0 - 4 \cdot 1 \\ &amp;= -4 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= (0) \sin 90^\circ + (4) \cos 90^\circ \\ &amp;= 0 \cdot 1 + 4 \cdot 0 \\ &amp;= 0 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The image of point <InlineMath math="B(0,4)" /> is <InlineMath math="B'(-4,0)" />.

## Rotating a Line by a Quarter Turn

Determine the image of the line <InlineMath math="y=2x" /> rotated about the origin <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />.

Let <InlineMath math="P(x,y)" /> be any point on the line <InlineMath math="y=2x" />. Its image, <InlineMath math="P'(x',y')" />, after a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> rotation about the origin is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= x \cos 90^\circ - y \sin 90^\circ \\ &amp;= x(0) - y(1) \\ &amp;= -y \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= x \sin 90^\circ + y \cos 90^\circ \\ &amp;= x(1) + y(0) \\ &amp;= x \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

From this, we get <InlineMath math="y = -x'" /> and <InlineMath math="x = y'" />.

Substitute <InlineMath math="x = y'" /> and <InlineMath math="y = -x'" /> into the **original line equation** <InlineMath math="y=2x" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(-x') = 2(y')" />
  <BlockMath math="-x' = 2y'" />
</MathContainer>

Replacing <InlineMath math="x'" /> and <InlineMath math="y'" /> back to <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, the equation of the image line is <InlineMath math="-x = 2y" /> or <InlineMath math="y = -\frac{1}{2}x" />.

## Exercises

The first two problems use a **positive quarter turn**. The last uses a half-turn, which reverses both coordinate signs. For a line, substitute into the original equation to map every point on it.

1.  A point <InlineMath math="A(3,0)" /> is rotated about the origin <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. Determine its image.
2.  Determine the image of the line <InlineMath math="y=4x" /> rotated about the origin <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> by <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />.
3.  Point <InlineMath math="P(-2, -5)" /> is rotated about the origin <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> by <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />. Determine the coordinates of its image!

### Solutions

Use the sine and cosine values for the given angle. For a line, **express the original coordinates in terms of the image coordinates** before substituting into the equation.

**Solution 1**

Point <InlineMath math="A(3,0)" />, <InlineMath math="\theta = 90^\circ" />. <InlineMath math="x=3, y=0" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= 3 \cos 90^\circ - 0 \sin 90^\circ \\ &amp;= 3(0) - 0(1) \\ &amp;= 0 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= 3 \sin 90^\circ + 0 \cos 90^\circ \\ &amp;= 3(1) + 0(0) \\ &amp;= 3 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, its image is <InlineMath math="A'(0,3)" />.

**Solution 2**

Line <InlineMath math="y=4x" />, <InlineMath math="\theta = 90^\circ" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = -y" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = x" />
</MathContainer>

So <InlineMath math="y = -x'" /> and <InlineMath math="x = y'" />.

Substitute into the line equation: <InlineMath math="(-x') = 4(y')" />.

Image line equation: <InlineMath math="-x = 4y" /> or <InlineMath math="y = -\frac{1}{4}x" />.

**Solution 3**

Point <InlineMath math="P(-2,-5)" />, <InlineMath math="\theta = 180^\circ" />. <InlineMath math="x=-2, y=-5" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\cos 180^\circ = -1" />
  <BlockMath math="\sin 180^\circ = 0" />
</MathContainer>

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= (-2) \cos 180^\circ - (-5) \sin 180^\circ \\ &amp;= (-2)(-1) - (-5)(0) \\ &amp;= 2 - 0 \\ &amp;= 2 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= (-2) \sin 180^\circ + (-5) \cos 180^\circ \\ &amp;= (-2)(0) + (-5)(-1) \\ &amp;= 0 + 5 \\ &amp;= 5 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the image of point <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="P'(2,5)" />.

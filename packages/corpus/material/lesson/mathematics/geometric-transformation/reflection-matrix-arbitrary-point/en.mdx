## Centering a Point Before Applying the Reflection Matrix

The image of a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> reflected about the point <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> is <InlineMath math="(-x+2a, -y+2b)" /> or <InlineMath math="(2a-x, 2b-y)" />.

If the reflection center is outside the origin, we need a translation in addition to multiplication by a <InlineMath math="2 \times 2" /> matrix.

**Translate to the center, negate, then translate back**. Track the coordinates through the three operations:

| Operation | Coordinates |
| --- | --- |
| Subtract the center | <InlineMath math="(x-a,y-b)" /> |
| Reflect over the origin | <InlineMath math="(-x+a,-y+b)" /> |
| Add the center | <InlineMath math="(-x+2a,-y+2b)" /> |

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x-a \\ y-b \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -(x-a) \\ -(y-b) \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -x+a \\ -y+b \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -x+2a \\ -y+2b \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The final vector gives <Highlight>the coordinate rule</Highlight> <InlineMath math="(x,y)\mapsto(2a-x,2b-y)" />.

Follow <InlineMath math="Q(2, 3)" /> through the three steps. Subtracting the center <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" /> gives <InlineMath math="(1, 2)" />, reflecting over the origin gives <InlineMath math="(-1, -2)" />, and adding the center back gives the image <InlineMath math="(0, -1)" />.

### Matrix Operation for Reflection over a Point

Multiplication by a <InlineMath math="2 \times 2" /> matrix alone always maps <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> to <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />. Reflection about a nonzero center maps the origin to <InlineMath math="(2a,2b)" />, so we need an **added translation vector**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix}x'\\y'\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}+2\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}" />

The formula splits the same move into two parts. The matrix <InlineMath math="-I" /> sends <InlineMath math="(2, 3)" /> to <InlineMath math="(-2, -3)" />, a reflection over the origin, and adding <InlineMath math="2(1, 1) = (2, 2)" /> slides that point to the image <InlineMath math="(0, -1)" />.

## Finding the Image of a Point

The point being reflected is <InlineMath math="(x,y) = (2,3)" /> and the center of reflection is <InlineMath math="(a,b) = (1,1)" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} + 2 \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}" />

</MathContainer>

## Exercises

Both problems use **a center that is not the origin**. The second one reflects a whole line, so the image line has to be written as an equation.

1.  Determine the image of point <InlineMath math="(3,1)" /> after reflection about point <InlineMath math="(-2,-1)" />.
2.  A line passes through points <InlineMath math="A(1,2)" /> and <InlineMath math="B(3,4)" />. Determine the equation of the image line after reflection about point <InlineMath math="C(0,1)" />.

### Solutions

Calculate **twice the center vector** before subtracting the point coordinates. For the line problem, two image points determine an equation. Check whether that equation describes a different line from the original.

**Solution 1**

Point <InlineMath math="(x,y) = (3,1)" />. Center <InlineMath math="(a,b) = (-2,-1)" />.

Using the formula <InlineMath math="x' = 2a-x" /> and <InlineMath math="y' = 2b-y" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = 2(-2) - 3 = -4 - 3 = -7" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = 2(-1) - 1 = -2 - 1 = -3" />
</MathContainer>

Its image is <InlineMath math="(-7,-3)" />. Or using matrix operations:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -1 &amp; 0 \\ 0 &amp; -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} + 2 \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} \\ &amp;= \begin{pmatrix} -7 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}" />

**Solution 2**

Center of reflection <InlineMath math="C(0,1)" />. (<InlineMath math="a=0, b=1" />)

Image of point <InlineMath math="A(1,2)" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x'_A = 2(0)-1 = -1" />
  <BlockMath math="y'_A = 2(1)-2 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

So <InlineMath math="A'(-1,0)" />.

Image of point <InlineMath math="B(3,4)" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x'_B = 2(0)-3 = -3" />
  <BlockMath math="y'_B = 2(1)-4 = -2" />
</MathContainer>

So <InlineMath math="B'(-3,-2)" />.

The image line passes through <InlineMath math="A'(-1,0)" /> and <InlineMath math="B'(-3,-2)" />.

Gradient <InlineMath math="m' = \frac{-2 - 0}{-3 - (-1)} = \frac{-2}{-2} = 1" />.

Equation of the line:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="y - y'_A = m'(x - x'_A)" />
  <BlockMath math="y - 0 = 1(x - (-1))" />
  <BlockMath math="y = x + 1" />
</MathContainer>

The same line has the standard form:

<BlockMath math="x - y + 1 = 0" />

The result is the original line <InlineMath math="y=x+1" /> again. The center <InlineMath math="C(0,1)" /> lies on it because <InlineMath math="1=0+1" />. A half-turn swaps points along any line through its center, so **the line as a set stays unchanged**.

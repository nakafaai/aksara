## The Center as Midpoint of a Point and Its Image

Reflection over a point, often called a half-turn rotation (<InlineMath math="180^\circ" />), is a geometric transformation where each point on an object is mapped to a new position such that <Highlight>the center of reflection becomes the midpoint</Highlight> between the original point and its image.

Suppose the center of reflection is <InlineMath math="P(a,b)" />. If a point <InlineMath math="Q(x,y)" /> is reflected over point <InlineMath math="P" />, its image <InlineMath math="Q'(x',y')" /> will lie on the line passing through <InlineMath math="Q" /> and <InlineMath math="P" />, with <InlineMath math="P" /> as the midpoint of the segment <InlineMath math="QQ'" />.

### Rule for Reflection over a Point

The center of reflection is the **midpoint** of the joining segment. The average of each pair of coordinates therefore equals the corresponding center coordinate:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{x+x'}{2}=a\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x'=2a-x" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac{y+y'}{2}=b\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y'=2b-y" />
</MathContainer>

A point at the center remains fixed. For example, <InlineMath math="Q(a,b)" /> gives <InlineMath math="Q'(2a-a,2b-b)=(a,b)" />.

A half-turn about a center turns a whole figure. Reflect triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> with <InlineMath math="A(2, 1)" />, <InlineMath math="B(4, 2)" /> and <InlineMath math="C(3, 4)" /> over <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" />: each image vertex is <InlineMath math="(2 - x, 2 - y)" />, so <InlineMath math="A'(0, 1)" />, <InlineMath math="B'(-2, 0)" /> and <InlineMath math="C'(-1, -2)" />. Every segment from a vertex to its image passes through the center and is cut in half there.

## Reflecting a Point over Another Point

Determine the image of a half-turn <InlineMath math="\sigma_{(2,3)}" /> for the point <InlineMath math="(5, 4)" />.

This means we are reflecting point <InlineMath math="Q(5,4)" /> over the center point <InlineMath math="P(2,3)" />.

Here, <InlineMath math="x=5" />, <InlineMath math="y=4" />, <InlineMath math="a=2" />, and <InlineMath math="b=3" />.

Calculate **twice each center coordinate**, then subtract the original point coordinate:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= 2a - x \\ &amp;= 2(2) - 5 \\ &amp;= 4 - 5 \\ &amp;= -1 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = 2b - y = 2(3) - 4 = 6 - 4 = 2" />
</MathContainer>

The image of point <InlineMath math="(5,4)" /> is <InlineMath math="(-1,2)" />.

## Reflecting a Line over a Point

Determine the image of a half-turn <InlineMath math="\sigma_{(1,3)}" /> for the line <InlineMath math="l" /> with the equation <InlineMath math="2x - y + 3 = 0" />.

Take an arbitrary point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" /> on line <InlineMath math="l" />. Its image, <InlineMath math="(x',y')" />, after reflection over point <InlineMath math="P(1,3)" /> is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= 2(1) - x \\ &amp;= 2 - x \\ &amp;\implies x = 2 - x' \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= 2(3) - y \\ &amp;= 6 - y \\ &amp;\implies y = 6 - y' \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Substitute <InlineMath math="x = 2 - x'" /> and <InlineMath math="y = 6 - y'" /> into the **original equation** of line <InlineMath math="l" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2(2 - x') - (6 - y') + 3 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="4 - 2x' - 6 + y' + 3 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="-2x' + y' + 1 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

Replacing <InlineMath math="x'" /> and <InlineMath math="y'" /> back to <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />, the equation of the image line <InlineMath math="l'" /> is:

<BlockMath math="-2x + y + 1 = 0" />

Alternatively, it can be written as <InlineMath math="2x - y - 1 = 0" />.

## Exercises

The problems use a **half-turn** about a given center. The third reflects a line, so its equation is transformed.

1.  Determine the image of a half-turn <InlineMath math="\sigma_{(-2,3)}" /> for the point <InlineMath math="(3, -4)" />.
2.  Point <InlineMath math="K(-5, 1)" /> is reflected over the origin <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />. Determine the coordinates of its image!
3.  Determine the image of a half-turn <InlineMath math="\sigma_{(1,-3)}" /> for the line <InlineMath math="l" /> with the equation <InlineMath math="2x + 5y + 6 = 0" />.

### Solutions

For a point, check that **the center is the midpoint** of the original and image. For a line, express the original coordinates in terms of the image coordinates before substituting.

**Solution 1**

Center <InlineMath math="P(-2,3)" />, point <InlineMath math="Q(3,-4)" />. So <InlineMath math="a=-2, b=3, x=3, y=-4" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= 2a - x \\ &amp;= 2(-2) - 3 \\ &amp;= -4 - 3 \\ &amp;= -7 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= 2b - y \\ &amp;= 2(3) - (-4) \\ &amp;= 6 + 4 \\ &amp;= 10 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the image of point <InlineMath math="Q(3,-4)" /> is <InlineMath math="(-7,10)" />.

**Solution 2**

Center <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" />, point <InlineMath math="K(-5,1)" />. So <InlineMath math="a=0, b=0, x=-5, y=1" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = 2(0) - (-5) = 0 + 5 = 5" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = 2(0) - 1 = 0 - 1 = -1" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the image of point <InlineMath math="K(-5,1)" /> is <InlineMath math="K'(5,-1)" />.

**Solution 3**

Center <InlineMath math="P(1,-3)" />. Line <InlineMath math="2x + 5y + 6 = 0" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= 2(1) - x \\ &amp;= 2 - x \\ &amp;\implies x = 2 - x' \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= 2(-3) - y \\ &amp;= -6 - y \\ &amp;\implies y = -6 - y' \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Substitute into the line equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2(2 - x') + 5(-6 - y') + 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="4 - 2x' - 30 - 5y' + 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="-2x' - 5y' - 20 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

Image line equation: <InlineMath math="-2x - 5y - 20 = 0" /> or <InlineMath math="2x + 5y + 20 = 0" />.

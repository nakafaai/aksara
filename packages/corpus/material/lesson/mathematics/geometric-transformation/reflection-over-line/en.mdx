## How a Mirror Line Pairs Points

In a line reflection, the **mirror line** is the symmetry boundary between a point and its image. A point on the mirror stays fixed. A point outside it maps to the opposite side at the same perpendicular distance.

If <InlineMath math="P" /> lies on the mirror, <InlineMath math="P'=P" />. Otherwise, draw the perpendicular from <InlineMath math="P" /> to the mirror and call its foot <InlineMath math="M" />. Continue the segment an equal distance beyond the mirror to locate the image <InlineMath math="P'" />:

<BlockMath math="PM=MP',\qquad PP'\perp m" />

Test both cases on the mirror <InlineMath math="m: y = \tfrac12 x + 1" />. The point <InlineMath math="Q(4, 3)" /> lies on <InlineMath math="m" />, because <InlineMath math="\tfrac12 \cdot 4 + 1 = 3" />, so its image is <InlineMath math="Q" /> itself. The point <InlineMath math="R(0, 6)" /> is off the mirror, and its image <InlineMath math="R'(4, -2)" /> lands on the other side at the same distance, with the midpoint <InlineMath math="(2, 2)" /> on <InlineMath math="m" /> and <InlineMath math="RR'" /> perpendicular to <InlineMath math="m" />.

### Finding the Perpendicular Foot

The mirror slope is <InlineMath math="\frac12" />. The **perpendicular slope** is therefore <InlineMath math="-2" />, since their product is <InlineMath math="-1" />. The perpendicular through <InlineMath math="P(2,4)" /> has equation:

<BlockMath math="y-4=-2(x-2)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=-2x+8" />

At the intersection, both equations give the same vertical coordinate:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac12x+1=-2x+8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac52x=7\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac{14}{5}" />
  <BlockMath math="y=\frac12\cdot\frac{14}{5}+1=\frac75+\frac55=\frac{12}{5},\qquad M\left(\frac{14}{5},\frac{12}{5}\right)" />
</MathContainer>

The perpendicular through <InlineMath math="P" /> has slope <InlineMath math="-2" />, so it is <InlineMath math="y = -2x + 8" />. It meets the mirror at the foot <InlineMath math="M\left(\tfrac{14}{5}, \tfrac{12}{5}\right)" />, and the small square at <InlineMath math="M" /> marks the right angle between the two lines.

### Finding and Checking the Image

Since <InlineMath math="M" /> is the **segment midpoint**, double its coordinates and subtract the coordinates of <InlineMath math="P" /> to find the image:

<BlockMath math="P'=2M-P=\left(2\cdot\frac{14}{5}-2,2\cdot\frac{12}{5}-4\right)=\left(\frac{18}{5},\frac45\right)" />

The distances to the mirror are the segment lengths to <InlineMath math="M" />. This calculation checks the equal lengths visible in the diagram:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="PM=\sqrt{\left(\frac{14}{5}-2\right)^2+\left(\frac{12}{5}-4\right)^2}=\sqrt{\frac{16+64}{25}}=\frac{4\sqrt5}{5}" />
  <BlockMath math="MP'=\sqrt{\left(\frac{18}{5}-\frac{14}{5}\right)^2+\left(\frac45-\frac{12}{5}\right)^2}=\frac{4\sqrt5}{5}" />
</MathContainer>

## What is Reflection over a Line?

Reflection over a line is a type of geometric transformation that moves every point on a plane to its mirror image position.

The line used as the reference for this reflection is called the **mirror line** or **axis of reflection**.

Imagine standing in front of a flat mirror. Your image in the mirror is the result of reflecting yourself over the surface of the mirror.

Mathematically, if we have a point <InlineMath math="P(x,y)" /> and a line <InlineMath math="m" /> as the axis of reflection, then the image point <InlineMath math="P'(x',y')" /> will have the following properties:

1.  **If point <InlineMath math="P" /> lies on the mirror line <InlineMath math="m" />**:

    Its image is the point itself.

    <BlockMath math="P' = P" />

2.  **If point <InlineMath math="P" /> does not lie on the mirror line <InlineMath math="m" />**:

    The line <InlineMath math="m" /> will be the perpendicular bisector of the line segment <InlineMath math="PP'" />. This means two important things:

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\overline{PP'} \perp m" />
      <BlockMath math="d(P,m) = d(P',m)" />
    </MathContainer>

    Consequently, the line <InlineMath math="m" /> intersects the line segment <InlineMath math="PP'" /> exactly at its midpoint. We call this intersection point <InlineMath math="M" />.

This concept is fundamental to understanding how the coordinates of a point change after being reflected over various types of lines. The most important thing to remember is the geometric relationship between the original point, the image point, and the mirror line.

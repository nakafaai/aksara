## Equal Distances on Either Side of a Horizontal Mirror

A point on the mirror line remains fixed. For a point outside the line, the original and image lie on opposite sides at <Highlight>equal perpendicular distances</Highlight>.

### Rule for Reflection over a Horizontal Line

The **midpoint** of the segment joining a point and its image lies on the mirror line. Both points share the coordinate measured along the line. Using the midpoint coordinate gives:

<BlockMath math="\frac{y+y'}{2}=h\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y+y'=2h\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y'=2h-y,\qquad x'=x" />

## Reflecting a Point over a Horizontal Line

Determine the image of point <InlineMath math="P(3,2)" /> by reflection over the line <InlineMath math="y=3" />.

In this case, <InlineMath math="x=3" />, <InlineMath math="y=2" />, and <InlineMath math="h=3" />.

Using the rule <InlineMath math="P'(x, 2h - y)" />:

<MathContainer>

## Exercises

Use the **mirror line coordinate** when substituting into the formula. In the last problem, the image coordinates are known and you work backwards to find the original point.

1.  Determine the image of point <InlineMath math="P(5,-4)" /> by reflection over the line <InlineMath math="y=-2" />.
2.  A point <InlineMath math="Q(-1, -3)" /> is reflected over the line <InlineMath math="y=0" /> (<InlineMath math="x" />-axis). Determine the coordinates of its image!
3.  The image of a point <InlineMath math="R(x,y)" /> after reflection over the line <InlineMath math="y=4" /> is <InlineMath math="R'(-2,5)" />. Determine the coordinates of point <InlineMath math="R" />!

### Solutions

Check the result using the **midpoint coordinate**. The average of the changing coordinates must equal the mirror line coordinate. The other coordinate stays unchanged.

**Solution 1**

Given <InlineMath math="P(5,-4)" /> and the mirror line <InlineMath math="y=-2" />. So <InlineMath math="x=5, y=-4, h=-2" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = x = 5" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= 2h - y \\ &amp;= 2(-2) - (-4) \\ &amp;= -4 + 4 \\ &amp;= 0 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the image of point <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="P'(5,0)" />.

**Solution 2**

Given <InlineMath math="Q(-1, -3)" /> and the mirror line <InlineMath math="y=0" />. So <InlineMath math="x=-1, y=-3, h=0" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = x = -1" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y' &amp;= 2h - y \\ &amp;= 2(0) - (-3) \\ &amp;= 0 + 3 \\ &amp;= 3 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the image of point <InlineMath math="Q" /> is <InlineMath math="Q'(-1,3)" />.

**Solution 3**

Given the image <InlineMath math="R'(-2,5)" /> and the mirror line <InlineMath math="y=4" />. So <InlineMath math="x'=-2, y'=5, h=4" />.

We know <InlineMath math="x' = x" /> and <InlineMath math="y' = 2h - y" />.

From <InlineMath math="x' = x" />, then <InlineMath math="x = -2" />.

From <InlineMath math="y' = 2h - y" />, then <InlineMath math="5 = 2(4) - y" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="5 = 8 - y" />
  <BlockMath math="y = 8 - 5" />
  <BlockMath math="y = 3" />
</MathContainer>

The coordinates of point <InlineMath math="R" /> are <InlineMath math="R(-2,3)" />.

## Equal Distances on Either Side of a Vertical Mirror

A point on the mirror line remains fixed. For a point outside the line, the original and image lie on opposite sides at <Highlight>equal perpendicular distances</Highlight>.

### Rule for Reflection over a Vertical Line

The **midpoint** of the segment joining a point and its image lies on the mirror line. Both points share the coordinate measured along the line. Using the midpoint coordinate gives:

<BlockMath math="\frac{x+x'}{2}=k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x+x'=2k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x'=2k-x,\qquad y'=y" />

## Reflecting a Point over a Vertical Line

Determine the image of point <InlineMath math="P(3,2)" /> by reflection over the line <InlineMath math="x=3" />.

In this case, <InlineMath math="x=3" />, <InlineMath math="y=2" />, and <InlineMath math="k=3" />.

Using the rule <InlineMath math="P'(2k - x, y)" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = 2(3) - 3 = 6 - 3 = 3" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = 2" />
</MathContainer>

The image of point <InlineMath math="P(3,2)" /> is <InlineMath math="P'(3,2)" />. <Highlight>The point lies on the mirror line</Highlight>, so its image is the point itself.

The next point does not lie on the mirror line. Determine the image of <InlineMath math="A(1,4)" /> after reflection across <InlineMath math="x=3" />.

Here, <InlineMath math="x=1" />, <InlineMath math="y=4" />, and <InlineMath math="k=3" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x' = 2(3) - 1 = 6 - 1 = 5" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = 4" />
</MathContainer>

The image of point <InlineMath math="A(1,4)" /> is <InlineMath math="A'(5,4)" />.

## Exercises

Use the **mirror line coordinate** when substituting into the formula. In the last problem, the image coordinates are known and you work backwards to find the original point.

1.  Determine the image of point <InlineMath math="P(3,2)" /> by reflection over the line <InlineMath math="x=5" />.
2.  A point <InlineMath math="B(-2, 5)" /> is reflected over the line <InlineMath math="x=1" />. Determine the coordinates of its image!
3.  The image of a point <InlineMath math="C(x,y)" /> after reflection over the line <InlineMath math="x=-2" /> is <InlineMath math="C'(0,3)" />. Determine the coordinates of point <InlineMath math="C" />!

### Solutions

Check the result using the **midpoint coordinate**. The average of the changing coordinates must equal the mirror line coordinate. The other coordinate stays unchanged.

**Solution 1**

Given <InlineMath math="P(3,2)" /> and the mirror line <InlineMath math="x=5" />. So <InlineMath math="x=3, y=2, k=5" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= 2k - x \\ &amp;= 2(5) - 3 \\ &amp;= 10 - 3 \\ &amp;= 7 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = y = 2" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the image of point <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="P'(7,2)" />.

**Solution 2**

Given <InlineMath math="B(-2, 5)" /> and the mirror line <InlineMath math="x=1" />. So <InlineMath math="x=-2, y=5, k=1" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x' &amp;= 2k - x \\ &amp;= 2(1) - (-2) \\ &amp;= 2 + 2 \\ &amp;= 4 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="y' = y = 5" />
</MathContainer>

Thus, the image of point <InlineMath math="B" /> is <InlineMath math="B'(4,5)" />.

**Solution 3**

Given the image <InlineMath math="C'(0,3)" /> and the mirror line <InlineMath math="x=-2" />. So <InlineMath math="x'=0, y'=3, k=-2" />.

We know <InlineMath math="x' = 2k - x" /> and <InlineMath math="y' = y" />.

From <InlineMath math="y' = y" />, then <InlineMath math="y = 3" />.

From <InlineMath math="x' = 2k - x" />, then <InlineMath math="0 = 2(-2) - x" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="0 = -4 - x" />
  <BlockMath math="x = -4" />
</MathContainer>

The coordinates of point <InlineMath math="C" /> are <InlineMath math="C(-4,3)" />.

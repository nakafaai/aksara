## Insurance Moves the Risk of Loss to an Insurer

**Risk** is the chance that a loss happens, such as an accident, a fire or a death. **Insurance** moves that risk to an insurance company. The policyholder pays a **premium** regularly, and the insurer pays **compensation** when a covered loss happens. The amount of cover in the policy is called the **face value** or sum insured. In the models here, you calculate premiums from a rate table, see the effect of a deductible and work out compensation with a coinsurance clause.

## Types of Insurance

| Type | Cover | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Life insurance | Death or permanent disability | Term life policy |
| Health insurance | Cost of medical treatment | Medical policy |
| Motor insurance | Damage to the vehicle and third-party claims | Comprehensive policy |
| Property insurance | Fire, flood or theft of property | Home fire policy |

Life and health insurance are called **life insurance**, while motor and property insurance are called **general insurance**.

### Example Working Out a Life Insurance Premium

Mr Hafiz is <InlineMath math="30" /> years old and wants a term life policy with a face value of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,150{,}000" />. Using the example table, work out **his annual premium**.

<BlockMath math="\frac{150{,}000}{1{,}000} \times 2.60 = 150 \times 2.60 = 390" />

His annual premium is <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,390" />. Check with the model: at <InlineMath math="r = 2.6" /> and <InlineMath math="f = 15" />, it reads <InlineMath math="390" />. If he bought the same policy at age <InlineMath math="45" />, the premium would be <InlineMath math="150 \times 6.00 = \text{RM}\,900" />.

## The Coinsurance Clause in Property Insurance

A property policy usually has a **coinsurance clause**: the owner must insure at least a set percentage of the property's value, for example <InlineMath math="80\%" />. If the sum insured is less than the required amount, compensation is reduced in proportion:

<BlockMath math="\text{Compensation} = \frac{\text{sum insured}}{\text{required insurance}} \times \text{loss} - \text{deductible}" />

### Example Compensation with Coinsurance

Mrs Lim's house is worth <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,400{,}000" /> and insured for <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,240{,}000" />. The policy has <InlineMath math="80\%" /> coinsurance and a deductible of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,1{,}000" />. A fire causes a loss of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,60{,}000" />. Work out **the compensation** and the part Mrs Lim bears herself.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Required amount} = 80\% \times 400{,}000 = 320{,}000" />
<BlockMath math="\text{Compensation} = \frac{240{,}000}{320{,}000} \times 60{,}000 - 1{,}000 = 45{,}000 - 1{,}000 = 44{,}000" />
<BlockMath math="\text{Borne by the owner} = 60{,}000 - 44{,}000 = 16{,}000" />
</MathContainer>

Check with the model: at <InlineMath math="i = 24" />, it reads <InlineMath math="44{,}000" />. If Mrs Lim insured at least <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,320{,}000" />, the compensation would rise to <InlineMath math="60{,}000 - 1{,}000 = \text{RM}\,59{,}000" />. This chart compares the two cases.

## Common Mistakes about Insurance

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | How to check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Using the property value, not the required amount, in the coinsurance fraction. | The denominator is the coinsurance percentage times the property value. | Work out the required amount first. |
| Forgetting the deductible. | The deductible is subtracted from the compensation for every claim. | Check the policy terms. |
| Dividing the face value by <InlineMath math="100" /> for the premium. | Rates are given per <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,1{,}000" />. | Read the units in the rate table. |
| Compensation larger than the sum insured. | The insurer never pays more than the sum insured. | Compare the answer with the face value. |

The first mistake is the most common one in coinsurance questions. **Work out the required amount before the fraction**.

## Practice on Insurance

These questions move from premiums to compensation. **State the amount the owner bears** in every compensation question.

1. Using the example table, work out the annual premium for a <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,200{,}000" /> life policy for someone aged <InlineMath math="40" />.

2. A car is damaged with a repair cost of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,3{,}500" />. The policy deductible is <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,500" />. Work out the compensation.

3. A property worth <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,250{,}000" /> is insured for <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,150{,}000" /> with <InlineMath math="80\%" /> coinsurance and a deductible of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,2{,}000" />. The loss is <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,40{,}000" />. Work out the compensation.

4. In question three, what is the minimum amount that should be insured so the compensation isn't reduced?

### Solutions

**Solution 1**: Divide the face value by <InlineMath math="1{,}000" />, then multiply by the rate:

<BlockMath math="\frac{200{,}000}{1{,}000} \times 4.40 = 200 \times 4.40 = 880" />

The annual premium is <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,880" />. Check with the model: at <InlineMath math="r = 4.4" /> and <InlineMath math="f = 20" />, it reads <InlineMath math="880" />.

**Solution 2**: Subtract the deductible from the loss:

<BlockMath math="3{,}500 - 500 = 3{,}000" />

The compensation is <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,3{,}000" />, and the owner **bears** <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,500" />.

**Solution 3**: Work out the required amount first:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="80\% \times 250{,}000 = 200{,}000" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{150{,}000}{200{,}000} \times 40{,}000 - 2{,}000 = 30{,}000 - 2{,}000 = 28{,}000" />
</MathContainer>

The compensation is <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,28{,}000" />, and the owner bears <InlineMath math="40{,}000 - 28{,}000 = \text{RM}\,12{,}000" />.

**Solution 4**: The minimum is **the required amount**, <InlineMath math="80\% \times 250{,}000 = \text{RM}\,200{,}000" />. At that amount, the coinsurance fraction becomes <InlineMath math="1" /> and only the deductible is subtracted.

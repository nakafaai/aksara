## Finding a Limit from the Limits of Its Parts

The **limit rules** calculate the limit of a sum, difference, product, or quotient from the limits of its parts. Check that the component limits exist. For a quotient, <Highlight>the denominator limit must also be nonzero</Highlight>.

## Basic Properties of Limits

Two rules cover the simplest cases, the limit of a **constant function** and the limit of the identity function. Both are read directly from the graph, and every other rule is built on top of them.

### Constant Property

The simplest property is the limit of a **constant function**. If <InlineMath math="k" /> is a constant, then:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to c} k = k" />

A constant does not change as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="c" />, so its limit is the constant itself.

### Identity Property

For the identity function, the following holds:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to c} x = c" />

When <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="c" />, the value of function <InlineMath math="f(x) = x" /> also approaches <InlineMath math="c" />.

Compare the constant function <InlineMath math="y=3" /> and the identity function <InlineMath math="y=x" /> as <InlineMath math="x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="2" />. The constant function has **unchanging output**. For the identity function, **output follows input**.

| Input | Constant function | Identity function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1.9" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="1.9" /> |
| <InlineMath math="1.99" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="1.99" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2.01" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="2.01" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2.1" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="2.1" /> |

## Arithmetic Operation Properties

Suppose <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L" /> and <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to c} g(x) = M" /> where <InlineMath math="L" /> and <InlineMath math="M" /> are real numbers. The rules then move the limit through a sum, a product, a **constant factor**, and a quotient, provided the denominator does not approach zero.

### Addition and Subtraction Properties

The limit of the **sum** or **difference** of two functions equals the sum or difference of the **limits of each function**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{x \to c} [f(x) + g(x)] \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to c} f(x) + \lim_{x \to c} g(x) \\ &amp;= L + M \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{x \to c} [f(x) - g(x)] \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to c} f(x) - \lim_{x \to c} g(x) \\ &amp;= L - M \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

> A compound limit becomes several simpler limits that can be evaluated separately.

For example, as <InlineMath math="x\to2" />, the limits of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="x+1" /> are <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />. Applying the rule gives:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\lim_{x\to2}[x+(x+1)]&=2+3=5\\\lim_{x\to2}[x-(x+1)]&=2-3=-1\end{aligned}" />

Watch the sum rule on two lines. As <InlineMath math="x \to 2" />, <InlineMath math="f(x) = \tfrac{x}{2} + 1" /> heads to <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="g(x) = 3 - x" /> heads to <InlineMath math="1" />. Their sum <InlineMath math="f(x) + g(x) = 4 - \tfrac{x}{2}" /> heads to <InlineMath math="3 = 2 + 1" />, exactly the sum of the two limits.

### Multiplication Property

The limit of the **product** of two functions equals the product of the **limits of each function**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{x \to c} [f(x) \cdot g(x)] \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to c} f(x) \cdot \lim_{x \to c} g(x) \\ &amp;= L \cdot M \end{aligned}" />

For example, as <InlineMath math="x\to2" />, the limits of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="x+1" /> are <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />. Applying the rule gives:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to2}x(x+1)=2\cdot3=6" />

The product rule works the same way. The curve <InlineMath math="f(x)g(x) = (\tfrac{x}{2} + 1)(3 - x)" /> passes through <InlineMath math="(2, 2)" />, because the limits multiply: <InlineMath math="2 \cdot 1 = 2" />. The product curve is no longer a line, yet its limit still comes straight from the two factors.

### Multiplication by Constant Property

A **constant** can be factored out from the limit sign:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to c} [k \cdot f(x)] = k \cdot \lim_{x \to c} f(x) = k \cdot L" />

With constant factor <InlineMath math="5" /> and <InlineMath math="x\to2" />, we obtain:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to2}5x=5\lim_{x\to2}x=5\cdot2=10" />

The constant factor stretches every height by the same amount. Near <InlineMath math="x = 2" /> the violet line <InlineMath math="y = x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="2" />, and the orange line <InlineMath math="y = 5x" /> approaches <InlineMath math="5 \cdot 2 = 10" />, five times as high at every input.

### Division Property

The limit of the **quotient** of two functions equals the quotient of the **limits of each function**, provided the limit of the denominator is **not zero**:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{\lim_{x \to c} f(x)}{\lim_{x \to c} g(x)} = \frac{L}{M}" />

The quotient rule applies when <InlineMath math="M \neq 0" />.

For example, as <InlineMath math="x\to2" />, the limits of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="x+1" /> are <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="3" />. Applying the rule gives:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x}{x+1}=\frac23" />

## Power and Root Properties

A power or a root passes through the limit when the result stays **inside the domain**. That domain condition keeps the root defined, so a negative value under an even root stops the rule from applying.

### Power Property

The limit of a **function raised to a power** equals the **power** of the limit of the function:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to c} [f(x)]^n = \left[\lim_{x \to c} f(x)\right]^n = L^n" />

In this rule, <InlineMath math="n" /> is a positive integer.

For the positive integer exponent <InlineMath math="3" />, calculate the limit from the inner limit:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to2}(x+1)^3=(2+1)^3=27" />

### Root Property

The limit of the **root of a function** equals the **root** of the limit of the function:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to c} \sqrt[n]{f(x)} = \sqrt[n]{\lim_{x \to c} f(x)} = \sqrt[n]{L}" />

**Domain condition**: The root must be defined for inputs near the point being approached.
- If <InlineMath math="n" /> is odd: the real root is defined for every real value of <InlineMath math="L" />
- If <InlineMath math="n" /> is even: the function under the root must remain nonnegative near the point, and <InlineMath math="L \geq 0" />

For comparison, a cube root accepts negative real input, whereas a real square root does not:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to-8}\sqrt[3]{x}=\sqrt[3]{-8}=-2" />

## Examples of Applying Limit Properties

Each example applies **one rule at a time** and names it, so you can follow the same order on a similar expression. The last example shows what changes when a root is involved.

### Limit of a Polynomial

Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 + 5x - 1)" />.

**Solution**:

Using limit properties:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 + 5x - 1) \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to 2} 3x^2 \\ &amp;\quad{}+ \lim_{x \to 2} 5x - \lim_{x \to 2} 1 \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="= 3 \lim_{x \to 2} x^2 + 5 \lim_{x \to 2} x - 1" />
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}  &amp;= 3(2)^2 + 5(2) - 1 \\ &amp;= 12 + 10 - 1 \\ &amp;= 21 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

### Limit of a Rational Function

Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{x\sqrt{x}}{x^2 + 3}" />.

**Solution**:

Using division and multiplication properties:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{x\sqrt{x}}{x^2 + 3} = \frac{\lim_{x \to 4} x\sqrt{x}}{\lim_{x \to 4} (x^2 + 3)}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{\lim_{x \to 4} x \cdot \lim_{x \to 4} \sqrt{x}}{\lim_{x \to 4} x^2 + \lim_{x \to 4} 3}" />
</MathContainer>

Now we substitute the value <InlineMath math="x = 4" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to4}\frac{x\sqrt{x}}{x^2+3}= \frac{4 \cdot \sqrt{4}}{4^2 + 3} = \frac{4 \cdot 2}{16 + 3} = \frac{8}{19}" />
</MathContainer>

In **decimal** form: <InlineMath math="\frac{8}{19} \approx 0.421" />

### Limit of a Radical Function

Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \sqrt{x^2 - 3x + 2}" />.

**Solution**:

Using the root property (since <InlineMath math="n = 2" /> is even, we need to ensure the result inside the root is not negative):

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{x \to 0} \sqrt{x^2 - 3x + 2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{\lim_{x \to 0} (x^2 - 3x + 2)} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Calculate the limit inside the root first:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \lim_{x \to 0} (x^2 - 3x + 2) &amp;= 0^2 - 3(0) + 2 \\ &amp;= 0 - 0 + 2 \\ &amp;= 2 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Since <InlineMath math="2 > 0" />, we can use the root property:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x\to0}\sqrt{x^2-3x+2}= \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414" />

## Exercises

Each problem asks for a limit that uses one or more of the rules above. Write down the **rule used at every step**.

1. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 3} (2x^2 - 4x + 1)" />

2. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{3x + 2}{x^2 + 1}" />

3. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 4} \sqrt{x + 5}" />

4. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 2} (x + 1)^3" />

5. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5x^2 + 3x}{2x + 1}" />

### Worked Solutions

**Problem 1**. Using **addition and multiplication** by constant properties:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{x \to 3} (2x^2 - 4x + 1) \\ &amp;= 2\lim_{x \to 3} x^2 \\ &amp;\quad{}- 4\lim_{x \to 3} x + \lim_{x \to 3} 1 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Substitute <InlineMath math="x = 3" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}  &amp;= 2(3)^2 - 4(3) + 1 \\ &amp;= 2(9) - 12 + 1 \\ &amp;= 18 - 12 + 1 \\ &amp;= 7 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

**Problem 2**. Using the division property:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{3x + 2}{x^2 + 1} = \frac{\lim_{x \to 1} (3x + 2)}{\lim_{x \to 1} (x^2 + 1)}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{3(1) + 2}{1^2 + 1} = \frac{5}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

In **decimal** form: <InlineMath math="\frac{5}{2} = 2.5" />

**Problem 3**. Using the **root property**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 4} \sqrt{x + 5} = \sqrt{\lim_{x \to 4} (x + 5)}" />
<BlockMath math="= \sqrt{4 + 5} = \sqrt{9} = 3" />
</MathContainer>

**Problem 4**. Using the **power property**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 2} (x + 1)^3 = \left[\lim_{x \to 2} (x + 1)\right]^3" />
<BlockMath math="= (2 + 1)^3 = 3^3 = 27" />
</MathContainer>

**Problem 5**. Using the **division property**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5x^2 + 3x}{2x + 1} = \frac{\lim_{x \to 0} (5x^2 + 3x)}{\lim_{x \to 0} (2x + 1)}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{5(0)^2 + 3(0)}{2(0) + 1} = \frac{0}{1} = 0" />
</MathContainer>

## When Substitution Resolves a Trigonometric Limit

**Trigonometric limits** ask the same local question as algebraic limits: what value does an expression approach near a chosen input? Sine and cosine are continuous everywhere, while tangent is continuous wherever cosine is nonzero, so direct substitution still works whenever the resulting expression is defined.

The special work begins when substitution produces an indeterminate form such as <InlineMath math="\frac{0}{0}" />. Near zero, use the **fundamental sine limit** and familiar [trigonometric identities](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-modeling/trigonometric-identity) to rewrite the expression into a form whose limit can be evaluated.

## Sine Limit at Zero

When an angle measured in radians approaches zero, <Highlight>the ratio of its sine to the angle</Highlight> approaches one. Measuring the angle in radians is what makes the value exactly one, and that single fact decides the value of every ratio built from sine and cosine.

### Basic Sine Limit

The limit is:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1" />

The expression is undefined at <InlineMath math="x=0" />, so direct substitution does not establish the limit. A geometric proof uses the unit circle and the squeeze theorem.

> In this theorem, measure <InlineMath math="x" /> in **radians**. Using degrees gives a different result.

On the unit circle, for <InlineMath math="0<x<\pi/2" />, comparing triangle and sector areas gives <InlineMath math="\sin x<x<\tan x" />. Dividing by positive quantities produces **two enclosing bounds**:

<BlockMath math="\cos x<\frac{\sin x}{x}<1" />

As <InlineMath math="x\to0^+" />, both bounds approach <InlineMath math="1" />. On the left, <InlineMath math="\sin(-x)/(-x)=\sin x/x" />, so the limit is the same. The graph illustrates this approach, while the expression remains undefined at <InlineMath math="x=0" />.

### Consequences of the Basic Limit

Use this limit to derive the following results:

Because <InlineMath math="\tan x=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}" /> and <InlineMath math="\cos x\to1" />:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = 1" />

Use the identity <InlineMath math="1-\cos x=2\sin^2\frac x2" />:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x} = 0" />

Use the identity <InlineMath math="1-\cos x=2\sin^2\frac x2" />:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} = \frac{1}{2}" />

The identity gives both cosine limits directly, without using **derivative rules** that are developed later:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\frac{1-\cos x}{x} \\ &amp;=\sin\frac{x}{2}\cdot\frac{\sin(x/2)}{x/2}\;\\ &amp;\longrightarrow\;0\cdot 1 \\ &amp;=0 \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\frac{1-\cos x}{x^2} =\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sin(x/2)}{x/2}\right)^2\; \\ &amp;\longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{2} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

## Properties of Trigonometric Limits

The **fundamental limit** determines several related ratios, because a constant factor inside the angle moves out of the limit and the reciprocal of the ratio behaves the same way.

### Trigonometric Ratios

For real constants <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b \neq 0" />, **all following limits are taken as <InlineMath math="x \to 0" />**:

| Limit | Result | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin ax}{bx}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{a}{b}" /> | Manipulation from basic theorem |
| <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan ax}{bx}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{a}{b}" /> | Because <InlineMath math="\tan ax = \frac{\sin ax}{\cos ax}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin ax}{\sin bx}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{a}{b}" /> | Combination of two sine limits |
| <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan ax}{\tan bx}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{a}{b}" /> | Combination of two tangent limits |

### Trigonometric Combinations

The mixed tangent and sine ratio follows the same rule. For <InlineMath math="a\ne0" /> and <InlineMath math="b\ne0" />, separate it into fundamental limits:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\tan ax}{\sin bx}&=\lim_{x\to0}\left(\frac{\tan ax}{ax}\cdot\frac{bx}{\sin bx}\cdot\frac ab\right)\\&=1\cdot1\cdot\frac ab=\frac ab\end{aligned}" />

If <InlineMath math="a=0" />, the **numerator** is always zero, so the result is zero. The final rule therefore holds for every real constant <InlineMath math="a" /> with <InlineMath math="b\ne0" />.

## Techniques for Solving Trigonometric Limits

Some trigonometric limits resolve by substitution, and others need an **identity or a known limit**. The expression itself tells you which case applies, because a sum or a difference inside the trigonometric function is the sign that an identity is needed.

### Substitution and Manipulation Techniques

When facing complex trigonometric limits, we often need to manipulate expressions to use the fundamental theorem.

**Calculate**: <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{3x}" />

We manipulate to obtain the standard form:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{3x} &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2 \sin 2x}{2 \cdot 3x} \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{\sin 2x}{2x} \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{2}{3} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{2x} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 1 = \frac{2}{3}" />
</MathContainer>

### Direct Substitution with a Nonzero Denominator

**Direct substitution** applies when the function is continuous at the target point and its denominator is nonzero. A limit in general does not require the function to be defined at that point.

**Calculate**: <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{4}} \frac{\sin x + \cos x}{\sin x}" />

Since <InlineMath math="\sin(\frac{\pi}{4}) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \neq 0" />, the denominator is not zero at <InlineMath math="x = \frac{\pi}{4}" />.

Direct substitution:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{4}} \frac{\sin x + \cos x}{\sin x} = \frac{\sin \frac{\pi}{4} + \cos \frac{\pi}{4}}{\sin \frac{\pi}{4}}" />
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}  &amp;= \frac{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} \\ &amp;= \frac{2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} \\ &amp;= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{2} \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{2}} \\ &amp;= 2 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

### Techniques for Special Forms

For limits involving the form <InlineMath math="\frac{0}{0}" />, we need special techniques.

**Calculate**: <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{2}} \frac{\sin^2(x - \frac{\pi}{2})}{x(x - \frac{\pi}{2})}" />

Let <InlineMath math="u = x - \frac{\pi}{2}" />, then when <InlineMath math="x \to \frac{\pi}{2}" />, we have <InlineMath math="u \to 0" /> and <InlineMath math="x = u + \frac{\pi}{2}" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{u \to 0} \frac{\sin^2 u}{(u + \frac{\pi}{2}) \cdot u} \\ &amp;= \lim_{u \to 0} \frac{\sin^2 u}{u^2} \cdot \frac{u}{u + \frac{\pi}{2}} \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="= \lim_{u \to 0} \left(\frac{\sin u}{u}\right)^2 \cdot \lim_{u \to 0} \frac{u}{u + \frac{\pi}{2}}" />
<BlockMath math="= 1^2 \cdot \lim_{u \to 0} \frac{u}{\frac{\pi}{2} + u} = 1 \cdot \frac{0}{\frac{\pi}{2}} = 0" />
</MathContainer>

## A Ratio of Cotangent Functions

Write **cotangent as cosine divided by sine**. The cosine ratio approaches one, while the sine ratio contributes the ratio of the angle factors.

**Calculate**: <InlineMath math="\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\cot 5t}{\cot 10t}" />

Using the definition of cotangent:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\cot 5t}{\cot 10t} &= \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\frac{\cos 5t}{\sin 5t}}{\frac{\cos 10t}{\sin 10t}} \\ &= \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\cos 5t \sin 10t}{\sin 5t \cos 10t}\end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="= \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\cos 5t}{\cos 10t} \cdot \frac{\sin 10t}{\sin 5t} = 1 \cdot \frac{10}{5} = 2" />
</MathContainer>

## Exercises

Each problem asks for a trigonometric limit. Decide whether substitution is enough or an identity is needed before calculating. Write the limit you use at every step, and check the result against the **fundamental limit** whenever the angle is scaled.

1. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{4x}" />

2. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan 2x}{\sin 5x}" />

3. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos 3x}{x^2}" />

4. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{6}} \frac{\sin x - \frac{1}{2}}{x - \frac{\pi}{6}}" />

5. Calculate <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x \cos x}{x}" />

### Matching the Angle and Denominator

Use algebraic manipulation to obtain the **standard form**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{4x} &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{3 \sin 3x}{3 \cdot 4x} \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{\sin 3x}{3x} \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{3}{4} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{3x} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot 1 = \frac{3}{4}" />
</MathContainer>

### A Tangent to Sine Ratio

Use **trigonometric ratio** properties:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan 2x}{\sin 5x} \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan 2x}{2x} \cdot \frac{5x}{\sin 5x} \cdot \frac{2x}{5x} \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan 2x}{2x} \cdot \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5x}{\sin 5x} \cdot \frac{2}{5}" />
<BlockMath math="= 1 \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{2}{5} = \frac{2}{5}" />
</MathContainer>

### A Cosine Difference over a Square

Use the **half angle identity** to write the cosine difference as a squared sine:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos 3x}{x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2\sin^2\frac{3x}{2}}{x^2}" />
<BlockMath math="= \lim_{x \to 0} 2 \cdot \left(\frac{\sin\frac{3x}{2}}{\frac{3x}{2}}\right)^2 \cdot \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2" />
<BlockMath math="= 2 \cdot 1^2 \cdot \frac{9}{4} = \frac{9}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

### Shifting the Angle to Zero

Change variables so the **angle approaches zero**, then use the fundamental sine limit. Let <InlineMath math="h = x - \frac{\pi}{6}" />, then <InlineMath math="x = h + \frac{\pi}{6}" /> and when <InlineMath math="x \to \frac{\pi}{6}" />, we have <InlineMath math="h \to 0" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sin(h + \frac{\pi}{6}) - \frac{1}{2}}{h}" />
<BlockMath math="= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sin h \cos\frac{\pi}{6} + \cos h \sin\frac{\pi}{6} - \frac{1}{2}}{h}" />
<BlockMath math="= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sin h \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \cos h \cdot \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}}{h}" />
<BlockMath math="= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin h + \frac{1}{2}(\cos h - 1)}{h}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sin h}{h} + \frac{1}{2} \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\cos h - 1}{h}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot 1 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

### Using the Double Angle Identity

Use the **double angle identity** before canceling the shared factor:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x \cos x}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{1}{2}\sin 2x}{x}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{2} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{x} = \frac{1}{2} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2 \sin 2x}{2x}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{2x} = 1 \cdot 1 = 1" />
</MathContainer>

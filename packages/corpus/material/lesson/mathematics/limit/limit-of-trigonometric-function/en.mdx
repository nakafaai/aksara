## Direct Substitution Cases

**Trigonometric limits** ask the same local question as algebraic limits: what value does an expression approach near a chosen input? Sine and cosine are continuous everywhere, while tangent is continuous wherever cosine is nonzero, so direct substitution works whenever the resulting expression is defined.

Special steps begin when substitution yields an indeterminate form such as <InlineMath math="\frac{0}{0}" />. Near zero, use the **fundamental sine limit** and familiar [trigonometric identities](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-modeling/trigonometric-identity) to rewrite the expression into a form whose limit evaluates cleanly.

## Sine Limit at Zero

When an angle measured in radians approaches zero, <Highlight>the ratio of its sine to the angle</Highlight> approaches one. Measuring in radians is what makes the limit exactly one, and that single result governs every trigonometric ratio near zero.

### Basic Sine Limit

The fundamental trigonometric limit reads:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1" />

Because the ratio is undefined at <InlineMath math="x = 0" />, direct substitution cannot prove the limit. A geometric proof uses the unit circle together with the **squeeze theorem**.

> In trigonometric limits, always measure <InlineMath math="x" /> in **radians** because degrees rescale the angle and change the limit.

On the unit circle for <InlineMath math="x" /> between <InlineMath math="0" /> and <InlineMath math="\pi/2" />, comparing triangle and sector areas establishes two ordered bounds:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\sin x < x < \tan x" />
  <BlockMath math="\cos x < \frac{\sin x}{x} < 1" />
</MathContainer>

As <InlineMath math="x \to 0^+" />, both outer bounds approach <InlineMath math="1" />. Because the ratio is even across <InlineMath math="x = 0" />, the left side approaches <InlineMath math="1" /> as well, even though an open hole remains at <InlineMath math="(0, 1)" />.

### Related Cosine Limits

Combine the basic sine limit with tangent and **half-angle identities** to derive three companion limits. Writing tangent as sine over cosine with <InlineMath math="\cos x \to 1" /> gives:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = 1" />

Now apply the half-angle cosine identity:

<BlockMath math="1 - \cos x = 2\sin^2\frac{x}{2}" />

Substituting this identity resolves both cosine ratios directly without needing derivative rules:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \sin\frac{x}{2} \cdot \frac{\sin(x/2)}{x/2} = 0 \cdot 1 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sin(x/2)}{x/2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

Check the second cosine limit numerically. At <InlineMath math="x = 1" />, <InlineMath math="0.5" />, <InlineMath math="0.1" />, and <InlineMath math="0.01" />, the ratio rises through <InlineMath math="0.460" />, <InlineMath math="0.490" />, <InlineMath math="0.4996" />, and <InlineMath math="0.499996" /> toward <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}" />.

## Limit Ratio Properties

Because a constant scale inside the angle factors out cleanly, four standard ratios follow immediately for real constants <InlineMath math="a" /> and <InlineMath math="b \neq 0" /> as <InlineMath math="x \to 0" />:

| Limit | Result | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin ax}{bx}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{a}{b}" /> | Manipulation from basic theorem |
| <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan ax}{bx}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{a}{b}" /> | Because <InlineMath math="\tan ax = \frac{\sin ax}{\cos ax}" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin ax}{\sin bx}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{a}{b}" /> | Combination of two sine limits |
| <InlineMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan ax}{\tan bx}" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{a}{b}" /> | Combination of two tangent limits |

The mixed tangent-sine ratio obeys the same formula. For <InlineMath math="a \neq 0" /> and <InlineMath math="b \neq 0" />, split it into fundamental pieces:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\tan ax}{\sin bx}&=\lim_{x\to0}\left(\frac{\tan ax}{ax}\cdot\frac{bx}{\sin bx}\cdot\frac ab\right)\\&=1\cdot1\cdot\frac ab=\frac ab\end{aligned}" />

When <InlineMath math="a = 0" />, the numerator stays zero so the limit is <InlineMath math="0" />, meaning the rule holds for every real <InlineMath math="a" /> with <InlineMath math="b \neq 0" />.

Cotangent ratios reduce the same way by rewriting **cotangent as cosine divided by sine**. Evaluate:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\cot 5t}{\cot 10t}" />

Separating the cosine and sine parts gives:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\cot 5t}{\cot 10t} &= \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\frac{\cos 5t}{\sin 5t}}{\frac{\cos 10t}{\sin 10t}} \\ &= \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\cos 5t \sin 10t}{\sin 5t \cos 10t}\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\cos 5t}{\cos 10t} \cdot \frac{\sin 10t}{\sin 5t} = 1 \cdot \frac{10}{5} = 2" />
</MathContainer>

## Trig Limit Techniques

Some trigonometric limits finish with substitution, while others require **angle scaling**, identities, or a change of variable. Inspecting the angle and denominator first reveals which technique fits.

### Angle Scaling

When an angle carries a **constant factor**, match the denominator to that same angle. Evaluate:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{3x}" />

Multiply top and bottom by <InlineMath math="2" /> to isolate the standard sine form:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{3x} &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2 \sin 2x}{2 \cdot 3x} \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{\sin 2x}{2x} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= \frac{2}{3} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{2x} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 1 = \frac{2}{3}" />
</MathContainer>

Testing numerically at <InlineMath math="x = 0.5" />, <InlineMath math="0.1" />, and <InlineMath math="0.01" /> gives <InlineMath math="0.561" />, <InlineMath math="0.662" />, and <InlineMath math="0.6666" />, closing in on <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{3}" />.

### Nonzero Denominators

**Direct substitution** works immediately whenever the expression is continuous at the target point and the denominator is nonzero. Evaluate:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{4}} \frac{\sin x + \cos x}{\sin x}" />

At <InlineMath math="\pi/4" />, the denominator sine equals <InlineMath math="\sqrt{2}/2" />, which is nonzero, so substitute directly:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{4}} \frac{\sin x + \cos x}{\sin x} = \frac{\sin \frac{\pi}{4} + \cos \frac{\pi}{4}}{\sin \frac{\pi}{4}}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;= \frac{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} \\ &amp;= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} = 2 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

### Variable Substitution

When the target point is not zero and substitution yields <InlineMath math="\frac{0}{0}" />, **shift the variable** so the new angle approaches zero. Evaluate:

<BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{2}} \frac{\sin^2\left(x - \frac{\pi}{2}\right)}{x\left(x - \frac{\pi}{2}\right)}" />

Set the shifted variable <InlineMath math="u" /> so that <InlineMath math="u \to 0" /> as <InlineMath math="x \to \pi/2" />:

<BlockMath math="u = x - \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad x = u + \frac{\pi}{2}" />

Rewriting the limit in terms of <InlineMath math="u" /> separates a squared basic sine limit:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{u \to 0} \frac{\sin^2 u}{(u + \frac{\pi}{2}) \cdot u} \\ &amp;= \lim_{u \to 0} \frac{\sin^2 u}{u^2} \cdot \frac{u}{u + \frac{\pi}{2}} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= \lim_{u \to 0} \left(\frac{\sin u}{u}\right)^2 \cdot \lim_{u \to 0} \frac{u}{u + \frac{\pi}{2}}" />
  <BlockMath math="= 1^2 \cdot \frac{0}{\frac{\pi}{2}} = 0" />
</MathContainer>

## Checking Trig Limit Values

Decide before calculating whether substitution works or whether scaling, identities, or a variable shift is needed.

1. Evaluate the scaled sine limit:

   <BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{4x}" />

2. Evaluate the mixed tangent and sine ratio:

   <BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan 2x}{\sin 5x}" />

3. Evaluate the quadratic cosine limit:

   <BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos 3x}{x^2}" />

4. Evaluate the shifted sine difference quotient:

   <BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{6}} \frac{\sin x - \frac{1}{2}}{x - \frac{\pi}{6}}" />

5. Evaluate the sine-cosine product limit:

   <BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x \cos x}{x}" />

Compare your steps against the worked solutions below.

**Problem 1**. Scale numerator and denominator by <InlineMath math="3" /> to match the **standard form**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{4x} &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{3 \sin 3x}{3 \cdot 4x} \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{\sin 3x}{3x} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= \frac{3}{4} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{3x} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot 1 = \frac{3}{4}" />
</MathContainer>

**Problem 2**. Split into two **fundamental ratio limits**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan 2x}{\sin 5x} \\ &amp;= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan 2x}{2x} \cdot \frac{5x}{\sin 5x} \cdot \frac{2x}{5x} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan 2x}{2x} \cdot \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5x}{\sin 5x} \cdot \frac{2}{5} = \frac{2}{5}" />
</MathContainer>

**Problem 3**. Use the **half-angle identity** to rewrite the numerator as a squared sine:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos 3x}{x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2\sin^2\frac{3x}{2}}{x^2}" />
  <BlockMath math="= \lim_{x \to 0} 2 \cdot \left(\frac{\sin\frac{3x}{2}}{\frac{3x}{2}}\right)^2 \cdot \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2" />
  <BlockMath math="= 2 \cdot 1^2 \cdot \frac{9}{4} = \frac{9}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

**Problem 4**. Shift the variable so the **angle approaches zero**, then expand using the sine addition formula:

<BlockMath math="h = x - \frac{\pi}{6}, \quad x = h + \frac{\pi}{6}, \quad h \to 0" />

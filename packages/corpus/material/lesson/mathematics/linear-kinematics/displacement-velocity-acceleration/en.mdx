## Differentiate to Go Down and Integrate to Go Up

For a particle moving along a straight line, the **displacement** <InlineMath math="s" />, **velocity** <InlineMath math="v" /> and **acceleration** <InlineMath math="a" /> are linked by calculus:

<BlockMath math="v = \frac{ds}{dt}, \qquad a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}" />

Differentiation takes you from <InlineMath math="s" /> to <InlineMath math="v" /> to <InlineMath math="a" />, and integration takes you the other way, <InlineMath math="v = \int a\,dt" /> and <InlineMath math="s = \int v\,dt" />. The particle is **momentarily at rest** when <InlineMath math="v = 0" />, and it usually **changes direction** at that moment. The distance travelled adds up every part of the journey, while the displacement is only the final position measured from the origin.

In these notes you move time forward and watch the gradient of the displacement graph, follow a particle that turns back, compare displacement with distance, and build velocity from acceleration.

## Displacement and Its Gradient

A particle moves along a straight line with displacement <InlineMath math="s = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t" /> metres from a fixed point <InlineMath math="O" />, with <InlineMath math="t" /> in seconds. Differentiate once for velocity and again for acceleration:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="v = \frac{ds}{dt} = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 = 3(t - 1)(t - 3)" />
<BlockMath math="a = \frac{dv}{dt} = 6t - 12" />
</MathContainer>

Velocity is **the gradient of the displacement-time graph**. Move time below. The tangent line becomes horizontal at <InlineMath math="t = 1" /> and <InlineMath math="t = 3" />, when the particle is momentarily at rest.

To find the distance in the first <InlineMath math="4" /> seconds, **split the journey at every moment the particle stops**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="0 \le t \le 1: \quad |s(1) - s(0)| = |4 - 0| = 4" />
<BlockMath math="1 \le t \le 3: \quad |s(3) - s(1)| = |0 - 4| = 4" />
<BlockMath math="3 \le t \le 4: \quad |s(4) - s(3)| = |4 - 0| = 4" />
<BlockMath math="\text{Distance} = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12\text{ m}" />
</MathContainer>

The displacement at <InlineMath math="t = 4" /> is only <InlineMath math="s(4) = 64 - 96 + 36 = 4\text{ m}" />, because the part to the left cancels some of the journey to the right.

## From Acceleration to Velocity and Displacement

If the acceleration is given, integrate to get velocity, then integrate again for displacement. Each integration gives a constant, and that constant is fixed by **the initial conditions**.

A particle has acceleration <InlineMath math="a = 4 - 2t" /> and initial velocity <InlineMath math="3\text{ m s}^{-1}" />, starting from <InlineMath math="O" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="v = \int (4 - 2t)\,dt = 4t - t^2 + c, \quad v(0) = 3 \Rightarrow c = 3" />
<BlockMath math="v = 3 + 4t - t^2" />
<BlockMath math="s = \int (3 + 4t - t^2)\,dt = 3t + 2t^2 - \frac{t^3}{3}, \quad s(0) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

The maximum velocity happens when <InlineMath math="a = 0" />, at <InlineMath math="t = 2" />, with <InlineMath math="v = 3 + 8 - 4 = 7\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. Change the initial velocity <InlineMath math="u" /> below and see when the particle stops.

## Common Mistakes in Kinematics

| Mistake | Why it is wrong | How to check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Treating distance as displacement. | Parts of the journey in opposite directions cancel in the displacement. | Find when the particle stops and split the journey. |
| Forgetting the constant of integration. | The initial velocity or displacement is lost from the answer. | Substitute <InlineMath math="t = 0" /> and compare with the initial condition. |
| Assuming the particle is at <InlineMath math="O" /> when <InlineMath math="v = 0" />. | <InlineMath math="v = 0" /> means at rest, not at the origin. | Work out <InlineMath math="s" /> at that time. |
| Assuming a negative acceleration means the particle slows down. | The particle speeds up when <InlineMath math="v" /> and <InlineMath math="a" /> have the same sign. | Compare the signs of <InlineMath math="v" /> and <InlineMath math="a" />. |
| Taking a negative root for time. | Time in these questions is not negative. | Reject any value with <InlineMath math="t < 0" />. |

The first mistake is the most common. **Every time the particle stops**, start a new part in the distance calculation.

## Kinematics of Linear Motion Practice

Write <InlineMath math="s" />, <InlineMath math="v" /> and <InlineMath math="a" /> side by side so that **the direction of differentiating or integrating is clear**.

1. A particle moves with displacement <InlineMath math="s = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t" /> metres. Find the times and positions when the particle is momentarily at rest, and the time when its acceleration is zero.

2. The velocity of a particle is <InlineMath math="v = 8 - 2t" /> metres per second, and it starts from <InlineMath math="O" />. Find the displacement at <InlineMath math="t = 6" /> and the distance travelled in the first <InlineMath math="6" /> seconds.

3. The acceleration of a particle is <InlineMath math="a = 6t - 4" />, with initial velocity <InlineMath math="-2\text{ m s}^{-1}" />. Find the velocity at <InlineMath math="t = 2" />.

4. The displacement of a particle is <InlineMath math="s = t^2 - 5t + 6" /> metres. Find the times the particle passes <InlineMath math="O" /> and its velocity at each of those times.

### Solutions

**Solution 1**: Differentiate:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="v = 6t^2 - 18t + 12 = 6(t - 1)(t - 2)" />
<BlockMath math="a = 12t - 18" />
</MathContainer>

The particle stops at <InlineMath math="t = 1" />, with <InlineMath math="s = 2 - 9 + 12 = 5\text{ m}" />, and at <InlineMath math="t = 2" />, with <InlineMath math="s = 16 - 36 + 24 = 4\text{ m}" />. The acceleration is zero at <InlineMath math="t = 1.5" /> seconds.

**Solution 2**: Integrate with <InlineMath math="s(0) = 0" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="s = 8t - t^2" />
<BlockMath math="s(6) = 48 - 36 = 12\text{ m}" />
</MathContainer>

The particle stops at <InlineMath math="t = 4" />, with <InlineMath math="s(4) = 16\text{ m}" />. The distance is <InlineMath math="16 + |12 - 16| = 20\text{ m}" />.

**Solution 3**: Integrate and use <InlineMath math="v(0) = -2" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="v = 3t^2 - 4t - 2" />
<BlockMath math="v(2) = 12 - 8 - 2 = 2\text{ m s}^{-1}" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 4**: The particle is at <InlineMath math="O" /> when <InlineMath math="s = 0" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="t^2 - 5t + 6 = (t - 2)(t - 3) = 0" />
<BlockMath math="v = 2t - 5, \quad v(2) = -1\text{ m s}^{-1}, \quad v(3) = 1\text{ m s}^{-1}" />
</MathContainer>

At <InlineMath math="t = 2" /> the particle passes <InlineMath math="O" /> **moving left**, and at <InlineMath math="t = 3" /> it passes <InlineMath math="O" /> again moving right.

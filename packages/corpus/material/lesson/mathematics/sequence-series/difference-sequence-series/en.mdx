## Sequence

### Concept

A sequence is an **ordered list of numbers** arranged according to a specific **pattern** or rule. Each number in the list is called a **term**.

Examples:

- Sequence of even numbers: <InlineMath math="2, 4, 6, 8, \dots" />
- Sequence of squares: <InlineMath math="1, 4, 9, 16, \dots" />

The focus is on **each individual term** and how the pattern forms.

Think of a sequence like **stairs**. Each step is a term, and there's a clear order from the first step, second step, and so on.

## Series

### Concept

With a series, we **add** the terms of a sequence. A series is the **sum expression** formed from those terms.

Examples (using the sequences above):

- Series of even numbers: <InlineMath math="2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + \dots" />
- Series of squares: <InlineMath math="1 + 4 + 9 + 16 + \dots" />

For a finite number of terms, the focus is the **partial sum** through the <InlineMath math="n" />-th term, usually denoted by <InlineMath math="S_n" />.

Back to the stairs picture. If the sequence represents the individual steps, the series represents the **total height** you've climbed after taking a certain number of steps.

## Core Difference

| Aspect      | Sequence                                                                                | Series                                                                           |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Form**    | Ordered list of numbers separated by commas, such as <InlineMath math="U_1, U_2, U_3, \dots" /> | Sum of numbers separated by plus signs, such as <InlineMath math="U_1 + U_2 + U_3 + \dots" /> |
| **Focus**   | Pattern and value of each individual term                                               | Result of summing the terms                                                      |
| **How to picture it** | Individual stair steps                                                                  | Total height climbed after taking stairs                                         |

## Partial Sums Connect the Two Ideas

The sum of the first <InlineMath math="n" /> terms of a sequence is its <InlineMath math="n" />th partial sum:

<BlockMath math="S_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_k" />

A sequence <InlineMath math="(a_n)" /> therefore creates a new sequence <InlineMath math="(S_n)" /> of partial sums. For <InlineMath math="a_n=n" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4,\ldots=1,2,3,4,\ldots" />
  <BlockMath math="S_1,S_2,S_3,S_4,\ldots=1,3,6,10,\ldots" />
</MathContainer>

The partial-sum sequence is neither the original sequence nor merely another notation for it.

## Finite and Infinite Sums

<InlineMath math="S_{20}" /> is a finite sum of twenty terms. For an infinite series, we instead ask whether <InlineMath math="S_n" /> approaches a limit as <InlineMath math="n\to\infty" />.

An infinite series is therefore defined through the limit of its partial sums rather than as a completed act of adding infinitely many terms one by one.

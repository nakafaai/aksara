## Arithmetic Series

An arithmetic series is the **sum** of the terms of an **arithmetic sequence**. Remember, an arithmetic sequence is one that has a constant **difference (common difference)** between its terms (<InlineMath math="b" />).

So, we are summing terms with the pattern: <InlineMath math="a, a+b, a+2b, a+3b, \dots" />.

The sum of the first <InlineMath math="n" /> terms (<InlineMath math="S_n" />) of an arithmetic series can be calculated using the formula:

<BlockMath math="S_n = \frac{n}{2}(2a + (n-1)b)" />

An equivalent formula is:

<BlockMath math="S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a + U_n)" />

Here, <InlineMath math="a" /> is the first term and <InlineMath math="U_n" /> is the <InlineMath math="n" />-th term.

Imagine you are stacking bricks. The first layer has <InlineMath math="1" /> brick, the second layer has <InlineMath math="3" /> bricks, the third layer has <InlineMath math="5" /> bricks, and so on (common difference <InlineMath math="b=2" />). An arithmetic series represents the **total** number of bricks needed to make a stack <InlineMath math="n" /> layers high.

## Geometric Series

A geometric series is the **sum** of the terms of a **geometric sequence**. Remember, a geometric sequence is one that has a constant **ratio (common ratio)** between its terms (<InlineMath math="r" />).

So, we are summing terms with the pattern: <InlineMath math="a, ar, ar^2, ar^3, \dots" />.

The sum of the first <InlineMath math="n" /> terms (<InlineMath math="S_n" />) of a geometric series can be calculated using the formula:

<BlockMath math="S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r-1}" />

This formula applies when <InlineMath math="r \neq 1" />. In the formula, <InlineMath math="a" /> is the first term and <InlineMath math="r" /> is the common ratio.

If <InlineMath math="r=1" />, every term equals <InlineMath math="a" />, so the special-case sum is:

<BlockMath math="S_n=na" />

Suppose a workshop produces <InlineMath math="1" />, then <InlineMath math="2" />, then <InlineMath math="4" /> new components in successive hours. The hourly output has ratio <InlineMath math="r=2" />. A geometric series gives the **cumulative** number of newly produced components over <InlineMath math="n \text{ hours}" />. During the first <InlineMath math="3 \text{ hours}" />, the workshop produces <InlineMath math="1+2+4=7 \text{ components}" /> in total.

## Arithmetic and Geometric Series

The table places the two sum formulas side by side, so the term being added and the resulting formula can be compared row by row.

| Feature                                   | Arithmetic Series                                                                  | Geometric Series                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Basis**                                 | Sum of terms in an arithmetic sequence (common difference <InlineMath math="b" />) | Sum of terms in a geometric sequence (common ratio <InlineMath math="r" />) |
| **Sum formula** <InlineMath math="S_n" /> | <InlineMath math="\frac{n}{2}(2a + (n-1)b)" />                                     | <InlineMath math="\frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r-1}" />                                |
| **Pattern**                               | Constant addition/subtraction                                                      | Constant multiplication/division                                            |

## Calculating Arithmetic and Geometric Sums

<BlockMath math="5+8+11+14+17" />

<BlockMath math="S_5=\frac52(5+17)=55" />

For the geometric series:

<BlockMath math="5+10+20+40+80" />

This series has the common ratio <InlineMath math="r=2" />. Its first five terms sum to:

<BlockMath math="S_5=5\frac{2^5-1}{2-1}=155" />

## Patterns of Terms and Partial Sums

Arithmetic terms depend linearly on the index, and their partial sums are quadratic when the difference is nonzero. In a geometric sequence, **each term is multiplied by a fixed ratio**. A positive ratio other than one gives an exponential pattern. A negative ratio alternates the signs.

For the two examples just calculated, compare the accumulated sums after each term is added:

| Number of terms | Arithmetic sum | Geometric sum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="5" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="13" /> | <InlineMath math="15" /> |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="24" /> | <InlineMath math="35" /> |
| <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="38" /> | <InlineMath math="75" /> |
| <InlineMath math="5" /> | <InlineMath math="55" /> | <InlineMath math="155" /> |

The name of a series comes from its sequence of terms. The partial sums <InlineMath math="S_1,S_2,S_3,\ldots" /> can follow a different pattern.

Now the geometric sums for <InlineMath math="5 + 10 + 20 + 40 + 80" />: <InlineMath math="5, 15, 35, 75, 155" />. Each new term is as large as all the earlier terms together plus <InlineMath math="5" />, so the total roughly doubles at every step. Compare the heights with the arithmetic chart: same first term, very different growth.

## Exercise

Classify each series and calculate its sum. <Highlight>Decide the type from the terms themselves</Highlight>, then apply the matching sum formula. Both series are short enough to check the formula against a direct addition.

1. <InlineMath math="30+25+20+15+10" />
2. <InlineMath math="2+6+18+54+162" />

### Solution

**Problem 1**. The first series is **arithmetic** with <InlineMath math="b=-5" />. Use its first and last terms to calculate the sum:

<BlockMath math="S_5=\frac52(30+10)=100" />

**Problem 2**. The second series is **geometric** with <InlineMath math="r=3" />. Use that ratio to calculate the sum:

<BlockMath math="S_5=2\frac{3^5-1}{3-1}=242" />

## Financial Management Starts with Goals and a Budget

**Financial management** is the process of planning and controlling how money is used to reach goals. It has five steps: **set goals, evaluate your financial position, build a plan, carry out the plan, and review it**. Its main tool is a **budget**, a list of income and spending for a period. The difference between income and spending is called **cash flow**. In the models here, you analyse a monthly budget, track cash flow and work out how long it takes to reach a savings target.

## SMART Financial Goals

A good goal is **SMART**: specific, measurable, attainable, realistic and time-bound. The goal I want to save money isn't clear enough. The goal I want to save <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,6{,}000" /> for an emergency fund in <InlineMath math="12" /> months is a SMART goal, because its amount and time frame are clear.

| Term | Time frame | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Short | Less than a year | Buying a mobile phone |
| Medium | One to five years | A car down payment |
| Long | More than five years | A house down payment or retirement |

### Example Working Out Cash Flow

Mr Amir's fixed expenses are rent of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,1{,}200" />, a car instalment of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,650" /> and insurance of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,150" />. His variable expenses total <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,1{,}800" />, and he saves <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,450" />. Work out **his monthly cash flow**.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Fixed expenses} = 1{,}200 + 650 + 150 = 2{,}000" />
<BlockMath math="\text{Cash flow} = 4{,}500 - 2{,}000 - 1{,}800 - 450 = 250" />
</MathContainer>

A cash flow of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,250" /> is a **surplus**, so Mr Amir lives within his means. Check: <InlineMath math="2{,}000 + 1{,}800 + 450 + 250 = 4{,}500" />.

### Example Time to Reach a Target

Mr Amir saves <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,450" /> and moves his <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,250" /> surplus into a savings account every month. How many months does he need to **collect** <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,6{,}000" />?

<BlockMath math="\frac{6{,}000}{450 + 250} = \frac{6{,}000}{700} = 8.57 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 9 \text{ months}" />

After <InlineMath math="8" /> months he has only <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,5{,}600" />, so the ninth month is needed. Check with the model: at <InlineMath math="m = 700" />, it reads <InlineMath math="9" />. He can reach his <InlineMath math="12" />-month goal early.

## Common Mistakes about Financial Management

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | How to check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Using gross salary in a budget. | A budget uses net income after deductions. | Check the amount that actually reaches the account. |
| Rounding the number of months down. | The last, partial month is still needed. | Multiply the number of months by the monthly savings. |
| Treating savings as whatever is left after spending. | Savings are planned first as part of the budget. | Savings must be listed in the budget. |
| A goal with no time frame. | A SMART goal needs a time frame so it can be measured. | Ask when the goal has to be reached. |

The second mistake is the most common one in questions on saving periods. **Round the number of months up**.

## Practice on Financial Management

These questions move from budgets to savings plans. **Check every answer by adding back up**.

1. Mrs Siti has a net income of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,3{,}800" />. Her fixed expenses are <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,1{,}650" />, variable expenses <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,1{,}400" /> and savings <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,380" />. Work out her cash flow.

2. Turn the goal I want to have lots of money into a SMART goal.

3. A laptop costs <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,3{,}200" />. If a student saves <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,350" /> a month, how many months are needed?

4. Under the <InlineMath math="50\% : 30\% : 20\%" /> guide, what are the monthly savings for a net income of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,3{,}800" />?

### Solutions

**Solution 1**: Subtract all spending and savings from income:

<BlockMath math="3{,}800 - 1{,}650 - 1{,}400 - 380 = 370" />

The cash flow is a **surplus** of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,370" />. Check: <InlineMath math="1{,}650 + 1{,}400 + 380 + 370 = 3{,}800" />.

**Solution 2**: For example, I want to save <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,2{,}400" /> in <InlineMath math="12" /> months by saving <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,200" /> every month. This goal is **specific, measurable and time-bound**.

**Solution 3**: Divide and round up:

<BlockMath math="\frac{3{,}200}{350} = 9.14 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 10 \text{ months}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="9 \times 350 = 3{,}150" /> isn't enough yet, but <InlineMath math="10 \times 350 = 3{,}500" /> is more than the price.

**Solution 4**: Take <InlineMath math="20\%" /> of net income:

<BlockMath math="0.20 \times 3{,}800 = 760" />

The suggested savings are <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,760" /> a month.

## How Operations Affect the Modulus

The modulus measures **distance from the origin**. Negation and conjugation preserve that distance. Products, quotients, and sums follow different rules, which we can derive from components and vector lengths.

### Modulus Under Negation and Conjugation

Negation and conjugation leave the modulus **unchanged**.

<BlockMath math="|z_1| = |-z_1| = |\bar{z_1}|" />

**Explanation**:

Recall that if <InlineMath math="z_1 = x + iy" />, then <InlineMath math="-z_1 = -x - iy" /> and <InlineMath math="\bar{z_1} = x - iy" />.

- <InlineMath math="|z_1| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}" />
- <InlineMath math="|-z_1| = \sqrt{(-x)^2 + (-y)^2} = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}" />
- <InlineMath math="|\bar{z_1}| = \sqrt{x^2 + (-y)^2} = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}" />

All three yield the same value.

### Modulus of Difference

The modulus of a difference measures the **distance between two points**, so reversing their order does not change that distance.

<BlockMath math="|z_1 - z_2| = |z_2 - z_1|" />

**Explanation**:

This is a direct consequence of the <InlineMath math="|-z| = |z|" /> property. We know <InlineMath math="z_1 - z_2 = -(z_2 - z_1)" />. Then:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z_1 - z_2| &amp;= |-(z_2 - z_1)| \\ &amp;= |z_2 - z_1| \end{aligned}" />

### Square of Modulus

The square of the modulus of a complex number is equal to the complex number multiplied by its **conjugate**.

<BlockMath math="|z_1|^2 = z_1 \times \bar{z_1}" />

**Explanation**:

If <InlineMath math="z_1 = x + iy" />, then <InlineMath math="\bar{z_1} = x - iy" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z_1 \times \bar{z_1} &amp;= (x + iy)(x - iy) \\ &amp;= x^2 - (iy)^2 \\ &amp;= x^2 - i^2y^2 \\ &amp;= x^2 - (-1)y^2 \\ &amp;= x^2 + y^2\end{aligned}" />

We also know that <InlineMath math="|z_1| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}" />, so <InlineMath math="|z_1|^2 = (\sqrt{x^2 + y^2})^2 = x^2 + y^2" />.

Both sides are equal.

### Modulus of Product

The modulus of the product of two complex numbers is equal to <Highlight>the product of their individual moduli</Highlight>.

<BlockMath math="|z_1 \times z_2| = |z_1| \times |z_2|" />

This follows from multiplying each number by its conjugate:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}|z_1z_2|^2&amp;=(z_1z_2)\overline{(z_1z_2)}\\&amp;=z_1\bar z_1z_2\bar z_2\\&amp;=|z_1|^2|z_2|^2\end{aligned}" />

Both moduli are non-negative, so taking the square root gives the product rule.

Take <InlineMath math="z_1 = 6 + 8i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = 3 - i" /> from the exercise. Their product is <InlineMath math="26 + 18i" />, and its length <InlineMath math="\sqrt{1000} = 10\sqrt{10}" /> equals <InlineMath math="|z_1| \cdot |z_2| = 10 \cdot \sqrt{10}" />.

### Modulus of Quotient

The modulus of the quotient of two complex numbers is equal to the **quotient of their individual moduli** (provided the denominator is non-zero).

<BlockMath math="\left| \frac{z_1}{z_2} \right| = \frac{|z_1|}{|z_2|}, \quad \text{for } z_2 \neq 0" />

The quotient rule follows from the product rule because <InlineMath math="z_1=(z_1/z_2)z_2" /> when <InlineMath math="z_2\neq0" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}|z_1|&amp;=\left|\frac{z_1}{z_2}\right||z_2|\\\left|\frac{z_1}{z_2}\right|&amp;=\frac{|z_1|}{|z_2|}\end{aligned}" />

### Triangle Inequality

The modulus of the sum of two complex numbers is **less than or equal to** the sum of their individual moduli.

<BlockMath math="|z_1 + z_2| \leq |z_1| + |z_2|" />

**Explanation**:

Geometrically, place the vectors <InlineMath math="z_1" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2" /> head to tail. The direct vector from the starting point to the endpoint is <InlineMath math="z_1 + z_2" />, and its length cannot exceed the sum <InlineMath math="|z_1| + |z_2|" /> of the other two side lengths.

The direct distance is shorter than the path through the right-angle corner:

<BlockMath math="|3+4i|=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\leq3+4=7" />

### Reverse Triangle Inequality

The **absolute difference between the moduli** of two complex numbers cannot exceed the distance between the numbers themselves.

<BlockMath math="\bigl||z_1|-|z_2|\bigr| \leq |z_1-z_2|" />

This inequality provides a lower bound for a distance or sum. For example, replacing <InlineMath math="z_2" /> with <InlineMath math="-z_2" /> gives <InlineMath math="\bigl||z_1|-|z_2|\bigr| \leq |z_1+z_2|" />.

To derive the lower bound, write the first number as the difference plus the second number, then use the triangle inequality:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}|z_1|&amp;=|(z_1-z_2)+z_2|\\&amp;\leq|z_1-z_2|+|z_2|\\|z_1|-|z_2|&amp;\leq|z_1-z_2|\end{aligned}" />

Swap the two numbers to bound the opposite difference. Together the two bounds give the absolute difference between the moduli.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}|z_2|-|z_1|&amp;\leq|z_2-z_1|=|z_1-z_2|\\\bigl||z_1|-|z_2|\bigr|&amp;\leq|z_1-z_2|\end{aligned}" />

## Using Modulus Properties

Suppose we are given the complex number <InlineMath math="z = \frac{1 - 2i}{3 + 4i}" />. Find <InlineMath math="|z|" />.

**Solution**:

We can view <InlineMath math="z = \frac{z_1}{z_2}" /> with <InlineMath math="z_1 = 1 - 2i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = 3 + 4i" />.

Using the **Modulus of Quotient** property:

<BlockMath math="|z| = \left| \frac{1 - 2i}{3 + 4i} \right| = \frac{|1 - 2i|}{|3 + 4i|}" />

Now we calculate the moduli of <InlineMath math="z_1" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z_1| &amp;= |1 - 2i| \\ &amp;= \sqrt{1^2 + (-2)^2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{1 + 4} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{5} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z_2| &amp;= |3 + 4i| \\ &amp;= \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{9 + 16} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{25} \\ &amp;= 5 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The calculation gives:

<BlockMath math="|z| = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}" />

The quotient rule avoids expanding the fraction with the conjugate of the denominator before calculating its modulus.

## Exercise

Choose the **modulus rule for the operation**. The first item needs a product of moduli, the second compares two calculations, and the third gives only lengths, so its answer is an interval.

1.  If <InlineMath math="z_1 = 6 + 8i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = 3 - i" />, calculate <InlineMath math="|z_1 \times z_2|" /> using the modulus properties.
2.  If <InlineMath math="z = 5 - 12i" />, prove that <InlineMath math="|z|^2 = z \times \bar{z}" />.
3.  If <InlineMath math="|z_1|=3" /> and <InlineMath math="|z_2|=5" />, determine the possible bounds for <InlineMath math="|z_1+z_2|" />.

### Worked Solutions

Calculate the moduli before multiplying, use conjugation for the squared modulus, then apply **upper and lower bounds** to the sum.

1.  We use the property <InlineMath math="|z_1 \times z_2| = |z_1| \times |z_2|" />.

    Calculate each modulus:

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z_1| &amp;= |6 + 8i| \\ &amp;= \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{36 + 64} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{100} \\ &amp;= 10 \end{aligned}" />
      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z_2| &amp;= |3 - i| \\ &amp;= \sqrt{3^2 + (-1)^2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{9 + 1} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{10} \end{aligned}" />
    </MathContainer>

    Then:

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z_1 \times z_2| &amp;= |z_1| \times |z_2| \\ &amp;= 10 \times \sqrt{10} \\ &amp;= 10\sqrt{10} \end{aligned}" />

2.  Given <InlineMath math="z = 5 - 12i" />.

    Calculate the left side (<InlineMath math="|z|^2" />):

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z| &amp;= |5 - 12i| \\ &amp;= \sqrt{5^2 + (-12)^2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{25 + 144} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{169} \\ &amp;= 13 \end{aligned}" />
      <BlockMath math="|z|^2 = 13^2 = 169" />
    </MathContainer>

    Calculate the right side (<InlineMath math="z \times \bar{z}" />):

    The conjugate of <InlineMath math="z" /> is <InlineMath math="\bar{z} = 5 + 12i" />.

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z \times \bar{z} &amp;= (5 - 12i)(5 + 12i) \\ &amp;= 5^2 - (12i)^2 \\ &amp;= 25 - 144i^2 \\ &amp;= 25 - 144(-1) \\ &amp;= 25 + 144 \\ &amp;= 169\end{aligned}" />

    Since the left side (<InlineMath math="169" />) equals the right side (<InlineMath math="169" />), the statement <InlineMath math="|z|^2 = z \times \bar{z}" /> is proven.

3.  The ordinary and reverse triangle inequalities give an upper and a lower bound:

    <BlockMath math="\bigl||z_1|-|z_2|\bigr|\leq|z_1+z_2|\leq|z_1|+|z_2|" />

    Substituting the known moduli gives <InlineMath math="2\leq|z_1+z_2|\leq8" />. The exact value depends on the angle between the two complex numbers.

Both endpoints are attainable: opposite directions give the lower bound, while the same direction gives the upper bound.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}|3+(-5)|&amp;=2\\|3+5|&amp;=8\end{aligned}" />

Turn <InlineMath math="z_2" /> with <InlineMath math="|z_2| = 5" /> around while <InlineMath math="z_1 = 3" /> stays fixed. The length <InlineMath math="|z_1 + z_2| = \sqrt{34 + 30\cos\theta}" /> falls from 8 at <InlineMath math="\theta = 0^\circ" /> to 2 at <InlineMath math="\theta = 180^\circ" />, so it always stays between <InlineMath math="5 - 3 = 2" /> and <InlineMath math="3 + 5 = 8" />.

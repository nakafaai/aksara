## Multiplying a Complex Number by Its Reciprocal

Every **nonzero** complex number <InlineMath math="z = x + iy" /> has a **multiplicative inverse**, written as <InlineMath math="z^{-1}" /> or <InlineMath math="1/z" />.

Multiplying the complex number <InlineMath math="z" /> by its inverse <InlineMath math="z^{-1}" /> gives <InlineMath math="1" />, <Highlight>the multiplicative identity</Highlight>.

<BlockMath math="z \times z^{-1} = 1" />

## Finding the Inverse Formula

We already know from the material on [properties of complex number multiplication](/en/subjects/mathematics/complex-number/properties-multiplication-complex-numbers#multiplicative-inverse) that for <InlineMath math="z = x + iy" />, its inverse is:

Multiply numerator and denominator by the **conjugate of the denominator**. The product of conjugates is the squared modulus, so the denominator becomes positive and real for every nonzero complex number:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac1z=\frac1{x+iy}\cdot\frac{x-iy}{x-iy}=\frac{x-iy}{x^2+y^2},\quad z\ne0" />

<BlockMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{x}{x^2+y^2} - i\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}" />
</MathContainer>

This formula can also be written as an ordered pair:

<BlockMath math="z^{-1} = \left( \frac{x}{x^2+y^2}, -\frac{y}{x^2+y^2} \right)" />

Remember also the other often useful form, using the conjugate (<InlineMath math="\bar{z} = x-iy" />) and the modulus squared (<InlineMath math="|z|^2 = x^2+y^2" />):

<BlockMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|^2}" />

The formula <InlineMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|^2}" /> builds the inverse in two moves. For <InlineMath math="z = 1 - i" />, the conjugate <InlineMath math="\bar{z} = 1 + i" /> flips it across the real axis, and dividing by <InlineMath math="|z|^2 = 2" /> halves its length, which lands at <InlineMath math="\tfrac12 + \tfrac12 i" />.

## Finding the Inverse of a Complex Number

Find the inverse of <InlineMath math="z = 1 - i" />.

**Solution**:

Here, <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="y=-1" />.

Using the first formula:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x^2+y^2 &amp;= (1)^2 + (-1)^2 \\ &amp;= 1 + 1 \\ &amp;= 2 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{x}{x^2+y^2} - i\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}" />
  <BlockMath math="z^{-1}=\frac12-i\frac{-1}{2}=\frac12+\frac12i" />
</MathContainer>

Using the **conjugate and modulus formula**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\bar{z} = 1 - (-1)i = 1+i" />
  <BlockMath math="|z|^2 = x^2+y^2 = 1^2 + (-1)^2 = 2" />
  <BlockMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|^2} = \frac{1+i}{2} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}i" />
</MathContainer>

Both formulas give the same reciprocal:

<ContentStack>

  <BlockMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}i \text{ or} \left( \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right)" />

  Verify <InlineMath math="z^{-1}" /> by multiplying it by the original number:

  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(1-i)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}i\right) \\ &amp;=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)i \\ &amp;=1 \end{aligned}" />

### Dividing with the Reciprocal

To **divide two complex numbers**, multiply the numerator by the reciprocal of the denominator. The denominator must be nonzero. In the following example, use distribution to calculate both the real and imaginary parts:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}\frac{1-i}{2+3i}&amp;=(1-i)\frac{2-3i}{2^2+3^2} \\ &amp;=\frac{2-3i-2i+3i^2}{13} \\ &amp;=\frac{-1-5i}{13}=-\frac1{13}-\frac5{13}i\end{aligned}" />

## Exercise

Given the complex numbers <InlineMath math="z_1 = 1-i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = 2+3i" />. Find the **inverse of the sum** <InlineMath math="z_1 + z_2" />. The result must satisfy <InlineMath math="(z_1 + z_2) \cdot w = 1" />, which is the check you can run on your answer.

### Worked Solutions

**Step 1**: Find <InlineMath math="z_1 + z_2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} z &amp;= z_1 + z_2 \\ &amp;= (1-i) + (2+3i) \\ &amp;= (1+2) + (-1+3)i \\ &amp;= 3+2i \end{aligned}" />

**Step 2**: Find the **inverse of the sum** <InlineMath math="z = 3+2i" />.
Here <InlineMath math="x=3" /> and <InlineMath math="y=2" />.
We use the formula <InlineMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|^2}" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\bar{z} = 3-2i" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z|^2 &amp;= x^2+y^2 \\ &amp;= 3^2 + 2^2 \\ &amp;= 9 + 4 \\ &amp;= 13 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{3-2i}{13} = \frac{3}{13} - \frac{2}{13}i" />
</MathContainer>

So, the inverse of <InlineMath math="z_1 + z_2" /> is <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{13} - \frac{2}{13}i" />.

Check by multiplying the original sum by the reciprocal you found:

<BlockMath math="(3+2i)\frac{3-2i}{13}=\frac{9-6i+6i-4i^2}{13}=\frac{13}{13}=1" />

In the exercise, <InlineMath math="z_1 + z_2 = 3 + 2i" /> has modulus <InlineMath math="\sqrt{13}" />, so its inverse <InlineMath math="\tfrac{3}{13} - \tfrac{2}{13}i" /> has modulus <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{13}}" />. It lies inside the unit circle and below the real axis, at the mirrored angle.

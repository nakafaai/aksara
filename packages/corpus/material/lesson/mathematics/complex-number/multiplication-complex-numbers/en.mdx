## Distributing Products Across Real and Imaginary Terms

Multiplying two complex numbers is similar to multiplying two binomial algebraic expressions. We can use <Highlight>the distributive property of multiplication</Highlight> over addition.

Start with <InlineMath math="z_1 = x_1 + iy_1" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = x_2 + iy_2" />, then distribute each term of the first factor across the second.

Replace the **square of the imaginary unit** by minus one, then group real and imaginary terms. The product of two imaginary terms contributes to the real part, not the imaginary part:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z_1z_2&amp;=(x_1+iy_1)(x_2+iy_2) \\ &amp;=x_1x_2+ix_1y_2+ix_2y_1+i^2y_1y_2 \\ &amp;=x_1x_2-y_1y_2+i(x_1y_2+x_2y_1)\end{aligned}" />

Multiplying by <InlineMath math="i" /> is a quarter turn. For <InlineMath math="z = 2 + i" /> the distributive law gives <InlineMath math="i(2 + i) = 2i + i^2 = -1 + 2i" />, the same arrow turned <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> counterclockwise and just as long.

## Two Ways to Calculate the Same Product

Let <InlineMath math="z_1 = 2+i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = 1-2i" />. Find <InlineMath math="z_1 \times z_2" />.

**Solution**:

Using the **distributive property**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z_1z_2&amp;=(2+i)(1-2i) \\ &amp;=2-4i+i-2i^2 \\ &amp;=2+2+(-4+1)i=4-3i\end{aligned}" />

The product lies at <InlineMath math="(4,-3)" /> in the complex plane. **Do not multiply coordinates separately**. The calculation <InlineMath math="(2\cdot1,1\cdot(-2))=(2,-2)" /> is not complex multiplication because it omits the cross terms and the sign change from the square of the imaginary unit.

<BlockMath math="z_1z_2=(2\cdot1-1\cdot(-2))+i(2\cdot(-2)+1\cdot1)=4-3i" />

## Exercise

This problem multiplies two complex numbers, one written in rectangular form and one with a fractional real part. Expand the product with the **distributive law**, replace <InlineMath math="i^2" /> with <InlineMath math="-1" />, and collect the real and imaginary parts.

Let <InlineMath math="z_1 = 1+i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = \frac{1}{2} - 2i" />. Find <InlineMath math="z_1 \times z_2" />.

### Worked Solution

Using the **distributive property**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}(1+i)\left(\frac12-2i\right)&amp;=\frac12-2i+\frac12i-2i^2 \\ &amp;=\left(\frac12+2\right)+\left(-2+\frac12\right)i \\ &amp;=\left(\frac12+\frac42\right)+\left(-\frac42+\frac12\right)i \\ &amp;=\frac52-\frac32i\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="z_1z_2=\left(1\cdot\frac12-1\cdot(-2)\right)+i\left(1\cdot(-2)+\frac12\cdot1\right)=\frac52-\frac32i" />
</MathContainer>

The worked product on the plane: <InlineMath math="z_1 = 1 + i" />, <InlineMath math="z_2 = \tfrac12 - 2i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_1z_2 = \tfrac52 - \tfrac32 i" />. The modulus of the product, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{\tfrac{17}{2}}" />, equals <InlineMath math="\sqrt2 \cdot \tfrac{\sqrt{17}}{2}" />, the product of the two moduli.

## Algebraic Laws for Complex Products

Complex multiplication follows the familiar commutative, associative, identity, and distributive laws. Let <InlineMath math="z_1, z_2," /> and <InlineMath math="z_3" /> be any complex numbers.

### Commutative Property

The commutative property means that the order in the multiplication of two complex numbers does not affect the result.

<BlockMath math="z_1 \times z_2 = z_2 \times z_1" />

**Example:**

Let <InlineMath math="z_1 = 1+2i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = 3-i" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z_1 \times z_2 &= (1+2i)(3-i) \\ &= 1(3-i) + 2i(3-i) \\ &= 3-i+6i-2i^2 \\ &= 3+5i-2(-1) \\ &= 5+5i\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z_2 \times z_1 &= (3-i)(1+2i) \\ &= 3(1+2i) - i(1+2i) \\ &= 3+6i-i-2i^2 \\ &= 3+5i-2(-1) \\ &= 5+5i\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Both products equal <InlineMath math="5+5i" />, which verifies commutativity in this example.

### Associative Property

The associative property states that when multiplying three or more complex numbers, the grouping of the multiplication does not change the result.

<BlockMath math="(z_1 \times z_2) \times z_3 = z_1 \times (z_2 \times z_3)" />

**Example:**

Let <InlineMath math="z_1 = i" />, <InlineMath math="z_2 = 2" />, and <InlineMath math="z_3 = 3-i" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}(z_1 \times z_2) \times z_3 &= (i \times 2) \times (3-i) \\ &= 2i(3-i) \\ &= 6i - 2i^2 \\ &= 6i - 2(-1) \\ &= 2+6i\end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z_1 \times (z_2 \times z_3) &= i \times (2 \times (3-i)) \\ &= i \times (6-2i) \\ &= 6i - 2i^2 \\ &= 6i - 2(-1) \\ &= 2+6i\end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Both groupings equal <InlineMath math="2+6i" />, which verifies associativity in this example.

### Multiplicative Identity

The complex number <InlineMath math="1 = 1+0i" /> is the identity element for multiplication. This means that any complex number multiplied by <InlineMath math="1" /> results in the complex number itself.

<BlockMath math="z \times 1 = z = 1 \times z" />

**Example:**

Let <InlineMath math="z = 4-7i" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;z \times 1 = (4-7i)(1+0i) \\ &amp;= 4(1) - (-7)(0) \\ &amp;+ i(4(0) + (-7)(1)) \\ &amp;= 4 - 7i \\ &amp;= z \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;1 \times z = (1+0i)(4-7i) \\ &amp;= 1(4) - (0)(-7) \\ &amp;+ i(1(-7) + 0(4)) \\ &amp;= 4 - 7i \\ &amp;= z \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

### Distributive Property of Multiplication over Addition

This property connects the operations of multiplication and addition of complex numbers.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;z_1 \times (z_2 + z_3) = (z_1 \times z_2) \\ &amp;+ (z_1 \times z_3) \end{aligned}" />

**Example:**

Let <InlineMath math="z_1=2" />, <InlineMath math="z_2 = 1+i" />, <InlineMath math="z_3 = 3-2i" />.

**Left side:** <InlineMath math="z_1 \times (z_2 + z_3)" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;z_1 \times (z_2 + z_3) \\ &amp;= 2 \times ((1+i) + (3-2i)) \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= 2 \times (1+3 + i-2i)" />
  <BlockMath math="= 2 \times (4-i)" />
  <BlockMath math="= 8-2i" />
</MathContainer>

**Right side:** <InlineMath math="(z_1 \times z_2) + (z_1 \times z_3)" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(z_1 \times z_2) + (z_1 \times z_3) = (2(1+i)) \\ &amp;+ (2(3-2i)) \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="= (2+2i) + (6-4i)" />
  <BlockMath math="= (2+6) + (2i-4i)" />
  <BlockMath math="= 8-2i" />
</MathContainer>

Both sides equal <InlineMath math="8-2i" />, which verifies the distributive law in this example.

## Example Proof Using Properties

The same laws prove algebraic identities. For any <InlineMath math="z_1, z_2" />, expand <InlineMath math="(z_1 + z_2)^2" /> as follows:

Write the square as a product of two equal factors:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(z_1+z_2)^2 \\ &amp;=(z_1+z_2)(z_1+z_2) \end{aligned}" />
  Apply the distributive property:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;=z_1(z_1+z_2) \\ &amp;+z_2(z_1+z_2) \end{aligned}" />
  Apply the distributive property:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;=(z_1z_1+z_1z_2) \\ &amp;+(z_2z_1+z_2z_2) \end{aligned}" />
  Write repeated equal factors as squares:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;=z_1^2+z_1z_2+z_2z_1+z_2^2 \end{aligned}" />
  Use commutativity, <InlineMath math="z_2z_1=z_1z_2" />:

<BlockMath math="=z_1^2+z_1z_2+z_1z_2+z_2^2" />
  Combine like terms:

<BlockMath math="= z_1^2 + 2z_1z_2 + z_2^2" />

## Multiplicative Inverse

Every non-zero complex number <InlineMath math="z = x + iy \neq 0" /> has a multiplicative inverse, denoted as <InlineMath math="z^{-1}" /> or <InlineMath math="1/z" />, such that <InlineMath math="z \times z^{-1} = 1" />.

Let <InlineMath math="z^{-1} = u + iv" />. Then:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="z \times z^{-1} = 1" />
  <BlockMath math="(x+iy)(u+iv) = 1 + 0i" />
  <BlockMath math="(xu - yv) + i(xv + uy) = 1 + 0i" />
</MathContainer>

Based on the equality of two complex numbers, we obtain the system of equations:

1.  <InlineMath math="xu - yv = 1" />
2.  <InlineMath math="xv + uy = 0" />

To isolate <InlineMath math="u" />, multiply the first equation by <InlineMath math="x" /> and the second by <InlineMath math="y" />, then add them:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2u-xyv=x" />
  <BlockMath math="xyv+y^2u=0" />
  <BlockMath math="(x^2+y^2)u=x" />
</MathContainer>

To isolate <InlineMath math="v" />, multiply the first equation by <InlineMath math="-y" /> and the second by <InlineMath math="x" />, then add them. Because <InlineMath math="z\neq0" />, we have <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2>0" /> and may divide by this value:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="-xyu+y^2v=-y" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2v+xyu=0" />
  <BlockMath math="(x^2+y^2)v=-y" />
</MathContainer>

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="u = \frac{x}{x^2+y^2}" />
  <BlockMath math="v = -\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}" />
</MathContainer>

So, the multiplicative inverse of <InlineMath math="z = x+iy" /> is:

<BlockMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{x}{x^2+y^2} - i\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}" />

Because <InlineMath math="x^2+y^2 = |z|^2" /> and <InlineMath math="x-iy = \bar{z}" />, the inverse formula can also be written as:

<BlockMath math="z^{-1} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|^2}" />

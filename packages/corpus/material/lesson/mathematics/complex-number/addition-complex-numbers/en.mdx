## Adding the Real and Imaginary Parts Separately

Start with two complex numbers:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="z_1 = x_1 + iy_1" />
  <BlockMath math="z_2 = x_2 + iy_2" />
</MathContainer>

To form <InlineMath math="z_1 + z_2" />, <Highlight>add the real parts to each other</Highlight> and do the same with the imaginary parts.

<BlockMath math="z_1 + z_2 = (x_1 + x_2) + i(y_1 + y_2)" />

### Adding Two Specific Complex Numbers

Let <InlineMath math="z_1 = 2 + 3i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = 1 - i" />.

- The **real part** of <InlineMath math="z_1" /> is <InlineMath math="2" />, the real part of <InlineMath math="z_2" /> is <InlineMath math="1" />.
- The **imaginary part** of <InlineMath math="z_1" /> is <InlineMath math="3" />, the imaginary part of <InlineMath math="z_2" /> is <InlineMath math="-1" />.

Then their sum is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} z_1 + z_2 &amp;= (2 + 1) + i(3 + (-1)) \\ &amp;= 3 + i(2) \\ &amp;= 3 + 2i \end{aligned}" />

### Adding on the Complex Plane

Using the **parallelogram rule**, the addition of complex numbers can be viewed geometrically on the complex plane. If we represent <InlineMath math="z_1" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2" /> as vectors (arrows) from the origin <InlineMath math="(0, 0)" />, then their sum, <InlineMath math="z_1 + z_2" />, is the diagonal vector of the parallelogram formed by <InlineMath math="z_1" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2" />.

## Scaling Negating and Subtracting

The same separation of **real and imaginary parts** also applies to scalar multiplication, negation, and subtraction. Multiplying by a real number scales both parts by that number, negation flips both signs, and subtraction combines negation with addition. Since each rule acts on the two parts independently, you never have to mix them.

### Scalar Multiplication

When a complex number <InlineMath math="z = x + iy" /> is multiplied by a real scalar <InlineMath math="c" />, the scalar multiplies **both parts by the same factor**.

<BlockMath math="cz = c(x + iy) = cx + i(cy)" />

Geometrically, this scales the vector <InlineMath math="z" /> in length by a factor of <InlineMath math="|c|" />. If <InlineMath math="c" /> is negative, the vector's direction is reversed.

For a zero scalar, both components become zero. The **zero vector** has no direction.

### Negative of a Complex Number

The negative of <InlineMath math="z = x + iy" /> is <InlineMath math="-z" />. This is the same as scalar multiplication by <InlineMath math="c = -1" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} -z &amp;= -(x + iy) \\ &amp;= -x + i(-y) \\ &amp;= -x - iy \end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="z\ne0" />, geometrically <InlineMath math="-z" /> is a vector with the same length as <InlineMath math="z" /> but pointing in the **opposite direction** (<InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> rotation).

### Subtraction of Two Complex Numbers

Subtracting <InlineMath math="z_2" /> from <InlineMath math="z_1" /> (<InlineMath math="z_1 - z_2" />) is the same as adding <InlineMath math="z_1" /> to the **negative** of <InlineMath math="z_2" /> (<InlineMath math="z_1 + (-z_2)" />).

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;z_1 - z_2 = z_1 + (-z_2) \\ &amp;= (x_1 + (-x_2)) + i(y_1 + (-y_2)) \\ &amp;= (x_1 - x_2) + i(y_1 - y_2) \end{aligned}" />

So, subtract the real parts and subtract the imaginary parts.

Geometrically, <InlineMath math="z_1 - z_2" /> is the vector from the tip of <InlineMath math="z_2" /> to the tip of <InlineMath math="z_1" />.

Subtraction adds the negative. For <InlineMath math="z_1 = 2 + 3i" /> and <InlineMath math="z_2 = 1 - i" />, attach <InlineMath math="-z_2 = -1 + i" /> at the tip of <InlineMath math="z_1" />. The arrow from the origin to the end is <InlineMath math="z_1 - z_2 = 1 + 4i" />.

### Combining Addition Subtraction and Scaling

Use the following two numbers:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="z_1 = 2 + \frac{1}{2}i" />
  <BlockMath math="z_2 = -3 + \sqrt{2}i" />
</MathContainer>

Calculate three expressions by handling the **real and imaginary parts separately**:

1.  <InlineMath math="2z_1" /> (**Scalar Multiplication**):

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 2z_1 &amp;= 2(2 + \frac{1}{2}i) \\ &amp;= 2(2) + i(2 \times \frac{1}{2}) \\ &amp;= 4 + i \end{aligned}" />

2.  <InlineMath math="z_1 + 3z_2" /> (**Addition and Scalar Multiplication**):

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z_1+3z_2&amp;=(2+\frac12i)+3(-3+\sqrt2i)\\&amp;=(2-9)+i(\frac12+3\sqrt2)\\&amp;=-7+i(\frac12+3\sqrt2)\end{aligned}" />

3.  <InlineMath math="2z_1 - z_2" /> (**Subtraction and Scalar Multiplication**):

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}2z_1-z_2&amp;=(4+i)-(-3+\sqrt2i)\\&amp;=(4-(-3))+i(1-\sqrt2)\\&amp;=7+i(1-\sqrt2)\end{aligned}" />

## Exercise

1.  <InlineMath math="z_1 + z_2" />

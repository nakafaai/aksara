For a nonzero vector, the following cases apply:

- Multiplying <InlineMath math="z" /> by a scalar <InlineMath math="c > 1" /> (like <InlineMath math="2" />) will **stretch** the vector <InlineMath math="z" /> in the same direction.
- Multiplying <InlineMath math="z" /> by a scalar <InlineMath math="0 < c < 1" /> will **shrink** the vector <InlineMath math="z" /> in the same direction.
- Multiplying <InlineMath math="z" /> by <InlineMath math="c = 0" /> sends the vector to the origin, so <InlineMath math="0z = 0" />.
- For <InlineMath math="z\neq0" />, multiplying by a scalar <InlineMath math="c < 0" /> (like <InlineMath math="-0.5" />) **reverses the direction** of the vector by <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> and multiplies its length by <InlineMath math="|c|" />.

A scalar of one leaves the vector unchanged. A zero scalar or a zero complex number produces the **zero vector with no direction**.

## Multiplying a Complex Number by a Scalar

If <InlineMath math="z = x + iy" /> is a complex number and <InlineMath math="c" /> is a scalar (a real number), then their scalar multiplication is:

<BlockMath math="cz = c(x + iy) = (cx) + i(cy)" />

The formula applies the **same factor to both components** by multiplying the scalar <InlineMath math="c" /> by the real part <InlineMath math="x" /> and the imaginary part <InlineMath math="y" /> separately.

The length is multiplied by the **absolute value of the scalar**, rather than its signed value. This follows from the modulus formula:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}|cz|&amp;=\sqrt{(cx)^2+(cy)^2}\\&amp;=\sqrt{c^2(x^2+y^2)}\\&amp;=|c|\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=|c||z|\end{aligned}" />

### Scaling Complex Numbers by Real Scalars

Track the **sign of each component**. In the second example, a negative scalar multiplies a negative imaginary component, so the imaginary component of the product is positive.

If <InlineMath math="z = -3 + 4i" /> and <InlineMath math="c = 3" />, then:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} cz &amp;= 3(-3 + 4i) \\ &amp;= 3(-3) + i(3 \times 4) \\ &amp;= -9 + 12i \end{aligned}" />

If <InlineMath math="z = 5 - i" /> and <InlineMath math="c = -2" />, then:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} cz &amp;= -2(5 - i) \\ &amp;= -2(5) + i(-2 \times -1) \\ &amp;= -10 + 2i \end{aligned}" />

## Stretching Shrinking and Reversing on the Complex Plane

A real scalar changes the length of the arrow and can also turn it around. The cases here separate <Highlight>the effects of stretching, shrinking, and reversing direction</Highlight>, so the size and the sign of the scalar each stay clear.

### Scalar Greater than One

A positive scalar preserves direction, while a value greater than one **stretches the vector**. For the example in the diagram, multiply both components by the same factor:

<BlockMath math="\frac32(-2+i)=\frac32(-2)+\frac32i=-3+\frac32i" />

### Scalar between Zero and One

A scalar between zero and one **shrinks the vector** without reversing its direction. Multiplication by three quarters leaves each component at three quarters of its starting value:

<BlockMath math="\frac34(3-2i)=\frac94-\frac64i=\frac94-\frac32i" />

## Rearranging Complex Expressions without Changing Their Value

Addition and scalar multiplication of complex numbers obey <Highlight>the same familiar algebraic rules</Highlight> as real numbers. Under those rules, expressions can be rearranged and simplified without changing their value.

Let <InlineMath math="z_1" />, <InlineMath math="z_2" />, and <InlineMath math="z_3" /> be any complex numbers, and let <InlineMath math="c" /> and <InlineMath math="d" /> be any scalars (real numbers).

## Addition Properties

Complex addition works **separately on the real and imaginary parts**. Real-number addition allows reordering and regrouping, so both rules apply to each complex component. For example, changing the order can be checked directly:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}(x_1+iy_1)+(x_2+iy_2)&amp;=(x_1+x_2)+i(y_1+y_2) \\ &amp;=(x_2+x_1)+i(y_2+y_1) \\ &amp;=(x_2+iy_2)+(x_1+iy_1)\end{aligned}" />

| Property | Rule | Worked example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commutative | <InlineMath math="z_1+z_2=z_2+z_1" /> | <InlineMath math="(2+i)+(1-3i)=3-2i=(1-3i)+(2+i)" /> |
| Associative | <InlineMath math="(z_1+z_2)+z_3=z_1+(z_2+z_3)" /> | <InlineMath math="((2+i)+(1-3i))+(-1+2i)=2=(2+i)+((1-3i)+(-1+2i))" /> |
| Identity | <InlineMath math="z+0=z" /> | <InlineMath math="(2+i)+(0+0i)=2+i" /> |
| Inverse | <InlineMath math="z+(-z)=0" /> | <InlineMath math="(5-2i)+(-5+2i)=(5-5)+(-2+2)i=0" /> |

## Scalar Multiplication

Scalar multiplication means **multiplying both components by the same factor**. For <InlineMath math="z=x+iy" />, the result is <InlineMath math="cz=cx+i(cy)" />. Every rule below applies to both components through real-number associativity and distributivity.

| Property | Rule | Worked example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Associative | <InlineMath math="c(dz)=(cd)z" /> | <InlineMath math="2(3(1+i))=2(3+3i)=6+6i=6(1+i)" /> |
| Distributive over scalars | <InlineMath math="(c+d)z=cz+dz" /> | <InlineMath math="(2+3)(1+i)=5+5i=(2+2i)+(3+3i)" /> |
| Distributive over complex numbers | <InlineMath math="c(z_1+z_2)=cz_1+cz_2" /> | <InlineMath math="2((1+i)+(2-i))=6=(2+2i)+(4-2i)" /> |
| Identity | <InlineMath math="1z=z" /> | <InlineMath math="1(1+i)=1+i" /> |
| Zero scalar | <InlineMath math="0z=0" /> | <InlineMath math="0(1+i)=0+0i=0" /> |

## Simplifying Expressions with Addition Properties

**Distributivity** carries a scalar into a sum term by term, and equal coefficients with opposite signs then cancel. Both moves keep the real part and the imaginary part unchanged, so any identity built from them holds for every complex number.

### Verifying the Additive Inverse

The **distributive property** combines two terms containing the same complex number. Adding their opposite coefficients leaves a zero scalar:

<BlockMath math="4z+(-4)z=(4+(-4))z=0z=0" />

## Exercise

Using the **addition properties**, prove that <InlineMath math="3z - \frac{1}{2}(2z) = 2z" /> for any complex number <InlineMath math="z" />. Work on the left side only, and name the property you apply at each step, so the argument covers every complex number.

### Worked Solution

Write subtraction as **addition of the opposite**, use associativity to regroup the scalars, and then use distributivity to combine their coefficients:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}3z-\frac12(2z)&amp;=3z+\left(-\frac12\right)(2z) \\ &amp;=3z+\left(\left(-\frac12\right)2\right)z \\ &amp;=3z+(-1)z \\ &amp;=(3-1)z=2z\end{aligned}" />

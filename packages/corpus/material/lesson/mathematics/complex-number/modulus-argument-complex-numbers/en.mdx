## The Length and Direction of a Complex Vector

A complex number <InlineMath math="z = x + iy" /> can be represented as a point <InlineMath math="(x, y)" /> on the complex plane (similar to the Cartesian plane). Besides being a point, we can also view it as a **vector** starting from the origin <InlineMath math="(0, 0)" /> to the point <InlineMath math="(x, y)" />.

This vector has a **length** and a **direction**. This length and direction are what we call the **Modulus** and **Argument**.

At the origin, the modulus is zero but the **argument is undefined**, because the zero vector has no direction.

## Modulus of a Complex Number

The **Modulus** of a complex number <InlineMath math="z = x + iy" />, written as <InlineMath math="|z|" />, is the **distance** from the origin <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> to the point <InlineMath math="(x, y)" /> on the complex plane. This is the same as the **length of the vector** representing <InlineMath math="z" />.

To calculate the modulus, <Highlight>we can use the Pythagorean Theorem</Highlight> on the right-angled triangle formed by the component lengths (<InlineMath math="|x|" /> and <InlineMath math="|y|" />), and the modulus (<InlineMath math="|z|" />) as the hypotenuse.

**Definition of Modulus:**

The modulus of the complex number <InlineMath math="z = x + iy" /> is:

<BlockMath math="|z| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}" />

The modulus is always **non-negative** (never negative) because it represents a distance.

### Calculating the Modulus

Square **both components with their signs**, add the squares, then take the nonnegative square root. A negative component does not make the distance negative.

1.  <InlineMath math="z_1 = 3 + 4i" />, with <InlineMath math="x=3, y=4" />

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z_1| &amp;= \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{9 + 16} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{25} \\ &amp;= 5 \end{aligned}" />

2.  <InlineMath math="z_2 = -1 - 2i" />, with <InlineMath math="x=-1, y=-2" />

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z_2| &amp;= \sqrt{(-1)^2 + (-2)^2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{1 + 4} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{5} \end{aligned}" />

3.  <InlineMath math="z_3 = 5" />, with <InlineMath math="x=5, y=0" />

    <BlockMath math="|z_3| = \sqrt{5^2 + 0^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5" />

    (The modulus of a real number is its absolute value).

4.  <InlineMath math="z_4 = -2i" />, with <InlineMath math="x=0, y=-2" />

    <BlockMath math="|z_4| = \sqrt{0^2 + (-2)^2} = \sqrt{4} = 2" />

## Argument of a Complex Number

The **Argument** of a non-zero complex number <InlineMath math="z = x + iy" />, written as <InlineMath math="\arg(z)" /> or <InlineMath math="\theta" />, is the **angle** formed by the vector <InlineMath math="z" /> with the **positive real axis** on the complex plane. This angle is usually measured in radians or degrees.

From basic trigonometry on the same right-angled triangle as in the modulus visualization, we know the relationships:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x = |z| \cos \theta" />
  <BlockMath math="y = |z| \sin \theta" />
  <BlockMath math="\tan \theta = \frac{y}{x} \quad (\text{if } x \neq 0)" />
</MathContainer>

When <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> are both nonzero, first calculate the positive reference angle:

<BlockMath math="\alpha = \arctan\left(\left|\frac{y}{x}\right|\right)" />

The ratio <InlineMath math="|y/x|" /> is positive and finite, so the reference angle is acute: <InlineMath math="0^\circ<\alpha<90^\circ" />.

The signs of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> locate the quadrant. They determine how the reference angle becomes the Principal Argument in <InlineMath math="(-180^\circ, 180^\circ]" />:

| Quadrant | Component signs | Principal argument |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | <InlineMath math="x>0,\ y>0" /> | <InlineMath math="\alpha" /> |
| II | <InlineMath math="x<0,\ y>0" /> | <InlineMath math="180^\circ-\alpha" /> |
| III | <InlineMath math="x<0,\ y<0" /> | <InlineMath math="-180^\circ+\alpha" /> |
| IV | <InlineMath math="x>0,\ y<0" /> | <InlineMath math="-\alpha" /> |

Points on an axis do not require a quotient <InlineMath math="y/x" />. Read their direction directly:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x>0,\ y=0 \Rightarrow \text{Arg}(z)=0^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="x=0,\ y>0 \Rightarrow \text{Arg}(z)=90^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="x<0,\ y=0 \Rightarrow \text{Arg}(z)=180^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="x=0,\ y<0 \Rightarrow \text{Arg}(z)=-90^\circ" />
</MathContainer>

### Calculating the Argument

For each point, first locate the **quadrant**, then compute the reference angle <InlineMath math="\alpha" /> from <InlineMath math="|y/x|" />, then apply the quadrant rule above.

1.  <InlineMath math="z_1 = 1 + i" />

    The point <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" /> is in Quadrant <InlineMath math="I" />.

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\tan \theta = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{1}{1} = 1" />
      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\theta = \arctan(1) = 45^\circ \text{ or } \frac{\pi}{4} \text{ radians} \end{aligned}" />
    </MathContainer>

2.  <InlineMath math="z_2 = -\sqrt{3} + i" />

    The point <InlineMath math="(-\sqrt{3}, 1)" /> is in Quadrant <InlineMath math="II" />.

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\tan \theta = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{1}{-\sqrt{3}}" />
      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Base angle} &amp;= \arctan\left(\left|\frac{1}{-\sqrt{3}}\right|\right) \\ &amp;= \arctan\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) \\ &amp;= 30^\circ \end{aligned}" />
      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \theta &amp;= 180^\circ - 30^\circ \\ &amp;= 150^\circ \text{ or } \frac{5\pi}{6} \text{ radians} \end{aligned}" />
    </MathContainer>

    Quadrant <InlineMath math="II" /> lies above the negative real axis, so subtract the reference angle from <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.

3.  <InlineMath math="z_3 = -1 - i\sqrt{3}" />

    The point <InlineMath math="(-1, -\sqrt{3})" /> is in Quadrant <InlineMath math="III" />.

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\tan \theta = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{-\sqrt{3}}{-1} = \sqrt{3}" />
      <BlockMath math="\text{Base angle} = \arctan(\sqrt{3}) = 60^\circ" />
      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Arg}(z_3) &amp;= -180^\circ + 60^\circ \\ &amp;= -120^\circ \\ &amp;= -\frac{2\pi}{3} \end{aligned}" />
    </MathContainer>

    Quadrant <InlineMath math="III" /> lies below the negative real axis, so its Principal Argument is negative.

4.  <InlineMath math="z_4 = 3 - 3i" />

    The point <InlineMath math="(3, -3)" /> is in Quadrant <InlineMath math="IV" />.

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="\tan \theta = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{-3}{3} = -1" />
      <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Base angle} &amp;= \arctan(|-1|) \\ &amp;= \arctan(1) \\ &amp;= 45^\circ \end{aligned}" />
      <BlockMath math="\text{Arg}(z_4) = -45^\circ = -\frac{\pi}{4}" />
    </MathContainer>

    Quadrant <InlineMath math="IV" /> lies below the positive real axis, so its Principal Argument is negative.

## Converting between Cartesian and Polar Forms

The modulus and argument give the **polar form** of a complex number. To return to Cartesian form, multiply the modulus by the cosine and sine of the angle. Use the same number to check both directions of conversion.

From Cartesian form:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z&amp;=2+2i\\r&amp;=\sqrt{2^2+2^2}=2\sqrt2,\quad\theta=45^\circ\\z&amp;=2\sqrt2(\cos45^\circ+i\sin45^\circ)\end{aligned}" />

From polar form:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}z&amp;=2\sqrt2\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+i\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)\\&amp;=2+2i\end{aligned}" />

## Exercise

Find the modulus and Principal Argument (in degrees) of the following complex numbers. For each number, compute the **distance from the origin** first, then choose the angle inside <InlineMath math="(-180^\circ, 180^\circ]" />.

1.  <InlineMath math="z_a = 2 + 2i" />
2.  <InlineMath math="z_b = -4" />
3.  <InlineMath math="z_c = -i" />

### Worked Solutions

Use **distance for the modulus** and the position of the point for its argument. Points on negative axes need different angles from a point in quadrant I.

1.  <InlineMath math="z_a = 2 + 2i" />:

    <InlineMath math="x=2, y=2" /> (Quadrant <InlineMath math="I" />) Modulus:

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} |z_a| &amp;= \sqrt{2^2 + 2^2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{4+4} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{8} \\ &amp;= 2\sqrt{2} \end{aligned}" />

    Argument:

    <BlockMath math="\tan\theta=\frac22=1" />

    The point is in Quadrant <InlineMath math="I" />, so its Principal Argument is the acute angle:

    <BlockMath math="\theta=\arctan(1)=45^\circ" />

2.  <InlineMath math="z_b = -4" />:

    <InlineMath math="z_b = -4 + 0i" />. <InlineMath math="x=-4, y=0" /> (Negative
    real axis) Modulus:

    <BlockMath math="|z_b| = \sqrt{(-4)^2 + 0^2} = \sqrt{16} = 4" />

    Argument: The point is on the negative real axis.

    <BlockMath math="\theta = 180^\circ" />

3.  <InlineMath math="z_c = -i" />:

    <InlineMath math="z_c = 0 - 1i" />. <InlineMath math="x=0, y=-1" /> (Negative
    imaginary axis) Modulus:

    <BlockMath math="|z_c| = \sqrt{0^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{1} = 1" />

    Argument: The point is on the negative imaginary axis.

    <BlockMath math="\text{Arg}(z_c) = -90^\circ" />

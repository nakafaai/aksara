## Understanding Chord

A chord is a line segment whose endpoints lie on a circle. A taut string stretched between two points on the circumference is a useful model. Each chord determines a minor arc and a major arc, and it divides the disk into a minor segment and a major segment.

Among chords of the same circle, a chord farther from the center is shorter. The longest chord passes through the center and is called a diameter.

## Useful Chord Configurations

The following configurations help organize chord problems:

1. **Non-diameter chord**: A chord that does not pass through the center. It is shorter than the diameter of the same circle.

2. **Diameter**: A chord that passes through the center. It is the longest chord in the circle and divides the disk into two semicircles.

3. **Parallel chords**: A relationship between two or more chords with parallel supporting lines. Their lengths are equal exactly when their perpendicular distances from the center are equal.

## Relationship Between Chord and Distance to Center

The relevant distance is the perpendicular distance from the center to the chord. The perpendicular from the center bisects the chord, creating a right triangle with legs <InlineMath math="d" /> and <InlineMath math="l/2" /> and hypotenuse <InlineMath math="r" />.

If <InlineMath math="d" /> is that perpendicular distance, <InlineMath math="r" /> is the radius, and <InlineMath math="l" /> is the chord length, the Pythagorean theorem gives:

<BlockMath math="l = 2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}" />

This formula shows that when <InlineMath math="d = 0" /> (chord passes through the center), then <InlineMath math="l = 2r" /> which is the diameter.

## Properties of Equal Length Chords

Equal chords in the same circle have equal perpendicular distances from the center. Conversely, chords at equal perpendicular distances from the center have equal lengths.

This property is very useful in solving various circle geometry problems. If two chords have the same length, then:

1. The distance of both chords to the center of the circle is the same
2. Their corresponding minor arcs have the same length
3. Their subtended minor central angles have the same measure

## Intersecting Chords Theorem

When two chords intersect inside a circle, there is a special relationship between the segments formed. If chords <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="CD" /> intersect at point <InlineMath math="P" />, then:

<BlockMath math="AP \times PB = CP \times PD" />

This theorem is known as the intersecting chords theorem and is very useful in solving various geometry problems.

## Practice Problems

1. A circle has a radius of <InlineMath math="13 \text{ cm}" />. If the distance of a chord to the center of the circle is <InlineMath math="5 \text{ cm}" />, determine the length of the chord.

2. In a circle with radius <InlineMath math="10 \text{ cm}" />, there is a chord with length <InlineMath math="16 \text{ cm}" />. Determine the distance of the chord to the center of the circle.

3. Two chords <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="CD" /> in the same circle have lengths of <InlineMath math="24 \text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="18 \text{ cm}" /> respectively. If the radius of the circle is <InlineMath math="15 \text{ cm}" />, determine the difference in distance between the two chords to the center of the circle.

4. Chords <InlineMath math="PQ" /> and <InlineMath math="RS" /> intersect at point <InlineMath math="T" /> in a circle. If <InlineMath math="PT = 6 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="TQ = 8 \text{ cm}" />, and <InlineMath math="RT = 4 \text{ cm}" />, determine the length of <InlineMath math="TS" />.

5. A circle has a diameter of <InlineMath math="26 \text{ cm}" />. Determine the length of a chord that is <InlineMath math="12 \text{ cm}" /> away from the center of the circle.

### Worked Solutions

1. **Answer: <InlineMath math="24 \text{ cm}" />**

   Given: <InlineMath math="r = 13 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="d = 5 \text{ cm}" />

   Using the chord length formula:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="l = 2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="l = 2\sqrt{13^2 - 5^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="l = 2\sqrt{169 - 25}" />
   <BlockMath math="l = 2\sqrt{144} = 2 \times 12 = 24 \text{ cm}" />
   </MathContainer>

2. **Answer: <InlineMath math="6 \text{ cm}" />**

   Given: <InlineMath math="r = 10 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="l = 16 \text{ cm}" />

   Using the chord length formula:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="16 = 2\sqrt{10^2 - d^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="8 = \sqrt{100 - d^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="64 = 100 - d^2" />
   <BlockMath math="d^2 = 100 - 64 = 36" />
   <BlockMath math="d = 6 \text{ cm}" />
   </MathContainer>

3. **Answer: <InlineMath math="3 \text{ cm}" />**

   Given: <InlineMath math="r = 15 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="l_1 = 24 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="l_2 = 18 \text{ cm}" />

   Finding the distance of the first chord:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="24 = 2\sqrt{15^2 - d_1^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="12 = \sqrt{225 - d_1^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="144 = 225 - d_1^2" />
   <BlockMath math="d_1^2 = 81, \text{ so} d_1 = 9 \text{ cm}" />
   </MathContainer>

   Finding the distance of the second chord:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="18 = 2\sqrt{15^2 - d_2^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="9 = \sqrt{225 - d_2^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="81 = 225 - d_2^2" />
   <BlockMath math="d_2^2 = 144, \text{ so} d_2 = 12 \text{ cm}" />
   </MathContainer>

   Difference in distance: <InlineMath math="d_2 - d_1 = 12 - 9 = 3 \text{ cm}" />

4. **Answer: <InlineMath math="12 \text{ cm}" />**

   Given: PT = <InlineMath math="6 \text{ cm}" />, TQ = <InlineMath math="8 \text{ cm}" />, RT = <InlineMath math="4 \text{ cm}" />

   Using the intersecting chords theorem:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="PT \times TQ = RT \times TS" />
   <BlockMath math="6 \times 8 = 4 \times TS" />
   <BlockMath math="48 = 4 \times TS" />
   <BlockMath math="TS = 12 \text{ cm}" />
   </MathContainer>

5. **Answer: <InlineMath math="10 \text{ cm}" />**

   Given: diameter = <InlineMath math="26 \text{ cm}" />, so <InlineMath math="r = 13 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="d = 12 \text{ cm}" />

   Using the chord length formula:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="l = 2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="l = 2\sqrt{13^2 - 12^2}" />
   <BlockMath math="l = 2\sqrt{169 - 144}" />
   <BlockMath math="l = 2\sqrt{25} = 2 \times 5 = 10 \text{ cm}" />
   </MathContainer>

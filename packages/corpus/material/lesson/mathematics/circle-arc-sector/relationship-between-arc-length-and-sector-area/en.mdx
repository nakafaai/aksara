## Arc and Sector Relationships

Arc length and sector area are controlled by the same central angle. On a turning bicycle wheel, a larger rotation makes a point on the rim travel farther and makes the radius sweep a larger area.

As the central angle changes, both quantities change by the same fraction of the full circumference and total circle area.

## Arc Length and Sector Area Proportion

Arc length and sector area take the same fraction of their full-circle values:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{\text{Arc Length}}{\text{Circle Circumference}} = \frac{\text{Sector Area}}{\text{Circle Area}} = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ}" />
</MathContainer>

All three fractions describe the same share of a complete circle.

## Mathematical Formulas

Based on this proportional relationship, we can derive formulas for calculating arc length and sector area:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Arc Length} = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r" />
<BlockMath math="\text{Sector Area} = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2" />
</MathContainer>

From these two formulas, we can find a direct relationship between arc length and sector area:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Sector Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Arc Length} \times r" />
</MathContainer>

## Relationship Between Arc and Sector

For a circle with radius <InlineMath math="r" /> and a nonzero central angle, dividing the sector area by its arc length gives <InlineMath math="r/2" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{\text{Sector Area}}{\text{Arc Length}} = \frac{r}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

Changing the central angle changes both quantities by the same factor, so their ratio stays <InlineMath math="r/2" />.

## Exercises

1. A circle has a radius of <InlineMath math="14 \text{ cm}" />. If the arc length of a sector is <InlineMath math="22 \text{ cm}" />, determine the area of that sector.

2. Given a sector area of <InlineMath math="154 \text{ cm}^2" /> and an arc length of <InlineMath math="22 \text{ cm}" />. Determine the radius of the circle.

3. Two cities lie on the same great circle and are <InlineMath math="1{,}000 \text{ km}" /> apart along that circle. If the corresponding angle at Earth's center is <InlineMath math="9^\circ" />, determine the estimated circumference of Earth.

### Worked Solutions

1. **Solution Steps:**

   Given: <InlineMath math="r = 14 \text{ cm}" />, arc length = <InlineMath math="22 \text{ cm}" />

   Using the relationship: <InlineMath math="\text{Sector Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Arc Length} \times r" />

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\text{Sector Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 22 \times 14" />
   <BlockMath math="= \frac{22 \times 14}{2}" />
   <BlockMath math="= \frac{308}{2}" />
   <BlockMath math="= 154 \text{ cm}^2" />
   </MathContainer>

2. **Solution Steps:**

   Given: sector area = <InlineMath math="154 \text{ cm}^2" />, arc length = <InlineMath math="22 \text{ cm}" />

   Using the relationship: <InlineMath math="\frac{\text{Sector Area}}{\text{Arc Length}} = \frac{r}{2}" />

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\frac{154}{22} = \frac{r}{2}" />
   <BlockMath math="7 = \frac{r}{2}" />
   <BlockMath math="r = 14 \text{ cm}" />
   </MathContainer>

3. **Solution Steps:**

   Given: distance = <InlineMath math="1{,}000 \text{ km}" />, angle = <InlineMath math="9^\circ" />

   Using the proportion: <InlineMath math="\frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Earth Circumference}} = \frac{9^\circ}{360^\circ}" />

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\frac{1{,}000}{\text{Earth Circumference}} = \frac{9^\circ}{360^\circ} = \frac{1}{40}" />
   <BlockMath math="\text{Earth Circumference} = 1{,}000 \times 40" />
   <BlockMath math="= 40{,}000 \text{ km}" />
   </MathContainer>

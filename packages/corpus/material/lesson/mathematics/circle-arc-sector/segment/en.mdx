## Basic Concept of Segment

When a straight cut removes part of a circular region, the separated shape resembles a circle segment.

A chord and either of the two arcs joining its endpoints bound a circle segment. The minor arc bounds the minor segment, while the remaining major arc bounds the major segment. A diameter is the limiting case in which both segments are semicircles.

## Segment Area Formula

To calculate the area of a minor segment, subtract the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord from its sector.

**Why is it different from the arc length formula?**
- **Arc Length** is <InlineMath math="\frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r" /> (units: cm, m, etc.)
- **Segment Area** <InlineMath math="\neq" /> Arc Length (because segment is a <InlineMath math="2\text{D}" /> area rather than a <InlineMath math="1\text{D}" /> line)

    <MathContainer>
    <BlockMath math="\text{Segment Area} = \text{Sector Area} - \text{Triangle Area}" />
    </MathContainer>

    In more detail, the formula can be written as:

    <MathContainer>
    <BlockMath math="\text{Segment Area} = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2 - \frac{1}{2} r^2 \sin \alpha" />
    </MathContainer>

    Here <InlineMath math="0^\circ < \alpha < 180^\circ" /> is the minor central angle in degrees and <InlineMath math="r" /> is the circle radius.

    For a major segment, first use the complementary minor angle <InlineMath math="\beta=360^\circ-\alpha" />, then subtract the corresponding minor segment from the whole circle:

    <MathContainer>
    <BlockMath math="\beta=360^\circ-\alpha" />
    <BlockMath math="A_{\mathrm{major}}=\pi r^2-\left(\frac{\beta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2-\frac{1}{2}r^2\sin\beta\right)" />
    </MathContainer>

**Note the difference in units:**
- Arc length: <InlineMath math="\text{cm}" />, <InlineMath math="\text{m}" /> (length units)
- Segment area: <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^2" />, <InlineMath math="\text{m}^2" /> (area units)

## Segment Area Calculation

To understand how to calculate the area of a segment, use an example with a central angle of <InlineMath math="\alpha = 90^\circ" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r = 10 \text{ cm}" />, according to the visualization of segment formation components above.

**Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Calculate Sector Area

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Sector Area} = \frac{90^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{4} \times \pi \times 10^2" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{4} \times \pi \times 100" />
<BlockMath math="= 25\pi \text{ cm}^2 \approx 78.5398 \text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

**Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Calculate Triangle Area

For a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> angle, the triangle formed is a right triangle with both perpendicular sides being radii:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Triangle Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times r^2 \times \sin 90^\circ" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{2} \times 10^2 \times 1" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{2} \times 100 \times 1" />
<BlockMath math="= 50 \text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

**Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Calculate Segment Area

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Segment Area} = \text{Sector Area} - \text{Triangle Area}" />
<BlockMath math="= 25\pi - 50" />
<BlockMath math="\approx 78.5398 - 50" />
<BlockMath math="\approx 28.5398 \text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

## Exercises

1. A circle has a radius of <InlineMath math="7 \text{ cm}" />. If there is a segment with a central angle of <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, determine the area of the segment.

2. A minor segment has an area of approximately <InlineMath math="15.3546 \text{ cm}^2" /> in a circle of radius <InlineMath math="5 \text{ cm}" />. Determine its minor central angle.

3. A horizontal cylindrical tank of radius <InlineMath math="2 \text{ m}" /> contains water to a depth of <InlineMath math="1.5 \text{ m}" /> measured from the bottom. Determine the cross-sectional area occupied by the water.

### Worked Solutions

1. **Solution Steps:**

   Given: <InlineMath math="r = 7 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="\alpha = 60^\circ" />

   Calculate sector area:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\text{Sector Area} = \frac{60^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \pi \times 7^2" />
   <BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{6} \times \pi \times 49" />
   <BlockMath math="= \frac{49\pi}{6} \text{ cm}^2" />
   </MathContainer>

   Calculate triangle area:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\text{Triangle Area} = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \sin 60^\circ" />
   <BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{2} \times 49 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" />
   <BlockMath math="= \frac{49\sqrt{3}}{4} \text{ cm}^2" />
   </MathContainer>

   Calculate segment area:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\text{Segment Area} = \frac{49\pi}{6} - \frac{49\sqrt{3}}{4}" />
   <BlockMath math="\approx 25.6563 - 21.2176 = 4.4387 \text{ cm}^2" />
   </MathContainer>

2. **Solution Steps:**

   Given: segment area <InlineMath math="\approx 15.3546 \text{ cm}^2" />, <InlineMath math="r = 5 \text{ cm}" />

   Using the formula: <InlineMath math="\text{Segment Area} = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2 - \frac{1}{2} r^2 \sin \alpha" />

   Solve this equation numerically on the minor-angle interval <InlineMath math="0^\circ<\alpha<180^\circ" />. This gives:
   <InlineMath math="\alpha \approx 120^\circ" />

   Verification:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\text{Sector Area} = \frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \pi \times 25 = \frac{25\pi}{3}" />
   <BlockMath math="\text{Triangle Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 25 \times \sin 120^\circ = \frac{25\sqrt{3}}{4}" />
   <BlockMath math="\text{Segment Area} = \frac{25\pi}{3} - \frac{25\sqrt{3}}{4} \approx 26.1799 - 10.8253 = 15.3546 \text{ cm}^2" />
   </MathContainer>

   This matches the stated area after rounding, so the central angle is approximately <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.

3. **Solution Steps:**

   Given: tank radius = <InlineMath math="2 \text{ m}" />, water depth = <InlineMath math="1.5 \text{ m}" />

   Since the water depth (<InlineMath math="1.5 \text{ m}" />) is less than the radius (<InlineMath math="2 \text{ m}" />), the filled part of the circular cross-section is a minor segment.

   For horizontal cylindrical tanks, we use a special segment formula with a specific height.

   Determine segment height from tank bottom:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="h = 1.5 \text{ m (water height from bottom)}" />
   </MathContainer>

   Using the segment area formula for water in a horizontal tank:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\text{Area} = r^2 \times \left[\arccos\left(\frac{r-h}{r}\right) - \frac{r-h}{r} \times \sqrt{1-\left(\frac{r-h}{r}\right)^2}\right]" />
   </MathContainer>

   Here the angle returned by <InlineMath math="\arccos" /> is measured in radians, as required by the area formula.

   Substitute values:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\frac{r-h}{r} = \frac{2-1.5}{2} = 0.25" />
   <BlockMath math="\arccos(0.25) \approx 1.3181 \text{ radians}" />
   <BlockMath math="\sqrt{1-0.25^2} = \sqrt{0.9375} \approx 0.9682" />
   </MathContainer>

   Calculate the water-filled cross-sectional area:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\text{Area} = 4 \times [\arccos(0.25) - 0.25\sqrt{0.9375}]" />
   <BlockMath math="\approx 4 \times 1.0760546" />
   <BlockMath math="\approx 4.3042 \text{ m}^2" />
   </MathContainer>

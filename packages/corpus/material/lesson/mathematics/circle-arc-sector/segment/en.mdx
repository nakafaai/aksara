## Regions Bounded by a Chord and an Arc

A line that intersects a circle at two points forms a [chord](/en/subjects/mathematics/circle-arc-sector/chord). The chord and either arc between its endpoints bound a circle segment. <Highlight>The minor arc bounds the minor segment</Highlight>, while the remaining major arc bounds the major segment. A diameter is the limiting case in which both segments are semicircles.

## Difference Between Arc and Segment

An arc is a **curved line**, while a segment is the region between an arc and its chord. Measure an arc in length units such as centimeters, but a segment in area units such as square centimeters.

For a fixed radius, the central angle distinguishes the two segments:
- **Minor Segment**: central angle <InlineMath math="< 180^\circ" />, segment area <InlineMath math="< \frac{1}{2}\pi r^2" />
- **Major Segment**: central angle <InlineMath math="> 180^\circ" />, segment area <InlineMath math="> \frac{1}{2}\pi r^2" />

## Segment Area Formula

To calculate the area of a minor segment, subtract the **triangle area from the sector area**.

Compare arc length and segment area before using their formulas.

| Quantity | Formula | Unit type |
| :------- | :------ | :-------- |
| Arc length | <InlineMath math="s=\frac{\alpha}{360^\circ}\times 2\pi r" /> | Length, such as <InlineMath math="\text{cm}" /> or <InlineMath math="\text{m}" /> |
| Segment area | <InlineMath math="A_{\mathrm{segment}}=A_{\mathrm{sector}}-A_{\mathrm{triangle}}" /> | Area, such as <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^2" /> or <InlineMath math="\text{m}^2" /> |

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Segment Area} &amp;= \text{Sector Area} \\ &amp;\quad{}- \text{Triangle Area} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Substituting the sector-area and triangle-area formulas gives:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Segment Area} &amp;= \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2 \\ &amp;\quad{}- \frac{1}{2} r^2 \sin \alpha \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Here <InlineMath math="0^\circ < \alpha < 180^\circ" /> is the minor central angle in degrees and <InlineMath math="r" /> is the circle radius.

For a major segment, first use the complementary minor angle <InlineMath math="\beta=360^\circ-\alpha" />, then subtract the corresponding minor segment from the whole circle:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\beta=360^\circ-\alpha" />
  <BlockMath math="A_{\mathrm{minor}}=\frac\beta{360^\circ}\pi r^2-\frac12r^2\sin\beta" />

  <BlockMath math="A_{\mathrm{major}}=\pi r^2-A_{\mathrm{minor}}" />
</MathContainer>

## Segment Area Calculation

Use the same **quarter-circle shape** as in the diagram, but with new dimensions: a central angle of <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> and radius <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" />. Calculate the sector and triangle areas separately.

**Step 1**: Calculate Sector Area

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Sector Area} = \frac{90^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{4} \times \pi \times 10^2" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{4} \times \pi \times 100" />
<BlockMath math="= 25\pi \text{ cm}^2 \approx 78.5398 \text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 2**: Calculate Triangle Area

For a <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> angle, the triangle formed is a right triangle with both perpendicular sides being radii:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Triangle Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times r^2 \times \sin 90^\circ" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{2} \times 10^2 \times 1" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{2} \times 100 \times 1" />
<BlockMath math="= 50 \text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 3**: Calculate Segment Area

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Segment Area} &amp;= \text{Sector Area} \\ &amp;\quad{}- \text{Triangle Area} \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="= 25\pi - 50" />
<BlockMath math="\approx 78.5398 - 50" />
<BlockMath math="\approx 28.5398 \text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

## Exercises

Problem one asks for **segment area**, problem two finds an angle from an area, and problem three finds the water-filled cross-section. In the tank, measure depth from the bottom to find the distance from the surface to the center.

1. A circle has a radius of <InlineMath math="7 \text{ cm}" />. If there is a segment with a central angle of <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, determine the area of the segment.

2. A minor segment has an area of approximately <InlineMath math="15.3546 \text{ cm}^2" /> in a circle of radius <InlineMath math="5 \text{ cm}" />. Determine its minor central angle.

3. A horizontal cylindrical tank of radius <InlineMath math="2 \text{ m}" /> contains water to a depth of <InlineMath math="1.5 \text{ m}" /> measured from the bottom. Determine the cross-sectional area occupied by the water.

### Worked Solutions

Use **sector minus triangle** for a minor segment area. When area is given, find the angle that makes the formula produce it. In the tank cross-section, first draw the chord at the water surface.

**Solution 1**

Given: <InlineMath math="r = 7 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="\alpha = 60^\circ" />

Calculate sector area:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Sector Area} = \frac{60^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \pi \times 7^2" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{6} \times \pi \times 49" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{49\pi}{6} \text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

Calculate triangle area:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Triangle Area} = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \sin 60^\circ" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{2} \times 49 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{49\sqrt{3}}{4} \text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

Calculate segment area:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\text{Segment Area} = \frac{49\pi}{6} - \frac{49\sqrt{3}}{4}" />
<BlockMath math="\approx 25.6563 - 21.2176 = 4.4387 \text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 2**

Given: segment area <InlineMath math="\approx 15.3546 \text{ cm}^2" />, <InlineMath math="r = 5 \text{ cm}" />

Using the formula: <InlineMath math="\text{Segment Area} = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2 - \frac{1}{2} r^2 \sin \alpha" />

Find the angle by **narrowing an interval**. Minor segment area increases as its angle grows. Substituting with a calculator in degree mode gives:

<BlockMath math="A(\alpha)=\frac{25\pi\alpha}{360^\circ}-\frac{25}{2}\sin\alpha" />

| Trial angle | Calculated area |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="110^\circ" /> | <InlineMath math="12.2521\text{ cm}^2" /> |
| <InlineMath math="130^\circ" /> | <InlineMath math="18.7860\text{ cm}^2" /> |
| <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> | <InlineMath math="15.3546\text{ cm}^2" /> |

The target lies between the values for 110 and 130 degrees. Try the interval midpoint, 120 degrees. Its area matches the target to four decimal places:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \text{Sector Area} &amp;= \frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \pi \times 25 \\ &amp;= \frac{25\pi}{3} \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\text{Triangle Area} \\ &amp;= \frac{1}{2} \times 25 \times \sin 120^\circ \\ &amp;= \frac{25\sqrt{3}}{4} \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\text{Segment Area} \\ &amp;= \frac{25\pi}{3} - \frac{25\sqrt{3}}{4} \\ &amp;\approx 26.1799 - 10.8253 \\ &amp;= 15.3546 \text{ cm}^2 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

This matches the stated area after rounding, so the central angle is approximately <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />.

Draw radii to both ends of the water surface. Let half the sector angle be <InlineMath math="\varphi" />. The right triangle on the right gives:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\cos\varphi=\frac{d}{r}=\frac{0.5}{2}=0.25,\qquad \varphi=\arccos(0.25)\approx1.3181161\text{ rad}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{c}{2}=\sqrt{r^2-d^2}=\sqrt{4-0.25}=\sqrt{3.75}\text{ m}" />
</MathContainer>

The sector with full angle <InlineMath math="2\varphi" /> has area <InlineMath math="r^2\varphi" />. The removed triangle has area equal to half the water width times the distance from the center to the surface:

<BlockMath math="A_{\triangle}=\frac{1}{2}\times(2\sqrt{3.75})\times0.5=0.5\sqrt{3.75}\text{ m}^2" />

Subtract the two areas. Use the angle **in radians** and keep full calculator precision until the final rounding:

<BlockMath math="A=4\arccos(0.25)-0.5\sqrt{3.75}\approx5.2724643-0.9682458\approx4.3042\text{ m}^2" />

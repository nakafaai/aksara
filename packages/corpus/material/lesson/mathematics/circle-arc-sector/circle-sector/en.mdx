## Sector Area Formula

A sector of a circle is the region bounded by **two radii and the arc** between them. Its area is a share of the circle's area, and the central angle <InlineMath math="\alpha" /> decides that share:

<BlockMath math="A_{\text{sector}} = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2" />

If the central angle is in radians, the formula becomes <InlineMath math="A = \frac{1}{2}\theta r^2" />. The <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> sector diagram shows a sector of a circle with radius <InlineMath math="3" />, which is one sixth of the circle.

## Finding a Sector Area Step by Step

There are three steps: find **the share of the circle** from the central angle, find the circle's area from the radius, then multiply. Keep <InlineMath math="\pi" /> as a symbol until the last step so you do not round halfway through.

**Example**. A circle with radius <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" /> has a sector with central angle <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />. Find the sector's area.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{60^\circ}{360^\circ} = \frac{1}{6}" />
  <BlockMath math="\pi r^2 = \pi \times 14^2 = 196\pi" />
  <BlockMath math="A = \frac{1}{6} \times 196\pi = \frac{98\pi}{3} \approx 102.63\text{ cm}^2" />
</MathContainer>

If a problem asks for <InlineMath math="\pi = \frac{22}{7}" />, the result changes slightly: <InlineMath math="A = \frac{1}{6} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 196 = \frac{308}{3} \approx 102.67\text{ cm}^2" />. The small gap between the two results comes from rounding <InlineMath math="\pi" />, not from a calculation error.

## Minor and Major Sectors

Two radii split a circle into **two sectors at once**. The minor sector has a central angle below <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, and the major sector takes the rest, <InlineMath math="360^\circ - \alpha" />. Their areas add up to the circle's area:

<BlockMath math="A_{\text{major}} = \pi r^2 - A_{\text{minor}}" />

The <InlineMath math="270^\circ" /> sector in the last diagram is a major sector whose partner is the <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> sector. Check with the table: <InlineMath math="9\pi - \frac{9\pi}{4} = \frac{27\pi}{4}" />, the area of the <InlineMath math="270^\circ" /> sector.

## Perimeter of a Sector

The perimeter of a sector is the length of its whole boundary, **two radii plus the arc**. Its unit is a length, not an area, so keep it apart from the sector area:

<BlockMath math="P_{\text{sector}} = 2r + \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r" />

**Example**. A sector has radius <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" /> and central angle <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />, with <InlineMath math="\pi = \frac{22}{7}" />. Its arc is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{4} \times 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 14 = 22\text{ cm}" /> long, so its perimeter is <InlineMath math="2 \times 14 + 22 = 50\text{ cm}" />. The arc length and the sector area are also linked directly through [the relationship between arc length and sector area](/en/subjects/mathematics/circle-arc-sector/relationship-between-arc-length-and-sector-area): for this sector, <InlineMath math="A = \frac{1}{2} \times 22 \times 14 = 154\text{ cm}^2" />.

## Worked Examples on Sector Area

Every example starts by **finding the share of the circle**. The last one runs backward, from the area to the angle.

**Example 1**. A circle with radius <InlineMath math="21\text{ cm}" /> has a sector with central angle <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />. Find its area.

<BlockMath math="A = \frac{120}{360} \times \pi \times 21^2 = \frac{1}{3} \times 441\pi = 147\pi \approx 461.81\text{ cm}^2" />

With <InlineMath math="\pi = \frac{22}{7}" />, the result is <InlineMath math="147 \times \frac{22}{7} = 462\text{ cm}^2" />.

**Example 2**. A round flatbread with diameter <InlineMath math="28\text{ cm}" /> is cut through its center into <InlineMath math="8" /> equal slices. Find the area of each slice.

The radius is half the diameter, <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" />, and each slice takes <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{8}" /> of the circle:

<BlockMath math="A = \frac{1}{8} \times \pi \times 14^2 = \frac{49\pi}{2} \approx 76.97\text{ cm}^2" />

Check: eight slices add up to <InlineMath math="8 \times \frac{49\pi}{2} = 196\pi" />, the area of the whole flatbread.

**Example 3**. A sector of a circle with radius <InlineMath math="14\text{ cm}" /> has area <InlineMath math="49\pi\text{ cm}^2" />. Find its central angle.

<BlockMath math="\frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} = \frac{49\pi}{196\pi} = \frac{1}{4} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha = 90^\circ" />

Check: <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{4} \times 196\pi = 49\pi" />.

## Common Mistakes

Before you calculate, check whether the problem gives **the radius or the diameter**.

| Mistake | Why it fails | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Using the diameter as <InlineMath math="r" /> | The area uses <InlineMath math="r^2" />, so the result is four times too large | Halve the diameter before you substitute |
| Using the circumference formula <InlineMath math="2\pi r" /> for area | Area uses <InlineMath math="\pi r^2" /> and square units | A sector area is in <InlineMath math="\text{cm}^2" />, not cm |
| Treating the perimeter as the arc length | The perimeter adds the two radii | A sector's perimeter is always more than <InlineMath math="2r" /> |
| Treating a sector as a segment | A sector is bounded by two radii, a [segment](/en/subjects/mathematics/circle-arc-sector/segment) by a chord | Check whether the region reaches the center |

The diameter mistake makes the sector area four times too large, but the result does not always exceed the circle's area. So write <InlineMath math="r = \frac{d}{2}" /> as the first step whenever a problem gives a diameter.

## Practice on Sector Area

For each problem, first find **the share of the circle** from the central angle. Use <InlineMath math="\pi = \frac{22}{7}" /> when the radius is a multiple of <InlineMath math="7" />.

1. Find the area of a <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> sector of a circle with radius <InlineMath math="7\text{ cm}" />.

2. On a circle with radius <InlineMath math="10\text{ cm}" />, the minor sector has a central angle of <InlineMath math="72^\circ" />. Find the area of the major sector in terms of <InlineMath math="\pi" />.

3. Find the perimeter of a <InlineMath math="60^\circ" /> sector of a circle with radius <InlineMath math="21\text{ cm}" />.

4. A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> sector has area <InlineMath math="154\text{ cm}^2" />. Find the radius of the circle.

### Answers

**Problem 1**. An angle of <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> takes **one eighth of the circle**, and the circle's area is <InlineMath math="\frac{22}{7} \times 7^2 = 154\text{ cm}^2" />:

<BlockMath math="A = \frac{1}{8} \times 154 = 19.25\text{ cm}^2" />

Check: eight such sectors add up to <InlineMath math="8 \times 19.25 = 154" />.

**Problem 2**. The major sector uses **the remaining angle** <InlineMath math="360^\circ - 72^\circ = 288^\circ" />:

<BlockMath math="A_{\text{major}} = \frac{288}{360} \times \pi \times 10^2 = \frac{4}{5} \times 100\pi = 80\pi\text{ cm}^2" />

Check with the minor sector: <InlineMath math="\frac{72}{360} \times 100\pi = 20\pi" />, and <InlineMath math="20\pi + 80\pi = 100\pi" />.

**Problem 3**. The perimeter is **two radii plus the arc**. The arc is <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{6} \times 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 21 = 22\text{ cm}" />:

<BlockMath math="P = 2 \times 21 + 22 = 64\text{ cm}" />

Check: the arc of <InlineMath math="22\text{ cm}" /> is one sixth of the circumference <InlineMath math="2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 21 = 132\text{ cm}" />.

**Problem 4**. A <InlineMath math="90^\circ" /> sector is one quarter of the circle, so **the circle's area is four times** <InlineMath math="154" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac{22}{7} r^2 = 4 \times 154 = 616" />
  <BlockMath math="r^2 = 616 \times \frac{7}{22} = 196 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r = 14\text{ cm}" />
</MathContainer>

Check: <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 14^2 = 154" />.

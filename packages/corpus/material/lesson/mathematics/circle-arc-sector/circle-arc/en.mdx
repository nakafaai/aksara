## Arc and Chord Relationship

Every circle arc has a close relationship with the chord that connects its two endpoints. A chord is a line segment connecting two endpoints of an arc, while an arc is a curved path along the circle's circumference. Imagine it like a bow and arrow, where the string is the straight line and the bow is the curved wood.

For minor arcs in the same circle, a larger central angle produces both a longer arc and a longer chord, but <Highlight>chord length is not directly proportional to arc length</Highlight>. Beyond <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, the major arc keeps getting longer while its chord becomes shorter, because the chord is shared with the complementary minor arc.

## Chord Length Formula

Draw a line from the center to the **midpoint of the chord**. It divides the isosceles triangle into two right triangles. In either triangle, the side opposite half the central angle is half the chord:

<BlockMath math="\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)=\frac{c/2}{r}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c=2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />



The variables in this formula are:
- <InlineMath math="c" /> = chord length
- <InlineMath math="r" /> = circle radius
- <InlineMath math="\theta" /> = central angle. Use degree mode for degrees and radian mode for radians

The formula determines a chord from a circular arc's radius and central angle. It can specify the geometry of an idealized circular window, arch, or other curved outline. A real structure still requires a separate analysis of its loads, materials, and supports.

## Arc Height and Sagitta

Arc height, or sagitta, is the distance from the chord's midpoint to the corresponding minor arc, measured **perpendicular to the chord**. Together with the chord, it specifies the shape of an idealized circular arc.

The diagram uses a minor arc with <InlineMath math="0&lt;\theta\leq180^\circ" />. The distance from the center to the chord midpoint is <InlineMath math="r\cos(\theta/2)" />. Subtract this distance from the radius to obtain the sagitta.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} h &amp;= r - r\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \\ &amp;= r\left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right) \end{aligned}" />

The variables in this formula are:
- <InlineMath math="h" /> = arc height (sagitta)
- <InlineMath math="r" /> = circle radius
- <InlineMath math="\theta" /> = central angle. Evaluate the cosine in the same angle unit used for <InlineMath math="\theta" />

The diagram marks the chord, its midpoint, and the perpendicular segment whose length is the sagitta.

## Arc in Coordinate System

For a circle **centered at the origin**, points on an arc follow these parametric equations. The parameter <InlineMath math="t" /> measures the angle counterclockwise from the positive horizontal axis.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="x = r\cos(t)" />
<BlockMath math="y = r\sin(t)" />
</MathContainer>

The parameter <InlineMath math="t" /> runs from the initial angle to the final angle. For the radius-3 circle in the diagram, these values locate points along the arc:

## Idealized Circular Arch Example

An idealized circular arch has radius <InlineMath math="25 \text{ meters}" /> and central angle <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />. The **chord gives the span** of the arch, and the sagitta gives its rise.

**Calculating chord length**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="c = 2r \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />
<BlockMath math="c = 2 \times 25 \times \sin\left(\frac{120^\circ}{2}\right)" />
<BlockMath math="c = 50 \times \sin(60^\circ)" />
<BlockMath math="c = 50 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 25\sqrt{3} \text{ meters}" />
</MathContainer>

**Calculating arc height**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="h = r\left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right)" />
<BlockMath math="h = 25\left(1 - \cos(60^\circ)\right)" />
<BlockMath math="h = 25\left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = 12.5 \text{ meters}" />
</MathContainer>

## Exercises

Mark the **given quantities** before choosing a formula. The first three problems use chord length or sagitta, while the last asks for coordinates along an arc.

1. An idealized circular arch has a radius of <InlineMath math="30 \text{ m}" /> and a central angle of <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. Calculate its chord length and arc height.

2. In an idealized circular cross-section of a dome, the arc height is <InlineMath math="8 \text{ m}" /> and the circle radius is <InlineMath math="15 \text{ m}" />. Determine the central angle of the arc.

3. A minor circular arc has a chord length of <InlineMath math="24 \text{ m}" /> and a radius of <InlineMath math="15 \text{ m}" />. Calculate its minor central angle and arc height.

4. In a coordinate system, an arc starts from point <InlineMath math="(4, 0)" /> and ends at point <InlineMath math="(0, 4)" /> on a circle centered at the origin. Determine a standard radian parameterization of the minor arc.

### Worked Solutions

Use **half the central angle** in the sine and cosine. In problem three, the right triangle gives an exact height without rounding the angle first.

**Solution 1**

Given: <InlineMath math="r = 30 \text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="\theta = 90^\circ" />

**Chord length**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} c &amp;= 2r \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \\ &amp;= 2 \times 30 \times \sin(45^\circ) \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="c = 60 \times \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 30\sqrt{2} \text{ m} \approx 42.43 \text{ m}" />
</MathContainer>

**Arc height**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} h &amp;= r\left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right) \\ &amp;= 30\left(1 - \cos(45^\circ)\right) \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="h = 30\left(1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) \approx 8.79 \text{ m}" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 2**

Given: <InlineMath math="h = 8 \text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="r = 15 \text{ m}" />

**Step 1**: Use the arc height formula

<BlockMath math="8 = 15\left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right)" />

**Step 2**: Isolate <InlineMath math="\cos" />

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{8}{15} = 1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />
<BlockMath math="\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = 1 - \frac{8}{15} = \frac{7}{15}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 3**: Calculate angle

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{\theta}{2} = \arccos\left(\frac{7}{15}\right) \approx 62.18^\circ" />
<BlockMath math="\theta \approx 124.36^\circ" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 3**

Given: <InlineMath math="c = 24 \text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="r = 15 \text{ m}" />

**Step 1**: Use the chord formula

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="24 = 2 \times 15 \times \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />
<BlockMath math="\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \frac{24}{30} = 0.8" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 2**: Calculate the minor central angle

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{\theta}{2} = \arcsin(0.8) \approx 53.13^\circ" />
<BlockMath math="\theta \approx 106.26^\circ" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 3**: Calculate arc height

<BlockMath math="OM=\sqrt{15^2-12^2}=\sqrt{81}=9\text{ m},\qquad h=15-9=6\text{ m}" />

**Solution 4**

- Starting point: <InlineMath math="(4, 0)" /> → <InlineMath math="t_1 = 0" />
- Ending point: <InlineMath math="(0, 4)" /> → <InlineMath math="t_2 = \frac{\pi}{2}" />
- Radius: <InlineMath math="r = 4" />

**Parametric equations**:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="x = 4\cos(t)" />
<BlockMath math="y = 4\sin(t)" />
<BlockMath math="0 \leq t \leq \frac{\pi}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

## Arc and Chord Relationship

Every circle arc has a close relationship with the chord that connects its two endpoints. A chord is a straight line connecting two endpoints of an arc, while an arc is a curved path along the circle's circumference. Imagine it like a bow and arrow, where the string is the straight line and the bow is the curved wood.

For minor arcs in the same circle, a larger central angle produces both a longer arc and a longer chord, but the relationship is not linear. Beyond <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, the major arc keeps getting longer while its chord becomes shorter, because the chord is shared with the complementary minor arc.

## Chord Length Formula

The radius and central angle determine the chord length:

<BlockMath math="c = 2r \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />

The variables in this formula are:
- <InlineMath math="c" /> = chord length
- <InlineMath math="r" /> = circle radius
- <InlineMath math="\theta" /> = central angle. Use degree mode for degrees and radian mode for radians

The formula determines a chord from a circular arc's radius and central angle. It can specify the geometry of an idealized circular window, arch, or other curved outline. A real structure still requires a separate analysis of its loads, materials, and supports.

## Arc Height and Sagitta

Arc height, or sagitta, is the distance from the chord's midpoint to the corresponding minor arc, measured perpendicular to the chord. Together with the chord, it specifies the shape of an idealized circular arc.

<BlockMath math="h = r - r\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = r\left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right)" />

The variables in this formula are:
- <InlineMath math="h" /> = arc height (sagitta)
- <InlineMath math="r" /> = circle radius
- <InlineMath math="\theta" /> = central angle. Evaluate the cosine in the same angle unit used for <InlineMath math="\theta" />

Where <InlineMath math="t" /> is a parameter that varies from the initial angle to the final angle of the arc.

## Idealized Circular Arch Example

An idealized circular arch has radius <InlineMath math="25 \text{ meters}" /> and central angle <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />. The chord gives its span, and the sagitta gives its rise.

**Calculating chord length:**

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="c = 2r \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />
<BlockMath math="c = 2 \times 25 \times \sin\left(\frac{120^\circ}{2}\right)" />
<BlockMath math="c = 50 \times \sin(60^\circ)" />
<BlockMath math="c = 50 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 25\sqrt{3} \text{ meters}" />
</MathContainer>

**Calculating arc height:**

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="h = r\left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right)" />
<BlockMath math="h = 25\left(1 - \cos(60^\circ)\right)" />
<BlockMath math="h = 25\left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = 12.5 \text{ meters}" />
</MathContainer>

## Exercises

1. An idealized circular arch has a radius of <InlineMath math="30 \text{ m}" /> and a central angle of <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. Calculate its chord length and arc height.

2. In an idealized circular cross-section of a dome, the arc height is <InlineMath math="8 \text{ m}" /> and the circle radius is <InlineMath math="15 \text{ m}" />. Determine the central angle of the arc.

3. A minor circular arc has a chord length of <InlineMath math="24 \text{ m}" /> and a radius of <InlineMath math="15 \text{ m}" />. Calculate its minor central angle and arc height.

4. In a coordinate system, an arc starts from point <InlineMath math="(4, 0)" /> and ends at point <InlineMath math="(0, 4)" /> on a circle centered at the origin. Determine a standard radian parameterization of the minor arc.

### Worked Solutions

1. **Solution:**

   Given: <InlineMath math="r = 30 \text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="\theta = 90^\circ" />

   **Chord length:**

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="c = 2r \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = 2 \times 30 \times \sin(45^\circ)" />
   <BlockMath math="c = 60 \times \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 30\sqrt{2} \text{ m} \approx 42.43 \text{ m}" />
   </MathContainer>

   **Arc height:**

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="h = r\left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right) = 30\left(1 - \cos(45^\circ)\right)" />
   <BlockMath math="h = 30\left(1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) \approx 8.79 \text{ m}" />
   </MathContainer>

2. **Solution:**

   Given: <InlineMath math="h = 8 \text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="r = 15 \text{ m}" />

   **Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Use the arc height formula

   <BlockMath math="8 = 15\left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right)" />

   **Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Isolate <InlineMath math="\cos" />

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\frac{8}{15} = 1 - \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />
   <BlockMath math="\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = 1 - \frac{8}{15} = \frac{7}{15}" />
   </MathContainer>

   **Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Calculate angle

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\frac{\theta}{2} = \arccos\left(\frac{7}{15}\right) \approx 62.18^\circ" />
   <BlockMath math="\theta \approx 124.36^\circ" />
   </MathContainer>

3. **Solution:**

   Given: <InlineMath math="c = 24 \text{ m}" /> and <InlineMath math="r = 15 \text{ m}" />

   **Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Use the chord formula

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="24 = 2 \times 15 \times \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />
   <BlockMath math="\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \frac{24}{30} = 0.8" />
   </MathContainer>

   **Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Calculate the minor central angle

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="\frac{\theta}{2} = \arcsin(0.8) \approx 53.13^\circ" />
   <BlockMath math="\theta \approx 106.26^\circ" />
   </MathContainer>

   **Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Calculate arc height

   <BlockMath math="h = 15\left(1 - \cos(53.13^\circ)\right) = 15(1 - 0.6) = 6 \text{ m}" />

4. **Solution:**

   - Starting point: <InlineMath math="(4, 0)" /> → <InlineMath math="t_1 = 0" />
   - Ending point: <InlineMath math="(0, 4)" /> → <InlineMath math="t_2 = \frac{\pi}{2}" />
   - Radius: <InlineMath math="r = 4" />

   **Parametric equations:**

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="x = 4\cos(t)" />
   <BlockMath math="y = 4\sin(t)" />
   <BlockMath math="0 \leq t \leq \frac{\pi}{2}" />
   </MathContainer>

## Property of Composition with Inverse

A function and its inverse <Highlight>undo each other</Highlight>. The order determines the set on which the identity applies: <InlineMath math="f\circ f^{-1}" /> is the identity on the range of <InlineMath math="f" />, while <InlineMath math="f^{-1}\circ f" /> is the identity on the domain of <InlineMath math="f" />.

**Step 1**. Composition of <InlineMath math="f" /> with <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" />.

<BlockMath math="(f \circ f^{-1})(x) = f(f^{-1}(x)) = x" />

This holds for all <InlineMath math="x" /> in the domain of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> (which is the range of <InlineMath math="f" />).

**Step 2**. Composition of <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> with <InlineMath math="f" />.

<BlockMath math="(f^{-1} \circ f)(x) = f^{-1}(f(x)) = x" />

This holds for all <InlineMath math="x" /> in the domain of <InlineMath math="f" />.

**Example**:

We know that if <InlineMath math="f(x) = 2x + 3" />, its inverse is <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(x) = \frac{x - 3}{2}" />. Verify the composition property:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}f(f^{-1}(x)) &= f\left(\frac{x - 3}{2}\right) \\ &= 2\left(\frac{x - 3}{2}\right) + 3 \\ &= (x - 3) + 3 \\ &= x\end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}f^{-1}(f(x)) &= f^{-1}(2x + 3) \\ &= \frac{(2x + 3) - 3}{2} \\ &= \frac{2x}{2} \\ &= x\end{aligned}" />

Both compositions return the original input <InlineMath math="x" />.

## Property of the Inverse of an Inverse

If we find the inverse of an inverse function, we get back the original function.

<BlockMath math="(f^{-1})^{-1}(x) = f(x)" />

Taking an inverse exchanges the input and output of each pair. Exchanging them a second time restores the original function.

The input and output undergo **two exchanges**. For the function above, follow the ordered pairs through the table.

## Property of the Inverse of a Composition

If we have a composition of two invertible functions with compatible domain and codomain, the inverse of the composition is the composition of their inverses, but in **reverse order**.

Let <InlineMath math="f" /> and <InlineMath math="g" /> be two functions with inverses <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> and <InlineMath math="g^{-1}" />. Then the inverse of the composition <InlineMath math="f \circ g" /> is:

<BlockMath math="(f \circ g)^{-1}(x) = (g^{-1} \circ f^{-1})(x)" />

Read the composition from right to left: <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" /> is applied first, then <InlineMath math="g^{-1}" />.

**Example**:

Let <InlineMath math="f(x) = x + 1" /> (its inverse is <InlineMath math="f^{-1}(x) = x - 1" />) and <InlineMath math="g(x) = 3x" /> (its inverse is <InlineMath math="g^{-1}(x) = \frac{x}{3}" />).

**Step 1**. Find <InlineMath math="(f \circ g)(x)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} (f \circ g)(x) &amp;= f(g(x)) \\ &amp;= f(3x) \\ &amp;= 3x + 1 \end{aligned}" />

**Step 2**. Find the inverse of <InlineMath math="(f \circ g)(x)" />.

Let <InlineMath math="y = 3x + 1" />. Swap <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />: <InlineMath math="x = 3y + 1" />.

Solve for <InlineMath math="y" />: <InlineMath math="x - 1 = 3y \implies y = \frac{x - 1}{3}" />.

So, <InlineMath math="(f \circ g)^{-1}(x) = \frac{x - 1}{3}" />.

**Step 3**. Find <InlineMath math="(g^{-1} \circ f^{-1})(x)" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} (g^{-1} \circ f^{-1})(x) &amp;= g^{-1}(f^{-1}(x)) \\ &amp;= g^{-1}(x - 1) \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="g^{-1}(x - 1) = \frac{x - 1}{3}" />
</MathContainer>

Since the results from steps 2 and 3 are the same, it is proven that <InlineMath math="(f \circ g)^{-1}(x) = (g^{-1} \circ f^{-1})(x)" />.

## Domain and Range Relationship

The domain of the original function <InlineMath math="f" /> becomes the range of its inverse function <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" />, and the range of the original function <InlineMath math="f" /> becomes the domain of its inverse function <InlineMath math="f^{-1}" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\text{Domain}(f) = \text{Range}(f^{-1}) \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\text{Range}(f) = \text{Domain}(f^{-1}) \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The **domain and range exchange roles** when we take the inverse. Take the following restricted quadratic function.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="q:[0,\infty)\to[1,\infty),\quad q(x)=x^2+1" />
  <BlockMath math="q^{-1}:[1,\infty)\to[0,\infty),\quad q^{-1}(x)=\sqrt{x-1}" />
</MathContainer>

| Function | Domain | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="q" /> | <InlineMath math="[0,\infty)" /> | <InlineMath math="[1,\infty)" /> |
| <InlineMath math="q^{-1}" /> | <InlineMath math="[1,\infty)" /> | <InlineMath math="[0,\infty)" /> |

The square root returns the original input because the quadratic function has a nonnegative domain. Without this restriction, the square root of a negative number squared returns its absolute value.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="q^{-1}(q(x))=\sqrt{x^2}=x,\quad x\geq0" />
  <BlockMath math="q(q^{-1}(y))=(\sqrt{y-1})^2+1=y,\quad y\geq1" />
</MathContainer>

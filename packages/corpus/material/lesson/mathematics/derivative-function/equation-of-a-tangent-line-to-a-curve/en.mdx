## Relationship Between Derivatives and Tangent Lines

The derivative describes how a function changes at a particular input. Geometrically, that change appears as the **slope** of the tangent line to the graph.

If we magnify a smooth curve near one point, a sufficiently small section looks almost straight. The line that captures this local direction is the **tangent line**. At <InlineMath math="x=a" /> on the curve <InlineMath math="y=f(x)" />, its slope is the derivative evaluated at that input: <InlineMath math="m=f'(a)" />.

## Determining the Equation of a Tangent Line

To determine a straight-line equation, we need one point on the line and its slope. For a tangent line, these are:

1.  **Point of Tangency:** A point <InlineMath math="P(a, b)" /> where the line touches the curve.
2.  **Gradient (m):** The slope of the line at that point, which we get from the derivative, <InlineMath math="m = f'(a)" />.

Substitute both values into the point-slope form of a line:

<BlockMath math="y - b = m(x - a)" />

> Differentiate the function, evaluate the derivative at the point of tangency, and use that slope with the point-slope equation.

## Worked Example

Determine the equation of the tangent line to the parabola <InlineMath math="y = 2 - 4x^2" /> at the point <InlineMath math="(1, -2)" />.

**Solution:**

**Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Find the gradient of the tangent line

Differentiate <InlineMath math="f(x) = 2 - 4x^2" /> to obtain the slope at any input.

<BlockMath math="f'(x) = -8x" />

The point of tangency has <InlineMath math="x=1" />, so evaluate the derivative there.

<BlockMath math="m = f'(1) = -8(1) = -8" />

The tangent line therefore has slope <InlineMath math="-8" />.

**Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Construct the equation

The required point and slope are now known:
-   Point of tangency <InlineMath math="(a, b) = (1, -2)" />
-   Gradient <InlineMath math="m = -8" />

Substitute them into the point-slope equation:

<MathContainer>
    <BlockMath math="y - b = m(x - a)" />
    <BlockMath math="y - (-2) = -8(x - 1)" />
    <BlockMath math="y + 2 = -8x + 8" />
    <BlockMath math="y = -8x + 6" />
</MathContainer>

The tangent line is therefore <InlineMath math="y = -8x + 6" />.

## Exercises

1.  Find the equation of the tangent line to the curve <InlineMath math="y = x^2 - 6\frac{1}{5}x + 14\frac{1}{2}" /> which is parallel to the line <InlineMath math="x + 2y + 3 = 0" />.

2.  Determine the tangent line equations to the parabola <InlineMath math="y = 2x^2 - 3x + 5" /> at the points whose <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate is <InlineMath math="4" />.

3.  The curve <InlineMath math="y = 3x - \frac{3}{x^2}" /> intersects the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis at <InlineMath math="P" />. Find the tangent line equation at that point.

4.  The curve <InlineMath math="y = (x^2 + 2)^2" /> intersects the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis at <InlineMath math="A" />. Show that the tangent line at <InlineMath math="A" /> is parallel to the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis and is <InlineMath math="4 \text{ units}" /> from the origin.

5.  Determine the point on the curve <InlineMath math="y = 2x^2 - 7x + 1" /> where the tangent line forms an angle of <InlineMath math="45^\circ" /> with the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis. Then determine that tangent line equation.

### Answer Key

1.  **Solution:**

    **Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Determine the gradient.

    The tangent line must be parallel to <InlineMath math="x + 2y + 3 = 0" />. Convert this equation into slope-intercept form <InlineMath math="y = mx + c" /> to read its slope.

    <MathContainer>
        <BlockMath math="2y = -x - 3" />
        <BlockMath math="y = -\frac{1}{2}x - \frac{3}{2}" />
    </MathContainer>

    From this, we know the gradient of the line is <InlineMath math="m = -\frac{1}{2}" />. Since the tangent line is parallel, its gradient is the same.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Find the point of tangency.

    The curve's slope at a point equals the derivative there. Differentiate <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2 - \frac{31}{5}x + \frac{29}{2}" /> first.

    <BlockMath math="f'(x) = 2x - \frac{31}{5}" />

    Next, we set this derivative equal to the known slope (<InlineMath math="m = -\frac{1}{2}" />) to find the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate of the point of tangency.

    <MathContainer>
        <BlockMath math="2x - \frac{31}{5} = -\frac{1}{2}" />
        <BlockMath math="2x = \frac{31}{5} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{62 - 5}{10} = \frac{57}{10}" />
        <BlockMath math="x = \frac{57}{20}" />
    </MathContainer>

    After getting the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate <InlineMath math="x=\frac{57}{20}" />, we substitute this value back into the *original* curve equation to find its <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate.

    <BlockMath math="y = (\frac{57}{20})^2 - \frac{31}{5}(\frac{57}{20}) + \frac{29}{2} = \frac{3249}{400} - \frac{1767}{100} + \frac{29}{2} = \frac{3249 - 7068 + 5800}{400} = \frac{1981}{400}" />

    The point of tangency is <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{57}{20}, \frac{1981}{400}\right)" />.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Construct the line equation.

    With the point <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{57}{20}, \frac{1981}{400}\right)" /> and gradient <InlineMath math="m = -\frac{1}{2}" />, the equation is:

    <MathContainer>
        <BlockMath math="y - \frac{1981}{400} = -\frac{1}{2}\left(x - \frac{57}{20}\right)" />
        <BlockMath math="y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{57}{40} + \frac{1981}{400}" />
        <BlockMath math="y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{570 + 1981}{400}" />
        <BlockMath math="y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{2551}{400}" />
    </MathContainer>

2.  **Solution:**

    **Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Find the point(s) of tangency.

    Since the ordinate is <InlineMath math="4" />, we set <InlineMath math="y=4" /> in the parabola's equation.

    <MathContainer>
        <BlockMath math="4 = 2x^2 - 3x + 5" />
        <BlockMath math="2x^2 - 3x + 1 = 0" />
        <BlockMath math="(2x-1)(x-1) = 0" />
    </MathContainer>

    From factorization, we get two x-values: <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x=\frac{1}{2}" />. This means there are two points of tangency: <InlineMath math="(1, 4)" /> and <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{1}{2}, 4\right)" />. Therefore, there will be two tangent line equations.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Calculate the gradient and create the equation for each point.

    We will process each point of tangency separately. The derivative of the function is <InlineMath math="f'(x) = 4x - 3" />.

    **Case One: Point <InlineMath math="(1, 4)" />**

    The gradient at this point is <InlineMath math="m = f'(1) = 4(1) - 3 = 1" />.

    Thus, the equation is:

    <BlockMath math="y - 4 = 1(x - 1) \implies y = x + 3" />

    **Case Two: Point <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{1}{2}, 4\right)" />**

    The gradient at this point is <InlineMath math="m = f'(\frac{1}{2}) = 4(\frac{1}{2}) - 3 = -1" />.

    Thus, the equation is:

    <BlockMath math="y - 4 = -1(x - \frac{1}{2}) \implies y = -x + \frac{9}{2}" />

3.  **Solution:**

    **Step <InlineMath math="1" />: Find point <InlineMath math="P" />.**

    The curve intersects the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis when <InlineMath math="y=0" />.

    <MathContainer>
        <BlockMath math="0 = 3x - \frac{3}{x^2}" />
        <BlockMath math="3x = \frac{3}{x^2} \implies 3x^3 = 3 \implies x^3 = 1 \implies x=1" />
    </MathContainer>

    The intersection point <InlineMath math="P" /> is <InlineMath math="(1, 0)" />.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Find the slope at <InlineMath math="P" />.

    Rewrite the function as <InlineMath math="f(x) = 3x - 3x^{-2}" /> so the power rule can be applied directly.

    <BlockMath math="f'(x) = 3 - (-2)(3)x^{-3} = 3 + \frac{6}{x^3}" />

    The gradient at <InlineMath math="x=1" /> is <InlineMath math="m = f'(1) = 3 + \frac{6}{1^3} = 9" />.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Construct the equation.

    With point <InlineMath math="(1, 0)" /> and gradient <InlineMath math="9" />, the equation is:

    <BlockMath math="y - 0 = 9(x - 1) \implies y = 9x - 9" />

4.  **Solution:**

    **Step <InlineMath math="1" />: Find point <InlineMath math="A" />.**

    The curve intersects the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis when <InlineMath math="x=0" />.

    <BlockMath math="y = (0^2 + 2)^2 = 4" />

    The intersection point <InlineMath math="A" /> is <InlineMath math="(0, 4)" />.

    **Step <InlineMath math="2" />: Prove the tangent line is parallel to the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.**

    A line parallel to the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis has slope <InlineMath math="0" />. Evaluate the derivative at <InlineMath math="A" />, where <InlineMath math="x=0" />.

    The derivative of <InlineMath math="f(x)=(x^2+2)^2" /> using the chain rule is <InlineMath math="f'(x) = 2(x^2+2)(2x) = 4x(x^2+2)" />.

    The gradient at <InlineMath math="x=0" /> is <InlineMath math="m = f'(0) = 4(0)(0^2+2) = 0" />.

    Since the gradient is zero, **it is proven that the tangent line is parallel to the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis**.

    **Step <InlineMath math="3" />: Prove its distance is <InlineMath math="4 \text{ units}" /> from the origin.**

    The equation of the tangent line at point <InlineMath math="(0,4)" /> with gradient <InlineMath math="m=0" /> is:

    <BlockMath math="y - 4 = 0(x - 0) \implies y = 4" />

    The line <InlineMath math="y=4" /> is a horizontal line. The distance from any point on this line to the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis (the line <InlineMath math="y=0" />) is <InlineMath math="4 \text{ units}" />. Since the origin <InlineMath math="(0,0)" /> lies on the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, the distance from this tangent line to the origin is also <InlineMath math="4 \text{ units}" />. **Proven.**

5.  **Solution:**

    **Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Determine the gradient from the angle.

    The relationship between the gradient (<InlineMath math="m" />) and the angle (<InlineMath math="\theta" />) a line makes with the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis is given by <InlineMath math="m = \tan(\theta)" />.

    <BlockMath math="m = \tan(45^\circ) = 1" />

    The required tangent line has slope <InlineMath math="1" />.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Find the coordinates of the point of tangency.

    The gradient is also the first derivative of the curve <InlineMath math="f(x) = 2x^2 - 7x + 1" />.

    <BlockMath math="f'(x) = 4x - 7" />

    We set it equal to the gradient we found:

    <MathContainer>
        <BlockMath math="4x - 7 = 1" />
        <BlockMath math="4x = 8 \implies x = 2" />
    </MathContainer>

    Now, find the <InlineMath math="y" />-value by plugging <InlineMath math="x=2" /> into the curve's equation:

    <BlockMath math="y = 2(2)^2 - 7(2) + 1 = 8 - 14 + 1 = -5" />

    The point of tangency is <InlineMath math="(2, -5)" />.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Determine the equation of the tangent line.

    Using the point <InlineMath math="(2, -5)" /> and gradient <InlineMath math="m=1" />:

    <MathContainer>
        <BlockMath math="y - (-5) = 1(x - 2)" />
        <BlockMath math="y + 5 = x - 2" />
        <BlockMath math="y = x - 7" />
    </MathContainer>

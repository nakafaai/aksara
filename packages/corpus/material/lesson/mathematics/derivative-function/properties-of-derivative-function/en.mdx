## From the Limit Definition to Practical Rules

The limit definition explains what a derivative means. Repeating that full limit calculation for every function, however, would hide the pattern we actually want to use. Derivative rules capture those recurring patterns without changing the underlying idea of instantaneous rate of change.

The important skill is not memorizing a long list. It is recognizing how a function is built, choosing the matching rule, and keeping each algebraic step visible.

## Constant, Power, and Linear-Combination Rules

### Constant and Power Functions

1. **Constant rule:** A constant function has a horizontal graph, so its slope is zero everywhere.

   <BlockMath math="f(x)=k \implies f'(x)=0" />

2. **Power rule:** For a constant <InlineMath math="a" />, multiply by the exponent, then reduce the exponent by one.

   <BlockMath math="f(x)=ax^n \implies f'(x)=anx^{n-1}" />

   This formula holds for every integer <InlineMath math="n" /> wherever <InlineMath math="x^n" /> is defined. For a general real exponent, it holds on <InlineMath math="x>0" /> and on any larger real domain where the power function is defined and differentiable.

### Linear Combinations

Let <InlineMath math="u(x)" /> and <InlineMath math="v(x)" /> be differentiable functions.

1. **Constant multiple:** A constant factor stays in front of the derivative.

   <BlockMath math="f(x)=k\,u(x) \implies f'(x)=k\,u'(x)" />

2. **Sum and difference:** Differentiate each term separately and keep the operation between them.

   <BlockMath math="f(x)=u(x)\pm v(x) \implies f'(x)=u'(x)\pm v'(x)" />

## Product, Quotient, and Chain Rules

### Product Rule

For a product, differentiating both factors and multiplying the results is not enough. Differentiate one factor at a time while leaving the other unchanged, then add the two terms.

<BlockMath math="f(x)=u(x)v(x) \implies f'(x)=u'(x)v(x)+u(x)v'(x)" />

### Quotient Rule

For a quotient, the order in the numerator matters. The denominator <InlineMath math="v(x)" /> must also be nonzero wherever the original function is defined.

<BlockMath math="f(x)=\frac{u(x)}{v(x)} \implies f'(x)=\frac{u'(x)v(x)-u(x)v'(x)}{[v(x)]^2}" />

### Chain Rule

A composite function applies one function inside another. Differentiate the outer function at the unchanged inner input, then multiply by the derivative of that inner function.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="f(x)=g(u(x)) \implies f'(x)=g'(u(x))\,u'(x)" />
  <BlockMath math="f(x)=[u(x)]^n \implies f'(x)=n[u(x)]^{n-1}u'(x)" />
</MathContainer>

## Worked Example

Differentiate <InlineMath math="f(x)=(x^2+1)^3" />.

The outer function raises its input to the third power. The inner function is <InlineMath math="u(x)=x^2+1" />. Apply the power rule to the outer function, then multiply by the derivative of the inner function.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="u'(x)=2x" />
  <BlockMath math="f'(x)=3[u(x)]^2u'(x)" />
  <BlockMath math="f'(x)=6x(x^2+1)^2" />
</MathContainer>

The factor <InlineMath math="2x" /> is essential. Leaving it out would mean differentiating only the outer function and stopping before the chain rule is complete.

## Practice

1. Differentiate <InlineMath math="h(x)=(2x-1)^4" />.

### Worked Solution

1. The outer function is a fourth power, while the inner function is <InlineMath math="u(x)=2x-1" /> with derivative <InlineMath math="u'(x)=2" />.

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="h'(x)=4[u(x)]^3u'(x)" />
     <BlockMath math="h'(x)=8(2x-1)^3" />
   </MathContainer>

   The result includes both parts of the composition: the derivative of the fourth power and the derivative of its inner linear function.

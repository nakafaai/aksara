## What Is a Circle?

A circle is the set of all points in a plane that are a fixed distance from one point. That point is the **center**, and the fixed distance is the **radius**.

If the center is <InlineMath math="O(a,b)" /> and the radius is <InlineMath math="r" />, the distance formula gives the standard equation:

<BlockMath math="(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2" />

**The four parts to recognize first:**

- **Center (<InlineMath math="O" />)**: the fixed point from which every radius is measured
- **Radius (<InlineMath math="r" />)**: a segment from the center to a point on the circle
- **Diameter (<InlineMath math="d" />)**: a chord through the center, so <InlineMath math="d = 2r" />
- **Chord**: a segment joining two points on the circle

## Circular Arcs

A circular arc is the part of a circle between two points on its circumference. We write an arc with a curved mark over its endpoints, for example <InlineMath math="\overset{\frown}{AB}" />.

**Classifying arcs by length:**

- **Minor arc**: shorter than half the circumference
- **Major arc**: longer than half the circumference
- **Semicircle**: exactly half the circumference

## Relationship Between Central Angle and Inscribed Angle

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Inscribed angle} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Central angle}" />
  <BlockMath math="\angle ACB = \frac{1}{2} \times \angle AOB" />
</MathContainer>

When a central angle and an inscribed angle intercept the same arc, the inscribed angle is half the central angle.

## Arc Length and Sector Area

### Arc Length

An arc occupies the same fraction of the circumference as its central angle occupies of a full turn. For a central angle of <InlineMath math="\alpha" /> degrees:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Arc length} = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r" />
  <BlockMath math="l = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r" />
</MathContainer>

Where:

- <InlineMath math="l" /> = arc length
- <InlineMath math="\alpha" /> = measure of central angle (in degrees)
- <InlineMath math="r" /> = radius of circle

### Sector Area

A sector is the region bounded by two radii and the arc between them. It occupies the same fraction of the circle's area as its central angle occupies of a full turn.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Sector area} = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2" />
  <BlockMath math="L = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2" />
</MathContainer>

## Calculating Arc Length and Sector Area

A circle has a radius of <InlineMath math="14 \text{ cm}" />. If the central angle that subtends an arc is <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />, determine:

1. Arc length
2. Sector area

**Solution:**

Given: <InlineMath math="r = 14 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="\alpha = 90^\circ" />

1. Arc length:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="l = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi r" />
     <BlockMath math="l = \frac{90^\circ}{360^\circ} \times 2\pi \times 14" />
     <BlockMath math="l = \frac{1}{4} \times 28\pi" />
     <BlockMath math="l = 7\pi \text{ cm} \approx 21.99 \text{ cm}" />
   </MathContainer>

2. Sector area:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="L = \frac{\alpha}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2" />
     <BlockMath math="L = \frac{90^\circ}{360^\circ} \times \pi \times 14^2" />
     <BlockMath math="L = \frac{1}{4} \times 196\pi" />
     <BlockMath math="L = 49\pi \text{ cm}^2 \approx 153.94 \text{ cm}^2" />
   </MathContainer>

## Practice Problems

1. A circle has a diameter of <InlineMath math="20 \text{ cm}" />. If an inscribed angle that subtends an arc is <InlineMath math="30^\circ" />, determine the measure of central angle that subtends the same arc!

2. In a circle with center <InlineMath math="O" /> and radius <InlineMath math="21 \text{ cm}" />, there is an arc <InlineMath math="AB" /> with central angle <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />. Calculate:

   - Arc length <InlineMath math="AB" />
   - Sector area <InlineMath math="AOB" />

3. Two inscribed angles subtend the same arc. If one inscribed angle measures <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />, determine the measure of the other inscribed angle!

### Worked Solutions

1. The diameter is not needed here. What matters is that both angles intercept the same arc. The central angle is twice the inscribed angle:

   <BlockMath math="\text{central angle}=2(30^\circ)=60^\circ" />

   So the central angle measures <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />.

2. The angle <InlineMath math="120^\circ" /> is one third of a full turn because <InlineMath math="\frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ}=\frac{1}{3}" />. Apply that fraction first to the circumference, then to the area of the circle.

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="l=\frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ}(2\pi)(21)=14\pi\text{ cm}\approx43.98\text{ cm}" />
     <BlockMath math="L=\frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ}\pi(21)^2=147\pi\text{ cm}^2\approx461.81\text{ cm}^2" />
   </MathContainer>

   Therefore, the arc length is <InlineMath math="14\pi\text{ cm}" /> and the sector area is <InlineMath math="147\pi\text{ cm}^2" />.

3. Inscribed angles that intercept the same arc are equal. Since the first angle is <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />, the second angle is also <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />.

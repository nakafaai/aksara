## A Central Angle Is Twice an Inscribed Angle

A central angle has its vertex at the center of a circle. An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle itself. When both angles intercept **the same arc**, the central angle is always twice the inscribed angle.

<BlockMath math="\angle AOB = 2 \times \angle ACB" />

In the relationship diagram, the arms of the central angle <InlineMath math="\angle AOB" /> and the arms of the inscribed angle <InlineMath math="\angle ACB" /> end at the same two points, <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />. Those points are the endpoints of arc <InlineMath math="AB" />. Follow each arm to the end of the arc, then compare the two angles.

The central angle in that diagram measures <InlineMath math="45^\circ" />. The inscribed angle that intercepts the minor arc <InlineMath math="AB" /> is half of it:

## Telling Central and Inscribed Angles Apart

You tell these two angles apart by **where the vertex sits** and by which segments form the arms. The arc an angle intercepts is the [arc of the circle](/en/subjects/mathematics/circle/circle-and-arc-circle) that lies inside the angle, between the two points where its arms meet the circle. That arc links the two kinds of angle, so find it before you calculate anything.

## Common Mistakes

Before you calculate, make sure you know **which arc each angle intercepts**.

| Mistake | Why it fails | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparing angles that intercept different arcs | The factor of two holds only for the same arc | Make sure the arms of both angles end at the same two points |
| Halving when you need the central angle | The central angle is the one that is twice as large | The central angle is always larger than the inscribed angle |
| Using the minor arc when the vertex sits on the minor arc | A vertex on the minor arc intercepts the major arc | Two inscribed angles on opposite sides add up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> |
| Calling an angle with its vertex inside the circle an inscribed angle | An inscribed angle needs its vertex exactly on the circle | Check whether the vertex lies on the circle |

The minor arc mistake is easy to spot from the answer. A vertex on the minor arc intercepts a major arc whose central angle is more than <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, so the inscribed angle must be more than <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. An acute answer in that situation means you used the wrong arc.

## Practice on Central and Inscribed Angles

For each problem, first find **the vertex and the arc it intercepts**. Problems 3 and 4 also need the angle sum of a triangle, <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.

1. A central angle of a circle measures <InlineMath math="120^\circ" />. What is the inscribed angle that intercepts the same arc?

2. The inscribed angle <InlineMath math="\angle ACB = 45^\circ" />. Find the central angle <InlineMath math="\angle AOB" /> on the same arc.

3. Segment <InlineMath math="AB" /> is a diameter of a circle and point <InlineMath math="C" /> lies on the circle. If <InlineMath math="\angle CAB = 35^\circ" />, find <InlineMath math="\angle ABC" />.

4. Points <InlineMath math="A" />, <InlineMath math="B" />, and <InlineMath math="C" /> lie on a circle with center <InlineMath math="O" />, and <InlineMath math="C" /> is on the major arc <InlineMath math="AB" />. If <InlineMath math="\angle OAB = 25^\circ" />, find <InlineMath math="\angle ACB" />.

### Answers

**Problem 1**. An inscribed angle on the same arc is **half the central angle**, so it measures <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2} \times 120^\circ = 60^\circ" />. Check by doubling: <InlineMath math="2 \times 60^\circ = 120^\circ" />.

**Problem 2**. The central angle and <InlineMath math="\angle ACB" /> both intercept arc <InlineMath math="AB" />, and the central angle is **twice the inscribed angle**:

<BlockMath math="\angle AOB=2\angle ACB=2(45^\circ)=90^\circ" />

**Problem 3**. The angle <InlineMath math="\angle ACB" /> intercepts the diameter <InlineMath math="AB" />, so it measures <InlineMath math="90^\circ" />. The angles of triangle <InlineMath math="ABC" /> add up to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, so:

<BlockMath math="\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ" />

Check the sum: <InlineMath math="35^\circ + 90^\circ + 55^\circ = 180^\circ" />.

**Problem 4**. Triangle <InlineMath math="OAB" /> is isosceles because <InlineMath math="OA" /> and <InlineMath math="OB" /> are both radii, so **its base angles are equal**: <InlineMath math="\angle OAB = \angle OBA = 25^\circ" />. The central angle is what remains of the triangle's angle sum:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\angle AOB = 180^\circ - 25^\circ - 25^\circ = 130^\circ" />
  <BlockMath math="\angle ACB = \frac{1}{2} \times 130^\circ = 65^\circ" />
</MathContainer>

Point <InlineMath math="C" /> lies on the major arc, so <InlineMath math="\angle ACB" /> does intercept the minor arc with central angle <InlineMath math="130^\circ" />. The answer checks out, because <InlineMath math="2 \times 65^\circ = 130^\circ" />.

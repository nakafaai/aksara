## How Chord Length and Centre Distance Relate

Within the **same circle**, chord length and perpendicular distance from the center determine each other. A smaller distance produces a longer chord, and the two quantities convert into each other through the Pythagorean theorem.

<Highlight>Two chords of equal length</Highlight> have the same distance from the center of the circle. Conversely, two chords at the same distance from the center have equal length.

## Chord Length

A chord and its central angle form an isosceles triangle with two radii. The formula below comes from **splitting that triangle in half**. The half-triangle gives half the chord length as <InlineMath math="r\sin(\theta/2)" />.

### Chord Length Formula

<BlockMath math="AB = 2r \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />

Here:

- <InlineMath math="r" /> = radius of the circle
- <InlineMath math="\theta" /> = the **smaller central angle** subtending the chord. Use the calculator mode that matches the angle unit

As the smaller central angle <InlineMath math="\theta" /> increases from <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />, the factor <InlineMath math="\sin(\theta/2)" /> increases from <InlineMath math="0" /> to <InlineMath math="1" />. The chord length therefore increases from <InlineMath math="0" /> to the diameter <InlineMath math="2r" />.

### Distance of Chord from Center

In the right triangle, the center distance is adjacent to half the central angle, while the radius is the hypotenuse. The **cosine of the half-angle** therefore gives:

<BlockMath math="d = r \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />

If the chord length <InlineMath math="l" /> is known, use **half the chord length** as one leg of the right triangle:

<BlockMath math="d^2+\left(\frac{l}{2}\right)^2=r^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d=\sqrt{r^2-\left(\frac{l}{2}\right)^2}" />

## Practice Problems

The first four problems use angles, center distances, or chord segments to find missing measurements. In the last problem, use the **bound on the sine function** to prove the maximum chord length.

1. A circle has a radius of <InlineMath math="10 \text{ cm}" />. If the central angle subtending a chord is <InlineMath math="60^\circ" />, determine the chord length and its distance from the center of the circle.

2. Two chords <InlineMath math="AB" /> and <InlineMath math="CD" /> intersect at point <InlineMath math="P" /> inside a circle. If <InlineMath math="PA = 4 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="PB = 6 \text{ cm}" />, and <InlineMath math="PC = 3 \text{ cm}" />, find the length of <InlineMath math="PD" />.

3. In a circle with radius <InlineMath math="13 \text{ cm}" />, there is a chord of length <InlineMath math="24 \text{ cm}" />. Calculate the distance of this chord from the center of the circle.

4. Two parallel chords in a circle are <InlineMath math="3 \text{ cm}" /> and <InlineMath math="4 \text{ cm}" /> away from the center respectively. If the radius of the circle is <InlineMath math="5 \text{ cm}" />, determine the lengths of both chords.

5. Prove that the longest chord in a circle is the diameter.

### Worked Solutions

**Solution 1**: Calculating **chord length** and its distance from center

Given: <InlineMath math="r = 10 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="\theta = 60^\circ = \frac{\pi}{3}" /> rad

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} l &amp;= 2r \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \\ &amp;= 2 \times 10 \times \sin\left(\frac{\pi/3}{2}\right) \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} l &amp;= 20 \times \sin(30^\circ) \\ &amp;= 20 \times 0.5 \\ &amp;= 10 \text{ cm} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="d = r \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = 10 \times \cos(30^\circ)" />
  <BlockMath math="d = 10 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3} \text{ cm}" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 2**: **Intersecting chords theorem**

Given: <InlineMath math="PA = 4 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="PB = 6 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="PC = 3 \text{ cm}" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="PA \times PB = PC \times PD" />
  <BlockMath math="4 \times 6 = 3 \times PD" />
  <BlockMath math="24 = 3 \times PD" />
  <BlockMath math="PD = 8 \text{ cm}" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 3**: Calculating **distance of chord from center**

Given: <InlineMath math="r = 13 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="l = 24 \text{ cm}" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{r^2 - \left(\frac{l}{2}\right)^2}" />
  <BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{13^2 - 12^2}" />
  <BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{169 - 144}" />
  <BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{25} = 5 \text{ cm}" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 4**: **Parallel chords**

Given: <InlineMath math="r = 5 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="d_1 = 3 \text{ cm}" />, <InlineMath math="d_2 = 4 \text{ cm}" />

For the first chord:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} l_1 &amp;= 2\sqrt{r^2 - d_1^2} \\ &amp;= 2\sqrt{25 - 9} \\ &amp;= 2\sqrt{16} \\ &amp;= 8 \text{ cm} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

For the second chord:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} l_2 &amp;= 2\sqrt{r^2 - d_2^2} \\ &amp;= 2\sqrt{25 - 16} \\ &amp;= 2\sqrt{9} \\ &amp;= 6 \text{ cm} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 5**: Proof that diameter is the longest chord

For any chord, take <InlineMath math="\theta" /> to be the **smaller central angle**, so <InlineMath math="0^\circ\leq\theta\leq180^\circ" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="l = 2r \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)" />
</MathContainer>

The maximum value of <InlineMath math="\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = 1" /> is achieved when <InlineMath math="\frac{\theta}{2} = 90^\circ" />, that is <InlineMath math="\theta = 180^\circ" />.

When <InlineMath math="\theta = 180^\circ" />, the chord **passes through the center** of the circle (diameter) with length:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="l_{\max} = 2r \times 1 = 2r" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, the diameter is the longest chord.

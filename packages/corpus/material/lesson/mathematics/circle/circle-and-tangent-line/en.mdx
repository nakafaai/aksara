## Equation of Circle Tangent Line

When both the radius and tangent have defined slopes, their slopes multiply to <InlineMath math="-1" />. For a vertical line, use the **point form** without dividing coordinates. All formulas below use a radius <InlineMath math="r>0" />.

### Tangent Line Through a Point on the Circle

If point <InlineMath math="(x_1, y_1)" /> **lies on the circle** <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = r^2" />, then the equation of the tangent line at that point is:

<BlockMath math="x_1 \cdot x + y_1 \cdot y = r^2" />

With the center at the origin, radius vector <InlineMath math="(x_1,y_1)" /> is perpendicular to the line direction <InlineMath math="(x-x_1,y-y_1)" />. Their dot product is zero. Also use <InlineMath math="x_1^2+y_1^2=r^2" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}x_1(x-x_1)+y_1(y-y_1)&amp;=0\\x_1x+y_1y&amp;=x_1^2+y_1^2=r^2\end{aligned}" />

The circle in this formula uses the standard form for a circle centered at the origin.

For a circle with center <InlineMath math="(a, b)" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x_1 - a)(x - a) + (y_1 - b)(y - b) \\ &amp;= r^2 \end{aligned}" />

### Tangent Line with a Given Gradient

The equation of the tangent line to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = r^2" /> with gradient <InlineMath math="m" /> is:

Write the line as <InlineMath math="mx-y+c=0" />. It is tangent when the **distance from the center to the line** equals the radius.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\frac{|c|}{\sqrt{m^2+1}}=r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c=\pm r\sqrt{m^2+1}" />

<BlockMath math="y = mx \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />
</MathContainer>

For a circle with center <InlineMath math="(a, b)" />:

<BlockMath math="y - b = m(x - a) \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />

### Length of Tangent Line

If <InlineMath math="P(x_1, y_1)" /> is a point outside the circle with center <InlineMath math="O(a, b)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r" />, then the length of the tangent segment from <InlineMath math="P" /> to the point of tangency is:

In right triangle <InlineMath math="OPT" />, segment <InlineMath math="OP" /> is the **hypotenuse** and <InlineMath math="OT=r" />. The Pythagorean theorem therefore gives:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="PT^2+OT^2=OP^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad PT=\sqrt{OP^2-r^2}" />

<BlockMath math="PT = \sqrt{(x_1 - a)^2 + (y_1 - b)^2 - r^2}" />
</MathContainer>

## Common Tangent Lines of Two Circles

Two circles can share tangent lines that cross between them or pass outside. Whether a common tangent exists at all depends on **how far apart** the two circles are.

Length of external common tangent line:

<BlockMath math="l = \sqrt{d^2 - (r_1 - r_2)^2}" />

In this formula, <InlineMath math="d" /> is the distance between the two circle centers.

This positive-length external tangent segment exists only when <InlineMath math="d>|r_1-r_2|" />. If <InlineMath math="d=|r_1-r_2|>0" />, the circles touch internally and the contact segment has zero length. The case <InlineMath math="d=0" /> with equal radii describes coincident circles.

## Determining Tangent Line Equations

The examples below build tangent equations from different starting information. Work through the substitution in each case. Check at the end of each example that the point of contact really lies on **both the line and the circle**.

### Determining Tangent Line Through a Point on the Circle

Find the equation of the tangent line to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 25" /> at point <InlineMath math="(3, 4)" />.

**Solution**:

Since point <InlineMath math="(3, 4)" /> lies on the circle (can be verified: <InlineMath math="3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25" />), the equation of the tangent line is:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x_1 \cdot x + y_1 \cdot y = r^2" />
  <BlockMath math="3x + 4y = 25" />
</MathContainer>

### Determining Tangent Line with a Given Gradient

Find the equation of the tangent line to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 16" /> that is parallel to line <InlineMath math="3x - 4y + 5 = 0" />.

**Solution**:

The gradient of line <InlineMath math="3x - 4y + 5 = 0" /> is <InlineMath math="m = \frac{3}{4}" />.

Equation of tangent line with gradient <InlineMath math="m = \frac{3}{4}" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="y = mx \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />
  <BlockMath math="y = \frac{3}{4}x \pm 4\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2}" />
  <BlockMath math="y = \frac{3}{4}x \pm 4\sqrt{1 + \frac{9}{16}}" />
  <BlockMath math="y = \frac{3}{4}x \pm 4\sqrt{\frac{25}{16}}" />
  <BlockMath math="y = \frac{3}{4}x \pm 4 \cdot \frac{5}{4}" />
  <BlockMath math="y = \frac{3}{4}x \pm 5" />
</MathContainer>

The equations of the tangent lines are:

- <InlineMath math="y = \frac{3}{4}x + 5" /> or <InlineMath math="3x - 4y + 20 = 0" />
- <InlineMath math="y = \frac{3}{4}x - 5" /> or <InlineMath math="3x - 4y - 20 = 0" />

### Calculating the Length of Tangent Line from External Point

Find the length of the tangent line from point <InlineMath math="P(7, 1)" /> to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 25" />.

**Solution**:

Circle center <InlineMath math="O(0, 0)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r = 5" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="PT = \sqrt{(x_1 - a)^2 + (y_1 - b)^2 - r^2}" />
  <BlockMath math="PT = \sqrt{(7 - 0)^2 + (1 - 0)^2 - 25}" />
  <BlockMath math="PT = \sqrt{49 + 1 - 25}" />
  <BlockMath math="PT = \sqrt{25}" />
  <BlockMath math="PT = 5" />
</MathContainer>

## Practice Problems

Distinguish problems asking for an equation from those asking for a segment length. For equations, use the **given point or slope**. For lengths, identify the right triangle and the relevant center distance.

1. Find the equation of the tangent line to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 36" /> at point <InlineMath math="(-3, 3\sqrt{3})" />!

2. Find the equations of both tangent lines to circle <InlineMath math="(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 25" /> that are perpendicular to line <InlineMath math="2x + y - 5 = 0" />!

3. From point <InlineMath math="A(10, 0)" /> tangent lines are drawn to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 36" />. Find the length of each tangent segment from <InlineMath math="A" /> and the coordinates of both tangent points.

4. Two circles are centered at <InlineMath math="O_1(-4, 0)" /> with radius <InlineMath math="2" /> and <InlineMath math="O_2(4, 0)" /> with radius <InlineMath math="3" />. Find the length of the external common tangent segment!

5. Find the equation of the tangent line to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0" /> that passes through point <InlineMath math="(8, -2)" />!

### Worked Solutions

**Solution 1**: Tangent line equation at a **point on the circle**

Verify point on circle:

<BlockMath math="(-3)^2 + (3\sqrt{3})^2 = 9 + 27 = 36 \space \checkmark" />

Tangent line equation:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="x_1 \cdot x + y_1 \cdot y = r^2" />
<BlockMath math="-3x + 3\sqrt{3}y = 36" />
<BlockMath math="-x + \sqrt{3}y = 12" />
<BlockMath math="x - \sqrt{3}y + 12 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 2**: Tangent line **perpendicular** to a given line

Gradient of line <InlineMath math="2x + y - 5 = 0" /> is <InlineMath math="m_1 = -2" />.

Since **perpendicular**, then <InlineMath math="m_2 = \frac{1}{2}" />.

Tangent line equation:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="y - b = m(x - a) \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />
<BlockMath math="y + 3 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 2) \pm 5\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{4}}" />
<BlockMath math="y + 3 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 2) \pm 5 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}" />
<BlockMath math="y + 3 = \frac{1}{2}x - 1 \pm \frac{5\sqrt{5}}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore: <InlineMath math="x - 2y - 8 \pm 5\sqrt{5} = 0" />

**Solution 3**: Tangent line from external point

Length of tangent line:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} AT &amp;= \sqrt{10^2 + 0^2 - 36} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{100 - 36} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{64} \\ &amp;= 8 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

To find the tangent points, let <InlineMath math="T(x,y)" /> be one of them. Since radius <InlineMath math="OT" /> is **perpendicular** to tangent <InlineMath math="AT" />, their direction vectors have **zero dot product**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(A-T)\cdot T=0" />
  <BlockMath math="(10-x)x-y^2=0" />
  <BlockMath math="10x-(x^2+y^2)=0" />
  <BlockMath math="10x-36=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac{18}{5}" />
  <BlockMath math="y=\pm\sqrt{36-\left(\frac{18}{5}\right)^2}=\pm\frac{24}{5}" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, the tangent points are <InlineMath math="T_1\left(\frac{18}{5},\frac{24}{5}\right)" /> and <InlineMath math="T_2\left(\frac{18}{5},-\frac{24}{5}\right)" />.

**Solution 4**: **External common tangent** line

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{(4-(-4))^2 + (0-0)^2} = 8" />
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} l &amp;= \sqrt{d^2 - (r_2 - r_1)^2} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{64 - 1} \\ &amp;= \sqrt{63} \\ &amp;= 3\sqrt{7} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

**Solution 5**: Tangent line through a **point on the circle**

**Complete the squares** to read the center and radius:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}x^2-6x+y^2+4y&amp;=12\\(x-3)^2-9+(y+2)^2-4&amp;=12\\(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2&amp;=25\end{aligned}" />

Center <InlineMath math="(3, -2)" />, radius <InlineMath math="r = 5" />

Verify that point <InlineMath math="(8, -2)" /> lies on the circle:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} (8-3)^2 + (-2+2)^2 &amp;= 25 + 0 \\ &amp;= 25 \end{aligned}" />

The left side equals <InlineMath math="r^2=25" />, so the point lies on the circle. Therefore the tangent line equation is:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(8-3)(x-3) \\ &amp;\quad{}+ (-2+2)(y+2) = 25 \end{aligned}" />
<BlockMath math="5(x-3) + 0 = 25" />
<BlockMath math="x - 3 = 5" />
<BlockMath math="x = 8" />
</MathContainer>

## Quotients of Polynomials

A rational function is a quotient of two polynomials. Its general form is

<BlockMath math="f(x)=\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}" />

In this form, <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> and <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> are polynomials, and <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> is not the zero polynomial.

The denominator adds one essential rule: every input that makes <InlineMath math="Q(x)=0" /> must be excluded. <Highlight>That restriction belongs to the function</Highlight> even when a common factor later cancels.

## A Rational Model from Area

Suppose Mr. Budi is planning a rectangular chicken coop with area <InlineMath math="100\text{ m}^2" />. Let its length be <InlineMath math="x\text{ m}" /> and its width be <InlineMath math="w\text{ m}" />. Then

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="xw=100" />

<BlockMath math="w(x)=\frac{100}{x}" />
</MathContainer>

The formula is rational because its numerator and denominator are polynomials. In the physical situation, both dimensions must be positive, so the meaningful domain is <InlineMath math="x>0" />. The condition <InlineMath math="x\neq 0" /> still includes negative lengths that do not fit the situation.

The model also shows how the two dimensions depend on each other: making the coop longer forces its width to become smaller if the area must stay fixed.

The dimensions are **inversely proportional**. A five-metre length requires a twenty-metre width, while a ten-metre length requires a ten-metre width.

## Common Forms

These examples have denominators of different degrees. The **denominator factors** determine excluded inputs. Check which factors cancel before deciding whether an exclusion creates a hole or a vertical asymptote.

| Form | Denominator restriction |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="\frac{k}{x}" /> | <InlineMath math="x\neq0" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}" /> | <InlineMath math="cx+d\neq0" /> |
| <InlineMath math="\frac{x^2+2x+1}{x-3}" /> | <InlineMath math="x\neq3" /> |

An example of reciprocal form is <InlineMath math="5/x" />. For a linear-over-linear example, <InlineMath math="(2x+3)/(x-1)" /> is undefined at <InlineMath math="x=1" />.

## Finding the Domain

To find the real domain of a rational function:

1. Set the **original denominator** equal to zero.
2. Solve for every value that makes it zero.
3. Exclude those values from the real numbers.

The three steps apply to a rational function whose denominator can be zero:

<BlockMath math="f(x)=\frac{x+2}{x-3}," />

Its denominator is zero when:

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="x-3=0" />

<BlockMath math="x=3." />
</MathContainer>

The calculation gives:

<BlockMath math="D_f=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid x\neq 3\}." />

## Simplifying Without Losing the Domain

You may cancel a **common factor** only where that factor is nonzero. The simplified expression gives the same output on the original domain, but it does not restore an excluded input.

### Removing a Common Power of the Variable

Simplify

<BlockMath math="f(x)=\frac{6x^2}{3x}." />

The original **denominator** requires <InlineMath math="x\neq 0" />. On that domain,

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="\frac{6x^2}{3x}=\frac{3x\cdot 2x}{3x}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{6x^2}{3x}=2x,\qquad x\neq 0." />
</MathContainer>

Although the expression <InlineMath math="2x" /> is defined at <InlineMath math="x=0" />, the original rational function is not.

### Canceling After Factoring

Simplify

<BlockMath math="g(x)=\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}." />

First record <InlineMath math="x\neq 2" />, then factor the **difference of squares**:

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}=x+2,\qquad x\neq 2." />
</MathContainer>

The graph agrees with the line <InlineMath math="y=x+2" /> except for a hole at <InlineMath math="x=2" />.

<InlineMath math="g(x) = \frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2}" /> has the graph of <InlineMath math="y = x + 2" /> with one point missing. At <InlineMath math="x = 2" /> the original fraction is undefined, so the line has a hole at <InlineMath math="(2, 4)" /> even though <InlineMath math="x + 2" /> would give 4 there.

## Operations on Rational Expressions

Adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing rational expressions follows the rules for fractions. The extra step is the domain, because a factor that cancels from the numerator and the denominator still removes one value from the allowed inputs.

Record the **nonzero denominator conditions** before simplifying. For example, multiplying these expressions cancels factors without allowing inputs zero or negative one.

<BlockMath math="\frac{x}{x+1}\cdot\frac{x+1}{x}=1,\qquad x\neq-1,0" />

### Addition and Subtraction

Use a **common denominator** and keep every restriction from the original denominators.

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="\frac{2}{x}+\frac{3}{x+1}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{2}{x}+\frac{3}{x+1}=\frac{2(x+1)+3x}{x(x+1)}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{2}{x}+\frac{3}{x+1}=\frac{5x+2}{x(x+1)},\qquad x\neq 0,-1." />
</MathContainer>

### Multiplication

Multiply numerators and denominators, then factor before canceling:

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="\frac{x+1}{x}\cdot\frac{2x}{x-1}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{x+1}{x}\cdot\frac{2x}{x-1}=\frac{2x(x+1)}{x(x-1)}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{x+1}{x}\cdot\frac{2x}{x-1}=\frac{2(x+1)}{x-1},\qquad x\neq 0,1." />
</MathContainer>

The canceled factor <InlineMath math="x" /> does not remove the **original restriction** <InlineMath math="x\neq 0" />.

### Division

Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal:

<BlockMath math="\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}=\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{d}{c}=\frac{ad}{bc}." />

This requires <InlineMath math="b\neq 0" /> and <InlineMath math="d\neq 0" /> so both original fractions exist, and <InlineMath math="c\neq 0" /> so the divisor is not zero.

The **divisor must also be nonzero**. In this example, both fractions exclude input zero, and the divisor adds the exclusion of input one.

<BlockMath math="\frac{x+1}{x}\div\frac{x-1}{x}=\frac{x+1}{x}\cdot\frac{x}{x-1}=\frac{x+1}{x-1},\quad x\neq0,1" />

## Exercises

Each problem gives a rational expression and asks for its **domain** or its simplified form. Find the excluded values first, then simplify.

1. Determine the domain of <InlineMath math="f(x)=\frac{x+3}{x^2-9}" />.

2. Simplify <InlineMath math="g(x)=\frac{x^2-1}{x+1}" /> and retain its original restriction.

3. Calculate <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{2}{x+1}" /> and state its domain restrictions.

4. A car travels <InlineMath math="300\text{ km}" /> at an average speed of <InlineMath math="v\text{ km/h}" />. Write travel time as a function of <InlineMath math="v" /> and state the physically meaningful domain.

### Worked Solutions

**Problem 1**. The **numerator** does not determine the domain. Factor the denominator:

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x-3)(x+3) = 0 \\ &amp;\Longrightarrow x = 3 \text{ or } x = -3. \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Both values make the original denominator zero, so

<BlockMath math="D_f=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid x\neq -3,\ x\neq 3\}." />

**Problem 2**. The original denominator gives <InlineMath math="x\neq -1" />. Factor and cancel only on that domain:

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="\frac{x^2-1}{x+1}=\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x+1}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{x^2-1}{x+1}=x-1,\qquad x\neq -1." />
</MathContainer>

The simplified formula still has a hole at the excluded **input** <InlineMath math="x=-1" />.

**Problem 3**. The original denominators require <InlineMath math="x\neq 1" /> and <InlineMath math="x\neq -1" />. Using <InlineMath math="(x-1)(x+1)" /> as the common **denominator** gives

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{2}{x+1}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{2}{x+1}=\frac{x+1-2(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+1)}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{2}{x+1}=\frac{x+1-2x+2}{(x-1)(x+1)}" />

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{2}{x+1}=\frac{3-x}{(x-1)(x+1)},\qquad x\neq -1,1." />
</MathContainer>

**Problem 4**. Time equals distance divided by speed:

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="\text{time}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}" />

<BlockMath math="t(v)=\frac{300}{v}." />
</MathContainer>

Algebraically, <InlineMath math="v\neq 0" />. For a moving car, **speed must be positive**, so the physical model uses <InlineMath math="v>0" />.

Travel times for 300 km from <InlineMath math="t(v) = \frac{300}{v}" />: 10 hours at 30 km/h, 6 at 50, 5 at 60, 4 at 75 and 3 at 100. Doubling the speed from 30 to 60 km/h halves the time.

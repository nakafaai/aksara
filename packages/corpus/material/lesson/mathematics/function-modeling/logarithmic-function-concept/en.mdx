## Finding the Exponent That Produces a Value

A logarithm answers a specific question: <Highlight>which exponent produces a given value</Highlight>? In that sense, logarithmic and [exponential functions](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-modeling/exponential-function) undo each other.

Start with this exponential equation:

<BlockMath math="2^3 = 8" />

The exponent that turns <InlineMath math="2" /> into <InlineMath math="8" /> is <InlineMath math="3" />. Logarithmic notation records the same relationship from the opposite direction:

<BlockMath math="\log_2 8 = 3" />

In general, exponential and logarithmic notation are related by:

<BlockMath math="y = b^x \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad x = \log_b y" />

## Definition and Types of Logarithms

A logarithmic function with base <InlineMath math="b" /> (where <InlineMath math="b > 0" /> and <InlineMath math="b \neq 1" />) is expressed as:

<BlockMath math="f(x) = \log_b x \quad \text{for every } x > 0" />

| Name | Base | Notation and example | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Common logarithm** | <InlineMath math="10" /> | <InlineMath math="\log100=2" /> | <InlineMath math="10^2=100" /> |
| **Natural logarithm** | <InlineMath math="e\approx2.7183" /> | <InlineMath math="\ln e=1" /> | <InlineMath math="e^1=e" /> |
| **Binary logarithm** | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="\log_2 8=3" /> | <InlineMath math="2^3=8" /> |

## Properties of Logarithms

Each property follows from the definition of the logarithm as the **inverse of the exponential function**, because undoing the exponential function must undo its rules too. The basic properties concern a single value, and the operational properties concern a product, a quotient, or a power.

### Reading a Single Logarithm

The first two identities read **exponents zero and one**. The next two undo logarithms and exponentials with the same base.

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="\log_b 1 = 0" />

<BlockMath math="\log_b b = 1" />

<BlockMath math="\log_b b^n = n" />

<BlockMath math="b^{\log_b x} = x" />
</MathContainer>

### Operational Properties

The rules below require **positive arguments**: <InlineMath math="x,y>0" />. Both bases satisfy <InlineMath math="a,b>0" /> and <InlineMath math="a,b\neq1" />, and <InlineMath math="n" /> can be any real number.

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="\log_b (xy) = \log_b x + \log_b y" />

<BlockMath math="\log_b \left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log_b x - \log_b y" />

<BlockMath math="\log_b x^n = n \cdot \log_b x" />

<BlockMath math="\log_b x = \frac{\log_a x}{\log_a b} \quad \text{(change of base)}" />
</MathContainer>

For example, **multiplication inside a logarithm** becomes addition of two logarithmic values.

<BlockMath math="\log_2(8\cdot4)=\log_2 8+\log_2 4=3+2=5" />

## Exponential Model from Two Data Points

[Indonesia's Ministry of Health](https://kemkes.go.id/id/positif-covid-19-usai-kontak-wn-jepang), or Kemenkes, reported two confirmed COVID-19 cases on 2 March 2020.

On 30 April 2020, [the ministry reported](https://kemkes.go.id/id/pasien-positif-covid-19-capai-10-ribu) 10,118 cumulative confirmed cases.

The dates are <InlineMath math="59 \text{ elapsed days}" /> apart.

To practise logarithms, we can fit a simple exponential curve through those two reported values. Let <InlineMath math="t" /> be the number of elapsed days after 2 March and <InlineMath math="P(t)" /> the fitted case count. Since <InlineMath math="P(0)=2" /> and <InlineMath math="P(59)=10{,}118" />, the model is:

To find the **rate fitted to the two data points**, start with the following model. The initial report determines the multiplier, and the day-59 report determines the rate.

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="P(t)=Ce^{kt},\quad P(0)=C=2" />

<BlockMath math="10118=2e^{59k}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad5059=e^{59k}" />

<BlockMath math="\ln5059=59k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac{\ln5059}{59}" />
</MathContainer>

If the same fitted rate were extended, logarithms would place the <InlineMath math="50{,}000" /> threshold at:

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="50000 = 2e^{\frac{1}{59}\ln(5059)t}" />

<BlockMath math="25000=e^{\frac{\ln5059}{59}t}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ln25000=\frac{\ln5059}{59}t" />

<BlockMath math="t = \frac{59 \cdot \ln(25000)}{\ln(5059)} \approx 70.1 \text{ days}" />
</MathContainer>

This calculation uses logarithms to solve the exponential model. Real confirmed case counts also depend on testing, reporting, behaviour, and public-health measures. A fixed exponential rate is useful only while its assumptions still fit the situation.

## Exercises

These four problems move from reading a single logarithm to solving an **exponential model**. Work each one before the worked solutions, then check your answers against the steps that follow.

1. Determine the value of <InlineMath math="\log_3 27" />, <InlineMath math="\log_5 \frac{1}{125}" />, and <InlineMath math="\ln e^3" />.

2. If <InlineMath math="\log_2 x = 4" />, determine the value of <InlineMath math="x" />.

3. Simplify: <InlineMath math="\log_2 8 + \log_2 4 - \log_2 2" />

4. An investment grows according to the formula <InlineMath math="A = 1000 \cdot 2^t" /> (in million rupiah). How many years are needed for the investment to become <InlineMath math="8 \text{ billion rupiah}" />?

### Reading exponents

Read each logarithm as an **exponent question**. The required powers are:

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="3^3=27\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\log_3 27=3" />

<BlockMath math="5^{-3}=\frac{1}{125}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\log_5\frac{1}{125}=-3" />

<BlockMath math="e^3=e^3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ln e^3=3" />
</MathContainer>

### Rewriting logarithmic form

Rewrite the logarithmic equation in **exponential form**:

<BlockMath math="\log_2 x=4\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=2^4=16" />

Substituting <InlineMath math="x=16" /> gives <InlineMath math="\log_2 16=4" />, so the result checks out.

### Combining logarithmic values

Evaluate the **three powers** of <InlineMath math="2" /> before combining them:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\log_2 8+\log_2 4-\log_2 2 \\ &amp;=3+2-1 \\ &amp;=4 \end{aligned}" />

### Converting investment units

The formula measures <InlineMath math="A" /> in **millions of rupiah**, so <InlineMath math="8 \text{ billion rupiah}=8000 \text{ million rupiah}" />. Substitute the target and isolate the exponential term:

<MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="8000=1000\cdot2^t" />

<BlockMath math="8=2^t" />

<BlockMath math="2^3=2^t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=3" />
</MathContainer>

The investment reaches the target after <InlineMath math="3 \text{ years}" />.

## Finding the Remainder by Evaluating the Polynomial

The **Remainder Theorem** often finds the remainder of a polynomial division without completing [long division](/en/subjects/mathematics/polynomial/division-polynomial) or [a Horner scheme](/en/subjects/mathematics/polynomial/horner-method). It connects the remainder directly to one value of the polynomial.

The theorem applies to a **linear divisor**. Substitute the input that makes that divisor zero.

### Statement of the Remainder Theorem

If a polynomial <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="(x - c)" />, then the remainder is <InlineMath math="S = P(c)" />.

To find the **remainder** when <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="(x-c)" />, evaluate <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> at <InlineMath math="x=c" />.

For <InlineMath math="P(x) = 2x^2 - 3x + 4" /> divided by <InlineMath math="x - 2" />, the remainder is <InlineMath math="P(2) = 6" />, so you can answer the question with one substitution.

<BlockMath math="P(2)=2(2)^2-3(2)+4=8-6+4=6" />

The theorem says the remainder is simply a height on the graph. For <InlineMath math="P(x) = 2x^2 - 3x + 4" /> divided by <InlineMath math="x - 2" />, go to <InlineMath math="x = 2" /> on the curve: the height there is <InlineMath math="P(2) = 6" />, which is the remainder.

### Why the Remainder Theorem Holds

This theorem stems directly from the **polynomial division algorithm** we already know:

<BlockMath math="P(x) = (x - c) \cdot H(x) + S" />

Where:

- <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is the dividend polynomial.
- <InlineMath math="(x - c)" /> is the divisor polynomial (degree <InlineMath math="1" />).
- <InlineMath math="H(x)" /> is the quotient.
- <InlineMath math="S" /> is the remainder (a constant, since the divisor is degree <InlineMath math="1" />).

Now, substitute <InlineMath math="x = c" /> into the division algorithm equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="P(c) = (c - c) \cdot H(c) + S" />
  <BlockMath math="P(c) = (0) \cdot H(c) + S" />
  <BlockMath math="P(c) = 0 + S" />
  <BlockMath math="P(c) = S" />
</MathContainer>

This proves that the value of the polynomial <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> at <InlineMath math="x = c" /> <Highlight>is equal to the remainder</Highlight> <InlineMath math="S" /> when <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="(x - c)" />.

For the earlier quadratic, the product of the divisor and quotient **vanishes when the input is two**. The remainder stays six throughout the division identity:

<BlockMath math="2x^2-3x+4=(x-2)(2x+1)+6" />

| Input | Polynomial value | Divisor times quotient | Remainder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="-3" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> |
| <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="13" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="6" /> |

For a divisor of degree two, the remainder may be linear. One polynomial value is therefore insufficient to determine the entire remainder in that case.

Split <InlineMath math="P" /> into its two parts. The violet curve <InlineMath math="(x - 2)(2x + 1)" /> is divisible by <InlineMath math="x - 2" /> and passes through <InlineMath math="(2, 0)" />. Adding the remainder lifts it by <InlineMath math="6" /> everywhere and gives the orange <InlineMath math="P(x)" />. That is why only the remainder is left at <InlineMath math="x = 2" />.

## Calculating with the Remainder Theorem

The theorem **determines the remainder directly** and also explains why polynomial evaluation appears in division problems. Substituting the value that makes the divisor zero turns the division into a single evaluation, which is usually the faster route.

Find the remainder if <InlineMath math="P(x) = 2x^5 + 5x^4 - 10x^3 + 9x^2 - 10" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="x + 4" />.

### Using the Horner Method

The divisor is <InlineMath math="x + 4" />, or <InlineMath math="x - (-4)" />, so <InlineMath math="c = -4" />.

Coefficients of <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> (**use zero for the missing term**): <InlineMath math="2, 5, -10, 9, 0, -10" />.

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
-4 & 2 & 5 & -10 & 9 & 0 & -10 \\
  &   & -8 & 12 & -8 & -4 & 16 \\
\hline
  & 2 & -3 & 2 & 1 & -4 & \boxed{6} \\
\end{array}
"
/>

From Horner's method, we get:

- Quotient: <InlineMath math="H(x) = 2x^4 - 3x^3 + 2x^2 + x - 4" />
- Remainder: <InlineMath math="S = \boxed{6}" />

Each number in the bottom row comes from the one before it: multiply by <InlineMath math="c = -4" /> and add the next coefficient. The first five bars are the quotient coefficients <InlineMath math="2, -3, 2, 1, -4" />, and the last bar is the remainder <InlineMath math="6" />.

### Using the Remainder Theorem

According to the **Remainder Theorem**, the remainder when <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="x - (-4)" /> is <InlineMath math="P(-4)" />.

Calculate <InlineMath math="P(-4)" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(-4) &amp;= 2(-4)^5 + 5(-4)^4 \\ &amp;\quad{}- 10(-4)^3 + 9(-4)^2 - 10 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(-4) &amp;= 2(-1024) + 5(256) \\ &amp;\quad{}- 10(-64) + 9(16) - 10 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(-4) &amp;= -2048 + 1280 \\ &amp;\quad{}+ 640 + 144 - 10 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="P(-4) = -2048 + 1920 + 144 - 10" />
  <BlockMath math="P(-4) = -128 + 144 - 10" />
  <BlockMath math="P(-4) = 16 - 10" />
  <BlockMath math="P(-4) = 6" />
</MathContainer>

Both methods give the remainder <InlineMath math="6" />. The Remainder Theorem reaches it without completing the full division scheme.

Evaluating <InlineMath math="P(c)" /> therefore gives exactly the remainder after division by <InlineMath math="(x-c)" />.

## Exercise

If <InlineMath math="P(x) = 3x^5 - 20x^4 - 6x^3 - 48x - 8" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="x - 7" />, determine the **remainder using the Remainder Theorem**. The divisor <InlineMath math="x - 7" /> corresponds to the value <InlineMath math="x = 7" />, so one substitution is enough. Evaluate the polynomial at that value and report the resulting number as the remainder.

### Worked Solution

According to the **Remainder Theorem**, the remainder when <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="x - 7" /> is <InlineMath math="P(7)" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(7) &amp;= 3(7)^5 - 20(7)^4 \\ &amp;\quad{}- 6(7)^3 - 48(7) - 8 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(7) &amp;= 3(16807) - 20(2401) \\ &amp;\quad{}- 6(343) - 336 - 8 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} P(7) &amp;= 50421 - 48020 - 2058 \\ &amp;\quad{}- 336 - 8 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="P(7) = 2401 - 2058 - 336 - 8" />
  <BlockMath math="P(7) = 343 - 336 - 8" />
  <BlockMath math="P(7) = 7 - 8" />
  <BlockMath math="P(7) = -1" />
</MathContainer>

So, the remainder is <InlineMath math="-1" />.

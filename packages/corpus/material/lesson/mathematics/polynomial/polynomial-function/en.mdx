## Polynomial Functions

A polynomial function maps each allowed input to the value of a polynomial expression.

## General Form of a Polynomial Function

A polynomial function in the variable <InlineMath math="x" /> is generally written in the form:

<BlockMath math="P(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + a_{n-2} x^{n-2} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0" />

The general form has the following components:

- **<InlineMath math="P(x)" />**:

  Function notation, read "P of x", indicating the function's value depends on the value of <InlineMath math="x" />.

- **<InlineMath math="x" />**:

  The variable of the polynomial function.

- **<InlineMath math="n" />**:

  The highest power of the variable <InlineMath math="x" />. This value <InlineMath math="n" /> must be a **non-negative integer** (<InlineMath math="0, 1, 2, 3, \dots" />). This non-negative integer <InlineMath math="n" /> also determines the **degree** of the polynomial function.

- **<InlineMath math="a_n, a_{n-1}, \dots, a_1, a_0" />**:

  The coefficients of the polynomial function. A **real polynomial function** has real-number coefficients. Polynomials can also be defined over other number systems, such as the complex numbers.

- **<InlineMath math="a_n x^n" />**:

  The term with the highest power. This term is called the **leading term**.

- **<InlineMath math="a_n" />**:

  The **leading coefficient** <InlineMath math="a_n" /> must be **nonzero**, so <InlineMath math="a_n\neq0" />, for the function to have degree <InlineMath math="n" />.

- **<InlineMath math="a_0" />**:

  The term without the variable <InlineMath math="x" /> (or can be considered <InlineMath math="a_0 x^0" />). This term is called the **constant term** or **constant**.

## Example of a Polynomial Function

Suppose we have the function: <InlineMath math="f(x) = 5x^3 - 2x^2 + 7x - 1" />

- This is a polynomial function in the variable <InlineMath math="x" />.
- Its degree is <InlineMath math="3" /> (the highest power of <InlineMath math="x" />).
- Its leading term is <InlineMath math="5x^3" />.
- Its leading coefficient is <InlineMath math="5" /> (<InlineMath math="a_3 = 5" />).
- Other coefficients are <InlineMath math="a_2 = -2" />, <InlineMath math="a_1 = 7" />.
- Its constant term is <InlineMath math="-1" /> (<InlineMath math="a_0 = -1" />).

A polynomial function has nonnegative integer exponents and a nonzero leading coefficient.

## Exercise

Consider the polynomial function:

<BlockMath math="p(t)=-2t^4+3t^2-t+8" />

Identify its degree, leading term, leading coefficient, coefficient of <InlineMath math="t^3" />, and constant term. Then calculate <InlineMath math="p(2)" />.

### Worked Solution

Write the missing power explicitly:

<BlockMath math="p(t)=-2t^4+0t^3+3t^2-t+8" />

- Degree: <InlineMath math="4" />
- Leading term: <InlineMath math="-2t^4" />
- Leading coefficient: <InlineMath math="-2" />
- Coefficient of <InlineMath math="t^3" />: <InlineMath math="0" />
- Constant term: <InlineMath math="8" />

Evaluate the function by substituting <InlineMath math="t=2" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="p(2)=-2(2)^4+3(2)^2-2+8" />
  <BlockMath math="=-32+12-2+8" />
  <BlockMath math="=-14" />
</MathContainer>

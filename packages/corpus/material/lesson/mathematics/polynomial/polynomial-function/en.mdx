Real coefficients and nonnegative integer exponents make a polynomial defined for **every real input**. There is no variable denominator or variable radical that requires excluding an input.

## General Form of a Polynomial Function

For a nonzero polynomial, the general form organizes **powers and coefficients**. The leading coefficient is nonzero, and the largest exponent determines the degree:

<BlockMath math="P(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_kx^k,\qquad a_n\ne0,\quad n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}" />

| Notation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> | Function value at input x. |
| <InlineMath math="x" /> | The input variable. |
| <InlineMath math="n" /> | Degree, a nonnegative integer. |
| <InlineMath math="a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_n" /> | Real coefficients for each power. |
| <InlineMath math="a_nx^n" /> | The leading term, with the highest power. |
| <InlineMath math="a_n" /> | The leading coefficient, which must be nonzero. |
| <InlineMath math="a_0" /> | The constant, equal to the value at input zero. |

The function <InlineMath math="P(x)=0" /> is also polynomial. However, the **zero polynomial has no nonzero leading term**, so the displayed nonzero form does not assign its degree.

## Example of a Polynomial Function

Suppose we have the function: <InlineMath math="f(x) = 5x^3 - 2x^2 + 7x - 1" />

- This is a polynomial function in the variable <InlineMath math="x" />.
- Its degree is <InlineMath math="3" /> (the highest power of <InlineMath math="x" />).
- Its leading term is <InlineMath math="5x^3" />.
- Its leading coefficient is <InlineMath math="5" /> (<InlineMath math="a_3 = 5" />).
- Other coefficients are <InlineMath math="a_2 = -2" />, <InlineMath math="a_1 = 7" />.
- Its constant term is <InlineMath math="-1" /> (<InlineMath math="a_0 = -1" />).

A nonzero polynomial has a <Highlight>nonzero leading coefficient</Highlight>. Other coefficients may be zero, as the cubic coefficient is in the exercise below.

## Exercise

<BlockMath math="p(t)=-2t^4+3t^2-t+8" />

Identify its degree, leading term, **leading coefficient**, coefficient of <InlineMath math="t^3" />, and constant term. Then calculate <InlineMath math="p(2)" />.

### Worked Solution

Write the **missing power** explicitly:

<BlockMath math="p(t)=-2t^4+0t^3+3t^2-t+8" />

- Degree: <InlineMath math="4" />
- Leading term: <InlineMath math="-2t^4" />
- Leading coefficient: <InlineMath math="-2" />
- Coefficient of <InlineMath math="t^3" />: <InlineMath math="0" />
- Constant term: <InlineMath math="8" />

Evaluate the function by substituting <InlineMath math="t=2" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}p(2)&amp;=-2(2)^4+3(2)^2-2+8\\&amp;=-32+12-2+8\\&amp;=-14\end{aligned}" />

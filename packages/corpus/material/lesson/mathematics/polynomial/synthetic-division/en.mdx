## Extending Integer Long Division to Polynomials

Polynomial long division <Highlight>finds the quotient and remainder</Highlight> by repeating the same divide, multiply, subtract, and bring-down pattern used in integer long division. Column alignment is what makes the pattern work, because only like terms may be subtracted from each other.

## Preparing for Long Division

Before starting the division, there are a few things to prepare:

1.  **Order Terms**:

    Write the dividend (<InlineMath math="P(x)" />) and the divisor (<InlineMath math="Q(x)" />) in descending order of variable powers (from highest power to lowest).

2.  **Complete Terms**:

    If a power is missing, <Highlight>insert its zero coefficient</Highlight> as a _placeholder_. This keeps like powers in the same column during subtraction.

    **Example**:

    If <InlineMath math="P(x) = 4x^4 + 17x^3 - 3x + 1" />, the <InlineMath math="x^2" /> term is missing.

    So we write it as <InlineMath math="P(x) = 4x^4 + 17x^3 + 0x^2 - 3x + 1" />.

3.  **Set Up Division**:

    Write the division in long division format, with <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> (the completed form) inside the division symbol and <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> outside.

Before dividing, the dividend needs a place for every power. <InlineMath math="4x^4 + 17x^3 - 3x + 1" /> has no <InlineMath math="x^2" /> term, so it is written <InlineMath math="4x^4 + 17x^3 + 0x^2 - 3x + 1" />. The chart shows the coefficient in each place, including the zero that keeps the columns lined up.

## Steps for Long Division

The divisor must be a **nonzero polynomial**. In each round, multiplication and subtraction eliminate the leading term of the current remainder. Stop when the remainder is zero or has lower degree than the divisor.

### Long Division Example

Divide <InlineMath math="P(x) = 4x^4 + 17x^3 - 3x + 1" /> by <InlineMath math="Q(x) = x^2 + 4x - 1" />.

**Step 1**. Preparation

<InlineMath math="P(x) = 4x^4 + 17x^3 + 0x^2 - 3x + 1" /> (complete the <InlineMath math="x^2" /> term)
<InlineMath math="Q(x) = x^2 + 4x - 1" />

**Step 2**. Division Process

The quotient is <InlineMath math="4x^2+x" /> and the remainder is <InlineMath math="-2x+1" />. The next steps show each multiplication and subtraction, including the zero coefficient of <InlineMath math="x^2" />.

**Step 3**. Step-by-Step Explanation

**Iteration <InlineMath math="1" />**:

**Divide**: Divide the first term <InlineMath math="4x^4" /> by the first term of the divisor <InlineMath math="x^2" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{4x^4}{x^2} = 4x^2" />

Write <InlineMath math="4x^2" /> as the first term of the quotient.

**Multiply**: Multiply <InlineMath math="4x^2" /> by the divisor <InlineMath math="x^2 + 4x - 1" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;4x^2(x^2 + 4x - 1) \\ &amp;= 4x^4 + 16x^3 - 4x^2 \end{aligned}" />

**Subtract**: Subtract the result from the initial polynomial:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(4x^4 + 17x^3 + 0x^2) \\ &amp;\quad{}- (4x^4 + 16x^3 - 4x^2)  \\ &amp;= x^3 + 4x^2 \end{aligned}" />

**Bring Down**: Bring down the next term (<InlineMath math="-3x" />) to get the new polynomial:

<InlineMath math="x^3 + 4x^2 - 3x" />

**Iteration <InlineMath math="2" />**:

**Divide**: Divide the first term of the new polynomial <InlineMath math="x^3" /> by the first term of the divisor <InlineMath math="x^2" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{x^3}{x^2} = x" />

Write <InlineMath math="+x" /> as the next term of the quotient.

**Multiply**: Multiply <InlineMath math="x" /> by the divisor <InlineMath math="x^2 + 4x - 1" />:

<BlockMath math="x(x^2 + 4x - 1) = x^3 + 4x^2 - x" />

**Subtract**: Subtract the result from the current polynomial:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x^3 + 4x^2 - 3x) \\ &amp;\quad{}- (x^3 + 4x^2 - x) = -2x \end{aligned}" />

**Bring Down**: Bring down the next term (<InlineMath math="+1" />) to get the temporary remainder:

<BlockMath math="-2x + 1" />

**Stop**: The degree of the remainder (<InlineMath math="-2x+1" />, degree <InlineMath math="1" />) is less than the degree of the divisor (<InlineMath math="x^2+4x-1" />, degree <InlineMath math="2" />), so the division stops.

**Step 4**. Result

The Quotient (<InlineMath math="H(x)" />) is <InlineMath math="4x^2 + x" />.
The Remainder (<InlineMath math="S(x)" />) is <InlineMath math="-2x + 1" />.

**Step 5**. Writing in Division Algorithm Form

Based on the division algorithm, we can write the result as:

Fraction Form:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\frac{4x^4 + 17x^3 - 3x + 1}{x^2 + 4x - 1} \\ &amp;= 4x^2 + x + \frac{-2x + 1}{x^2 + 4x - 1} \end{aligned}" />

Multiplication Form:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;4x^4 + 17x^3 - 3x + 1 \\ &amp;= (x^2 + 4x - 1)(4x^2 + x) \\ &amp;\quad{}+ (-2x + 1) \end{aligned}" />

Long division records every quotient, multiplication, and subtraction step. That explicit layout works for divisors of any positive degree and makes the reconstruction <InlineMath math="P(x)=Q(x)H(x)+S(x)" /> easy to check.

The fractional form is valid only for inputs with <InlineMath math="Q(x)\ne0" />. The multiplication identity remains valid for every input.

Each step of the long division lowers the degree of what is left. The dividend <InlineMath math="4x^4 + 17x^3 - 3x + 1" /> has degree <InlineMath math="4" />. After subtracting <InlineMath math="4x^2(x^2 + 4x - 1)" /> the rest has degree <InlineMath math="3" />, and after subtracting <InlineMath math="x(x^2 + 4x - 1)" /> only <InlineMath math="-2x + 1" /> remains, degree <InlineMath math="1" />. That is below the divisor's degree <InlineMath math="2" />, so the division stops.

## Exercise

Find the **quotient polynomial** <InlineMath math="H(x)" /> and the remainder polynomial <InlineMath math="S(x)" /> after dividing <InlineMath math="P(x) = x^3 - x + 9" /> by <InlineMath math="Q(x) = x^2 - 2x + 3" />. Confirm the result by multiplying the divisor with the quotient and adding the remainder.

State the result in the form <InlineMath math="P(x) = Q(x) \cdot H(x) + S(x)" />.

### Worked Solution

**Insert the zero coefficient** in <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> to become <InlineMath math="x^3 + 0x^2 - x + 9" />.

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{l}
\qquad\qquad\qquad x + 2 \\
x^2-2x+3\overline{\big)x^3 + 0x^2 - x + 9} \\
\qquad\qquad\underline{-(x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x)} \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad 2x^2 - 4x + 9 \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\underline{-(2x^2 - 4x + 6)} \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad 3 \\
\end{array}
"
/>

First divide the leading terms to obtain <InlineMath math="x^3 \div x^2 = x" />. After multiplying and subtracting, the new leading term is <InlineMath math="2x^2" />, so the next quotient term is <InlineMath math="2x^2 \div x^2 = 2" />. The final remainder <InlineMath math="3" /> has lower degree than the divisor, so the division stops.

- Quotient: <InlineMath math="H(x) = x + 2" />
- Remainder: <InlineMath math="S(x) = 3" />
- Division Algorithm Form:

  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x^3 - x + 9 \\ &amp;= (x^2 - 2x + 3)(x + 2) + 3 \end{aligned}" />

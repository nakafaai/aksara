## Combining Like Terms

Polynomial addition and subtraction combine only **like terms**, just as they do in other algebraic expressions. Two terms are like terms when the same variable carries the same exponent, and combining them changes only the coefficient.

### Recognising Like Terms

**Like terms** are terms that have the **exact same variables and variable powers**. The coefficients of these terms can be different.

**Examples of Like Terms:**

- <InlineMath math="3x, \space -7x, \space -\frac{1}{5}x" /> (all have the variable <InlineMath math="x" /> to
  the power of <InlineMath math="1" />)
- <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}x^3, \space 4x^3, \space -2x^3" /> (all have the variable <InlineMath math="x" /> to
  the power of <InlineMath math="3" />)
- <InlineMath math="2x^2yz^3, \space \frac{3}{4}x^2yz^3, \space -5z^3x^2y" /> (all
  have the variable <InlineMath math="x" /> to the power of <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="y" /> to
  the power of <InlineMath math="1" />, and <InlineMath math="z" /> to the power of <InlineMath math="3" />)

**Examples of Unlike Terms:**

- <InlineMath math="11x" /> and <InlineMath math="4x^2" /> (different powers of <InlineMath math="x" />
  )
- <InlineMath math="4x^3" /> and <InlineMath math="-2x^4" /> (different powers of <InlineMath math="x" />
  )
- <InlineMath math="4x^2" /> and <InlineMath math="-x^5" /> (different powers of <InlineMath math="x" />
  )
- <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{4}x^2yz^3" /> and <InlineMath math="2x^2y" /> (missing
  the variable <InlineMath math="z^3" />)
- <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{4}x^2yz^3" /> and <InlineMath math="-7xy^2z^3" /> (different
  powers of <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" />)

## Polynomial Addition

To add two polynomials, <Highlight>add the coefficients of like terms</Highlight> while keeping their variable parts unchanged.

**Horizontal Method:**

1.  Write both polynomials in parentheses connected by a plus sign.
2.  Remove the parentheses.
3.  Group the like terms.
4.  Add the coefficients of each group of like terms (use the distributive property).

**Example:**

Find the result of <InlineMath math="(2x^3 + 7x^2 + 3x + 5) + (6x^3 + 2x^2 + 4x + 1)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}(2x^3+7x^2+3x+5)+(6x^3+2x^2+4x+1)&amp;=(2+6)x^3+(7+2)x^2+(3+4)x+(5+1) \\ &amp;=8x^3+9x^2+7x+6\end{aligned}" />

## Polynomial Subtraction

To subtract two polynomials, we **change the sign of each term in the polynomial being subtracted**, then add them as usual.

**Horizontal Method:**

1.  Write both polynomials in parentheses connected by a minus sign.
2.  Remove the parentheses. **Remember:** change the sign of each term in the second parenthesis (distribute the negative sign).
3.  Group the like terms.
4.  Add the coefficients of each group of like terms.

**Example:**

Find the result of <InlineMath math="(9x^3 + 4x^2 + 6x + 5) - (2x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 4)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}(9x^3+4x^2+6x+5)-(2x^3+3x^2+3x+4)&amp;=9x^3+4x^2+6x+5-2x^3-3x^2-3x-4 \\ &amp;=(9-2)x^3+(4-3)x^2+(6-3)x+(5-4) \\ &amp;=7x^3+x^2+3x+1\end{aligned}" />

## Vertical Method

Besides the horizontal method, polynomial addition and subtraction can also be done using the vertical method, similar to adding and subtracting regular numbers.

**Steps:**

1.  Arrange both polynomials vertically.
2.  Ensure **like terms align in the same column**.
3.  If a term is missing in one of the polynomials, leave a blank space or write a coefficient of <InlineMath math="0" />.
4.  Add or subtract the coefficients in each column.

**Example of Vertical Addition:**

<BlockMath
  math="\begin{array}{rrrrr}
    & 2x^3 & +7x^2 & +3x & +5 \\
  + & 6x^3 & +2x^2 & +4x & +1 \\
  \hline
    & 8x^3 & +9x^2 & +7x & +6
  \end{array}"
/>

**Example of Vertical Subtraction:**

<BlockMath
  math="\begin{array}{rrrrr}
    & 9x^3 & +4x^2 & +6x & +5 \\
  - & 2x^3 & +3x^2 & +3x & +4 \\
  \hline
    & 7x^3 & +1x^2 & +3x & +1
  \end{array}"
/>

The horizontal method groups like terms inside each polynomial before combining them. The vertical method aligns equal powers in columns. Both methods add or subtract the same coefficients and therefore give the same result.

### Sketching the Graph of the Sum and Difference

Read **both graph heights at the same input**. At <InlineMath math="x=1" />, for example, <InlineMath math="f(1)=2" /> and <InlineMath math="g(1)=-1/2" />. The sum graph therefore has height <InlineMath math="2-1/2=3/2" />, while the difference graph has height <InlineMath math="2-(-1/2)=5/2" />.

| Input | First value | Second value | Sum | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -2 | -1 | 1 | 0 | -2 |
| -1 | 0 | -0.5 | -0.5 | 0.5 |
| 0 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| 1 | 2 | -0.5 | 1.5 | 2.5 |
| 2 | 3 | 1 | 4 | 2 |

Each resulting point keeps the original input. Connect the points to make a sketch. Since the table contains only a few inputs, the curve between them remains an approximation until you check more points or the function equation.

## Exercise

Let

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="A(x)=3x^3-2x^2+5x-4" />
  <BlockMath math="B(x)=-x^3+6x^2-3x+7" />
</MathContainer>

Find the **sum and difference** <InlineMath math="A(x)+B(x)" /> and <InlineMath math="A(x)-B(x)" />.

### Worked Solution

For addition, combine **coefficients of like powers**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}A(x)+B(x)&amp;=(3-1)x^3+(-2+6)x^2+(5-3)x+(-4+7) \\ &amp;=2x^3+4x^2+2x+3\end{aligned}" />

For subtraction, distribute the negative sign to every term of <InlineMath math="B(x)" /> before combining like terms:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}A(x)-B(x)&amp;=3x^3-2x^2+5x-4-(-x^3+6x^2-3x+7) \\ &amp;=(3+1)x^3+(-2-6)x^2+(5+3)x+(-4-7) \\ &amp;=4x^3-8x^2+8x-11\end{aligned}" />

## Structure of a Monomial

A **monomial** is one term containing a coefficient and variables with nonnegative integer exponents: <InlineMath math="0,1,2,3,\ldots" />. A coefficient may be negative or fractional, but the variable exponents must satisfy that condition.

In <InlineMath math="2x^2y" />, the **coefficient** is <InlineMath math="2" />. The exponent of <InlineMath math="x" /> is <InlineMath math="2" />, while the unwritten exponent of <InlineMath math="y" /> is <InlineMath math="1" />. A constant is also a monomial because it does not depend on a variable.

| Expression | Variable exponents | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="2x^2y" /> | <InlineMath math="2,1" /> | Monomial |
| <InlineMath math="-8" /> | No variables | Monomial |
| <InlineMath math="1.24k^4" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> | Monomial |
| <InlineMath math="\sqrt[3]{p}=p^{1/3}" /> | <InlineMath math="1/3" /> | Not a monomial |
| <InlineMath math="\frac2m=2m^{-1}" /> | <InlineMath math="-1" /> | Not a monomial |
| <InlineMath math="5a^{-6}" /> | <InlineMath math="-6" /> | Not a monomial |

## Definition of a Polynomial

A **polynomial** is a finite sum of monomials. A single monomial is also a polynomial. Check each term to see whether every variable exponent satisfies the condition.

| Expression | Term check | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="4x^3y-3x^2" /> | Both terms have nonnegative integer exponents. | Polynomial |
| <InlineMath math="x+2\sqrt{x}" /> | The radical term has exponent <InlineMath math="1/2" />. | Not a polynomial |
| <InlineMath math="2x^3-5x^{-2}+1" /> | A negative exponent occurs in <InlineMath math="-5x^{-2}" />. | Not a polynomial |

### Subtraction as Addition

A minus sign does not stop an expression from being a polynomial. Subtraction means <Highlight>adding a term with a negative coefficient</Highlight>. Its variables and their exponents remain unchanged:

<BlockMath math="4x^3y-3x^2=4x^3y+(-3x^2)" />

Try <InlineMath math="x=1" /> and <InlineMath math="y=2" />. Both forms give <InlineMath math="8-3=8+(-3)=5" />. Only the notation for the operation changes, not its value or the variable exponents.

## Exercise

Decide whether each expression is a polynomial in the stated variable. Explain which exponent rule determines your answer. Check the **exponent of every variable** in each term, including the terms written as a fraction or a root, before you decide.

1. <InlineMath math="7x^4-2x+9" /> in <InlineMath math="x" />
2. <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x}+1" /> in <InlineMath math="x" />
3. <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{x^2}-5" /> in <InlineMath math="x" />
4. <InlineMath math="5y^2z^3-2yz+4" /> in <InlineMath math="y" /> and <InlineMath math="z" />

### Worked Solution

Each step below names the deciding condition, so the exponent rule that settles the answer appears next to every expression.

1. **Polynomial**. The exponents of <InlineMath math="x" /> are <InlineMath math="4" />, <InlineMath math="1" />, and <InlineMath math="0" />.
2. **Not a polynomial**. Since <InlineMath math="\sqrt{x}=x^{1/2}" />, the exponent <InlineMath math="1/2" /> is not a non-negative integer.
3. **Not a polynomial**. Since <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{x^2}=3x^{-2}" />, the exponent <InlineMath math="-2" /> is negative.
4. **Polynomial**. Every exponent of <InlineMath math="y" /> and <InlineMath math="z" /> is a non-negative integer. A term may omit a variable because that variable then has exponent <InlineMath math="0" />.

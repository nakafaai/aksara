## Quotient and Remainder in Integer Division

Polynomial division is similar to the division of integers we are familiar with. When we divide one number by another, we get a quotient and a remainder.

For example, when dividing <InlineMath math="7" /> by <InlineMath math="4" />:

- As a mixed number, <InlineMath math="\frac{7}{4}" /> = <InlineMath math="1 \frac{3}{4}" />. The integer quotient is <InlineMath math="1" />, and the remainder is <InlineMath math="3" />.
- For the division algorithm, we write the equivalent statement <InlineMath math="7 = 4 \cdot 1 + 3" />.

Here:

- <InlineMath math="7" /> is the **dividend**
- <InlineMath math="4" /> is the **divisor**
- <InlineMath math="1" /> is the **quotient**
- <InlineMath math="3" /> is the **remainder**

The same concept applies to polynomials.

## Polynomial Division Algorithm

The division algorithm states the relationship between the dividend polynomial, the divisor, the quotient, and the remainder.

If <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> (the dividend) and <InlineMath math="Q(x)" /> (the divisor) are two polynomials, with <InlineMath math="Q(x) \neq 0" />, then there exist unique polynomials <InlineMath math="H(x)" /> (the quotient) and <InlineMath math="S(x)" /> (the remainder) such that:

<BlockMath math="P(x) = Q(x) \cdot H(x) + S(x)" />

The equivalent fractional form is:

<BlockMath math="\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = H(x) + \frac{S(x)}{Q(x)}" />

The polynomial identity <InlineMath math="P=QH+S" /> is valid for every <InlineMath math="x" />. The fractional form is defined only at values where <InlineMath math="Q(x)\neq0" />. In both forms, the degree of <InlineMath math="S(x)" /> is less than the degree of <InlineMath math="Q(x)" />, or <InlineMath math="S(x)=0" /> when there is no remainder.

The divisor must be a **nonzero polynomial**. It may still evaluate to zero at a particular input, as <InlineMath math="x+3" /> does at <InlineMath math="x=-3" />. The restriction on division by zero applies to the fractional form, not the polynomial identity.

**Terminology**:

- <InlineMath math="P(x)" />: Dividend Polynomial
- <InlineMath math="Q(x)" />: Divisor Polynomial
- <InlineMath math="H(x)" />: Quotient Polynomial
- <InlineMath math="S(x)" />: Remainder Polynomial

**Illustrative Example**:

The division of <InlineMath math="x^3 + 4x^2 + 5x + 8" /> by <InlineMath math="x + 3" /> yields:

- Quotient <InlineMath math="H(x) = x^2 + x + 2" />
- Remainder <InlineMath math="S(x) = 2" />

To obtain the quotient, **divide the leading terms**, multiply by the divisor, and subtract that entire product. Repeat until the remainder has a lower degree than the divisor.

| Quotient term | Polynomial subtracted | Current remainder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="x^3/x=x^2" /> | <InlineMath math="x^3+3x^2" /> | <InlineMath math="x^2+5x+8" /> |
| <InlineMath math="x^2/x=x" /> | <InlineMath math="x^2+3x" /> | <InlineMath math="2x+8" /> |
| <InlineMath math="2x/x=2" /> | <InlineMath math="2x+6" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> |

The division result can be written in two forms according to the algorithm:

1.  Fractional form:

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\frac{x^3 + 4x^2 + 5x + 8}{x + 3} \\ &amp;= x^2 + x + 2 + \frac{2}{x + 3} \end{aligned}" />

2.  Multiplication form:

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x^3 + 4x^2 + 5x + 8 \\ &amp;= (x + 3)(x^2 + x + 2) + 2 \end{aligned}" />

The remainder <InlineMath math="S(x)=2" /> has degree <InlineMath math="0" />, which is <Highlight>less than the degree of the divisor</Highlight> <InlineMath math="x+3" />, namely <InlineMath math="1" />. This is the condition that makes the division complete.

### Verifying the Division Algorithm

Check the result using the **distributive property**. Multiply each quotient term by each divisor term, then collect like terms and add the remainder:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}(x+3)(x^2+x+2)+2&amp;=x(x^2+x+2)+3(x^2+x+2)+2 \\ &amp;=x^3+x^2+2x+3x^2+3x+6+2 \\ &amp;=x^3+(1+3)x^2+(2+3)x+8 \\ &amp;=x^3+4x^2+5x+8\end{aligned}" />

The quadratic coefficient becomes <InlineMath math="1+3=4" />, the linear coefficient becomes <InlineMath math="2+3=5" />, and the constant becomes <InlineMath math="6+2=8" />. Every term now matches the original polynomial.

  & 2 & 1 & 6 & \boxed{7}

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}(x-2)(2x^2+x+6)+7&amp;=2x^3+x^2+6x-4x^2-2x-12+7 \\ &amp;=2x^3-3x^2+4x-5\end{aligned}" />

## Building a Finite List of Possible Rational Roots

By the [Factor Theorem](/en/subjects/mathematics/polynomial/factor-theorem), <InlineMath math="(x-c)" /> is a factor of <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> exactly when <InlineMath math="c" /> is a zero of the polynomial. For a high-degree polynomial, the first task is to produce a finite list of values worth testing.

Testing arbitrary numbers does not give a finite search plan. The **Rational Zero Theorem** (or Rational Root Theorem) uses the leading and constant coefficients to produce <Highlight>a finite list of possible rational roots</Highlight>.

## Rational Zero Theorem

Let <InlineMath math="P(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0" /> be a polynomial where all coefficients (<InlineMath math="a_n, a_{n-1}, \dots, a_1, a_0" />) are **integers**, with <InlineMath math="a_n \neq 0" /> and <InlineMath math="a_0 \neq 0" />.

If the polynomial <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> has a rational zero (root) of the form <InlineMath math="\frac{p}{q}" /> (where <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" /> are integers, <InlineMath math="q \neq 0" />, and <InlineMath math="\frac{p}{q}" /> is a fraction in simplest form), then:

- <InlineMath math="p" /> must be a factor of the constant term <InlineMath math="a_0" />.
- <InlineMath math="q" /> must be a factor of the leading coefficient <InlineMath math="a_n" />.

This theorem only provides a list of **possible** rational roots. Not all values of <InlineMath math="\frac{p}{q}" /> from the list are necessarily actual roots of the polynomial. We still need to test them.

## Steps for Using the Rational Zero Theorem

**A candidate is not yet a root**. Use positive and negative divisors of the constant as numerators and positive divisors of the leading coefficient as denominators. Reduce the fractions so that equal values are not tested repeatedly. Check each candidate by substitution or [Horner’s method](/en/subjects/mathematics/polynomial/horner-method).

If the constant is zero, factor out <InlineMath math="x" /> first. For example, <InlineMath math="x^3-2x^2=x^2(x-2)" /> has zero as a root, and the remaining factor gives the root two.

If no candidate works, the conclusion is only **no rational roots**. The candidates for <InlineMath math="x^2-2" /> are <InlineMath math="\pm1,\pm2" />, but none makes the polynomial zero. It still has the real roots <InlineMath math="\pm\sqrt2" />.

### Using the Factor Theorem and Rational Zero Theorem

Factor the polynomial <InlineMath math="P(x) = x^3 + 2x^2 - 9x - 18" /> completely.

**Step 1**. Identify Coefficients

The coefficients are integers. <InlineMath math="a_0 = -18" /> and <InlineMath math="a_n = 1" />.

**Step 2**. Factors of <InlineMath math="p" /> (from <InlineMath math="a_0 = -18" />)

<BlockMath math="\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \pm 6, \pm 9, \pm 18" />

**Step 3**. Factors of <InlineMath math="q" /> (from <InlineMath math="a_n = 1" />)

<BlockMath math="\pm 1" />

**Step 4**. Possible Roots <InlineMath math="\frac{p}{q}" />

Dividing all <InlineMath math="p" /> by <InlineMath math="q = \pm 1" /> yields:

<BlockMath math="\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \pm 6, \pm 9, \pm 18" />

**Step 5**

| Candidate | Substitution result | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <InlineMath math="1+2-9-18=-24" /> | Not a root |
| -1 | <InlineMath math="-1+2+9-18=-8" /> | Not a root |
| 2 | <InlineMath math="8+8-18-18=-20" /> | Not a root |
| -2 | <InlineMath math="-8+8+18-18=0" /> | Root |
| 3 | <InlineMath math="27+18-27-18=0" /> | Root |

Use the root <InlineMath math="3" /> for the division. The root <InlineMath math="-2" /> is also valid and reappears when you factor the quotient.

**Step 6**. Factor Further (using the root <InlineMath math="x = 3" />)

Divide <InlineMath math="P(x)" /> by <InlineMath math="(x-3)" /> using Horner's (<InlineMath math="c = 3" />).

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
3 & 1 & 2 & -9 & -18 \\
  &   & 3 & 15 & 18 \\
\hline
  & 1 & 5 & 6 & \boxed{0} \\
\end{array}
"
/>

The quotient is <InlineMath math="H(x) = x^2 + 5x + 6" />.

Thus, <InlineMath math="P(x) = (x-3)(x^2 + 5x + 6)" />.

**Step 7**. Factor the Quotient

Factor <InlineMath math="x^2 + 5x + 6" />.

<BlockMath math="x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x+2)(x+3)" />

**Step 8**. Complete Factorization

<BlockMath math="P(x) = (x-3)(x+2)(x+3)" />

## Exercise

Factor <InlineMath math="P(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 12x + 20" /> completely using the **Rational Zero Theorem** and the Factor Theorem. List the possible roots from the factors of the constant and the leading coefficient, then test them in order and divide out each root you confirm.

### Answer Key

**Step 1**. Identify Coefficients <InlineMath math="a_0 = 20" />, <InlineMath math="a_n = 2" />.

**Step 2**. Factors of <InlineMath math="p" /> (from <InlineMath math="20" />) <InlineMath math="\pm 1" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 2" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 4" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 5" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 10" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 20" />.

**Step 3**. Factors of <InlineMath math="q" /> (from <InlineMath math="2" />) <InlineMath math="\pm 1" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 2" />.

**Step 4**. Possible Roots <InlineMath math="\frac{p}{q}" /> <InlineMath math="\pm 1" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 2" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 4" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 5" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 10" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 20" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 1/2" />, <InlineMath math="\pm 5/2" />.

**Step 5**. Test Roots

Try <InlineMath math="x = 2" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="P(2) = 2(2)^3 - 3(2)^2 - 12(2) + 20" />
  <BlockMath math="P(2) = 2(8) - 3(4) - 24 + 20" />
  <BlockMath math="P(2) = 16 - 12 - 24 + 20" />
  <BlockMath math="P(2) = 4 - 4 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

Since <InlineMath math="P(2)=0" />, <InlineMath math="x=2" /> is a root and <InlineMath math="(x-2)" /> is a factor.

**Step 6**. Divide using Horner (<InlineMath math="c = 2" />)

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
2 & 2 & -3 & -12 & 20 \\
  &   & 4 & 2 & -20 \\
\hline
  & 2 & 1 & -10 & \boxed{0} \\
\end{array}
"
/>

The quotient is <InlineMath math="H(x) = 2x^2 + x - 10" />.

<InlineMath math="P(x) = (x-2)(2x^2 + x - 10)" />.

**Step 7**. Factor the Quotient

Factor <InlineMath math="2x^2 + x - 10" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}2x^2+x-10&amp;=2x^2-4x+5x-10 \\ &amp;=2x(x-2)+5(x-2) \\ &amp;=(2x+5)(x-2)\end{aligned}" />

**Step 8**. Complete Factorization

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="P(x) = (x-2)(2x+5)(x-2)" />
  <BlockMath math="P(x) = (x-2)^2 (2x+5)" />
</MathContainer>

The root <InlineMath math="2" /> occurs twice because <InlineMath math="x-2" /> is squared. The other root is <InlineMath math="-5/2" />, obtained from <InlineMath math="2x+5=0" />.

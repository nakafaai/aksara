## Dividing a Polynomial Using Its Coefficients

Horner's method <Highlight>divides a polynomial by a linear divisor</Highlight> such as <InlineMath math="(x-c)" /> using only its ordered coefficients. Each step multiplies the latest result by <InlineMath math="c" /> and adds the next coefficient, so the calculation does not repeat the variables and powers used in long division. The last number of the scheme is **the remainder**, which equals <InlineMath math="P(c)" />, and the numbers before it are the coefficients of the quotient.

### Comparison with Long Division

To see how Horner's method simplifies the process, compare the division of <InlineMath math="P(x) = x^3 - 7x + 8" /> (or <InlineMath math="x^3 + 0x^2 - 7x + 8" />) by <InlineMath math="x-2" /> using both methods:

**Long Division**:

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{l}
\qquad\quad\space\space x^2 + 2x - 3 \\
x-2\overline{\big)x^3 + 0x^2 - 7x + 8} \\
\quad\space\space \underline{-(x^3 - 2x^2)} \\
\qquad\qquad 2x^2 - 7x \\
\qquad\quad \underline{-(2x^2 - 4x)} \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad -3x + 8 \\
\qquad\qquad\quad \underline{-(-3x + 6)} \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad 2 \\
\end{array}
"
/>

**Horner's Method**:

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
2 & 1 & 0 & -7 & 8 \\
  &   & 2 & 4 & -6 \\
\hline
  & 1 & 2 & -3 & \boxed{2} \\
\end{array}
"
/>

Notice how Horner's method only focuses on the coefficients <InlineMath math="(1, 0, -7, 8)" /> and the value <InlineMath math="c=2" />. The bottom row in Horner's method <InlineMath math="(1, 2, -3)" /> directly gives the coefficients of the quotient <InlineMath math="1x^2 + 2x - 3" />, and the last number <InlineMath math="\boxed{2}" /> is the remainder.

This method can only be directly used if the divisor is a linear polynomial of degree one, i.e., in the form <InlineMath math="(x-c)" /> or <InlineMath math="(ax-b)" /> (which can be transformed).

## Preparing to Use the Horner Method

The **coefficient order** must include every power, from the highest down to the constant. A missing term still needs a position, filled by zero. For <InlineMath math="2x^3+5x^2+6" />, use:

| Power | Coefficient |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="x^3" /> | 2 |
| <InlineMath math="x^2" /> | 5 |
| <InlineMath math="x" /> | 0 |
| <InlineMath math="1" /> | 6 |

Place the **root of the divisor** on the left of the scheme. The divisor <InlineMath math="x+3=x-(-3)" /> gives <InlineMath math="c=-3" />, not three. Write the polynomial coefficients across the top row.

## Division with the Horner Method

In each column, **multiply the previous result and add the next coefficient**. This rule comes from writing the polynomial as nested products:

<BlockMath math="2x^3+5x^2+6=((2x+5)x+0)x+6" />

Start with the first coefficient, two. After every coefficient has been processed, the last result is the polynomial value at the divisor’s root. By the Remainder Theorem, it is the remainder. The earlier results give the quotient coefficients.

## Using the Horner Method

Divide <InlineMath math="P(x) = 2x^3 + 5x^2 + 6" /> by <InlineMath math="x+3" /> using both methods.

**Long Division**:

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{l}
\qquad\quad\space 2x^2 - x + 3 \\
x+3\overline{\big)2x^3 + 5x^2 + 0x + 6} \\
\quad\space \underline{-(2x^3 + 6x^2)} \\
\qquad\qquad -x^2 + 0x \\
\qquad\quad \underline{-(-x^2 - 3x)} \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad 3x + 6 \\
\qquad\qquad\quad \underline{-(3x + 9)} \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad -3 \\
\end{array}
"
/>

**Horner's Method**:

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
-3 & 2 & 5 & 0 & 6 \\
  &   & -6 & 3 & -9 \\
\hline
  & 2 & -1 & 3 & \boxed{-3} \\
\end{array}
"
/>

| Next coefficient | Multiplication | Addition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | <InlineMath math="2(-3)=-6" /> | <InlineMath math="5-6=-1" /> |
| 0 | <InlineMath math="(-1)(-3)=3" /> | <InlineMath math="0+3=3" /> |
| 6 | <InlineMath math="3(-3)=-9" /> | <InlineMath math="6-9=-3" /> |

The final number is the **remainder**, <InlineMath math="-3" />. The preceding numbers, <InlineMath math="2,-1,3" />, give the quotient <InlineMath math="H(x)=2x^2-x+3" />. Therefore:

<BlockMath math="2x^3+5x^2+6=(x+3)(2x^2-x+3)-3" />

Check the remainder by evaluating the polynomial directly: <InlineMath math="P(-3) = 2(-27) + 5(9) + 6 = -54 + 45 + 6 = -3" />. That equals the remainder, as the [remainder theorem](/en/subjects/mathematics/polynomial/remainder-theorem) says. The graph of <InlineMath math="y = P(x)" /> shows the same link: the point of the graph at <InlineMath math="x = -3" /> sits exactly at height <InlineMath math="-3" />.

### A Divisor with a Leading Coefficient Other Than One

For a divisor <InlineMath math="ax-b" /> with <InlineMath math="a\ne0" />, use <InlineMath math="c=b/a" /> in the scheme. However, **divide the resulting quotient by the divisor’s leading coefficient**. The remainder stays unchanged.

For example, use the same scheme for <InlineMath math="2x+6=2(x+3)" />. To preserve the product, divide the previous quotient by two:

<BlockMath math="2x^3+5x^2+6=(2x+6)\left(x^2-\frac12x+\frac32\right)-3" />

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Using <InlineMath math="c = 3" /> for the divisor <InlineMath math="x + 3" /> | <InlineMath math="x + 3 = x - (-3)" />, so <InlineMath math="c = -3" /> | Substitute <InlineMath math="x = -3" /> into <InlineMath math="x + 3" />, the result must be <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| Skipping a missing term | <InlineMath math="2x^3 + 5x^2 + 6" /> needs a zero coefficient for <InlineMath math="x" /> | <InlineMath math="2x^3 + 5x^2 + 6" /> must have four coefficients: <InlineMath math="2, 5, 0, 6" /> |
| Adding before multiplying | Each column uses the previous result times <InlineMath math="c" /> | Redo one column: multiply first, then add |
| Forgetting to divide the quotient for a divisor <InlineMath math="ax - b" /> | The scheme uses <InlineMath math="x - \frac{b}{a}" />, not <InlineMath math="ax - b" /> | Multiply the divisor by the quotient and add the remainder |

A simple check is **evaluating <InlineMath math="P(c)" /> directly**. The result must equal the last number of the scheme.

## Exercise

Find the **quotient and remainder** from the division of <InlineMath math="x^4 + 4" /> by <InlineMath math="x - 1" /> using Horner's method and long division.

State the result in the form <InlineMath math="P(x) = Q(x) \cdot H(x) + S(x)" />. Both methods must give identical values, and the degree check tells you how many terms the quotient can have.

Also work through these with the Horner method, then **check each remainder with <InlineMath math="P(c)" />**.

2. Divide <InlineMath math="x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6" /> by <InlineMath math="x - 2" />.

3. Divide <InlineMath math="2x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x - 5" /> by <InlineMath math="x + 1" />.

4. Divide <InlineMath math="x^3 + 2x^2 - 5" /> by <InlineMath math="2x - 1" />.

### Answer Key

- Dividend: <InlineMath math="P(x) = x^4 + 4 = x^4 + 0x^3 + 0x^2 + 0x + 4" />.
- Divisor: <InlineMath math="Q(x) = x - 1" />, so <InlineMath math="c = 1" />.

**Long Division**:

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{l}
\qquad\quad\space\space x^3 + x^2 + x + 1 \\
x-1\overline{\big)x^4 + 0x^3 + 0x^2 + 0x + 4} \\
\quad\space\space \underline{-(x^4 - x^3)} \\
\qquad\qquad x^3 + 0x^2 \\
\qquad\quad \underline{-(x^3 - x^2)} \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad x^2 + 0x \\
\qquad\qquad\quad \underline{-(x^2 - x)} \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad x + 4 \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad \underline{-(x - 1)} \\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad 5 \\
\end{array}
"
/>

**Horner's Method**:

<BlockMath
  math="
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 4 \\
  &   & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
\hline
  & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & \boxed{5} \\
\end{array}
"
/>

**Result**:

- Quotient: <InlineMath math="H(x) = x^3 + x^2 + x + 1" />.
- Remainder: <InlineMath math="S = \boxed{5}" />.

**Writing in Division Algorithm Form**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x^4 + 4 \\ &amp;= (x-1)(x^3 + x^2 + x + 1) + 5 \end{aligned}" />

**Problem 2**. The Horner scheme with <InlineMath math="c = 2" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{c|cccc} 2 & 1 & -6 & 11 & -6 \\ & & 2 & -8 & 6 \\ \hline & 1 & -4 & 3 & \boxed{0} \end{array}" />

The bottom row is <InlineMath math="1, -4, 3, 0" />. The quotient is <InlineMath math="x^2 - 4x + 3" /> and the remainder is <InlineMath math="0" />. Check: <InlineMath math="P(2) = 8 - 24 + 22 - 6 = 0" />. A remainder of zero means <InlineMath math="x - 2" /> is a factor, as the [factor theorem](/en/subjects/mathematics/polynomial/factor-theorem) says.

**Problem 3**. The divisor <InlineMath math="x + 1" /> gives <InlineMath math="c = -1" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{c|cccc} -1 & 2 & -3 & 4 & -5 \\ & & -2 & 5 & -9 \\ \hline & 2 & -5 & 9 & \boxed{-14} \end{array}" />

The bottom row is <InlineMath math="2, -5, 9, -14" />. The quotient is <InlineMath math="2x^2 - 5x + 9" /> and the remainder is <InlineMath math="-14" />. Check: <InlineMath math="P(-1) = -2 - 3 - 4 - 5 = -14" />.

**Problem 4**. Use <InlineMath math="c = \frac12" /> with the coefficients <InlineMath math="1, 2, 0, -5" />. 

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{c|cccc} \frac12 & 1 & 2 & 0 & -5 \\ & & \frac12 & \frac54 & \frac58 \\ \hline & 1 & \frac52 & \frac54 & \boxed{-\frac{35}{8}} \end{array}" />

The bottom row is <InlineMath math="1, \frac52, \frac54, -\frac{35}{8}" />. Since the divisor is <InlineMath math="2x - 1 = 2\left(x - \frac12\right)" />, divide the quotient coefficients by <InlineMath math="2" />:

<BlockMath math="x^3 + 2x^2 - 5 = (2x - 1)\left(\frac12x^2 + \frac54x + \frac58\right) - \frac{35}{8}" />

Check: <InlineMath math="P\left(\frac12\right) = \frac18 + \frac12 - 5 = -\frac{35}{8}" />.

## Why Direction Matters When Adding Displacements

Vector addition differs from scalar addition. In scalar addition, we only add magnitudes without considering direction. For example, <InlineMath math="2 \text{ kg}" /> of sugar plus <InlineMath math="3 \text{ kg}" /> of sugar equals <InlineMath math="5 \text{ kg}" /> of sugar. However, in vector addition, we must consider both magnitude and direction.

For instance, if someone walks <InlineMath math="2 \text{ m}" /> east and then <InlineMath math="3 \text{ m}" /> west, the result differs from walking <InlineMath math="2 \text{ m}" /> east and then <InlineMath math="3 \text{ m}" /> east again. The result of vector addition is called the **resultant vector**.

## Vector Addition Using the Triangle Method

In the triangle method, arrange the vectors in sequence:

1. Draw the first vector <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" />
2. Translate the second vector <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> until its tail is at the tip of the first vector
3. Draw the resultant from the tail of the first vector to the tip of the second

Mathematically, if <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}" /> and <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{BC}" /> are two vectors, then:

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{AB} + \overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{AC} = \vec{a} + \vec{b}" />

Vector addition satisfies the commutative property:

<BlockMath math="\vec{a} + \vec{b} = \vec{b} + \vec{a}" />

## Vector Addition Using the Parallelogram Method

Another method for adding vectors is the parallelogram method. The steps are:

1. Draw both vectors with their tails coinciding
2. Create a parallelogram using the two vectors as sides
3. The resultant vector is the diagonal of the parallelogram passing through the common origin of both vectors

## Boat Velocity in a River

Vector addition can determine the velocity of a boat crossing a river:

1. If the river current velocity is zero, the boat's velocity relative to the shore equals its velocity relative to the water
2. If the river current velocity is nonzero, the boat's velocity relative to the shore is the vector sum of its water-relative velocity and the current velocity. Whether its direction, magnitude, or both change depends on the current's direction

A sequence of displacement vectors can likewise be added. Its resultant gives the net displacement. Route length and the shortest route must be determined from the path of the journey.

## Difference Between Scalar and Vector Addition

Both scalar addition and vector addition produce a unique result for fixed inputs. The difference is that scalar inputs carry only magnitude, while vector inputs also carry direction. Changing a vector's direction therefore changes the input and can change the resultant.

**Example of scalar addition**:

For sugar, <InlineMath math="3 \text{ kg} + 4 \text{ kg} = 7 \text{ kg}" />.

**Example of vector addition**:

For displacement in the same direction, <InlineMath math="3 \text{ m east} + 4 \text{ m east} = 7 \text{ m east}" />. In opposite directions, <InlineMath math="3 \text{ m east} + 4 \text{ m west} = 1 \text{ m west}" />.

Vector addition calculates resultant displacement, velocity, acceleration, and force.

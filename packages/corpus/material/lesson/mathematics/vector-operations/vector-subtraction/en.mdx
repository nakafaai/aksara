## Basic Concept of Vector Subtraction

Vector subtraction gives the difference between two vectors. When we subtract <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> from <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" />, we obtain the vector that must be added to <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> to produce <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" />.

Mathematically, vector subtraction is defined as:

<BlockMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b} = \vec{a} + (-\vec{b})" />

In other words, subtracting <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> from <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" /> is equivalent to adding <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" /> to the negative of <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" />.

## Geometric Vector Subtraction

The following four steps show two equivalent constructions of <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b}" />:

1. Draw <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" /> and <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> with the same initial point.
2. Draw the arrow from the endpoint of <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> to the endpoint of <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" />. That arrow is <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b}" />.
3. For the second construction, reverse <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> to form <InlineMath math="-\vec{b}" />, then translate it so its initial point meets the endpoint of <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" />.
4. Draw the arrow from the common origin to the final endpoint. It is <InlineMath math="\vec{a}+(-\vec{b})=\vec{a}-\vec{b}" />.

The visualization shows both constructions. The two arrows labeled <InlineMath math="a-b" /> have the same components, length, and direction even though they begin at different points.

## Algebraic Vector Subtraction

Algebraically, subtract corresponding components. Suppose we have two vectors:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\vec{a} = (a_1, a_2, a_3)" />
  <BlockMath math="\vec{b} = (b_1, b_2, b_3)" />
</MathContainer>

Then vector subtraction <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b}" /> can be calculated as:

<BlockMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b} = (a_1 - b_1, a_2 - b_2, a_3 - b_3)" />

For two-dimensional vectors, the equation becomes:

<BlockMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b} = (a_1 - b_1, a_2 - b_2)" />

## Example of Vector Subtraction Calculation

Suppose there are two vectors:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\vec{a} = (3, 4)" />
  <BlockMath math="\vec{b} = (1, 2)" />
</MathContainer>

Vector subtraction <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b}" /> is:

<BlockMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b} = (3, 4) - (1, 2) = (3-1, 4-2) = (2, 2)" />

While vector subtraction <InlineMath math="\vec{b} - \vec{a}" /> is:

<BlockMath math="\vec{b} - \vec{a} = (1, 2) - (3, 4) = (1-3, 2-4) = (-2, -2)" />

Notice that <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b} \neq \vec{b} - \vec{a}" /> in general. Reversing the order reverses the direction: <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b} = -(\vec{b} - \vec{a})" />.

## Applications of Vector Subtraction

Vector subtraction appears whenever we compare two vector quantities:

1. **Calculating Displacement**: If <InlineMath math="\vec{a}" /> is the final position and <InlineMath math="\vec{b}" /> is the initial position, then <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b}" /> is the displacement vector.

2. **Calculating Distance**: In a game, subtracting the bird's position from the target's position gives the direction toward the target. The magnitude of that difference vector gives the straight-line distance.

3. **Comparing Velocities**: In physics, the velocity of object <InlineMath math="A" /> relative to object <InlineMath math="B" /> is <InlineMath math="\vec{v}_{A/B}=\vec{v}_A-\vec{v}_B" />. Reversing the order reverses the relative-velocity vector.

## Vector Subtraction to Find Displacement

The displacement between two positions is found by subtracting the initial position vector from the final position vector.

Suppose an object moves from initial position <InlineMath math="\vec{p}_1" /> to final position <InlineMath math="\vec{p}_2" />. The displacement vector of the object is:

<BlockMath math="\vec{d} = \vec{p}_2 - \vec{p}_1" />

This vector points from the initial position to the final position. Its magnitude is the straight-line distance between those positions, which may be shorter than the path actually traveled.

## Properties of Vector Subtraction

Vector subtraction has several important properties:

1. <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b} = \vec{a} + (-\vec{b})" />
2. <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{a} = \vec{0}" /> (zero vector)
3. <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{0} = \vec{a}" />
4. <InlineMath math="\vec{0} - \vec{a} = -\vec{a}" />
5. <InlineMath math="\vec{a} - \vec{b} = -(\vec{b} - \vec{a})" />

## Example Problem

A monkey is at position <InlineMath math="(3, 4)" /> and wants to get a banana located at position <InlineMath math="(2, 1)" />. Determine the displacement vector of the monkey to reach the banana.

**Solution:**

Monkey's position: <InlineMath math="\vec{m} = (3, 4)" />

Banana's position: <InlineMath math="\vec{p} = (2, 1)" />

Displacement vector of the monkey to the banana:

<BlockMath math="\vec{d} = \vec{p} - \vec{m} = (2, 1) - (3, 4) = (2-3, 1-4) = (-1, -3)" />

Therefore, the monkey needs to move <InlineMath math="1 \text{ unit}" /> in the negative <InlineMath math="x" />-axis direction and <InlineMath math="3 \text{ units}" /> in the negative <InlineMath math="y" />-axis direction to reach the banana.

The magnitude of the displacement vector can be calculated using the Pythagorean theorem:

<BlockMath math="|\vec{d}| = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + (-3)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10} \approx 3.16" />

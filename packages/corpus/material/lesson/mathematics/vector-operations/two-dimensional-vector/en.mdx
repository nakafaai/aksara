## Two-Dimensional Vector Concept

In the Cartesian coordinate system, each point on a plane can be represented by a pair of numbers <InlineMath math="(x, y)" />, where <InlineMath math="x" /> is the horizontal position and <InlineMath math="y" /> is the vertical position. The origin point is <InlineMath math="O(0, 0)" />.

If we draw a directed line segment from the origin <InlineMath math="O" /> to another point, for example <InlineMath math="Q(x, y)" />, we obtain a **vector**. This vector is written as <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{OQ}" />. Its **magnitude** is the segment's length, and its **direction** runs from the initial point toward the arrowhead.

To simplify, we use **unit vectors**. Unit vectors have a length of <InlineMath math="1 \text{ unit}" />.

- <InlineMath math="\mathbf{i}" /> is the unit vector along the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis (horizontal).
- <InlineMath math="\mathbf{j}" /> is the unit vector along the positive <InlineMath math="y" />-axis (vertical).

Vector <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{OQ}" /> can be expressed as a combination of horizontal movement of <InlineMath math="x" /> and vertical movement of <InlineMath math="y" />. In unit vector form, we write:

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{OQ} = x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j}" />

## Vector Components and Magnitude

The values <InlineMath math="x" /> and <InlineMath math="y" /> in vector <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{OQ} = x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j}" /> are called the vector **components**.

- <InlineMath math="x" /> is the **horizontal scalar component**. The corresponding vector projection onto the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis is <InlineMath math="x\mathbf{i}" />.
- <InlineMath math="y" /> is the **vertical scalar component**. The corresponding vector projection onto the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis is <InlineMath math="y\mathbf{j}" />.

A vector with these two components is called a **two-dimensional vector**.

The **length** or **magnitude** of vector <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{OQ}" />, written as <InlineMath math="|\overrightarrow{OQ}|" />, is the distance from the origin <InlineMath math="O" /> to the endpoint <InlineMath math="Q" />. If <InlineMath math="Q(x,y)" /> is the endpoint and <InlineMath math="R(x,0)" /> is the projection of <InlineMath math="Q" /> onto the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, the right triangle <InlineMath math="ORQ" /> gives its magnitude through the Pythagorean theorem:

<BlockMath math="|\overrightarrow{OQ}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}" />

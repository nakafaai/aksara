## Definition of Unit Vector

A unit vector has length or magnitude <InlineMath math="1 \text{ unit}" />. It represents direction without carrying the original magnitude. From any nonzero vector, we obtain a unit vector in the same direction by dividing the vector by its length.

## Unit Vector Formula and Direction

Consider vector <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PQ}" /> in a Cartesian coordinate system. The unit vector of <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PQ}" /> is defined as vector <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PQ}" /> divided by its length.

<BlockMath math="\mathbf{u}_{PQ} = \frac{\overrightarrow{PQ}}{|\overrightarrow{PQ}|}" />

Where <InlineMath math="|\overrightarrow{PQ}|" /> is the length or magnitude of vector <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{PQ}" />.

A unit vector always points in the same direction as the original vector but has its length normalized to <InlineMath math="1 \text{ unit}" />.

## Calculating a Unit Vector

To determine a unit vector:

1. Identify the original vector
2. Calculate the length of the vector
3. Divide the vector by its length

### Normalizing a Three Dimensional Vector

Take the vector <InlineMath math="\vec{v} = (3, 6, 4)" />.

First, calculate its magnitude:

<BlockMath math="|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{3^2 + 6^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 36 + 16} = \sqrt{61}" />

Then divide the vector by that magnitude:

<BlockMath math="\hat{v} = \frac{\vec{v}}{|\vec{v}|} = \frac{(3, 6, 4)}{\sqrt{61}}" />

The unit vector is therefore:

<BlockMath math="\hat{v} = \left(\frac{3}{\sqrt{61}}, \frac{6}{\sqrt{61}}, \frac{4}{\sqrt{61}}\right)" />

## Properties of Unit Vectors

### Length of a Unit Vector

A unit vector always has a length equal to <InlineMath math="1" />. This can be proven by calculating the length of the unit vector:

<BlockMath math="|\hat{v}| = \sqrt{\left(\frac{3}{\sqrt{61}}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{6}{\sqrt{61}}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{4}{\sqrt{61}}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\frac{9 + 36 + 16}{61}} = \sqrt{\frac{61}{61}} = 1" />

### Unit Vectors on Coordinate Axes

In a three-dimensional coordinate system, there are three standard unit vectors that point along the positive coordinate axes:

- <InlineMath math="\hat{\imath}" /> is the unit vector in the positive <InlineMath math="x" />-axis direction
- <InlineMath math="\hat{\jmath}" /> is the unit vector in the positive <InlineMath math="y" />-axis direction
- <InlineMath math="\hat{k}" /> is the unit vector in the positive <InlineMath math="z" />-axis direction

Every vector in <InlineMath math="\mathbb{R}^3" /> can be expressed as a linear combination of these three unit vectors.

## Applications of Unit Vectors

### Indicating Direction

A unit vector records direction with magnitude <InlineMath math="1" />. In physics, it can specify the direction of a force, velocity, or acceleration separately from that quantity's magnitude.

### Physics Calculations

Unit vectors express a force as components along the <InlineMath math="x" />, <InlineMath math="y" />, and <InlineMath math="z" /> axes. Each coefficient gives the signed magnitude along one axis.

## Reversing a Vector without Changing Its Length

For a nonzero vector <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" />, its **opposite vector** has the same magnitude but <Highlight>points in exactly the opposite direction</Highlight>. It is written as <InlineMath math="-\vec{v}" />.

If <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" /> describes a displacement from left to right, then <InlineMath math="-\vec{v}" /> describes an equally long displacement from right to left. Their lengths match, and their directions are opposite.

## Formula for an Opposite Vector

Multiplying a vector by the scalar <InlineMath math="-1" /> **reverses the sign of every component**:

<BlockMath math="-\vec{v}=(-1)\vec{v}" />

If <InlineMath math="\vec{v}=(v_1,v_2,v_3)" />, then:

<BlockMath math="-\vec{v}=(-v_1,-v_2,-v_3)" />

Here:

- <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" /> is the original vector.
- <InlineMath math="-\vec{v}" /> is its opposite vector.
- The factor <InlineMath math="-1" /> reverses the direction without changing the magnitude.

## Finding the Opposite of a Vector

Suppose <InlineMath math="\overrightarrow{AB}=6\mathbf{i}+8\mathbf{j}" />.

Its magnitude is:

<BlockMath math="|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=\sqrt{100}=10" />

Multiply **every component** by <InlineMath math="-1" /> to find the opposite vector:

<BlockMath math="-\overrightarrow{AB}=-(6\mathbf{i}+8\mathbf{j})=-6\mathbf{i}-8\mathbf{j}" />

Both vectors have magnitude <InlineMath math="10" />, but their directions differ by <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.

## Properties of Opposite Vectors

1. Their magnitudes are equal:

   <BlockMath math="|-\vec{v}|=|\vec{v}|" />

2. For a nonzero vector, their directions differ by <InlineMath math="180^\circ" />.

3. Their sum is the **zero vector**:

   <BlockMath math="\vec{v}+(-\vec{v})=\vec{0}" />

4. Taking the opposite twice returns the original vector:

   <BlockMath math="-(-\vec{v})=\vec{v}" />

The zero vector is its own opposite because <InlineMath math="-\vec{0}=\vec{0}" />.

## Applications of Opposite Vectors

Opposite vectors appear whenever a direction is reversed:

1. **Displacement**: a return trip along the same straight path has the opposite displacement vector.

2. **Force equilibrium**: two equal forces in opposite directions add to the zero vector.

3. **Vector equations**: subtraction can be rewritten as addition of an opposite vector, <InlineMath math="\vec{a}-\vec{b}=\vec{a}+(-\vec{b})" />.

Subtraction becomes addition of an opposite. ## Unit Vectors and Opposite Direction

A unit vector is built from a nonzero vector by **dividing that vector by its length**. Multiplying the result by <InlineMath math="-1" /> reverses its direction while keeping the same line.

### Unit Vector

For a **nonzero vector** <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" />, its unit vector is:

<BlockMath math="\hat{v}=\frac{\vec{v}}{|\vec{v}|}" />

It has magnitude <InlineMath math="1" /> and points in the same direction as <InlineMath math="\vec{v}" />.

### Relationship with the Opposite Vector

The unit vector in the **opposite direction** is <InlineMath math="-\hat{v}" />. Multiplying it by the original magnitude gives the opposite vector:

<BlockMath math="-\vec{v}=|\vec{v}|(-\hat{v})" />

This separates the two ideas clearly: <InlineMath math="|\vec{v}|" /> determines the length, while <InlineMath math="-\hat{v}" /> determines the opposite direction.

## State the Domain Before Using a Rule

Exponent rules depend on the **kind of exponent** and the **sign of the base**. The following seven identities are useful, but their conditions are part of each statement.

| Property | Identity | Conditions used here |
| -------- | -------- | -------------------- |
| Same-base product | <InlineMath math="a^m a^n=a^{m+n}" /> | <InlineMath math="a\neq0" />, <InlineMath math="m,n\in\mathbb{Z}" /> |
| Same-base quotient | <InlineMath math="\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}" /> | <InlineMath math="a\neq0" />, <InlineMath math="m,n\in\mathbb{Z}" /> |
| Power of a power | <InlineMath math="(a^m)^n=a^{mn}" /> | <InlineMath math="a\neq0" />, <InlineMath math="m,n\in\mathbb{Z}" /> |
| Power of a product | <InlineMath math="(ab)^m=a^m b^m" /> | <InlineMath math="a,b\neq0" />, <InlineMath math="m\in\mathbb{Z}" /> |
| Power of a quotient | <InlineMath math="\left(\frac ab\right)^m=\frac{a^m}{b^m}" /> | <InlineMath math="a,b\neq0" />, <InlineMath math="m\in\mathbb{Z}" /> |
| Equal-denominator rational powers | <InlineMath math="a^{m/n}a^{p/n}=a^{(m+p)/n}" /> | <InlineMath math="a>0" />, <InlineMath math="m,n,p\in\mathbb{Z}" />, <InlineMath math="n\neq0" /> |
| General rational powers | <InlineMath math="a^{m/n}a^{p/q}=a^{(mq+pn)/(nq)}" /> | <InlineMath math="a>0" />, <InlineMath math="m,n,p,q\in\mathbb{Z}" />, <InlineMath math="n,q\neq0" /> |

The integer rules can sometimes be extended to other domains, but those extensions require extra conditions. Under the conditions listed in the table, every expression is well-defined over the real numbers.

## Proofs for Integer Exponents

The proofs below write each power as **repeated multiplication**. That form shows directly why the exponents add, subtract, or multiply. Read each proof as a short chain of equalities, and check that every step uses only the definition of a power.

### Same Base Product

For positive integers <InlineMath math="m" /> and <InlineMath math="n" />, write each power as **repeated multiplication**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="a^m=\underbrace{a\cdot a\cdots a}_{m\text{ factors}}" />
  <BlockMath math="a^n=\underbrace{a\cdot a\cdots a}_{n\text{ factors}}" />
  <BlockMath math="a^m a^n=\underbrace{a\cdot a\cdots a}_{m+n\text{ factors}}=a^{m+n}" />
</MathContainer>

The zero case follows from <InlineMath math="a^0=1" />. Negative integer exponents follow from <InlineMath math="a^{-k}=\frac1{a^k}" />, which is why <InlineMath math="a" /> must be nonzero.

### Same Base Quotient

Division removes <InlineMath math="n" /> **factors from the numerator** when <InlineMath math="m\geq n" />:

<BlockMath math="\frac{a^m}{a^n}=\frac{\overbrace{a\cdots a}^{m\text{ factors}}}{\underbrace{a\cdots a}_{n\text{ factors}}}=a^{m-n}" />

If <InlineMath math="m<n" />, the uncancelled factors stay in the denominator. The negative-exponent definition gives the same result:

<BlockMath math="\frac{a^m}{a^n}=\frac1{a^{n-m}}=a^{-(n-m)}=a^{m-n}" />

### Power of a Power

For positive <InlineMath math="n" />, the factor <InlineMath math="a^m" /> **appears** <InlineMath math="n" /> times:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} (a^m)^n &amp;=\underbrace{a^m a^m\cdots a^m}_{n\text{ factors}} \\ &amp;=a^{m+m+\cdots+m} \\ &amp;=a^{mn} \end{aligned}" />

The zero exponent covers <InlineMath math="n=0" />, and the reciprocal covers negative <InlineMath math="n" />.

### Power of a Product and Quotient

For positive <InlineMath math="m" />, **regrouping the factors** gives:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(ab)^m=\underbrace{(ab)(ab)\cdots(ab)}_{m\text{ factors}}=a^m b^m" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(\frac ab\right)^m=\underbrace{\frac ab\cdot\frac ab\cdots\frac ab}_{m\text{ factors}}=\frac{a^m}{b^m}" />
</MathContainer>

The zero and negative cases again follow from <InlineMath math="x^0=1" /> and <InlineMath math="x^{-k}=\frac1{x^k}" />. Requiring nonzero bases makes all needed reciprocals valid.

## Proofs for Rational Exponents

For rational exponents, use a **positive base**. We write both factors as integer powers of the same positive root. Take the denominators to be positive. If a denominator starts negative, first multiply both numerator and denominator by negative one.

### Equal Denominators

Let <InlineMath math="t=\sqrt[n]{a}>0" />. Since <InlineMath math="a^{m/n}=t^m" /> and <InlineMath math="a^{p/n}=t^p" />, we can apply the proven **integer-power rule**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} a^{m/n}a^{p/n} &=t^m t^p \\ &=t^{m+p} \\ &=a^{(m+p)/n} \end{aligned}" />

### Different Denominators

Take the **common root** <InlineMath math="t=\sqrt[nq]{a}>0" />. Since <InlineMath math="(t^q)^n=a" /> and <InlineMath math="(t^n)^q=a" />, the positive root <InlineMath math="\sqrt[n]{a}" /> equals <InlineMath math="t^q" />, and <InlineMath math="\sqrt[q]{a}" /> equals <InlineMath math="t^n" />. Therefore:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} a^{m/n}a^{p/q} &=(t^q)^m(t^n)^p \\ &=t^{mq}t^{np} \\ &=t^{mq+np} \\ &=a^{(mq+pn)/(nq)} \end{aligned}" />

## Using and Verifying Exponent Rules

The examples below apply the rules and then **check the result**. Each one names the rule used, so the steps can be retraced. Cover the answer, work the problem yourself, and use the named rule to find where the two solutions differ.

### Determining an Unknown Exponent

To find an exponent, write both sides with **the same positive base other than one**. Under this condition, different exponents produce different function values. The multiplication example between the two equations also checks the rule for adding exponents.

1. Determine <InlineMath math="p" /> in <InlineMath math="(3^4)^2=3^p" />.

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="(3^4)^2=3^{4\cdot2}=3^8" />
      <BlockMath math="p=8" />
    </MathContainer>

2. Verify <InlineMath math="b^4 b^5=b^9" /> for <InlineMath math="b\neq0" />.

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="b^4 b^5=b^{4+5}" />
      <BlockMath math="b^{4+5}=b^9" />
    </MathContainer>

    Both sides have the same base and exponent, so the identity is verified.

3. Determine <InlineMath math="p" /> in <InlineMath math="(3\pi)^p=27\pi^3" />.

    <MathContainer>
      <BlockMath math="27\pi^3=3^3\pi^3=(3\pi)^3" />
      <BlockMath math="p=3" />
    </MathContainer>

### Simplifying Expressions

Each expression below combines several exponent rules. Work from the **inside out**, and reduce the numbers and the variables separately so a mistake in one part does not hide in the other.

1. Simplify <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{2^4 3^6}{2^3 3^2}\right)^3" />.

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{2^4 3^6}{2^3 3^2}\right)^3 &=\left(2^{4-3}3^{6-2}\right)^3 \\ &=\left(2\cdot3^4\right)^3 \\ &=2^3 3^{12} \end{aligned}" />

2. Simplify <InlineMath math="(3u^3v^5)(9u^4v)" />.

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} (3u^3v^5)(9u^4v) &=27u^{3+4}v^{5+1} \\ &=27u^7v^6 \end{aligned}" />

3. For <InlineMath math="n\neq0" /> and <InlineMath math="r\neq0" />, simplify <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{n^{-1}r^4}{5n^{-6}r^{-4}}\right)^2" />.

    <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{n^{-1}r^4}{5n^{-6}r^{-4}}\right)^2 &=\left(\frac{n^{-1-(-6)}r^{4-(-4)}}5\right)^2 \\ &=\left(\frac{n^5r^8}{5}\right)^2 \\ &=\frac{n^{10}r^{16}}{25} \end{aligned}" />

## What Rationalizing Changes

Rationalizing a denominator rewrites a fraction in an equivalent form whose denominator contains no radical. The value does not change because the numerator and denominator are multiplied by the same nonzero expression.

For a two-term denominator, the key identity is:

<BlockMath math="(u+v)(u-v)=u^2-v^2" />

The expressions <InlineMath math="u+v" /> and <InlineMath math="u-v" /> are conjugates. Their product removes the middle terms.

### One Radical in the Denominator

For <InlineMath math="b>0" />, multiply by the factor <InlineMath math="\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}=1" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac a{\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}=\frac{a\sqrt b}{b}" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac5{\sqrt3}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}=\frac{5\sqrt3}{3}" />
</MathContainer>

The matching radical factor is a form of one. It is not called a conjugate because it has only one term.

### A Sum or Difference of Two Radicals

Assume <InlineMath math="a,b\geq0" /> and <InlineMath math="a\neq b" />. Multiply by the conjugate of the denominator:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac c{\sqrt a+\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{\sqrt a-\sqrt b}{\sqrt a-\sqrt b}=\frac{c(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)}{a-b}" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac c{\sqrt a-\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{\sqrt a+\sqrt b}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b}=\frac{c(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)}{a-b}" />
</MathContainer>

For example:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac4{\sqrt7+\sqrt3}=\frac{4(\sqrt7-\sqrt3)}{7-3}=\sqrt7-\sqrt3" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac2{\sqrt5-\sqrt2}=\frac{2(\sqrt5+\sqrt2)}{5-2}=\frac{2(\sqrt5+\sqrt2)}3" />
</MathContainer>

### A Rational Term and a Radical Term

Assume <InlineMath math="b\geq0" /> and <InlineMath math="a^2\neq b" />. The conjugate again creates a difference of squares:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac c{a+\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{a-\sqrt b}{a-\sqrt b}=\frac{c(a-\sqrt b)}{a^2-b}" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac c{a-\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{a+\sqrt b}{a+\sqrt b}=\frac{c(a+\sqrt b)}{a^2-b}" />
</MathContainer>

For example:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac6{4+\sqrt3}=\frac{6(4-\sqrt3)}{16-3}=\frac{6(4-\sqrt3)}{13}" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac3{2-\sqrt5}=\frac{3(2+\sqrt5)}{4-5}=-3(2+\sqrt5)" />
</MathContainer>

## Simplifying Radical and Exponent Expressions

Assume the variables in the following exercises are positive. This lets us use fractional exponent rules without introducing absolute-value cases.

1. Simplify:

   <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{8x^5y^{-4}}{16y^{-1/4}}\right)^{1/2}" />

2. Simplify:

   <BlockMath math="\left(5\sqrt{x^5}\right)\left(3\sqrt[3]x\right)" />

3. Simplify:

   <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{p^5q^{-10}}{p^5q^{-4}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{p^{1/4}q^{-1/2}}{p^{-1/2}q^{-1/2}}\right)^{1/2}" />

### Worked Solutions

1. Combine the coefficient and each exponent before taking the square root:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{8x^5y^{-4}}{16y^{-1/4}}\right)^{1/2}=\left(\frac12x^5y^{-15/4}\right)^{1/2}" />
     <BlockMath math="=2^{-1/2}x^{5/2}y^{-15/8}" />
     <BlockMath math="=\frac{x^{5/2}}{\sqrt2\,y^{15/8}}" />
   </MathContainer>

2. Convert both radicals to fractional exponents:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="\left(5\sqrt{x^5}\right)\left(3\sqrt[3]x\right)=15x^{5/2}x^{1/3}" />
     <BlockMath math="=15x^{15/6+2/6}=15x^{17/6}" />
     <BlockMath math="=15x^2\sqrt[6]{x^5}" />
   </MathContainer>

3. Simplify each parenthesis before multiplying:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="\left(q^{-6}\right)^{1/2}\left(p^{3/4}\right)^{1/2}=q^{-3}p^{3/8}" />
     <BlockMath math="=\frac{p^{3/8}}{q^3}" />
     <BlockMath math="=\frac{\sqrt[8]{p^3}}{q^3}" />
   </MathContainer>

## Rationalization Exercises

1. For <InlineMath math="b>0" />, rationalize:

   <BlockMath math="\frac2{\sqrt{b^3}}" />

2. Rationalize:

   <BlockMath math="\frac2{\sqrt3+\sqrt5}" />

3. For <InlineMath math="m\geq0" /> and <InlineMath math="m\neq n^2" />, rationalize:

   <BlockMath math="\frac m{\sqrt m+n}" />

### Worked Solutions

1. Because <InlineMath math="\sqrt{b^3}\sqrt b=\sqrt{b^4}=b^2" /> for <InlineMath math="b>0" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="\frac2{\sqrt{b^3}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}=\frac{2\sqrt b}{\sqrt{b^4}}" />
     <BlockMath math="=\frac{2\sqrt b}{b^2}" />
   </MathContainer>

2. Multiply by the conjugate:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="\frac2{\sqrt3+\sqrt5}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3-\sqrt5}{\sqrt3-\sqrt5}=\frac{2(\sqrt3-\sqrt5)}{3-5}" />
     <BlockMath math="=\sqrt5-\sqrt3" />
   </MathContainer>

3. The denominator's conjugate is <InlineMath math="\sqrt m-n" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="\frac m{\sqrt m+n}\cdot\frac{\sqrt m-n}{\sqrt m-n}=\frac{m(\sqrt m-n)}{m-n^2}" />
     <BlockMath math="=\frac{m\sqrt m-mn}{m-n^2}" />
   </MathContainer>

## How to Judge the Final Form

- The denominator should contain no radical.
- Every multiplying factor must equal one and must be defined under the stated conditions.
- An equivalent exact form is the goal. Rationalizing does not automatically make a decimal approximation more accurate.

These checks explain both the procedure and its limits, so the manipulation remains valid rather than becoming a pattern followed blindly.

## What Rationalizing Changes

Rationalizing a denominator rewrites a fraction in an equivalent form <Highlight>whose denominator contains no radical</Highlight>. The value does not change because the numerator and denominator are multiplied by the same nonzero expression.

For a two-term denominator, the key identity is:

<BlockMath math="(u+v)(u-v)=u^2-v^2" />

The expressions <InlineMath math="u+v" /> and <InlineMath math="u-v" /> are **conjugates**. Their product removes the middle terms.

### One Radical in the Denominator

For <InlineMath math="b>0" />, multiply by the factor <InlineMath math="\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}=1" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac a{\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}=\frac{a\sqrt b}{b}" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac5{\sqrt3}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}=\frac{5\sqrt3}{3}" />
</MathContainer>

The matching radical factor is **a form of one**. It is not called a conjugate because it has only one term.

Rationalizing <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2} = \tfrac{\sqrt 2}{2}" /> changes the form, never the length. The segment from a corner of the unit square to its center is half the diagonal, <InlineMath math="\tfrac{\sqrt 2}{2}" />, and that same segment also measures <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}" />.

### A Sum or Difference of Two Radicals

Assume <InlineMath math="a,b\geq0" /> and <InlineMath math="a\neq b" />. Multiply by the **conjugate of the denominator**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac c{\sqrt a+\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{\sqrt a-\sqrt b}{\sqrt a-\sqrt b} &=\frac{c(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)}{a-b} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac c{\sqrt a-\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{\sqrt a+\sqrt b}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b} &=\frac{c(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)}{a-b} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

For example:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac4{\sqrt7+\sqrt3} &amp;=\frac{4(\sqrt7-\sqrt3)}{7-3} \\ &amp;=\sqrt7-\sqrt3 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac2{\sqrt5-\sqrt2} &amp;=\frac{2(\sqrt5+\sqrt2)}{5-2} \\ &amp;=\frac{2(\sqrt5+\sqrt2)}3 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The conjugate works because of an area. A rectangle with sides <InlineMath math="\sqrt 3 + \sqrt 2" /> and <InlineMath math="\sqrt 3 - \sqrt 2" /> has area <InlineMath math="(\sqrt 3)^2 - (\sqrt 2)^2 = 3 - 2 = 1" />, exactly the unit square drawn beside it. Multiplying by the conjugate turns a sum of radicals into a whole number.

### A Rational Term and a Radical Term

Assume <InlineMath math="b\geq0" /> and <InlineMath math="a^2\neq b" />. The conjugate again creates a **difference of squares**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac c{a+\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{a-\sqrt b}{a-\sqrt b}=\frac{c(a-\sqrt b)}{a^2-b}" />
  <BlockMath math="\frac c{a-\sqrt b}\cdot\frac{a+\sqrt b}{a+\sqrt b}=\frac{c(a+\sqrt b)}{a^2-b}" />
</MathContainer>

For example:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\frac6{4+\sqrt3}=\frac{6(4-\sqrt3)}{16-3}=\frac{6(4-\sqrt3)}{13}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac3{2-\sqrt5} &amp;=\frac{3(2+\sqrt5)}{4-5} \\ &amp;=-3(2+\sqrt5) \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The conjugate turns <InlineMath math="(4 + \sqrt3)(4 - \sqrt3)" /> into <InlineMath math="4^2 - (\sqrt3)^2" />. As areas, that is a <InlineMath math="4" /> by <InlineMath math="4" /> square with a <InlineMath math="\sqrt3" /> by <InlineMath math="\sqrt3" /> square cut from its corner: <InlineMath math="16 - 3 = 13" /> square units remain, the denominator of <InlineMath math="\frac{6(4-\sqrt3)}{13}" />.

## Simplifying Radical and Exponent Expressions

Assume the variables in the following exercises are **positive**. This assumption avoids the absolute-value cases that can appear when using fractional exponents.

1. Simplify:

   <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{8x^5y^{-4}}{16y^{-1/4}}\right)^{1/2}" />

2. Simplify:

   <BlockMath math="\left(5\sqrt{x^5}\right)\left(3\sqrt[3]x\right)" />

3. Simplify:

   <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{p^5q^{-10}}{p^5q^{-4}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{p^{1/4}q^{-1/2}}{p^{-1/2}q^{-1/2}}\right)^{1/2}" />

### Solutions for Simplifying Radicals

Each solution below rewrites the radicand as a product of powers before taking the root. Combine the numeric coefficients first, then handle each variable exponent on its own, and move any negative exponent into the denominator.

1. **Combine coefficients and exponents** before taking the square root:

   <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{8x^5y^{-4}}{16y^{-1/4}}\right)^{1/2}&=\left(\frac12x^5y^{-15/4}\right)^{1/2} \\ &=2^{-1/2}x^{5/2}y^{-15/8} \\ &=\frac{x^{5/2}}{\sqrt2\,y^{15/8}} \end{aligned}" />

2. Convert both radicals to fractional exponents:

   <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \left(5\sqrt{x^5}\right)\left(3\sqrt[3]x\right)&=15x^{5/2}x^{1/3} \\ &=15x^{15/6+2/6}=15x^{17/6} \\ &=15x^2\sqrt[6]{x^5} \end{aligned}" />

3. Simplify **each fraction separately** before taking roots and multiplying:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="\frac{p^5q^{-10}}{p^5q^{-4}}=p^{5-5}q^{-10-(-4)}=q^{-6}" />
     <BlockMath math="\frac{p^{1/4}q^{-1/2}}{p^{-1/2}q^{-1/2}}=p^{1/4+1/2}q^{-1/2+1/2}=p^{3/4}" />
   </MathContainer>

   Take the square root of each result, then multiply:

   <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \left(q^{-6}\right)^{1/2}\left(p^{3/4}\right)^{1/2}&=q^{-3}p^{3/8} \\ &=\frac{p^{3/8}}{q^3} \\ &=\frac{\sqrt[8]{p^3}}{q^3} \end{aligned}" />

## Rationalization Exercises

The exercises below ask you to remove a radical from the denominator. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same expression so the **value of the fraction does not change**, and choose that expression so the resulting denominator becomes rational.

1. For <InlineMath math="b>0" />, rationalize:

   <BlockMath math="\frac2{\sqrt{b^3}}" />

2. Rationalize:

   <BlockMath math="\frac2{\sqrt3+\sqrt5}" />

3. For <InlineMath math="m\geq0" /> and <InlineMath math="m\neq n^2" />, rationalize:

   <BlockMath math="\frac m{\sqrt m+n}" />

### Solutions for Rationalizing Denominators

Each solution below multiplies by a **fraction equal to one**. Its numerator and denominator are the same radical or the same conjugate expression. Watch how the chosen factor clears the root without changing the value of the fraction.

1. Because <InlineMath math="\sqrt{b^3}\sqrt b=\sqrt{b^4}=b^2" /> for <InlineMath math="b>0" />:

   <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac2{\sqrt{b^3}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}&=\frac{2\sqrt b}{\sqrt{b^4}} \\ &=\frac{2\sqrt b}{b^2} \end{aligned}" />

2. Multiply by the conjugate:

   <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac2{\sqrt3+\sqrt5}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3-\sqrt5}{\sqrt3-\sqrt5} &=\frac{2(\sqrt3-\sqrt5)}{3-5} \\ &=\sqrt5-\sqrt3 \end{aligned}" />

3. The denominator's conjugate is <InlineMath math="\sqrt m-n" />:

   <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac m{\sqrt m+n}\cdot\frac{\sqrt m-n}{\sqrt m-n}&=\frac{m(\sqrt m-n)}{m-n^2} \\ &=\frac{m\sqrt m-mn}{m-n^2} \end{aligned}" />

## How to Judge the Final Form

- The denominator should contain no radical.
- Every **multiplying factor must equal one** and must be defined under the stated conditions.
- An equivalent exact form is the goal. Rationalizing does not automatically make a decimal approximation more accurate.

These checks show why each step is valid and when the procedure can be used.

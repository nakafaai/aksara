## A Simple Transmission Story

Imagine that one infected person passes the infection to exactly <InlineMath math="3" /> other people. In the next phase, each of those three people infects three more. For a clean calculation, suppose that no two people infect the same person, nobody recovers, and the factor of three never changes.

Now the pattern is easy to follow: every phase has three times as many newly infected people as the phase before it. Real outbreaks are messier. Contacts overlap, people recover, and transmission changes over time. The story isolates the exponential pattern. It does not predict an outbreak.

## New Infections in Each Phase

Each number below tells us how many people become infected **during that phase**. It is not the running total:

- **Phase** <InlineMath math="1" />: <InlineMath math="3=3^1" /> people newly infected
- **Phase** <InlineMath math="2" />: <InlineMath math="9=3^2" /> people newly infected
- **Phase** <InlineMath math="3" />: <InlineMath math="27=3^3" /> people newly infected
- **Phase** <InlineMath math="4" />: <InlineMath math="81=3^4" /> people newly infected
- **Phase** <InlineMath math="5" />: <InlineMath math="243=3^5" /> people newly infected

If <InlineMath math="N(x)" /> is the number of people newly infected in phase <InlineMath math="x" />, then:

<BlockMath math="N(x)=3^x" />

If we add the new infections from phase <InlineMath math="1" /> through phase <InlineMath math="x" />, we get this geometric sum:

<BlockMath math="S(x)=3+3^2+\cdots+3^x=\frac{3^{x+1}-3}{2}" />

The two formulas answer different questions. <InlineMath math="N(x)" /> describes one phase, while <InlineMath math="S(x)" /> adds every completed phase.

## Worked Questions

1. In this simple story, how many people become infected in phase <InlineMath math="20" />?

   <BlockMath math="N(20)=3^{20}=3{,}486{,}784{,}401" />

2. If we add phases <InlineMath math="1" /> through <InlineMath math="20" />, how many new infections occur during those phases?

   <BlockMath math="S(20)=\frac{3^{21}-3}{2}=5{,}230{,}176{,}600" />

3. Which part of the “multiply by three each phase” rule makes the exponential curve fit?

   The exponential model multiplies the previous phase by <InlineMath math="3" />. A linear model would add the same amount each phase. Multiplication by a constant factor and addition of a constant amount are different processes.

Phase <InlineMath math="20" /> alone contains <InlineMath math="3{,}486{,}784{,}401" /> newly infected people in our example. Adding phases <InlineMath math="1" /> through <InlineMath math="20" /> gives <InlineMath math="5{,}230{,}176{,}600" /> new infections across those twenty phases. That sum does not include the one person who was already infected before phase one began.

Those huge numbers come from our tidy assumptions: every newly infected person infects exactly three others, nobody is infected twice, and nobody has recovered yet. The pattern is useful for learning how exponential growth works, but it cannot tell us how many people a real outbreak will infect.

## Properties of Exponential Functions

For <InlineMath math="f(x)=a^x" /> with <InlineMath math="a>0" /> and <InlineMath math="a\neq1" />:

1. The domain is <InlineMath math="\mathbb{R}" /> and the range is <InlineMath math="(0,\infty)" />.
2. The graph passes through <InlineMath math="(0,1)" /> because <InlineMath math="a^0=1" />.
3. If <InlineMath math="a>1" />, the function is increasing.
4. If <InlineMath math="0<a<1" />, the function is decreasing.
5. For <InlineMath math="a>1" />, a larger value of <InlineMath math="a" /> gives more growth per interval. For <InlineMath math="0<a<1" />, a smaller value of <InlineMath math="a" /> gives more decay per interval.

## Where Exponential Models Appear

Exponential functions can model compound interest, radioactive decay, repeated doubling, and short phases of population or transmission change. Before using one, identify whether the process really applies a roughly constant **factor** over equal intervals. If the rate, resources, or interactions change, the model must also change.

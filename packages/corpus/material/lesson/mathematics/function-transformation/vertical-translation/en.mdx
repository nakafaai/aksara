## Lifting or Lowering the Whole Graph

A **vertical shift** by <InlineMath math="k" /> turns <InlineMath math="y = f(x)" /> into <InlineMath math="y = f(x) + k" />. Every point <InlineMath math="(x_0, y_0)" /> moves to <InlineMath math="(x_0, y_0 + k)" />, so **every x-coordinate stays the same**. The graph moves up when <InlineMath math="k > 0" /> and down when <InlineMath math="k < 0" />. The domain does not change, while the range, a horizontal asymptote, and the y-intercept all move by <InlineMath math="k" />. The first graph lifts the parabola <InlineMath math="x^2 - 4x + 3" /> by five units, and its vertex and its zeros respond in different ways.

<BlockMath math="(x_0, y_0) \longmapsto (x_0,\ y_0 + k)" />

Adding <InlineMath math="k" /> changes every height by the same amount, including the heights of points on the x-axis. That is why zeros do not simply move by <InlineMath math="k" />. A zero of <InlineMath math="f" /> solves <InlineMath math="f(x) = 0" />, but a zero of <InlineMath math="f(x) + k" /> solves <InlineMath math="f(x) = -k" />, which is a different equation.

## Zeros That Merge or Vanish

To find the zeros after a shift, **solve the new equation for zero**. Take the family <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2 - 4" /> and add a constant <InlineMath math="k" />. The zeros satisfy <InlineMath math="x^2 - 4 + k = 0" />, or <InlineMath math="x^2 = 4 - k" />. The number of solutions depends on <InlineMath math="k" />. For <InlineMath math="k = 0" /> there are two zeros, <InlineMath math="\pm 2" />. For <InlineMath math="k = 3" /> they are <InlineMath math="\pm 1" />, and for <InlineMath math="k = 4" /> the only zero is <InlineMath math="0" />, where the parabola touches the x-axis. For <InlineMath math="k = 5" /> the equation <InlineMath math="x^2 = -1" /> has no real solution.

## Mistakes When Adding a Constant

The table lists **five mistakes**, why each one fails, and how to catch it.

| Mistake | Why it fails | How to catch it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adding <InlineMath math="k" /> inside the bracket | <InlineMath math="f(x + k)" /> is a horizontal shift, which moves the graph sideways | A vertical shift changes only the second coordinate of each point |
| Changing the domain | Adding a constant to the output never makes an input allowed or forbidden | Compare the domain of <InlineMath math="f(x) + k" /> with the domain of <InlineMath math="f" /> |
| Moving the zeros by <InlineMath math="k" /> | The new zeros solve <InlineMath math="f(x) = -k" />, a different equation | Substitute an old zero into the new rule, which gives <InlineMath math="k" />, not zero |
| Keeping the asymptote at <InlineMath math="y = 0" /> | The asymptote moves with the graph by the same amount | Read the height that the new function approaches |
| Reading a negative <InlineMath math="k" /> as an upward shift | A negative constant lowers every point | Read the sign of <InlineMath math="k" /> as the direction of the move |

**Problem 2**. The shift is <InlineMath math="k = -7" />, so the new rule is <InlineMath math="3x + 2 - 7 = 3x - 5" />. The y-intercept is <InlineMath math="(0, -5)" />.

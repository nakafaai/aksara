## Mirroring a Graph Across the x-Axis

A **vertical reflection** across the x-axis turns <InlineMath math="y = f(x)" /> into <InlineMath math="y = -f(x)" />. Every point <InlineMath math="(x_0, y_0)" /> moves to <InlineMath math="(x_0, -y_0)" />, so **the x-coordinate stays the same** and every height changes sign. Zeros do not move, because their height is zero. A highest point becomes a lowest point. The first graph shows <InlineMath math="x^2 - 1" /> beside its mirror image <InlineMath math="-x^2 + 1" />, so you can follow one point across the x-axis.

<BlockMath math="(x_0, y_0) \longmapsto (x_0,\ -y_0)" />

The formula gives the same result. If the point <InlineMath math="(x_0, y_0)" /> lies on <InlineMath math="f" />, then <InlineMath math="y_0 = f(x_0)" />. The new function gives <InlineMath math="-f(x_0) = -y_0" /> at the same input, so the point <InlineMath math="(x_0, -y_0)" /> lies on <InlineMath math="-f(x)" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} g(x) &= -f(x) \\ &= -(x^2 - 2x - 3) \\ &= -x^2 + 2x + 3 \end{aligned}" />

## Ranges and Asymptotes That Flip

Take <InlineMath math="f(x) = 2^x - 1" />. Its horizontal asymptote is <InlineMath math="y = -1" />, its range is <InlineMath math="(-1, \infty)" />, and it passes through <InlineMath math="(0, 0)" />. Multiply the whole function by negative one:

<BlockMath math="g(x) = -f(x) = -2^x + 1" />

The asymptote moves to <InlineMath math="y = 1" />, because the height <InlineMath math="-1" /> becomes <InlineMath math="1" />. **The range flips** to <InlineMath math="(-\infty, 1)" />, since every value of <InlineMath math="f" /> above <InlineMath math="-1" /> becomes a value below <InlineMath math="1" />. The point <InlineMath math="(0, 0)" /> stays where it is, because it lies on the x-axis. ## Outside and Inside the Negative Sign

A negative sign outside the function and a negative sign inside it **do different jobs**. The factor outside, as in <InlineMath math="-f(x)" />, flips the heights. The sign inside, as in <InlineMath math="f(-x)" />, flips the inputs. Take <InlineMath math="f(x) = 2^x" /> and the point <InlineMath math="P(1, 2)" />. The vertical reflection <InlineMath math="-2^x" /> sends <InlineMath math="P" /> to <InlineMath math="(1, -2)" />, while the horizontal reflection <InlineMath math="2^{-x}" /> sends it to <InlineMath math="(-1, 2)" />. At <InlineMath math="x = 1" />, the two results differ: <InlineMath math="-f(1) = -2" /> and <InlineMath math="f(-1) = \tfrac{1}{2}" />.

Start each problem by **writing the whole output multiplied by negative one**. Then simplify, and check one point before you finish.

1. Let <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2 + 4x + 3" />. Find the equation of its vertical reflection and the vertex of the reflection.
2. Let <InlineMath math="g(x) = 3^x + 2" />. Find the equation of its vertical reflection, its range, and its asymptote.
3. Let <InlineMath math="h(x) = \sqrt{x + 1}" />. Find the equation of its vertical reflection, its y-intercept, and its range.
4. The graph of <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^3 - 3x" /> contains <InlineMath math="(-1, 2)" /> and <InlineMath math="(1, -2)" />. Find the images of these points under the vertical reflection, and show that <InlineMath math="f(-x) = -f(x)" />.

### Worked Solutions

**Problem 1**. **The whole output** is multiplied by negative one, so <InlineMath math="-f(x) = -x^2 - 4x - 3" />. The vertex of <InlineMath math="f" /> is at <InlineMath math="x = -2" />, where <InlineMath math="f(-2) = -1" />. The reflected vertex is the highest point <InlineMath math="(-2, 1)" />. Check: <InlineMath math="-f(-2) = 1" />.

**Problem 2**. **Every value** of <InlineMath math="g" /> is multiplied by negative one, so <InlineMath math="-g(x) = -3^x - 2" />. The range of <InlineMath math="g" /> is <InlineMath math="(2, \infty)" />, so the range of the reflection is <InlineMath math="(-\infty, -2)" />. The asymptote moves from <InlineMath math="y = 2" /> to <InlineMath math="y = -2" />.

**Problem 3**. **The height** of every point is multiplied by negative one, so <InlineMath math="-h(x) = -\sqrt{x + 1}" />. At <InlineMath math="x = 0" />, <InlineMath math="h(0) = 1" />, so the y-intercept becomes <InlineMath math="(0, -1)" />. The square root is never negative, so its reflection is never positive, and the range is <InlineMath math="(-\infty, 0]" />.

**Problem 4**. **The height** of <InlineMath math="(-1, 2)" /> becomes <InlineMath math="-2" />, so its image is <InlineMath math="(-1, -2)" />. The height of <InlineMath math="(1, -2)" /> becomes <InlineMath math="2" />, so its image is <InlineMath math="(1, 2)" />. For the identity, <InlineMath math="f(-x) = (-x)^3 - 3(-x) = -x^3 + 3x = -f(x)" />, so the two reflections give the same graph.

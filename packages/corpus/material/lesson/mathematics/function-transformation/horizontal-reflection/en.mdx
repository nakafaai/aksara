## Mirroring a Graph Across the y-Axis

A **horizontal reflection** across the y-axis turns <InlineMath math="y = f(x)" /> into <InlineMath math="y = f(-x)" />. Every point <InlineMath math="(x_0, y_0)" /> moves to <InlineMath math="(-x_0, y_0)" />, so **the height stays the same** and the graph swaps its left and right sides. Points that lie on the y-axis do not move at all. The first graph shows the parabola <InlineMath math="x^2 + 3x + 2" /> beside its mirror image, which is the graph of <InlineMath math="x^2 - 3x + 2" />, so you can compare the zeros on both sides.

<BlockMath math="(x_0, y_0) \longmapsto (-x_0,\ y_0)" />

The formula confirms the picture. The new function <InlineMath math="g(x) = f(-x)" /> returns <InlineMath math="f(x_0)" /> when its input is <InlineMath math="-x_0" />, because <InlineMath math="g(-x_0) = f(-(-x_0)) = f(x_0)" />. Each point of <InlineMath math="f" /> therefore has a partner at the same height on the opposite side of the y-axis.

## Turning the Plane Half a Turn

A reflection across the y-axis in three dimensions is the same as **a half turn of the plane** about the y-axis. Put the graph of <InlineMath math="y = \sqrt{x}" /> in the plane <InlineMath math="z = 0" />, and turn the plane around the y-axis. A point <InlineMath math="(x, y, 0)" /> travels along a half circle and comes to rest at <InlineMath math="(-x, y, 0)" />. Use the controls to turn the scene and follow the three paths drawn on it.

Each path keeps the same height the whole time, because the turn is around the vertical axis. That is why the mirror image of <InlineMath math="y = \sqrt{x}" /> is the graph of <InlineMath math="y = \sqrt{-x}" />. Points on the y-axis stay where they are, because a half turn about that axis leaves them fixed.

## Even and Odd Functions Under the Mirror

Some functions are unchanged by the mirror. A function is **even** when <InlineMath math="f(-x) = f(x)" />, as with <InlineMath math="x^2" /> and <InlineMath math="|x|" />. Its graph is symmetric about the y-axis, so each mirror image lies on the original graph. A function is **odd** when <InlineMath math="f(-x) = -f(x)" />, as with <InlineMath math="x^3 - x" />. For an odd function, the mirror image is the graph of <InlineMath math="-f(x)" />, so a horizontal reflection gives the same result as a vertical reflection.

## Domains That Change Sides

Let <InlineMath math="f(x) = \sqrt{x - 2}" />. Its domain is <InlineMath math="x \ge 2" />, so the graph starts at <InlineMath math="(2, 0)" />. After the reflection, <InlineMath math="g(x) = f(-x) = \sqrt{-x - 2}" />. The condition <InlineMath math="-x - 2 \ge 0" /> gives <InlineMath math="x \le -2" />, so **the new domain is** <InlineMath math="(-\infty, -2]" />, and the range stays <InlineMath math="[0, \infty)" />. The point <InlineMath math="(6, 2)" /> on <InlineMath math="f" /> moves to <InlineMath math="(-6, 2)" />, because <InlineMath math="g(-6) = \sqrt{6 - 2} = 2" />.

The absolute value function gives a second check. For <InlineMath math="f(x) = |x + 2|" />, the vertex is <InlineMath math="(-2, 0)" />. Its mirror function is <InlineMath math="f(-x) = |-x + 2| = |x - 2|" />, whose vertex is <InlineMath math="(2, 0)" />. The point <InlineMath math="(-4, 2)" /> on <InlineMath math="f" /> moves to <InlineMath math="(4, 2)" />.

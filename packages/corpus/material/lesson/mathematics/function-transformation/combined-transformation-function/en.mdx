## Applying Several Transformations One at a Time

Combining transformations means applying several transformations one at a time, with **each step acting on the result of the previous step**. For <InlineMath math="a \neq 0" /> and <InlineMath math="b \neq 0" />, the general form is:

<BlockMath math="g(x) = a\,f\big(b(x - h)\big) + k" />

Starting from the graph of <InlineMath math="f" />, stretch or compress horizontally by the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{b}" />, shift horizontally by <InlineMath math="h" />, stretch or compress vertically by the factor <InlineMath math="a" />, and then shift vertically by <InlineMath math="k" />. In the 3D view, the graph of <InlineMath math="y = x^2" /> is compressed and shifted horizontally in two different orders. Turn the view until you look down from above, and compare where the vertex of each order stops.

A shift and a stretch on the same axis give a different result when you swap them, unless the factor is <InlineMath math="1" /> or the shift is <InlineMath math="0" />, while **horizontal and vertical steps can be swapped**.

A negative <InlineMath math="a" /> also reflects the graph across the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, and a negative <InlineMath math="b" /> reflects it across the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis. For example, <InlineMath math="a = -2" /> stretches the graph vertically by the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> and also reflects it across the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis.

## Order on the Same Axis Changes the Result

Two vertical steps are enough to show the effect of order. The function <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2" /> gets a [vertical stretch](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-transformation/vertical-dilation) by the factor <InlineMath math="2" /> and a [vertical shift](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-transformation/vertical-translation) of <InlineMath math="1" /> unit up. Order A multiplies the output by <InlineMath math="2" /> first and then adds <InlineMath math="1" />. Order B adds <InlineMath math="1" /> first and then multiplies the result by <InlineMath math="2" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="g_A(x) = 2f(x) + 1 = 2x^2 + 1" />
<BlockMath math="g_B(x) = 2\big(f(x) + 1\big) = 2x^2 + 2" />
</MathContainer>

Track the point <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" /> on the graph of <InlineMath math="f" />. Order A moves that point to <InlineMath math="(1, 2)" /> and then to <InlineMath math="(1, 3)" />, and indeed <InlineMath math="g_A(1) = 3" />. Order B moves it to <InlineMath math="(1, 2)" /> and then to <InlineMath math="(1, 4)" />, and <InlineMath math="g_B(1) = 4" />. The graphs differ because in order B **the stretch also doubles the shift** that has already happened:

<BlockMath math="a\big(f(x) + k\big) = a\,f(x) + ak" />

So for a stretch by the factor <InlineMath math="a" /> followed by a shift of <InlineMath math="k" />, write <InlineMath math="a\,f(x) + k" />. If the shift comes first, the final shift becomes <InlineMath math="ak" />.

## Compress First or Shift First

On the horizontal axis, order decides the result as well. Start again from <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2" />. A [horizontal compression](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-transformation/horizontal-dilation) by the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" /> squeezes the graph toward the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis by replacing <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="2x" />. A [horizontal shift](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-transformation/horizontal-translation) of <InlineMath math="3" /> units to the right replaces <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="x - 3" />. <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y &= f(2x) = (2x)^2 \\ y &= f\big(2(x - 3)\big) = \big(2(x - 3)\big)^2 \end{aligned}" />

## Reading the Order from the Equation

When an equation already has the form <InlineMath math="y = a\,f\big(b(x - h)\big) + k" />, you can read the order of the steps directly. An equation such as <InlineMath math="y = f(2x - 6)" /> is not in that form yet, so **factor the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> out of the parentheses first**:

<BlockMath math="f(2x - 6) = f\big(2(x - 3)\big)" />

The right side reads as a compression by the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" /> followed by a shift of <InlineMath math="3" /> units to the right. The same equation can also be read in the reverse order, as a shift of <InlineMath math="6" /> units to the right followed by the compression, because a compression after the shift halves the shift of <InlineMath math="6" /> to <InlineMath math="3" />. Both readings are correct as long as the size of the shift matches the order.

Each basic step changes the equation and the points in a fixed way.

| Step | New equation | The point <InlineMath math="(x_0, y_0)" /> moves to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Horizontal stretch or compression by the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{b}" /> | <InlineMath math="y = f(bx)" /> | <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac{x_0}{b}, y_0\right)" /> |
| Horizontal shift by <InlineMath math="h" /> | <InlineMath math="y = f(x - h)" /> | <InlineMath math="(x_0 + h, y_0)" /> |
| Vertical stretch or compression by the factor <InlineMath math="a" /> | <InlineMath math="y = a\,f(x)" /> | <InlineMath math="(x_0, a y_0)" /> |
| Vertical shift by <InlineMath math="k" /> | <InlineMath math="y = f(x) + k" /> | <InlineMath math="(x_0, y_0 + k)" /> |

When the four steps run in the order of the general form, every point <InlineMath math="(x_0, y_0)" /> on the graph of <InlineMath math="f" /> ends up at:

<BlockMath math="(x_0, y_0) \longmapsto \left(\frac{x_0}{b} + h,\ a y_0 + k\right)" />

The order of the steps shows in that formula. The value <InlineMath math="x_0" /> is divided by <InlineMath math="b" /> first and then <InlineMath math="h" /> is added, while <InlineMath math="y_0" /> is multiplied by <InlineMath math="a" /> first and then <InlineMath math="k" /> is added.

As an example, the function <InlineMath math="g(x) = 2\sqrt{2x + 6} - 1" /> is broken into steps from <InlineMath math="f(x) = \sqrt{x}" />. The coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> comes out of the radicand first:

<BlockMath math="g(x) = 2\sqrt{2(x + 3)} - 1 = 2f\big(2(x + 3)\big) - 1" />

So <InlineMath math="a = 2" />, <InlineMath math="b = 2" />, <InlineMath math="h = -3" />, and <InlineMath math="k = -1" />. The graph of <InlineMath math="y = \sqrt{x}" /> is compressed horizontally by the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" />, shifted <InlineMath math="3" /> units to the left, stretched vertically by the factor <InlineMath math="2" />, and shifted <InlineMath math="1" /> unit down. The point formula moves the starting point of the curve and the point <InlineMath math="(4, 2)" />:

<BlockMath math="(4, 2) \longmapsto \left(\tfrac{4}{2} - 3,\ 2 \cdot 2 - 1\right) = (-1, 3)" />
</MathContainer>

## Horizontal and Vertical Steps Can Be Swapped

A horizontal step only changes the <InlineMath math="x" />-coordinate of each point, and a vertical step only changes the <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinate. Because they change different coordinates, **horizontal and vertical steps can be done in either order**. Shift <InlineMath math="y = x^2" /> by <InlineMath math="1" /> unit to the right and then <InlineMath math="2" /> units up, and you get <InlineMath math="y = (x - 1)^2 + 2" />. If you shift <InlineMath math="2" /> units up first, you get <InlineMath math="y = x^2 + 2" />, and the shift to the right turns <InlineMath math="x^2 + 2" /> into <InlineMath math="(x - 1)^2 + 2" /> as well.

Two reflections can also be swapped. A [reflection across the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-transformation/vertical-reflection) changes the sign of the output, while a [reflection across the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-transformation/horizontal-reflection) changes the sign of the input. In either order, the two reflections send every point to the point with both coordinates negated:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="(x, y) \longmapsto (x, -y) \longmapsto (-x, -y)" />
<BlockMath math="y = f(x) \longmapsto y = -f(x) \longmapsto y = -f(-x)" />
</MathContainer>

Together, the two reflections form a [rotation](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-transformation/rotation) by <InlineMath math="180^\circ" /> about the origin, because every point <InlineMath math="(x, y)" /> moves to <InlineMath math="(-x, -y)" />.

In algebraic terms, the general form <InlineMath math="a\,f\big(b(x - h)\big) + k" /> is a [function composition](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-composition-inverse-function/function-composition). The inner function <InlineMath math="B" /> processes the input before <InlineMath math="f" /> acts, and the outer function <InlineMath math="A" /> processes the output of <InlineMath math="f" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} g(x) &= A\big(f(B(x))\big) \\ B(x) &= b(x - h) \\ A(u) &= au + k \end{aligned}" />

Because <InlineMath math="B" /> acts on the input, points of the graph move horizontally by the inverse of <InlineMath math="B" />, which is <InlineMath math="x_0 \mapsto \tfrac{x_0}{b} + h" />. That inverse divides by <InlineMath math="b" /> first and adds <InlineMath math="h" /> afterward. This is why **the steps inside the parentheses run in the reverse of their written order**: <InlineMath math="b(x - h)" /> is written with the subtraction of <InlineMath math="h" /> first, but the graph is stretched or compressed horizontally by the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{b}" /> first.

## Building an Equation from a Description

When the transformations are given in words, **write one intermediate function for each step**. For example, the graph of <InlineMath math="f(x) = |x|" /> is shifted <InlineMath math="2" /> units to the left, reflected across the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis, stretched vertically by the factor <InlineMath math="3" />, and then shifted <InlineMath math="4" /> units up. Each step acts on the previous intermediate function:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="f_1(x) = f(x + 2) = |x + 2|" />
<BlockMath math="f_2(x) = -f_1(x) = -|x + 2|" />
<BlockMath math="f_3(x) = 3f_2(x) = -3|x + 2|" />
<BlockMath math="g(x) = f_3(x) + 4 = -3|x + 2| + 4" />
</MathContainer>

Track the point <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" /> on the graph of <InlineMath math="f" />. Step by step, it moves to <InlineMath math="(-1, 1)" />, <InlineMath math="(-1, -1)" />, <InlineMath math="(-1, -3)" />, and finally <InlineMath math="(-1, 1)" />. The final equation gives <InlineMath math="g(-1) = -3|1| + 4 = 1" />, which matches the last point. The vertex <InlineMath math="(0, 0)" /> ends up at <InlineMath math="(-2, 4)" />, and <InlineMath math="g(-2) = 4" />.

The order of the words decides the equation. If the shift of <InlineMath math="4" /> units up happens before the reflection, **the later reflection also flips that shift**:

<BlockMath math="-3\big(|x + 2| + 4\big) = -3|x + 2| - 12" />

The vertex then lands at <InlineMath math="(-2, -12)" />, far from the point <InlineMath math="(-2, 4)" /> that the description asked for.

## Mistakes When Ordering the Steps

A stretch, a compression, or a reflection acts on the whole result of the steps before it, so **a shift done earlier on the same axis changes too**. To catch an ordering mistake, **track one point through every step**. After each step, the point's new position must satisfy that step's intermediate function, and its last position must satisfy the final equation.

| Mistake | Why it fails | How to catch it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading <InlineMath math="f(2x - 3)" /> as a shift of <InlineMath math="3" /> units | A compression after the shift halves that shift | Write <InlineMath math="f\big(2(x - \tfrac{3}{2})\big)" />, or solve <InlineMath math="2x - 3 = 0" /> |
| Shifting before multiplying in <InlineMath math="a\,f(x) + k" /> | The shift is multiplied by <InlineMath math="a" />, because <InlineMath math="a\big(f(x) + k\big) = a\,f(x) + ak" /> | Track the point <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" /> on <InlineMath math="y = x^2" /> |
| Forgetting that a reflection flips an earlier shift | <InlineMath math="-\big(f(x) + k\big) = -f(x) - k" /> | Track the vertex through every step |
| Writing the final equation directly | A swapped step cannot be checked on its own | Write <InlineMath math="f_1" />, <InlineMath math="f_2" />, and so on, then test one point |

## Exercises

In problems 1 and 2 you build an equation from steps described in words. Problems 3 and 4 go the other way: you read the steps from an equation and use them to find a vertex and two domains. Write the intermediate functions or the order of steps first, and **test your result with one point** before you read the worked solutions.

1. The function <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2" /> is shifted <InlineMath math="3" /> units up and then compressed vertically by the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" />. Find the equation of the resulting function.

2. The function <InlineMath math="f(x) = 2^x" /> is reflected across the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis and then shifted <InlineMath math="1" /> unit to the right. Find the equation of the resulting function.

3. Break <InlineMath math="r(x) = (3x + 6)^2 - 4" /> into steps from <InlineMath math="f(x) = x^2" />, then find the vertex of the graph of <InlineMath math="r" />.

4. Find the domains of <InlineMath math="p(x) = \sqrt{2x - 4}" /> and <InlineMath math="q(x) = \sqrt{2(x - 4)}" />. Explain the difference between the two domains with the order of steps from <InlineMath math="f(x) = \sqrt{x}" />.

### Worked Solutions

**Problem 1**. The first step adds <InlineMath math="3" /> to the output, and the second step **multiplies that whole result** by <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="f_1(x) = x^2 + 3" />
<BlockMath math="g(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}f_1(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}(x^2 + 3) = \tfrac{1}{2}x^2 + \tfrac{3}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

The shift of <InlineMath math="3" /> is also multiplied by <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{2}" />, because the compression comes after it. Track the point <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" />: it moves to <InlineMath math="(1, 4)" /> and then to <InlineMath math="(1, 2)" />, and <InlineMath math="g(1) = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{3}{2} = 2" />. So <InlineMath math="g(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}x^2 + \tfrac{3}{2}" />.

**Problem 2**. The reflection across the <InlineMath math="y" />-axis replaces <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="-x" />. The shift to the right then **replaces every <InlineMath math="x" /> with <InlineMath math="x - 1" />** in that result:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="f_1(x) = 2^{-x}" />
<BlockMath math="g(x) = f_1(x - 1) = 2^{-(x - 1)} = 2^{1 - x}" />
</MathContainer>

The exponent <InlineMath math="-x" /> becomes <InlineMath math="-(x - 1)" />, so the result is <InlineMath math="2^{1 - x}" />, and the form <InlineMath math="2^{-x - 1}" /> is wrong. Track the point <InlineMath math="(1, 2)" /> on the graph of <InlineMath math="f" />: the reflection moves that point to <InlineMath math="(-1, 2)" />, the shift moves it to <InlineMath math="(0, 2)" />, and <InlineMath math="g(0) = 2^1 = 2" />. So <InlineMath math="g(x) = 2^{1 - x}" />.

**Problem 3**. Factor the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> out of the parentheses so that you can read the general form:

<BlockMath math="r(x) = (3x + 6)^2 - 4 = \big(3(x + 2)\big)^2 - 4 = f\big(3(x + 2)\big) - 4" />

So <InlineMath math="b = 3" />, <InlineMath math="h = -2" />, and <InlineMath math="k = -4" />. The graph of <InlineMath math="y = x^2" /> is compressed horizontally by the factor <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{3}" />, shifted <InlineMath math="2" /> units to the left, and shifted <InlineMath math="4" /> units down. The vertex <InlineMath math="(0, 0)" /> stays put during the compression, moves to <InlineMath math="(-2, 0)" />, and ends at **the vertex <InlineMath math="(-2, -4)" />**. Check: <InlineMath math="r(-2) = (-6 + 6)^2 - 4 = -4" />. The point <InlineMath math="(1, 1)" /> also fits, because it moves to <InlineMath math="\left(\tfrac{1}{3}, 1\right)" />, <InlineMath math="\left(-\tfrac{5}{3}, 1\right)" />, and then <InlineMath math="\left(-\tfrac{5}{3}, -3\right)" />, and <InlineMath math="r\left(-\tfrac{5}{3}\right) = (-5 + 6)^2 - 4 = -3" />.

**Problem 4**. Factor the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> out of the radicand, <InlineMath math="p(x) = \sqrt{2x - 4} = \sqrt{2(x - 2)}" />.

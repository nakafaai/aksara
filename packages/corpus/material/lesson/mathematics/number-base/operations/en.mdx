## Carrying When a Spike Fills

You can add and subtract directly in base <InlineMath math="b" />, without converting to base ten first. It works like the column addition you already know, only the limit changes: **carry one to the left whenever a column reaches <InlineMath math="b" />**, not ten. In subtraction, one borrowed from the column on the left is worth <InlineMath math="b" />. Slide the base <InlineMath math="b" /> and the column total <InlineMath math="s" /> below to see when a column carries <InlineMath math="1" /> and what digit stays behind.

Start at base <InlineMath math="5" /> with a column total of <InlineMath math="7" />. ## Column Addition in Any Base

Column addition uses this carry rule in every column. Add each column from the right, including any carry from the column before. If the total is less than <InlineMath math="b" />, write it down. If it's <InlineMath math="b" /> or more, **write the total minus <InlineMath math="b" /> and carry <InlineMath math="1" />**. The carry is never more than <InlineMath math="1" />, because one column of two numbers holds at most <InlineMath math="(b - 1) + (b - 1) + 1 = 2b - 1" />.

### A Chain of Carries

Work out <InlineMath math="4675_8 + 2307_8" />. Every digit here is in base eight, so each column's limit is <InlineMath math="8" />. The small row on top records the carry going into each column.

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{rcccc} & {\scriptstyle 1} & {\scriptstyle 1} & {\scriptstyle 1} & \\ & 4 & 6 & 7 & 5 \\ + & 2 & 3 & 0 & 7 \\ \hline & 7 & 2 & 0 & 4 \end{array}" />

| Column | Column total | In base eight | Write | Carry |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ones | <InlineMath math="5 + 7 = 12" /> | <InlineMath math="12 = 1 \times 8 + 4" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| Eights | <InlineMath math="7 + 0 + 1 = 8" /> | <InlineMath math="8 = 1 \times 8 + 0" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| The <InlineMath math="8^2" /> column | <InlineMath math="6 + 3 + 1 = 10" /> | <InlineMath math="10 = 1 \times 8 + 2" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| The <InlineMath math="8^3" /> column | <InlineMath math="4 + 2 + 1 = 7" /> | <InlineMath math="7 < 8" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |

Look at the eights column. Without a carry, <InlineMath math="7 + 0" /> wouldn't need to carry anything. The carry from the ones makes that column exactly <InlineMath math="8" />, so it carries as well. One carry **runs across two places**, just like a full abacus spike sending a bead to a spike that's almost full. The result is <InlineMath math="4675_8 + 2307_8 = 7204_8" />.

Base ten habits sneak in most often in the ones column. A total of <InlineMath math="12" /> tempts you to write <InlineMath math="2" /> and carry <InlineMath math="1" />, but here the limit is <InlineMath math="8" />, and <InlineMath math="12 = 1 \times 8 + 4" />, so you write <InlineMath math="4" />. A quick test catches this slip: **every digit of the answer must be smaller than the base**.

### A Check in Base Ten

Convert all three numbers to base ten and see whether the sum matches.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 4675_8 &= 4 \times 512 + 6 \times 64 + 7 \times 8 + 5 = 2{,}493 \\ 2307_8 &= 2 \times 512 + 3 \times 64 + 0 \times 8 + 7 = 1{,}223 \\ 7204_8 &= 7 \times 512 + 2 \times 64 + 0 \times 8 + 4 = 3{,}716 \end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="2{,}493 + 1{,}223 = 3{,}716" />, **the base eight sum is right**. This check takes longer than the addition itself, but it catches a forgotten carry, because a lost carry makes the result short by a whole place value.

Try one in base five, where carries come quickly: work out <InlineMath math="2434_5 + 1213_5" />. Add the columns from the right with a limit of <InlineMath math="5" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{rcccc} & {\scriptstyle 1} & {\scriptstyle 1} & {\scriptstyle 1} & \\ & 2 & 4 & 3 & 4 \\ + & 1 & 2 & 1 & 3 \\ \hline & 4 & 2 & 0 & 2 \end{array}" />

Ones: <InlineMath math="4 + 3 = 7 = 12_5" />, write <InlineMath math="2" /> carry <InlineMath math="1" />. Fives: <InlineMath math="3 + 1 + 1 = 5 = 10_5" />, write <InlineMath math="0" /> carry <InlineMath math="1" />. Third column: <InlineMath math="4 + 2 + 1 = 7 = 12_5" />, write <InlineMath math="2" /> carry <InlineMath math="1" />. Fourth column: <InlineMath math="2 + 1 + 1 = 4" />. So **<InlineMath math="2434_5 + 1213_5 = 4202_5" />**. Check through base ten: <InlineMath math="369 + 183 = 552" />, and <InlineMath math="4202_5 = 500 + 50 + 0 + 2 = 552" />.

## Borrowing in Subtraction

Subtraction runs the abacus the other way. If the top digit of a column is smaller than the bottom one, **take one bead from the spike on the left and trade it for <InlineMath math="b" /> beads** in that column. Work out <InlineMath math="1302_5 - 244_5" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{rcccc} & 1 & 3 & 0 & 2 \\ - & & 2 & 4 & 4 \\ \hline & 1 & 0 & 0 & 3 \end{array}" />

The ones column asks for <InlineMath math="2 - 4" />, so it has to borrow. The fives spike is empty, so the borrow comes from the third spike: its <InlineMath math="3" /> drops to <InlineMath math="2" />, and the fives spike gets five beads. The fives spike then lends one bead to the ones. Now the fives hold <InlineMath math="4" /> and the ones hold <InlineMath math="2 + 5 = 7" />.

| Column | After borrowing | Subtraction | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ones | <InlineMath math="2 + 5 = 7" /> | <InlineMath math="7 - 4" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |
| Fives | <InlineMath math="0 + 5 - 1 = 4" /> | <InlineMath math="4 - 4" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| The <InlineMath math="5^2" /> column | <InlineMath math="3 - 1 = 2" /> | <InlineMath math="2 - 2" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| The <InlineMath math="5^3" /> column | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="1 - 0" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |

So <InlineMath math="1302_5 - 244_5 = 1003_5" />. Check by adding back in base five: <InlineMath math="1003_5 + 244_5" /> gives ones <InlineMath math="3 + 4 = 7 = 12_5" />, fives <InlineMath math="0 + 4 + 1 = 5 = 10_5" />, third column <InlineMath math="0 + 2 + 1 = 3" /> and fourth column <InlineMath math="1" />, which is <InlineMath math="1302_5" /> again. Base ten agrees too: <InlineMath math="202 - 74 = 128 = 1003_5" />.

Two slips show up here. If a borrow is counted as ten, the ones become <InlineMath math="12 - 4 = 8" />, a digit that doesn't exist in base five: **a borrow is always worth <InlineMath math="b" />**. If the digit that lends isn't reduced, you get <InlineMath math="1103_5" />, which is worth <InlineMath math="153" /> instead of <InlineMath math="128" />. Adding the answer back to the number you subtracted catches both, because you stay in the same base the whole time.

Now a subtraction with two borrows in base eight: work out <InlineMath math="5032_8 - 1647_8" />. Subtract the columns from the right, and every borrow is worth <InlineMath math="8" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{rcccc} & 5 & 0 & 3 & 2 \\ - & 1 & 6 & 4 & 7 \\ \hline & 3 & 1 & 6 & 3 \end{array}" />

Ones: <InlineMath math="2 < 7" />, so borrow from the eights, giving <InlineMath math="2 + 8 - 7 = 3" />, and the eights column drops to <InlineMath math="2" />. Eights: <InlineMath math="2 < 4" />, but the <InlineMath math="8^2" /> column is empty, so borrow from the <InlineMath math="8^3" /> column. Its <InlineMath math="5" /> becomes <InlineMath math="4" />, the <InlineMath math="8^2" /> column becomes <InlineMath math="8 - 1 = 7" />, and the eights become <InlineMath math="2 + 8 - 4 = 6" />. The <InlineMath math="8^2" /> column: <InlineMath math="7 - 6 = 1" />. The <InlineMath math="8^3" /> column: <InlineMath math="4 - 1 = 3" />. So **<InlineMath math="5032_8 - 1647_8 = 3163_8" />**. Check through base ten: <InlineMath math="2{,}586 - 935 = 1{,}651" />, and <InlineMath math="3163_8 = 1{,}536 + 64 + 48 + 3 = 1{,}651" />.

## Finding a Hidden Base

Sometimes a problem doesn't give the base and asks you to find it. Write the numeral in expanded form with the letter <InlineMath math="b" /> as the base, then solve the equation. Take <InlineMath math="123_b = 38" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 1 \times b^2 + 2 \times b + 3 &= 38 \\ b^2 + 2b - 35 &= 0 \\ (b + 7)(b - 5) &= 0 \end{aligned}" />

You solve this quadratic by [factoring a quadratic equation](/en/subjects/mathematics/quadratic-function/quadratic-equation-factorization), and it has two roots: <InlineMath math="b = -7" /> and <InlineMath math="b = 5" />. Only one can be a base. **A base must be a whole number bigger than the largest digit**, which is <InlineMath math="3" /> here, so <InlineMath math="b = 5" />. So before you accept a root as a base, **compare it with the largest digit**. A hidden base can also sit inside a sum. Given <InlineMath math="45_b + 36_b = 103_b" />, write each numeral in expanded form with base <InlineMath math="b" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} (4b + 5) + (3b + 6) &= b^2 + 0 \times b + 3 \\ 7b + 11 &= b^2 + 3 \\ b^2 - 7b - 8 &= 0 \\ (b - 8)(b + 1) &= 0 \end{aligned}" />

The roots are <InlineMath math="b = 8" /> and <InlineMath math="b = -1" />. The base must be bigger than the largest digit, <InlineMath math="6" />, so <InlineMath math="b = 8" />. Check by adding in base eight: ones <InlineMath math="5 + 6 = 11 = 13_8" />, write <InlineMath math="3" /> carry <InlineMath math="1" />, then <InlineMath math="4 + 3 + 1 = 8 = 10_8" />. The result is <InlineMath math="103_8" />, the same as the right-hand side.

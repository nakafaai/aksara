## Carrying When a Spike Fills

You can add and subtract directly in base <InlineMath math="b" />, without converting to base ten first. It works like the column addition you already know, only the limit changes: **carry one to the left whenever a column reaches <InlineMath math="b" />**, not ten.

In subtraction, one borrowed from the column on the left is worth <InlineMath math="b" />. Slide the base <InlineMath math="b" /> and column total <InlineMath math="s" /> below to see when a column carries <InlineMath math="1" /> and what digit stays behind.

Start at base <InlineMath math="5" /> with a column total of <InlineMath math="7" />. The point sits inside the shaded carry band above <InlineMath math="s = b" />, so the column sends <InlineMath math="1" /> to the left and writes <InlineMath math="7 - 5 = 2" />.

Slide the base up to <InlineMath math="8" /> without changing the total. The same <InlineMath math="7" /> drops below <InlineMath math="s = b" />, so no carry happens and you write <InlineMath math="7" /> straight down. **The base sets the exact wall where carrying begins**.

> In any base, every carry sends one full group to the left, and every borrow breaks one left group into <InlineMath math="b" /> units.

## Column Addition in Any Base

Column addition applies this carry rule to every column. Add from the right including any incoming carry: if the total is under <InlineMath math="b" />, write it down. If it reaches <InlineMath math="b" /> or more, **write the total minus <InlineMath math="b" /> and carry <InlineMath math="1" />**.

When adding two numbers, the carry is never more than <InlineMath math="1" />, because the largest possible column total stays below <InlineMath math="2b" />.

<BlockMath math="(b - 1) + (b - 1) + 1 = 2b - 1" />

### A Chain of Carries

Work out <InlineMath math="4675_8 + 2307_8" />. Every digit here is in base eight, so each column's limit is <InlineMath math="8" />. The small row on top records the carry going into each column.

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{rcccc} & {\scriptstyle 1} & {\scriptstyle 1} & {\scriptstyle 1} & \\ & 4 & 6 & 7 & 5 \\ + & 2 & 3 & 0 & 7 \\ \hline & 7 & 2 & 0 & 4 \end{array}" />

| Column | Column total | In base eight | Write | Carry |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ones | <InlineMath math="5 + 7 = 12" /> | <InlineMath math="12 = 1 \times 8 + 4" /> | <InlineMath math="4" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| Eights | <InlineMath math="7 + 0 + 1 = 8" /> | <InlineMath math="8 = 1 \times 8 + 0" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| The <InlineMath math="8^2" /> column | <InlineMath math="6 + 3 + 1 = 10" /> | <InlineMath math="10 = 1 \times 8 + 2" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| The <InlineMath math="8^3" /> column | <InlineMath math="4 + 2 + 1 = 7" /> | <InlineMath math="7 < 8" /> | <InlineMath math="7" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |

Look at the eights column. Without a carry, <InlineMath math="7 + 0" /> wouldn't carry anything. The carry from the ones makes that column exactly <InlineMath math="8" />, so one carry **runs across two places** and gives <InlineMath math="7204_8" />.

Base ten habits sneak in most often in the ones column. A total of <InlineMath math="12" /> tempts you to write <InlineMath math="2" />, but here the limit is <InlineMath math="8" />, so you write <InlineMath math="4" />.

<BlockMath math="12 = 1 \times 8 + 4" />

A quick test catches this slip: **every digit of the answer must be smaller than the base**.

### A Check in Base Ten

Convert all three numbers to base ten and see whether the sum matches.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 4675_8 &= 4 \times 512 + 6 \times 64 + 7 \times 8 + 5 = 2{,}493 \\ 2307_8 &= 2 \times 512 + 3 \times 64 + 0 \times 8 + 7 = 1{,}223 \\ 7204_8 &= 7 \times 512 + 2 \times 64 + 0 \times 8 + 4 = 3{,}716 \end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="2{,}493 + 1{,}223 = 3{,}716" />, **the base eight sum is right**. This check catches a forgotten carry, because a lost carry makes the result short by a whole place value.

Try one in base five, where carries come quickly: work out <InlineMath math="2434_5 + 1213_5" />. Add the columns from the right with a limit of <InlineMath math="5" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{rcccc} & {\scriptstyle 1} & {\scriptstyle 1} & {\scriptstyle 1} & \\ & 2 & 4 & 3 & 4 \\ + & 1 & 2 & 1 & 3 \\ \hline & 4 & 2 & 0 & 2 \end{array}" />

Record each column from right to left, then verify through base ten.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 4 + 3 &= 7 = 12_5 & &\text{(write } 2\text{, carry } 1\text{)} \\ 3 + 1 + 1 &= 5 = 10_5 & &\text{(write } 0\text{, carry } 1\text{)} \\ 4 + 2 + 1 &= 7 = 12_5 & &\text{(write } 2\text{, carry } 1\text{)} \\ 2 + 1 + 1 &= 4 \end{aligned}" />

So **<InlineMath math="2434_5 + 1213_5 = 4202_5" />**. Base ten agrees: <InlineMath math="369 + 183 = 552" />.

<BlockMath math="4202_5 = 500 + 50 + 0 + 2 = 552" />

## Borrowing in Subtraction

Subtraction runs the abacus the other way. If the top digit of a column is smaller than the bottom one, **take one bead from the spike on the left and trade it for <InlineMath math="b" /> beads** in that column. Work out <InlineMath math="1302_5 - 244_5" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{rcccc} & 1 & 3 & 0 & 2 \\ - & & 2 & 4 & 4 \\ \hline & 1 & 0 & 0 & 3 \end{array}" />

The ones column asks for <InlineMath math="2 - 4" />, so it must borrow. The fives spike is empty, so the borrow comes from the third spike: <InlineMath math="3" /> drops to <InlineMath math="2" />, giving the fives spike five beads. It lends one to the ones, leaving <InlineMath math="4" /> fives and <InlineMath math="2 + 5 = 7" /> ones.

| Column | After borrowing | Subtraction | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ones | <InlineMath math="2 + 5 = 7" /> | <InlineMath math="7 - 4" /> | <InlineMath math="3" /> |
| Fives | <InlineMath math="0 + 5 - 1 = 4" /> | <InlineMath math="4 - 4" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| The <InlineMath math="5^2" /> column | <InlineMath math="3 - 1 = 2" /> | <InlineMath math="2 - 2" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| The <InlineMath math="5^3" /> column | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="1 - 0" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |

So <InlineMath math="1302_5 - 244_5 = 1003_5" />. Check by adding <InlineMath math="1003_5 + 244_5" /> back in base five, and confirm through base ten.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 1003_5 + 244_5 &= 1302_5 \\ 202 - 74 &= 128 = 1003_5 \end{aligned}" />

Two slips show up here. Counting a borrow as ten makes the ones <InlineMath math="12 - 4 = 8" />, a digit that doesn't exist in base five: **a borrow is always worth <InlineMath math="b" />**.

Forgetting to reduce the lending digit gives <InlineMath math="1103_5" /> (worth <InlineMath math="153" /> instead of <InlineMath math="128" />). Adding the answer back to the number you subtracted catches both slips at once.

Now a subtraction with two borrows in base eight: work out <InlineMath math="5032_8 - 1647_8" />. Subtract the columns from the right, and every borrow is worth <InlineMath math="8" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{array}{rcccc} & 5 & 0 & 3 & 2 \\ - & 1 & 6 & 4 & 7 \\ \hline & 3 & 1 & 6 & 3 \end{array}" />

In the ones column, <InlineMath math="2 < 7" />, so borrow from the eights, leaving <InlineMath math="2" /> there. Since <InlineMath math="2 < 4" /> and the <InlineMath math="8^2" /> column is empty, borrow from the <InlineMath math="8^3" /> column so <InlineMath math="5" /> drops to <InlineMath math="4" /> and the <InlineMath math="8^2" /> column keeps <InlineMath math="7" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 2 + 8 - 7 &= 3 & &\text{(ones)} \\ 2 + 8 - 4 &= 6 & &\text{(eights)} \\ 7 - 6 &= 1 & &\text{(} 8^2 \text{ column)} \\ 4 - 1 &= 3 & &\text{(} 8^3 \text{ column)} \end{aligned}" />

So **<InlineMath math="5032_8 - 1647_8 = 3163_8" />**. Check through base ten: <InlineMath math="2{,}586 - 935 = 1{,}651" />.

<BlockMath math="3163_8 = 1{,}536 + 64 + 48 + 3 = 1{,}651" />

## Finding a Hidden Base

Sometimes a problem doesn't give the base and asks you to find it. Write the numeral in expanded form with the letter <InlineMath math="b" /> as the base, then solve the equation. Take <InlineMath math="123_b = 38" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 1 \times b^2 + 2 \times b + 3 &= 38 \\ b^2 + 2b - 35 &= 0 \\ (b + 7)(b - 5) &= 0 \end{aligned}" />

Solve this quadratic by [factoring a quadratic equation](/en/subjects/mathematics/quadratic-function/quadratic-equation-factorization) to get <InlineMath math="b = -7" /> and <InlineMath math="b = 5" />. **A base must be a whole number bigger than the largest digit** (<InlineMath math="3" /> here), so <InlineMath math="b = 5" />.

Before accepting a root as a base, **compare it with the largest digit**. A hidden base can also sit inside a sum like <InlineMath math="45_b + 36_b = 103_b" />. Write each numeral in expanded form with base <InlineMath math="b" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} (4b + 5) + (3b + 6) &= b^2 + 0 \times b + 3 \\ 7b + 11 &= b^2 + 3 \\ b^2 - 7b - 8 &= 0 \\ (b - 8)(b + 1) &= 0 \end{aligned}" />

The roots are <InlineMath math="b = 8" /> and <InlineMath math="b = -1" />. The base must be bigger than the largest digit <InlineMath math="6" />, so <InlineMath math="b = 8" />. Check by adding directly in base eight.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} 5 + 6 &= 11 = 13_8 & &\text{(write } 3\text{, carry } 1\text{)} \\ 4 + 3 + 1 &= 8 = 10_8 \end{aligned}" />

The result is <InlineMath math="103_8" />, matching the right-hand side.

## Finding Roots from Linear Factors

Factoring rewrites a quadratic equation from <InlineMath math="ax^2 + bx + c = 0" /> as a product. When it can be written as <InlineMath math="a(x - p)(x - q) = 0" />, the numbers <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" /> are its roots.

The roots are the values of <InlineMath math="x" /> that make the equation equal to zero. In product form, find the roots by <Highlight>setting each factor equal to zero</Highlight>.

## Basic Principles of Factorization

A quadratic equation in standard form is written as:

<BlockMath math="ax^2 + bx + c = 0" />

In this standard form, <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" /> are constants and <InlineMath math="a \neq 0" />.

Factorization uses the zero-product property: if a product equals zero, <Highlight>at least one factor must equal zero</Highlight>.

This means if <InlineMath math="(x - p)(x - q) = 0" />, then:

- <InlineMath math="x - p = 0" /> or <InlineMath math="x - q = 0" />
- Therefore <InlineMath math="x = p" /> or <InlineMath math="x = q" />

### Reading factors as areas

## Steps for Factoring Quadratic Equations

For integer coefficients, look for **two integers** whose product is <InlineMath math="ac" /> and whose sum is <InlineMath math="b" />. If such a pair exists, the following four steps give a factorization by grouping.

1. Ensure the equation is in standard form with the right side equal to zero
2. Find two numbers that when multiplied give <InlineMath math="ac" /> and when added give <InlineMath math="b" />
3. Split the middle term with those two numbers, then factor by grouping
4. Determine the roots of the equation from these factors

### Examples of factoring quadratic equations

These three examples use pairs with product <InlineMath math="ac" /> and sum <InlineMath math="b" />. The leading coefficient and the sign of the constant change, but the reason for **grouping** remains the same.

**Example 1**

<BlockMath math="x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0" />

In this equation, <InlineMath math="a = 1" />, <InlineMath math="b = 5" />, and <InlineMath math="c = 6" />.

**Step 1**: The equation is already in standard form with the right side equal to zero.

**Step 2**: We need to find two numbers that:

- When multiplied give <InlineMath math="ac = 1 \times 6 = 6" />
- When added give <InlineMath math="b = 5" />

Factors of <InlineMath math="6" /> are <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="2" />, <InlineMath math="3" />, and <InlineMath math="6" />.
Possible **factor pairs** are <InlineMath math="(1, 6)" /> and <InlineMath math="(2, 3)" />.
The pair <InlineMath math="(2, 3)" /> gives a sum of <InlineMath math="5" />, which matches the value of <InlineMath math="b" />.

**Step 3**: We can write the equation as:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + 2x + 3x + 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="x(x + 2) + 3(x + 2) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(x + 3)(x + 2) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: From the factored form above, we get:

- <InlineMath math="x + 3 = 0" /> → <InlineMath math="x = -3" />
- <InlineMath math="x + 2 = 0" /> → <InlineMath math="x = -2" />

Therefore, the roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -3" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -2" />.

**Example 2**

<BlockMath math="3x^2 + 13x - 10 = 0" />

In this equation, <InlineMath math="a = 3" />, <InlineMath math="b = 13" />, and <InlineMath math="c = -10" />.

**Step 1**: The equation is already in standard form with the right side equal to zero.

**Step 2**: We need to find two numbers that:

- When multiplied give <InlineMath math="ac = 3 \times (-10) = -30" />
- When added give <InlineMath math="b = 13" />

Factors of <InlineMath math="-30" /> are pairs of numbers with **opposite signs**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(1, -30), \; (-1, 30), \; (2, -15), \\ &amp;\; (-2, 15), \\ &amp;(3, -10), \; (-3, 10), \; (5, -6), \\ &amp;\; (-5, 6) \end{aligned}" />

The pair <InlineMath math="(15, -2)" /> gives a sum of <InlineMath math="13" />, which matches the value of <InlineMath math="b" />.

**Step 3**: We can write the equation as:

<BlockMath math="3x^2 + 15x - 2x - 10 = 0" />

We can group the terms:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3x^2 + 15x - 2x - 10 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="3x(x + 5) - 2(x + 5) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(3x - 2)(x + 5) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: From the factored form above, we get:

- <InlineMath math="3x - 2 = 0" /> → <InlineMath math="x = \frac{2}{3}" />
- <InlineMath math="x + 5 = 0" /> → <InlineMath math="x = -5" />

Therefore, the roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = \frac{2}{3}" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -5" />.

**Example 3**

<BlockMath math="2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0" />

In this equation, <InlineMath math="a = 2" />, <InlineMath math="b = 5" />, and <InlineMath math="c = -3" />.

**Step 1**: Calculate <InlineMath math="ac = 2 \times (-3) = -6" />

**Step 2**: Find a pair of factors of <InlineMath math="-6" /> that when added give <InlineMath math="5" />:

Factors of <InlineMath math="-6" />: <InlineMath math="(1, -6), (-1, 6), (2, -3), (-2, 3)" />

The pair <InlineMath math="(6, -1)" /> gives a sum of <InlineMath math="5" />, which matches the value of <InlineMath math="b" />.

**Step 3**: Split the term <InlineMath math="5x" /> into <InlineMath math="6x - x" />:

<BlockMath math="2x^2 + 6x - x - 3 = 0" />

**Step 4**: Factor by **grouping**:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 + 6x - x - 3 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2x(x + 3) - 1(x + 3) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(2x - 1)(x + 3) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 5**: Determine the roots of the equation:

- <InlineMath math="2x - 1 = 0" /> → <InlineMath math="x = \frac{1}{2}" />
- <InlineMath math="x + 3 = 0" /> → <InlineMath math="x = -3" />

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = \frac{1}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -3" />.

## Factoring When One Root Is Known

If we know one of the roots of a quadratic equation, we can use this information to find the complete factorization.

**Example**: One of the roots of the equation <InlineMath math="2x^2 - bx - 6 = 0" /> is <InlineMath math="6" />

If <InlineMath math="x = 6" /> is a root of the equation, then <InlineMath math="(x - 6)" /> is one of its factors.

We can substitute <InlineMath math="x = 6" /> into the original equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2(6)^2 - b(6) - 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2 \times 36 - 6b - 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="72 - 6b - 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="66 = 6b" />
  <BlockMath math="b = 11" />
</MathContainer>

Now we can write the equation as <InlineMath math="2x^2 - 11x - 6 = 0" />.

Using the factorization method, we factor it as:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 - 11x - 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 - 12x + x - 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2x(x - 6) + 1(x - 6) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(2x + 1)(x - 6) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -\frac{1}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 6" />.

## Special Cases of Factorization

**Example 1**

Form <InlineMath math="ax^2 + bx = 0" />

For equations without a constant term, we can factor out <InlineMath math="x" /> directly:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="ax^2 + bx = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="x(ax + b) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

The roots are <InlineMath math="x = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -\frac{b}{a}" />.

**Example**: <InlineMath math="2x^2 - 18x = 0" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 - 18x = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2x(x - 9) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

The roots are <InlineMath math="x = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 9" />.

**Example 2**

Form <InlineMath math="ax^2 - c = 0" />

For equations without an <InlineMath math="x" /> term, first isolate <InlineMath math="x^2" />. Over the real numbers, the square-root step requires <InlineMath math="\frac{c}{a}\ge0" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="ax^2 - c = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="ax^2 = c" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 = \frac{c}{a}" />
  <BlockMath math="x = \pm \sqrt{\frac{c}{a}}" />
</MathContainer>

- If <InlineMath math="\frac{c}{a}>0" />, there are two distinct real roots and the expression factors into real linear factors.
- If <InlineMath math="\frac{c}{a}=0" />, <InlineMath math="x=0" /> is a repeated root.
- If <InlineMath math="\frac{c}{a}<0" />, there are no real roots and no factorization into real linear factors. Over the complex numbers, the roots are <InlineMath math="x=\pm i\sqrt{-\frac{c}{a}}" />.

**Example**: <InlineMath math="2x^2 - 18 = 0" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 - 18 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 = 18" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 = 9" />
  <BlockMath math="x = \pm 3" />
</MathContainer>

 The roots are <InlineMath math="x = 3" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -3" />.

 **Example with complex roots**: <InlineMath math="2x^2 + 18 = 0" />

 <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="2x^2 + 18 = 0" />
   <BlockMath math="2x^2 = -18" />
   <BlockMath math="x^2 = -9" />
 </MathContainer>

 The roots are <InlineMath math="x = 3i" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -3i" />. Check against the formula above: <InlineMath math="c/a = -9" />, so <InlineMath math="x = \pm i\sqrt{9} = \pm 3i" />.

## When Integer Factor Pairs Do Not Work

Not every quadratic equation has rational factors. When no suitable factor pair exists, use another method such as [the quadratic formula](/en/subjects/mathematics/quadratic-function/quadratic-equation-formula):

<BlockMath math="x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}" />

For integer coefficients, the quadratic has rational roots exactly when the discriminant <InlineMath math="b^2 - 4ac" /> is a **nonnegative perfect square**.

## Practice Problems

Look for two numbers with **product** <InlineMath math="ac" /> and **sum** <InlineMath math="b" />, rather than a product equal to the constant alone. For the fifth problem, use the difference of two squares directly. Then set each factor equal to zero.

Factor the following quadratic equations:

1. <InlineMath math="x^2 + 6x + 5 = 0" />
2. <InlineMath math="2x^2 + 5x + 2 = 0" />
3. <InlineMath math="3x^2 - x - 2 = 0" />
4. <InlineMath math="2x^2 - 8x + 6 = 0" />
5. <InlineMath math="x^2 - 9 = 0" />

### Worked Solutions

**Problem 1**. Solve the equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + 6x + 5 = 0" />.

**Step 1**: Identify the coefficients

<BlockMath math="a = 1, b = 6, c = 5" />

**Step 2**: Find a **factor pair** whose product is <InlineMath math="ac = 5" /> and whose sum is <InlineMath math="b = 6" />.

Choose factors of <InlineMath math="5" /> with the required sum:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;1\times5=5 \\ &amp;1+5=6 \end{aligned}" />

**Step 3**: Factorization

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + 6x + 5 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + x + 5x + 5 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="x(x + 1) + 5(x + 1) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(x + 5)(x + 1) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: Determine the roots of the equation

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x + 5 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -5" />
  <BlockMath math="x + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -1" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -5" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -1" />.

**Problem 2**. Solve the equation <InlineMath math="2x^2 + 5x + 2 = 0" />.

**Step 1**: Identify the coefficients

<BlockMath math="a = 2, b = 5, c = 2" />

**Step 2**: Find a **factor pair** whose product is <InlineMath math="ac = 2 \times 2 = 4" /> and whose sum is <InlineMath math="b = 5" />.

Choose factors of <InlineMath math="4" /> with the required sum:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;1\times4=4 \\ &amp;1+4=5 \end{aligned}" />

**Step 3**: Factorization

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 + 5x + 2 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 + x + 4x + 2 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="x(2x + 1) + 2(2x + 1) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(2x + 1)(x + 2) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: Determine the roots of the equation

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2x + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -\frac{1}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="x + 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -2" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -\frac{1}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -2" />.

**Problem 3**. Solve the equation <InlineMath math="3x^2 - x - 2 = 0" />.

**Step 1**: Identify the coefficients

<BlockMath math="a = 3, b = -1, c = -2" />

**Step 2**: Find a **factor pair** whose product is <InlineMath math="ac = 3 \times (-2) = -6" /> and whose sum is <InlineMath math="b = -1" />.

Choose factors of <InlineMath math="-6" /> with the required sum:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(-3)\times2=-6 \\ &amp;(-3)+2=-1 \end{aligned}" />

**Step 3**: Factorization

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3x^2 - x - 2 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="3x^2 - 3x + 2x - 2 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="3x(x - 1) + 2(x - 1) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(3x + 2)(x - 1) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: Determine the roots of the equation

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3x + 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -\frac{2}{3}" />
  <BlockMath math="x - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 1" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -\frac{2}{3}" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 1" />.

**Problem 4**. Solve the equation <InlineMath math="2x^2 - 8x + 6 = 0" />.

**Step 1**: Identify the coefficients

<BlockMath math="a = 2, b = -8, c = 6" />

**Step 2**: Find a **factor pair** whose product is <InlineMath math="ac = 2 \times 6 = 12" /> and whose sum is <InlineMath math="b = -8" />.

Choose factors of <InlineMath math="12" /> with the required sum:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(-6)\times(-2)=12 \\ &amp;(-6)+(-2)=-8 \end{aligned}" />

**Step 3**: Factorization

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 - 8x + 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2x^2 - 6x - 2x + 6 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2x(x - 3) - 2(x - 3) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(2x - 2)(x - 3) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="2(x - 1)(x - 3) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: Determine the roots of the equation

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 1" />
  <BlockMath math="x - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = 1" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 3" />.

**Problem 5**. Solve the equation <InlineMath math="x^2 - 9 = 0" />.

**Step 1**: Identify as a **difference of squares**

<BlockMath math="x^2 - 9 = x^2 - 3^2" />

**Step 2**: Use the **difference of squares** formula <InlineMath math="a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)" />

<BlockMath math="x^2 - 3^2 = (x + 3)(x - 3) = 0" />

**Step 3**: Determine the roots of the equation

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x + 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -3" />
  <BlockMath math="x - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -3" /> and <InlineMath math="x = 3" />.

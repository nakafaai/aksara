## Quadratic Equation Roots

In a quadratic equation <InlineMath math="ax^2 + bx + c = 0" /> with <InlineMath math="a \neq 0" />, a root is any value of <InlineMath math="x" /> that satisfies the equation: plug it in and both sides match. For example, <InlineMath math="x = 2" /> in <InlineMath math="x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0" /> gives <InlineMath math="4 - 10 + 6 = 0" />, so 2 is a root. Over the real numbers alone, an equation with a negative discriminant has no root. But over the complex numbers, every quadratic equation has two roots. When both roots are the same, as in <InlineMath math="x^2 - 6x + 9 = 0" /> below, we count that repeated root twice. [The quadratic formula](/en/subjects/mathematics/quadratic-function/quadratic-equation-formula) computes both roots directly from <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" />:

<BlockMath math="x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}" />

The expression <InlineMath math="b^2 - 4ac" /> is the <Highlight>discriminant</Highlight>, denoted by <InlineMath math="D" />. When <InlineMath math="D > 0" /> the roots are two distinct reals. When <InlineMath math="D = 0" /> the root is repeated. When <InlineMath math="D < 0" /> the roots are nonreal complex. So the sign of <InlineMath math="D" /> already tells you the root type before you finish evaluating the formula.

### Different Real Roots

If <InlineMath math="D > 0" /> (or <InlineMath math="b^2 > 4ac" />), then the quadratic equation has two different real roots.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x_1 = \frac{-b + \sqrt{D}}{2a} \\ &amp;\text{and} \\ &amp;x_2 = \frac{-b - \sqrt{D}}{2a} \end{aligned}" />

Example: <InlineMath math="x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0" />

- <InlineMath math="a = 1, b = -5, c = 6" />
- <InlineMath math="D = (-5)^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 6 = 25 - 24 = 1 > 0" />
- The roots are: <InlineMath math="x_1 = 3" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2 = 2" />

### Equal or Repeated Roots

If <InlineMath math="D = 0" /> (or <InlineMath math="b^2 = 4ac" />), then the quadratic equation has one real repeated root (two roots with the same value).

<BlockMath math="x_1 = x_2 = \frac{-b}{2a}" />

Example: <InlineMath math="x^2 - 6x + 9 = 0" />

- <InlineMath math="a = 1, b = -6, c = 9" />
- <InlineMath math="D = (-6)^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 9 = 36 - 36 = 0" />
- The roots are: <InlineMath math="x_1 = x_2 = 3" />

### Two Nonreal Complex Roots

If <InlineMath math="D < 0" /> (or <InlineMath math="b^2 < 4ac" />), then the quadratic equation has two distinct non-real complex roots. For real coefficients, they form a conjugate pair.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x_1 = \frac{-b + i\sqrt{|D|}}{2a} \\ &amp;\text{and} \\ &amp;x_2 = \frac{-b - i\sqrt{|D|}}{2a} \end{aligned}" />

Here <InlineMath math="i^2=-1" /> defines the imaginary unit.

Example: <InlineMath math="x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0" />

- <InlineMath math="a = 1, b = 2, c = 5" />
- <InlineMath math="D = 2^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 5 = 4 - 20 = -16 < 0" />
- The roots are: <InlineMath math="x_1 = -1 + 2i" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2 = -1 - 2i" />

## Relationship Between Roots and Coefficients

If <InlineMath math="x_1" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2" /> are the roots of the quadratic equation <InlineMath math="ax^2 + bx + c = 0" />, then:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x_1 + x_2 = -\frac{b}{a}" />
  <BlockMath math="x_1 \times x_2 = \frac{c}{a}" />
</MathContainer>

These relationships can verify calculated roots or determine the coefficient ratios <InlineMath math="b/a" /> and <InlineMath math="c/a" />. A specific equation is fixed once the leading coefficient <InlineMath math="a" /> is also known.

## Exercise

The problem asks you to classify first and solve second, so compute the discriminant before taking any square root. Checking the sum and the product afterwards shows whether the two roots belong together.

Classify and solve the equation <InlineMath math="2x^2-4x+5=0" />. Then verify the roots using their sum and product.

### Worked Solution

The coefficients are <InlineMath math="a=2" />, <InlineMath math="b=-4" />, and <InlineMath math="c=5" />. Its discriminant is

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} D &amp;=(-4)^2-4\cdot2\cdot5 \\ &amp;=16-40 \\ &amp;=-24 \end{aligned}" />

Because <InlineMath math="D<0" />, the equation has two non-real complex roots. Apply the quadratic formula and use <InlineMath math="\sqrt{-24}=2i\sqrt6" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac{-(-4)\pm\sqrt{-24}}{2\cdot2}" />
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac{4\pm2i\sqrt6}{4}=1\pm\frac{i\sqrt6}{2}" />
</MathContainer>

The calculation gives:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x_1=1+\frac{i\sqrt6}{2} \\ &amp;\text{and} \\ &amp;x_2=1-\frac{i\sqrt6}{2} \end{aligned}" />

Their sum and product agree with Vieta's formulas:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x_1+x_2=2=-\frac{b}{a}" />
  <BlockMath math="x_1x_2=1+\frac{6}{4}=\frac52=\frac{c}{a}" />
</MathContainer>

Both checks use the original coefficients, so they also catch a lost sign or an incorrect denominator in the quadratic formula.

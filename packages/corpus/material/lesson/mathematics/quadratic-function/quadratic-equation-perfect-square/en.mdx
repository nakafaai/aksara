## Rewriting a Quadratic Equation as a Square

Completing the square is <Highlight>a method for solving quadratic equations</Highlight> by converting the equation from the form <InlineMath math="ax^2 + bx + c = 0" /> to the form <InlineMath math="(x + p)^2 = q" />. This method is particularly useful for quadratic equations that are difficult to factor using regular factorization.

A perfect square trinomial follows the pattern <InlineMath math="x^2 + 2px + p^2 = (x + p)^2" />. Once the left side is a square, solve for the roots by taking the square root of both sides. When the middle term is <InlineMath math="2px" />, add <InlineMath math="p^2" /> to complete the square.

## Quadratics That Are Hard to Factor

We do not need a rational factor pair because we can <Highlight>add the same value to both sides</Highlight>:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}x^2+4x+2&amp;=0\\x^2+4x+4&amp;=2\\(x+2)^2&amp;=2\\x&amp;=-2\pm\sqrt2\end{aligned}" />

Since <InlineMath math="\sqrt2" /> is irrational, both roots are irrational. That explains why no rational factor pair exists.

## Steps for Completing the Square

Start with <InlineMath math="a\ne0" />. Divide the whole equation by <InlineMath math="a" />, then write <InlineMath math="m=b/a" /> and <InlineMath math="k=c/a" />. The completing term uses the <Highlight>coefficient after division</Highlight>, namely <InlineMath math="m" />, rather than the original coefficient <InlineMath math="b" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}x^2+mx+k&amp;=0\\x^2+mx&amp;=-k\\x^2+mx+\frac{m^2}{4}&amp;=-k+\frac{m^2}{4}\\\left(x+\frac m2\right)^2&amp;=\frac{m^2}{4}-k\end{aligned}" />

Two rectangles each have area <InlineMath math="px" />. Placing them beside a square of area <InlineMath math="x^2" /> leaves a corner of area <InlineMath math="p^2" />. Adding that corner completes a square with side <InlineMath math="x+p" />.

## Solving Quadratic Equations by Completing the Square

Each equation below is solved by turning the left side into a **perfect square**. The first has a leading coefficient of one, and the later examples divide by the coefficient first.

### Equation with Leading Coefficient One

Solve the equation: <InlineMath math="x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0" />

**Step 1**: The coefficient <InlineMath math="a = 1" />, so we proceed to the next step.

**Step 2**: Move the constant to the right side.

<BlockMath math="x^2 + 5x = -6" />

**Step 3**: Add the square of half the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> to both sides.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Half the coefficient of } x = \frac{5}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{25}{4}" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + 5x + \frac{25}{4} = -6 + \frac{25}{4}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: Factor the left side into a perfect square.

<BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{5}{2}\right)^2 = -6 + \frac{25}{4}" />

**Step 5**: Simplify the right side.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="-6 + \frac{25}{4} = \frac{-24 + 25}{4} = \frac{1}{4}" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{5}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 6**: Take the square root of both sides.

<BlockMath math="x + \frac{5}{2} = \pm\frac{1}{2}" />

**Step 7**: Solve for the value of <InlineMath math="x" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x + \frac{5}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad x + \frac{5}{2} = -\frac{1}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{5}{2} = -2 \\ &amp;\text{or} \\ &amp;x = -\frac{1}{2} - \frac{5}{2} = -3 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -2" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -3" />.

### Equation with Leading Coefficient Not One

Solve the equation: <InlineMath math="2x^2 + 6x + 3 = 0" />

**Step 1**: Divide all terms by the coefficient <InlineMath math="a = 2" />

<BlockMath math="x^2 + 3x + \frac{3}{2} = 0" />

**Step 2**: Move the constant to the right side

<BlockMath math="x^2 + 3x = -\frac{3}{2}" />

**Step 3**: Add the square of half the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> to both sides

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Half the coefficient of } x = \frac{3}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{4}" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + 3x + \frac{9}{4} = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{9}{4}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: Factor the left side into a perfect square

<BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{9}{4}" />

**Step 5**: Simplify the right side

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="-\frac{3}{2} + \frac{9}{4} = \frac{-6 + 9}{4} = \frac{3}{4}" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{3}{4}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 6**: Take the square root of both sides

<BlockMath math="x + \frac{3}{2} = \pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" />

**Step 7**: Solve for the value of <InlineMath math="x" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\ &amp;\text{or} \\ &amp;x + \frac{3}{2} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{3}{2} = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\ &amp;\text{or} \\ &amp;x = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{3}{2} = -\frac{3}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -\frac{3}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" />.

## Checks for Common Errors

Check <Highlight>coefficient normalization, addition on both sides, and the final form</Highlight> in these three examples.

1. For equations with coefficient of <InlineMath math="x^2" /> not equal to <InlineMath math="1" />: Always divide the entire equation by the coefficient <InlineMath math="a" /> first. Example: <InlineMath math="3x^2 + 6x + 2 = 0" /> becomes <InlineMath math="x^2 + 2x + \frac{2}{3} = 0" />

2. **Constant to be added**: Always add the square of half the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> to both sides. Example: For <InlineMath math="x^2 + 8x = 5" />, add <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{8}{2}\right)^2 = 16" /> to both sides.

3. **Final form**: The equation will transform into the form <InlineMath math="\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}" />. Example: <InlineMath math="x^2 + 6x + 8 = 0" /> becomes <InlineMath math="\left(x + 3\right)^2 = 1" />

## Special Cases and Variations

Once the equation is <InlineMath math="(x+p)^2=q" />, the <Highlight>sign of the right-hand side</Highlight> determines the real solutions. A positive <InlineMath math="q" /> gives two roots, zero gives a repeated root, and a negative value gives no real roots.

### When the Discriminant is Negative

If <InlineMath math="b^2 - 4ac < 0" />, then the equation has **no real roots**.

**Concrete example:** <InlineMath math="x^2 + 2x + 2 = 0" />

Completing the square:

Add <InlineMath math="1" /> to both sides:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + 2x + 1 = -2 + 1" />
  <BlockMath math="(x + 1)^2 = -1" />
</MathContainer>

Since no real number has a square of <InlineMath math="-1" />, this equation has no real roots.

Over the complex numbers, use <InlineMath math="i^2=-1" /> to obtain:

<BlockMath math="(x+1)^2=-1\quad\Rightarrow\quad x+1=\pm i\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=-1\pm i" />

### For Incomplete Quadratic Equations

For equations of the form <InlineMath math="ax^2 + c = 0" />, we can **isolate the square directly**.

**Concrete example:** <InlineMath math="3x^2 - 12 = 0" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="3x^2 = 12" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 = 4" />
  <BlockMath math="x = \pm 2" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = 2" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -2" />.

## Practice Problems

At each step, perform the <Highlight>same operation on both sides</Highlight>. After making the leading coefficient one, square half of the new linear coefficient, then use both square-root signs.

Solve the following quadratic equations using the completing the square method:

1. <InlineMath math="x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0" />
2. <InlineMath math="2x^2 + 6x + 3 = 0" />
3. <InlineMath math="6x^2 + 2x + \frac{1}{6} = 0" />
4. <InlineMath math="x^2 - 12x - 15 = 0" />
5. <InlineMath math="\frac{3}{2}x^2 - 8x - 6 = 0" />

### Answer Key

**Problem 1**

<InlineMath math="x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0" />

**Step 1**: Move the constant to the right side:

<BlockMath math="x^2 + 5x = -6" />

**Step 2**: Add the square of half the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> to both sides:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Half the coefficient of } x = \frac{5}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{25}{4}" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + 5x + \frac{25}{4} = -6 + \frac{25}{4}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 3**: Factor the left side into a perfect square:

<BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{5}{2}\right)^2 = -6 + \frac{25}{4}" />

**Step 4**: Simplify the right side:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="-6 + \frac{25}{4} = \frac{-24 + 25}{4} = \frac{1}{4}" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{5}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 5**: Take the square root of both sides:

<BlockMath math="x + \frac{5}{2} = \pm\frac{1}{2}" />

**Step 6**: Solve for the value of <InlineMath math="x" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x + \frac{5}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad x + \frac{5}{2} = -\frac{1}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{5}{2} = -2 \\ &amp;\text{or} \\ &amp;x = -\frac{1}{2} - \frac{5}{2} = -3 \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, the roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -2" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -3" />.

**Problem 2**

<InlineMath math="2x^2 + 6x + 3 = 0" />

**Step 1**: Divide all terms by the coefficient <InlineMath math="a = 2" />:

<BlockMath math="x^2 + 3x + \frac{3}{2} = 0" />

**Step 2**: Move the constant to the right side:

<BlockMath math="x^2 + 3x = -\frac{3}{2}" />

**Step 3**: Add the square of half the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> to both sides:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{Half the coefficient of } x = \frac{3}{2}" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{4}" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + 3x + \frac{9}{4} = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{9}{4}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: Factor the left side into a perfect square:

<BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{9}{4}" />

**Step 5**: Simplify the right side:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="-\frac{3}{2} + \frac{9}{4} = \frac{-6 + 9}{4} = \frac{3}{4}" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{3}{4}" />

**Step 6**: Take the square root of both sides:

<BlockMath math="x + \frac{3}{2} = \pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" />

<MathContainer>

Therefore, the roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -\frac{3}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}" />.

**Problem 3**

<InlineMath math="6x^2 + 2x + \frac{1}{6} = 0" />

**Step 1**: Divide all terms by the coefficient <InlineMath math="a = 6" />:

<BlockMath math="x^2 + \frac{1}{3}x + \frac{1}{36} = 0" />

<BlockMath math="x^2 + \frac{1}{3}x = -\frac{1}{36}" />

<BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{1}{6}\right)^2 = 0" />

**Problem 4**

<InlineMath math="x^2 - 12x - 15 = 0" />

**Step 1**: Move the constant to the right side:

<BlockMath math="x^2 - 12x = 15" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(-6)^2 = 36" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 - 12x + 36 = 15 + 36" />

**Step 3**: Factor the left side into a perfect square:

<BlockMath math="(x - 6)^2 = 51" />

**Problem 5**

<InlineMath math="\frac{3}{2}x^2 - 8x - 6 = 0" />

**Step 1**: Divide all terms by the coefficient <InlineMath math="a = \frac{3}{2}" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 - \frac{16}{3}x - 4 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 2**: Move the constant to the right side:

<BlockMath math="x^2 - \frac{16}{3}x = 4" />

**Step 3**: Add the square of half the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> to both sides:

Half the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> is <InlineMath math="-\frac83" />.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\left(-\frac{8}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{64}{9}" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 - \frac{16}{3}x + \frac{64}{9} = 4 + \frac{64}{9}" />

**Step 4**: Factor the left side into a perfect square:

<BlockMath math="\left(x - \frac{8}{3}\right)^2 = 4 + \frac{64}{9}" />

**Step 5**: Simplify the right side:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="4 + \frac{64}{9} = \frac{36 + 64}{9} = \frac{100}{9}" />

**Step 6**: Take the square root of both sides:

<BlockMath math="x - \frac{8}{3} = \pm\frac{10}{3}" />

**Step 7**: Solve for the value of <InlineMath math="x" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x = \frac{8}{3} + \frac{10}{3} = \frac{18}{3} = 6 \\ &amp;\text{or} \\ &amp;x = \frac{8}{3} - \frac{10}{3} = -\frac{2}{3} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x = 6" /> and <InlineMath math="x = -\frac{2}{3}" />.

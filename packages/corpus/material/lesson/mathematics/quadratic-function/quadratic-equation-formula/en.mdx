## Calculating Roots from Quadratic Coefficients

The coefficients <InlineMath math="a" />, <InlineMath math="b" />, and <InlineMath math="c" /> here are real numbers.

A quadratic equation has the form <InlineMath math="ax^2 + bx + c = 0" />, where <InlineMath math="a \neq 0" />, with the following coefficients:

- <InlineMath math="a" /> is the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x^2" />
- <InlineMath math="b" /> is the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" />
- <InlineMath math="c" /> is the constant term

To solve a quadratic equation <InlineMath math="ax^2 + bx + c = 0" />, we can use the formula:

<BlockMath math="x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}" />

Over the real numbers alone, an equation with a negative discriminant has no root. But over the complex numbers, the two signs give two roots. When the discriminant is zero, both expressions below give the same repeated root, and we count that repeated root twice:

- <InlineMath math="x_1 = \frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}" /> (using the plus sign)
- <InlineMath math="x_2 = \frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}" /> (using the minus sign)

The part <InlineMath math="b^2 - 4ac" /> is called the <Highlight>discriminant</Highlight> and determines the nature of the roots:

- If <InlineMath math="b^2 - 4ac > 0" />: Two distinct real roots
- If <InlineMath math="b^2 - 4ac = 0" />: One real root (a repeated root)
- If <InlineMath math="b^2 - 4ac < 0" />: No real roots, but two non-real complex conjugate roots

## Deriving the Quadratic Formula

To derive the quadratic formula, [complete the square](/en/subjects/mathematics/quadratic-function/quadratic-equation-perfect-square) in the general form below:

<BlockMath math="ax^2 + bx + c = 0" />

**Step 1**: Divide all terms by <InlineMath math="a" /> (the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x^2" />):

<BlockMath math="x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x + \frac{c}{a} = 0" />

**Step 2**: Move the constant term to the right side:

<BlockMath math="x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x = -\frac{c}{a}" />

**Step 3**: Add the square of half the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x" /> to both sides:

<BlockMath math="x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x + \frac{b^2}{4a^2} = -\frac{c}{a} + \frac{b^2}{4a^2}" />

**Step 4**: The left side now forms a perfect square:

<BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2}{4a^2} - \frac{c}{a}" />

**Step 5**: Simplify the right side:

<BlockMath math="\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}" />

**Step 6**: Take the square root of both sides:

<BlockMath math="x + \frac{b}{2a} = \pm\frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}" />

For a nonnegative discriminant, <InlineMath math="\sqrt{4a^2}=2|a|" />. Because we use <Highlight>both square-root signs</Highlight>, replacing <InlineMath math="2|a|" /> by <InlineMath math="2a" /> only exchanges the two solutions when <InlineMath math="a<0" />. A negative discriminant is handled over the complex numbers.

**Step 7**: Solve for <InlineMath math="x" />:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x &amp;= -\frac{b}{2a} \pm \frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \\ &amp;= \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \end{aligned}" />

We obtain the quadratic formula:

<BlockMath math="x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}" />

### Solving with the Quadratic Formula

**Example 1**: Solve the equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0" />

Identify the values: <InlineMath math="a = 1" />, <InlineMath math="b = 5" />, and <InlineMath math="c = 6" />

Substitute into the formula:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x &amp;= \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{5^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 6}}{2 \cdot 1} \\ &amp;= \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{25 - 24}}{2} \\ &amp;= \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{1}}{2} \\ &amp;= \frac{-5 \pm 1}{2} \end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="x_1" />, take the positive sign:

<BlockMath math="x_1 = \frac{-5 + 1}{2} = \frac{-4}{2} = -2" />

For <InlineMath math="x_2" />, take the negative sign:

<BlockMath math="x_2 = \frac{-5 - 1}{2} = \frac{-6}{2} = -3" />

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x_1 = -2" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2 = -3" />

**Example 2**: Solve the equation <InlineMath math="2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0" />

Identify the values: <InlineMath math="a = 2" />, <InlineMath math="b = -7" />, and <InlineMath math="c = 3" />

Substitute into the formula:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned}x &= \frac{-(-7) \pm \sqrt{(-7)^2 - 4 \cdot 2 \cdot 3}}{2 \cdot 2} \\ &= \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{49 - 24}}{4} \\ &= \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{25}}{4} \\ &= \frac{7 \pm 5}{4}\end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="x_1" />, take the positive sign:

<BlockMath math="x_1 = \frac{7 + 5}{4} = \frac{12}{4} = 3" />

For <InlineMath math="x_2" />, take the negative sign:

<BlockMath math="x_2 = \frac{7 - 5}{4} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}" />

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x_1 = 3" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2 = \frac{1}{2}" />

## Relationship Between Roots and Coefficients

The **sum and product** of the two roots can be read off the coefficients without solving the equation first. That gives you a quick check on a solution or a way to find a missing value.

If <InlineMath math="x_1" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2" /> are the roots of the quadratic equation <InlineMath math="ax^2 + bx + c = 0" />, then:

1. Sum of the roots: <InlineMath math="x_1 + x_2 = -\frac{b}{a}" />
2. Product of the roots: <InlineMath math="x_1 \cdot x_2 = \frac{c}{a}" />

### Proving the Relationships

From the quadratic formula, we know that:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;x_1 = \frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \\ &amp;\text{and} \\ &amp;x_2 = \frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \end{aligned}" />

When we add, the <Highlight>radical terms cancel</Highlight>:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x_1+x_2 &amp;= \frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \\ &amp;\quad+ \frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \\ &amp;= \frac{-2b}{2a} \\ &amp;= -\frac{b}{a} \end{aligned}" />

Multiplying the roots:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x_1x_2 &amp;= \frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \\ &amp;\quad{}\cdot \frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \\ &amp;= \frac{b^2 - (\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac})^2}{4a^2} \\ &amp;= \frac{b^2 - (b^2 - 4ac)}{4a^2} \\ &amp;= \frac{4ac}{4a^2} \\ &amp;= \frac{c}{a} \end{aligned}" />

## Creating New Quadratic Equations from Known Roots

If <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" /> are <Highlight>known roots</Highlight>, one monic quadratic equation with those roots is:

<BlockMath math="(x - p)(x - q) = 0" />

Or in standard form:

<BlockMath math="x^2 - (p+q)x + pq = 0" />

### Changing the Roots of a Quadratic Equation

**Problem 1**

The quadratic equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + 4x + 3 = 0" /> has roots <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" />.

Find the quadratic equation with roots <InlineMath math="2p" /> and <InlineMath math="2q" />.

**Step 1**: Find the values of <InlineMath math="p + q" /> and <InlineMath math="p \cdot q" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="p + q = -\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{4}{1} = -4" />
  <BlockMath math="p \cdot q = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{3}{1} = 3" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 2**: Calculate the sum and product of the new roots

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="2p + 2q = 2(p + q) = 2(-4) = -8" />
  <BlockMath math="2p \cdot 2q = 4(p \cdot q) = 4 \cdot 3 = 12" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 3**: Create the new quadratic equation

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 - (-8)x + 12 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + 8x + 12 = 0" />
</MathContainer>

**Problem 2**

The quadratic equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + 4x - 21 = 0" /> has roots <InlineMath math="p" /> and <InlineMath math="q" />.

Find the quadratic equation with roots <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2p}" /> and <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2q}" />.

**Step 1**: Find the values of <InlineMath math="p + q" /> and <InlineMath math="p \cdot q" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="p + q = -\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{4}{1} = -4" />
  <BlockMath math="p \cdot q = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{-21}{1} = -21" />
</MathContainer>

The product <InlineMath math="pq=-21" /> is nonzero. Therefore <Highlight>both roots are nonzero</Highlight>, so the following reciprocals are defined.

**Step 2**: Calculate the sum and product of the new roots

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2p} + \frac{1}{2q} &amp;= \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{q + p}{pq}\right) \\ &amp;= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{-4}{-21} \\ &amp;= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{21} \\ &amp;= \frac{4}{42} \\ &amp;= \frac{2}{21} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2p} \cdot \frac{1}{2q} &amp;= \frac{1}{4pq} \\ &amp;= \frac{1}{4 \cdot (-21)} \\ &amp;= \frac{1}{-84} \\ &amp;= -\frac{1}{84} \end{aligned}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 3**: Create the new quadratic equation

<BlockMath math="x^2 - \frac{2}{21}x - \frac{1}{84} = 0" />

## Practice Problems

Each equation is already in standard form, so read the **coefficients with their signs** before you substitute them into the formula. Check the value under the square root first to see how many real solutions to expect.

Solve the following quadratic equations using the quadratic formula:

1. <InlineMath math="x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0" />
2. <InlineMath math="2x^2 + 6x + 3 = 0" />
3. <InlineMath math="6x^2 + 2x + \frac{1}{6} = 0" />
4. <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}x^2 + 4x + 6 = 0" />
5. <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{3}x^2 + 2x - 12 = 0" />

### Answer Key

**Problem 1**

Solution to the quadratic equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0" />

Identify: <InlineMath math="a = 1" />, <InlineMath math="b = 5" />, <InlineMath math="c = 6" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x_{1,2} &amp;= \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{5^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 6}}{2 \cdot 1} \\ &amp;= \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{25 - 24}}{2} \\ &amp;= \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{1}}{2} \\ &amp;= \frac{-5 \pm 1}{2} \end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="x_1" />:

<BlockMath math="x_1 = \frac{-5 + 1}{2} = \frac{-4}{2} = -2" />

For <InlineMath math="x_2" />:

<BlockMath math="x_2 = \frac{-5 - 1}{2} = \frac{-6}{2} = -3" />

Therefore, the roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x_1 = -2" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2 = -3" />.

**Problem 2**

Solution to the quadratic equation <InlineMath math="2x^2 + 6x + 3 = 0" />

Identify: <InlineMath math="a = 2" />, <InlineMath math="b = 6" />, <InlineMath math="c = 3" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x_{1,2} &amp;= \frac{-6 \pm \sqrt{6^2 - 4 \cdot 2 \cdot 3}}{2 \cdot 2} \\ &amp;= \frac{-6 \pm \sqrt{36 - 24}}{4} \\ &amp;= \frac{-6 \pm \sqrt{12}}{4} \\ &amp;= \frac{-6 \pm 2\sqrt{3}}{4} \\ &amp;= \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{3}}{2} \end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="x_1" />:

<BlockMath math="x_1 = \frac{-3 + \sqrt{3}}{2}" />

For <InlineMath math="x_2" />:

<BlockMath math="x_2 = \frac{-3 - \sqrt{3}}{2}" />

Therefore, the roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x_1 = \frac{-3 + \sqrt{3}}{2}" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2 = \frac{-3 - \sqrt{3}}{2}" />.

**Problem 3**

Solution to the quadratic equation <InlineMath math="6x^2 + 2x + \frac{1}{6} = 0" />

Identify: <InlineMath math="a = 6" />, <InlineMath math="b = 2" />, <InlineMath math="c = \frac{1}{6}" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x_{1,2} &amp;= \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{2^2 - 4 \cdot 6 \cdot \frac{1}{6}}}{2 \cdot 6} \\ &amp;= \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 4}}{12} \\ &amp;= \frac{-2 \pm 0}{12} \\ &amp;= \frac{-2}{12} = -\frac{1}{6} \end{aligned}" />

Since the discriminant <InlineMath math="b^2 - 4ac = 0" />, the equation has one root (a repeated root).

The equation has the repeated root <InlineMath math="x_1 = x_2 = -\frac{1}{6}" />.

**Problem 4**

Solution to the quadratic equation <InlineMath math="\frac{1}{2}x^2 + 4x + 6 = 0" />

Identify: <InlineMath math="a = \frac{1}{2}" />, <InlineMath math="b = 4" />, <InlineMath math="c = 6" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x_{1,2} &amp;= \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{4^2 - 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6}}{2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}} \\ &amp;= \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 12}}{1} \\ &amp;= \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{4}}{1} \\ &amp;= -4 \pm 2 \end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="x_1" />:

<BlockMath math="x_1 = -4 + 2 = -2" />

For <InlineMath math="x_2" />:

<BlockMath math="x_2 = -4 - 2 = -6" />

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x_1 = -2" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2 = -6" />.

**Problem 5**

Solution to the quadratic equation <InlineMath math="\frac{2}{3}x^2 + 2x - 12 = 0" />

Identify: <InlineMath math="a = \frac{2}{3}" />, <InlineMath math="b = 2" />, <InlineMath math="c = -12" />

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} x_{1,2} &amp;= \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{2^2 - 4 \cdot \frac{2}{3} \cdot (-12)}}{2 \cdot \frac{2}{3}} \\ &amp;= \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 32}}{4/3} \\ &amp;= \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{36}}{4/3} \\ &amp;= \frac{-2 \pm 6}{4/3} \\ &amp;= \frac{3(-2 \pm 6)}{4} \end{aligned}" />

For <InlineMath math="x_1" />:

<BlockMath math="x_1 = \frac{3(-2 + 6)}{4} = \frac{3 \cdot 4}{4} = 3" />

For <InlineMath math="x_2" />:

<BlockMath math="x_2 = \frac{3(-2 - 6)}{4} = \frac{3 \cdot (-8)}{4} = -6" />

The roots of the equation are <InlineMath math="x_1 = 3" /> and <InlineMath math="x_2 = -6" />.

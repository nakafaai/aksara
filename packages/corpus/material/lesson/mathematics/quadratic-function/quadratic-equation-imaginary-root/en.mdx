## Extending Beyond the Real Numbers

Start with the equation:

<BlockMath math="x^2+1=0" />

To satisfy this equation, <InlineMath math="x^2" /> would have to equal <InlineMath math="-1" />. No real number has a negative square, so the equation has no real solution. We extend the number system with the imaginary unit <InlineMath math="i" />, which satisfies the following definition:

<BlockMath math="i^2=-1" />

A complex number has the form

<BlockMath math="z=a+bi\qquad(a,b\in\mathbb R)" />

The number <InlineMath math="2+5i" /> is complex. It is not purely imaginary because its real part is not zero. A number such as <InlineMath math="3i" /> is purely imaginary.

## A Negative Discriminant

For a quadratic equation <InlineMath math="ax^2+bx+c=0" /> with real coefficients, the discriminant is

<BlockMath math="D=b^2-4ac" />

If <InlineMath math="D<0" />, the equation has no real roots. It has two distinct non-real complex roots, and those roots form a conjugate pair.

We still begin with the quadratic formula:

<BlockMath math="x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{D}}{2a}" />

Because <InlineMath math="D<0" />, write <InlineMath math="D=-|D|" />. Then

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\sqrt D=\sqrt{-|D|}=i\sqrt{|D|}" />
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac{-b\pm i\sqrt{|D|}}{2a}" />
</MathContainer>

## Finding and Classifying Nonreal Roots

A negative discriminant determines the form of the two nonreal roots and shows that they form a conjugate pair.

### Finding Two Nonreal Roots

Solve

<BlockMath math="x^2+4x+5=0" />

The coefficients are <InlineMath math="a=1" />, <InlineMath math="b=4" />, and <InlineMath math="c=5" />. First calculate the discriminant:

<BlockMath math="D=4^2-4\cdot1\cdot5=16-20=-4" />

Since <InlineMath math="D=-4<0" />, the roots are non-real. Substitute the values into the quadratic formula:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{-4}}{2\cdot1}" />
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac{-4\pm2i}{2}=-2\pm i" />
</MathContainer>

The calculation gives:

<BlockMath math="x_1=-2+i\qquad\text{and}\qquad x_2=-2-i" />

We can check one root directly:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(-2+i)^2+4(-2+i)+5" />
  <BlockMath math="=4-4i+i^2-8+4i+5=0" />
</MathContainer>

The other root follows by changing the sign of the imaginary part.

### Classifying Roots Before Solving

Consider

<BlockMath math="2x^2+x+3=0" />

Here <InlineMath math="a=2" />, <InlineMath math="b=1" />, and <InlineMath math="c=3" />. Its discriminant is

<BlockMath math="D=1^2-4\cdot2\cdot3=1-24=-23" />

The negative discriminant tells us that the equation has two non-real complex roots. Their exact values are

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{-23}}{2\cdot2}" />
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac{-1\pm i\sqrt{23}}4" />
</MathContainer>

The calculation gives:

<BlockMath math="x_1=\frac{-1+i\sqrt{23}}4\qquad\text{and}\qquad x_2=\frac{-1-i\sqrt{23}}4" />

## What the Real Graph Shows

The first example can be rewritten as

<BlockMath math="f(x)=x^2+4x+5=(x+2)^2+1" />

For every real <InlineMath math="x" />, the value of <InlineMath math="(x+2)^2" /> is nonnegative, so <InlineMath math="f(x)\ge1" />. The graph stays above the <InlineMath math="x" />-axis and has no real intercepts. The complex roots are not extra points on this real graph. They belong to the extended number system.

## Why the Roots Come in Conjugate Pairs

For real coefficients, the quadratic formula contains

<BlockMath math="-b\pm i\sqrt{|D|}" />

The two choices of the sign change only the imaginary part. This produces <InlineMath math="u+vi" /> and <InlineMath math="u-vi" />, called complex conjugates. If one is a root of a polynomial with real coefficients, its conjugate is also a root.

## Practice Problem

Solve <InlineMath math="2x^2+2x+5=0" /> over the complex numbers.

### Worked Solution

Start with the discriminant:

<BlockMath math="D=2^2-4\cdot2\cdot5=4-40=-36" />

Because <InlineMath math="D<0" />, use <InlineMath math="\sqrt{-36}=6i" /> in the quadratic formula:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{-36}}{2\cdot2}" />
  <BlockMath math="x=\frac{-2\pm6i}{4}=-\frac12\pm\frac32i" />
</MathContainer>

So the conjugate roots are

<BlockMath math="x_1=-\frac12+\frac32i\qquad\text{and}\qquad x_2=-\frac12-\frac32i" />

Their sum is <InlineMath math="-1=-\frac ba" /> and their product is <InlineMath math="\frac52=\frac ca" />, which checks the result against Vieta's formulas.

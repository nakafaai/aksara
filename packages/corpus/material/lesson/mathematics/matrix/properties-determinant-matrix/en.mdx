## Discovering Properties of Matrix Determinants

Suppose we have two matrices, <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />, as follows:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="A = \begin{bmatrix} -5 & 2 \\ 1 & -3 \end{bmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="B = \begin{bmatrix} 9 & -4 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

### Determinant of the Product of Two Matrices

Investigate the relationship between the determinant of the product of two matrices (<InlineMath math="|AB|" />) and the product of their individual determinants (<InlineMath math="|A||B|" />).

**Step <InlineMath math="1" />: Calculate the Determinant of Matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> (<InlineMath math="|A|" />)**

The determinant of matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> is:

<BlockMath math="|A| = (-5 \cdot -3) - (2 \cdot 1) = 15 - 2 = 13" />

**Step <InlineMath math="2" />: Calculate the Determinant of Matrix <InlineMath math="B" /> (<InlineMath math="|B|" />)**

The determinant of matrix <InlineMath math="B" /> is:

<BlockMath math="|B| = (9 \cdot -1) - (-4 \cdot 1) = -9 - (-4) = -9 + 4 = -5" />

**Step <InlineMath math="3" />: Determine the Product of Matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> (<InlineMath math="AB" />)**

Matrix <InlineMath math="AB" /> is obtained by multiplying matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="AB = \begin{bmatrix} -5 & 2 \\ 1 & -3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 9 & -4 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="AB = \begin{bmatrix} (-5)(9) + (2)(1) & (-5)(-4) + (2)(-1) \\ (1)(9) + (-3)(1) & (1)(-4) + (-3)(-1) \end{bmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="AB = \begin{bmatrix} -45 + 2 & 20 - 2 \\ 9 - 3 & -4 + 3 \end{bmatrix}" />
  <BlockMath math="AB = \begin{bmatrix} -43 & 18 \\ 6 & -1 \end{bmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

**Step <InlineMath math="4" />: Calculate the Determinant of Matrix <InlineMath math="AB" /> (<InlineMath math="|AB|" />)**

Now, calculate the determinant of the product matrix <InlineMath math="AB" />:

<BlockMath math="|AB| = (-43 \cdot -1) - (18 \cdot 6) = 43 - 108 = -65" />

**Step <InlineMath math="5" />: Compare <InlineMath math="|AB|" /> with <InlineMath math="|A||B|" />**

We have obtained <InlineMath math="|A| = 13" />, <InlineMath math="|B| = -5" />, and <InlineMath math="|AB| = -65" />.

Calculate <InlineMath math="|A||B|" />:

<BlockMath math="|A||B| = 13 \cdot (-5) = -65" />

Notice that the value of <InlineMath math="|AB|" /> is the same as the value of <InlineMath math="|A||B|" />.

**Formula for the Property of Determinant of Matrix Product**

If <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> are two square matrices of the same order, then the determinant of the product of matrices <InlineMath math="A" /> and <InlineMath math="B" /> is equal to the product of their individual determinants.

<BlockMath math="|AB| = |A||B|" />

### Determinant of a Matrix with Scalar Multiplication

Now, investigate what happens to the determinant of a matrix if each element of the matrix is multiplied by a scalar (constant).

Suppose we use matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> from the previous example and a scalar <InlineMath math="k=2" />.

<BlockMath math="A = \begin{bmatrix} -5 & 2 \\ 1 & -3 \end{bmatrix}" />

We already know that <InlineMath math="|A| = 13" />. Matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> is a <InlineMath math="2 \times 2" /> order matrix, so <InlineMath math="n=2" />.

**Step <InlineMath math="1" />: Determine Matrix <InlineMath math="kA" />**

Multiply each element of matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> by the scalar <InlineMath math="k=2" />:

<BlockMath math="kA = 2A = 2\begin{bmatrix} -5 & 2 \\ 1 & -3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2(-5) & 2(2) \\ 2(1) & 2(-3) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -10 & 4 \\ 2 & -6 \end{bmatrix}" />

**Step <InlineMath math="2" />: Calculate the Determinant of Matrix <InlineMath math="kA" /> (<InlineMath math="|kA|" />)**

The determinant of matrix <InlineMath math="kA" /> is:

<BlockMath math="|kA| = (-10 \cdot -6) - (4 \cdot 2) = 60 - 8 = 52" />

**Step <InlineMath math="3" />: Compare <InlineMath math="|kA|" /> with <InlineMath math="k^n|A|" />**

We have <InlineMath math="|kA| = 52" />. The scalar <InlineMath math="k=2" />, the order of the matrix <InlineMath math="n=2" />, and <InlineMath math="|A|=13" />.

Calculate <InlineMath math="k^n|A|" />:

<BlockMath math="k^n|A| = 2^2 \cdot 13 = 4 \cdot 13 = 52" />

Notice that the value of <InlineMath math="|kA|" /> is the same as the value of <InlineMath math="k^n|A|" />.

**Formula for the Property of Determinant of Scalar Multiplication**

If <InlineMath math="A" /> is a square matrix of order <InlineMath math="n \times n" /> and <InlineMath math="k" /> is a scalar, then the determinant of matrix <InlineMath math="kA" /> is <InlineMath math="k^n" /> multiplied by the determinant of matrix <InlineMath math="A" />.

<BlockMath math="|kA| = k^n |A|" />

Here, <InlineMath math="n" /> is the order (number of rows or columns) of the square matrix <InlineMath math="A" />.

## Other Determinant Properties

The product and whole-matrix scaling laws are part of a larger set of useful rules. In the table, <InlineMath math="A" /> is square and every row operation produces a new matrix <InlineMath math="B" />.

| Change or matrix form | Effect on the determinant |
| :-------------------- | :------------------------ |
| Transpose | <InlineMath math="\det(A^T)=\det(A)" /> |
| Inverse, when <InlineMath math="\det(A)\neq0" /> | <InlineMath math="\det(A^{-1})=1/\det(A)" /> |
| Triangular matrix <InlineMath math="U" /> | <InlineMath math="\det(U)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}u_{ii}" /> |
| Swap two rows, <InlineMath math="R_i\leftrightarrow R_j" /> | <InlineMath math="\det(B)=-\det(A)" /> |
| Multiply one row by <InlineMath math="k" /> | <InlineMath math="\det(B)=k\det(A)" /> |
| Add a multiple of one row to another, <InlineMath math="R_i\leftarrow R_i+kR_j" /> | <InlineMath math="\det(B)=\det(A)" /> |
| Two equal rows, or one zero row | <InlineMath math="\det(A)=0" /> |

Do not confuse scaling one row with scaling the whole matrix. In an <InlineMath math="n\times n" /> matrix, <InlineMath math="kA" /> scales all <InlineMath math="n" /> rows by <InlineMath math="k" />, so the factor <InlineMath math="k" /> appears <InlineMath math="n" /> times and gives <InlineMath math="k^n" />.

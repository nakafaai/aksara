## Turning Matrix Rows into Columns

A matrix transpose <Highlight>is formed by swapping rows and columns</Highlight>. If entry <InlineMath math="a_{ij}" /> is in row <InlineMath math="i" /> and column <InlineMath math="j" /> of <InlineMath math="A" />, the same entry is in row <InlineMath math="j" /> and column <InlineMath math="i" /> of <InlineMath math="A^T" />.

The transpose of <InlineMath math="A" /> is written as <InlineMath math="A^T" /> or <InlineMath math="A'" />. If <InlineMath math="A" /> has dimensions <InlineMath math="m \times n" />, then <InlineMath math="A^T" /> has dimensions <InlineMath math="n \times m" /> and satisfies <InlineMath math="(A^T)_{ji} = a_{ij}" />.

## How to Determine the Matrix Transpose

To obtain the matrix transpose, follow these steps:

1.  Write the **first row** of the original matrix as the first column of the transpose matrix.
2.  Write the second row of the original matrix as the second column of the transpose matrix.
3.  Continue this process for all rows in the original matrix.
### General Matrix

Suppose we have matrix <InlineMath math="A" />:

<BlockMath math="A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}" />

Then, the transpose of matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> is:

<BlockMath math="A^T = \begin{bmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{bmatrix}" />

The first row <InlineMath math="\begin{bmatrix} a & b \end{bmatrix}" /> becomes the **first column**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix}" />

The second row <InlineMath math="\begin{bmatrix} c & d \end{bmatrix}" /> becomes the second column:

<BlockMath math="\begin{bmatrix} c \\ d \end{bmatrix}" />
### Matrix with Different Order

Given matrix <InlineMath math="B" /> with **order** <InlineMath math="2 \times 3" />:

<BlockMath math="B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix}" />

The transpose of matrix <InlineMath math="B" />, denoted <InlineMath math="B^T" />, will have order <InlineMath math="3 \times 2" />:

<BlockMath math="B^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix}" />

- The first row of <InlineMath math="B" /> (<InlineMath math="\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}" />) becomes the first column of <InlineMath math="B^T" />.
- The second row of <InlineMath math="B" /> (<InlineMath math="\begin{bmatrix} 4 & 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix}" />) becomes the second column of <InlineMath math="B^T" />.

Place every entry where it sits on the page: its column number across and its row number down. The six entries of <InlineMath math="B" /> fill two rows. Transposing reflects each entry across the main diagonal, the line through <InlineMath math="b_{11}" /> and <InlineMath math="b_{22}" />. The <InlineMath math="3" /> in row 1, column 3 travels to row 3, column 1. The <InlineMath math="2" /> and the <InlineMath math="4" /> trade places. The <InlineMath math="1" /> and the <InlineMath math="5" /> lie on the diagonal, so they stay put.

### Column Matrix to Row Matrix

If <InlineMath math="C" /> is a column matrix:

<BlockMath math="C = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}" />

Then its transpose, <InlineMath math="C^T" />, is a **row matrix**:

<BlockMath math="C^T = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}" />
### Transpose of a Square Matrix

Given a **square matrix** <InlineMath math="D" />:

<BlockMath math="D = \begin{bmatrix} -7 & 8 & 1 & 3 \\ 5 & 9 & 7 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 1 & -6 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}" />

Then its transpose, <InlineMath math="D^T" />, is also a square matrix:

<BlockMath math="D^T = \begin{bmatrix} -7 & 5 & 2 & 1 \\ 8 & 9 & 2 & -6 \\ 1 & 7 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 2 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}" />
## Properties of Matrix Transpose

Each identity assumes compatible dimensions for the stated operations. For a product, the **order of the factors reverses** after transposing.

| Identity | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <InlineMath math="(A^T)^T = A" /> | The transpose of a transpose matrix is the matrix itself |
| <InlineMath math="(A + B)^T = A^T + B^T" /> | Transpose of the sum of two matrices |
| <InlineMath math="(A - B)^T = A^T - B^T" /> | Transpose of the subtraction of two matrices |
| <InlineMath math="(kA)^T = kA^T" /> | , where <InlineMath math="k" /> is a scalar |
| <InlineMath math="(AB)^T = B^T A^T" /> | Transpose of the product of two matrices, note the reversed order |

### Why the Factor Order Reverses

Take the row matrix <InlineMath math="E=[1\;2]" /> and column matrix <InlineMath math="F=[3\;4]^T" />. The product <InlineMath math="EF" /> has **only one entry**. Its transpose must still have one entry.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="EF=[1(3)+2(4)]=[11]" />
  <BlockMath math="F^TE^T=\begin{bmatrix}3&amp;4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\end{bmatrix}=[3(1)+4(2)]=[11]" />
  <BlockMath math="E^TF^T=\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3&amp;4\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}3&amp;4\\6&amp;8\end{bmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

Without reversing the order, we obtain a <InlineMath math="2\times2" /> matrix, rather than the <InlineMath math="1\times1" /> transpose of the product.
## Exercises

Determine the transpose of the following matrices and state the **type of the resulting matrix** (e.g., row matrix, column matrix, square matrix).

1.  Matrix <InlineMath math="A" />:

    <BlockMath math="A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & -5 \end{bmatrix}" />

2.  Matrix <InlineMath math="B" />:

    <BlockMath math="B = \begin{bmatrix} 9 & -1 \\ 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 5 \end{bmatrix}" />

3.  Matrix <InlineMath math="C" />:

    <BlockMath math="C = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 6 & -2 \\ 4 & 5 & -7 \end{bmatrix}" />
### Worked Solutions

1.  The transpose of <InlineMath math="A" /> is:

    <BlockMath math="A^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \\ -5 \end{bmatrix}" />

    The single row of <InlineMath math="A" /> becomes a **single column**. Thus, <InlineMath math="A^T" /> has order <InlineMath math="3 \times 1" /> and is a column matrix.

2.  The transpose of <InlineMath math="B" /> is:

    <BlockMath math="B^T = \begin{bmatrix} 9 & 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 & 5 \end{bmatrix}" />

    Each row of <InlineMath math="B" /> becomes a column of <InlineMath math="B^T" />. The dimensions change from <InlineMath math="3 \times 2" /> to <InlineMath math="2 \times 3" />, so the result is a rectangular matrix.

3.  The transpose of <InlineMath math="C" /> is:

    <BlockMath math="C^T = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 & 5 \\ 1 & -2 & -7 \end{bmatrix}" />

    Reading the columns of <InlineMath math="C" /> as the rows of <InlineMath math="C^T" /> gives the matrix above. Its order remains <InlineMath math="3 \times 3" />, so it is still a square matrix.

## Matrix

Tables often organize information into rows and columns. A class score sheet, for example, can place one student in each row and one subject in each column. A **matrix** uses the same rectangular arrangement to organize numbers for mathematical work.

The numbers inside a matrix are its **elements** or **entries**. We usually enclose them in parentheses <InlineMath math="()" /> or square brackets <InlineMath math="[]" />. An entry's row and column determine which quantity it represents, so moving it changes its meaning.

Consider the Mathematics and English scores of three students:

| Student | Mathematics | English |
| :------ | :---------- | :------ |
| Aisyah  | <InlineMath math="80" /> | <InlineMath math="75" /> |
| Alex    | <InlineMath math="70" /> | <InlineMath math="95" /> |
| Wayan   | <InlineMath math="95" /> | <InlineMath math="75" /> |

Keeping only the numerical entries gives the matrix:

<BlockMath math="A = \begin{bmatrix} 80 & 75 \\ 70 & 95 \\ 95 & 75 \end{bmatrix}" />

The same matrix can also be written with parentheses:

<BlockMath math="A = \begin{pmatrix} 80 & 75 \\ 70 & 95 \\ 95 & 75 \end{pmatrix}" />

<InlineMath math="80, 75, 70, 95, 95, 75" /> are the elements of matrix <InlineMath math="A" />.

## Rows Columns and Elements

Three terms describe a matrix's structure:

1.  A **row** is a horizontal sequence of elements.
2.  A **column** is a vertical sequence of elements.
3.  An **element** is one entry at a particular row and column.

For matrix <InlineMath math="A" />:

<BlockMath math="A = \begin{bmatrix} 80 & 75 \\ 70 & 95 \\ 95 & 75 \end{bmatrix}" />

- **Row** <InlineMath math="1" /> is <InlineMath math="[80 \ 75]" />
- **Row** <InlineMath math="2" /> is <InlineMath math="[70 \ 95]" />
- **Row** <InlineMath math="3" /> is <InlineMath math="[95 \ 75]" />

- **Column** <InlineMath math="1" /> is <InlineMath math="\begin{bmatrix} 80 \\ 70 \\ 95 \end{bmatrix}" />
- **Column** <InlineMath math="2" /> is <InlineMath math="\begin{bmatrix} 75 \\ 95 \\ 75 \end{bmatrix}" />

An element of matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> is written <InlineMath math="a_{ij}" />. The first index <InlineMath math="i" /> gives the row, and the second index <InlineMath math="j" /> gives the column.

<BlockMath math="A=[a_{ij}]_{m\times n},\qquad 1\le i\le m,\qquad 1\le j\le n" />

This notation also records the valid index range: a row index cannot exceed <InlineMath math="m" />, and a column index cannot exceed <InlineMath math="n" />.

For the matrix above:

- <InlineMath math="a_{11}" /> is in row <InlineMath math="1" />, column <InlineMath math="1" />, so <InlineMath math="a_{11}=80" />.
- <InlineMath math="a_{12}" /> is in row <InlineMath math="1" />, column <InlineMath math="2" />, so <InlineMath math="a_{12}=75" />.
- <InlineMath math="a_{21}" /> is in row <InlineMath math="2" />, column <InlineMath math="1" />, so <InlineMath math="a_{21}=70" />.
- <InlineMath math="a_{22}" /> is in row <InlineMath math="2" />, column <InlineMath math="2" />, so <InlineMath math="a_{22}=95" />.
- <InlineMath math="a_{31}" /> is in row <InlineMath math="3" />, column <InlineMath math="1" />, so <InlineMath math="a_{31}=95" />.
- <InlineMath math="a_{32}" /> is in row <InlineMath math="3" />, column <InlineMath math="2" />, so <InlineMath math="a_{32}=75" />.

## Dimensions of a Matrix

The **dimensions** of a matrix state its number of rows first and columns second. A matrix with <InlineMath math="m" /> rows and <InlineMath math="n" /> columns has dimensions <InlineMath math="m \times n" />, read "<InlineMath math="m" /> by <InlineMath math="n" />."

For matrix <InlineMath math="A" />:

<BlockMath math="A = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 80 & 75 \\ 70 & 95 \\ 95 & 75 \end{bmatrix}}_{\text{2 columns}} \hspace{0.1em} \left. \vphantom{\begin{bmatrix} 80 \\ 70 \\ 95 \end{bmatrix}} \right\} \hspace{0.1em} \text{3 rows}" />

Matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> has <InlineMath math="3" /> rows and <InlineMath math="2" /> columns, so its dimensions are <InlineMath math="3 \times 2" />. We may write this as <InlineMath math="A_{3 \times 2}" />.

**Another example:**

Suppose a semester attendance table records excused, sick, and unexcused absences:

| Student | Excused | Sick | Unexcused |
| :------ | :------ | :--- | :-------- |
| Aisyah  | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="0" /> |
| Alex    | <InlineMath math="3" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| Wayan   | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="2" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |

Its numerical entries form matrix <InlineMath math="B" />:

<BlockMath math="B = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{\text{3 columns}} \hspace{0.1em} \left. \vphantom{\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}} \right\} \hspace{0.1em} \text{3 rows}" />

Matrix <InlineMath math="B" /> has <InlineMath math="3" /> rows and <InlineMath math="3" /> columns. Its dimensions are therefore <InlineMath math="3 \times 3" />, written <InlineMath math="B_{3 \times 3}" />.

For example:

- <InlineMath math="b_{11} = 2" /> is the element in row <InlineMath math="1" />, column <InlineMath math="1" />.
- <InlineMath math="b_{23} = 1" /> is the element in row <InlineMath math="2" />, column <InlineMath math="3" />.
- <InlineMath math="b_{32} = 2" /> is the element in row <InlineMath math="3" />, column <InlineMath math="2" />.

## General Matrix Notation

A matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> with <InlineMath math="m" /> rows and <InlineMath math="n" /> columns can be written as:

<BlockMath math="A_{m \times n} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} & \dots & a_{1n} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} & \dots & a_{2n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ a_{m1} & a_{m2} & \dots & a_{mn} \end{bmatrix}}_{\text{n columns}} \hspace{0.1em} \left. \vphantom{\begin{bmatrix} a_{11} \\ a_{21} \\ \vdots \\ a_{m1} \end{bmatrix}} \right\} \hspace{0.1em} \text{m rows}" />

The notation tells us:

- <InlineMath math="A_{m \times n}" /> is matrix <InlineMath math="A" /> with dimensions <InlineMath math="m \times n" />.
- <InlineMath math="m" /> is the number of rows.
- <InlineMath math="n" /> is the number of columns.
- <InlineMath math="a_{ij}" /> is the element in row <InlineMath math="i" /> and column <InlineMath math="j" />.

Matrix types and operations use row and column positions to identify which entries are compared, combined, or transformed.

## Mathematical Modelling Turns a Problem into an Equation

**Mathematical modelling** is the process of using mathematics to represent and solve problems such as taxi fares, garden design and bacterial growth. A model is an equation or function that links the variables in the problem. The process repeats: after a model is tested against data, it's **refined** if its predictions aren't accurate enough. Common forms are **linear** for a constant rate of change, **quadratic** for optimisation problems and **exponential** for growth at a constant percentage. In the models here, you fit a line to taxi fare data, find the largest area for a garden and tune an exponential model to bacteria data.

## The Modelling Process

The modelling process has six steps that form a cycle.

Assumptions simplify a problem. For example, we might assume the fuel price stays fixed or that there's no traffic congestion. Assumptions that are too simple give a less accurate model, so **every assumption must be stated**.

### Example Building and Using a Linear Model

From the data, the fare rises by <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,2.40" /> for every <InlineMath math="2\text{ km}" />. Build **the fare model** and predict the fare for a <InlineMath math="15\text{ km}" /> trip.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="m = \frac{7.8 - 5.4}{4 - 2} = 1.2" />
<BlockMath math="5.4 = 1.2(2) + c \quad\Rightarrow\quad c = 3" />
<BlockMath math="y = 1.2(15) + 3 = 21" />
</MathContainer>

The model <InlineMath math="y = 1.2x + 3" /> means a base fare of <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,3" /> and <InlineMath math="\text{RM}\,1.20" /> per kilometre. Verify with the last point: <InlineMath math="1.2(10) + 3 = 15" />, the same as the data.

### Assumptions of the Fare Model

The model assumes **no extra charges** for waiting, tolls or night trips. If the company charges a peak-hour surcharge, the model needs refining with an extra variable.

### Example The Largest Area

Find **the width that gives the largest area** and that area.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="A = 40x - 2x^2 = -2(x - 10)^2 + 200" />
<BlockMath math="x = 10 \quad\Rightarrow\quad A_{\max} = 200\text{ m}^2" />
</MathContainer>

The length is <InlineMath math="40 - 2(10) = 20\text{ m}" />. Check with the model: at <InlineMath math="w = 10" />, the area reads <InlineMath math="200" />, and at <InlineMath math="w = 9" /> or <InlineMath math="w = 11" />, the area is <InlineMath math="198" />, a little less.

### Assumptions of the Garden Model

The model assumes **the wall is long enough** and all <InlineMath math="40\text{ m}" /> of fencing is used. If the wall is only <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" />, the garden's length can't be more than <InlineMath math="15\text{ m}" /> and the solution changes. In that case, <InlineMath math="40 - 2x \le 15" /> gives <InlineMath math="x \ge 12.5" />, so the best width is <InlineMath math="12.5\text{ m}" /> with an area of <InlineMath math="12.5 \times 15 = 187.5\text{ m}^2" />.

### Example Verifying an Exponential Model

<BlockMath math="N = 200(1.3)^t, \qquad N(5) = 200(1.3)^5 = 742.6" />

The actual count at hour five is <InlineMath math="743" />, so the model is good enough. ### Assumptions of the Bacteria Model

## Common Mistakes about Mathematical Modelling

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | How to check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Not stating assumptions. | Assumptions decide when the model can be trusted. | List every assumption before building the model. |
| Choosing a linear model for growth at a constant percentage. | Constant-percentage growth gives a constant ratio, not a constant difference. | Work out the differences and ratios between consecutive data. |
| Using a model far outside the range of the data. | Predictions outside the range may break the assumptions. | Compare the input with the range of the original data. |
| Stopping once the equation is found. | A model must be verified against data before it's used. | Substitute data values into the model. |

The second mistake is the most common one in model choice questions. **A constant difference means linear, a constant ratio means exponential**.

## Practice on Mathematical Modelling

These questions move from linear to exponential models. **State one assumption** for every model.

1. A tank holds <InlineMath math="500\text{ L}" /> of water and leaks <InlineMath math="12\text{ L}" /> an hour. Build a model for the volume and find when the tank is empty.

2. A <InlineMath math="60\text{ cm}" /> wire is bent into a rectangle. Find the dimensions that give the largest area.

3. A town's population is <InlineMath math="10{,}000" />, <InlineMath math="10{,}500" />, <InlineMath math="11{,}025" /> over three consecutive years. Decide whether a linear or an exponential model fits better, and build it.

4. Explain why the model in question three might need refining after <InlineMath math="30" /> years.

### Solutions

**Solution 1**: The volume falls at a constant rate, so use a linear model <InlineMath math="V = 500 - 12t" />:

<BlockMath math="0 = 500 - 12t \quad\Rightarrow\quad t = \frac{500}{12} = 41.7 \text{ hours}" />

**Assumption**: the leak rate stays the same even as the water level falls.

**Solution 2**: If the width is <InlineMath math="x" />, the length is <InlineMath math="30 - x" />:

<BlockMath math="A = x(30 - x) = -(x - 15)^2 + 225" />

The largest area is <InlineMath math="225\text{ cm}^2" /> when <InlineMath math="x = 15\text{ cm}" />, a square. **Assumption**: the wire doesn't overlap at the corners.

**Solution 3**: The differences are <InlineMath math="500" /> and <InlineMath math="525" />, not constant, but the ratios are <InlineMath math="\dfrac{10{,}500}{10{,}000} = 1.05" /> and <InlineMath math="\dfrac{11{,}025}{10{,}500} = 1.05" />, constant. So an exponential model fits better:

<BlockMath math="P = 10{,}000(1.05)^t" />

**Assumption**: the growth rate stays at <InlineMath math="5\%" /> a year.

**Solution 4**: The model predicts <InlineMath math="10{,}000(1.05)^{30} = 43{,}219" /> people. Over a long period, **land, housing and jobs are limited**, so the growth rate usually falls. The model needs refining with new data.

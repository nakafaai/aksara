## Understanding the Concept of Integrals

Differentiation turns a position function <InlineMath math="s(t)" /> into its velocity <InlineMath math="v(t)=s'(t)" />. Indefinite integration reverses that step: from <InlineMath math="v(t)" />, it recovers a family of possible position functions. This reverse process is **antidifferentiation**, and the resulting family is the **indefinite integral**. An initial position is still needed to select one member of that family.

## Why the Constant Matters

Compare these functions and their derivatives.

| Function <InlineMath math="F(x)" /> | Derivative <InlineMath math="f(x) = F'(x)" /> |
| :--- | :--- |
| <InlineMath math="F(x) = 2x^2 + 1" /> | <InlineMath math="f(x) = 4x" /> |
| <InlineMath math="F(x) = 2x^2 + 5" /> | <InlineMath math="f(x) = 4x" /> |
| <InlineMath math="F(x) = 2x^2 - 10" /> | <InlineMath math="f(x) = 4x" /> |
| <InlineMath math="F(x) = 2x^2" /> | <InlineMath math="f(x) = 4x" /> |

All four <InlineMath math="F(x)" /> functions have the same derivative, <InlineMath math="f(x) = 4x" />. The reason is that the derivative of a constant, such as <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="5" />, or <InlineMath math="-10" />, is zero.

Differentiation therefore erases the original constant. When we reverse the process, the derivative alone cannot tell us whether that constant was <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="5" />, <InlineMath math="-1000" />, or another value. The symbol <InlineMath math="C" /> represents every possibility, so the result is a family of antiderivatives rather than one function.

## Formal Notation of Integrals

The formal notation records the integrand, the variable of integration, and the full family of antiderivatives:

<BlockMath math="\int f(x) \, dx = F(x) + C" />

Each part has a specific role:
- **<InlineMath math="\int" />** is the **integral symbol**; it looks like an elongated 'S', symbolizing "summation" or "accumulation."
- **<InlineMath math="f(x)" />** is the function we are going to integrate, called the **integrand**.
- **<InlineMath math="dx" />** is called the **differential** of <InlineMath math="x" />. This is a crucial part that indicates we are integrating with respect to the variable <InlineMath math="x" />. Without it, the integral expression is incomplete.
- **<InlineMath math="F(x) + C" />** is the **indefinite integral** of <InlineMath math="f(x)" />. This is our answer, where <InlineMath math="F(x)" /> is the antiderivative and <InlineMath math="C" /> is the **constant of integration**.

The defining relationship is:

<BlockMath math="\frac{d}{dx} [F(x)] = f(x) \quad \iff \quad \int f(x) \, dx = F(x) + C" />

> If a function <InlineMath math="F(x)" /> has derivative <InlineMath math="f(x)" />, then the indefinite integral of <InlineMath math="f(x)" /> is the family of all possible <InlineMath math="F(x)" /> functions, which we write as <InlineMath math="F(x) + C" />.

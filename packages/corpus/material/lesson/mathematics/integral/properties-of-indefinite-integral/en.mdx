## Power Rule

For a power of the integration variable, use:

<BlockMath math="\int x^n \, dx = \frac{1}{n+1}x^{n+1} + C, \quad \text{where } n \neq -1" />

Increase the exponent by one, then divide by that new exponent.

The value <InlineMath math="n=-1" /> is excluded because it would make the denominator zero. That case follows the logarithmic rule <InlineMath math="\int x^{-1}\,dx=\ln|x|+C" />.

For example, evaluate <InlineMath math="\int x^5 \, dx" />.

Here, <InlineMath math="n=5" />, so:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\int x^5 \, dx = \frac{1}{5+1}x^{5+1} + C" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{6}x^6 + C" />
</MathContainer>

## Constant Multiple Rule

A constant factor can be moved outside an integral:

<BlockMath math="\int a \cdot f(x) \, dx = a \int f(x) \, dx" />

> The rule requires <InlineMath math="a" /> to be constant with respect to the integration variable.

## Sum and Difference Rule

A sum or difference can be integrated term by term.

<BlockMath math="\int [f(x) \pm g(x)] \, dx = \int f(x) \, dx \pm \int g(x) \, dx" />

For example, to solve <InlineMath math="\int (x^4 - x^3) \, dx" />, we first separate them:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\int (x^4 - x^3) \, dx = \int x^4 \, dx - \int x^3 \, dx" />
<BlockMath math="= \left( \frac{1}{4+1}x^5 \right) - \left( \frac{1}{3+1}x^4 \right) + C" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{5}x^5 - \frac{1}{4}x^4 + C" />
</MathContainer>

Write one constant **<InlineMath math="C" />** at the end. Formally, we could use separate constants <InlineMath math="C_1" /> and <InlineMath math="C_2" /> for the two antiderivatives, but their sum or difference is again an arbitrary constant. A single **<InlineMath math="C" />** therefore covers every possible value of that constant.

## Substitution Rule

When an integrand contains a composite expression together with its derivative or a constant multiple of it, set the inner expression equal to a new variable, usually <InlineMath math="u=g(x)" />. Then <InlineMath math="du=g'(x)\,dx" /> replaces the matching derivative factor.

<BlockMath math="\int f(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \, dx = \int f(u) \, du" />

This change of variable is called **u-substitution**. It turns the nested integrand into <InlineMath math="f(u)" /> when the matching derivative factor is present.

## Exercises

1.  Find the result of <InlineMath math="\int 6x^2 \, dx" />!
2.  Find the result of <InlineMath math="\int (3x^2 + 2x - 5) \, dx" />!
3.  Solve the integral <InlineMath math="\int 2x(x^2 + 3)^4 \, dx" /> using the substitution rule!

### Worked Solutions

1.  To solve <InlineMath math="\int 6x^2 \, dx" />, we can use the **Constant Multiple Rule** and the **Power Rule**.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Pull the constant <InlineMath math="6" /> out of the integral.

    <BlockMath math="\int 6x^2 \, dx = 6 \int x^2 \, dx" />

    **Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Use the power rule on <InlineMath math="\int x^2 \, dx" />, where <InlineMath math="n=2" />.

    <MathContainer>
    <BlockMath math="6 \left( \frac{1}{2+1}x^{2+1} \right) + C" />
    <BlockMath math="= 6 \left( \frac{1}{3}x^3 \right) + C" />
    </MathContainer>

    **Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Multiply the constants to get the final result.

    <BlockMath math="= 2x^3 + C" />

2.  For the integral <InlineMath math="\int (3x^2 + 2x - 5) \, dx" />, we use the **Sum and Difference Rule** to break it down into three separate integrals.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Separate each term into its own integral.

    <BlockMath math="\int 3x^2 \, dx + \int 2x \, dx - \int 5 \, dx" />

    **Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Solve each integral one by one using the power and constant rules.

    <MathContainer>
    <BlockMath math="= 3\left(\frac{1}{3}x^3\right) + 2\left(\frac{1}{2}x^2\right) - 5x + C" />
    <BlockMath math="= x^3 + x^2 - 5x + C" />
    </MathContainer>

    So, the answer is <InlineMath math="x^3 + x^2 - 5x + C" />.

3.  The integral <InlineMath math="\int 2x(x^2 + 3)^4 \, dx" /> is a classic example for the **Substitution Rule**.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Choose a part of the function to be <InlineMath math="u" />. A good choice is the part inside the parentheses.

    Let: <InlineMath math="u = x^2 + 3" />.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Find the derivative of <InlineMath math="u" /> with respect to <InlineMath math="x" />, which is <InlineMath math="du/dx" />.

    <BlockMath math="\frac{du}{dx} = 2x" />

    From this, we can write <InlineMath math="du = 2x \, dx" />.

    **Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Perform the substitution. Replace <InlineMath math="x^2+3" /> with <InlineMath math="u" /> and <InlineMath math="2x \, dx" /> with <InlineMath math="du" />.

    <BlockMath math="\int (x^2 + 3)^4 (2x \, dx) = \int u^4 \, du" />

    **Step** <InlineMath math="4" />: Solve the simplified integral using the power rule.

    <BlockMath math="\int u^4 \, du = \frac{1}{5}u^5 + C" />

    **Step** <InlineMath math="5" />: Substitute <InlineMath math="u" /> back to its original form.

    <BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{5}(x^2 + 3)^5 + C" />

    This is the final result.

## Power Rule

For a power of the integration variable, use:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\int x^n \, dx = \frac{1}{n+1}x^{n+1} + C, \\ &amp;\text{where } n \neq -1 \end{aligned}" />

Increase the exponent by one, then divide by the **new exponent**. Apply the formula on an interval where the integrand is defined. For an arbitrary real exponent, we can use an interval with <InlineMath math="x>0" />.

The value <InlineMath math="n=-1" /> is excluded because it would make the denominator zero. That case follows [the logarithmic rule](/en/subjects/mathematics/exponential-logarithm/logarithm-definition) <InlineMath math="\int x^{-1}\,dx=\ln|x|+C" />.

For example, evaluate <InlineMath math="\int x^5 \, dx" />.

Here, <InlineMath math="n=5" />, so:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\int x^5 \, dx = \frac{1}{5+1}x^{5+1} + C" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{6}x^6 + C" />
</MathContainer>

## Constant Multiple Rule

A constant factor <Highlight>can be moved outside an integral</Highlight>:

If <InlineMath math="F'(x)=f(x)" />, multiplying an antiderivative by a constant gives:

<BlockMath math="\int a f(x)\,dx=aF(x)+C" />

Keep the constant of integration, including when <InlineMath math="a=0" />. For example, the factor <InlineMath math="-3" /> in <InlineMath math="-3x^2" /> remains after integration:

<BlockMath math="\int -3x^2\,dx=-3\frac{x^3}{3}+C=-x^3+C" />

> The rule requires <InlineMath math="a" /> to be constant with respect to the integration variable.

## Sum and Difference Rule

A sum or difference can be integrated **term by term**.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;\int [f(x) \pm g(x)] \, dx = \int f(x) \, dx \\ &amp;\pm \int g(x) \, dx \end{aligned}" />

For example, to solve <InlineMath math="\int (x^4 - x^3) \, dx" />, we first separate them:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\int (x^4 - x^3) \, dx = \int x^4 \, dx - \int x^3 \, dx" />
<BlockMath math="= \left( \frac{1}{4+1}x^5 \right) - \left( \frac{1}{3+1}x^4 \right) + C" />
<BlockMath math="= \frac{1}{5}x^5 - \frac{1}{4}x^4 + C" />
</MathContainer>

Write one constant <InlineMath math="C" /> at the end. Formally, we could use separate constants <InlineMath math="C_1" /> and <InlineMath math="C_2" /> for the two antiderivatives, but their sum or difference is again an arbitrary constant. A single <InlineMath math="C" /> therefore covers every possible value of that constant.

## Substitution Rule

When an integrand contains a [composite expression](/en/subjects/mathematics/function-composition-inverse-function/function-composition) together with its derivative or a constant multiple of it, set the inner expression equal to a new variable, usually <InlineMath math="u=g(x)" />. Then <InlineMath math="du=g'(x)\,dx" /> replaces the matching derivative factor.

<BlockMath math="\int f(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \, dx = \int f(u) \, du" />

This change of variable is called **u-substitution**. It turns the nested integrand into <InlineMath math="f(u)" /> when the matching derivative factor is present.

## Exercises

The first two problems use **linearity of integration** and the power rule. In the third, the expression <InlineMath math="x^2+3" /> appears alongside its derivative <InlineMath math="2x" />, suggesting substitution.

1.  Find the result of <InlineMath math="\int 6x^2 \, dx" />!
2.  Find the result of <InlineMath math="\int (3x^2 + 2x - 5) \, dx" />!
3.  Solve the integral <InlineMath math="\int 2x(x^2 + 3)^4 \, dx" /> using the substitution rule!

### Integrating a Constant Multiple

To solve <InlineMath math="\int 6x^2 \, dx" />, we can use the **Constant Multiple Rule** and the **Power Rule**.

**Step 1**: Pull the constant <InlineMath math="6" /> out of the integral.

<BlockMath math="\int 6x^2 \, dx = 6 \int x^2 \, dx" />

**Step 2**: Use the power rule on <InlineMath math="\int x^2 \, dx" />, where <InlineMath math="n=2" />.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\int 6x^2\,dx=6\left(\frac{x^{2+1}}{2+1}\right)+C" />
<BlockMath math="= 6 \left( \frac{1}{3}x^3 \right) + C" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 3**: Multiply the constants to get the final result.

<BlockMath math="\int 6x^2\,dx=2x^3+C" />

### Integrating Three Terms

For the integral <InlineMath math="\int (3x^2 + 2x - 5) \, dx" />, we use the **Sum and Difference Rule** to break it down into three separate integrals.

**Step 1**: Separate each term into its own integral.

<BlockMath math="\int(3x^2+2x-5)\,dx=\int3x^2\,dx+\int2x\,dx-\int5\,dx" />

**Step 2**: Solve each integral one by one using the power and constant rules.

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\int(3x^2+2x-5)\,dx=3\left(\frac{x^3}{3}\right)+2\left(\frac{x^2}{2}\right)-5x+C" />
<BlockMath math="= x^3 + x^2 - 5x + C" />
</MathContainer>

So, the answer is <InlineMath math="x^3 + x^2 - 5x + C" />.

### Changing the Variable by Substitution

The integral <InlineMath math="\int 2x(x^2 + 3)^4 \, dx" /> allows **substitution** because the factor <InlineMath math="2x" /> is the derivative of the expression in parentheses.

**Step 1**: Choose a part of the function to be <InlineMath math="u" />. A good choice is the part inside the parentheses.

Let: <InlineMath math="u = x^2 + 3" />.

**Step 2**: Find the derivative of <InlineMath math="u" /> with respect to <InlineMath math="x" />, which is <InlineMath math="du/dx" />.

<BlockMath math="\frac{du}{dx} = 2x" />

From this, we can write <InlineMath math="du = 2x \, dx" />.

**Step 3**: Perform the substitution. Replace <InlineMath math="x^2+3" /> with <InlineMath math="u" /> and <InlineMath math="2x \, dx" /> with <InlineMath math="du" />.

<BlockMath math="\int (x^2 + 3)^4 (2x \, dx) = \int u^4 \, du" />

**Step 4**: Solve the simplified integral using the power rule.

<BlockMath math="\int u^4 \, du = \frac{1}{5}u^5 + C" />

**Step 5**: Substitute <InlineMath math="u" /> back to its original form.

<BlockMath math="\int2x(x^2+3)^4\,dx=\frac15(x^2+3)^5+C" />

Check the answer by **differentiating it**. The chain rule returns the original integrand:

<BlockMath math="\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac15(x^2+3)^5+C\right]=\frac15\cdot5(x^2+3)^4\cdot2x=2x(x^2+3)^4" />

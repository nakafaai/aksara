## Understanding Tangent Lines to Circles

Imagine riding around a circular track. If you leave the track without turning the handlebars, your direction at that instant follows the track's **tangent line**. It is not just any line through that point: it is the unique line perpendicular to the radius there.

Mathematically, a tangent line meets a circle at exactly one point, called the **point of tangency**. The tangent is always **perpendicular** to the radius drawn to that point.

That perpendicular relationship is the thread through this lesson. We will use it to construct a tangent at a known point, find tangents with a given slope, and draw both tangents through a point outside the circle.

## Tangent Line Through a Point on the Circle

Start by checking that the proposed point of tangency really lies on the circle. Its radius vector then becomes a normal vector for the tangent.

For the circle <InlineMath math="(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2" /> and point of tangency <InlineMath math="T(x_1,y_1)" />, proceed as follows.

**Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Verify that <InlineMath math="T" /> lies on the circle.

<BlockMath math="(x_1-a)^2+(y_1-b)^2=r^2" />

**Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: The radius vector is normal to the tangent.

<BlockMath math="\vec{OT}=(x_1-a,\,y_1-b)" />

**Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Use that normal vector in the line equation through <InlineMath math="T" />.

<BlockMath math="(x_1-a)(x-x_1)+(y_1-b)(y-y_1)=0" />

Using the point-on-circle condition from Step <InlineMath math="1" />, this becomes the **point-tangent form**:

<BlockMath math="(x - a)(x_1 - a) + (y - b)(y_1 - b) = r^2" />

> Because this derivation uses a normal vector instead of a slope, it also handles vertical and horizontal tangents without a special case.

## Tangent Line with Given Slope

If the point of tangency is unknown but the slope is given, the intercept must place the line exactly one radius away from the center. This often appears when the tangent must be parallel to another line.

For circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = r^2" /> and known slope <InlineMath math="m" />, we substitute the line equation <InlineMath math="y = mx + c" /> into the circle equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + (mx + c)^2 = r^2" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + m^2x^2 + 2mcx + c^2 = r^2" />
  <BlockMath math="(1 + m^2)x^2 + 2mcx + (c^2 - r^2) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

Since the line is tangent to the circle, the discriminant of this quadratic equation must be zero:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\Delta = (2mc)^2 - 4(1 + m^2)(c^2 - r^2) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="4m^2c^2 - 4(1 + m^2)(c^2 - r^2) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="m^2c^2 - (1 + m^2)c^2 + (1 + m^2)r^2 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="-c^2 + (1 + m^2)r^2 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="c^2 = (1 + m^2)r^2" />
</MathContainer>

Solving for the intercept gives:

<BlockMath math="c = \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />

Thus, for every finite slope <InlineMath math="m" />, the centered circle has **two parallel tangent lines**:

<BlockMath math="y = mx \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />

Let <InlineMath math="P(x_0,y_0)" /> lie outside the circle <InlineMath math="(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2" />. Its **polar line** is:

<BlockMath math="(x-a)(x_0-a) + (y-b)(y_0-b) = r^2" />

For an outside point, this polar is the **chord of contact**, the line through both points of tangency. The construction is therefore:

1. Write the polar line.
2. Intersect it with the circle to find both points of tangency.
3. Use each contact point to construct one tangent through <InlineMath math="P" />.

> The polar is not itself one of the two tangents. It is the line joining their contact points.

## Worked Example

Find both tangents from <InlineMath math="B(2,6)" /> to the circle <InlineMath math="L \equiv (x+1)^2+(y-2)^2=9" />.

**Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Determine the polar line of <InlineMath math="B(2,6)" /> with respect to the circle.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(x-(-1))(2-(-1)) + (y-2)(6-2) = 9" />
  <BlockMath math="(x+1)(3) + (y-2)(4) = 9" />
  <BlockMath math="3x + 3 + 4y - 8 = 9" />
  <BlockMath math="3x + 4y = 14" />
</MathContainer>

**Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Find the intersection points of the polar line <InlineMath math="3x + 4y = 14" /> with the circle. From the line equation, we get <InlineMath math="x = \frac{14-4y}{3}" />. Substitute into the circle equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(\frac{14-4y}{3} + 1)^2 + (y-2)^2 = 9" />
  <BlockMath math="(\frac{17-4y}{3})^2 + (y-2)^2 = 9" />
</MathContainer>

**Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Solve the quadratic for the two possible <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinates.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(17-4y)^2+9(y-2)^2=81" />
  <BlockMath math="25y^2-172y+244=0" />
  <BlockMath math="y=\frac{172\pm\sqrt{172^2-4(25)(244)}}{50}=\frac{172\pm72}{50}" />
  <BlockMath math="y_1=\frac{122}{25}\quad\text{and}\quad y_2=2" />
</MathContainer>

**Step** <InlineMath math="4" />: Substitute each value into the polar line to obtain the points of tangency.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x_1=\frac{14-4\left(\frac{122}{25}\right)}{3}=-\frac{46}{25}" />
  <BlockMath math="x_2=\frac{14-4(2)}{3}=2" />
  <BlockMath math="T_1\left(-\frac{46}{25},\frac{122}{25}\right),\qquad T_2(2,2)" />
</MathContainer>

**Step** <InlineMath math="5" />: Use each point in the point-tangent form of the circle.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="-\frac{21}{25}(x+1)+\frac{72}{25}(y-2)=9\quad\Longrightarrow\quad -7x+24y=130" />
  <BlockMath math="3(x+1)=9\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=2" />
</MathContainer>

Both lines pass through <InlineMath math="B(2,6)" /> and touch the circle once, so these are the required tangents.

## Practice

1. Determine the equation of the tangent line to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 25" /> at point <InlineMath math="(3, 4)" />.

2. Find the equation of tangent lines to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 16" /> that are parallel to line <InlineMath math="y = 2x + 5" />.

3. Determine the equation of tangent lines to circle <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 8" /> passing through point <InlineMath math="(4, 2)" />.

4. A circle has the equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y - 3 = 0" />. Determine the equation of tangent lines that are perpendicular to line <InlineMath math="x + 2y = 7" />.

### Worked Solutions

1. **Solution**:

   For circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 25" /> with center <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r = 5" />, and tangent point <InlineMath math="T(3,4)" />.

   Using the tangent line formula: <InlineMath math="(x-a)(x_1-a) + (y-b)(y_1-b) = r^2" />

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="(x-0)(3-0) + (y-0)(4-0) = 25" />
     <BlockMath math="3x + 4y = 25" />
   </MathContainer>

   Therefore, the tangent line equation is <InlineMath math="3x + 4y = 25" />.

2. **Solution**:

   A line parallel to <InlineMath math="y = 2x + 5" /> has slope <InlineMath math="m = 2" />.

   For circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 16" /> with <InlineMath math="r = 4" />, using the formula:

   <BlockMath math="y = mx \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="y = 2x \pm 4\sqrt{1 + 4}" />
     <BlockMath math="y = 2x \pm 4\sqrt{5}" />
   </MathContainer>

   Therefore, there are two tangent lines: <InlineMath math="y = 2x + 4\sqrt{5}" /> and <InlineMath math="y = 2x - 4\sqrt{5}" />.

3. **Solution**:

   Circle <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 8" /> has center <InlineMath math="(1,-2)" /> and <InlineMath math="r^2 = 8" />.

   Polar line equation for point <InlineMath math="(4,2)" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="(x-1)(4-1) + (y-(-2))(2-(-2)) = 8" />
     <BlockMath math="3(x-1) + 4(y+2) = 8" />
     <BlockMath math="3x - 3 + 4y + 8 = 8" />
     <BlockMath math="3x + 4y = 3" />
   </MathContainer>

   Translate the center to the origin with <InlineMath math="u=x-1" /> and <InlineMath math="v=y+2" />. The circle and polar line become:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="u^2+v^2=8,\qquad 3u+4v=8" />
     <BlockMath math="u=\frac{8-4v}{3}" />
     <BlockMath math="25v^2-64v-8=0" />
     <BlockMath math="v=\frac{32\pm6\sqrt{34}}{25}" />
   </MathContainer>

   Substituting back gives the two points of tangency:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="T_1\left(\frac{49-8\sqrt{34}}{25},\frac{-18+6\sqrt{34}}{25}\right)" />
     <BlockMath math="T_2\left(\frac{49+8\sqrt{34}}{25},\frac{-18-6\sqrt{34}}{25}\right)" />
   </MathContainer>

   The slopes from <InlineMath math="(4,2)" /> to those points are <InlineMath math="12-2\sqrt{34}" /> and <InlineMath math="12+2\sqrt{34}" />. Therefore, the required tangent lines are:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="y-2=(12-2\sqrt{34})(x-4)" />
     <BlockMath math="y-2=(12+2\sqrt{34})(x-4)" />
   </MathContainer>

4. **Solution**:

   Convert the circle equation to standard form by completing the square:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y - 3 = 0" />
     <BlockMath math="(x^2 - 4x + 4) + (y^2 + 6y + 9) = 3 + 4 + 9" />
     <BlockMath math="(x-2)^2 + (y+3)^2 = 16" />
   </MathContainer>

   Center <InlineMath math="(2,-3)" />, radius <InlineMath math="r = 4" />.

   Line <InlineMath math="x + 2y = 7" /> can be written as <InlineMath math="y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{7}{2}" />, so its slope is <InlineMath math="m_1 = -\frac{1}{2}" />.

   A line perpendicular to it has slope <InlineMath math="m = 2" />.

   For the circle with center <InlineMath math="(2,-3)" />, the tangent line equation with slope <InlineMath math="2" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="y - (-3) = 2(x - 2) \pm 4\sqrt{1 + 4}" />
     <BlockMath math="y + 3 = 2x - 4 \pm 4\sqrt{5}" />
     <BlockMath math="y = 2x - 7 \pm 4\sqrt{5}" />
   </MathContainer>

   Therefore, the two tangent lines are: <InlineMath math="y = 2x - 7 + 4\sqrt{5}" /> and <InlineMath math="y = 2x - 7 - 4\sqrt{5}" />.

## Following the Tangent When Leaving a Circular Path

Imagine moving around a circular track. At each position, your **instantaneous direction of motion** follows the tangent at that point. Many lines pass through one point, but only one is tangent to the circle there.

Mathematically, a tangent line meets a circle at exactly one point, called the **point of tangency**. The tangent is always **perpendicular** <Highlight>to the radius drawn to that point</Highlight>.

The perpendicular relationship is used in all three cases: constructing a tangent at a known point, finding tangents with a given slope, and drawing both tangents through a point outside the circle.

For the circle <InlineMath math="(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2" /> with center <InlineMath math="C(a,b)" /> and point of tangency <InlineMath math="T(x_1,y_1)" />, proceed as follows.

**Step 1**: Verify that <InlineMath math="T" /> lies on the circle.

<BlockMath math="(x_1-a)^2+(y_1-b)^2=r^2" />

**Step 2**: The radius vector is normal to the tangent.

<BlockMath math="\overrightarrow{CT}=(x_1-a,\,y_1-b)" />

**Step 3**: Use that normal vector in the line equation through <InlineMath math="T" />.

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x_1-a)(x-x_1) +(y_1-b)(y-y_1) \\ &amp;=0 \end{aligned}" />

Using the point-on-circle condition from Step 1, this becomes the **point-tangent form**:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x - a)(x_1 - a) + (y - b)(y_1 - b) \\ &amp;= r^2 \end{aligned}" />

> This derivation uses a normal vector, so the same steps work for vertical and horizontal tangents.

For circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = r^2" /> and known slope <InlineMath math="m" />, we substitute the line equation <InlineMath math="y = mx + c" /> into the circle equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + (mx + c)^2 = r^2" />
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + m^2x^2 + 2mcx + c^2 = r^2" />
  <BlockMath math="(1 + m^2)x^2 + 2mcx + (c^2 - r^2) = 0" />
</MathContainer>

Since the line is tangent to the circle, the discriminant of this quadratic equation must be zero:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \Delta &amp;= (2mc)^2 - 4(1 + m^2)(c^2 - r^2) \\ &amp;= 0 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="4m^2c^2 - 4(1 + m^2)(c^2 - r^2) = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;m^2c^2 - (1 + m^2)c^2 + (1 + m^2)r^2 \\ &amp;= 0 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="-c^2 + (1 + m^2)r^2 = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="c^2 = (1 + m^2)r^2" />
</MathContainer>

Solving for the intercept gives:

<BlockMath math="c = \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />

For every finite slope <InlineMath math="m" />, the centered circle has **two parallel tangent lines**:

<BlockMath math="y = mx \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />

Let <InlineMath math="P(x_0,y_0)" /> lie outside the circle <InlineMath math="(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2" />. Its **polar line** is:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x-a)(x_0-a) + (y-b)(y_0-b) \\ &amp;= r^2 \end{aligned}" />

For an outside point, this polar is the **chord of contact**, the line through both points of tangency. The construction is therefore:

1. Write the polar line.
2. Intersect it with the circle to find both points of tangency.
3. Use each contact point to construct one tangent through <InlineMath math="P" />.

> The polar is not itself one of the two tangents. It is the line joining their contact points.

## Finding Two Tangents with the Polar Line

Find both tangents from <InlineMath math="B(2,6)" /> to the circle <InlineMath math="L \equiv (x+1)^2+(y-2)^2=9" />.

**Step 1**: Determine the polar line of <InlineMath math="B(2,6)" /> with respect to the circle.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x-(-1))(2-(-1)) \\ &amp;\quad{}+ (y-2)(6-2) = 9 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="(x+1)(3) + (y-2)(4) = 9" />
  <BlockMath math="3x + 3 + 4y - 8 = 9" />
  <BlockMath math="3x + 4y = 14" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 2**: Find the **intersection points of the polar line** <InlineMath math="3x + 4y = 14" /> with the circle. From the line equation, we get <InlineMath math="x = \frac{14-4y}{3}" />. Substitute into the circle equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(\frac{14-4y}{3} + 1)^2 + (y-2)^2 = 9" />
  <BlockMath math="(\frac{17-4y}{3})^2 + (y-2)^2 = 9" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 3**: Solve the quadratic for the two possible <InlineMath math="y" />-coordinates.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(17-4y)^2+9(y-2)^2=81" />
  <BlockMath math="25y^2-172y+244=0" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} y &amp;=\frac{172\pm\sqrt{172^2-4(25)(244)}}{50} \\ &amp;=\frac{172\pm72}{50} \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="y_1=\frac{122}{25}\quad\text{and}\quad y_2=2" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 4**: Substitute each value into the polar line to obtain the points of tangency.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x_1=\frac{14-4\left(\frac{122}{25}\right)}{3}=-\frac{46}{25}" />
  <BlockMath math="x_2=\frac{14-4(2)}{3}=2" />
  <BlockMath math="T_1\left(-\frac{46}{25},\frac{122}{25}\right),\qquad T_2(2,2)" />
</MathContainer>

**Step 5**: Use each point in the **point-tangent form** of the circle.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;-\frac{21}{25}(x+1)+\frac{72}{25}(y-2)=9 \\ &amp;\Longrightarrow \\ &amp;-7x+24y=130 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="3(x+1)=9\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=2" />
</MathContainer>

Both lines pass through <InlineMath math="B(2,6)" /> and touch the circle once, so these are the required tangents.

## Practice

Identify the **information that determines the tangents** in each problem. A point on the circle determines one tangent. A slope determines two parallel tangents, while an outside point determines two lines through that point. Use parallel or perpendicular relationships to find the slope when needed.

1. Determine the equation of the tangent line to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 25" /> at point <InlineMath math="(3, 4)" />.

2. Find the equation of tangent lines to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 16" /> that are parallel to line <InlineMath math="y = 2x + 5" />.

3. Determine the equation of tangent lines to circle <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 8" /> passing through point <InlineMath math="(4, 2)" />.

4. A circle has the equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y - 3 = 0" />. Determine the equation of tangent lines that are perpendicular to line <InlineMath math="x + 2y = 7" />.

### Worked Solutions

**Solution 1**:

For circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 25" /> with center <InlineMath math="O(0,0)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r = 5" />, and tangent point <InlineMath math="T(3,4)" />.

Using the tangent line formula: <InlineMath math="(x-a)(x_1-a) + (y-b)(y_1-b) = r^2" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x-0)(3-0) + (y-0)(4-0) \\ &amp;= 25 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="3x + 4y = 25" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, the tangent line equation is <InlineMath math="3x + 4y = 25" />.

**Solution 2**:

A line parallel to <InlineMath math="y = 2x + 5" /> has slope <InlineMath math="m = 2" />.

For circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 16" /> with <InlineMath math="r = 4" />, using the formula:

<BlockMath math="y = mx \pm r\sqrt{1 + m^2}" />

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="y = 2x \pm 4\sqrt{1 + 4}" />
  <BlockMath math="y = 2x \pm 4\sqrt{5}" />
</MathContainer>

Therefore, there are two tangent lines: <InlineMath math="y = 2x + 4\sqrt{5}" /> and <InlineMath math="y = 2x - 4\sqrt{5}" />.

**Solution 3**:

Circle <InlineMath math="(x-1)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 8" /> has center <InlineMath math="(1,-2)" /> and <InlineMath math="r^2 = 8" />.

Polar line equation for point <InlineMath math="(4,2)" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x-1)(4-1) \\ &amp;\quad{}+ (y-(-2))(2-(-2)) = 8 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="3(x-1) + 4(y+2) = 8" />
  <BlockMath math="3x - 3 + 4y + 8 = 8" />
  <BlockMath math="3x + 4y = 3" />
</MathContainer>

Translate the center to the origin with <InlineMath math="u=x-1" /> and <InlineMath math="v=y+2" />. The circle and polar line become:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="u^2+v^2=8,\qquad 3u+4v=8" />
  <BlockMath math="u=\frac{8-4v}{3}" />
  <BlockMath math="\left(\frac{8-4v}{3}\right)^2+v^2=8" />
<BlockMath math="64-64v+16v^2+9v^2=72" />
<BlockMath math="25v^2-64v-8=0" />
  <BlockMath math="v=\frac{64\pm\sqrt{(-64)^2-4(25)(-8)}}{50}=\frac{64\pm12\sqrt{34}}{50}" />
<BlockMath math="v=\frac{32\pm6\sqrt{34}}{25}" />
</MathContainer>

Substituting back gives the two points of tangency:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="T_1\left(\frac{49-8\sqrt{34}}{25},\frac{-18+6\sqrt{34}}{25}\right)" />
  <BlockMath math="T_2\left(\frac{49+8\sqrt{34}}{25},\frac{-18-6\sqrt{34}}{25}\right)" />
</MathContainer>

The slopes from <InlineMath math="(4,2)" /> to those points are <InlineMath math="12-2\sqrt{34}" /> and <InlineMath math="12+2\sqrt{34}" />. Therefore, the required tangent lines are:

For each contact point, use the **vertical difference divided by the horizontal difference** from the outside point <InlineMath math="(4,2)" />:

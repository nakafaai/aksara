## Understanding Point Position Relative to a Circle

Given a circle and a point, exactly one of three cases applies: the point lies inside the circle, on its circumference, or outside it.

The same comparison appears in simplified coverage models, such as deciding whether a location lies inside an idealized circular signal or radar range. Once the circular model is chosen, coordinate geometry gives an exact answer.

The circle equation and the point coordinates give an exact classification.

## Power of a Point Concept

To classify the point, we use its **power with respect to the circle**. This is a signed algebraic quantity, not an ordinary distance. For a circle with center <InlineMath math="(a,b)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r" />:

<BlockMath math="K_A = (x_0-a)^2 + (y_0-b)^2 - r^2" />

If we have point <InlineMath math="A(x_0, y_0)" /> and a circle with general equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0" />, then the power of point <InlineMath math="A" /> is defined as:

<BlockMath math="K_A = x_0^2 + y_0^2 + Dx_0 + Ey_0 + F" />

Substituting the coordinates once gives a value whose sign determines the point's location.

## Three Possible Point Positions

Based on the power of a point value, there are three possible positions:

1. **The point is inside the circle** when <InlineMath math="K_A < 0" />.

2. **The point is on the circle** when <InlineMath math="K_A = 0" />.

3. **The point is outside the circle** when <InlineMath math="K_A > 0" />.

## How to Determine Point Position

First identify the circle equation and the coordinates of the point. Then substitute the coordinates into the equation and inspect the sign of the result.

If the result is negative, the point is inside. If zero, the point is on the circle. If positive, the point is outside the circle.

Let's try with a concrete example. Say we have point <InlineMath math="A(1, -2)" /> and circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 25" />.

First compute the squared distance to the center, then subtract <InlineMath math="r^2" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="d_A^2 = 1^2 + (-2)^2 = 1 + 4 = 5" />
  <BlockMath math="K_A = d_A^2-r^2 = 5-25 = -20" />
</MathContainer>

Because <InlineMath math="K_A = -20 < 0" />, point <InlineMath math="A(1, -2)" /> lies inside the circle.

> For <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = r^2" />, comparing <InlineMath math="d_A^2" /> with <InlineMath math="r^2" /> is equivalent to checking the sign of <InlineMath math="K_A=d_A^2-r^2" />.

## Application for General Form

If the circle is in general form <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0" />, the method is the same. Just substitute the point coordinates into the entire equation and look at the sign of the result.

For example, for point <InlineMath math="A(1, -2)" /> and circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 8x - 2y - 8 = 0" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="K_A = 1^2 + (-2)^2 - 8(1) - 2(-2) - 8" />
  <BlockMath math="K_A = 1 + 4 - 8 + 4 - 8 = -7" />
</MathContainer>

Since <InlineMath math="K_A = -7 < 0" />, then point <InlineMath math="A(1, -2)" /> is located inside the circle.

## Practice Problems

1. Determine the position of point <InlineMath math="A(3, 4)" /> relative to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 16" />.

2. Investigate whether point <InlineMath math="B(6, -8)" /> lies on circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 100" />.

3. Determine the position of point <InlineMath math="C(2, 1)" /> relative to circle <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y - 12 = 0" />.

4. A circle has equation <InlineMath math="(x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 25" />. Determine the position of point <InlineMath math="D(7, 2)" /> relative to that circle.

### Worked Solutions

1. **Solution**:

   Substitute coordinates of point <InlineMath math="A(3, 4)" /> into circle equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 16" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="d_A^2 = 3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25" />
     <BlockMath math="K_A = d_A^2-r^2 = 25-16 = 9" />
   </MathContainer>

   Since <InlineMath math="K_A = 9 > 0" />, point <InlineMath math="A(3, 4)" /> lies outside the circle.

2. **Solution**:

   Substitute coordinates of point <InlineMath math="B(6, -8)" /> into circle equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 100" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="d_B^2 = 6^2 + (-8)^2 = 36 + 64 = 100" />
     <BlockMath math="K_B = d_B^2-r^2 = 100-100 = 0" />
   </MathContainer>

   Since <InlineMath math="K_B = 0" />, point <InlineMath math="B(6, -8)" /> lies exactly on the circle.

3. **Solution**:

   Substitute coordinates of point <InlineMath math="C(2, 1)" /> into circle equation <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y - 12 = 0" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="K_C = 2^2 + 1^2 - 4(2) + 6(1) - 12" />
     <BlockMath math="K_C = 4 + 1 - 8 + 6 - 12 = -9" />
   </MathContainer>

   Since <InlineMath math="K_C = -9 < 0" />, then point <InlineMath math="C(2, 1)" /> is located inside the circle.

4. **Solution**:

   For circle <InlineMath math="(x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 25" /> with center <InlineMath math="(3, -2)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r = 5" />.

   Substitute point <InlineMath math="D(7, 2)" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="d_D^2 = (7-3)^2 + (2-(-2))^2 = 4^2 + 4^2" />
     <BlockMath math="d_D^2 = 16 + 16 = 32" />
     <BlockMath math="K_D = d_D^2-r^2 = 32-25 = 7" />
   </MathContainer>

   Since <InlineMath math="K_D = 7 > 0" />, point <InlineMath math="D(7, 2)" /> lies outside the circle.

## What is a Hyperbola?

The vertical outline of a cooling tower often resembles a hyperbola. A conical shock wave from a supersonic aircraft can also trace a hyperbolic path where it intersects the ground. These examples give us a first picture of an otherwise abstract curve.

Geometrically, a hyperbola appears when a plane cuts both halves of a double cone. Unlike an ellipse, which is closed, a hyperbola has **two separate branches** facing away from one another.

Mathematically, a hyperbola is defined as the locus of points where **the absolute difference of distances to two fixed points is always constant**. These two fixed points are called the **foci** of the hyperbola. For every point <InlineMath math="P" /> on the hyperbola, the difference <InlineMath math="|PF_1 - PF_2| = 2a" /> (constant), whereas in an [ellipse](/en/subjects/mathematics/analytic-geometry/ellipse) the sum of distances is constant: <InlineMath math="PF_1 + PF_2 = 2a" />.

Read the graph through these five features:

1. **Center of hyperbola** is the midpoint between the two foci, usually denoted by <InlineMath math="O" />.

2. **Foci** (<InlineMath math="F_1" /> and <InlineMath math="F_2" />) are the two fixed points in the definition of a hyperbola. If <InlineMath math="c" /> is the distance from the center to either focus, the distance between the foci is <InlineMath math="2c" />.

3. **Vertices** (<InlineMath math="A_1" /> and <InlineMath math="A_2" />) are the points where each branch comes closest to the center. They lie on the transverse axis.

4. **Transverse axis** is the line segment through the center whose endpoints are the two vertices. The foci lie on the line containing this segment.

5. **Asymptotes** are lines that are approached by the branches of the hyperbola as they extend to infinity. The hyperbola never touches its asymptotes, but the farther from the center, the closer the curve approaches the asymptote lines.

> The main difference between a hyperbola and an ellipse: a hyperbola has asymptotes and consists of two separate branches, while an ellipse is a closed curve without asymptotes.

## Important Properties of Hyperbola

The three parameters of every hyperbola satisfy:

<BlockMath math="c^2 = a^2 + b^2" />

In this notation:
- <InlineMath math="a" /> is the distance from center to vertex (transverse semiaxis)
- <InlineMath math="b" /> is the conjugate semiaxis that determines the asymptote slopes
- <InlineMath math="c" /> is the distance from center to focus

This relationship is different from an ellipse which uses <InlineMath math="c^2 = a^2 - b^2" />.

**Eccentricity** of a hyperbola is defined as:

<BlockMath math="e = \frac{c}{a}" />

The eccentricity of a hyperbola is always <InlineMath math="e > 1" /> because <InlineMath math="c^2 = a^2 + b^2" /> implies <InlineMath math="c > a" />. More precisely, <InlineMath math="e^2 = 1 + \frac{b^2}{a^2}" />, so increasing <InlineMath math="e" /> increases the ratio <InlineMath math="b/a" /> that also determines the asymptote slopes. For comparison, a circle has <InlineMath math="e = 0" />, an ellipse has <InlineMath math="0 < e < 1" />, a parabola has <InlineMath math="e = 1" />, and a hyperbola has <InlineMath math="e > 1" />.

**Asymptote equations** for a hyperbola with center at <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="y = \pm \frac{b}{a}x \quad \text{(horizontal transverse axis)}" />
  <BlockMath math="y = \pm \frac{a}{b}x \quad \text{(vertical transverse axis)}" />
</MathContainer>

> The asymptotes describe the limiting directions of the two branches. As the distance from the center grows, each branch approaches an asymptote without intersecting it.

## Exercises

1. Determine the equation of a hyperbola with center at <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />, vertices at <InlineMath math="(\pm 2, 0)" />, and foci at <InlineMath math="(\pm 3, 0)" />.

2. Given the hyperbola <InlineMath math="\frac{x^2}{25} - \frac{y^2}{9} = 1" />. Determine the coordinates of the foci, eccentricity, and asymptote equations.

3. A hyperbola has its center at <InlineMath math="(1, -2)" />, horizontal transverse axis, <InlineMath math="a = 3" />, and <InlineMath math="b = 4" />. Determine the equation of the hyperbola.

4. Determine the asymptote equations of the hyperbola <InlineMath math="\frac{(y+1)^2}{4} - \frac{(x-2)^2}{9} = 1" />.

### Worked Solutions

1. **Solution**:

   Given:
   - Center at <InlineMath math="(0,0)" />
   - Vertices at <InlineMath math="(\pm 2, 0)" />, so <InlineMath math="a = 2" />
   - Foci at <InlineMath math="(\pm 3, 0)" />, so <InlineMath math="c = 3" />
   - Horizontal transverse axis (since vertices and foci are on the <InlineMath math="x" /> axis)

   Use the relationship <InlineMath math="c^2 = a^2 + b^2" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="3^2 = 2^2 + b^2" />
     <BlockMath math="9 = 4 + b^2" />
     <BlockMath math="b^2 = 5" />
   </MathContainer>

   Equation of a hyperbola with a horizontal transverse axis:

   <BlockMath math="\frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{y^2}{5} = 1" />

2. **Solution**:

   From the equation <InlineMath math="\frac{x^2}{25} - \frac{y^2}{9} = 1" />:
   - <InlineMath math="a^2 = 25" />, so <InlineMath math="a = 5" />
   - <InlineMath math="b^2 = 9" />, so <InlineMath math="b = 3" />

   Since it has the form <InlineMath math="\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1" />, the transverse axis is horizontal.

   Calculate <InlineMath math="c" />:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = 25 + 9 = 34" />
     <BlockMath math="c = \sqrt{34} \approx 5.831" />
   </MathContainer>

   Coordinates of foci: <InlineMath math="(\pm \sqrt{34}, 0)" />

   Eccentricity:

   <BlockMath math="e = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{\sqrt{34}}{5} \approx \frac{5.831}{5} \approx 1.166" />

   Asymptote equations (horizontal transverse axis):

   <BlockMath math="y = \pm \frac{b}{a}x = \pm \frac{3}{5}x" />

3. **Solution**:

   Given:
   - Center: <InlineMath math="(h,k) = (1, -2)" />
   - Horizontal transverse axis
   - <InlineMath math="a = 3" />, so <InlineMath math="a^2 = 9" />
   - <InlineMath math="b = 4" />, so <InlineMath math="b^2 = 16" />

   Equation of a hyperbola with center <InlineMath math="(h,k)" /> and a horizontal transverse axis:

   <BlockMath math="\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1" />

   Substituting values:

   <BlockMath math="\frac{(x-1)^2}{9} - \frac{(y+2)^2}{16} = 1" />

4. **Solution**:

   From the equation <InlineMath math="\frac{(y+1)^2}{4} - \frac{(x-2)^2}{9} = 1" />:
   - Center: <InlineMath math="(h,k) = (2, -1)" />
   - <InlineMath math="a^2 = 4" />, so <InlineMath math="a = 2" />
   - <InlineMath math="b^2 = 9" />, so <InlineMath math="b = 3" />
   - Vertical transverse axis (since it has the form <InlineMath math="\frac{(y-k)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(x-h)^2}{b^2} = 1" />)

   For a hyperbola with center <InlineMath math="(h,k)" /> and a vertical transverse axis, the asymptote equations are:

   <BlockMath math="y - k = \pm \frac{a}{b}(x - h)" />

   Substituting values:

   <MathContainer>
     <BlockMath math="y - (-1) = \pm \frac{2}{3}(x - 2)" />
     <BlockMath math="y + 1 = \pm \frac{2}{3}(x - 2)" />
     <BlockMath math="y = -1 \pm \frac{2}{3}(x - 2)" />
   </MathContainer>

    So the asymptote equations are:

    <MathContainer>
       <BlockMath math="y = -1 + \frac{2}{3}(x - 2)" />
       <BlockMath math="y = -1 - \frac{2}{3}(x - 2)" />
     </MathContainer>

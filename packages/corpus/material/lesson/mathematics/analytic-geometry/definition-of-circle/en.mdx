## Points at a Fixed Distance from the Center

A circle is the collection of all points on a plane that have <Highlight>the same distance from one fixed point</Highlight>. This fixed point is called the **center of the circle**, while the equal distance from the center to every point on the circle is called the **radius**.

Imagine you tie a string to a nail, then pull the string tight and draw a complete curve around the nail. The curve that forms is what we call a circle, the nail is its center, and the length of the string is its radius.

The three distances <InlineMath math="PA" />, <InlineMath math="PB" />, and <InlineMath math="PC" /> are all equal to <InlineMath math="r" />. That shared distance is exactly what places the three points on one circle.

## Circle as Points at a Fixed Distance

We can now state the idea precisely. A circle with center <InlineMath math="P(a, b)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r>0" /> is the set of all points <InlineMath math="(x, y)" /> that satisfy:

<BlockMath math="d(P, (x,y)) = r" />

Here, <InlineMath math="d(P, (x,y))" /> is the distance from the center <InlineMath math="P" /> to the point <InlineMath math="(x, y)" />.

If we use the **distance formula** in the Cartesian coordinate system, we can write it like this:

<BlockMath math="\sqrt{(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2} = r" />

## Circle Equation

From the mathematical definition above, we can derive the **circle equation** by squaring both sides:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\sqrt{(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2} = r" />
  <BlockMath math="(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2" />
</MathContainer>

This is the **standard equation of a circle** with center <InlineMath math="(a, b)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r" />. For a point <InlineMath math="(x,y)" />, compare <InlineMath math="(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2" /> with <InlineMath math="r^2" />. If the first value is smaller, the point is inside the circle. Equal values put it on the circle, while a larger first value puts it outside.

### Special Form of Circle Equation

When the center is the **origin** <InlineMath math="(0, 0)" />, both shift terms disappear and the equation becomes:

<BlockMath math="x^2 + y^2 = r^2" />

This shorter form is useful whenever the center is already known to be the origin.

## Basic Elements of a Circle

Three geometric elements appear throughout circle problems:

1. **Center of circle** is the fixed reference point for all points on the circle. Every point on the circle has the same distance to this center.

2. **Radius** is the distance from the center of the circle to any point on the circle. In one circle, all radii have the same length.

3. **Diameter** is a line segment that connects two points on the circle and passes through the center. Its length is twice the radius, or <InlineMath math="d = 2r" />.

## Finding an Equation from Center and Radius

Substitute the given center and radius into the standard form.

**Example**: Determine the equation of a circle centered at <InlineMath math="(3, -2)" /> with radius <InlineMath math="5" />.

**Solution**:
Substitute the given center and radius into the standard equation:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2" />
  <BlockMath math="(x-3)^2 + (y-(-2))^2 = 5^2" />
  <BlockMath math="(x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 25" />
</MathContainer>

So the circle equation is <InlineMath math="(x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 25" />.

Check the point <InlineMath math="(8,-2)" />, which is <InlineMath math="5" /> units to the right of the center. **Substituting its coordinates** must give the squared radius:

<BlockMath math="(8-3)^2+(-2+2)^2=5^2+0^2=25" />

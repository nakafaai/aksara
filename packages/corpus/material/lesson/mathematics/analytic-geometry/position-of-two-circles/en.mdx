## Seven Positions of Two Circles

The position of two circles depends on the **distance between the centers <InlineMath math="d" />** compared with the **sum of the radii <InlineMath math="r_1 + r_2" />** and the **difference of the radii <InlineMath math="|r_1 - r_2|" />**. For the centers <InlineMath math="(x_1, y_1)" /> and <InlineMath math="(x_2, y_2)" />, the distance between them is

<BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}" />

Once you know <InlineMath math="d" />, match it with this table. Every pair of circles with positive radii falls into exactly one row.

| Position | Condition | Common points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Separate, outside each other | <InlineMath math="d > r_1 + r_2" /> | none |
| Externally tangent | <InlineMath math="d = r_1 + r_2" /> | one |
| Intersecting | <InlineMath math="\lvert r_1 - r_2 \rvert < d < r_1 + r_2" /> | two |
| Internally tangent | <InlineMath math="d = \lvert r_1 - r_2 \rvert > 0" /> | one |
| One circle inside the other | <InlineMath math="0 < d < \lvert r_1 - r_2 \rvert" /> | none |
| Concentric | <InlineMath math="d = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="r_1 \ne r_2" /> | none |
| Coincident | <InlineMath math="d = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="r_1 = r_2" /> | every point |

Imagine sliding the second circle straight toward the first. If the radii differ, the distance <InlineMath math="d" /> shrinks and the position changes **exactly in the order of the table's rows**, from separate to concentric. If the radii are equal, the slide jumps from intersecting straight to coincident.

The condition is

<BlockMath math="|r_1 - r_2| < d < r_1 + r_2" />

This condition comes from a triangle. An intersection point <InlineMath math="A" />, the center <InlineMath math="P_1" />, and the center <InlineMath math="P_2" /> form a triangle with sides <InlineMath math="r_1" />, <InlineMath math="r_2" />, and <InlineMath math="d" />. Each side of a triangle is shorter than the sum of the other two, so <InlineMath math="d < r_1 + r_2" /> and <InlineMath math="d > |r_1 - r_2|" />. When one <InlineMath math="<" /> becomes <InlineMath math="=" />, **the triangle flattens into a single line**, and the two intersection points merge into one point of contact. Conversely, when the inequality holds, a triangle with sides <InlineMath math="r_1" />, <InlineMath math="r_2" />, and <InlineMath math="d" /> can be built on either side of the line <InlineMath math="P_1P_2" />, and its two apexes are the intersection points.

## Example of a Position from the Equations

Find the position of the circles <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 9" /> and <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 8y = 0" />.

The first circle has center <InlineMath math="(0, 0)" /> and <InlineMath math="r_1 = 3" />. For the second circle, complete the square as in the [equation of a circle](/en/subjects/mathematics/analytic-geometry/equation-of-circle):

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 8y = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} &amp;(x^2 - 6x + 9) + (y^2 - 8y + 16) \\ &amp;= 9 + 16 \end{aligned}" />
  <BlockMath math="(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25" />
</MathContainer>

The second circle has center <InlineMath math="(3, 4)" /> and <InlineMath math="r_2 = 5" />. Compute the distance between the centers, then compare:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{(3 - 0)^2 + (4 - 0)^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5" />
  <BlockMath math="|r_1 - r_2| = |3 - 5| = 2, \qquad r_1 + r_2 = 3 + 5 = 8" />
</MathContainer>

Since <InlineMath math="2 < 5 < 8" />, the circles **intersect at two points**.

## Example of the Radius for Tangent Circles

The circle <InlineMath math="L_1: x^2 + y^2 = 25" /> and a circle <InlineMath math="L_2" /> with center <InlineMath math="P(6, 8)" /> and radius <InlineMath math="r" /> are given. Find <InlineMath math="r" /> so that the circles are tangent, then find the point of contact.

The distance between the centers is <InlineMath math="d = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = 10" />. External and internal tangency give two equations:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="\text{external: } 5 + r = 10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r = 5" />
  <BlockMath math="\text{internal: } |r - 5| = 10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r = 15" />
</MathContainer>

The value <InlineMath math="r = -5" /> from the internal equation is rejected, because **a radius must be positive**. For <InlineMath math="r = 5" />, the formula for the point of contact gives <InlineMath math="T = (0, 0) + \frac{5}{10}(6, 8) = (3, 4)" />. For <InlineMath math="r = 15" />, the circle <InlineMath math="L_2" /> is the larger one, so <InlineMath math="T = (6, 8) + \frac{15}{10}(-6, -8) = (-3, -4)" />.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparing <InlineMath math="d" /> only with <InlineMath math="r_1 + r_2" /> | <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 25" /> and <InlineMath math="(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1" /> have <InlineMath math="d = 1 < 6" /> but do not intersect | Also compute <InlineMath math="\lvert r_1 - r_2 \rvert = 4" /> |
| Writing <InlineMath math="r_1 - r_2" /> without the absolute value | For <InlineMath math="r_1 = 3" /> and <InlineMath math="r_2 = 5" />, the result is <InlineMath math="-2" /> and the lower bound disappears | Use <InlineMath math="\lvert 3 - 5 \rvert = 2" /> |
| Reading <InlineMath math="r^2" /> as <InlineMath math="r" /> | <InlineMath math="(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25" /> has radius <InlineMath math="5" />, not <InlineMath math="25" /> | Take the square root of the right side |
| Completing the square when the coefficient of <InlineMath math="x^2" /> is not <InlineMath math="1" /> | The center of <InlineMath math="2x^2 + 2y^2 - 8x = 0" /> comes out wrong | Divide by <InlineMath math="2" /> first to get <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 4x = 0" /> |
| Treating concentric circles as coincident | Concentric circles have different radii and no common point | Coincident needs <InlineMath math="d = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="r_1 = r_2" /> |

For two intersecting circles, **subtracting the two equations** is a useful check. The result is the line that contains both intersection points, and that line must cut each circle at two points.

## Practice Problems

Find the centers, the radii, and the distance between the centers first, then **match them with the table of positions**.

1. Find the position of the circles <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 4" /> and <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 10x + 16 = 0" />. If they are tangent, find the point of contact.

2. Find the position of the circles <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 4y - 4 = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 14x - 20y + 140 = 0" />, then find the shortest gap between their circumferences.

3. Find the position of the circles <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 36" /> and <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 2y + 1 = 0" />.

4. The circles <InlineMath math="(x - a)^2 + y^2 = 16" /> and <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 1" /> are tangent. Find every possible value of <InlineMath math="a" />.

### Solutions

**Problem 1**. The first circle has center <InlineMath math="O" /> and <InlineMath math="r_1 = 2" />. The second circle can be written as <InlineMath math="(x - 5)^2 + y^2 = 9" />, so it has center <InlineMath math="(5, 0)" /> and <InlineMath math="r_2 = 3" />. The distance is <InlineMath math="d = 5 = 2 + 3" />, so the circles are **externally tangent**. The point of contact is <InlineMath math="T = (0, 0) + \frac{2}{5}(5, 0) = (2, 0)" />. Check: <InlineMath math="2^2 + 0^2 = 4" /> and <InlineMath math="(2 - 5)^2 + 0^2 = 9" />.

**Problem 2**. Complete the square in both equations:

  <BlockMath math="(x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 1 + 4 + 4 = 9" />
  <BlockMath math="(x - 7)^2 + (y - 10)^2 = 49 + 100 - 140 = 9" />
</MathContainer>

Both circles have radius <InlineMath math="3" />, with centers <InlineMath math="(1, 2)" /> and <InlineMath math="(7, 10)" />. The distance is <InlineMath math="d = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = 10" />, greater than <InlineMath math="3 + 3 = 6" />, so the circles are **separate, outside each other**. The shortest gap is <InlineMath math="10 - 6 = 4" />. Check: the point <InlineMath math="(1, 2) + \frac{3}{10}(6, 8) = \left(\frac{14}{5}, \frac{22}{5}\right)" /> on the first circle and the point <InlineMath math="(7, 10) - \frac{3}{10}(6, 8) = \left(\frac{26}{5}, \frac{38}{5}\right)" /> on the second circle are <InlineMath math="\sqrt{\left(\frac{12}{5}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{16}{5}\right)^2} = 4" /> apart. Both points do lie on their circles, because <InlineMath math="\left(\frac{9}{5}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{12}{5}\right)^2 = 9" /> and <InlineMath math="\left(-\frac{9}{5}\right)^2 + \left(-\frac{12}{5}\right)^2 = 9" />.

**Problem 3**. The first circle has center <InlineMath math="O" /> and <InlineMath math="r_1 = 6" />. The second circle can be written as <InlineMath math="(x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 4" />, so it has center <InlineMath math="(2, 1)" /> and <InlineMath math="r_2 = 2" />. The distance is <InlineMath math="d = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.24" />, and <InlineMath math="0 < \sqrt{5} < |6 - 2| = 4" />, so the small circle **lies inside the large one without touching it**. Check: the point of the small circle farthest from <InlineMath math="O" /> is <InlineMath math="\sqrt{5} + 2 \approx 4.24" /> away, still less than <InlineMath math="6" />.

**Problem 4**. The distance between the centers is <InlineMath math="d = |a|" />, with radii <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="1" />. External tangency gives <InlineMath math="|a| = 4 + 1 = 5" />, and internal tangency gives <InlineMath math="|a| = 4 - 1 = 3" />. So <InlineMath math="a = 5" />, <InlineMath math="a = -5" />, <InlineMath math="a = 3" />, or <InlineMath math="a = -3" />. Check for <InlineMath math="a = 3" />: the point <InlineMath math="(-1, 0)" /> satisfies <InlineMath math="(-1)^2 + 0^2 = 1" /> and <InlineMath math="(-1 - 3)^2 + 0^2 = 16" />, so the circles meet there, and the small circle lies inside the large one.

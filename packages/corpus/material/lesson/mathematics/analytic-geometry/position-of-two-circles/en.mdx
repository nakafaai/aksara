## Relationship Between Circles

Two soap bubbles provide a useful picture of the possible relationships between circles. Their boundaries may cross, touch at one point, or remain separate.

In analytic geometry, the centers and radii are enough to determine the relationship precisely. The decisive quantity is the distance between the two centers.

The same comparison appears when checking whether circular regions overlap, touch, or leave a gap, for example in coverage maps or geometric designs.

## Intersecting Circles

Two circles are **intersecting** when they have exactly two common points. Their disks overlap, while their circumferences cross at those two points.

For two circles with radii <InlineMath math="r_1" /> and <InlineMath math="r_2" /> and distance between centers <InlineMath math="d" />, the intersection condition occurs when:

<BlockMath math="|r_1 - r_2| < d < r_1 + r_2" />

The two strict bounds explain the condition:

- **Upper bound**: At <InlineMath math="r_1 + r_2" />, the circles are externally tangent
- **Lower bound**: At <InlineMath math="|r_1 - r_2|" />, the smaller circle is internally tangent to the larger circle
- **Between the bounds**: The circumferences cross at two points

2. **Internal tangency** occurs when <InlineMath math="d = |r_1 - r_2| > 0" />. The smaller circle is inside the larger one and they touch at one point.

For concentric circles, the distance between centers is zero (<InlineMath math="d = 0" />) but the radii are different (<InlineMath math="r_1 \ne r_2" />).

**Coincident circles** have the same center and the same radius. Their circumferences lie exactly on top of each other, so they appear as one circle.

The coincident condition occurs when:

## How to Determine Position

To classify two circles, calculate the distance between their centers and compare it with the sum and absolute difference of their radii.

Suppose the first circle is centered at <InlineMath math="(x_1, y_1)" /> with radius <InlineMath math="r_1" />, and the second circle is centered at <InlineMath math="(x_2, y_2)" /> with radius <InlineMath math="r_2" />.

The distance between centers is calculated using the formula:

<BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}" />

Assuming both radii are positive, the following conditions are exhaustive and do not overlap:

- **Coincident**: <InlineMath math="d = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="r_1 = r_2" /> (the same circumference)
- **Concentric but distinct**: <InlineMath math="d = 0" /> and <InlineMath math="r_1 \ne r_2" /> (the same center, different radii)
- **Contained without touching and nonconcentric**: <InlineMath math="0 < d < |r_1-r_2|" /> (one circle lies strictly inside the other)
- **Internally tangent**: <InlineMath math="d = |r_1-r_2| > 0" /> (one internal point of contact)
- **Intersecting**: <InlineMath math="|r_1-r_2| < d < r_1+r_2" /> (two common points)
- **Externally tangent**: <InlineMath math="d = r_1+r_2" /> (one external point of contact)
- **Externally separate**: <InlineMath math="d > r_1+r_2" /> (the two disks do not meet)

### Application Example

Classify the circles with equations <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 = 9" /> and <InlineMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 8y = 0" />.

**Step** <InlineMath math="1" />: Identify the center and radius of each circle.

First circle: center <InlineMath math="(0, 0)" />, radius <InlineMath math="r_1 = 3" />

For the second circle, we complete the square:

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 8y = 0" />
  <BlockMath math="(x^2 - 6x + 9) + (y^2 - 8y + 16) = 9 + 16" />
  <BlockMath math="(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25" />
</MathContainer>

Second circle: center <InlineMath math="(3, 4)" />, radius <InlineMath math="r_2 = 5" />

**Step** <InlineMath math="2" />: Calculate the distance between centers.

<BlockMath math="d = \sqrt{(3-0)^2 + (4-0)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5" />

**Step** <InlineMath math="3" />: Compare with position conditions.

<MathContainer>
  <BlockMath math="r_1 + r_2 = 3 + 5 = 8" />
  <BlockMath math="|r_1 - r_2| = |3 - 5| = 2" />
</MathContainer>

Since <InlineMath math="2 < 5 < 8" />, the two circles are **intersecting**.

As a check, substitute the three values into <InlineMath math="|r_1 - r_2| < d < r_1 + r_2" />:
- <InlineMath math="|3 - 5| = 2" />
- <InlineMath math="3 + 5 = 8" />
- <InlineMath math="2 < 5 < 8" /> <InlineMath math="\checkmark" /> (intersection condition satisfied)

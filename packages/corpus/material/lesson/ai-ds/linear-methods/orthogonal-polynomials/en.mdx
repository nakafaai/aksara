## Orthogonality for Functions

Let <InlineMath math="w(x)>0" /> on an interval <InlineMath math="[a,b]" />. The weighted inner product and its induced norm are

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\langle f,g\rangle_w=\int_a^b f(x)g(x)w(x)\,dx" />
<BlockMath math="\|f\|_w=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle_w}" />
</MathContainer>

Two functions are orthogonal when <InlineMath math="\langle f,g\rangle_w=0" />. This weighted <InlineMath math="L^2" /> geometry measures the squared error across the interval

<BlockMath math="\|f-g\|_\infty=\max_{x\in[a,b]}|f(x)-g(x)|" />

The distinction matters. Orthogonal projection minimizes the weighted <InlineMath math="L^2" /> error. Chebyshev polynomials also have a separate minimax property, but the weighted inner product and the maximum norm are not interchangeable.

## Projection onto an Orthogonal Basis

Suppose <InlineMath math="p_0,ldots,p_n" /> are mutually orthogonal. The degree-<InlineMath math="n" /> projection of <InlineMath math="f" /> is

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\Pi_n f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n c_kp_k(x)" />
<BlockMath math="c_k=\frac{\langle f,p_k\rangle_w}{\langle p_k,p_k\rangle_w}" />
</MathContainer>

Because cross terms vanish, each coefficient can be computed independently. This is the functional analogue of resolving a vector along perpendicular coordinate axes.

## Three Term Recurrence from Gram Schmidt

Applying Gram-Schmidt to <InlineMath math="1,x,x^2,ldots" /> produces a monic orthogonal sequence <InlineMath math="\pi_k" />. Under a positive inner product it satisfies

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\pi_0(x)=1" />
<BlockMath math="\pi_1(x)=x-\alpha_0" />
<BlockMath math="\pi_{k+1}(x)=(x-\alpha_k)\pi_k(x)-\beta_k\pi_{k-1}(x)" />
</MathContainer>

The recurrence coefficients are

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\alpha_k=\frac{\langle x\pi_k,\pi_k\rangle_w}{\|\pi_k\|_w^2}" />
<BlockMath math="\beta_k=\frac{\|\pi_k\|_w^2}{\|\pi_{k-1}\|_w^2}>0" />
</MathContainer>

The three-term form is not an arbitrary shortcut. Multiplication by <InlineMath math="x" /> raises the degree by one, while orthogonality removes all components below <InlineMath math="\pi_{k-1}" />.

## Chebyshev Polynomials of the First Kind

On <InlineMath math="[-1,1]" />, choose

<BlockMath math="w(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}" />

The Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind are

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="T_0(x)=1,\qquad T_1(x)=x" />
<BlockMath math="T_{k+1}(x)=2xT_k(x)-T_{k-1}(x)" />
<BlockMath math="T_k(x)=\cos\bigl(k\arccos x\bigr)" />
</MathContainer>

Their weighted orthogonality is

<BlockMath math="\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_j(x)T_k(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx=\begin{cases}\pi,&j=k=0,\\\frac{\pi}{2},&j=k>0,\\0,&j\ne k.\end{cases}" />

Since <InlineMath math="|T_k(x)|\le1" /> on <InlineMath math="[-1,1]" /> and the leading coefficient is <InlineMath math="2^{k-1}" /> for <InlineMath math="k\ge1" />, the monic polynomial <InlineMath math="2^{1-k}T_k" /> has the smallest maximum norm among all monic degree-<InlineMath math="k" /> polynomials. This is the precise connection to minimax approximation.

## Legendre Polynomials

For the unweighted inner product on <InlineMath math="[-1,1]" />,

<BlockMath math="\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)g(x)\,dx" />

For this inner product, the standard Legendre polynomials satisfy

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="P_0(x)=1,\qquad P_1(x)=x" />
<BlockMath math="(k+1)P_{k+1}(x)=(2k+1)xP_k(x)-kP_{k-1}(x)" />
</MathContainer>

Their orthogonality and normalization are

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\int_{-1}^{1}P_j(x)P_k(x)\,dx=\frac{2}{2k+1}\delta_{jk}" />
<BlockMath math="\varphi_k(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2k+1}{2}}P_k(x)" />
<BlockMath math="\langle\varphi_j,\varphi_k\rangle=\delta_{jk}" />
</MathContainer>

For example,

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="P_2(x)=\frac12(3x^2-1)" />
<BlockMath math="x^2=\frac13P_0(x)+\frac23P_2(x)" />
</MathContainer>

The second identity gives the exact orthogonal expansion.

## Changing the Interval of Orthogonal Polynomials

Use the affine change of variables

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="x=\frac{a+b}{2}+\frac{b-a}{2}t" />
<BlockMath math="t=\frac{2x-a-b}{b-a}" />
<BlockMath math="dx=\frac{b-a}{2}\,dt" />
</MathContainer>

If <InlineMath math="\varphi_k" /> is orthonormal on <InlineMath math="[-1,1]" />, then

<BlockMath math="\widetilde{\varphi}_k(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{b-a}}\,\varphi_k\left(\frac{2x-a-b}{b-a}\right)" />

The function <InlineMath math="\widetilde{\varphi}_k" /> is orthonormal on <InlineMath math="[a,b]" /> because

<BlockMath math="\int_a^b\widetilde{\varphi}_j(x)\widetilde{\varphi}_k(x)\,dx=\int_{-1}^{1}\varphi_j(t)\varphi_k(t)\,dt=\delta_{jk}" />

## Connection to Gaussian Quadrature

Let <InlineMath math="p_n" /> be the degree-<InlineMath math="n" /> polynomial orthogonal for the same positive weight and interval as an integral. Its <InlineMath math="n" /> roots are the Gaussian quadrature nodes. Suitable positive weights <InlineMath math="w_i" /> then give

<BlockMath math="\int_a^b f(x)w(x)\,dx\approx\sum_{i=1}^n w_i f(x_i)" />

The rule is exact for every polynomial of degree at most <InlineMath math="2n-1" />. Legendre roots produce Gauss-Legendre quadrature for the weight <InlineMath math="w(x)=1" />. Chebyshev roots produce Gauss-Chebyshev quadrature for <InlineMath math="w(x)=1/\sqrt{1-x^2}" />.

| Basis | Weight on <InlineMath math="[-1,1]" /> | Best suited to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chebyshev <InlineMath math="T_k" /> | <InlineMath math="1/\sqrt{1-x^2}" /> | stable interpolation grids, near-minimax approximation, spectral methods |
| Legendre <InlineMath math="P_k" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> | unweighted <InlineMath math="L^2" /> projection and Gauss-Legendre quadrature |

Always match the polynomial family, inner product, approximation objective, and quadrature weight. Changing one of them changes the problem being solved.

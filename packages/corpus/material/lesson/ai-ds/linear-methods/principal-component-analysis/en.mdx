## Start with Centered Data

Assume <InlineMath math="m\ge2" /> so the sample covariance denominator is nonzero.

Let <InlineMath math="X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}" /> contain <InlineMath math="m" /> observations of <InlineMath math="n" /> features. <InlineMath math="\mathrm{PCA}" /> begins by subtracting each feature mean:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\mu_j=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m X_{ij}" />
<BlockMath math="X_c=X-\mathbf{1}\mu^T" />
</MathContainer>

The sample covariance matrix is

<BlockMath math="C=\frac{1}{m-1}X_c^TX_c" />

It is symmetric positive semidefinite. It may be singular when features are linearly dependent or when <InlineMath math="n>m-1" />.

Centering is mandatory. <Highlight>Standardizing each feature to unit variance</Highlight> is a <Highlight>separate modeling choice</Highlight> that is often appropriate when features use incomparable units. It changes the question from directions of largest absolute variance to directions of largest standardized variance.

Take three observations <InlineMath math="(2,3)" />, <InlineMath math="(4,4)" />, and <InlineMath math="(6,8)" />. The feature means are <InlineMath math="4" /> and <InlineMath math="5" />, so centering subtracts <InlineMath math="(4,5)" /> from each row and gives <InlineMath math="(-2,-2)" />, <InlineMath math="(0,-1)" />, and <InlineMath math="(2,3)" />. The cloud keeps its shape but now sits around the origin.

## Principal Directions

By the spectral theorem,

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="Cv_i=\lambda_i v_i" />
<BlockMath math="\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n\ge0" />
<BlockMath math="V=(v_1\ \,\cdots\ \,v_n),\qquad V^TV=I" />
<BlockMath math="C=V\Lambda V^T" />
</MathContainer>

The first principal direction solves

<BlockMath math="v_1=\underset{\|v\|_2=1}{\operatorname{argmax}}\;v^TCv" />

Each later direction maximizes the same variance subject to being **orthogonal to all earlier directions**. The transformed coordinates, or scores, are

<BlockMath math="Z=X_cV" />

The sample covariance of the scores <InlineMath math="Z" /> is diagonal:

<BlockMath math="\frac{1}{m-1}Z^TZ=V^TCV=\Lambda" />

The principal components are therefore uncorrelated. They are not generally independent. For a jointly Gaussian random vector, a fixed eigenbasis of its population covariance gives independent transformed components. Zero sample correlations alone do not prove population independence.

## Explained Variance and Dimension Reduction

The ratios require **positive total variance**, <InlineMath math="\operatorname{tr}(C)>0" />. If every centered observation is zero, every eigenvalue is zero and explained-variance ratios are undefined.

The proportion of total sample variance explained by component <InlineMath math="j" /> is

<BlockMath math="\operatorname{EVR}_j=\frac{\lambda_j}{\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i}=\frac{\lambda_j}{\operatorname{tr}(C)}" />

Keeping the first <InlineMath math="k" /> eigenvectors in <InlineMath math="V_k" /> gives the scores and reconstruction

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="Z_k=X_cV_k" />
<BlockMath math="\widehat{X}_c=Z_kV_k^T=X_cV_kV_k^T" />
<BlockMath math="\widehat{X}=\widehat{X}_c+\mathbf{1}\mu^T" />
</MathContainer>

Among all linear reconstructions of rank at most <InlineMath math="k" />, this choice **minimizes squared reconstruction error**. With the sample-covariance convention above,

<BlockMath math="\|X_c-\widehat{X}_c\|_F^2=(m-1)\sum_{j=k+1}^n\lambda_j" />

Each principal component explains its eigenvalue's share of the total variance. In the worked example below, <InlineMath math="\lambda_1=9" /> and <InlineMath math="\lambda_2=2.25" />, so the first component explains <InlineMath math="\tfrac9{11.25}=80\%" /> and the second the remaining <InlineMath math="20\%" />.

## Equivalent Computation with Singular Value Decomposition

<InlineMath math="\mathrm{PCA}" /> can also be computed through the following thin singular value decomposition:

<BlockMath math="X_c=U\Sigma V^T" />

The **right singular vectors** give the principal directions, while the eigenvalues satisfy:

<BlockMath math="\lambda_j=\frac{\sigma_j^2}{m-1}" />

Using <InlineMath math="\mathrm{SVD}" /> avoids forming <InlineMath math="X_c^TX_c" />, which would square the condition number. <InlineMath math="\mathrm{SVD}" /> also works well when <InlineMath math="X_c" /> is rectangular or rank deficient.

## Worked Two Dimensional Covariance

The covariance ellipse is rotated by the following angle:

<BlockMath math="\theta=\frac{\pi}{6}" />

The variances along the two principal axes are <InlineMath math="9" /> and <InlineMath math="2.25" />. The rotation and covariance matrices are:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="R=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}" />
<BlockMath math="C=R\begin{pmatrix}9&0\\0&2.25\end{pmatrix}R^T" />
<BlockMath math="C=\frac1{16}\begin{pmatrix}117&27\sqrt3\\27\sqrt3&63\end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

The principal directions are the columns of <InlineMath math="R" />:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="v_1=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt3/2\\1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad\lambda_1=9" />
<BlockMath math="v_2=\begin{pmatrix}-1/2\\\sqrt3/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad\lambda_2=2.25" />
</MathContainer>

The **variance ratio** explained by the first component is calculated from the two eigenvalues:

<BlockMath math="\frac{9}{9+2.25}=0.8=80\%" />

The result <InlineMath math="80\%" /> is the fraction of the total variance explained by the first component.

## Interpretation Boundaries

| Check | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Center with training-set means only | prevents data leakage and preserves the learned coordinate system |
| Review feature units and scaling | high numerical variance can otherwise dominate for the wrong reason |
| Choose <InlineMath math="k" /> from validation needs | explained variance does not measure predictive relevance |
| Remember eigenvector signs are arbitrary | <InlineMath math="v_j" /> and <InlineMath math="-v_j" /> define the same component |
| Inspect nonlinear structure and outliers | <InlineMath math="\mathrm{PCA}" /> is a linear, variance-based summary and can be sensitive to both |

<InlineMath math="\mathrm{PCA}" /> identifies directions with large variance. <Highlight>Variance alone does not establish a cause</Highlight>. One principal component may combine many original features. A direction with low variance may still carry a signal needed by a later prediction or classification task.

## Definitions

A real square matrix <InlineMath math="Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}" /> is orthogonal when

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="Q^TQ=QQ^T=I" />
<BlockMath math="Q^{-1}=Q^T" />
</MathContainer>

A complex square matrix <InlineMath math="U\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}" /> is unitary when

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="U^HU=UU^H=I" />
<BlockMath math="U^{-1}=U^H" />
</MathContainer>

Here <InlineMath math="U^H=\overline{U}^{,T}" /> is the conjugate transpose. A real orthogonal matrix is a unitary matrix whose entries happen to be real.

The defining equations are equivalent to saying that the columns form an orthonormal basis. The rows are orthonormal as well.

## What These Transformations Preserve

For real vectors <InlineMath math="x,y" /> and an orthogonal matrix <InlineMath math="Q" />,

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\langle Qx,Qy\rangle=(Qx)^T(Qy)=x^TQ^TQy=x^Ty" />
<BlockMath math="\|Qx\|_2=\|x\|_2" />
<BlockMath math="\|Qx-Qy\|_2=\|x-y\|_2" />
</MathContainer>

The same statements hold over <InlineMath math="\mathbb{C}" /> with conjugate inner products and <InlineMath math="U^H" />. Consequently, orthogonal and unitary transformations preserve lengths, distances, angles, and orthogonality.

All their singular values equal <InlineMath math="1" />, so

<BlockMath math="\|Q\|_2=1,\qquad\kappa_2(Q)=1" />

Multiplication by such a matrix does not amplify relative errors in the Euclidean norm. This is why QR factorizations rely on Householder reflectors and Givens rotations.

## Determinants and Orientation

For a unitary matrix,

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="1=\det(U^HU)=\overline{\det U}\det U=|\det U|^2" />
<BlockMath math="|\det U|=1" />
</MathContainer>

For a real orthogonal matrix, the determinant is real, so

<BlockMath math="\det Q\in\{-1,1\}" />

The case <InlineMath math="\det Q=1" /> preserves orientation. The case <InlineMath math="\det Q=-1" /> reverses orientation and contains a reflection component.

## Eigenvalues and Spectral Structure

If <InlineMath math="Uv=\lambda v" /> with <InlineMath math="v\ne0" />, then

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\|v\|_2=\|Uv\|_2=\|\lambda v\|_2=|\lambda|\|v\|_2" />
<BlockMath math="|\lambda|=1" />
</MathContainer>

Every eigenvalue therefore lies on the complex unit circle. Nonreal eigenvalues of a real orthogonal matrix occur in conjugate pairs. A unitary matrix is normal, so the complex spectral theorem gives

<BlockMath math="U=V\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta_1},ldots,e^{i\theta_n})V^H" />

with unitary <InlineMath math="V" />. Over the real numbers, an orthogonal matrix can instead be reduced to blocks consisting of <InlineMath math="1" />, <InlineMath math="-1" />, and planar rotations.

## Rotation Example

A counterclockwise planar rotation by <InlineMath math="\alpha" /> is

<BlockMath math="R(\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha&-\sin\alpha\\\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{pmatrix}" />

Its columns are orthonormal, and direct multiplication gives

<BlockMath math="R(\alpha)^TR(\alpha)=I" />

The characteristic polynomial is

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\chi_R(t)=t^2-2t\cos\alpha+1" />
<BlockMath math="\lambda_{1,2}=\cos\alpha\pm i\sin\alpha=e^{\pm i\alpha}" />
</MathContainer>

Unless <InlineMath math="\alpha" /> is a multiple of <InlineMath math="\pi" />, the rotation has no real eigenvector. That is expected: no nonzero real direction remains fixed or merely changes sign under a genuine planar rotation.

## Reflection Example

For a unit vector <InlineMath math="u\in\mathbb{R}^n" />, the Householder matrix

<BlockMath math="H=I-2uu^T" />

reflects a vector across the hyperplane perpendicular to <InlineMath math="u" />. Since

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="H^T=H" />
<BlockMath math="H^2=(I-2uu^T)^2=I" />
</MathContainer>

we have <InlineMath math="H^TH=I" />. Its eigenvalue in the direction <InlineMath math="u" /> is <InlineMath math="-1" />, while every vector perpendicular to <InlineMath math="u" /> has eigenvalue <InlineMath math="1" />. Therefore <InlineMath math="\det H=-1" />.

## Equivalent Checks and Consequences

| Statement | Orthogonal case | Unitary case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inverse | <InlineMath math="Q^{-1}=Q^T" /> | <InlineMath math="U^{-1}=U^H" /> |
| Columns | real orthonormal basis | complex orthonormal basis |
| Determinant | <InlineMath math="\pm1" /> | modulus <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| Eigenvalues | unit circle, conjugate pairs when nonreal | unit circle |
| Singular values | all <InlineMath math="1" /> | all <InlineMath math="1" /> |
| Two-norm condition number | <InlineMath math="1" /> | <InlineMath math="1" /> |

Products and inverses remain in the same class:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="(Q_1Q_2)^T(Q_1Q_2)=I" />
<BlockMath math="Q^{-1}=Q^T" />
</MathContainer>

These closure and stability properties make orthogonal and unitary matrices central to QR algorithms, least-squares solvers, Fourier transforms, quantum mechanics, and geometry processing.

## Existence of QR Decomposition

The <InlineMath math="\mathrm{QR}" /> decomposition factors a matrix into an orthogonal factor and an upper-triangular factor. Orthogonal multiplication preserves lengths and angles, so it is well suited to stable numerical calculations.

Let <InlineMath math="A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}" /> satisfy <InlineMath math="m \geq n" /> and <InlineMath math="\operatorname{rank}(A)=n" />. Then there is an orthogonal matrix <InlineMath math="Q \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}" /> with <InlineMath math="Q^TQ=I" /> and an upper-triangular matrix <InlineMath math="R \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}" /> whose first <InlineMath math="n" /> diagonal entries are positive, such that

<BlockMath math="A = Q \cdot R" />

This factorization is the <InlineMath math="\mathrm{QR}" /> decomposition of <InlineMath math="A" />.

### Proof with Gram Schmidt Orthogonalization

Let <InlineMath math="a_j" /> denote column <InlineMath math="j" /> of <InlineMath math="A" />. Gram-Schmidt removes the components in the directions already found, then normalizes what remains:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\tilde{q}_j := a_j - \sum_{k=1}^{j-1} \langle a_j, q_k \rangle \cdot q_k" />
<BlockMath math="q_j := \frac{\tilde{q}_j}{\|\tilde{q}_j\|_2}" />
</MathContainer>

Full column rank guarantees <InlineMath math="\widetilde q_j\neq0" /> at every step. The normalized vectors form the columns of <InlineMath math="Q_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}" /> and satisfy <InlineMath math="Q_1^TQ_1=I_n" />. Each original column can be reconstructed as

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="a_j = \sum_{k=1}^{j-1} \langle a_j, q_k \rangle \cdot q_k + \tilde{q}_j" />
<BlockMath math="= \sum_{k=1}^{j-1} \langle a_j, q_k \rangle \cdot q_k + \|\tilde{q}_j\|_2 \cdot q_j" />
<BlockMath math="= \sum_{k=1}^{j-1} q_k \cdot r_{kj} + q_j \cdot r_{jj}" />
</MathContainer>

<InlineMath math="A=Q_1R_1" />, where <InlineMath math="R_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}" /> is upper triangular and <InlineMath math="r_{ii}=\|\widetilde q_i\|_2>0" />.

Complete <InlineMath math="Q_1" /> with <InlineMath math="m-n" /> orthonormal columns to form <InlineMath math="Q=(Q_1\quad Q_2)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}" />. Appending <InlineMath math="m-n" /> zero rows to <InlineMath math="R_1" /> gives <InlineMath math="R=\begin{pmatrix}R_1\\0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}" />, so <InlineMath math="A=Q_1R_1=QR" />.

## Full and Reduced QR Decomposition

For <InlineMath math="A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}" /> with <InlineMath math="m\geq n" />, the full and reduced forms describe the same factorization with different matrix sizes.

### Full QR Decomposition

The full form keeps all <InlineMath math="m" /> columns of <InlineMath math="Q" />:

<BlockMath math="A = Q \cdot R" />

Here <InlineMath math="Q\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}" /> is orthogonal and <InlineMath math="R\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}" /> contains an upper-triangular <InlineMath math="n\times n" /> block above <InlineMath math="m-n" /> zero rows.

### Reduced QR Decomposition

The reduced form removes those zero rows and the matching unused columns of <InlineMath math="Q" />:

<BlockMath math="A = Q \cdot R = (Q_1 \quad Q_2) \cdot \begin{pmatrix} R_1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = Q_1 \cdot R_1" />

Here <InlineMath math="Q_1\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}" /> contains the first <InlineMath math="n" /> columns of <InlineMath math="Q" />, and <InlineMath math="R_1\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}" /> is upper triangular. Storing <InlineMath math="Q_1" /> instead of the full <InlineMath math="m\times m" /> matrix <InlineMath math="Q" /> saves memory and avoids operations on blocks that are known to be zero.

The unused columns <InlineMath math="Q_2\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(m-n)}" /> form an orthonormal basis for the null space of <InlineMath math="A^T" />:

<BlockMath math="A^T Q_2 = R_1^T Q_1^T Q_2 = 0" />

The null space of <InlineMath math="A^T" /> is the set of vectors <InlineMath math="x" /> that satisfy <InlineMath math="A^Tx=0" />.

### Uniqueness Property

For a full-column-rank matrix, the reduced factorization <InlineMath math="A=Q_1R_1" /> is unique when every diagonal entry of <InlineMath math="R_1" /> is positive.

## Worked Thin QR Example

Let


<BlockMath math="A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=(a_1\ \,a_2)" />

Gram-Schmidt first gives

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="q_1=\frac{a_1}{\|a_1\|_2}=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}" />
<BlockMath math="r_{12}=q_1^Ta_2=\frac1{\sqrt2}" />
<BlockMath math="\widetilde q_2=a_2-r_{12}q_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\-1/2\\1\end{pmatrix}" />
<BlockMath math="q_2=\frac1{\sqrt6}\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\2\end{pmatrix}" />
</MathContainer>

The calculation gives:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="Q_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/\sqrt2&1/\sqrt6\\1/\sqrt2&-1/\sqrt6\\0&2/\sqrt6\end{pmatrix}" />
<BlockMath math="R_1=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt2&1/\sqrt2\\0&\sqrt6/2\end{pmatrix}" />
<BlockMath math="Q_1^TQ_1=I,\qquad Q_1R_1=A" />
</MathContainer>

## Householder Method for QR Decomposition

Classical Gram-Schmidt gives a direct derivation, but nearly dependent columns can lose orthogonality in floating-point arithmetic. Modified Gram-Schmidt reduces that error. Dense numerical software commonly uses Householder reflectors, while Givens rotations are useful when only selected entries need to be eliminated.

### Householder Transformation

For a unit vector <InlineMath math="v\in\mathbb{R}^m" />, define the Householder matrix

<BlockMath math="S = I - 2vv^T \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}" />

The outer product <InlineMath math="vv^T" /> multiplies an <InlineMath math="m\times1" /> column by a <InlineMath math="1\times m" /> row, so it produces an <InlineMath math="m\times m" /> rank-one matrix. The reversed product <InlineMath math="v^Tv" /> is the scalar <InlineMath math="1" />.

## Properties of Householder Transformation

For <InlineMath math="S=I-2vv^T" /> and <InlineMath math="\|v\|_2=1" />, the following properties hold:

1. <InlineMath math="S" /> is symmetric: <InlineMath math="S^T = S" />

2. <InlineMath math="S" /> is an orthogonal matrix: <InlineMath math="S^T S = I" />

3. <InlineMath math="Sx" /> reflects <InlineMath math="x\in\mathbb{R}^m" /> across the hyperplane perpendicular to <InlineMath math="v" />

4. <InlineMath math="\text{cond}_2(S) = 1" />

## Householder Procedure

Let <InlineMath math="A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}" /> satisfy <InlineMath math="m\geq n" /> and <InlineMath math="\operatorname{rank}(A)=n" />. Each Householder step zeros the entries below one diagonal position while leaving the zeros created by earlier steps unchanged.

Start with <InlineMath math="A_1=A" />. Reflect its first column onto a multiple of the first coordinate vector:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\tilde{a}_1 \text{ first column of } A_1" />
<BlockMath math="\pm\|\tilde{a}_1\|_2 \cdot e_1 \in \mathbb{R}^m \text{ target vector}" />
<BlockMath math="v_1 = u_1/\|u_1\|_2" />
</MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="u_1 = \tilde{a}_1 \mp \|\tilde{a}_1\|_2 \cdot e_1" />

### Iterative Transformation Process

With <InlineMath math="S_1=I_m-2v_1v_1^T" />, the first column below the diagonal becomes zero:

<BlockMath math="A_2 = S_1 \cdot A_1 = \begin{pmatrix} r_{11} & * \\ 0 & \tilde{A}_2 \end{pmatrix}" />

In the matrix <InlineMath math="A_2" />, <InlineMath math="r_{11} = \pm\|\tilde{a}_1\|_2" /> and <InlineMath math="\tilde{A}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{m-1 \times n-1}" />.

Apply the next reflector only to the remaining lower-right submatrix:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\tilde{a}_2 \text{ first column of } \tilde{A}_2" />
<BlockMath math="\pm\|\tilde{a}_2\|_2 \cdot \tilde{e}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{m-1}" />
<BlockMath math="v_2 = u_2/\|u_2\|_2" />
</MathContainer>

<BlockMath math="u_2 = \tilde{a}_2 \mp \|\tilde{a}_2\|_2 \cdot \tilde{e}_1" />

Embed the second reflector in the lower-right block of the identity matrix:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\tilde{S}_2 = I_{m-1} - 2v_2v_2^T \in \mathbb{R}^{m-1 \times m-1}" />
<BlockMath math="S_2 = \begin{pmatrix} I_1 & 0 \\ 0 & \tilde{S}_2 \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}" />
</MathContainer>

The second column below the diagonal then becomes zero:

<BlockMath math="A_3 = S_2 \cdot A_2 = \begin{pmatrix} r_{11} & * & * \\ 0 & r_{22} & * \\ 0 & 0 & \tilde{A}_3 \end{pmatrix}" />

Here <InlineMath math="r_{22}=\pm\|\widetilde a_2\|_2" /> and <InlineMath math="\widetilde A_3\in\mathbb{R}^{(m-2)\times(n-2)}" />. Repeating the same operation leaves the final active column

<BlockMath math="\tilde{A}_n = \begin{pmatrix} \tilde{a}_{nn} \\ \vdots \\ \tilde{a}_{mn} \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{m-(n-1) \times 1}" />

After the last reflection, the transformed matrix is upper triangular:

<BlockMath math="A_{n+1} = R = \begin{pmatrix} r_{11} & & * \\ & \ddots & \\ 0 & & r_{nn} \\ 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & & \vdots \\ 0 & \cdots & 0 \end{pmatrix} = S_n \cdot \ldots \cdot S_1 \cdot A = Q^T \cdot A" />

Because each reflector is orthogonal and symmetric, their product gives

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="A = Q \cdot R" />
<BlockMath math="Q = (S_n \cdot \ldots \cdot S_1)^T = S_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot S_n" />
</MathContainer>

The matrix <InlineMath math="Q" /> is orthogonal because it is a product of orthogonal reflectors.

## Householder Algorithm

Initialize the active matrix and accumulated orthogonal factor:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="A_1 := A" />
<BlockMath math="Q_1 := I" />
</MathContainer>

At step <InlineMath math="i" />, take the active part of column <InlineMath math="i" />:

<BlockMath math="\tilde{a}_i = \begin{pmatrix} a_{ii} \\ \vdots \\ a_{mi} \end{pmatrix} := \begin{pmatrix} (A_i)_{ii} \\ \vdots \\ (A_i)_{mi} \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{m-i+1}" />

Compute its norm, choose a sign that avoids cancellation, and form the Householder vector:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="\sigma := \|\tilde{a}_i\|_2" />
<BlockMath math="s_i:=\begin{cases}1,&\tilde a_{ii}\ge0,\\-1,&\tilde a_{ii}<0,\end{cases}" />
<BlockMath math="u_i := \tilde{a}_i+s_i\sigma e_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{m-i+1}" />
<BlockMath math="\hat{u}_i := \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \\ u_i \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^m" />
</MathContainer>

The corresponding scale factor is

<BlockMath math="\beta := \frac{2}{u_i^Tu_i}=\frac{1}{\sigma(\sigma+|\tilde a_{ii}|)}" />

The normalized vector and the full reflector are

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="v_i=\frac{u_i}{\|u_i\|_2}" />
<BlockMath math="\tilde{S}_i = I_{m-i+1} - 2v_i v_i^T = I_{m-i+1} - \beta u_i u_i^T" />
<BlockMath math="S_i = I_m - \beta \hat{u}_i \hat{u}_i^T" />
</MathContainer>

Update the active matrix and the accumulated factor without forming an extra dense product:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="A_{i+1} := S_i A_i = (I_m - \beta \hat{u}_i \hat{u}_i^T) A_i = A_i - \hat{u}_i (\beta \hat{u}_i^T A_i)" />
<BlockMath math="Q_{i+1} := Q_i S_i = Q_i (I_m - \beta \hat{u}_i \hat{u}_i^T) = Q_i - (Q_i \hat{u}_i) \beta \hat{u}_i^T" />
</MathContainer>

After <InlineMath math="n" /> steps,

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="R := A_{n+1}" />
<BlockMath math="Q := Q_{n+1}" />
</MathContainer>

## Numerical Aspects and Householder Complexity

### Computational Complexity

For <InlineMath math="m\ge n" />, a <InlineMath math="\mathrm{QR}" /> decomposition with Householder reflectors requires approximately

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="2mn^2-\frac{2}{3}n^3\text{ floating-point operations}" />
<BlockMath math="\frac{4}{3}n^3\text{ floating-point operations when }m=n" />
</MathContainer>

### Numerical Properties

1. Orthogonal multiplication preserves singular values, so <InlineMath math="\operatorname{cond}_2(R)=\operatorname{cond}_2(A)" /> for a full-column-rank matrix.

2. At step <InlineMath math="i" />, the new diagonal entry is <InlineMath math="\pm\|\widetilde a_i\|_2" />. Choose the sign below while computing the reflector to avoid subtracting nearly equal numbers:

   <MathContainer>
   <BlockMath math="u_i = \tilde{a}_i + s_i\|\tilde{a}_i\|_2e_1" />
   <BlockMath math="s_i=1\text{ when }\tilde a_{ii}\ge0,\qquad s_i=-1\text{ otherwise}" />
   </MathContainer>

   After the factorization, matching signs in one column of <InlineMath math="Q" /> and one row of <InlineMath math="R" /> can be flipped to make the diagonal of <InlineMath math="R" /> positive. If <InlineMath math="\|\widetilde a_i\|_2=0" />, the matrix lacks full column rank and this uniqueness condition no longer applies. A column-pivoted, rank-revealing <InlineMath math="\mathrm{QR}" /> decomposition is more informative when numerical rank matters.

3. A compact implementation stores each Householder vector <InlineMath math="u_i" /> below the diagonal of the working copy of <InlineMath math="A" /> and stores the diagonal entries separately. The reflectors can then be applied without constructing <InlineMath math="Q" /> explicitly.

4. The reduced factorization keeps the first <InlineMath math="n" /> columns of <InlineMath math="Q" /> and the first <InlineMath math="n" /> rows of <InlineMath math="R" />.

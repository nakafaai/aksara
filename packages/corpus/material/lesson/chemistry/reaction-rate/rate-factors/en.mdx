## Measuring Reaction Rates

A fireworks flare flashes away in a fraction of a second while an iron gate rusts over years. The **rate of reaction** tracks how quickly a reactant disappears or a product forms per unit time, governed at the molecular scale by **collision theory**.

<BlockMath math="\text{Rate} = \frac{\Delta\text{quantity}}{\Delta t}" />

Chemists track gas volume, mass loss, or precipitate timing to control industrial yields and slow food spoilage. Two complementary views describe any reaction curve: the curve height shows total product formed, whereas the steepness shows how fast it forms.

## Collision Theory Factors

Reactant particles react only during an **effective collision**: they must collide with both the **correct orientation** and kinetic energy at or above the **activation energy** <InlineMath math="E_a" />.

| Factor raised | Microscopic change | Why rate increases |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Surface area | More exposed solid faces | Higher collision frequency at the surface |
| Concentration | More particles per unit volume | Higher collision frequency throughout solution |
| Gas pressure | Closer gas molecules | Higher collision frequency inside the vessel |
| Temperature | Faster particles with wider energy tail | More collisions plus larger fraction above activation energy |

> Raising temperature never lowers the activation energy barrier, instead it pushes far more particles past that existing threshold.

## Catalysts and Pathways

A **catalyst** speeds up a chemical reaction without being permanently consumed. Rather than heating particles up, a catalyst opens an **alternative reaction pathway** with a lower activation energy <InlineMath math="E_a'" />.

> A catalyst lowers the energy mountain peak without shifting reactant energy, product energy, enthalpy change, or final product yield.

## Rate Calculation Checks

Verify two core rate and energy-profile calculations below.

**Hydrogen rate check.** Zinc reacts with sulfuric acid to release <InlineMath math="36\text{ cm}^3" /> of hydrogen gas in <InlineMath math="90\text{ s}" />. Find the average rate of reaction.

<BlockMath math="\text{Average rate} = \frac{36}{90} = 0.40\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" />

Multiplying <InlineMath math="0.40\text{ cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}" /> by <InlineMath math="90\text{ s}" /> recovers the full <InlineMath math="36\text{ cm}^3" /> collected.

**Catalysed barrier check.** An exothermic reaction has uncatalysed activation energy <InlineMath math="80\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, catalysed activation energy <InlineMath math="50\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />, and enthalpy change <InlineMath math="-30\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />. Find the height of the catalysed peak above the product level.

<BlockMath math="E_{\text{reverse}}' = 50 + 30 = 80\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" />

The catalyst trims <InlineMath math="30\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> off the peak while leaving the <InlineMath math="30\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}" /> gap between reactants and products untouched.

## Polymers Are Built from Monomers

A **polymer** is a large molecule formed when many small molecules, **monomers**, join into a long chain. The process is called **polymerisation**. The relative molecular mass of a polymer is <InlineMath math="n" /> times the mass of its repeating unit, where <InlineMath math="n" /> is the number of repeating units. In these notes you rotate monomer models, count the monomers in a chain, compare addition with condensation polymerisation, and see how natural rubber is made stronger.

| Polymer | Monomer | Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Starch | Glucose | Natural |
| Protein | Amino acids | Natural |
| Natural rubber | Isoprene | Natural |
| Polythene | Ethene | Synthetic |
| Polyvinyl chloride, PVC | Chloroethene | Synthetic |
| Polypropene | Propene | Synthetic |
| Nylon | Hexanedioic acid and hexane-1,6-diamine | Synthetic |

## Addition Polymerisation

In addition polymerisation, the double bond of each monomer opens and the monomers join **without losing any atoms**. Ethene becomes polythene:

<BlockMath math="n\,\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2} \rightarrow \mathrm{-[CH_2{-}CH_2]_n-}" />

## Condensation Polymerisation

In **condensation polymerisation**, each monomer has two functional groups, and every time two monomers join, a small molecule such as water is **eliminated**. Nylon-6,6 forms from hexanedioic acid and hexane-1,6-diamine:

<BlockMath math="n\,\mathrm{HOOC(CH_2)_4COOH} + n\,\mathrm{H_2N(CH_2)_6NH_2} \rightarrow \mathrm{-[OC(CH_2)_4CONH(CH_2)_6NH]_n-} + 2n\,\mathrm{H_2O}" />

One mole of the acid has a mass of <InlineMath math="146\text{ g}" /> and one mole of the diamine <InlineMath math="116\text{ g}" />, <InlineMath math="262\text{ g}" /> in total. The nylon repeating unit has a mass of <InlineMath math="226\text{ g}" />, so <InlineMath math="36\text{ g}" /> leaves as two moles of water. ## Natural Rubber and Vulcanisation

**Natural rubber** is a polymer of isoprene that comes from the **latex** of the rubber tree. Latex is a suspension of rubber particles surrounded by a **negatively charged** protein membrane. That negative charge makes the particles repel each other, so latex stays liquid.

When acid is added, <InlineMath math="\mathrm{H^+}" /> ions neutralise the negative charge. The rubber particles no longer repel, so they collide and the **latex coagulates**. Latex also coagulates slowly on its own because bacteria produce lactic acid. To stop it coagulating in storage, **ammonia** is added: its <InlineMath math="\mathrm{OH^-}" /> ions neutralise the acid that forms, so the membranes stay negatively charged.

**Vulcanisation** is heating rubber with sulfur. Sulfur atoms form **cross-links** between the polymer chains. Those cross-links stop the chains sliding past each other, so vulcanised rubber is:

| Property | Unvulcanised rubber | Vulcanised rubber |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Elasticity | Less elastic, stays stretched | More elastic, returns to its shape |
| Strength | Weak and soft | Stronger and harder |
| Heat resistance | Turns sticky when hot | Resists heat |
| Resistance to oxidation | Oxidises easily | More resistant |

**Synthetic rubber**, such as neoprene, resists oil and heat, so it's used for oil hoses and wetsuits. But most synthetic polymers are **not biodegradable**. Burning PVC releases toxic hydrogen chloride, so plastics need to be reduced, reused and recycled.

## Common Mistakes about Polymers

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | How to check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Every monomer can undergo addition polymerisation. | Addition polymerisation needs a <InlineMath math="\mathrm{C{=}C}" /> double bond. | Ethane has no double bond, so it forms no polymer. |
| The repeating unit of polythene is <InlineMath math="\mathrm{-CH_2{=}CH_2-}" />. | The double bond opens during polymerisation, so the repeating unit is <InlineMath math="\mathrm{-CH_2{-}CH_2-}" />. | Every carbon atom in the chain must have four bonds. |
| A condensation polymer has the same mass as the monomers. | A small molecule such as water is eliminated, so the polymer is lighter. | Subtract the mass of water from the total monomer mass. |
| Ammonia is added to coagulate latex. | Ammonia keeps the membranes negatively charged, so latex stays liquid. | It's acid that coagulates latex. |
| Vulcanisation makes rubber softer. | Sulfur cross-links make rubber stronger, more elastic and heat-resistant. | Compare a car tyre with an old rubber band. |

The fourth mistake comes up most often. Keep this pair in mind: **acid coagulates latex, ammonia keeps it liquid**.

## Practice on Polymers

These questions move from counting monomers to types of polymerisation and rubber. **Show the bond that opens** when you write a repeating unit.

1. A PVC chain has a relative molecular mass of <InlineMath math="125\,000" />. Calculate the number of chloroethene monomers, <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHCl}" />, in the chain. The relative atomic mass of Cl is <InlineMath math="35.5" />.

2. Name the monomer of polypropene and write its repeating unit.

3. Explain why ammonia is added to freshly tapped latex.

4. Explain why vulcanised rubber is more elastic than unvulcanised rubber.

### Solutions

**Solution 1**: Work out the monomer's mass, then divide:

<MathContainer>
<BlockMath math="M(\mathrm{C_2H_3Cl}) = 2 \times 12 + 3 \times 1 + 35.5 = 62.5" />
<BlockMath math="n = \frac{125\,000}{62.5} = 2000" />
</MathContainer>

Check with the mass graph: the PVC reading at <InlineMath math="n = 2000" /> is <InlineMath math="62.5 \times 2000 = 125\,000" />.

**Solution 2**: The monomer of polypropene is **propene**, <InlineMath math="\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHCH_3}" />. The double bond opens, so the repeating unit has a methyl group as a branch:

<BlockMath math="\mathrm{-[CH_2{-}CH(CH_3)]_n-}" />

Check: the repeating unit has three carbon atoms and six hydrogen atoms, the same as propene.

**Solution 3**: Bacteria in latex produce acid, and the <InlineMath math="\mathrm{H^+}" /> ions neutralise the negatively charged protein membranes. **Ammonia** is an alkali, so its <InlineMath math="\mathrm{OH^-}" /> ions neutralise that acid. The membranes **stay negatively charged**, the rubber particles keep repelling each other, and the latex doesn't coagulate.

**Solution 4**: In vulcanised rubber, **sulfur cross-links** join the polymer chains. When the rubber is stretched, the chains can't slide far past each other. When the force is removed, the cross-links **pull the chains back** to where they were, so the rubber returns to its shape.

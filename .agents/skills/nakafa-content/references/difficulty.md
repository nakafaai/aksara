# Try-out difficulty

Nakafa try-outs are known for hard items. Every item sits at or above the
level of the hardest items of the real exam, so a learner who handles a
Nakafa set is ready for the real sitting. This reference owns that bar. The
[question bank](questions.md#assessment-review) owns the general review rules,
and [try-out sets](sets.md) owns originality and the set format.

## Evidence for the bar

- TKA SMA 2025 national averages out of 100, as reported on 22 December 2025:
  Matematika 37.23, Bahasa Indonesia 57.39, Bahasa Inggris 26.71
  ([Medcom](https://www.medcom.id/pendidikan/news-pendidikan/akWmogWN-rerata-nilai-tka-bahasa-inggris-terjeblok-siswa-cuma-dapat-nilai-20-an)).
  The Pusmendik head said the English reading items turned harder in the later
  passages, where conclusions span paragraphs and infographics. A typical real
  TKA item is therefore answered correctly by fewer than half of the test
  takers.
- UTBK-SNBT publishes timing but no item statistics: 160 items in 195
  minutes, from 45 seconds per item (Pengetahuan dan Pemahaman Umum) to 127.5
  seconds (Penalaran Matematika), per the
  [SNPMB framework](http://snpmb.id/fr). Time pressure is the official
  calibration, so a Nakafa item must use most of its time budget.
- The TU Darmstadt T-Kurs sample has 8 six-option tasks in 40 minutes
  ([sample](https://www.stk.tu-darmstadt.de/media/stk/beispieleprfungen/atmatbeispiel.pdf)).
  Most sample tasks need one or two steps; the hard ones prune a root or rank
  unknowns. Other Studienkollegs set parameter cases, radical and exponential
  equations, and full curve sketches, so a five-minute Darmstadt task carries
  three to five linked steps.

## What every item needs

- **Two or more linked decisions.** The answer needs at least two dependent
  steps, and no step is a single formula plug-in or a lookup. A
  knowledge-and-understanding item tests a concept through a trap, never by
  recall alone.
- **A plausible wrong route behind every distractor.** Each wrong option is
  the result of one named slip a capable candidate makes: stopping one step
  early, the wrong base for a percentage, a plain average of rates, a boundary
  or strictness error, a sign flip, a swapped order of operations or
  transformations, a lost case, a root lost by dividing by a factor that can
  be zero, an extraneous root kept after squaring, a condition in the wrong
  direction, area scaling used for volume, or a per-period rate read as
  cumulative. The worked answer names the slip for each option.
- **A moved admissible set where it fits.** A domain, an integer condition, a
  strict inequality, a quadrant, a capacity, a unit, or a data condition
  changes which candidate answer survives.
- **Every statement earns its verdict.** In select-all and Benar/Salah items
  each statement needs its own proof or counterexample, true and false
  statements mix, and at least one statement holds or fails only under a
  condition a quick reading misses.
- **Real time pressure.** A well-prepared candidate needs most of the
  official time per item. A set never contains a run of items that a strong
  candidate answers in seconds.

Hard never means obscure wording, oversized arithmetic, a hidden trick that
the prompt does not support, a data dump that no question uses, options one
unit apart, or two defensible answers. Every item still states everything it
needs and has exactly the intended answers.

## Bar per section

| Section | Official budget | What the hard item looks like | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SNBT Penalaran Umum | 30 items, 60 s each, three 10-item blocks | Arrangements with interacting constraints and several cases to count; exactly-one-true puzzles; integer optimisation with thresholds; data inference whose tempting reading overreaches; Benar/Salah sets that need every valid case checked | Options unrelated to the stem; one-step arithmetic; a pattern visible at a glance |
| SNBT Pengetahuan dan Pemahaman Umum | 20 items, 45 s each | Analogies whose wrong options share one feature with the key; figurative versus literal sense; reverse logic where four options read the argument and one misreads it | Labeling connectives or head nouns; definitions a dictionary answers |
| SNBT Pemahaman Bacaan dan Menulis | 20 items, 75 s each | Reference chains for sentence order; concision that keeps a named nuance; deletion or insertion by repetition or reference; diction that changes meaning | Title items that are off topic; spelling trivia |
| SNBT Pengetahuan Kuantitatif | 20 items, 60 s each | Exact counts or extremes on a restricted domain; hidden sign or domain conditions; ordered versus unordered and with versus without replacement; data sufficiency where a single statement almost suffices; numeric entries with four steps and no options to check against | One-substitution algebra; plain percent or intercept lookups |
| SNBT Literasi dalam Bahasa Indonesia | 30 items, 85 s each | Category verdicts that each need their own evidence; causal claims that the data cannot settle; denominator and scope of a percentage; implied intent across paragraphs | Main-idea items with absolute wording; facts stated in one sentence |
| SNBT Literasi dalam Bahasa Inggris | 20 items, 60 s each | Figurative phrases with literal traps; relations between two texts (qualify versus reject, shared versus one-sided view); the tempting naive reading the passage itself corrects | Attitude items with extreme distractors; echo answers |
| SNBT Penalaran Matematika | 20 items, 127.5 s each | Capped stock or battery where order matters; whole packs after a reserve or fee; the first time a running total crosses a target; claims true only under a hidden condition; integer entries limited by a floor or bottleneck | Single-operation items; options one unit apart |
| TKA Matematika | 25 items, 180 s each | Certainty over all consistent data; integer optimum away from the corner, with sensitivity statements; strict thresholds; dependent stages in probability; composed transformations; similarity scaling of area and volume | Frequency-table means; one-formula plug-ins |
| TKA Matematika Tingkat Lanjut | 25 items, 144 s each | Hidden polynomial structure; limit existence that sets parameter conditions; composed transformations with clipping; case splits from domain conditions | One-substitution determinants or derivatives |
| TKA Bahasa Indonesia | 25 items, 180 s each | Two-text synthesis where distractors keep half of the shared ground; select-all overreach; non-linear chronology; projected data read as records | Absurd distractors around one real contrast |
| TKA Bahasa Inggris | 25 items, 180 s each | Numeric reading with unit or partial-sum traps; select-all with condition or category errors; evidence status (supported, contradicted, not stated); multi-paragraph conclusions and infographics | Echo answers; absolute distractors |
| Aufnahmeprüfung TU Darmstadt | 8 tasks, 300 s each, no aids | A hidden condition that prunes one candidate answer (extraneous or lost roots, isolated points, quadrant); parameter cases; unstated geometric auxiliaries; moving-base percentages and exponential thresholds; nested roots | One-formula plug-ins; arithmetic that a calculator would decide |

## Calibration from the pilot

The pilot rewrote Aufnahmeprüfung set 1 and TKA Matematika set 1 and rated
old and new items together in blinded panels (two raters, items mixed and
unlabeled, keys shown). Mean estimated success of real test takers and mean
level against the real exam:

| Set | Old | Rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aufnahmeprüfung set 1, final | 79 percent, level 1.9 | 34 percent, level 3.9, every item at least 3.5 |
| TKA Matematika set 1, second round | 47 percent, level 2.8 | 34 percent, level 3.4, still under the bar |

For Aufnahmeprüfung set 1 the odds of a wrong answer rose from 0.27 to 1.92,
seven times the old odds, and a well-prepared candidate needs 2.4 times as long.

The same old set rated level 1.75 to 2.0 and 2.8 to 3.1 across four panels,
so a panel mean moves by about 0.2 between runs. What lifted an item to level 4
in every round was a second, different idea: a condition that removes a
candidate, a case split, a carried result, or a statement judged over all
consistent data. A single known trick (a reflection for the shortest path, two
reflections as a rotation, the cosine rule, a two-row Bayes table, a lap length,
a vertex from two intercepts) stayed at level 3. Pairs of old and new items on
the same skill show the step:

- Inequalities: old, a linear inequality; new, a fraction with a double root
  and a pole, where the double root is an isolated solution and the pole is
  excluded.
- Percentages: old, a rise and a fall of the same percent; new, a solution
  replaced by water twice, where the linear reading and the one-step reading
  give the tempting wrong answers and a second root exceeds the container.
- Functions: old, read a vertex; new, the number of solutions of an absolute
  value equation, which needs the reflected graph and a count over every level.
- Linear programming: old, find the optimum; new, the optimum plus sensitivity
  statements on a profit change and a capacity change (level 4.5).

## Measuring a set

- Two blind solvers on a key-free view must agree with the key on every item,
  as the [release gate](verification.md#release-gate) requires. A strong
  solver model answers old and new items alike, so its errors do not measure
  difficulty, and its wall time follows server load: the same eight items took
  52 seconds in one run and about 1,100 seconds in another.
- Difficulty is measured by a blinded rater panel: two raters score the old
  and the new set mixed in one unlabeled view, with keys shown, for the
  percent of real test takers who would answer fully correctly, the minutes a
  well-prepared candidate needs, the dependent steps, and a level from 1 to 5
  against the real exam (3 a typical real item, 5 the hardest real items).
- A rewritten set passes when, averaged over both raters, no item rates below
  3.5, the mean level is at least 3.9, and the mean estimated success is at
  most the target below. Where no public results exist, as for the
  Aufnahmeprüfung, the odds of a wrong answer must instead be at least four
  times the old set's odds in the same panel. Rewrite every item that misses
  its own bar and rate the set again.

| Sections | Mean success at most | No item above |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mathematics and quantitative sections, Penalaran Umum | 25 percent | 40 percent |
| TKA Bahasa Inggris | 20 percent | 35 percent |
| Other literacy, reading and language sections | 35 percent | 50 percent |

- Run the similarity check on every set: it reports items that read alike,
  reused passages, and formula pairs, the same formulas with new numbers in
  another set and in any language.
- Keep the panel records, the blind solves and the originality reviews outside
  the publication source, and cite them in the pull request.
